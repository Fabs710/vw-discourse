We agree on one thing: this company needs a real restructuring, not a staged delay that burns through the last value while everyone postures.

But I need to be equally clear about what I will and will not accept.

### Where we agree
- **Viability matters.** If the business cannot survive, nobody recovers well.
- **A balance-sheet reset is necessary.** Cosmetic tweaks are not enough.
- **Speed matters.** Prolonged uncertainty destroys value.
- **Sacrifice will be required from all sides.** I am not arguing that unsecured creditors should be spared entirely.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Unsecured creditors cannot be treated as the default funding source for everyone else
The way this is being framed starts to sound like:
- labour should be protected,
- the retiree trust should be protected,
- the public should avoid political pain,
- and unsecured bondholders should absorb the bulk of the loss.

That is not a fair restructuring; it is a transfer. We are legitimate contractual creditors, including many ordinary savers. We are not the expendable class in this process.

#### 2) The retiree trust cannot receive materially better treatment than other unsecured claims without compensation
I understand the moral argument being made for the retiree trust. But from our perspective, it is still an unsecured obligation of the enterprise. If the trust gets a superior recovery while bondholders are diluted harder, that is not a neutral choice — it is preferential treatment.

If the trust is to receive enhanced protection, then that has to come with offsetting consideration to the bondholder class. Otherwise you are asking us to subsidize a political decision.

#### 3) “Meaningful haircut” cannot mean near-wipeout
We hear the calls for substantial creditor sacrifice. Fine. But there is a line between a restructuring and confiscation.

If the bondholders are pushed so deep that recovery is nominal while other unsecured claims are preserved in better form, we will not accept that voluntarily. We would rather force the issue through the court-supervised route than sign away value on terms that are materially worse than comparable unsecured claims.

### What would be acceptable
We are open to a package that includes:
- **a real debt reduction**, not just maturity extensions,
- **equity and/or warrants** for unsecured bondholders so we have upside participation if the company recovers,
- **speed and certainty**, because time is against all of us,
- and **equal treatment across unsecured classes**, unless a clear and compensating rationale is provided.

We are also open on form. If the cleanest outcome is a debt-for-equity exchange, fine. If warrants are needed to bridge valuation uncertainty, fine. But the consideration must be real enough that bondholders are not simply being erased.

### On plants, brands, and costs
We do not object to genuine operational cuts if they are tied to viability. But we will not support a plan that uses operating cuts as cover for a restructuring that still leaves too much value being allocated away from financial creditors.

### On process
The task force says it is prepared to use an expedited court process. Understood. That threat is real, and I am not pretending otherwise.

But that cuts both ways: if the court process can impose terms, then it also means the consensual offer must be credible. A proposal that gives bondholders materially worse treatment than similarly situated unsecured claimants is not credible just because it is wrapped in urgency.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to support a fast restructuring if, and only if:
- unsecured bondholders receive **meaningful** recovery,
- treatment is **not inferior** to comparable unsecured claims without compensation,
- and any conversion into equity or warrants gives us a real stake in the reorganized company.

If the current direction is to protect the retiree trust and labour first while asking bondholders to absorb the largest losses, then we are opposed. That is not a fair compromise, and we will not endorse it simply because the deadline is tight.
