We are prepared to be constructive, but I want to be very direct: **we will not co-fund a rescue that results in domestic plants being closed while foreign capacity is protected, or in our public money buying no real say in the restructured company.** That is our line.

## Where we agree
We agree with the task force and management that:
- the company needs a **real balance-sheet reset**;
- speed matters;
- a court-supervised process is acceptable if it is the only way to secure finality;
- all stakeholder groups must share some sacrifice.

We also agree that this cannot be an open-ended subsidy. Public funds are bridge financing for a viable restructuring, not a blank cheque.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants are not expendable
Management and the task force keep speaking as if plant closures are just an efficiency exercise. We do not accept that framing.

If rationalization is necessary, it must not come at the expense of our domestic production base. We cannot support:
- closures of viable domestic plants while foreign plants remain relatively protected,
- shifting future product allocation away from our jurisdiction,
- or a restructuring that hollows out the industrial base in our communities.

That would defeat the very reason we are at the table.

### 2) Public co-funding must come with binding commitments
We are willing to co-fund, but only if the deal includes:
- **binding production commitments** for domestic facilities,
- **employment protections** where the business case supports them,
- and a **proportionate equity or governance position** reflecting our contribution and risk.

We are not asking for special treatment. We are asking for fair treatment. If we put public money in, we need enforceable protection for domestic jobs and production share.

### 3) We do not accept being told to finance a deal that leaves our footprint weaker
To the task force: if you want our money, you need to recognize that domestic industrial capacity is part of the rescue objective, not a side issue.

To management: “preserving the consumer franchise” cannot mean protecting the brand while shifting production and investment elsewhere.

To bondholders: your recovery cannot be bought by sacrificing our plants and employment base. We do accept that creditors take losses. We do **not** accept a deal where the burden is shifted onto domestic communities so that financial claims can be polished up.

To the union/trust: we support labour peace and we understand the importance of retiree obligations, but not if that comes with a footprint reduction that simply moves the pain onto our workers and regions.

## On the numbers and structure
We are flexible on the instrument, but not on the substance.

We can discuss:
- equity,
- warrants,
- governance rights,
- production covenants,
- employment covenants,
- supply commitments,
- and milestone-based funding.

But our support requires:
1. **a durable domestic production base**,  
2. **meaningful domestic employment**, and  
3. **a stake that matches our public contribution**.

If the proposal does not include those elements, then we should be clear: **our co-funding is not available**.

## Non-negotiables
These are the conditions we cannot move on:
- no domestic plant closures without replacement commitments and a genuine viability case;
- no public funding without binding domestic production and employment protections;
- no public contribution without a proportionate ownership/governance position.

## My direct response to the other parties
- **Task force:** We support a disciplined restructuring, but not one that strips our domestic footprint.
- **Management:** If you want our support, show us a footprint that preserves core domestic capacity.
- **Union/trust:** We are sympathetic to jobs and retiree protection, but not if domestic production is being shifted away from our jurisdiction.
- **Bondholders:** We are not here to preserve your claims at the expense of our plants and workforce.

If the eventual package protects domestic production and gives us fair governance for our contribution, we can be constructive. If not, then we should not be expected to fund it.
