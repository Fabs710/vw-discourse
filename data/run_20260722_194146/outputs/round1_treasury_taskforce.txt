The government task force’s position is straightforward:

We will support a restructuring only if it is deep enough, fast enough, and credible enough to produce a viable company without recurring taxpayer support.

That means:

- **No open-ended subsidy** for an unrestructured carmaker.
- **Sacrifices from all private claimants**: management, labour, and unsecured creditors.
- **A meaningful balance-sheet reset**, not just operating cuts.
- **A plan that can be completed within the 60-day window**, or, if necessary, through an expedited court-supervised process that can impose terms.

Our priorities are:

1. **Viability**
   - The company must come out with a cost structure and debt burden it can actually sustain.
   - Plant, brand, and dealer cuts need to be substantial enough to match demand and cash generation.

2. **Fair burden-sharing**
   - Labour has to accept real concessions.
   - The retiree health obligation cannot remain untouched in its current form if it makes the company unworkable.
   - Unsecured bondholders must take a meaningful haircut. They cannot be protected at taxpayer expense.

3. **Speed and finality**
   - Delay destroys value and increases systemic risk.
   - We will not use public funds to finance prolonged bargaining over who gets spared.

4. **Taxpayer protection**
   - Public money is bridge financing, not permanent capital.
   - Any equity received by the public side should reflect the scale of support and restructuring risk.

What we are **not** prepared to do:

- Keep funding the company while stakeholders hold out for better treatment.
- Preserve every plant, brand, or claim and pretend the business is still viable.
- Accept a plan that shifts losses onto the public while creditors and labour are made whole.

So my opening position is: **bring us a genuinely aggressive restructuring proposal with real concessions from labour and creditors, and we can support it. If not, we are prepared to proceed through the court-supervised route and impose the necessary terms.**
