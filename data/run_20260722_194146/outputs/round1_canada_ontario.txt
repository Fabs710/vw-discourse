We are prepared to support a rescue, but only on terms that protect our domestic production footprint and employment in a real, enforceable way.

### Where we agree
- **The company needs a deep restructuring.** Cosmetic cuts will not work.
- **Speed matters.** Delay only destroys value and jobs.
- **All stakeholders must share sacrifice.** That includes labour, creditors, management, and the public side.
- **A court-supervised route is acceptable if it is the only way to get to a credible deal.**

### Where we disagree

#### 1) Domestic plants cannot be collateral damage
We cannot support a plan that closes plants in our jurisdiction while leaving foreign sites relatively protected. That is exactly the outcome we are here to prevent.

If plant rationalization is necessary, it must be:
- tied to objective viability needs,
- balanced across the footprint,
- and accompanied by commitments to preserve core domestic production and jobs.

A restructuring that shifts volume, investment, and future production away from our plants is not acceptable, even if it is dressed up as “efficiency.”

#### 2) We will not fund the rescue without binding commitments
We are willing to co-fund, but not as a blank cheque.

If we are asked to put public money into this rescue, then we need:
- **binding production commitments** for domestic facilities,
- **employment protections** where the business case supports them,
- and **a proportionate ownership position** in the restructured company reflecting our contribution and risk.

We are not asking for special treatment. We are asking for fair treatment in exchange for real money and real political responsibility.

#### 3) Fair burden-sharing must include the footprint, not just the balance sheet
I hear the bondholders’ concern about being singled out, and I agree they should not be made whole at taxpayer expense. But it is equally unacceptable if the burden-sharing is achieved by hollowing out domestic industry while financial investors are given a cleaner path through the restructuring.

Likewise, I agree with labour that the retiree obligation should not be treated as disposable. But protecting labour and retirees while cutting domestic production capacity would simply shift the pain onto our economies and workforces. That is not an equitable solution.

### On the structure of any deal
We are flexible on the instrument:
- equity,
- warrants,
- supply commitments,
- production covenants,
- employment undertakings,
- governance rights.

But the outcome must secure:
1. **a durable domestic production base**,  
2. **meaningful domestic employment**, and  
3. **a stake that matches our financing contribution**.

If those conditions are not in the deal, then we should not co-fund it.

### On process
We are not attached to out-of-court resolution for its own sake. But if the process moves toward court supervision, then that process must still preserve the domestic footprint. We will not accept a court outcome as a reason to disregard our interests.

### Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:

- We are prepared to co-fund.
- We are prepared to accept restructuring.
- We are prepared to work within the lead funder’s process.

But we are **not** prepared to finance a deal that results in:
- domestic plant closures without replacement commitments,
- loss of domestic production share,
- or a public contribution without a proportionate stake and enforceable protections.

If those commitments are in the package, we can be constructive. If not, we should be clear that our support is not available.
