We agree on the central objective: **the company has to survive as a going concern**, and survival now requires a **real balance-sheet reset**, not another delay masked as a plan.

But I need to be equally direct about where I agree and where I do not.

## Where I agree
- **The task force is right** that the existing capital structure is not supportable.
- **The union is right** that retirees and workers are not just a line item and that we need a durable solution, not a hollow promise.
- **Bondholders are right** that they are legitimate creditors and should receive real consideration, not symbolic treatment.
- **Neighbouring governments are right** that domestic production matters and that any public money should buy enforceable commitments.

## Where I disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot remain intact in its current form
I agree it is deferred compensation, not a speculative investment. But if we keep the current retiree obligation anywhere near its present shape and size, the company will not be viable.

So I do **not** support:
- preserving the trust “whole” while forcing most of the burden onto bondholders or the public,
- or turning the company into a permanently cash-drained entity.

What I do support is:
- converting the retiree obligation into a **reduced but durable instrument**,
- with **some equity or equity-linked value** so retirees have a real stake,
- but sized so the company can actually finance operations.

That is the only way the promise becomes credible.

### 2) Bondholders must take a deep haircut
I agree with the task force here: the unsecured debt burden is too large. We need a **meaningful reduction**, not maturity gimmicks.

But I also agree with the bondholders on one point: they should not be singled out as the only class absorbing pain. They will not be.

So my position is:
- **deep haircut on unsecured debt**,  
- with the remaining value converted into **new equity and warrants**,  
- but not so punitive that it destroys market credibility for the reorganized company.

If we want financing certainty and future access to capital, the bondholder treatment must be tough but still intelligible.

### 3) Labour concessions are necessary, but they must be tied to viability
We cannot carry legacy wage and work-rule structures as if nothing happened. That would just postpone insolvency.

So I support:
- wage moderation,
- work-rule changes,
- staffing reductions aligned with plant consolidation,
- and changes to legacy cost structures.

But I do **not** support demands that strip the workforce to the bone or use “fairness” rhetoric to justify destroying labour peace. A workforce that believes it has been sacrificed will not help rebuild this company.

### 4) Plant and brand cuts must be deep, but selective
I strongly disagree with any approach that preserves everything. That is not a restructuring.

But I also disagree with indiscriminate cuts that damage the customer franchise and consumer confidence.

We should:
- protect the **strongest brands**,
- keep the **core plants** that have viable product lines,
- rationalize the **dealer network** to match demand,
- and eliminate weak, redundant, or unprofitable capacity.

We should **not**:
- close core facilities that are essential to the future portfolio,
- or take actions that trigger consumer flight by making the company look like it is disintegrating.

## On the public side
I understand the task force’s insistence on taxpayer protection. That is proper.

But public funding has to be used to **bridge** to a viable company, not to finance endless bargaining or to force a liquidation by attrition. If the court-supervised route is needed to get finality, we should be prepared to use it.

## My position on the structure
At this stage, the outline I can support is:

- **Unsecured bondholders:** deep haircut, with most of the residual value in new equity and warrants.
- **Retiree health trust:** reduced and restructured into a durable instrument, with some equity-linked participation.
- **Labour:** material but bounded concessions on wages, work rules, and staffing.
- **Plants / brands / dealers:** substantial consolidation, but focused on preserving the profitable core.
- **Public financing:** bridge only, with governance protections and a clear exit path.
- **Process:** if voluntary agreement cannot be locked quickly, we should use the expedited court process.

## Non-negotiables from management
These are the limits I will not cross:
- No outcome that destroys the going concern.
- No plan that leaves the company with a debt and cash burden it cannot carry.
- No indiscriminate plant or brand cuts that undermine the consumer franchise.
- No “consensus” that is really just delay and denial.

## What I need next
To move this forward, we need actual numbers on:
1. the bondholder exchange ratio,
2. the size and form of the retiree trust conversion,
3. the labour cost reductions,
4. the plant/brand closures and protections,
5. and the public equity/governance package.

If the parties want management to recommend a deal, it has to preserve the business we are trying to save. If it does that, we can move. If it doesn’t, I will say so plainly.
