### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **real restructuring**, not cosmetic cuts.
- There is shared support for **speed**, **finality**, and avoiding a drawn-out collapse.
- All parties accept that **some sacrifice is required** from multiple stakeholder groups.
- There is broad agreement that **unsecured bondholders must take losses**.
- There is general openness to a **court-supervised process** if out-of-court negotiations do not produce a credible deal.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Relative priority of claims**:
  - The union/trust wants the retiree health obligation treated as a real deferred compensation claim, not subordinated to unsecured creditors.
  - Bondholders object to being used as the main source of loss absorption and reject inferior treatment versus other unsecured claims.
- **Extent of labour and retiree concessions**:
  - Government and management want deep restructuring of labour costs and retiree obligations.
  - The union will accept concessions only if they are bounded and paired with fair treatment.
- **Plant and footprint reductions**:
  - Management and the government want substantial cuts.
  - The union and neighbouring governments want protections for viable jobs, core plants, and domestic production.
- **Public support terms**:
  - The lead funder and co-funders want strong restructuring and protection for taxpayer/public funds.
  - Co-funders want binding domestic commitments and a proportionate stake.
- **Fairness of burden-sharing**:
  - Each side believes the current framing risks shifting too much burden onto it.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to balance **bondholder haircuts** against **retiree trust protection**.
- How much **labour concession** is necessary for viability, and what protections or compensation would offset it.
- Which **plants, brands, or dealers** can be cut without damaging the business or domestic economies.
- What level of **public financing** is justified, and what **equity/governance rights** accompany it.
- Whether domestic production protections can be written as **binding covenants** without making the restructuring unworkable.
- How to define “meaningful recovery” for unsecured creditors while still achieving a viable capital structure.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to numbers and structure:
- propose a **capital structure** showing creditor recoveries and equity allocation,
- specify the **labour/retiree concessions** being requested,
- identify the **plant and brand footprint** to be retained or closed,
- define any **public financing terms** and related governance rights,
- and indicate which items are **non-negotiable** versus open to compromise.
