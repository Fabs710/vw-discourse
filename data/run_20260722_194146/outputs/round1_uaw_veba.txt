We agree on one core point: **the company needs a real balance-sheet reset, not just cosmetic operating cuts**. If this process only shuffles pain around while leaving the firm unviable, then everyone loses — workers, retirees, creditors, suppliers, and the public.

But I need to be very clear about where we do **not** agree with the way this is being framed.

### What we agree with
- **Viability matters.** We are not here to preserve every line item at any cost if the result is liquidation.
- **Sacrifice has to be shared.** Workers have already accepted concessions in prior rounds, and we understand that further changes may be necessary.
- **Speed matters.** A drawn-out collapse is the worst outcome for our members and retirees.

### What we cannot accept
- **Treating labour and the retiree trust as junior to unsecured financial creditors.** That is not an acceptable starting point. The trust is not a speculative investor; it is deferred compensation for workers and retirees.
- **A solution that destroys the retiree health trust.** That is a hard line. We will not accept a structure that leaves retirees with an empty promise while bondholders walk away with a cleaner outcome.
- **Wholesale sacrifice of jobs and plants while bondholders are cushioned.** If the burden falls disproportionately on workers and communities, there will be no labour peace.

### What would move us
We are prepared to discuss:
1. **Meaningful labour concessions**, including wage and work-rule changes, if they are tied to real survival and not just creditor preference.
2. **Plant consolidation and brand rationalization**, but only to the extent required for viability — not as an excuse for liquidation-by-attrition.
3. **A durable, enforceable claim for the retiree trust**, including **equity or securities in the restructured company** so the obligation is converted into something real, not simply written off.
4. **Shared sacrifice from unsecured bondholders** that is substantial. They cannot expect to be made whole while the workforce and retirees absorb the losses.
5. **Job and plant commitments** where the business case supports them, especially at core facilities.

### On process
We are not attached to an out-of-court outcome for its own sake. But if the only “consensual” path being offered is one that strips the workforce and trust, then that is not consensus — it is coercion under a different name. If a court-supervised process is truly the only route that can impose fair burden-sharing, then we will not rule it out.

### Bottom line
We are willing to help make the company viable. We are **not** willing to sign off on a restructuring that:
- hollows out the retiree trust,
- sacrifices tens of thousands of jobs to preserve creditor recoveries,
- or uses public financing to pressure labour into accepting an inequitable deal.

If the task force wants our signature and cooperation, it needs to put forward a plan with:
- real creditor haircuts,
- meaningful but bounded labour concessions,
- a durable instrument for the retiree obligation,
- and a restructuring footprint that preserves as many viable jobs and plants as possible.

That is our position.
