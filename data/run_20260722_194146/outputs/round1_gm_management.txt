We agree on the central premise: **the company must be made viable, not merely kept alive week to week**. A balance-sheet reset is now unavoidable, and the old plan was clearly not sufficient.

Where I part company with the union/trust is on the implied hierarchy of claims.

### On labour and the retiree trust
I understand — and largely share — the view that the retiree obligation is not a speculative financial asset. It reflects deferred compensation and long-standing commitments. But we cannot pretend it is insulated from restructuring if the alternative is insolvency.

If the trust remains in its current form and size, and if labour costs are not adjusted materially, then the company’s cash burden will still be too heavy. That would simply postpone collapse. So yes:  
- **labour concessions are necessary**,  
- **the retiree claim must be restructured in a durable way**, and  
- **some conversion into equity or another contingent instrument may be appropriate** if that is what preserves the enterprise.

What I cannot accept is a structure that preserves the trust intact while shifting the required pain almost entirely onto unsecured creditors or the public balance sheet. That would not be viable, and it would not be fair either.

### On unsecured bondholders
I agree with the task force that **bondholders must take a meaningful haircut**. The company cannot carry its current debt load and survive. We need a genuine reduction in unsecured claims, not a cosmetic maturity extension.

At the same time, I want to be clear: if the restructuring is pushed too far toward punitive treatment of creditors, we risk destabilizing the process and prolonging it. We need enough recovery terms to be credible and executable, but not so generous that viability is sacrificed.

### On plants, brands, and dealers
I strongly support **deep but selective cuts**. Not every plant, brand, or dealer network element can be preserved. But we should protect:
- the strongest consumer-facing brands,
- the core manufacturing footprint,
- and the dealer network needed for continued sales and service.

I oppose indiscriminate cuts that would trigger consumer flight or destroy the franchise we are trying to save. The cuts must be tied to actual demand and profitability, not to symbolic “toughness.”

### On process
I am prepared to accept a **court-supervised process if that is the only way to secure the needed concessions quickly and credibly**. I have no ideological attachment to avoiding that route if consensual negotiations fail. What matters is that the process produces:
- financing certainty,
- a viable capital structure,
- and protection for the consumer franchise during the transition.

### My bottom line
I support:
- meaningful labour concessions,
- a real restructuring of the retiree health obligation,
- substantial unsecured creditor haircuts,
- and necessary plant/brand rationalization.

But I will not support:
- an outcome that destroys the going concern,
- a liquidation dressed up as restructuring,
- or cuts that undermine consumer confidence and wipe out the company’s core franchise.

If the task force’s financing is contingent on deeper sacrifice, then the sacrifices must be real. But they must also be calibrated to preserve the company that remains.
