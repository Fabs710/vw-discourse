We are past the stage of generalities. The company needs a capital structure and an operating footprint that can survive without recurring public support. That means each stakeholder class has to take real pain. If the numbers do not do that, we will use the court-supervised route.

Here is where we stand.

## 1) What we agree with
- The company needs a **true balance-sheet reset**.
- **Time is critical**; delay is value destruction.
- **All stakeholder groups must contribute**.
- A **court-supervised process** is acceptable if it is the cleanest way to impose finality.

## 2) What we reject
### To the union / retiree trust
We do **not** accept the idea that the retiree trust is insulated while unsecured creditors absorb the bulk of the losses. It is an unsecured enterprise obligation, and if it remains too large, the company is not viable. We are prepared to recognize its importance, but not to preserve it in full at taxpayer expense.

We also do **not** accept a footprint where labor costs remain structurally too high. There will be concessions on wages, work rules, and staffing.

### To bondholders
We do **not** accept the notion that a “meaningful haircut” is somehow unfair if it is large. It will be large. The current debt load is not supportable. A voluntary deal must reflect that reality. If bondholders reject it and force the court route, they should not expect a better economic outcome.

### To neighbouring governments
We do **not** accept a rescue conditioned on preserving every domestic plant regardless of viability. Domestic production matters, but plants exist to produce profitable vehicles, not to be preserved as monuments. Any public co-funding must buy a viable footprint, not a frozen one.

### To management
We already removed the prior CEO because the prior plan was inadequate. The new leadership must present a plan that reflects the reality on the ground, not defend legacy structures. If it does not, we will impose the restructuring through the court process.

## 3) The structure we are prepared to support
This is the framework we want to see built out in numbers immediately:

### Capital structure
- **Unsecured bondholders:** deep haircut, with the bulk of remaining value converted into **new equity and/or warrants**. No expectation of face-value recovery.
- **Retiree health trust:** converted into a **reduced but durable instrument** tied to the viability of the reorganized company, not left as an untouched cash burden. If some portion is preserved, it must be in a form that does not derail the business.
- **Public financing:** bridge support only, not permanent capital. Public participation should receive **controlling or near-controlling equity protection** in the restructured entity until exit conditions are met.
- **Management:** no residual claim to defend legacy economics. New leadership must be aligned with the restructured capital structure.

### Labour / retiree concessions
We expect:
- material wage and work-rule concessions,
- staffing reductions aligned with plant consolidation,
- reform of legacy cost structures,
- and a restructuring of retiree obligations so they are financeable.

If those concessions are not sufficient to make the company cash-generative at the new demand level, they are not sufficient.

### Plant / brand footprint
We want:
- substantial plant consolidation,
- elimination of weak or redundant brands,
- dealer network rationalization,
- and preservation only of the core manufacturing and sales footprint that supports profitability.

We are not going to support a plan that keeps too many plants open and then asks the taxpayer to fill the gap.

### Public financing / governance
Any additional public money will be conditioned on:
- verified restructuring milestones,
- strong oversight rights,
- and equity or equivalent governance rights that protect the public interest.

If neighbouring governments want to co-fund, they may do so, but not to veto necessary restructuring. Their contribution can be recognized through equity, governance, and binding production commitments that are consistent with viability.

## 4) Non-negotiables
These are not bargaining chips:
- No open-ended subsidy.
- No capital structure that leaves the company unviable.
- No preservation of all unsecured claims at the expense of the business.
- No plan that protects labor or the retiree trust in a way that makes the enterprise collapse.
- No delay beyond the 60-day window without a court-supervised fallback.

## 5) What we need next
Bring us:
1. a concrete exchange ratio for bondholders,
2. the proposed treatment of the retiree trust,
3. the specific labor cost reductions,
4. the plant/brand closures,
5. and the equity split among public funders, labor-related claims, and creditors.

If the parties cannot produce a credible proposal quickly, we will proceed through the expedited court process and impose the terms necessary to preserve a viable company and protect taxpayers.
