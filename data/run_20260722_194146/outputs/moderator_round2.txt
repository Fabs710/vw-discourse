## 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **real balance-sheet reset** to remain a going concern.
- **Speed matters**; delay is widely seen as value-destructive.
- **All stakeholder groups must share some sacrifice**.
- A **court-supervised process** is an acceptable fallback if a voluntary deal cannot be reached quickly.
- **Unsecured bondholders will take a material haircut**.
- The current **retiree health obligation cannot remain unchanged** and likely must be restructured into a more durable form.
- **Labor concessions** will be part of any deal, including wage/work-rule/staffing changes.
- **Plant and brand consolidation** is broadly accepted as necessary, though not on the same scale or scope by all parties.
- **Public financing must be bridge financing**, with oversight and governance protections.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment:**  
  The union wants a durable, meaningful instrument with equity-like value; the task force and bondholders reject any treatment that makes the trust materially better off than other unsecured claims. Management wants it reduced enough to be financeable.
- **Relative priority among unsecured claims:**  
  Bondholders insist on parity or at least non-inferior treatment versus the retiree trust. The union insists the trust is deferred compensation and should not be treated as ordinary unsecured debt.
- **Size and form of bondholder recovery:**  
  Bondholders reject a near-wipeout or token equity/warrants. The task force wants a deep haircut and substantial conversion.
- **Extent of labor concessions:**  
  All accept concessions, but labor disputes any plan that shifts too much of the burden onto workers and retirees.
- **Plant footprint and domestic production:**  
  The neighboring governments require binding domestic production and employment commitments; the task force and management want broader closure authority based on viability.
- **Public funding and governance:**  
  Governments want meaningful equity/governance protection and domestic safeguards; others are more focused on restructuring flexibility and taxpayer protection.

## 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to split the pain among unsecured bondholders, the retiree trust, and labor** without triggering legal or political rejection.
- **How much domestic capacity can be preserved** while still achieving a viable cost structure.
- **Whether public money buys control/protection** for governments, and if so, how much.
- **How to convert the retiree obligation** into something durable without making the company unfinanceable.
- **What bondholder exchange ratio is acceptable** in exchange for the equity/warrant package.
- **Which plants/brands are clearly core versus redundant**, and how closures will be justified and sequenced.
- **How to define “bounded” labor concessions** in measurable terms.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single integrated term sheet**.
- Put forward **quantified proposals** on:
  1. bondholder exchange ratio,
  2. retiree trust conversion,
  3. labor cost reductions,
  4. plant/brand closures,
  5. public equity/governance split.
- Explicitly test whether the package meets three conditions at once:
  - **viability** of the reorganized company,
  - **acceptable burden-sharing** across stakeholder classes,
  - **political and public-funding conditions** for the governments involved.
