We are prepared to engage on numbers and structure, but let me be equally clear: **we will not sign onto a deal that makes workers and retirees the residual shock absorber while unsecured creditors and public funders dictate the outcome.** That is not burden-sharing; that is forced transfer.

## Where we agree with the task force
- The company needs a **real balance-sheet reset**.
- **Delay is destructive**.
- **A court-supervised route is acceptable** if it is the only way to secure finality and impose a fair result.
- Unsecured bondholders must take **real losses**.

## Where we disagree
### 1) The retiree trust is not just another unsecured claim
You keep saying the trust is “an unsecured enterprise obligation” as if that ends the discussion. It does not.

This obligation is **deferred compensation** earned by workers over a lifetime of service. We accept that it may need to be restructured into a durable form, but we **do not accept** a solution that simply wipes it down to whatever is most convenient for bondholders or the public balance sheet.

What we can consider:
- a **reduced but durable instrument**,
- preferably with **equity participation** or a secured/priority-like claim on the restructured entity,
- and a structure that gives retirees a real, enforceable recovery rather than a political promise.

What we will not accept:
- an outcome where the trust is hollowed out to protect unsecured creditor recoveries,
- or where the trust is forced to absorb disproportionate loss simply because it is politically easier to do so.

### 2) Labour costs can move, but not into the ditch
We know the company cannot survive with legacy costs frozen in place. We are not pretending otherwise.

We are prepared to discuss:
- wage moderation,
- work-rule changes,
- staffing rationalization tied to plant consolidation,
- and productivity improvements.

But the concessions must be **bounded** and linked to actual survival and job preservation. We will not accept demands that:
- destroy the wage framework,
- force the workforce to fund the entire rescue through concessions,
- or use “viability” as a blanket justification for stripping contractual protections.

### 3) Bondholders are not entitled to the cleanest exit
You are right that bondholders should take losses. But they are not entitled to a structure that leaves them better protected than workers and retirees.

If the package requires deep creditor haircuts, then that is what it requires. What you cannot have is:
- labour and retirees taking the deepest long-term hit,
- while bondholders receive a comparatively generous equity/warrant package,
- and the public side gets to claim discipline.

That is not a fair hierarchy of sacrifice.

## The structure we can support

### Capital structure
We are prepared to discuss a package along these lines:

- **Unsecured bondholders:** substantial haircut, with part of the remainder converted into **new equity and warrants**.
- **Retiree health trust:** converted into a **durable instrument** with real value, not an empty promise; this should include **equity or equity-linked securities** in the restructured company.
- **Public financing:** bridge financing only, with a limited and transparent role. If public money is being used, then the public should not be demanding that workers and retirees absorb the whole burden.
- **Equity allocation:** the trust must receive a meaningful stake reflecting the value of the obligation being restructured; labour-related claims should not be treated as junior scrap.

We are not putting a fixed percentage on the table yet because that depends on the plant footprint and total debt reduction. But the principle is non-negotiable: **the trust must receive something durable and valuable, not just a write-down.**

### Labour / retiree concessions
We are prepared to accept:
- targeted wage and work-rule changes,
- benefit restructuring where necessary,
- and workforce reductions tied to genuinely redundant capacity.

We are **not** prepared to accept:
- wholesale wage destruction,
- a blanket reset of all labor protections,
- or retiree obligations being stripped down below a meaningful level of security.

### Plant / brand footprint
We accept that some consolidation is necessary, but we will not bless a plan that uses “efficiency” as a pretext for liquidation by attrition.

Our position is:
- preserve **core plants** with viable product lines,
- protect facilities that are essential to the company’s future and to industrial communities,
- and close only those operations that are genuinely redundant or structurally unsustainable.

We want a footprint that supports a viable company **and** preserves as many jobs and plants as possible. That is not a sentimental ask; it is how you preserve productive capacity and labor peace.

### On public financing and governance
If public funds are being used, then yes, there should be governance protections. But public capital does **not** give the task force a blank check to dictate the allocation of pain exclusively onto labour and retirees.

If the public side wants:
- oversight rights,
- milestone-based funding,
- and governance protections,

then it also has to accept that:
- workers and retirees are not the only ones who must sacrifice,
- and the trust’s claim cannot be casually subordinated.

## Non-negotiables
Let me state these plainly:

- **No destruction of the retiree health trust.**
- **No settlement that sacrifices the workforce wholesale to creditor recoveries.**
- **No plan that treats labour as junior to unsecured financial claims as a matter of principle.**
- **No deal that preserves too many protections for creditors while hollowing out jobs, wages, and retiree security.**

## What would move us
If you want our signature and labour peace, we need to see:
1. **Meaningful unsecured creditor haircuts**;
2. **A durable, enforceable instrument for the retiree trust**, preferably with equity or equity-like participation;
3. **Bounded labour concessions** tied to the actual viability case;
4. **A plant footprint that preserves core domestic capacity and viable jobs**;
5. **A public financing structure that does not force the workforce and retirees to subsidize creditor recoveries.**

If those elements are in the package, we can be constructive. If the direction remains “workers and retirees carry the heaviest load because they are easiest to pressure,” then we will oppose it — including through the court-supervised route if necessary.

If you want us to get specific on exchange ratios and claim conversions, put a concrete proposal on the table that respects those boundaries.
