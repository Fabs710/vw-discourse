We appreciate that the discussion has finally moved from slogans to structure. That said, our position remains hard: **bondholders will not accept being the principal loss reservoir while other unsecured claims, especially the retiree trust, are treated more favorably without compensation.** That is not a restructuring; it is a redistribution.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **true balance-sheet reset**.
- **Speed matters**; delay destroys value.
- A **court-supervised process** is a real fallback and we understand that.
- The current debt load is unsustainable and **bondholders will take a haircut**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot be prioritized over other unsecured creditors without offset
The union and management keep saying the trust is “deferred compensation,” and we do not dispute the moral sympathy behind that. But legally and economically, it is still an **unsecured enterprise obligation**.

So no, we do **not** accept:
- the trust being preserved more fully than bondholders,
- the trust receiving a “durable instrument” with better economics than the unsecured bonds,
- or the trust being made whole while bondholders are pushed into the ditch.

If the trust is granted enhanced treatment, then **bondholders must receive offsetting value**. Otherwise you are choosing winners and losers among unsecured creditors based on politics, not claim priority.

### 2) “Meaningful haircut” cannot mean a near wipeout
The task force says the haircut will be large and bondholders should not expect a better result by resisting. We hear that. But there is a difference between a deep restructuring and confiscation.

We will not accept:
- a token equity stub,
- nominal warrants with no realistic upside,
- or an exchange ratio that leaves unsecured bondholders materially worse off than comparable unsecured claimants.

If the court route is used to impose a wipeout, then we will contest that outcome. The threat of process is real, but so is the risk that a bad voluntary deal is simply dressed up as inevitability.

### 3) Labour and domestic production concerns cannot be financed mainly by bondholder sacrifice
We recognize the need for plant rationalization and labour concessions. We are not asking to preserve everything.

But we reject the structure implied by the other parties:
- labour takes some cuts,
- the retiree trust is protected in a “durable” way,
- the public gets governance and maybe equity,
- neighbouring governments get production commitments,
- and bondholders absorb the bulk of the dilution.

That is not balance. That is overloading unsecured financial creditors to subsidize a political settlement.

## What would be acceptable
We are prepared to consider a deal only if it includes:

1. **Real parity among unsecured claims**
   - If the retiree trust is restructured into an instrument with meaningful value, bondholders must receive **economically comparable consideration**.
   - No materially better treatment for the trust without compensation.

2. **A genuine recovery for unsecured bondholders**
   - Not a cosmetic exchange.
   - We need **new equity and/or warrants** with actual upside, not just paper.

3. **Transparent capital structure**
   - We need to see the full allocation among:
     - bondholders,
     - retiree trust,
     - public funding,
     - labour-related claims,
     - and any residual equity.
   - If the numbers do not show a credible recovery for unsecureds, we will not consent.

4. **No hidden transfer to the public side at our expense**
   - If public money is coming in, fine — but that should not be used as an excuse to strip unsecured creditors further while preserving other constituencies.

## On the specific proposals

### To the task force
We reject your framing that bondholders should simply accept a large haircut because the company is distressed. Distress does not erase equal treatment principles. If you want consent, bring a deal that leaves unsecured creditors with real value.

### To the union/trust
We respect the workforce’s contribution, but the trust is not sacred relative to other unsecured claims. If you insist on a durable instrument and equity participation for the trust, then bondholders must be treated at least as well on an equivalent basis. We will not be the class paying for a politically preferred unsecured claim.

### To management
We agree the company needs consolidation. But your language about “preserving the consumer franchise” should not become a code for protecting operating stakeholders while financial creditors are flattened. If the company survives, unsecured creditors must recover something meaningful from that survival.

### To neighbouring governments
If you are co-funding, that is your choice. But your production covenants cannot be financed by pushing the loss onto bondholders. If your public money buys a stake, fine — but don’t use it to justify superior treatment for domestic footprint politics at our expense.

## Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- We are prepared to take a **real haircut**.
- We are prepared to accept **equity and/or warrants**.
- We are prepared to move quickly.
- We are **not** prepared to accept **inferior treatment relative to the retiree trust** or a structure that effectively wipes out the unsecured bondholder class.

If the current direction remains “everyone sacrifices, but bondholders sacrifice most,” then we will oppose it and force the issue through the court-supervised route. We do not welcome that path, but we are not going to sign away contractual recovery just because the deadline is short.
