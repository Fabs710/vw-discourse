We agree with the task force and management on the basic diagnosis: **the company needs a deep restructuring, and the 60-day window is real**. We also agree that the old structure cannot be preserved by wishful thinking.

But I need to be equally direct: **we will not co-fund a deal that protects foreign capacity while sacrificing our domestic plants, or one that asks us to pay without a commensurate ownership position and binding footprint commitments.**

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **credible, not cosmetic, restructuring**.
- There must be **shared sacrifice** from labour, creditors, and public funds.
- A **consensual deal is preferable** if it can be done quickly and fairly.
- The retiree obligation and unsecured debt both need **material reworking**.
- A fallback to the court-supervised route may be necessary if no one is serious.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants cannot be treated as optional
Task force, your “no veto over rationalization” framing is too broad. We are not asking for a veto over every efficiency move. We are saying something narrower and firmer:

- **No co-funding if our domestic production base is disproportionately cut**
- **No co-funding if our plants are closed while foreign plants are insulated**
- **No co-funding without binding minimum production and employment commitments**

That is not jurisdictional free-riding. That is a legitimate condition for public money from elected governments accountable to workers and regions.

### 2) Proportionate equity is not negotiable
If we are putting in real funding to protect domestic industry, then a **meaningful equity stake proportional to our contribution** is the minimum acceptable term. Not a token warrant package, not a symbolic advisory seat, but real ownership that reflects the risk and public value we are taking on.

If the post-restructuring equity is being divided among the lead funder, labour trust instruments, and creditors, then our contribution must be recognized on the same basis. We are not junior helpers.

### 3) Plant cuts must be even-handed, not politically convenient
Management, we accept that not every plant can be saved. But we will oppose any footprint plan that loads the burden onto our jurisdictions while preserving capacity elsewhere for convenience. If closures are required, then the burden must be shared according to industrial logic, not political distance from the lead funder.

If the company wants our money, it needs to show:
- which plants are staying,
- which are closing,
- why,
- and how domestic production share is being preserved over the long term.

## On the retiree trust and bondholders
We understand the need for a deeper haircut and a durable new structure. But we are not taking a side in a class war between the union and bondholders. What matters to us is that the restructuring be **viable and fair**, and that it **does not hollow out domestic production** to make another claim class or another geography comfortable.

That means:
- the retiree claim can be reworked if necessary,
- bondholders can take a haircut,
- management can accept smaller footprint and tighter terms,

but **our domestic plants cannot be the quiet sacrificial class**.

## What would move us
To support co-funding, we need a package with all of the following:
1. **Binding domestic footprint commitments** for minimum production and employment.
2. **A proportionate equity stake** for the co-funding governments.
3. **A footprint plan that shares cuts fairly** across regions and facilities.
4. **A viable, funded restructuring** that is not simply deferred pain.

If those conditions are not met, then we should be honest: **we will not support the rescue**, even if that means the court-supervised route is used without our co-funding.

## Bottom line
We are constructive, but our support is conditional and substantive:
- **Protect domestic production**
- **Protect domestic employment**
- **Give co-funders real ownership**
- **Do not ask us to subsidize plant closures in our jurisdictions**

If the deal cannot do that, then it is not a partnership.
