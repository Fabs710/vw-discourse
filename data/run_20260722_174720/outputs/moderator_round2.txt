### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company requires a **deep, rapid restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that the **initial management plan was insufficient**.
- All stakeholders accept that:
  - some **plant, brand, and capacity reductions** are unavoidable,
  - the **retiree health obligation must be reworked**,
  - **unsecured creditors must take a meaningful haircut**,
  - **public funding cannot simply preserve the old structure**,
  - a **consensual deal is preferable only if it is real and executable**.
- There is also shared recognition that **court-supervised restructuring remains a fallback** if no agreement is reached quickly.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health claim:**  
  - Government task force and management want a **material reduction** and conversion into a sustainable funded/equity-linked form.  
  - The union insists the trust must retain **durable, enforceable value** and cannot be reduced to a hollow or nominal recovery.  
  - Bondholders object if the retiree trust is treated **more favorably than unsecured debt**.

- **Bondholder recovery:**  
  - Bondholders accept a haircut in principle but want **meaningful recovery and upside**, not a token exchange.  
  - They oppose being the class that absorbs the largest loss while other stakeholders preserve value.

- **Plant/footprint rationalization:**  
  - Management and the task force want closures based on commercial viability.  
  - The union wants cuts to be **shared and not used to gut the industrial base**.  
  - Co-funding governments want **domestic plants protected** and reject a deal that shifts cuts away from their jurisdictions.

- **Co-funding terms:**  
  - Governments want **binding domestic production/employment commitments** and a **proportionate equity stake**.  
  - The task force says public co-funding does **not buy a veto** over rationalization.  
  - Management supports footprint commitments only if they remain commercially defensible.

- **Process leverage:**  
  - The task force is signaling a willingness to move to **expedited court-supervised restructuring** if consensus delays finalization.  
  - Labour and bondholders both indicate they will contest any process they view as unfair.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much retiree protection can be funded without making the company unfinanceable**
- **How to split value between retiree claims, unsecured creditor recovery, and new equity**
- **Which plants/brands are closed, and whether closures are distributed by business logic or political compromise**
- **What level of domestic footprint commitment is commercially workable**
- **How much equity each new money provider receives, including governments and any trust-linked instruments**
- **Final ownership structure** post-restructuring and how sacrifice is allocated across stakeholders

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please return with a **numbers-based package** that shows, in one place:
- proposed **plant/brand closures and capacity reductions**,
- the **cash vs. non-cash treatment** of the retiree claim,
- the **size of unsecured creditor haircut** and any equity/warrant participation,
- the **co-funders’ equity stake** and any binding domestic footprint commitments,
- the **final post-restructuring ownership split**.

The next round should focus on **quantified trade-offs and final allocation**, not general principles.
