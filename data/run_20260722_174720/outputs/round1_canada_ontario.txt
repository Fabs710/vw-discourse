We are prepared to co-fund, but I want to be clear that our support is **conditional**, not automatic.

### Where we agree
- **The restructuring must be deep and credible.** We agree with the task force and management that cosmetic cuts will not work.
- **Speed matters.** We do not have the luxury of a prolonged process.
- **Shared sacrifice is necessary.** Labour, creditors, management, and public funds all have to contribute.

### What we cannot accept
Our core concern is straightforward: **we will not fund a restructuring that sacrifices domestic production and employment while foreign plants are protected or while we are asked to pay without a commensurate say.**

That means:

1. **No public co-funding without binding domestic footprint commitments**  
   If the restructuring results in closures or major reductions in our domestic plants while other plants elsewhere are insulated, we cannot support it. The domestic production base and the jobs attached to it must be protected in a measurable way.

2. **No contribution without a proportionate ownership position**  
   If we are asked to put real money into the rescue, we expect an equity stake that reflects that contribution. We are not interested in being a junior payer with no meaningful voice in the restructured company.

3. **No plan that treats our plants as expendable collateral**  
   We understand that capacity must be rationalized, but that cannot mean our jurisdiction bears the burden while others are sheltered for political convenience. If plants are to close, the burden must be shared in a way that is defensible and even-handed.

### On labour, creditors, and the trust
We are not trying to pick winners between the union and bondholders. But we do insist that any settlement preserve a viable domestic industrial base. That is the long-term condition for jobs, tax revenues, and supplier stability.

- We support **real concessions from labour and creditors**.
- We support **restructuring the retiree obligation** if it is necessary for viability.
- We do **not** support a solution where those sacrifices are made to preserve a company footprint that leaves our domestic plants hollowed out.

### On process
We prefer a consensual solution, but only if the terms include:
- binding commitments on domestic production and employment,
- a fair equity allocation for co-funding governments,
- and a restructuring that does not outsource the pain to our plants.

If those conditions are not met, then we would rather see the court-supervised route used than endorse a deal that locks in a permanent loss of domestic industrial capacity.

### Bottom line
We are constructive co-funders, but our position is firm:

- **No funding without domestic production guarantees**
- **No funding without proportional equity**
- **No restructuring that sacrifices our plants to save others**

If the task force wants our support, it needs to build these commitments into the deal from the start.
