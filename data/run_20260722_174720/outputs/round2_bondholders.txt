We agree on the core premise: **this company needs a deep restructuring, quickly, or value keeps evaporating**. Nobody here should pretend the old capital structure or cost base can survive intact.

But I need to be equally clear about where the unsecured bondholders stand.

## Where we agree
- The company needs **major capacity, brand, and cost cuts**.
- The company needs a **real balance-sheet reset**, not a cosmetic extension.
- The retiree health obligation **cannot remain untouched**.
- Speed matters, and a **consensual solution is preferable only if it is actually fair**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) We are not the residual political loss bucket
The task force keeps speaking as though unsecured creditors should absorb a “real haircut” by default because we are unsecured. We understand the legal position. We do **not** accept the political framing that bondholders are the convenient class to be crushed so everyone else can preserve what they want.

We are legitimate contractual creditors. A very large share of these bonds are held by ordinary savers, not hedge funds. We will not consent to a structure that treats us as the easy source of pain while labor, the retiree trust, and public funders secure better relative treatment.

### 2) The retiree trust cannot be favored over us
This is the central issue. If the retiree health claim is converted into some durable instrument, fine — but it must be **comparable in economic reality** to what unsecured creditors receive. What we will not accept is:
- the trust receiving materially better treatment than unsecured bonds,
- the trust being given priority-like value while we are written down harder,
- or the trust being protected through our dilution without compensation.

That would be unequal treatment dressed up as labor peace, and we reject it.

### 3) “Meaningful recovery” cannot mean symbolic equity crumbs
We are open to equity, warrants, or a debt-for-equity exchange. We are **not** open to a token allocation in a heavily diluted shell company that is then described as fair because it has upside in theory.

If we are taking a major haircut, then the recovery must be real:
- a meaningful equity stake,
- warrants with genuine upside,
- and a capital structure that does not immediately relegate unsecured creditors to irrelevance.

### 4) The court-supervised route is not a threat only to us
The task force keeps implying that court is the fallback if creditors hold out. That cuts both ways. If the consensual proposal is unfair, we are prepared to test it in the expedited process rather than volunteer for a wipe-out.

We are not bluffing. We have enough claim size to matter, and we will not accept a confiscatory exchange just because the deadline is short.

## On the retiree trust and labour
We recognize the trust was negotiated in a labor context. That does **not** make it sacrosanct, and it does **not** entitle it to better economic treatment than other unsecured claims. If the trust is to receive equity-linked value or a funded instrument, then bondholders must receive **parity in principle** and a commensurate share of the restructured upside.

If the union’s position is that the trust deserves special protection while unsecured creditors bear the heavier loss, then there is no deal from our side.

## On public co-funding and domestic plants
We are not in a position to decide domestic plant policy for the neighboring governments, but we do object to a structure where co-funders demand protected local plants while unsecured creditors are asked to subsidize the broader political bargain.

If public money is coming in, it should **not** be used to subordinate legitimate creditor recoveries to geographically favored industrial policy.

## What would move us
We are not rigid on form. We can accept equity, warrants, or a mix. But to have any chance of consent, the package must include:
- a **real unsecured recovery**, not a token one;
- **no worse treatment than the retiree trust without compensation**;
- a capital structure that gives creditors genuine upside participation;
- and enough certainty that the company survives, so the recovery is not purely theoretical.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to support a deep restructuring. We are **not** prepared to endorse one in which:
- unsecured bondholders are the primary sacrificial class,
- the retiree trust is treated more favorably,
- and the resulting consideration is little more than diluted paper.

If the task force wants our support, it needs to bring forward a package that shows **credible parity, meaningful recovery, and real upside**. Otherwise, the expedited court process remains an option we are fully prepared to consider.
