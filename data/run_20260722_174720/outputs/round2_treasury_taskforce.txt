We appreciate that every stakeholder now accepts the basic reality: **the company must be restructured deeply, quickly, and with shared sacrifice**. That is the correct starting point. But we are not here to validate competing claims; we are here to get to a **viable capital structure and cost base** within 60 days, or to move to a court-supervised process that can impose one.

Let me be direct on the unresolved issues.

## 1) Retiree health obligation: durable, yes; untouchable, no
We agree the retiree obligation cannot simply be erased. But we **do not** accept the position that it must be preserved in a form that leaves the company burdened beyond recovery.

What we need is:
- a **material reduction** in the cash and balance-sheet burden,
- conversion of at least part of the claim into a **real, funded instrument** tied to the restructured company,
- and a structure that is **sustainable under stress**, not just acceptable in good years.

The union’s point that retirees deserve protection is understood. But protection has to be compatible with survival. A promise that makes the business unfinanceable is not protection; it is deferred failure.

## 2) Bondholder recovery: meaningful, but not at taxpayer expense
We reject the idea that bondholders are being singled out unfairly. Unsecured creditors are unsecured creditors. They will take a haircut because the company is insolvent and because public funds are not there to make them whole.

That said, we also reject a “dump everything on the bondholders” approach if it leaves the restructuring politically convenient but financially unstable. We need:
- a **real reduction** in principal claims,
- some **upside participation** through equity or warrants,
- and a recovery that is consistent with a company that can actually survive.

What we will not do is engineer a deal where bondholders are crushed only so that another claim class can be preserved at an economically irrational level.

## 3) Domestic footprint: no blank cheque, no veto over rationalization
To the neighbouring governments: we hear your concern about domestic plants. But let’s be clear — **public co-funding does not buy a veto over restructuring**. If a plant is uneconomic, it cannot be kept open simply because a government wants the jobs preserved locally.

What we can consider is:
- **binding commitments on minimum domestic production or investment levels** where they are commercially defensible,
- a **proportionate equity stake** for any co-funder,
- and a footprint plan that shares burden across the network rather than dumping it on one jurisdiction.

What we cannot accept is a deal where public co-funders insist on insulation for their own facilities while demanding that others absorb the cuts. That is not a restructuring; it is jurisdictional cost-shifting.

## 4) Management and capacity: cuts must be deeper than the first plan
Management knows this already, but let me restate it plainly: the initial plan was insufficient. We need:
- deeper capacity reduction,
- further brand rationalization,
- tighter dealer/network economics,
- and a balance sheet reset that matches the post-restructuring earnings power.

If the parties are still bargaining over preserving the old structure, we are wasting the 60-day window.

## 5) The process: consent is preferred only if it is real
We prefer a negotiated outcome **only if** it delivers finality quickly and credibly. If parties use “consensus” as a way to delay loss allocation, we will not extend bridge financing indefinitely.

The fallback remains the same:
- expedited court-supervised restructuring,
- imposed terms if necessary,
- and financing only to carry the company through that process.

That is not a threat; it is the credible path available if the deal is not sufficiently aggressive.

## What we need in the next round
Bring us a package with actual numbers on:
1. **plant/brand closures and capacity reductions,**
2. **the split between cash and non-cash treatment of the retiree claim,**
3. **the size of the unsecured creditor haircut and their equity/warrant upside,**
4. **any co-funder equity and domestic footprint commitment,**
5. **the final post-restructuring ownership structure.**

## Our bottom line
We will support:
- a **deep operational reset**,
- a **material rewrite of the retiree obligation**,
- a **substantial unsecured creditor haircut**,
- and a **clean equity structure** that reflects sacrifice.

We will not support:
- an unrestructured company,
- an open-ended public subsidy,
- or a deal that preserves legacy claims at the expense of viability.

If the parties cannot bring a credible, quantitatively defensible package quickly, then the court-supervised route becomes the only responsible option.
