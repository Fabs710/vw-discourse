We will support a restructuring only if it is **deep, fast, and credible enough to leave the company viable without ongoing taxpayer dependence**.

Our priorities are clear:

1. **Viability over symbolism**
   - The company must emerge with a cost structure, product footprint, and balance sheet that can actually survive.
   - We will not finance an unrestructured or only cosmetically reworked business.

2. **Shared sacrifice**
   - Management has already changed, and more discipline is expected.
   - Labour, including the retiree health obligation, must accept material concessions.
   - Unsecured creditors must take a real haircut. They will not be paid in full at public expense.

3. **Taxpayer protection**
   - Public funds are bridge financing, not an open-ended subsidy.
   - Any solution must minimize the total public cost and accelerate an eventual exit by the government.

4. **Speed and finality**
   - The 60-day window is real.
   - We prefer a negotiated agreement, but if stakeholders hold out, we are prepared to use the court-supervised route to impose a restructuring.

What we will argue for:
- A **much deeper reduction** in costs, plants, brands, and excess capacity than the company’s initial plan proposed.
- A **fundamental restructuring of unsecured debt**, not merely an extension.
- A **materially revised treatment of the retiree health claim** so it is sustainable.
- A **clean, viable equity structure** for the restructured company that reflects the sacrifices made.

What we will argue against:
- Any proposal that preserves the old cost base and asks taxpayers to absorb the loss.
- Any plan that protects one stakeholder class while forcing the public to underwrite everyone else.
- Delay tactics or attempts to relitigate the need for major restructuring.

If stakeholders want support, they need to bring us a plan that is aggressive, verifiable, and final. If not, we will proceed on the assumption that a court-supervised restructuring is the only credible path left.
