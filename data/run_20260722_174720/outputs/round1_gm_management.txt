We agree with the task force on the central point: **the company must be restructured deeply and quickly enough to survive without ongoing taxpayer dependence**. We also agree with the union that the old structure cannot simply be preserved and that a workable settlement has to preserve a real future for the company, not just shift losses around.

But I need to be equally direct about what the company can and cannot support.

### Where management agrees
- **Deep restructuring is necessary.** The initial plan was not enough. We accept that.
- **Shared sacrifice is unavoidable.** Management, labour, retirees, creditors, and the public all have to contribute.
- **Speed matters.** We do not have time for extended brinkmanship. The company is running out of cash.

### Where we disagree with the union
1. **The company cannot promise preservation of every job, plant, or brand.**  
   That is not a viable starting point. We have too much fixed cost and too much excess capacity. Some closures and eliminations are unavoidable if we want a going concern rather than an eventual liquidation.

2. **The retiree health obligation cannot remain in its current form.**  
   We respect the history of that commitment, but it is simply too large relative to the company’s reduced earnings power. If it is not materially restructured, it will continue to threaten survival.  
   A conversion into some form of durable claim may be part of the answer, but only if it reduces near-term cash drain and balance-sheet burden enough to make the company viable.

3. **Bondholders cannot be asked to carry the burden alone, but they must take a real haircut.**  
   There is no path where unsecured creditors are largely protected while labour and the public absorb the deepest pain. That would not be credible, and the task force will not fund it.

### Where we disagree with the idea of an “equitable” preservation of the past
The company’s priority is not to preserve every legacy promise in its original form. It is to preserve:
- the strongest brands,
- the core manufacturing footprint,
- the dealer network,
- warranties and consumer confidence,
- and enough capacity to compete.

If we fail to make the cost base fit the business reality, none of those things are protected.

### On the process
We would prefer a consensual settlement if it gives us:
- financing certainty,
- a credible balance-sheet reset,
- sufficient plant/brand reductions,
- and a transition that protects customers and suppliers.

But if consensus means delaying hard choices or preserving claims the company cannot honor, then we should not pretend it is a solution. In that case, a court-supervised process may be necessary to get the necessary finality.

### Bottom line
Management is prepared to support a **deeper restructuring than originally proposed**, including:
- meaningful capacity cuts,
- brand rationalization,
- a materially reworked retiree health obligation,
- and a substantial creditor haircut.

What we will not support is any outcome that:
- leaves the company too expensive to survive,
- protects one constituency at the expense of the enterprise,
- or undermines consumer and dealer confidence through uncertainty.

If the task force is prepared to finance a real restructuring, and if the union is prepared to accept that survival requires hard cuts, then we can move quickly toward a viable deal.
