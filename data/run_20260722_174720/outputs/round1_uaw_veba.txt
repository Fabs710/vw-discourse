We agree on one central point: **the company needs a deep restructuring, and delay is not an option**. The union is not here to defend the status quo or pretend the old structure can be preserved intact. We understand the severity of the situation and the need for shared sacrifice.

But let me be equally clear about where we **do not** agree.

### What we can support
We are prepared to discuss:
- **Meaningful concessions on wages and work rules** where they are truly tied to survival.
- **Plant and brand rationalization** where the business case is real and the alternatives are worse.
- A **restructured retiree health obligation** that is durable and fundable.
- A settlement that gives the company a viable path forward without endless public support.

### What we cannot accept
1. **The retiree health trust cannot be destroyed.**  
   That obligation was negotiated in return for years of labour peace and sacrifice. It is not a discretionary claim to be wiped out because creditors want to maximize recoveries. We will not accept a solution that strips retirees of protection and leaves them exposed to future insolvency risk.

2. **Workers cannot be treated as junior to unsecured creditors.**  
   The burden must be shared. If bondholders are taking haircuts, they do not get to insist that labour and retirees absorb the deepest cuts while they preserve disproportionate value for themselves.

3. **Jobs and industrial capacity matter.**  
   We will not sign onto a plan that sacrifices the workforce wholesale or hollows out the company beyond what is necessary for viability. The goal is a surviving manufacturer, not liquidation by another name.

### On the form of the retiree obligation
We are open to converting part of the retiree health claim into a **durable equity or security-based claim** on the restructured company, provided that:
- it is real value, not paper in a shell;
- it has enforceable standing;
- it protects the trust over the long term;
- and it is paired with company commitments that keep the business alive.

That is the kind of structure that can work. A mere promise, a vague future IOU, or a deep nominal write-down with no durable backing will not.

### On the process
We prefer a **consensual restructuring** if it can preserve jobs, plants, and the trust’s claim in a credible way. But let me be blunt: if the proposal amounts to workers and retirees bearing the brunt while creditors are sheltered, then we will not call that consensus. In that case, the court-supervised route may be the only way to ensure the sacrifice is actually shared.

### Bottom line
The union is willing to be constructive and to move significantly, but only if the outcome includes:
- a real future for the company,
- a meaningful number of preserved jobs and plants,
- a durable and enforceable settlement for retiree health,
- and fair sacrifice from unsecured creditors and management.

If the task force wants speed and finality, it should not confuse that with imposing an unfair deal on labour. We are ready to negotiate seriously, but not to sign away the workforce and retirees to make the capital stack easier.
