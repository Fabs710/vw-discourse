### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **deep, fast restructuring** to remain viable.
- There is broad agreement that the **current cost structure is unsustainable** and that **plant/brand/capacity reductions** are likely necessary.
- All parties accept that **shared sacrifice** is unavoidable.
- There is general agreement that the **retiree health obligation must be materially reworked** rather than left unchanged.
- All parties prefer a **consensual solution if possible**, but accept that a **court-supervised process** may be used if no deal is reached quickly.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **How deep the cuts should be**, especially regarding plants, brands, jobs, and excess capacity.
- **How the retiree health claim should be treated**:
  - union wants it preserved in a durable form,
  - bondholders want it reworked but not favored,
  - management and the task force want it materially reduced for viability.
- **How losses are allocated across stakeholders**:
  - task force wants major concessions from labour and unsecured creditors,
  - union objects to workers/retirees bearing the brunt,
  - bondholders object to being the main residual loss absorber,
  - neighbouring governments object to domestic plants being disproportionately sacrificed.
- **Whether unsecured creditors receive a meaningful recovery** or are overly diluted.
- **Whether public co-funding is conditional on domestic footprint protections and equity stakes** for co-funders.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The balance between **viability and preservation of jobs/industrial capacity**.
- The balance between **taxpayer protection and stakeholder recoveries**.
- The balance between **retiree security and creditor parity**.
- The balance between **domestic plant protection and overall rationalization of the footprint**.
- The balance between **speed/finality and negotiated fairness**.
- The exact **capital structure of the restructured company**, including who gets equity, warrants, or other upside.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific numbers and structures**: capacity reductions, cost savings, claim treatment, and equity allocation.
- Ask each party to specify its **minimum acceptable terms** and **largest concession it can make**.
- Focus on three unresolved design questions:
  1. **Retiree obligation:** what durable form can be funded?
  2. **Creditor recovery:** what level of haircut and upside participation is acceptable?
  3. **Domestic footprint:** what binding commitments, if any, can support co-funding?
- The next round should aim to identify a **single package of trade-offs** that is viable, fundable, and executable within the 60-day window.
