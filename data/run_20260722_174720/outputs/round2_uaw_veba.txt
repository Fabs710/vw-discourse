We agree with the task force on one thing: **this must be deep, fast, and real**. We also agree that the initial management plan was too weak, and that nobody gets to walk away whole. The company cannot be saved by pretending the old structure still works.

But I need to draw a hard line on the parts of the task force’s framing that would shove the full burden onto workers and retirees.

## Where we agree
- **A meaningful restructuring is necessary.**
- **Some plant, brand, and capacity reductions are unavoidable.**
- **The retiree health obligation must be reworked into a durable form.**
- **Unsecured creditors must take a real haircut and not be made whole by public money.**
- **A consensual deal is preferable if it is genuinely fair and executable.**

## Where we disagree
### 1) The retiree trust cannot be “materially reduced” into near-meaninglessness
You say protection must be compatible with survival. We agree. But compatibility is not the same as stripping the trust down to a hollow promise.

This obligation was earned through years of labour sacrifice. It is not a political convenience. Any solution that simply reduces it to a thin claim with no serious backing would be unacceptable.

What we can consider is:
- converting part of the obligation into **real equity or equity-linked securities** in the restructured company;
- structuring the rest as a **funded, enforceable claim** with actual value, not an unsecured IOU;
- and pairing that with a company that is lean enough to carry the obligation.

What we cannot accept is a “funded instrument” that is just paper in a shell while the trust bears the risk of the company failing again.

### 2) Workers cannot be treated as the residual shock absorber
The task force says bondholders are unsecured and should take a haircut. Fine. But that cannot become a formula where labour gives up wages, work rules, plants, and retiree security while creditors still preserve disproportionate upside.

If sacrifice is real, it must be shared:
- management already changed,
- creditors take a substantial haircut,
- labour makes concessions tied to survival,
- and the trust receives durable value, not symbolic treatment.

### 3) “No veto over rationalization” is not the same as “take our plants”
To the neighbouring governments: we understand your concern about domestic production. But your position cannot mean that other communities, workers, and suppliers are automatically sacrificed so your own plants are protected.

That said, we do support **shared and defensible footprint rationalization**, not one-sided destruction of industrial capacity. Any closure plan must be based on actual business need, not political convenience.

### 4) The expedited court process cannot be used as leverage to force a bad deal
We understand it exists. But if the court route is being used to pressure labour and retirees into accepting worse treatment than creditors or co-funders, then that is not neutral discipline; it is coercion.

If the plan ends up in court, we will defend the workforce and the trust there too. So if the task force wants speed, it should not mistake that for permission to impose an unfair allocation of pain.

## What we would need to see
Since the moderator asked for actual structures and numbers, here is our side plainly:

### Retiree trust
We could consider:
- a **substantial equity stake or equity-linked instrument** for the trust,
- plus a **secured or funded claim** for the remainder,
- provided the package has real enforceability and long-term value.

We cannot accept:
- elimination of the obligation,
- a purely nominal recovery,
- or a claim so diluted that retirees are left exposed to the next downturn.

### Jobs and plants
We can accept:
- targeted capacity cuts,
- elimination of clearly uneconomic brands or facilities,
- and changes in work rules and wage terms where necessary.

We cannot accept:
- wholesale plant destruction,
- or a restructuring that leaves a “viable” company by gutting the industrial base and shipping the pain to workers.

### Creditor treatment
Bondholders should take a meaningful haircut, yes. But the allocation must still leave room for:
- a surviving company,
- a trust with real value,
- and a workforce that can continue to build the product.

If bondholders get a large share of the post-reorg upside while labour takes the deepest permanent losses, we will oppose that.

## On process
We remain open to a consensual settlement **if** it includes:
- durable trust treatment,
- meaningful jobs and plant retention,
- real creditor sacrifice,
- and a viable operating plan.

If the next round brings a package that meets those conditions, we can move. If it brings a proposal that treats the trust and workers as the easiest sources of pain, then we will not bless it for the sake of speed.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to be constructive and to concede where survival requires it. But we will not agree to a restructuring that:
- destroys the retiree trust,
- sacrifices workers wholesale to creditor recoveries,
- or uses “viability” as a pretext to transfer the burden onto labour alone.

Bring back a real package: one that preserves as many jobs and plants as possible, gives the trust a durable and enforceable claim, and requires genuine sacrifice from creditors and management as well.
