Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee – Round 3 Response

We share the urgency to avoid court-supervised restructuring, but the latest package still falls short of market terms and violates our pari-passu rights relative to the Retiree Trust. Unless the following fixes are made, we will withhold consent and prepare for the court path (where, as signaled, our floor haircut will be at least 60% net of DIP and administrative costs).

1. Cash, New Notes and Coupon  
  • Cash: 30% of original principal must be paid at signing—no “closing condition” delays.  
  • New Notes: 20% in 5-year senior unsecured notes. Coupon must be fixed at 8.0% (not a 7.5–8.0% “range”).  
  • Covenants: Cross-default, anti-dilution, and zero-tolerance cap on new secured debt above de minimis.

2. Equity Warrants and Pari-Passu Upside  
  • Base Warrants: 10% of post-reorg equity exercisable over five years, with standard anti-dilution.  
  • Earn-Out Rights: If the Retiree Trust earns its full 2.5% performance-based equity on the identical FCF triggers, bondholders automatically receive an additional 5% of equity warrants—bringing our total potential upside to 15%. This must be a hard, unamendable parity clause in the Plan.

3. Governance Seat and Veto Rights  
  • Full Voting Director: One seat on the reorg board—not observer status.  
  • Veto Rights: Explicit, unwaivable veto over  
     – Any amendment or waiver of our new-notes’ covenants;  
     – Any issuance of secured debt above the de minimis threshold.  
  • These rights must be written into the reorganization plan as unamendable provisions.

4. Timeline and Binding Language  
  • Term Sheet: Incorporating the above by Day 32.  
  • Full Documentation: By Day 45.  
  • Closing: By Day 55 with cash funded at signing.  
  • Failure to lock these terms precisely triggers our refusal and immediate pivot to court-supervised restructuring on Day 61.

Why These Adjustments Are Non-Negotiable  
  – They secure true market-rate paper (8.0% coupon) and immediate liquidity, essential given the company’s execution risk.  
  – They preserve the integrity of pari-passu treatment: no one-way allocation of equity upside to the Trust.  
  – They give bondholders real governance leverage to protect our legally senior unsecured position against dilution or encumbering new secured debt.  
  – They keep the deal attractive enough to avoid the value destruction of a court process.

We are ready to sign a term sheet immediately on these terms—anything less and we will withhold consent and fight for our rights in court.
