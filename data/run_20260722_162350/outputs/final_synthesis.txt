1. Decision Context  
Within a 60-day window, a government task force must secure a consensual restructuring of a major carmaker facing imminent collapse.  Bridge financing is conditional on all stakeholders—labour, retirees, unsecured bondholders, management, and co-funding governments—accepting material cuts: ~20% labour-cost savings, a 40% PV reduction in retiree‐health obligations, a 50% haircut on unsecured debt, and 15% plant-capacity rationalization.  If no consensus emerges, the task force will shift to a court-supervised plan capable of imposing terms.

2. Stakeholder Assembly  
• Government Task Force (lead funder)  
• Company Management (newly installed leadership)  
• Industrial Union & Retiree Health Trust  
• Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee  
• Neighbouring Governments (co-funders)

3. Final Positions  
• Government Task Force: Holds firm on 20% labour-cost savings by Month 15, 40% retiree-health cut with 7.5% + 2.5% earn-out equity, 15% capacity cuts under a single viability test, and bonds at a 50% haircut with 30% cash/20% new notes.  Will convert only limited veto demands into automatic, non-delaying “re-openers.”  
• Company Management: Mirrors the task force’s non-negotiables, adds a 5% “Labour Partnership Pool” for upskilling, offers 18-month regional grace for closures, and embeds all triggers in unamendable plan covenants—rejects open vetoes.  
• Industrial Union & Retiree Trust: Insists on 8.0% base equity (plus 2.5% earn-out), an 18-month phasing (M-12 to M-18) with a 70% productivity floor, two-year no-closure covenants for high-unemployment plants, and full veto rights on labour, retiree, and closure terms.  
• Unsecured Bondholders: Demand 30% cash at signing, new notes at a fixed 8.0% coupon, 10% base warrants plus 5% parity upside tied to retirees’ earn-out, a full voting board seat, and unamendable veto over covenants and secured debt issuance.  
• Neighbouring Governments: Require five-year no-closure guarantees for plants that pass the test, equity pari-passu with the central government, full voting board seats with veto on closures/divestitures, and workforce-capacity floors (90% jobs, 85% capacity).

4. Genuine Conflicts  
• Veto vs. Re-Opener: Labour, bondholders, and co-funders demand hard veto rights; task force and management insist on formula-driven re-openers only.  
• No-Closure Duration: 18 months (management/task force) vs. 24 months (union) vs. 60 months (co-funders).  
• Equity Shares & Governance: 7.5% vs. 7.75% vs. 8% retiree equity; 7.5% vs. 10%+ warrants for bondholders; pari-passu vs. subordinated equity for co-funders.  
• Labour-Cost Phasing: Final 5% cut by Month 15 (management/task force) vs. Month 18 (union).

5. Areas of Convergence  
• Depth of sacrifice: 20% labour-cost, 40% retiree-health, 50% bond haircut, 15% capacity cuts.  
• Timeline: term sheet by Day 35, full docs by Day 45, close by Day 55.  
• Governance architecture: nine-member board with independent directors and stakeholder seats.  
• Binding enforcement: unamendable plan covenants, performance metrics, and oversight committees.  
• Upskilling/redeployment fund: broad support for a dedicated equity or cash pool and joint oversight.

6. Key Trade-Offs for the Decision-Maker  
• Enforcement vs. Flexibility: Hard vetoes guarantee security but risk paralysis; re-openers protect timing but limit corrective power.  
• Community Protection vs. Global Restructuring: Longer no-closure covenants shield regions but constrain footprint optimization.  
• Equity Allocation: Bigger stakes for retirees and co-funders boost support but dilute value for bondholders and management.  
• Labour-Cost Timing: Slower phasing aids redeployment but postpones cash savings and risks creditor confidence.

7. Integrative Pathways  
• Sunset Veto Model: Grant 24-month full veto rights that convert to re-openers thereafter, balancing immediate security with long-term flexibility.  
• Tiered No-Closure Covenant: Universal three-year protection, plus automatic two-year extensions in high-unemployment regions if redeployment metrics aren’t met.  
• Blended Equity Upside: Set retiree base at 7.75% + 2.5% earn-out; bond warrants at 7.5% + 2.5% parity earn-out with an 8.0% coupon trade-off; co-funders at pari-passu minus a minor reserve.  
• Mid-Phasing Compromise: Final labour-cost cut by Month 16 with a 70% productivity floor and a 5% upskilling equity pool, enforced by automatic re-openers if breached.

8. Open Questions  
• Which re-opener triggers beyond Month 15 would the task force accept to broaden protection to three-plus years?  
• Would the union and trust accept 7.75% base equity and Month 16 phasing if secured in unamendable covenants and backed by a labour pool?  
• Will bondholders trade down to a 7.75% coupon for parity warrants and a two-year sunset veto seat?  
• Can co-funders reduce their covenant from five to four years if paired with stronger oversight and performance extensions?

9. Recommended Next Step  
Convene a minister-level “sprint summit” with each principal empowered to decide on one of the integrative pathways above.  Direct the working group to draft a unified term sheet by Day 32, focusing only on these four open questions, so that the plenary can sign off by Day 35 and preserve the consensual path.

10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Deliberation  
• Re-openers emerged as a critical “second-best” to vetoes, preserving enforceability and schedule discipline.  
• Stakeholders value symbolic equity slices almost as much as economic recovery, making a 0.25–0.5% shift politically potent.  
• Community and labour protections can be calibrated via performance-linked extensions rather than flat guarantees, unlocking compromise.  
• Transparency and oversight mechanisms (monthly FCF reporting, joint committees) became essential trade instruments, allowing flexibility on other dimensions.
