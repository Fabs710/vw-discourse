As neighbouring co-funders, our overriding goal is to safeguard our domestic production footprint and the jobs tied to it. We stand ready, in principle, to contribute alongside the lead government—but only on terms that lock in binding commitments for our plants and workforce, and give us an ownership and governance position commensurate with our financial contribution. Here’s where we sit today:

1. Conditionality: Binding Commitments on Domestic Footprint and Employment  
   • No‐Closure Guarantee: For all plants in our jurisdiction that pass the jointly agreed viability test, there must be a legally enforceable “no‐closure” covenant for at least five years post-restructuring.  
   • Minimum Production Share: We insist on a floor of 85% of our region’s current capacity—and at least 90% of the current workforce—being preserved in active production, subject only to the transparent, metric-based viability test (three quarters of consecutive EBIT losses even after cost cuts).  
   • Community Transition Fund: Any plant that does close under the viability test triggers a dedicated regional transition pool—financed 50% by central government, 30% by company, 20% by creditors—with joint oversight from our provincial/federal labour and economic-development agencies.

2. Equity Stake Proportional to Co-Funding  
   • We propose to co-finance up to €X billion (≈Y% of total rescue financing). In return, we expect an equity stake of Y% in the reorganized company (fully pre-emptive on future offerings), mirroring the lead government’s planned stake and exit timetable.  
   • Equity Instrument: Shares should be pari-passu with those issued to the central government, including dividend and voting rights identical to the taxpayer stake, to ensure our region’s interests are treated equally.

3. Governance Rights and Board Representation  
   • Board Seat: One full board seat (voting) with the right to veto any subsequent plant‐closure proposal affecting our region, and an observer seat on the audit & restructuring committee.  
   • Joint Oversight Committees:  
     – Labour & Restructuring Committee: We will appoint a member alongside the union and central government to monitor implementation of labour-cost and productivity measures in our plants.  
     – Community Investment Committee: To oversee the regional transition fund and modernization investments.

4. Alignment with Other Stakeholders  
   • We support the management’s proposal for a ~12% global capacity reduction, provided closures follow the agreed viability test and our “no-closure” exceptions for viable domestic plants are explicitly carved out.  
   • We back the union’s and retiree-trust’s call for secured status and equity stakes, so long as parity or adequate compensation is built in for unsecured bondholders (as they have outlined) to avoid litigation over claim ranking.  
   • We agree with the government task force that all co-funders must take identical economic and governance terms—no special carve-outs—but we insist that “identical terms” include the binding footprint and employment guarantees above.

5. Timing and Process  
   • We are ready to join the four-party working group immediately (government task force, management, union/trust, bondholders) on the understanding that our conditional term sheet is incorporated up front.  
   • All binding documents reflecting these commitments must be signed by Day 40, with financing and equity issuances closing no later than Day 50, leaving time for any regulatory approvals.

What’s unacceptable to us  
   – Any deal that permits closure of a viable domestic plant without our explicit consent and adherence to the viability test.  
   – A financing structure that dilutes our stake below what we contribute or relegates our equity to a junior class.  
   – Open-ended or non-binding “memoranda of understanding” on footprint or jobs.

In sum, we will co-fund the rescue only if—and only if—a package emerges that: (a) enshrines our domestic footprint and workforce protections in enforceable covenants; (b) grants us an equity stake and board seat proportional to our funding; and (c) aligns with the broader sacrifice and governance framework that the task force, labour, and creditors accept. If those conditions are met, we will provide our share of the bridge financing immediately. Otherwise, we must reserve our funds and let the court-supervised path proceed.
