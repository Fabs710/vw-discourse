Below is our assessment of the Union’s Round 3 demands and our final, realistic counter-offer—one that preserves the deal’s viability, meets the Task Force’s non-negotiables, and addresses the Union’s core concerns where we can, without blowing holes in the turn-around plan:

1. Retiree-Health Obligation  
  • We cannot move off the Task Force’s 40% PV haircut or the 7.5% base equity stake plus 2.5% earn-out. Those numbers are locked.  
  • To strengthen retiree confidence, we will commit to:  
     – Monthly, transparent reporting on Free Cash Flow against the Year 3/4 targets;  
     – A mid-Year 2 review by the joint Cap-&-Fund Oversight Committee to assess earn-out likelihood and adjust funding plans if needed.  
  • All Trust governance rights (senior secured status, cap-&-fund veto mechanisms) will be in unamendable Plan covenants.  

2. Labour-Cost Savings  
  • We understand the need to protect take-home pay and upskilling—but we cannot slip past Month 15 for the final 5% of cuts. Instead, we propose:  
     – 15% of total savings by Month 12; 5% by Month 15 (as the Task Force requires);  
     – A firm productivity floor: at least 70% of the 20% PV savings must derive from productivity/work-rule reforms and redeployment.  
     – Joint “Labour Partnership Pool” funds (5% of equity) pre-allocated to upskilling; managed by our CBA-enshrined committee.  

3. Regional Plant-Closure Safeguards  
  • We share the Union’s concern for high-unemployment communities, but blanket two-year no-closure covenants conflict with the global viability test. Instead:  
     – Maintain the three-quarter EBIT-loss “viability” test for all plants.  
     – For regions with unemployment > 7%, we will extend the “grace period” from 12 to 18 months post-closing—during which any proposed closure must be accompanied by a redeployment/retraining plan jointly approved by company and local labour reps.  
     – Local labour representation will sit on the Community-Transition Fund Oversight Committee.  

4. Board Rights and Re-Opener Triggers  
  • We confirm one full voting seat each for the Union and the Retiree Trust—and one non-voting bondholder observer. Management will have no board seat.  
  • We cannot grant open-ended veto rights. Instead, both Union and Trust seats will carry sharply limited “re-opener” triggers: if we materially breach the CBA phasing/productivity clauses or the 18-month regional grace-period commitment, either seat may demand immediate renegotiation under penalty of work-stoppage authorizations.  
  • These triggers will be laid out in the Plan documents as unamendable covenants.  

Why this works:  
  – It keeps the core rescue parameters exactly as the Task Force set them—40% retiree-health cut, 20% labour savings front-loaded by Month 15, 15% capacity rationalization on the viability test, 50% bond haircut with 30% cash/20% notes/10% warrants plus earn-out parity.  
  – It embeds a robust productivity requirement to protect lower-paid employees.  
  – It gives high-unemployment regions a meaningful window (18 months) to adapt, with local-labour oversight of redeployment plans.  
  – It secures essential veto-style protections via re-opener triggers, without gutting board governance or jeopardizing global footprint optimization.  
  – All commitments are contractually locked in unamendable Plan covenants and enforceable through the joint Oversight Committees.

If the Union will accept this package by close of business tomorrow, we will finalize the term sheet by Day 35 and move immediately to full documentation. If not, there is no path to a consensual deal and we will be forced—alongside the Task Force—to proceed via the court-supervised restructuring on Day 61 under these exact parameters.
