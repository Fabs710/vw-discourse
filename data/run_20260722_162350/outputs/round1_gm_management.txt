Thank you both for your constructive opening positions.  We share the government’s and the union’s urgency: without a binding deal in 60 days, we face either a court-imposed plan that destroys value or collapse of the entire enterprise.  Our goal remains a consensual restructuring that: (a) secures emergency financing; (b) binds in labour, retiree, creditor and management concessions; and (c) preserves as much of our production footprint, brands and dealer network as is viable.  

Below is our counter-proposal, balancing the task force’s depth of sacrifice requirements with the union’s need to protect jobs and retiree security—and keeping the path reversible and credible:

1. Labour-Cost Savings (Target: 20% PV reduction)  
   • Phase 1 (Immediate—Year 1): Across-the-board reduction of 10% in base labor cost via wage/productivity re-opener clauses, tighter overtime rules, and tiered work-rule adjustments tied to plant-specific performance.  
   • Phase 2 (Deferred—Years 2–3): Additional 10% savings contingent on meeting agreed productivity milestones (line-speed improvements, flexible staffing, joint upskilling/training programs).  
   • Protections:  – Job-security guarantees on all plants passing our jointly agreed “viability test” (loss-making three quarters post cost-savings).  – A retraining & transition fund financed by an equity-backed “labour partnership pool” (5% of reorganized equity, see below).  

2. Retiree-Health Obligation (Target: ~37.5% PV reduction)  
   • Cut: A 37.5% present-value reduction in existing retiree-health claims, phased over five years.  
   • Secured Claim: Trust remains senior to all unsecured creditors on the reorganized balance sheet.  
   • Equity: 5% of new common shares issued to the Trust, with full pre-emptive rights in future equity raises.  
   • Governance: Joint oversight committee (company + Trust appointees) to manage annual “cap & fund” contributions—fixed dollar obligations rising modestly over time but capped at inflation plus productivity gains.  

3. Capacity and Plant Footprint (Target: ~12% capacity reduction)  
   • Criteria-Based Closures: We will close or divest ~12% of capacity (plants demonstrably non-viable under our cost structure).  All other facilities receive the 90-day guaranteed “no-closure” covenant.  
   • Viability Test: Transparent, metric-driven (three consecutive quarters of EBIT loss, even after agreed cost cuts).  Applied equally across regions.  
   • Transition Support: Company will fund local transition plans for affected communities in partnership with government grants and a modest creditor-funded community pool (1% of post-reorg bond face).

4. Unsecured Bondholder Haircut (Target: 50% face reduction)  
   • 50% principal reduction, with 25% cash recovery and 25% rolled into new bonds bearing market-consistent yields plus warrants convertible into up to 10% of post-reorg equity.  
   • This package delivers fresh liquidity of ~€X billion up-front, meets the government’s 40–60% haircut requirement, and fairly matches labour/retiree sacrifices.

5. Governance and Board Structure  
   • Board Seats: Two seats reserved—one for the union, one for the retiree-trust—each with limited veto rights only on future material changes to labour or retiree-benefit commitments.  
   • Independent Oversight: Government appointee plus two independent directors; bondholder representative with observer rights.  
   • Management Incentives: All existing equity wiped out; new management plan re-priced on clear cash-break-even and return-on-capital milestones over three years.

6. Path Forward  
   • We propose an immediate four-party working group (government task force, management, union/trust, bondholders) to finalize legal documentation by Day 40, leaving a 20-day buffer for approvals.  
   • Should any party decline to sign, we must shift to the court-supervised route—risking deeper cuts, consumer flight, and loss of brand value.

Why this works:  
– It meets the task force’s call for 40–50% retiree-health reduction, 40–60% bond haircut, 20% labour cost savings, and 15%–20% capacity cuts in aggregate.  
– It secures crucial equity stakes and governance rights for the union and retirees.  
– It preserves over 88% of capacity, protecting core brands, dealers and customer confidence.  
– It leaves us with a credible path to cash-break-even and profitability within 18–24 months.

We believe this framework is the only way to thread the needle between too little sacrifice (rejected by the task force) and too much (destroying the franchise).  We stand ready to convene the working group today to convert these terms into binding agreements.
