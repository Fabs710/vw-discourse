We share the urgency and the government’s drive for a binding, 60-day consensual solution—but several of the lead-funder’s “final” guardrails directly conflict with the commitments we must secure for our active members and retirees.  We remain at the table, but only if the deal both (a) protects jobs and plants in our footprint and (b) delivers a durable retiree‐health package.  Below is where we stand:

1. Retiree-Health Obligation  
  • Present-Value Cut: We cannot accept a 40% PV reduction.  Our absolute ceiling is 35%, conditional on a secured claim and enhanced equity.  
  • Equity Stake: To compensate for that deeper cut, the Trust must receive 8% (not 7.5%) of reorganized common stock, with full pre‐emptive rights.  
  • Seniority: We accept senior secured status ahead of unsecured creditors—but only if the annual “cap & fund” mechanism is enshrined in the Plan and overseen 50/50 by Trust and company appointees.  

2. Labour-Cost Savings  
  • Total Reduction: We will agree to 20% PV labour-cost savings, provided at least 70% of the savings come from productivity/work-rule reforms (not pure wage cuts).  That protects real take-home pay for lower-paid employees.  
  • Phasing: We can live with 15% by Month 12, but the final 5% must be achieved no earlier than Month 24 (not Month 15) to allow time for joint upskilling and multi-plant redeployment.  
  • Job Security: We reject “viability test only” carve-outs.  We insist on a five-year no-closure guarantee for 90% of our footprint, except in plants that fail the agreed test AND are in regions with unemployment below 6%.  (For high-unemployment regions, no closures at all.)  

3. Capacity and Plant Closures  
  • We will accept a 15% global capacity reduction via the common viability test, but we require specific regional safeguards: plants in high-unemployment or strategically important communities get absolute protection for five years.  
  • All closures must trigger a community-transition fund (50% government / 30% company / 20% creditors), with Trust and local labour reps on the oversight committee.

4. Governance and Board Rights  
  • Seats: We confirm one full voting seat for the union and one for the Trust.  
  • Veto Rights: Each seat must carry a limited veto on any future change “materially affecting worker or retiree benefits or the agreed funding mechanism.”  
  • Cap & Fund Oversight: Joint Trust–company committee must have board-level recognition in the Plan documents.

5. Alignment with Bondholders  
  • We accept the Trust’s senior status alongside the proposed bond package (50% haircut, 30% cash/20% new notes/10% warrants), on the clear understanding that any breach of our five-year plant guarantees or the cap & fund triggers an immediate collective reopener.  

6. Bottom Lines and Next Steps  
  • If the government will raise the Trust’s equity stake to 8%, reduce the PV cut to 35%, revise the labour-phasing as above, and carve out the regional no-closure guarantees, we can sign on immediately.  
  • Without those core protections for our retirees and workforce, we must withhold consent—and would oppose any court-supervised plan that deepens cuts to benefits or sacrifices our plants beyond these narrowly drawn limits.

We stand ready to join the working group today—with binding term-sheet amendments reflecting the above—or we will have no choice but to prepare to fight any imposed restructuring that fails to honor these commitments.
