1. Retiree-Health Obligation  
  – PV Reduction: firm 40% cut. No lower.  
  – Equity: 7.5% of post-reorg common stock, full pre-emptive rights, senior secured status—and no flat uplift to 8%.  
  – Performance Upside: we will retain the management-proposed earn-out (up to +2.5% equity if Year 3/4 FCF hurdles are cleared). That mechanism stands exactly as drafted; there is no symmetric “flat” swap to 8%.  

   Why: 35% PV doesn’t deliver the taxpayer savings we need; 7.5% base equity plus conditional upside is already a generous bridge between management’s 5% and the union’s 10% ask.  

2. Labour-Cost Savings  
  – 20% PV target on today’s base costs.  
  – Front-loading: 15% by Month 12; remaining 5% by Month 15, all legally binding in the new CBA with productivity/work-rule standards. No extension to Month 18 or Month 24.  
  – Job-security carve-outs limited strictly to plants that fail the common viability test; grace-period relief only in very high-unemployment regions (12-month window), as management proposed.  

   Why: we cannot further delay cuts or weaken enforceability without jeopardizing the turnaround.  

3. Capacity Rationalization & Regional Guarantees  
  – 15% global capacity reduction via the common three-quarter EBIT-loss test—no additional regional “no-closure” covenants or veto rights.  
  – Community-transition fund remains 50% gov’t /30% company /20% creditors with joint oversight.  

   Why: any carve-outs beyond the viability metric undermine the global footprint optimization we must achieve.  

4. Unsecured Bondholders  
  – Haircut & consideration: 50% principal cut, with 30% cash at closing; 20% rolled into new 5-year notes at 7.5–8.0%; warrants for 10% of post-reorg equity (five-year life, anti-dilution).  
  – Covenants: standard cross-default, cap on new secured debt.  
  – Governance: one non-voting observer seat with explicit veto over covenant waivers and any new secured financing above a de minimis threshold.  

  Concession: we will link the bondholders’ warrants to the same earn-out trigger as the Trust’s. If the Retiree Trust earns its full +2.5% on performance, bondholders may likewise receive additional warrants up to an equivalent value.  

   Why: we refuse a full voting board seat or a permanent 15% equity stake. This earn-out parity is the only upside enhancement consistent with our commitment to balanced sacrifice.  

5. Co-Funders  
  – Equity pari-passu with central government, proportional to your funding share; identical dividend, voting, pre-emptive rights; exit by Year 5.  
  – No additional veto rights on plant closures beyond your board seat and the viability test; existing community-transition funding and committee representation remain as agreed.  

   Why: unconditional regional vetos or “85% capacity preservation” carve-outs cannot be reconciled with a uniform, performance-based restructuring.  

6. Governance & Timeline  
  – Board of nine: 3 independents (incl. Chair), 1 government, 1 management, 1 union, 1 retiree-trust, 1 co-funder, 1 bondholder observer.
