We recognize the urgency you’ve laid out: the 60-day clock, the need for a fully credible, binding plan, and sacrifices on all sides if we’re to avoid a court-imposed process.  Our members and retirees want this company to survive—but they will not shoulder a disproportionate burden, nor will we allow the retiree-health trust to be gutted.  Here is where we stand:

1.  Shared Sacrifice—but Fairly Allocated  
    •  Labour-Cost Savings:  We are prepared to negotiate new work-rules, moderate wage adjustments tied to productivity, and a phased 15% reduction in overall labour costs—conditional on:  
       –  Job-security guarantees and strict “no-plant-closure” tenets for up to 90% of our footprint;  
       –  A retraining fund for displaced workers, funded by an equity-backed “labour pool” in the reorganized company.  
    •  Retiree-Health Obligation:  We can discuss a present-value reduction in the 25–30% range—no deeper—only if:  
       –  The Trust receives a secured claim senior to unsecured bondholders on the reorganized balance sheet;  
       –  The Trust is issued a meaningful equity stake (we propose no less than 8–10% of new common shares) plus preemptive rights in any future equity offerings;  
       –  A durable “cap & fund” mechanism is established—with a clearly defined annual company funding obligation and joint oversight by Trust and company appointees.  

2.  Capacity, Brands, and Plant Closures  
    •  We cannot accept an across-the-board 15–20% capacity cut.  We will agree to targeted closures or brand exits only where plants are demonstrably nonviable (loss-making for three consecutive quarters even after cost-reduction measures).  We estimate this will cap closures at roughly 8–10% of capacity.  
    •  Any closure proposal must include:  
       –  A transparent, criteria-based “viability test” applied equally in all regions;  
       –  A community transition plan, funded in part by government support but with company and creditor contributions.  

3.  Governance and Board Representation  
    •  We insist on two seats on the reorganized board (one for the labour side, one for the retiree-trust side), with veto rights on any future plan that materially alters worker or retiree benefits.  
    •  We support independent board chairs and the inclusion of creditor and government representatives—but not at the expense of our agreed seats.  

4.  Alignment with Bondholder Sacrifices  
    •  We note your 40–60% haircut target for unsecured bondholders.  Our willingness to moderate retiree-health cuts to 25–30% depends on bondholders absorbing at least a 50% principal reduction, with the balance rolled over into new bonds or equity at market-consistent yields.  Any shortfall on bond-sacrifice must translate one-for-one into reduced cuts for our members and retirees.  

5.  Path to a Consensual Deal  
    •  We are committed to a consensual restructuring, but only one that shares pain equitably, locks in long-term funding for retiree health, and protects the core workforce.  
    •  If you move to impose deeper cuts on our benefits without the secured claim, equity stake, board seats, and fair sharing with bondholders we’ve outlined, we will have no choice but to withhold our consent—and we would vigorously oppose a court-driven plan that sacrifices retirees and workers more deeply than any creditor.  

We stand ready to enter a working group immediately—alongside management, creditors and your oversight team—to hammer out the precise terms of the funding-and-equity structure, the capacity-closure criteria, and the legally binding labour and retiree agreements.  But unless these core protections for jobs and benefits are in the final deal, we cannot sign on.
