Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee – Response, Round 2

Thank you for the detailed positions. We share everyone’s urgency—value is literally burning off by the day—and we want a consensual deal, but only on terms that protect our recovery and respect pari-passu treatment of unsecured claims.  Here’s where we stand:

1. Haircut and Consideration  
   a. We can live with a 50% headline haircut on face value—but only if it delivers meaningful, timely cash and real upside:  
      • 30% of original principal paid in cash at closing (not 25%), to get immediate liquidity into creditors’ hands.  
      • 20% rolled into new senior unsecured notes at a true market-clearing coupon (we propose 7.5–8.0%), with a five-year tenor.  
      • Equity upside: warrants or equity rights convertible into no less than 15% of post-reorg common shares (up from management’s 10%), with standard anti-dilution protection and a five-year exercise window.  
   b. This structure matches the government’s 40–60% haircut target, but shifts more recovery into immediate cash and upside optionality to compensate us for residual risk and delayed repayment.  

2. Ranking and Equal Treatment with Retiree Trust  
   a. We reject any proposal that gives the Retiree Health Trust a senior secured claim while fully subordinating unsecured bondholders.  If the Trust must be senior, then unsecured creditors require compensating economic or governance benefits.  Specifically:  
      • Either the Trust is restructured as an unsecured or pari-passu claim in the reorganized capital structure, or  
      • If the Trust remains secured, bondholders must receive an additional 2.5% of post-reorg equity (taking total equity upside to 17.5%) and placement of a bondholder-protective covenant that limits further secured borrowings above a de minimis threshold.  
   b. Anything less would be a treaty violation among unsecured creditors and effectively confiscatory.  

3. Governance and Protections  
   a. Board Representation: We request a full voting seat on the new board (or, at minimum, a board observer with veto rights on any further debt-or-equity issuances that dilute unsecured recoveries).  
   b. Protective Covenants: The new notes must include standard events-of-default and anti-dilution covenants—and a cross-default back to the existing bond documents to guard against opportunistic senior financings.  

4. Timing and Certainty  
   a. Upfront cash must be funded upon deal signing, subject only to customary closing conditions.  No prolonged “closing mechanics.”  
   b. We propose the same Day-40 target for documentation, but insist that our cash component be non-contingent on external government or neighbour-government approvals beyond an agreed term sheet.  

5. If We Cannot Get These Terms  
   a. We reserve all rights to withhold consent and pursue the expedited court process, where the task force’s stated floor is a 40–60% haircut anyway—likely at the 60% end once fees and DIP financing costs are imposed.  
   b. Our committee will litigate aggressively to protect pari-passu recovery, meaning the Trust’s senior claim will be challenged or its recovery capped to match our own.  

Why This Is Reasonable  
– It meets the government’s 40–60% haircut mandate and the management plan’s 50% target.  
– It accelerates real cash back to creditors when they need it most.  
– It preserves upside via equity, aligning all private stakeholders in the company’s future success.  
– It protects the integrity of pari-passu unsecured claims and forestalls destructive litigation over ranking.  
– It can be documented in time for a consensual close well before Day 60—avoiding a value-destroying court route.

We remain ready to join the four-party working group today, but only if our enhanced recovery, pari-passu protections, and governance rights are baked into the mandate from Day 1. Otherwise, we will have no choice but to prepare for the court-supervised restructuring.
