As lead funder, our core requirements remain non‐negotiable. We appreciate the progress so far but must sharpen these terms now:

1. Labour-Cost Reductions  
   •   We will not accept a deferred, multi-year ramp-up. We require legally binding labour-cost savings totalling 20% of today’s base labour expense within the first twelve months.  
   •   Phasing must be front-loaded: a 15% reduction by Month 12 and the remaining 5% by Month 15, all locked into new collective‐bargaining agreements with enforceable productivity and work-rule standards.  
   •   Job-security covenants remain limited to plants that pass the agreed “viability test.” Any “no-closure” carve-out beyond that test undermines the credibility of our global footprint rationalization and will not be tolerated.

2. Retiree-Health Obligation  
   •   We insist on at least a 40% present-value reduction of the existing retiree-health debt, fully implemented via amendments to the Trust agreements. No lower percentage will meet our fiscal constraint.  
   •   The Trust will hold a senior secured claim ahead of all unsecured creditors on the reorganized balance sheet.  
   •   We will allocate 7.5% of reorganized common equity to the Retiree Health Trust (with full pre-emptive rights), rather than the 5% initially proposed—this is our final offer to bridge management’s 5% and the union’s 8–10% ask.  
   •   The annual “cap & fund” mechanism, co-oversighted by company and Trust representatives, must be enshrined in the Plan documents.

3. Unsecured Bondholders  
   •   Haircut: 50% principal reduction, aligning with the upper half of our 40–60% target.  
   •   Consideration:  
       – 30% of original principal in cash at closing;  
       – 20% of original principal rolled into new senior unsecured notes (5-year tenor) at a market clearing coupon (7.5–8.0%);  
       – Warrants exercisable into 10% of post-reorg equity (five-year expiry, standard anti-dilution protection).  
   •   Ranking: The Trust’s senior secured status stands. Bondholders receive no seniority but will get robust protective covenants in the new notes (cross-default, maximum secured debt cap) and one non-voting observer seat on the new board and full voting rights to block any covenant waivers.  
   •   We will not entertain any further equity upside above 10% or a full voting board seat.

4. Capacity Rationalization and Plant-Closure Covenants  
   •   Target 15% of global capacity closed or divested (to fall within the original 15–20% range).  
   •   All closures determined by the common viability test (three quarters of EBIT losses post-cost cuts).  
   •   No region-specific “no-closure” carve-outs beyond that test. Binding community-transition funding pools will be set up—financed 50% by central government, 30% by company, 20% by creditors.

5. Equity Stakes & Governance  
   •   Government equity stake: up to 25% on closing, with a clear exit path by Year 5.  
   •   Retiree Health Trust: 7.5% of common equity.  
   •   Labour Partnership Pool: 5% of common equity to fund retraining, attached to new labour contracts.  
   •   Co-funders: equity stakes identical to their financing share, fully pari-passu with central government equity.  
   •   Board composition (9 seats): 3 independents (chair + 2), 1 government, 1 management, 1 union, 1 retiree-trust, 1 co-funder, and 1 non-voting bondholder observer.

6. Timeline and Process  
   •   We require executed term sheets reflecting these final terms by Day 35, full documentation by Day 45, and closing no later than Day 55.  
   •   Any hold-out beyond these dates triggers our commitment to the court-supervised route, where we will impose these same parameters—and likely deeper cuts as DIP and administrative expenses mount.

If any party cannot live within these guardrails, we will not delay. We will initiate the court-supervised restructuring the day after Day 60 and enforce these sacrifices unilaterally. We stand ready to convene the working group immediately on these final terms.
