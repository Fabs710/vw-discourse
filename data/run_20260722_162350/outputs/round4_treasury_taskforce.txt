In direct response to the Moderator’s question—“Which, if any, of the union’s veto-style protections could be recast as structured ‘re-opener’ triggers without undermining your need for strict enforceability and no delay past Month 15?”—here is what we are prepared to do, and what remains non-negotiable:

1. Phasing/Productivity Covenants  
   • Non-Compliant Trigger: If by Month 12 the company has failed to deliver at least 70% of the 15% front-loaded labour-cost savings through bona fide productivity/work-rule reforms (as measured by agreed metrics in the CBA), the Union seat may invoke a “re-opener” that compels immediate remedial negotiations on deployment and work-rule standards.  
   • Boundaries: This does not delay the Month 15 deadline for the final 5% cut—even under re-opener, the last tranche must still be implemented by Month 15.  

2. Upskilling and Redeployment Plans  
   • Non-Delivery Trigger: If, at any point during the 18-month regional grace period, the company fails to propose or execute a jointly approved redeployment/upskilling plan for a high-unemployment plant, the Union may trigger a re-negotiation of that plant’s transition measures.  
   • Boundaries: This mechanism replaces a full veto over closures; it does not alter the viability test or grant automatic extension of labour-cuts beyond Month 15.  

3. Community-Transition Fund Oversight  
   • Failure-to-Fund Trigger: If the community fund is not capitalized or governed in strict accordance with the Plan covenants, the Union seat may reopen discussions on funding contributions—but may not block or delay the deal close.  

What We Will Not Convert to Re-Openers  
   – The 40% PV cut to the retiree-health obligation and the 7.5% + 2.5% earn-out equity package (these remain firm).  
   – The Month 15 deadline for the final 5% labour-cost savings.  
   – The single, three-quarter-EBIT global viability test for capacity rationalization.  
   – Any plant-closure decisions that pass the viability test after the 18-month regional grace window.  

Why This Preserves Enforceability  
   • Enforcement remains judicially binding: re-openers are automatic, formula‐driven, and cannot delay cut-dates.  
   • No wholesale veto: the Union gains only targeted “course-correction” rights on productivity and redeployment—not the power to re-negotiate fundamentals.  
   • Speed is protected: all labour-cost measures still conclude by Month 15; capacity rationalization by Day 55; equity and retiree terms by Day 35–45.  

In short, we will convert only those narrow, process-oriented veto demands into structured re-openers—strictly limited to productivity metrics and redeployment plan delivery—while holding firm on all our core deal mechanics and timelines. Any further expansion of veto authority or delay beyond Month 15 is unacceptable and would force us onto the court-supervised path.
