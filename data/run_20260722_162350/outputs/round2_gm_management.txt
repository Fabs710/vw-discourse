Below is our revised counter‐proposal, reflecting the government’s “final” guardrails while addressing the union’s core concerns on phasing, equity and enforceable protections. We believe it secures the financing, locks in binding concessions and preserves the enterprise as a going concern—yet remains reversible if performance targets aren’t met.

1. Retiree-Health Obligation  
  • Present‐Value Reduction: We accept the task force’s 40% PV haircut, fully implemented in the amended Trust agreements on closing.  
  • Equity Stake: The Trust receives 7.5% of reorganized common stock with full pre-emptive rights.  
  • Contingent Restoration (“Re-opener”): If annual free cash flow before dividends exceeds €X in both Year 3 and Year 4, the Trust will recover up to an additional 2.5% equity (for a maximum 10% total) or equivalent cap-&-fund top-up. This protects retirees if the turnaround outperforms.  
  • Governance: The Trust’s senior secured claim stands; a 50/50 Trust–company Cap & Fund Oversight Committee is explicitly enshrined in the Plan.

2. Labour-Cost Savings  
  • Total Target: 20% present-value savings on today’s base labour cost.  
  • Phasing:  
     – 15% reduction achieved by Month 12 via wage-productivity clauses and work-rule reforms.  
     – Final 5% by Month 18 (rather than Month 15), allowing joint upskilling, redeployment and minimal pure wage cuts.  
  • Productivity Floor: At least 70% of the aggregate savings must come from productivity/work-rule enhancements.  
  • Job-Security Guarantees: All plants passing the common viability test (three consecutive quarters of EBIT losses post–cost cuts) are subject to that test only; no additional carve-outs. However, for plants in regions with unemployment above 7%, any closure will trigger a 12-month “grace period” before implementation, during which the company must propose redeployment or retraining plans.  
  • Labour Partnership Pool: 5% of new equity allocated to finance retraining, overseen by a joint Trust-company committee.

3. Capacity Rationalization (15% Global)  
  • Closure/Divestiture: 15% of global capacity, determined solely by the metric-based viability test.  
  • Community Transition Fund: 50% government / 30% company / 20% creditors, with local labour and government reps on the oversight board.

4. Unsecured Bondholders  
  • Haircut and Consideration (50%):  
     – 30% cash at closing.  
     – 20% in new 5-year senior unsecured notes at 7.5–8.0% coupon.  
     – Warrants for 10% of post-reorg equity (5-year expiry, anti-dilution).  
  • Protective Covenants: Cross-default back to existing bonds; cap on future secured debt.  
  • Board Rights: One non-voting observer seat with explicit blocking rights over any covenant waiver or new secured financing above a de minimis threshold.

5. Equity Stakes & Board Composition (9 seats)  
  • Central Government: up to 25% equity, exit by Year 5.  
  • Retiree Health Trust: 7.5% (plus up to 2.5% earn-out).  
  • Labour Partnership Pool: 5%.  
  • Co-funding Neighbouring Governments: equity proportional to their financing share, pari-passu with the central government stake.  
  • Management: 0%.  
  • Independent Directors (3, including Chair), plus one each for government, union, retiree trust, and one non-voting bondholder observer.

6. Triggers & Re-openers  
  • Retiree Re-opener: on FCF thresholds (Year 3–4).  
  • Union Re-opener: if Cap & Fund contributions or job-security commitments are materially breached.  

7. Timeline  
  • Term Sheets Executed by Day 33.  
  • Full Documentation by Day 45.  
  • Closing by Day 55.  
  • Any hold-out or deviation from these milestones will force us—and the task force—to move to the court-supervised restructuring on Day 61, enforcing these terms unilaterally.

Why This Works  
  – Meets the task force’s 40% retiree-health cut, 20% labour savings and 15% capacity reduction within the required windows.  
  – Safeguards retiree interests with both seniority and performance-linked restoration.  
  – Balances the union’s phasing and productivity floor with the government’s need for front-loaded savings.  
  – Delivers bondholders immediate cash, market-rate paper and equity upside—while preserving pari-passu integrity.  
  – Secures finance, preserves ~85% of capacity under viable footprints, and retains brand and dealer confidence.
