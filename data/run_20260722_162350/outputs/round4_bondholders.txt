Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee Response – Round 4

We share the imperative to avoid a value-destroying court path, but Management’s Round-4 proposal still materially under-protects our legally senior unsecured claims.  We remain anchored to the core terms below and cannot accept any further dilution of governance or economic rights:

1. Cash at Signing and New‐Notes Economics  
 • 30% of original principal must be paid in cash at signing (Day 55) with no conditions or financing outs.  
 • 20% rolled into new 5-year senior unsecured notes at a fixed 8.0% coupon.  The 7.5–8.0% range is unacceptable—only 8.0% will match market comparables and compensate for execution risk.  
 • Covenants: full cross-default, zero-tolerance cap on new secured debt above de minimis, plus standard anti-dilution protections on our rolled notes.  

2. Equity Warrants & Pari-Passu Upside  
 • Base Warrants: 10% of post-reorg common stock, exercisable over five years with full anti-dilution rights.  
 • Performance Earn-Out: If—and only if—the Retiree Trust earns its full 2.5% performance equity on the identical FCF triggers, we must automatically receive an additional 5% warrants.  This parity uplift must be a hard, unamendable clause in the Plan.  

3. Governance & Veto Rights  
 • Full Voting Director: A bona fide board seat, not observer status.  No one-way demotion to observer; our seat must carry the same voting rights as other equity holders.  
 • Explicit Veto: That director must have unwaivable veto authority over any amendment or waiver of our new-notes’ covenants and over any issuance of secured debt above the agreed de minimis threshold.  These veto rights must be written into the Plan as non-amendable provisions.  

4. Timeline & Binding Language  
 • Term-Sheet: Incorporating all of the above by close of business Day 32.  
 • Full Documentation: By Day 45, with cash funded at signing (Day 55).  
 • Failure to lock these terms precisely triggers our refusal and immediate pivot to court-supervised restructuring on Day 61, where our floor haircut will be at least 60% net of DIP and administrative costs.  

Why We Cannot Compromise Further  
 • Observer status—even with covenant vetoes—provides no real check on future dilution or secured financings.  Only a voting director can enforce our contractual rights.  
 • A fixed 8.0% coupon and immediate cash are pre-requisites for avoiding liquidity and execution risks that would decimate our recoveries.  
 • Without full parity in economic upside, we cannot accept a one-way allocation of warrants to the Retiree Trust.  

We stand ready to execute a consensual plan on these terms—but will not move an inch on governance, coupon or equity upside.  Anything less and we will prepare for the court process, where our leverage—and the likely recovery floor—will be significantly higher than under any “compromise” on these points.
