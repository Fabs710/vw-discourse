Here is a neutral summary of where the four-party talks have hit a wall, three possible middle-path packages that could bring the government task force, management, the union/retiree trust, bondholders and co-funders back to the table, and one focused question for each stakeholder group:

1. Core Impasse  
   • Retiree-health equity: 7.5% base (plus earn-out) vs. the union’s firm 8.0% base.  
   • Labour-cost swing: government/management insist on Month 15 for the last 5% vs. the union’s push to Month 18 (and a higher productivity floor).  
   • Regional closures: a single global EBIT-loss test vs. union/co-funders’ multi-year no-closure covenants in high-unemployment regions.  
   • Governance and veto: Task Force and management want limited re-opener triggers or observer seats; union, retirees, bondholders and co-funders demand full, unamendable veto rights.  
   • Bondholder economics and board rights: coupon range (7.5–8.0%) and observer status vs. fixed 8.0% + full voting seat + stronger vetoes.

2. Three Facilitator-Proposed Compromise Packages  
   Compromise A: “Hybrid Equity and Transparency”  
     – Retiree-trust base stake at 7.75% with the same 2.5% performance earn-out.  
     – All earn-out hurdles, monthly FCF reporting and all governance rights locked in unamendable plan covenants.  
     – Keeps taxpayer savings almost intact while splitting the 0.5% gap.  

   Compromise B: “Staggered Phasing with Upskilling Fund”  
     – 15% labour-cost savings by Month 12; final 5% by Month 16 (a midpoint between 15 and 18).  
     – Maintain at least 70% of savings from productivity/redeployment.  
     – A dedicated “Labour Partnership Pool” (5% of new equity) for retraining, overseen by the CBA committee.  

   Compromise C: “Tiered Regional Safeguards”  
     – Continue the three-quarter EBIT test globally.  
     – In regions >7% unemployment: an 18-month grace window (vs. 12 or 24), after which any closure triggers a redeployment plan approved by local labour reps.  
     – Co-funders get a 3-year no-closure covenant for plants that “pass,” rather than five, but retain veto over any extension of secured financings that would undermine regional commitments.

3. Pointed Questions for Each Stakeholder  
   – To the Government Task Force:  
     “Which, if any, of the union’s veto-style protections could be recast as structured ‘re-opener’ triggers without undermining your need for strict enforceability and no delay past Month 15?”

   – To the Union & Retiree Trust:  
     “Would you consider moving to an 18-month phasing for the last 5% if the parties enshrine a 70% productivity floor and create a funded upskilling pool, all in unamendable CBA covenants?”

   – To Company Management:  
     “If bondholders receive a fixed 8.0% coupon on new notes, would you accept their governance rights remaining as a non-voting observer plus narrowly drawn covenant vetoes?”

   – To the Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee:  
     “Could you accept capped additional earn-out warrants (for instance 5% instead of 10%) in exchange for agreeing to observer-status governance plus anti-dilution and secured-debt veto covenants?”

   – To the Co-Funding Governments:  
     “If your no-closure guarantee were limited to three years rather than five—paired with stronger redeployment oversight and your board veto on regional plant divestitures—would you fund under the lead funder’s core economics?”
