**Neighbouring Governments (Co-Funders) — Round 4 (Final Round)**

We are, as the Committee put it, close but not closed. We'll take stock of what's actually been settled in our favor, press on the one sequencing problem that threatens to blow through the deadline before our core condition can be tested, and restate our floor precisely, because this is the last round and vagueness now costs us later.

**On the Task Force's ruling against Management's carve-outs.** We support this fully, for the same reason the Committee does: a management incentive plan sized by the party that benefits from it, taken off the top before proportionality is even calculated for contributing parties, is not proportional treatment — it's queue-jumping dressed as standard practice. We'd add one thing the Committee didn't emphasize: this ruling matters to us too, even though the incentive plan draws primarily from the Committee's and Trust's side of the pool rather than ours. It matters because it establishes the precedent we're relying on for our own ask below — that Management does not get to price its own convenience first and ask contributing parties to accept what's left. If that discipline holds for the incentive plan, it has to hold for the capital-raise reserve's effect on our stake too.

**On the reserve, consent rights, and Management's counterproposal.** We accept the workable-threshold logic — majority-inclusive, time-bound, not unanimous veto. That's a sensible answer to a real problem. But we need the same explicit statement Management gave the Trust, given to us in equally explicit terms. Management confirmed the Trust's converted stake is included in "converted-equity holders" for consent purposes. We are not a converted claimant — we are a new-capital contributor pricing $4.1 billion against a stated pool. We asked in round three for our board representative to be included in the consent mechanism over reserve issuance affecting our stake specifically. We want that confirmed now, in the same direct language Management used for the Trust: **yes, Neighbouring Governments' board representative is included in the consent threshold over any issuance from the 12% reserve, on the same footing as the Committee and the Trust.** If the answer is anything less than that — if our $4.1 billion is treated as new capital for pricing purposes but excluded from the protections new capital would reasonably expect against future dilution — that is exactly the asymmetry the Task Force has ruled out twice now, and we will not accept it silently.

**On the sequencing problem — this is the one that actually worries us, and we want it addressed directly rather than assumed away.**

Here is the arithmetic problem nobody has yet named plainly. The Task Force has ruled: facility data delivered within five days, then a genuine joint review — Management proposes seven days, we don't object to that clock in principle. That's twelve days minimum before the closable-versus-structural determination on our plants is even finalized. Management has also said, correctly and honestly, that it cannot produce our final equity percentage in the allocation table until that same determination is fixed. The Task Force has said it will file for court supervision on day sixty if the four deliverables aren't closed with real numbers by then.

We need to know: **does the twelve-day facility review-and-determination clock fit inside the time remaining before day sixty, or does our final number simply not exist by the deadline?** If it doesn't fit, we are being asked to either sign a number that isn't yet real, or watch the whole process go to court over a timing gap that has nothing to do with disagreement on substance. Neither of those is acceptable to us, and we don't think either is what the Task Force actually wants either, given its own statement that it doesn't want technical calendar mechanics forcing a court filing over a dispute that isn't substantive.

Our proposal to close this gap: **provisionally lock our $4.1 billion contribution and equity pricing at the figures already on the table now, structured with a true-up mechanism** — if the joint facility review, completed within its stated window, shows our plants are structural and some portion of our capital is no longer needed to protect capacity that no longer exists, our contribution and stake adjust downward accordingly at that point, without reopening the rest of the closed deal. If the review shows our plants are closable and our $600 million retooling offer is the right answer, our number holds as stated. This lets the overall settlement close on schedule while preserving the actual substance of the test we care about — we are not asking to delay closing to relitigate; we are asking that closing not require us to guess the answer to a question the process itself says needs twelve more days to answer honestly.

**On footprint depth and the "fuller viability lens."** We align with the Union's framing more than we did in round three, now that we've heard Management's response. Management says it already models supplier concentration risk and will make that visible in the joint review — good, we'll test that claim directly rather than take it on faith, the same way we're testing our own plants' data. We are not asking the Task Force to protect a floor as a matter of comfort. We are asking, as the Union did, that the review Management just committed to for individual facilities also honestly incorporate capability and supply-chain depth for the system as a whole, not just near-term unit cost. If it does, and the number that comes out is deeper than what's on the table today, we will not treat that as a broken promise from Management — we'll evaluate it against our own plants' specific data as agreed. If it doesn't — if the review turns out to be the same unilateral model with a new label — our objection is the same as the Union's and needs no separate restating.

**On the warranty backstop.** No objection to Management's $1.1–1.4 billion sizing or its structure. We'd simply repeat our interest: a company that keeps selling cars in our jurisdictions during this transition protects the jobs our capital is meant to protect, so we have every reason to want this mechanism to work, and no reason to complicate it.

**On the incentive plan benchmark.** We support the Union's and Committee's request that the benchmark set not be chosen or curated by Management alone. Consistency in what gets independently verified matters to whether we sign this in good faith or discover after the fact that "standard" meant "convenient."

**Where our floor stands, restated for the last time in this process.** We do not need a general governance seat over the whole company, and we haven't asked for one. We need: our contribution and equity priced and protected on the same proportional, non-diluted basis as every other contributing or converting party; a genuine joint review of our plants' data, completed inside a defined window, before any closure determination on them is treated as final; and confirmation — not implication — that our consent rights over the reserve match what's been given to the Trust and the Committee. If those three things are in the closing paper, with the sequencing gap resolved through the true-up mechanism we've proposed rather than left to chance, we sign and we co-fund. If our plants are shown structural through that fair process, we accept closure — that has not moved since round two and will not move now. But if the twelve-day review doesn't happen, or happens as a formality after the fact, or our consent protections turn out to be weaker than what other contributing parties received, we withdraw our $4.1 billion in full. That is not new leverage introduced at the last minute — it is the same condition we have stated in every round of this process, and it stands.
