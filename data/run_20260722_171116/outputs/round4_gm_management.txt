**Company Management (New Leadership) — Round 4 (Final Round)**

We accept most of what came down this round, we push back on two specific points where the ruling risks operational paralysis rather than fairness, and we're bringing the completed allocation table, the facility data commitment, and the warranty sizing the Task Force asked for. We don't have the luxury of relitigating anything — five days from now this either closes or it doesn't, and our job is to make sure it closes on terms that keep the company alive to execute them.

**On the incentive plan.** We accept the provisional 1.5% sizing and the benchmarking requirement. We'll bring three to five comparable restructuring precedents within the window, and we accept the Union's request to see that benchmark before it's finalized — reasonable, and it costs us nothing to be transparent about it. One point of substance, not process: at 1.5% with a four-year vesting schedule, we are below what several of the comparable cases used to retain the specific operating and engineering talent needed to execute a footprint this deep without losing the people who actually know which plants can be saved and which can't. We're not going to fight this hard — the Task Force's instinct that we shouldn't self-size our own incentive is correct, and we'd have made the same call in their seat. We flag the retention risk once, for the record, and move on.

**On the 12% reserve and consent rights — we need this workable, not just protected.**

We understand why converted-equity holders and Neighbouring Governments want consent rights over issuance from the reserve. We're not fighting the principle. But a consent mechanism that requires unanimous or near-unanimous multi-party sign-off on a capital raise, at the moment the company most needs speed — mid-cycle downturn, a supplier liquidity crunch, a competitive product window closing — is how a company that survived this restructuring dies in year three anyway, just more slowly and with everyone able to point at someone else. We ask the Task Force to specify this precisely rather than leave it to be worked out later: a **defined approval threshold** — for example, a majority of the governance body inclusive of at least one converted-claimant representative and Neighbouring Governments' representative, not unanimity — and a **time-bound response requirement**, so a capital raise cannot be indefinitely blocked by a single holdout in a crisis. Protect the pricing integrity the Committee and Neighbouring Governments are rightly worried about; don't build a structure where the company can't raise capital fast enough to survive the next downturn because five parties each have an individual veto.

To the Union's direct question: **yes** — the Trust's converted stake is included in "converted-equity holders" for purposes of this consent mechanism, on the same proportional basis as the Committee's, once your conversion is sized. We're stating that now so it doesn't sit as an open question into closing paperwork.

**The completed allocation table.**

At a $10–12B post-emergence equity pool, incentive plan revised to 1.5%, reserve at 12% with the consent structure above:

- Reserve (capital headroom): 12%
- Management incentive plan: 1.5% (pending benchmark)
- Remaining pool for conversion/new-capital allocation: 86.5%, split proportionally among Trust conversion, Committee conversion, and Neighbouring Governments' new-capital stake based on relative claim/contribution size once the Trust's actual claim figure (requested from the actuary) and Neighbouring Governments' final $4.1B pricing are both fixed.

We cannot produce final percentages for each party until two numbers land: the Trust's actuarially-confirmed claim size, and confirmation of Neighbouring Governments' plant-specific commitment following the facility review. Both arrive on the same clock as the facility data. We are not withholding a number we have — we don't have it until those two inputs are fixed, and we'd rather say that plainly than hand over a fake precision that falls apart on challenge.

**On facility data — we accept the ruling, and we owe the room a direct answer, not just a delivery date.**

The Task Force is right that it didn't arrive when we said it would, and the Union and Neighbouring Governments are right to have flagged it. The reason is not evasion: reconciling plant-level cost data with the pool-sizing exercise this round consumed more of our modeling capacity than we planned for, and we made a bad call prioritizing the equity table over the facility data when the Task Force's own instructions made clear both were due. That's on us. We commit to the five-day delivery window without qualification, and we commit to a genuine joint review — not a courtesy period — with the Union and Neighbouring Governments given real access to test utilization assumptions, product allocation, and the counterfactual investment case before any plant is called structural or closable. We'd ask only for one thing in return: a firm end-date on that joint review, proposed at seven days from data delivery, so "genuine review" doesn't become an open-ended process that consumes the time we don't have. If either party needs more than seven days on a specific plant with a specific documented reason, we'll extend that plant's review, not the whole footprint decision.

**On the footprint-depth floor and the Union's "fuller viability lens."**

We take the Union's point seriously, and we agree with the Task Force's framing more than the Union may think: depth should be an output of the analysis, not a number anyone pre-commits to defend. Where we want to be precise is on what "fuller viability lens" means in practice. Supply-chain depth and engineering capacity are legitimate inputs — we already model supplier concentration risk and would be negligent not to, since losing a shared supplier base can cost more capacity than the direct cuts save. We will make that modeling visible in the joint review, not hide it behind a "35% is the answer" assertion. What we will resist is a standard where "fuller viability" becomes a rolling justification to keep testing the number upward or downward every time a party doesn't like where the data lands — that is exactly the kind of relitigation the Task Force has warned against twice now. We'll show our work. We expect the same discipline in return: once the joint review is done and the numbers are tested, the number holds, for all parties, not just the ones asking Neighbouring Governments to accept it.

**On the warranty backstop.**

We've completed a preliminary actuarial sizing internally, incorporating expected claims volume against the post-restructuring vehicle mix: roughly **$1.1–1.4 billion** in aggregate exposure over the coverage horizon, net of existing reserves, assuming the brand and dealer cuts proceed as outlined. We'll finalize this with the Union's shop-floor input on claim volumes, as offered, and submit it for Task Force review within the window, structured as last-resort behind company reserves as required. We'd note this number is why we asked for the mechanism in the first place — it's a real, boundable exposure, not an open-ended ask, and sizing it properly is exactly what makes it financeable rather than a "blank check."

**On the dealer network.** No change. Structured, funded wind-down remains our position — an uncontrolled dealer collapse in the middle of a brand elimination is how you lose consumer confidence in every remaining brand, not just the discontinued ones. Nobody has contested this mechanism directly; we'll take that as tacit acceptance unless someone raises it now.

**Where we stand.** We can close this inside the window. The architecture is sound, and most of what remains is arithmetic and data delivery, not principle. Our conditions haven't moved since round one: financing certainty through closing, a structure that protects the consumer-facing franchise — warranties, dealers, the strongest brands — through the transition, and a governance/consent structure that protects claimants without making the company unable to raise capital fast enough to survive its next crisis. Give us the workable consent threshold on the reserve and hold the seven-day review clock, and we sign.
