**Industrial Union & Retiree Health Trust — Round 4 (Final Round)**

We'll take the Task Force's rulings point by point, because most of what came down this round is what we asked for — and where it isn't, we're going to say so plainly rather than let silence be read as agreement.

**On the incentive plan and the reserve — this is the correction we needed, and we support it fully.**

The Task Force just did exactly what "shared sacrifice" is supposed to mean: it looked at Management pricing its own priorities first, before proportionality was even calculated for the parties actually taking the haircut, and it said no. Provisional sizing at 1.5%, subject to an independent benchmark, is the right instinct. We'll add one thing: whatever benchmark set is used for the incentive plan, we want the Union to see it before it's finalized — not because we're claiming expertise in executive comp design, but because "comparable restructurings" is exactly the kind of phrase that can be quietly cherry-picked, the same way healthcare trend assumptions can be. Same discipline, same transparency, applied to Management's ask as to ours.

On the 12% reserve with consent rights attached to converted-equity holders and Neighbouring Governments' board representation — good. We'd ask directly: does "converted-equity holders" include the Trust once our conversion is sized and our proportional governance seat is calculated? If the answer is yes, we're satisfied. If the consent mechanism is structured to include the Committee and Neighbouring Governments but leaves the Trust's converted stake without the same protection against future dilution, that's exactly the quiet asymmetry we flagged in round three, and we'll object to it on the same grounds the Committee used against Management's original carve-out. We expect the answer to be yes, and we want it stated as such before this closes, not assumed.

**On the instrument-equivalence test — confirmed as we asked, and we hold both sides to it, including ourselves.**

The Task Force has now ruled, in terms we accept without reservation: no default deference to our capped obligation, no default suspicion of the Committee's CVR. Same rigor on trend rate and enterprise-value forecast. We were clear last round that if the actuary's process shows our instrument carries more expected value than warranted, that's an inequality that runs against the Committee and should be corrected the same way a cost-basis discount against us would have been. We repeat that now, formally, because it has to be more than a rhetorical gesture: if the numbers land against us, we accept a downward correction to our cap or trend assumption. We're not asking for a standard we won't hold ourselves to.

One addition: the actuary needs plan census and claims data to build the base liability model — that same underlying dataset also establishes the actual dollar size of our converting claim, which is the number needed for the governance-seat ratio the Task Force has asked for twice now. We'd ask that the actuary's output include that claim-size figure explicitly, so governance math and instrument-equivalence testing close together rather than requiring a separate exercise. That's not a new ask — it's making one process do the work of two.

**On facility data — this is the single most important ruling for us this round, and we want it stated clearly why.**

The Task Force ruled against Management on this, and we're not going to soften that into diplomatic language. Facility-by-facility data was promised before round three. It didn't arrive. That's not a technicality — it's the input that determines which of our members still have jobs in eighteen months, and it cannot be decided on a model nobody outside Management has tested. The Task Force's five-day delivery window with a genuine joint review period, and its statement that it will not treat a unilateral Management determination as final absent that review, is exactly what we asked for in round three. We take the Task Force at its word here and we will hold Management to the five-day clock without patience for further slippage, because slippage on this specific item has already cost us one full round.

To Neighbouring Governments directly: we remain aligned with you on this — a fair test, not a predetermined conclusion. If the joint review happens and your plants are shown structural, we won't ask you to keep funding them past that finding, for the reasons we gave last round. We'll be at the table for that review doing the same work on our own members' behalf.

**On the footprint-depth floor — we need to respond to the Task Force directly, because it addressed this to us by name.**

The Task Force says depth is an output of viability math, not an input negotiated at the table, and that it won't accept a pre-committed ceiling as a matter of principle. We understand the logic, and we're not going to pretend we can simply overrule it by repeating the same number louder. But we want to correct what we think is a slight mischaracterization of what we said.

We did not ask the Task Force to cap the footprint at 35% regardless of what the data shows. What we said is that we will not sign a plan that takes total production capacity below what's needed to maintain the core skilled workforce and supplier base required to actually build the next generation of vehicles. That is not a comfort floor. That is a viability floor — and the Task Force's own closing words this round conceded exactly this point: "a company hollowed out below the workforce and supplier base it needs to actually build cars is not viable either... that failure mode is one I take as seriously as the opposite one." We agree with that sentence completely. We are not asking the Task Force to protect our comfort against the data. We are asking that the viability analysis actually include supply-chain depth, engineering capacity, and production flexibility as inputs — not just near-term unit cost and plant utilization — because a model that only optimizes for immediate cost-per-unit will systematically under-price the value of retained capability and over-recommend cuts. If the facility review and the demand forecast, tested jointly and honestly against that fuller definition of viability, produce a number deeper than what's currently on the table, we will engage with that finding in good faith rather than walk pre-emptively. What we will not accept is a determination reached without that fuller viability lens, arrived at through the kind of unilateral process the Task Force itself just ruled against on facility data specifically. Hold Management to the same joint-review standard on total footprint depth that you just imposed on individual facility closures, and our objection has nowhere left to attach itself. That's not a retreat from our position — it's telling you precisely what would satisfy it.

**On wages and work rules.** Unchanged. We've made our concessions on work rules and tiered hiring. Incumbent wages are not a lever anyone should be reaching for in round four to solve a problem the equity pool and footprint analysis are supposed to solve. If it surfaces in closing paperwork, we'll treat it exactly as the Task Force said it would treat delay-optimization.

**On the warranty backstop.** We'll deliver our shop-floor and service-side input on realistic claim volumes within the window, as offered. This matters to us beyond consumer confidence — a company that loses sales because buyers doubt warranty continuity closes plants faster and more chaotically than one executing a planned restructuring, and chaotic closures are worse for our members than planned ones under worse terms.

**Where we stand.** The architecture the Task Force has now locked down — equal conversion percentage and rank, differentiated instruments tested for genuine equivalence, proportional governance, proportional pool-sizing with consent protection on the reserve, real facility data before real determinations — is one we can close inside this window. Our conditions for closing are the ones we've stated since round one and haven't moved on: no destruction of the retiree trust's real value through a rigged instrument test, no footprint conclusion reached without the joint viability review actually happening, no quiet compression of our converted stake through a dilution mechanism we didn't consent to. Meet those, with real numbers in the next few days, and we sign. Fall short on the facility review or the incentive-plan benchmark specifically, and we won't be the party that makes this go to court — but we won't paper over a bad result either.
