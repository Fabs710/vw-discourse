**Unsecured Bondholders' Committee — Round 4 (Final Round)**

We're close to a deal we can sign. We'll say that plainly, because pretending otherwise would misstate where we are. But "close" is not "closed," and there are four items in this round's record that need to be nailed down precisely, not left to closing paperwork, before we put our name on anything.

**On the Task Force's ruling against Management's carve-outs — this is the correct call, and we want to make sure it actually sticks.**

The Task Force did exactly what we asked: it refused to let Management price its own priorities before proportionality was calculated for the parties taking the actual haircut. Provisional incentive plan at 1.5%, benchmarked independently — good. We'll review the comparables when they arrive, and we expect the benchmark set to be chosen by someone other than Management's own advisors, the same way the actuary's assumptions have to be sourced independently rather than proposed by either side of that test. If the "comparable restructurings" turn out to be three cases hand-picked because they support 3%, we'll say so.

**On the reserve and consent rights — Management's counterproposal is reasonable, and we accept it, with one addition.**

We asked for consent rights, not paralysis. A majority-inclusive threshold with a converted-claimant representative and Neighbouring Governments' representative, plus a time-bound response requirement, is a workable answer to the legitimate point Management raises — we don't want to survive this restructuring only to watch the company die in year three because it couldn't raise capital fast enough. We accept that structure. The one addition we need: the threshold and the response window need to be fixed in the closing documents now, not left as "to be worked out" — Management said "for example, a majority" and "for example, seven days." Those aren't examples anymore by the time we sign. Put numbers in the paper.

**On the Trust's question to Management — we want the same confirmation extended to us explicitly.**

Management confirmed the Trust's converted stake is included in "converted-equity holders" for consent purposes. Good — we'd have objected immediately if it weren't. We want it stated with equal explicitness that our converted stake carries the identical consent right, on the identical proportional basis, calculated once our claim size and Neighbouring Governments' final contribution are fixed. Not implied by parallel structure. Stated.

**On the instrument-equivalence test — we accept the Union's formal reciprocity, and we're stating our own version of it now, on the record.**

The Union said that if the actuary's process shows their instrument carries more expected value than warranted, they accept a downward correction. We'll match that commitment exactly: if the actuary's process, run with the symmetric scrutiny the Task Force confirmed, shows our CVR carries more expected value than the Trust's funded obligation at the same nominal percentage, we accept the correction too. We said in round three that neither side gets to be the default "reasonable" instrument, and we meant it in both directions. What we will not accept is a result where the correction only ever seems to run one way in practice because one side's assumptions get external benchmarking and the other's get waved through as "actuarially standard." Trend rate and enterprise-value forecast — both externally sourced, both disclosed, both stress-tested. We'll hold the actuary to that as hard as we hold Management to the facility-data clock.

**On facility data and footprint depth — this is the Union's and Neighbouring Governments' fight more directly than ours, but we have a stake in it, and we'll say why.**

Our recovery depends on enterprise value at emergence. A footprint decision reached badly — either too shallow to restore viability, or so deep it guts the capability the company needs to actually sell cars in three years — hits our CVR and our converted equity just as hard as it hits anyone else's stake. So we support the Task Force's ruling that this gets decided on genuinely joint-reviewed data, not a unilateral model, and we support Management's seven-day review clock as a sensible discipline against this running past the deadline. We'd only note: if the footprint number moves materially in either direction as a result of that review, the enterprise-value forecast feeding our CVR's valuation moves with it, and the actuary's equivalence test has to be run against the number that actually emerges from that process, not the placeholder used today. We're not asking to delay closing on that basis — we're flagging that the actuary's work and the facility review are linked, and the sign-off sequence needs to respect that link rather than lock in a CVR valuation against stale enterprise-value assumptions.

**On the completed allocation table — we need one more thing before we can sign, not a new demand, the same one we made last round.**

Management is right that they can't produce final percentages until the Trust's claim size and Neighbouring Governments' final contribution are fixed. We accept that sequencing — it's honest, and manufactured precision would be worse. But we want to be explicit about what "closing" requires from our side: **we sign once we see our actual net percentage after the 12% reserve and 1.5% incentive plan are subtracted, compared directly against the Trust's net percentage on the same basis.** Not the 55–60% headline. The number after carve-outs. If that comparison shows equal treatment — same conversion percentage, same rank, equivalent-value instruments, proportional governance, proportional reserve protection — we sign inside this window, and we mean that as a real commitment, not a hedge. If it shows our net recovery coming in materially below the Trust's once everything is netted, that is the exact asymmetry the Task Force has ruled out twice now, and we will contest it in this room in the time remaining — not silently accept it to avoid being the party blamed for court.

**On the warranty backstop.** Management's $1.1–1.4 billion sizing is a real number we can evaluate, not an open-ended ask — that's the right posture. Our condition from round three stands unchanged: confirm it sits last-resort behind company reserves and does not create a claim senior to our converted position. If that's confirmed in the actual term sheet, we have no objection.

**Where we stand.** Most of what's left is arithmetic, sequencing, and discipline in applying rules already agreed — not a live dispute over principle. That's real progress and we're not going to pretend otherwise for effect. Our position hasn't moved because it hasn't needed to: same percentage as the Trust, same rank, equivalent value tested symmetrically, proportional governance and proportional dilution protection, no quiet erosion through carve-outs sized by the party that benefits from them. Every one of those principles has now been ruled in our favor by the Task Force. We expect the final numbers to reflect the rulings already made. If they do, we sign before day sixty. If the completed table shows otherwise, we say so immediately, in this room, while there's still time to fix it before the alternative is a courtroom neither side fully controls.
