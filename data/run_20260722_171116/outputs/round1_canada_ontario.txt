We've been listening, and there's one thing we want to correct before this goes further: nobody at this table has yet put the domestic footprint question on the record with the specificity it needs, and we're not going to let it get resolved as a residual line item after everyone else's claims are settled.

Let's be plain about our position. We're prepared to co-fund this restructuring — real money, alongside the Task Force's — but that commitment is conditioned on two things that are not negotiable for us: binding production and employment commitments for the plants on our territory, and an equity position proportionate to what we put in. We are not a passive junior partner writing a check to backstop someone else's restructuring plan while decisions about which plants live and die get made in a room we're not fully in.

To the Task Force: we support your core architecture — deep restructuring, shared sacrifice, equity calibrated to what's given up. That's a sound framework and we're not here to weaken it. But when you say plant and dealer network cuts should go "as deep as viability requires," we need to know what that means concretely for the plants we're being asked to help fund. If viability is achieved partly by closing domestic capacity here while protected capacity survives elsewhere, that is not a restructuring we can co-finance. We are not going to subsidize the preservation of production and jobs somewhere else at the direct expense of production and jobs here. That's not a rhetorical line — it's the actual condition on which our participation depends.

To Management: we heard your framing that footprint decisions and cost-structure decisions are simultaneous unknowns that have to be worked together in the room. We agree with that logic, but it cuts in our direction too — our co-financing and our conditions need to be part of that same simultaneous negotiation, not bolted on afterward as an afterthought once the "real" stakeholders have settled terms. If plant cuts are being decided in parallel with labour and bondholder concessions, our production and employment commitments need to be decided in that same parallel process, not presented to us as a fait accompli.

To the Union: we're aligned with you more than with anyone else in this room. You want a genuine seat in deciding which plants close, with real weight given to communities and to keeping a viable manufacturing core. That is precisely our position too, translated into intergovernmental terms. We are not neutral about which plants survive — we have a legitimate, elected-government claim to protecting this production base and the workforce attached to it, and we consider that claim every bit as legitimate as the trust's claim or the bondholders' claim, even though our leverage here is money and jurisdiction rather than a strike threat or a legal filing.

To the Bondholders' Committee: we take no position on your recovery mechanics, but we'll note that your demand for proportional treatment relative to the trust is structurally the same demand we're making relative to whatever protections exist for plants outside our jurisdiction. Equal treatment among similarly situated claimants is a principle we'd ask you to extend to us as governments bearing risk on behalf of a workforce and a regional economy, not just among creditor classes.

So, concretely: we need three things before we commit financing. First, binding commitments on domestic production volumes and plant employment levels, not aspirational language. Second, an equity stake proportionate to our contribution — not a symbolic sliver, calibrated the same way the Task Force says everyone else's equity should be calibrated to their sacrifice. Third, a genuine partnership role in the ongoing restructuring decisions that affect our plants, not notification after the fact.

We understand we are a junior partner in this process and we're not trying to drive the Task Force's terms. We're flexible on the instrument — direct equity, warrants, a funded commitment structure, whatever mechanism works for the overall deal. What we are not flexible on is the substance: no funding from us without enforceable production and employment protection for our domestic footprint. Absent that, we stay on the sidelines, and this restructuring proceeds without our money and without our plants' capacity being part of the solution — which we don't think serves anyone's stated goal of a viable, surviving company.
