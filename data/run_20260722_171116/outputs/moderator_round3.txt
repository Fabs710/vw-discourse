# Moderator Summary — Close of Round 3

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Cost-basis discount rejected.** No formal or informal discounting of the Committee's conversion terms based on acquisition history. This ruling stands and all parties have accepted it as closed.
- **Differentiated instrument, equal value principle.** Same conversion percentage and claim rank for Trust and Committee; the *form* of the non-equity residual may differ (funded obligation with cost-indexed cap vs. CVR/deferred note) provided independent valuation confirms economic equivalence.
- **Governance seats tied strictly to proportional converting-claim size** — no fixed floor for either the Trust or the Committee, seat count to be calculated once relative claim sizes are stated.
- **Pool-sizing shrinkage, if required by capital headroom, applies proportionally across all converting/contributing parties** (Trust, Committee, Neighbouring Governments) — not absorbed disproportionately by one party.
- **Neighbouring Governments' new-capital equity prices separately against new capital contributed**, sits alongside rather than dilutes the conversion pool — confirmed by all parties.
- **Warranty-assurance mechanism supported in principle** by all parties, conditional on actuarial sizing, last-resort (not first-dollar) government exposure, and non-interference with restructuring terms.
- **Independent actuary engaged and scoped**; data requirements identified (plan census/demographics, historical claims experience, CVR/note term sheet, post-emergence enterprise value range).

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Burden of proof in the instrument-equivalence test.** The Committee objects to any framing where its CVR is scrutinized while the Trust's capped obligation gets default deference; both sides now want symmetric scrutiny of their respective key assumptions (healthcare trend rate vs. enterprise-value forecast).
- **Equity pool ceiling and pre-allocation carve-outs.** Management's presented $10–12B pool with 12% capital-raise reserve and 3% management incentive plan, taken off the top before proportionality is calculated, is contested by the Committee and questioned by Neighbouring Governments as untested and potentially eroding headline recovery/stake value.
- **Dilution protection over the capital-raise reserve.** Both the Committee and Neighbouring Governments want consent rights or anti-dilution protection over future issuance of the 12% reserve; this is unresolved and now a live governance question distinct from board-seat proportionality.
- **Facility data delivery and joint review.** Management's committed facility-by-facility data has not yet arrived. The Union and Neighbouring Governments both insist the closable/structural determination cannot be treated as final without genuine joint review of assumptions (utilization, product allocation, counterfactual investment).
- **Footprint depth floor.** The Union has now stated an explicit floor (no material movement beyond four brands/35%/40% dealer network) as a room-breaking condition, paralleling Neighbouring Governments' walk-away condition on plant closure.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **The actual allocation table**: Management's presentation was cut off before showing how the $10–12B pool splits across Trust conversion, Committee conversion, Neighbouring Governments' new-capital stake, the 12% reserve, and the 3% incentive plan simultaneously. Until this single table exists, no party can confirm whether its headline percentage survives netting.
- **Trade-off between capital-raise headroom and converting-claimant value certainty** — the reserve exists to solve a real year-three problem, but as structured it currently transfers dilution risk onto claimants/investors without their consent, an unresolved tension rather than a settled allocation.
- **Independent testing standard applied unevenly**: facility data (Union/Neighbouring Governments), trend-rate assumptions (Committee), and the management incentive plan (Committee/Neighbouring Governments) have all been flagged for independent verification, but no common process or timeline has been set for all three simultaneously.

## 4. Refocus for Round Four

The Task Force has set four deliverables; the discussion above sharpens what "closure" requires on each:

1. **Complete allocation table** — total pool, reserve, incentive plan, and each party's net effective percentage after carve-outs, not just a headline conversion rate.
2. **Actuary's equivalence result** — applying symmetric scrutiny to both the Trust's trend/cap assumptions and the Committee's enterprise-value/discount assumptions.
3. **Facility data delivered and jointly reviewed** (not unilaterally determined) before the closable/structural finding is treated as dispositive.
4. **Resolution of dilution/consent rights** over the capital-raise reserve — for the Committee's converted stake and Neighbouring Governments' new-capital stake alike.

Parties should come prepared to close on these four items with numbers, not restated principles. The Task Force has signaled that reopening cost-basis or footprint-depth in round four will be treated as delay-optimization.
