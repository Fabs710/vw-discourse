**Government Task Force — Round 2**

Good. Round one did what it needed to do: everyone stated their floor. Now I'll respond specifically, because vague alignment on "shared sacrifice" isn't worth anything without numbers, and I'm not going to let this drift into another round of principled positioning without commitments.

**To Management, on the deadline.** No blanket extension, and no signal that the sixty days is soft. What I will say: if, on day fifty-five, we have a substantively complete deal held up by documentation mechanics — closing conditions, final indenture language, signature logistics — I am not going to blow up a real deal over a paperwork clock. That is a narrow, technical accommodation, not a negotiating cushion. If I sense parties are pacing their concessions to test whether the deadline is real, I will let it lapse and go to court exactly as I've said. Don't confuse the one for the other, and don't build your negotiating strategy around the assumption I'll blink. I won't.

**To the Trust and the Bondholders' Committee, on equal treatment.** The Bondholders' Committee makes a point I have to take seriously: as a strict matter of legal priority, unsecured is unsecured, and I am not funding a plan that hands the Trust materially better economic terms than the Committee purely because one has a union behind it and the other has dispersed retail holders. That asymmetry, if it exists without justification, is exactly what a bankruptcy court would strike down on equitable grounds — and you're right that it invites exactly the scrutiny I'd rather avoid by going consensual. So let me be precise: governance weight and structural protections calibrated to the size and durability of each claim are fine and expected. Governance weight or inflation protection granted to the Trust as a category privilege, unconnected to the actual economics of what's converting, is not something I will approve. Same rank, same treatment, adjusted only by the actual size and terms of what each side puts into equity versus what it gives up in claims. I'd want both of you to bring me a term sheet that survives that test, not one that assumes I'll rubber-stamp differential treatment because one of you can strike and the other can't. That kind of leverage-driven inequality is precisely the sort of embedded unfairness that makes a plan un-financeable from where I sit, taxpayer-side.

That said — the Union is not wrong that the Trust represents deferred wages already earned by retirees who have no ability to negotiate or strike again; that's a different equitable posture than a bond bought at arm's length in capital markets. I'm not deciding this in the abstract. Bring me actual conversion percentages, actual governance terms, actual caps, side by side, and I'll test them against equal-rank logic myself. Stop asserting the principle and start showing me the number.

**To Neighbouring Governments.** I want your money, and proportional equity for it is fine in principle. But I will say clearly: I am not going to let your domestic-protection condition become a veto over footprint decisions sized to overall company viability. If the plan needs to close capacity somewhere to be viable, and the only way to avoid closing it in your jurisdiction is to close more elsewhere in a way that breaks the arithmetic, that is not a condition I can accommodate just because it's politically necessary for you. Your seat at the table is earned by your capital, and I welcome it — but "partnership" does not mean co-authorship of the whole restructuring; it means your funding is contingent on defensible terms for the plants you're actually paying for. Bring me your contribution number and the specific plants you want protected, and I'll test whether that's compatible with a viable footprint. If it isn't, you don't fund, and we proceed without you — the restructuring does not wait on your internal politics.

**To everyone: we are past the point where position statements earn credit.** I want, before round three, from each of you:

- Bondholders and Trust: actual conversion percentages and governance asks, not principles.
- Management: the specific plant/brand list with a cost-structure delta, not a promise of depth.
- Neighbouring Governments: a specific dollar figure and specific plants, not a conditional framework.

Absent that, I have no basis to judge whether this consensual path is real or simply everyone waiting for someone else to move first while the sixty days burns. I'd rather have this fight now, with numbers on the table, than discover on day fifty-eight that nobody actually committed to anything. The court option remains fully live, and it becomes my default the moment I conclude this room is optimizing for delay rather than resolution.
