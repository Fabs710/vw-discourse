**Company Management (New Leadership) — Round 2**

We asked for numbers from ourselves last round, so we'll lead with them. But first, three responses that bear directly on whether the numbers we're about to give are even executable.

**To the Task Force, on the deadline accommodation.** Understood and accepted — narrow, technical, documentation-only. We won't test that boundary, and we won't pace concessions hoping you blink. That's not our posture; we don't have the standing for it and we know it.

**On the equal-treatment debate between the Trust and the Bondholders.** I want to flag something operational, not legal, because both sides are arguing this as a distributive question between two claimants, and from where we sit it's also a *solvency* question about what's left over. Every percentage point of equity that goes to the Trust, to the Committee, and now potentially to Neighbouring Governments as a condition of their capital, comes out of the same finite pool. If the combined asks — Trust equity plus warrants, Committee's proportional match, Neighbouring Governments' proportional stake for their contribution — exceed what a viable post-restructuring balance sheet can support alongside room for new capital to come in later, we end up with a company that's technically de-levered but has no equity currency left to attract the investment it will need in year three or four. I'm not taking a side in the acquisition-cost argument — that's a legitimate fight for the Task Force to referee, and the Union's point about deferred compensation versus discretionary market purchase is a real distinction, not a leverage grab dressed up as principle. What I need on record is this: whatever the final split, we need the company to emerge with enough unencumbered equity headroom to raise capital post-emergence, or we've solved the balance sheet and broken the growth story. Size the total conversion pool with that constraint in view, not just the two-way fairness question.

**To Neighbouring Governments.** We take your conditions seriously and we're not going to hand you a fait accompli — but I want to be candid: the plant list I'm about to give reflects a viability-first sizing exercise, and we built it before your specific contribution number and plant list existed. We're willing to adjust which specific facilities close within a given regional allocation, based on your capital commitment and their relative cost/efficiency — but we can't hold the entire footprint decision open indefinitely waiting for your number. Bring your figure and your plant list this round or next, and we'll run the comparison against our cost model transparently. If your protected plants are cost-competitive with what we'd otherwise keep, this is easy. If they're not, your equity contribution has to cover the delta, not just buy a place at the table.

**The footprint plan, as requested.**

**Brands:** We are eliminating four of our eight brands entirely — winding them down in an orderly fashion over 12–18 months, not abruptly, specifically to avoid a disorderly signal to the market. The remaining four brands are the ones with positive contribution margin, the most defensible dealer networks, and the clearest path to distinct market positioning. This is deeper than our rejected plan, which proposed cutting two.

**Plants:** We are closing roughly 35% of assembly and powertrain capacity — a reduction from our current network down to a footprint sized to a realistic, de-risked demand forecast rather than the pre-crisis volume assumption our rejected plan still implicitly relied on. We will bring the Union and Neighbouring Governments a specific facility-by-facility list before round three, with utilization and cost data, for the joint review process the Union asked for. We are not committing to a veto for either party, for the same reason the Task Force gave: the footprint has to be sized to viability, or none of the rest of this matters. But we will commit to genuine information-sharing and consultation before the list is final, and we will factor community and manufacturing-depth considerations into *which* facilities close within a viability-consistent total, not *whether* the total is achieved.

**Dealers:** This is where I have to be most insistent, because it's the point closest to our core survival risk. We are proposing a reduction of dealer count by roughly 40%, but structured as a negotiated wind-down with terminated dealers compensated from a dedicated fund, not a unilateral termination notice. The reason is not sentiment — it's that a chaotic dealer cull, done publicly and without structure, is exactly the kind of event that triggers the consumer flight that kills a restructuring before it closes. Buyers do not purchase a car from a company they believe won't be there to honor the warranty or service the vehicle. This is the one place I will push back hard on "as deep as viability requires, full stop" — depth here has to be paired with structure, or the cut destroys more revenue than it saves in cost.

**Cost structure delta:** Combined with the Union's proposed further work-rule flexibility and expanded lower-tier hiring, plus corporate overhead reduction of roughly 30% and a flattened management structure, we project a per-vehicle cost reduction sufficient to reach operating breakeven at a materially lower volume than our current structure requires — the actual multiple depends on final labor and trust numbers, which we now have a first cut of from the Union and will model formally before round three.

**One explicit ask back to the Task Force, because it's the condition that makes all of this financeable rather than just arithmetically balanced:** we need a mechanism — funded by the bridge financing or structured as a government-backed guarantee — that assures consumers the warranty will be honored regardless of how the restructuring resolves, including if it goes through the court process. Without that, every day of public uncertainty between now and closing bleeds sales, and the viability math we're all building degrades in real time. This is not a nice-to-have; it is, in our view, as load-bearing to survival as the balance-sheet restructuring itself. Tell us if that's something you'll fund, because it changes how aggressively we can move on brands and dealers without triggering the exact collapse this whole process is meant to prevent.
