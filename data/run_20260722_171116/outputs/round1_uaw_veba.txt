We hear the Task Force clearly, and I want to be equally direct back: we didn't wait for this crisis to start conceding. Over the last several rounds of bargaining, our members have already given back wage increases, given up jobs bank protections, accepted lower-tier wage structures for new hires, and watched plants close. This isn't a workforce that's been coasting while everyone else sacrifices. When you say "shared sacrifice," understand that labour's sacrifice is already substantially banked. What's left on the table now is the retiree health trust — and that requires a different kind of care than another wage concession.

Let me be precise about what we can and cannot accept.

On the trust obligation: we are not wedded to cash-for-cash. We've done this kind of conversion before and we understand a claim against an insolvent company is worth less than a real stake in a viable one — that argument lands with us, it doesn't need repeating. What we will not accept is a haircut dressed up as a "capped instrument" that leaves retirees exposed to inflation, to funding shortfalls, or to a claim that's subordinate to everyone else's in practice. If a meaningful share converts to equity, it needs to be equity with real governance weight and real value — not a paper instrument the company can quietly starve. We will need seats or influence proportional to what we're giving up, same principle you're proposing for creditors. If the trust bears risk like an owner, it needs the protections and upside of an owner.

On wages and work rules: we've already moved a long way. Further concessions are possible if — and only if — they're matched by verifiable sacrifice from bondholders and management, not asked of us first as a good-faith gesture while creditors negotiate from a position of near-par expectations. I'll say plainly: if bondholders think they're getting anywhere close to full recovery while our retirees take a structural haircut, that is the outcome we will fight, publicly and by withholding our signature. That is not posturing. Our signature is required on this plan, and we will use that leverage.

On plant and brand cuts: we understand the market doesn't support the current footprint. We're not going to pretend otherwise — pretending got us the rejected plan. But depth of cuts and location of cuts are different questions. We want a genuine seat in deciding which plants close, with real weight given to communities and to keeping a viable manufacturing core, not a unilateral list handed down after the fact.

On ownership: agreed in principle — whoever bears sacrifice should hold equity upside calibrated to what they give up. We will hold you to that same standard for us as for bondholders.

One more thing on sequencing: we would rather do this consensually too. But "consensual" has to mean real bargaining, not us pre-committing to depth of cuts before we see the bondholders' committed sacrifice on paper. Show us their real numbers alongside ours, in the same room, at the same time.
