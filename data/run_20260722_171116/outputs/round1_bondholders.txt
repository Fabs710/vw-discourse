We've listened carefully, and now we'll be equally direct: nobody in this room should mistake our silence in round one for acquiescence to the framing that's emerged. Three parties have now spoken, and each has, in one way or another, positioned the bondholders as the stakeholder with the least sympathetic claim and the most room to give. We reject that framing, and here's why.

First, on the substance of our claim. We are not a subordinated interest, and we are not a discretionary cost item like a wage rate or a brand line that can be cut because the market changed. We hold contractual, unsecured debt — money lent in good faith, much of it by ordinary savers who bought this paper believing in the credit of an industrial icon, not speculators looking for a workout. When the Task Force says our instrument is "unsecured debt in a company that would otherwise liquidate," that's true, but it's equally true of the trust's claim. Both are unsecured. Both sit in the same priority class as a matter of law. So when I hear the Union and Management both pre-committing to the principle that bondholders must take a deeper hit "so retirees don't take a structural haircut," I want to be explicit: we will not accept materially worse treatment than a claimant of equal legal rank, dressed up as fairness because one class has a union and a seat at the table and we have dispersed retail holders with no natural voice. Equal treatment of equally ranked unsecured claims is not a negotiating opener for us — it is the floor.

Second, on the debt-for-equity conversion. We are not reflexively opposed to it. We understand the arithmetic: a claim against an insolvent company is worth less than a real stake in a viable one, and if the equity is real — with governance weight, with warrants or upside participation, with actual value calibrated to the size of what we're giving up — that can be a rational trade for us. What we will not accept is a token equity sliver that leaves us economically wiped out while other unsecured claimants convert into a materially richer instrument. If the trust gets equity with governance weight and inflation protection and priority-like treatment in practice, we expect the same proportional treatment, not a discount because we're dispersed and lack the leverage of a strike threat.

Third, on the court alternative. The Task Force has said, correctly, that it's prepared to fund an expedited court process if consensual terms fail. We take that threat seriously — it is credible, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. But everyone in this room should understand what that cuts both ways: a court-supervised process examining relative treatment of similarly ranked unsecured claims will scrutinize exactly the kind of asymmetry being proposed here — better treatment for the trust, worse for us, absent a legally sufficient justification. If the parties want to avoid that scrutiny and the delay it brings, the path is a deal that treats us proportionally, not a deal that assumes we'll fold because we're dispersed and impatient.

Fourth, on sequencing and information. We agree with the Union on one thing directly: numbers should be on the table together, not sequentially. But we want the same transparency applied to the trust's claim that's being demanded of ours. Show us the trust's proposed conversion terms — equity share, governance rights, funding cap — in the same room, same time, so we can compare like with like. We are not committing to a number in the dark while other claims are negotiated behind a curtain of "solvency facts" we haven't seen.

So our position, plainly: we're willing to move substantially off par, we're willing to take equity in significant size, and we're willing to move fast — speed and certainty matter to us, our recovery value is bleeding out every week this drags on. What moves us is more consideration, real upside participation, and demonstrable equal footing with the trust. What does not move us is being asked to subsidize a proportionally better deal for another unsecured class, or being told that our claim is more disposable because we lack a union card. That is a line we will hold, in this room or in front of a judge.
