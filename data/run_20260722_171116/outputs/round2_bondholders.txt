**Unsecured Bondholders' Committee — Round 2**

We'll give the Task Force what it asked for — numbers — but first we need to stop the acquisition-cost argument from hardening into an accepted premise, because if it survives unchallenged into round three, it becomes the basis for exactly the unequal treatment we said we would not accept.

**On the Union's "cost basis" test.** We understand the emotional force of the argument — deferred compensation, no second bargaining round, can't strike again. We do not dispute the Union's overall solidarity story about the workforce's prior sacrifices. But the specific mechanism proposed — testing each claim's *acquisition cost and timing* to determine equitable treatment among equally-ranked unsecured claims — is not a legitimate variable in this analysis, and we'd ask the Task Force to see why before it becomes the deciding test.

First, as a factual matter, it proves nothing about our committee as a whole. A very large share of this USD 27 billion is held by original purchasers — pension funds, insurance portfolios, and retail savers who bought this paper at issuance or near-par, exactly as retirees "bought" their deferred compensation by working for lower current wages. Some claims have traded hands since distress became public; that's true of essentially every large bond issue in any distressed company, including, if we're honest, positions the Trust itself may have hedged or adjusted through its own asset managers. Punishing a claim class because *some* holders bought secondary isn't a coherent equitable principle — it's an audit nobody can actually perform fairly, and if the Task Force tries, it will spend the next thirty days litigating cost-basis attribution across thousands of dispersed holders instead of closing a deal. That is precisely the delay none of us can afford.

Second, and more fundamentally: bankruptcy priority has never turned on why or when a claim was acquired. A claim purchased at a discount is still a full legal claim for its face value — that's black-letter law, and for good reason: if it weren't, no distressed debt market could function, and every creditor would face an arbitrary "how sympathetic is your ownership history" test with no stable answer. The Task Force said it would test "actual economics of what converts, not category privilege." We agree with that standard — but the actual economics of *our claim* is its face value and its legal priority, not a retrospective judgment about who currently holds the paper. If the Task Force starts adjusting recoveries based on holder sympathy rather than claim priority, that is a court finding waiting to happen, and not in the Trust's favor — it invites exactly the equitable-treatment challenge we flagged in round one.

**Our concrete conversion ask**, since numbers are now expected:

- Convert unsecured bond claims into equity plus warrants at the **same proportional rate** the Trust is requesting for its own claim — roughly 55–60% of claim value into equity and warrants, sized by our proportional share of total unsecured claims converting, using the identical formula the Union proposed for itself. We're not asking for a better rate. We're asking for the same rate, applied to the same legal rank, without a cost-basis discount.
- On the remaining 40–45%: we're open to a longer-dated instrument — a contingent value right or deferred note tied to post-emergence performance — rather than demanding cash. We are not insisting on cash-on-emergence for the residual. That is a real concession on our part toward certainty and speed.
- Governance: board representation proportional to our converted equity stake, on the same basis the Trust is requesting for itself — not a majority claim, but a real seat, not an observer role.

That is proportional parity, not favoritism toward ourselves. If the Trust's number is 55–60% and ours is materially lower for the same claim rank, that is the asymmetry we will contest — in this room first, in court if necessary.

**To Management, on the finite equity pool.** We accept your point and it cuts in favor of discipline, not in favor of shrinking our proportional share specifically. If the total pool has to be sized to leave headroom for future capital-raising, that's a sizing question applied *equally* across all converting claims — Trust, us, and Neighbouring Governments — not a justification for compressing the bondholders' allocation while the Trust's stays intact. Size the whole pool down proportionally if you must; don't solve the headroom problem by cutting one claimant's slice disproportionately.

**To Neighbouring Governments.** We'd flag a structural point: your equity stake should come from *new capital* you're contributing, priced against the post-restructuring balance sheet — not carved out of the pool being allocated to existing unsecured claims converting their pre-existing debt. Those are two different transactions: we are exchanging a claim we already hold for equity in the same enterprise; you are buying new equity with new money. If your stake dilutes the unsecured creditors' conversion pool rather than sitting alongside it as fresh capital, that is a cost being imposed on creditors to finance a subsidy for domestic plants we have no say over. We're not opposed to your participation — we're opposed to it being funded out of our recovery.

**To the Task Force, directly.** You said you'd test proposals against equal-rank logic yourself once you had real numbers. You now have ours, and the Trust's. They are close in headline structure — both roughly 55–60% into equity/warrants — which suggests convergence is achievable without a cost-basis carve-out. Test it on that basis: same claim rank, same conversion percentage, proportional governance. If the Trust's ask stays where it is and ours is treated the same, we can move quickly. If a cost-basis discount gets applied to us specifically, we will not sign, and we will make that argument in front of whatever court hears this — where, as you yourselves noted, this exact asymmetry is the kind of thing that gets struck down.

We are not asking for near-par. We said that plainly in round one and we're not moving off it. We are asking for the same haircut, the same instrument, the same governance logic as the other unsecured claimant in this room. That is the floor, and it has not moved.
