**Neighbouring Governments (Co-Funders) — Round 2**

We'll give you the number, because the Task Force asked for numbers and we said we'd deliver rather than hide behind conditional language. But first, three responses.

**To the Task Force.** We want to correct a mischaracterization before it hardens: we are not asking for a veto over the overall footprint, and we never have. What we said, and what we repeat now, is that our financing is conditioned on binding production and employment commitments for *our* plants specifically. That is not co-authorship of your restructuring. It is the ordinary logic of conditional capital — every other party in this room is attaching conditions to what they'll accept, and so are we. You are free to proceed without our money if our condition is incompatible with your viability arithmetic. We are equally free to decline to fund a plan that closes our capacity while other capacity survives. That is not us holding your process hostage; that is us declining to subsidize an outcome that harms the people we're accountable to. Don't confuse a hard condition on our own capital with an attempt to control yours.

**To the Bondholders' Committee — and this matters, because you raised a structural point we actually agree with.** You're right that our equity stake should come from new capital we contribute, priced against the post-restructuring balance sheet, not carved out of the pool being allocated to existing unsecured claims converting pre-existing debt. We are not trying to dilute the Trust's or your recovery, and we're not asking to be treated as a converting claimant — we hold no claim against this company today. We are buying in with fresh money, and our equity should be priced and sized on that basis, sitting alongside the conversion pool rather than competing with it. We'll take that framing on the record as agreed and expect Management and the Task Force to size our stake accordingly.

**To Management, on the plant list and the "cost-competitiveness" test.** We understand you built the initial 35% capacity reduction before you had our number, and we're not asking you to unwind that exercise. But we want to be precise about what "cover the delta" means, because there's a version of that test we can work with and a version we cannot.

If a plant on our territory is marginally less cost-competitive than one elsewhere, and the gap is closeable through investment — retooling, line-speed upgrades, product allocation — we are prepared to have our capital contribution fund that gap directly, as capital investment in the plant rather than as a subsidy for keeping an uncompetitive facility open indefinitely. That is a legitimate use of our money and we'll commit to it. What we will not accept is a framing where "cover the delta" means we simply pay a premium in perpetuity to keep open a facility you'd otherwise close for good structural reasons, while a comparable facility elsewhere gets the investment and the product mandate. That is not partnership; that is paying to lose slowly instead of quickly, and it fails our own solvency logic as surely as it would fail yours.

**Concrete terms, since numbers are now expected of everyone:**

- **Contribution:** we are prepared to commit **$4.1 billion** in co-financing, structured as new capital into the restructured entity — not a claim conversion, consistent with the Bondholders' point above.
- **Production/employment condition:** this financing is contingent on retaining our two largest assembly plants and our powertrain facility at combined utilization no lower than 80% of pre-crisis volume, and on maintaining domestic production at a floor of our current share of total North American output, plus or minus normal cyclical variation — not an exact unit count, but a defensible floor, reviewed jointly on a rolling basis, not renegotiated unilaterally mid-restructuring.
- **Equity:** proportionate to our contribution relative to total new capital raised in the restructuring — we're not demanding a specific percentage in isolation; we're asking that the same calibration principle the Task Force has applied to every other party's sacrifice-to-equity ratio apply to our new capital as well. Price it fairly against the post-restructuring cap table and we'll accept the math.
- **Governance:** a board seat or formal observer-plus-consent right specifically over decisions affecting the plants our financing supports — closure, major investment reallocation, product mandate changes. Not a general governance seat over the whole company; a targeted right tied to what our capital is protecting.
- **Investment commitment:** we'll fund up to $600 million of the $4.1 billion specifically as retooling/competitiveness investment in our facilities, if Management's cost data shows a closeable gap rather than a structural one.

**Where our floor is, plainly, so nobody discovers it on day fifty-eight:** if the facility-by-facility data Management brings to round three shows our flagship plants are structurally uncompetitive — not closeable by investment, but fundamentally unviable regardless of capital — we will not pay to keep them open as a jobs program, because that isn't sustainable for us or for the company. But if the data shows a closeable gap and Management or the Task Force nonetheless proceeds to close our capacity while preserving comparably-costed capacity elsewhere, that is the point at which we withdraw our financing entirely. We are not bluffing about that, and we won't be moved off it by an appeal to overall viability math that hasn't actually been tested against our investment offer.

We'll have the facility-specific list matched to Management's data before round three, as requested.
