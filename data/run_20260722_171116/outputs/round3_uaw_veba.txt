**Industrial Union & Retiree Health Trust — Round 3**

We'll take the Task Force's ruling in the spirit it was given — decisive, not evasive — and respond point by point, because pretending we got everything we asked for would be dishonest, and conceding more than we should would be a mistake we can't undo.

**On the cost-basis ruling.** We hear it, and we're not going to relitigate it — the Task Force said the argument is closed, and we accept that it's closed as a *legal* matter. But we want to be precise about what we're accepting and what we're not. We are not accepting that the Trust's claim is economically identical to a bond claim — we're accepting that the *mechanism* we proposed (discounting recovery based on acquisition history) isn't administrable and isn't the vehicle for recognizing that difference. The Task Force's alternative — same conversion percentage, same rank, but a *different instrument* for the residual, calibrated to the actual nature of the claim and tested for economic equivalence — is one we can work with. That was, in substance, what we proposed in round two: a trusteed funded obligation with a cost-indexed cap, not a discount on the equity percentage. So we're not being asked to give up something we asked for; the Task Force has confirmed the structure we already put forward. Good. We'll hold the Committee to the same standard we're held to: if their CVR/deferred note is shown by independent valuation to carry *more* expected value than our funded obligation at the same nominal percentage, that inequality runs the other way, and we expect the Task Force to correct it with the same rigor it would apply against us.

**[SUMMON: Independent actuary/valuation expert]** — to test the economic equivalence of the Trust's proposed funded obligation (with cost-indexed cap) against the Committee's CVR/deferred note, at the proposed 55–60% conversion rate, before round four. We want this instructed jointly by the Task Force, not commissioned unilaterally by Management or the Committee, because the assumptions built into healthcare cost trend projections are exactly the kind of thing that can be quietly gamed to make a cap look generous on paper while eroding real value over twenty years. We'll accept the result if the process is genuinely independent. We will contest it if it isn't.

**On governance seats.** We understand the logic — proportional to converted equity stake, not a fixed floor. We're comfortable with that math *if it's actually calculated on the full converting pool*, including our understanding that the Trust's claim, while smaller in absolute dollars than the Committee's $27 billion, converts into a materially significant share of total unsecured claims given the size of the retiree population involved. We're not going to guess at the ratio in this room — bring the actual claim-size comparison next round, and we'll take our seats as they fall out of that math, not as a demand for two regardless of size. What we will insist on, regardless of seat count: whatever board presence we get includes real information and consent rights over any future adjustment to the funding cap or escalator mechanism — that's not a governance-privilege ask, it's the minimum needed for a trustee to actually do its job protecting retirees rather than finding out about a cap change after the fact.

**On pool sizing.** Agreed, and we'll say plainly: this is the correct answer. If the total pool has to shrink to preserve post-emergence capital headroom, it shrinks proportionally across the Trust, the Committee, and whatever new-capital equity Neighbouring Governments receive — not disproportionately onto the claimant with the least ability to fight it in a courtroom. We take some satisfaction that this is where the Task Force landed, because it is the test of whether "shared sacrifice" actually means something or is just a phrase everyone uses until the pool gets sized and somebody's slice quietly comes out smaller. We'll hold everyone, including ourselves, to that discipline.

**On the footprint and Neighbouring Governments' plants.** We want to be direct with the Task Force here, because there's a version of "let the data decide" we support and a version we don't. We support the closable-gap-versus-structural-gap test on objective cost data — that's the right process. What we do not accept is that process being run entirely by Management's internal model without the joint labor-management review we asked for in round two actually happening before the determination is treated as final. Management committed to sharing facility-by-facility data with us before this round; we haven't seen it yet as of this statement. We want it on record: if that data arrives and the determination is made that a plant is "structural" without our side having had a genuine opportunity to test the assumptions — capacity utilization assumptions, product allocation choices, the counterfactual investment case — we will contest the finding, not the principle. A number produced unilaterally and called "objective" is not the same as one tested jointly, and we're not going to rubber-stamp Management's model just because it arrives with confident language.

To Neighbouring Governments directly: we are aligned with you more than with a pure spreadsheet-viability calculus, and we said so last round — but we also recognize your floor is legitimate. If your plants are shown, through a fair joint process, to be structurally unviable, we won't ask you to bankroll a jobs program indefinitely, because that both fails your solvency logic and, more importantly to us, doesn't actually save the jobs — it just delays the closure by a few years under worse terms. What we want is the same thing you want: a fair test, not a predetermined conclusion dressed up as one.

**On the total footprint depth.** We're not going to pretend four brands, 35% capacity, and 40% dealer reduction is comfortable — it isn't, and it will cost real jobs in real communities. We accept that some version of this depth is necessary; we said as much in round one. But we want a floor stated plainly, the same way Neighbouring Governments stated theirs: **we will not sign a plan that takes total North American production capacity below a level consistent with maintaining the core skilled workforce and supplier base needed to actually build the next generation of vehicles this company needs to compete.** If the 35% number, once tested against actual demand forecasts and Neighbouring Governments' investment offer, turns into 45% or 50% through further rounds of "just a bit deeper," we're out of the consensual room. That is not a bluff, and it's not a renegotiation of numbers already on the table — it's a statement of where shared sacrifice becomes liquidation-by-installments, which we said in round one we would not accept.

**On wages and work rules.** No change from what we said last round: further work-rule flexibility and expanded lower-tier hiring, yes. Further cuts to incumbent hourly wages, no — and we'd note that number was never where the real solvency math lives. If any party in round four tries to reopen incumbent wages as a lever to close a gap that should be closed through the equity pool or the footprint decisions instead, we'll treat that the same way the Task Force says it will treat delay-optimization: as bad faith, not negotiation.

**On the warranty backstop.** We support Management's ask, and we support the Task Force's conditional willingness to fund it. A collapse in consumer confidence doesn't just hurt shareholders — it accelerates plant closures and job losses beyond what the footprint plan already contemplates, because a company that can't sell cars closes capacity faster and less carefully than one executing a planned restructuring. We'd ask that the actuarial sizing exercise include input from our side on realistic warranty claim volumes given the vehicle mix post-restructuring — we have shop-floor and service-side knowledge that shouldn't be modeled without us.

**Where we stand heading into round four:** the cost-basis fight is closed, and we're not reopening it. The instrument-equivalence test, the proportional governance math, and the proportional pool-sizing discipline are all things we can work with, provided they're applied with the rigor the Task Force has promised. What we will not accept, and want stated as clearly as Neighbouring Governments stated their walk-away point: a footprint depth that goes materially beyond what's already on the table, or a valuation exercise on the Trust's residual instrument that isn't genuinely independent. Those are the two places this deal breaks for us. Everything else, we're prepared to close.
