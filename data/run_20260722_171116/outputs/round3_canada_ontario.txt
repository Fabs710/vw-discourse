**Neighbouring Governments (Co-Funders) — Round 3**

We'll be direct about what's been settled in our favor, what's still open, and where we saw something in this round's record that concerns us enough to flag now rather than let slide into round four.

**On the Task Force's ruling regarding our new-capital framing.** Confirmed and appreciated — our equity prices against new capital, sits alongside the conversion pool, doesn't dilute it. That's now settled three ways: us, the Committee, and the Task Force all agree on the structural principle. Good. We take that as locked.

**On the Task Force's warning that "we'll walk" cannot be leverage to relitigate overall footprint size.** We want to correct this characterization once more, plainly, because it keeps getting restated as something broader than what we actually said. We have never asked to relitigate the total capacity cut required for viability. We asked for a fair, jointly-tested determination of whether *our specific plants* fall on the closable-gap or structural-gap side of that line, with our $600 million retooling offer actually run against the data before a conclusion is reached. If the test shows our plants are structural, we accept closure — we said that explicitly last round, and we repeat it now so there is no ambiguity. What we will not accept is a predetermined conclusion dressed up as an objective test, or a test that never actually happens because the data doesn't arrive. That is not us holding your process hostage. That is us insisting the process you yourself said you'd run — Task Force-supported, jointly reviewed — actually occurs before the number is treated as final.

Which brings us to a real problem: **Management committed to delivering facility-by-facility data before this round. It has not arrived.** The Union flagged this too, from their own angle, and we align with them on it. We are not accusing Management of bad faith — we understand the pool-sizing exercise consumed this round's bandwidth — but we want it on record: the closable-gap-versus-structural-gap test cannot be "decided," as the Task Force put it, on data nobody outside Management has yet seen. If that data arrives in round four stamped as final without the joint review actually happening, we will contest the finding on process grounds, not reopen the viability principle. Those are different objections and we want the distinction preserved.

**On the equity pool ceiling Management presented.** This is where we need to slow down, because Management's number — $10–12 billion post-emergence equity, with 12% reserved for future capital and 3% for a management incentive plan, both off the top — directly affects how our $4.1 billion prices against the cap table, and Management's presentation stopped before reaching an actual allocation table. We need that completed before we can confirm our number is real rather than illustrative.

We also want to associate ourselves with a point the Committee just made, because it applies to us as much as to them: if 15% of the equity pool is reserved before proportionality is calculated for *any* contributing or converting party — us included — our equity stake needs the same protection against future dilution that the Committee is asking for on its converted claim. We are putting in $4.1 billion of new capital priced against a stated pool size. If 12% of that pool gets issued in year three at a price we had no consent rights over, the stake we're buying today is worth less than what we're pricing it against now. We want either: (a) our governance right — the targeted board seat/consent right over decisions affecting our plants — extended to include consent over timing and pricing of that capital-raise reserve as it affects our stake's dilution, or (b) confirmation that the reserve is sized and structured so our proportional stake is preserved through any future issuance, not just at closing. We are not asking for a general anti-dilution veto over the whole company's capital structure — that would go beyond what our contribution justifies. We are asking that the same discipline the Task Force applied to pool-sizing across converting claimants apply to us as a new-capital contributor as well: proportional treatment, tested transparently, not diluted quietly by a reserve nobody sized against our stake specifically.

**On the management incentive plan carve-out.** We take no strong position on whether 3% is right or wrong in isolation — that's closer to the Committee's fight than ours, since it comes out of the same pool their conversion draws from, not primarily ours. But we'll note for the record that we support the Committee's request that it be tested independently against comparable restructurings rather than accepted as "standard," for the same reason we'd want our own $600 million retooling commitment tested against real cost data rather than accepted on our own assertion. Consistency in what gets independently verified versus what gets waved through matters to whether this whole process holds together.

**On governance.** No change from our round-two position: a targeted seat or consent right over decisions affecting the plants our capital protects — closure, investment reallocation, product mandate changes — plus now, as noted above, consent over dilution mechanics affecting our stake specifically. We are not asking for a general governance seat over the whole company, and we won't push for one; that was never our ask and isn't now.

**On the warranty backstop.** We have no objection to the mechanism and no direct stake in its structure, beyond a general interest in the company actually selling cars in our jurisdictions during the restructuring window — plant employment depends on that as much as balance-sheet math. We'd simply note, aligned with the Committee, that if it's structured as a government-backed guarantee, it should be sized to an actuarial estimate and not create a senior claim that complicates the cap table our equity is pricing against.

**Where our floor stands, restated plainly for round four.** If the facility-specific data arrives, is genuinely tested against our retooling offer through the joint process Management committed to, and shows our plants structurally unviable — we accept closure and we do not consider that a broken promise; we said so and we mean it. If the data shows a closable gap and our capital and the joint review are set aside in favor of a predetermined closure list, we withdraw our $4.1 billion, in full, immediately. That has not changed since round two and will not change based on how much time has elapsed in this process. We expect the facility data — actually delivered, actually reviewed jointly — to be the first item resolved in round four, ahead of finalizing our equity percentage, because our number depends on knowing which plants we're actually protecting.
