**Government Task Force — Round 4 (Final Round)**

We are inside the last week of the window. This round has to produce closure or produce clarity that closure isn't coming — I'm not going to let a fifth round happen that doesn't exist. I'll rule on every open item, because that's what my mandate here requires, and then state plainly what happens next depending on what actually gets delivered.

**On the allocation table and Management's carve-outs — I am not accepting this as presented.**

The Committee and Neighbouring Governments are both right, and I'm ruling in their favor on this one. Management came into this round with a $10–12 billion pool estimate and immediately reserved 15% of it — 12% capital headroom, 3% incentive plan — before a single converting claimant's proportional share was calculated. That is not proportional treatment; that is Management pricing its own priorities first and asking creditors to divide what's left. I said last round that pool-sizing constraints apply proportionally across every converting or contributing party. Management is not exempt from that discipline just because it drafted the slide.

Here is my ruling:

- **The 3% management incentive plan is not approved at that size or on that basis.** I will not fund or endorse an incentive plan sized by the party that benefits from it and defended as "standard." Bring me a comparison against three to five comparable restructurings of similar scale, prepared or reviewed independently — I will treat this the same way I treated the cost-basis question: no assertion, no approval. Until that benchmark exists, treat the incentive plan as provisionally sized at no more than 1.5%, subject to upward revision only if the benchmark supports it.
- **The 12% capital-raise reserve is directionally right but currently unprotected, and that has to be fixed before I'll sign off on it.** A company that emerges with zero equity currency repeats this crisis — I'm not reversing that logic. But the Committee and Neighbouring Governments are correct that "authorized but unissued" is not free; it's deferred dilution risk sitting over claims being priced today. So: **the reserve is approved at up to 12%, but only with consent rights attached** — any issuance from that reserve within three years of emergence requires approval by a governance mechanism that includes the converted-equity holders and Neighbouring Governments' board representation, not unilateral management or ordinary board majority action alone. That protects the pricing integrity of what's being agreed today without eliminating the headroom Management needs. If Management believes it needs unrestricted discretion over that reserve, it hasn't explained why, and I'm not granting it on assertion.

**On the instrument-equivalence test — symmetric scrutiny, as both the Committee and the actuary have now said. I confirm it.** The Trust does not get default deference because its instrument sounds more conservative, and the Committee's CVR does not get treated with suspicion because it's simpler. Same rigor, same transparency of assumptions, both sides' key sensitivities — trend rate, enterprise-value forecast — tested against externally sourced benchmarks, not either party's preferred number. I'm not ruling on the number itself; I'm confirming the process the actuary described is the one I expect, applied even-handedly. If either side tries to get a friendlier assumption in through the back door in round four's closing paperwork, I'll catch it in the sign-off review.

**On governance seats — proportional math, as ruled. Bring me the ratio, not another round of restating the principle.** I note the Union's clarification that they're not asking for a floor regardless of size, and the Committee's acknowledgment that this concession has to hold both directions. Good. Calculate it and close it.

**On facility data — this is the one place I am genuinely concerned, and I'm ruling against Management here too.**

Management committed to delivering facility-by-facility data before round three closed. It did not arrive. Both the Union and Neighbouring Governments flagged this, and they're right to. I am not going to let a footprint determination — the single largest driver of job losses and plant closures in this entire restructuring — be decided on data that arrived unilaterally, late, and untested. That is exactly the kind of process shortcut that gets a plan challenged and unwound later, which costs more time and money than doing it right now.

So: **Management delivers full facility-by-facility data within five days of this round closing.** The Union and Neighbouring Governments get a genuine joint review window — not a courtesy comment period — to test utilization assumptions, product allocation choices, and the counterfactual investment case, specifically for the plants Neighbouring Governments' $4.1 billion is meant to protect. If that review happens and the data shows a plant is structural, it closes — Neighbouring Governments have said they accept that outcome, and I will hold them to it, but only because the test was real. If Management fails to deliver usable data in that window, I will not treat any footprint conclusion Management asserts unilaterally as final, and I reserve the right to commission an independent facility assessment on the government's own dime and timeline — which will cost us time we don't have, and which I would rather not do.

**On the Union's stated floor.** I hear it, and I want to be direct: I am not accepting a pre-committed ceiling on footprint depth as a matter of principle. The depth of the cuts is set by what the viability math requires once the facility data and demand forecasts are actually tested — not by what any single stakeholder is prepared to tolerate stated in advance as a room-breaking line. If the tested data shows 35% is sufficient, it stays at 35%. If it shows more is required for the company to be viable, I will not artificially cap it at 35% to keep the Union in the room, any more than I'll deepen it further than the data requires to satisfy anyone else's comfort. The number is an output of the analysis, not an input negotiated at the table. I'd note, for what it's worth, that I have no interest in gratuitous over-cutting — a company hollowed out below the workforce and supplier base it needs to actually build cars is not viable either, and that failure mode is one I take as seriously as the opposite one. But that's a conclusion the data has to support, not a floor I'm pre-committing to protect a party's walk-away threat.

**On the warranty backstop.** Bring the actuarial sizing, including the Union's shop-floor input on realistic claim volumes, before this window closes. Structured as last-resort behind reserves, capped, non-senior to the estate — as I said last round. If it arrives sized properly, it's in the package. If it doesn't arrive sized, it's not.

**Where this stands.** Most of the architecture is now genuinely settled — conversion percentages, ranking, the differentiated-instrument principle, proportional governance, proportional pool-sizing discipline, the new-capital framing for Neighbouring Governments. That is real progress and I want to acknowledge it plainly. What remains is not a disagreement about principles; it's whether the numbers get delivered, tested, and closed inside the days left.

I will not extend the sixty days for substance. If, by the deadline, I have: a completed allocation table with the incentive plan benchmarked and the reserve properly consent-protected; the actuary's equivalence result; genuinely jointly-reviewed facility data with a determination on Neighbouring Governments' plants; and actuarial sizing on the warranty mechanism — I will fund the consensual plan and consider this room to have done its job. If any one of those four fails to materialize with real numbers rather than more position statements, I will file for the court-supervised process on day sixty, on the terms already on the table as the baseline, and let the court impose what this room could not finish. That is not a threat aimed at any single party — it's the honest statement of what "no open-ended subsidy of an unrestructured company" has meant from day one.
