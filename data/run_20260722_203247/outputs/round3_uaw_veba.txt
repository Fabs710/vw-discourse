We are prepared to move this forward, but let me be equally clear: **we will not sign onto a plan that preserves bondholder expectations while turning workers and retirees into the shock absorber for everyone else’s losses.**

## Where we agree
- The company must emerge **smaller and viable**.
- The legacy structure is broken; **the retiree health obligation cannot remain as an open-ended operating burden**.
- **Real labour concessions** are on the table.
- **Meaningful creditor haircuts** are unavoidable.
- Time is short, and a deal that closes within the window matters.

## Where we disagree
### 1) The retiree trust is not a disposable nuisance
To the Task Force and management: yes, the obligation must be restructured. But your language keeps drifting toward “bounded” in a way that sounds like “made insignificant.”

That is not acceptable.

The trust must receive a **real substitute value**, not a symbolic claim. That means a structure with enforceable economic substance:
- preferred equity with real protections, and/or
- a limited secured or quasi-secured instrument,
- plus contingent upside if the company performs.

What we will **not** accept is a haircut that leaves retirees holding the bag while bondholders preserve a better residual position.

### 2) Workers cannot be asked to do the deepest cutting
We are willing to discuss:
- wage moderation,
- work-rule flexibility,
- targeted productivity changes,
- and capacity rationalization.

But we will not accept:
- wholesale plant destruction,
- job cuts that go well beyond what is needed for viability,
- or a restructuring that treats labour as the easiest constituency to sacrifice.

The footprint must be **smaller**, yes. It must also be **defensible industrially and socially**.

### 3) Bondholders cannot claim parity with the trust and workforce
To the bondholders: your claims are contractual, but so are ours. You do not get to insist on a “real recovery” while opposing a durable trust instrument that protects retirees.

You will take a haircut. That is not in dispute. What is in dispute is whether your package is meaningful enough to reflect shared sacrifice. If the trust gets converted into a durable economic claim, then bondholders must accept a real residual claim, but **not one that comes at the expense of workers and retirees being crushed**.

### 4) Domestic plants are not bargaining chips to be casually thrown away
To the neighbouring governments: we understand why you want binding commitments. So do we. Communities, suppliers, and workers are not abstractions.

But we will not support a plan that uses “viability” as a blanket excuse to strip out productive domestic capacity while preserving softer options elsewhere. If plants are closing, the case must be made on real economics, not convenience.

If co-funding is offered, it should help preserve a **viable domestic core**, not subsidize an industrial hollowing-out.

## What a workable package must include
If you want our consent, the structure needs to reflect these principles:

1. **A materially smaller but still industrially coherent footprint**
   - not cosmetic cuts, but not indiscriminate closures either.

2. **Real labour concessions**
   - wages, flexibility, and work rules adjusted in good faith.

3. **A durable retiree trust replacement**
   - preferred equity or an enforceable secured/quasi-secured claim,
   - with protections against dilution and value erosion.

4. **Meaningful unsecured creditor losses**
   - but not a structure that protects creditors while wiping out labour and retirees.

5. **Domestic production commitments**
   - where operations remain viable, those plants should be protected as part of the settlement.

6. **Equity/governance that reflects sacrifice**
   - if public funders and creditors receive upside, the workforce/trust cannot be pushed to the back of the line.

## On the Task Force’s proposed framework
We are willing to work within a bracketed term sheet, but not one written as if labour and retirees are merely another cost to be minimized.

### On the retiree obligation
A **hybrid package** is the only credible path:
- a **material reduction** in the legacy claim,
- plus **preferred equity and/or a limited secured note**,
- plus **contingent value rights** tied to recovery performance.

That is the minimum structure that gives the trust something durable without pretending the old promise can survive intact.

### On bondholders
A real recovery is possible, but it must be **subordinate to the viability of the workforce and the trust**. We will not agree to a package where bondholders receive enough value to call it a fair compromise while the trust gets a weak placeholder and workers absorb disproportionate job losses.

### On plants and employment
We want actual numbers, but the operating footprint must be assessed by:
- plant productivity,
- product mix,
- supplier integration,
- and regional industrial impact.

If a site is viable, it should be preserved where possible. If it is not, then we need real mitigation — not just closure notices.
