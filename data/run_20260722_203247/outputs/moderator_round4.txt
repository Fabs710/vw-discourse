## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether the company must shrink; all parties now accept that it must. The impasse is over **how the economic sacrifice is allocated within the unsecured layer and how much protection the retiree trust receives without making the deal unfinanceable**.

More specifically, the sticking points are:

1. **Retiree trust protection vs. bondholder recovery**
   - The union/trust wants a durable instrument with real value and protections.
   - Bondholders argue that any such protection cannot put them in a worse substantive position.
   - The Task Force and management say the trust must be bounded enough not to recreate insolvency.

2. **Operational footprint vs. political commitments**
   - The specialist and management want plant-by-plant economic pruning.
   - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production commitments and a meaningful domestic core.
   - The Task Force insists economics must drive the footprint, not politics.

3. **Missing term-sheet-level numbers**
   - Everyone is speaking in principles.
   - No party has yet put forward a single integrated package with plant decisions, labour savings, trust haircut, bondholder recovery, and public/co-funder capital in one bracketed draft.

## Facilitator proposals for bridging positions

### Proposal 1: Split the trust package into a bounded base plus performance upside
A possible bridge is to structure the retiree trust claim as:
- a **material haircut** to the legacy obligation,
- a **bounded base instrument** such as preferred equity or a subordinated/quasi-secured note with a hard cap,
- plus **contingent upside** through CVRs or a small equity kicker.

This would aim to satisfy:
- the union/trust’s request for real economic value,
- the Task Force and management’s need for financeability,
- and bondholders’ concern that the trust should not become a de facto senior claim.

### Proposal 2: Give bondholders a defined recovery floor in exchange for accepting trust value
A possible bridge is to pair any protected trust instrument with a **defined bondholder recovery package** that includes:
- equity,
- warrants,
- and perhaps a modest note component,

so the unsecured class does not appear to be the sole residual absorber of value.

This would aim to address:
- bondholders’ parity concerns,
- while preserving room for the trust to receive something more durable than a token claim.

### Proposal 3: Tie domestic commitments to a verified reduced footprint
A possible bridge is to allow neighbouring governments to obtain:
- **binding domestic production commitments**
- and a **proportionate equity/governance stake**,

but only for the subset of domestic plants that survive a plant-by-plant economics test.

This would aim to reconcile:
- the governments’ demand for domestic protection,
- the Task Force’s insistence on viability,
- and management’s need to close structurally uneconomic sites.
