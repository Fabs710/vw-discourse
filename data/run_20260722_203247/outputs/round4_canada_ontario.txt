We agree on the central need for a **smaller, viable company** and on the fact that the old burden structure cannot survive intact. We also agree that the court-supervised route must remain available if the parties cannot close quickly.

But I want to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that preserves foreign production while treating domestic plants as the default casualty.** If we are co-funding this rescue, domestic production and employment must be protected in binding form.

## Our position on the main issues

### 1) On viability and footprint
We accept the specialist’s basic point: the company needs fewer plants, higher utilization, a simpler platform/brand structure, and a tighter dealer base. That is necessary.

What we do **not** accept is a footprint decision made primarily for political convenience. If a plant is retained, it should be because it is economically viable in the post-restructuring business. If a plant is closed, the case must be made plant by plant.

That said, **economic necessity is not the same as regional sacrifice being spread evenly for appearances**. We need to see that domestic sites are not being singled out simply because they are easier to cut.

### 2) On the retiree trust vs. bondholders
We are not taking sides in the bondholder-trust dispute as a matter of creditor doctrine. Our concern is different: the capital structure must fit a viable operating footprint, and it must not be financed by hollowing out the domestic industrial base we are helping defend.

So:
- We accept that the retiree obligation must be converted into a bounded instrument.
- We accept that bondholders will take meaningful haircuts.
- We do **not** accept a package that forces domestic plant closures so that other constituencies can preserve a better recovery.

If the trust gets a durable replacement, fine — but that structure cannot be funded by stripping out domestic capacity that is still viable.

### 3) On public funding and ownership
This point is non-negotiable for us: **no funding without commitments protecting the domestic production footprint**.

If we are putting public money into the rescue, we need:
- binding domestic production commitments,
- employment protections tied to retained domestic operations,
- and a **proportionate equity and governance stake** reflecting our contribution and risk.

We are not asking for a veto over every operating decision. We are asking not to be treated as a passive cheque writer while others control the upside and the decisions.

### 4) On labour
We support an orderly restructuring that preserves as many jobs as possible consistent with viability. We understand labour must make concessions, but those concessions should not be used to justify a plan that exports domestic production and leaves our jurisdictions carrying the burden.

### 5) On process
We are prepared to keep working toward a consensual solution, but only if the next draft includes:
- a plant-by-plant retained/close rationale,
- identified domestic sites to be preserved,
- the production commitments attached to them,
- and the equity/governance treatment for co-funders.

If that does not materialize, we would rather move to the court-supervised path than fund a deal that undermines our core interests.

## What would move us
A workable package for us would need all of the following:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - not vague aspirations.

2. **Protected domestic employment where operations continue**
   - not a guarantee of every job, but real protection for the sites we are financing.

3. **A proportionate ownership position**
   - real equity, with governance rights aligned to our contribution.

4. **A footprint that is smaller but still includes a meaningful domestic core**
   - not a hollowed-out company with our plants sacrificed first.

5. **A financeable restructuring overall**
   - we are not asking for permanence; we are asking for an orderly, durable settlement.

## Where we disagree with the others

- To the Task Force: viability cannot be defined in a way that ignores domestic industrial policy and the jobs we are financing.
- To management: “economics” cannot become a blanket justification for cutting domestic capacity while preserving other options elsewhere.
- To labour: we share your interest in keeping domestic operations alive, but we need a deal that is financeable and durable.
- To bondholders: our funding is not there to subsidize a creditor outcome that leaves domestic plants as collateral damage.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund this rescue, but only if it protects the domestic production footprint in a binding way and gives us a proportionate stake for the capital and political risk we are taking.

If the next term sheet does not reflect that, then it is not a package we can support.
