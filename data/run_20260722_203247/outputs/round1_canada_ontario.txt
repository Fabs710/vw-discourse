We are prepared to co-fund this restructuring, but only if it protects a real domestic production footprint and gives the co-funders a commensurate seat in the outcome.

### Where we agree
- **The company needs a real restructuring.** We do not support preserving an unsustainable cost structure.
- **Labour, creditors, and the public sector all need to share pain.** That is unavoidable.
- **Speed matters.** The sixty-day window is real, and delay is not a strategy.
- **A court-supervised route may be necessary** if a consensual deal cannot be reached quickly.

### Where we differ
#### 1) Plant cuts cannot be decided purely on balance-sheet logic
We understand the need for a smaller footprint, but we will not support a plan where domestic plants are closed while foreign capacity is protected. We have jurisdictional responsibility for domestic jobs and regional stability. If our governments are asked to co-fund this rescue, then the domestic production base must be preserved in a meaningful way.

So let me be direct:  
**No funding without commitments protecting domestic production and employment.**  
That is not a side issue; it is the condition for our participation.

#### 2) We need a proportionate ownership position
If we are contributing public capital, and if our plants and workforce are part of what is being preserved, then we expect an ownership position that reflects that contribution. We are not asking for control for its own sake, but we are not prepared to subsidize a restructuring and be left without a meaningful equity stake.

If the company, the lead funder, and the creditors are sharing the burden, then the co-funders should not be treated as passive cash providers.

#### 3) “Viability” must include industrial viability in our jurisdictions
The task force is right that the company cannot be rebuilt on an unrestructured balance sheet. But viability also means a durable industrial base in the affected domestic regions. A restructuring that protects creditors’ recoveries while hollowing out our manufacturing footprint would be unacceptable.

#### 4) We will not support a deal that asks for public money without binding safeguards
We need:
- binding production commitments for domestic plants,
- employment protection to the extent operations justify it,
- a proportionate equity position,
- and clear governance rights attached to our contribution.

### On the other stakeholders
- **To management:** We support hard restructuring, but not at the expense of our domestic footprint. “Viability” cannot be a euphemism for shifting production away from our jurisdictions.
- **To labour:** We are aligned on protecting jobs and preserving industrial communities. We support durable employment commitments, though we expect flexibility where necessary.
- **To bondholders:** We do not accept the idea that your recovery concerns override the real economy. Your claims matter, but they do not entitle you to a deal that strips our plants and workers to protect paper value.

### Our bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund an orderly restructuring, but only on these terms:
1. **Binding protection for domestic plants and employment**
2. **A proportionate equity stake reflecting our contribution**
3. **A viable operating footprint, not a relocation of pain onto our jurisdictions**
4. **A process that delivers finality quickly**

If those conditions are not met, then we should not be expected to write a cheque.
