We appreciate the progress in the discussion, but I need to be very direct: **we are still not at a financeable package**. We agree on the destination — a materially smaller, viable company — but not yet on the structure that gets us there without recreating insolvency.

## Where we agree

### On viability and footprint
We agree with the Task Force and the specialist that the company cannot survive in its current shape. It must be:
- smaller,
- simpler,
- higher-utilization,
- and less burdened by legacy cost.

That means:
- plant rationalization,
- brand simplification,
- dealer network reduction,
- and meaningful labour flexibility.

We are not defending the old structure.

### On the retiree obligation
We also agree the retiree health burden cannot remain an open-ended operating liability. It must be converted into a bounded, durable instrument.

### On creditor sacrifice
We agree unsecured bondholders must take meaningful haircuts. The company cannot emerge with a pre-crisis capital structure.

### On process
We accept that if consensus cannot be reached quickly, the court-supervised route may be necessary.

## Where we disagree

### 1) We cannot accept a trust package that is so rich it breaks the company
To the union and retiree trust: we recognize the obligation is real. But a replacement instrument that is too senior, too cash-draining, or too protected will simply recreate the same insolvency under another label.

That is not a settlement. It is a delayed collapse.

So we are open to:
- a material haircut,
- preferred equity,
- a limited subordinated or quasi-secured instrument,
- and contingent upside.

But we are **not** open to a structure that effectively gives the trust priority so high that it forces deeper cuts to the operating business or makes the deal unfinanceable.

### 2) Bondholders cannot demand protection from the necessary reset
To the bondholders: your claims are contractual, and we respect that. But this is an insolvent company, not a balance-sheet polish-up. There will be deep losses.

What we cannot accept is a demand that the trust be stripped back to preserve unsecured recoveries at a higher level. If the trust receives durable value, then bondholders will also receive a real recovery — likely equity and warrants — but not a recovery that preserves old expectations.

That said, I am also clear that **bondholders are not a dumping ground** for every other constituency’s losses. The package has to be internally consistent, not punitive for its own sake.

### 3) On domestic plants, viability must come first
To the neighbouring governments: we understand the importance of domestic production and employment. We are prepared to work with binding commitments where they fit the smaller viable company.

But we cannot preserve capacity that the market will not support. If a plant is retained, it must earn its place on economics and future volume. If it does not, we will not keep it open just because the politics are difficult.

We will preserve the strongest plants and the strongest brands consistent with viability. We will not preserve all domestic sites at any cost.

### 4) We need plant-by-plant numbers, not generalities
To the specialist and to all parties: your framework is useful, but we still need a **term-sheet-level operating plan**.

We need:
- retain / close / consolidate decisions by plant,
- utilization assumptions,
- fixed cost per unit,
- retooling needs,
- labour savings,
- dealer reductions,
- and product/brand rationalization.

Without that, the capital structure discussion is floating without an anchor.

## What management can support

We can support a package with these elements:

1. **A materially smaller operating footprint**
   - fewer plants,
   - fewer brands,
   - fewer dealers,
   - and higher utilization at the retained sites.

2. **Real labour concessions**
   - wage moderation,
   - work-rule flexibility,
   - staffing changes,
   - and workforce reductions where required.

3. **A bounded retiree trust replacement**
   - preferably a hybrid of:
     - preferred equity,
     - a limited subordinated or quasi-secured component,
     - and contingent value rights.
   - But with a hard cap on cash burden and no open-ended drain.

4. **Meaningful bondholder haircuts**
   - with equity/warrant upside, not nominal protection.

5. **Bridge financing with finality**
   - not a perpetual rescue.

6. **Equity/governance that reflects sacrifice**
   - but no constituency should receive control rights that block restructuring.

## What we cannot support

We cannot support:
- a retiree instrument that is effectively senior enough to crowd out the rest of the structure,
- a bondholder package that is left too thin to be credible,
- or domestic commitments that freeze excess capacity in place.

We also cannot support a deal that simply shifts insolvency from the balance sheet into the future.
