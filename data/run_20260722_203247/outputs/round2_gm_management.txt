We appreciate the specialist input, and we agree with the core structural point: the retiree health obligation cannot simply remain an open-ended operating liability if the company is to survive. A conversion is necessary.

But I need to be equally direct about where management stands.

## Where we agree
- **The company needs a smaller, viable footprint.**
  We are not defending the current cost base.
- **Labour concessions are required.**
  Wages, work rules, and flexibility all have to move.
- **Bondholders must take real losses.**
  There is no credible rescue on a near-unchanged balance sheet.
- **The retiree trust needs real, durable value.**
  We are open to a conversion into equity, a secured-like instrument, or a hybrid.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The trust cannot be structured in a way that recreates insolvency
The specialist is right that “durable” cannot mean “symbolic.” But it also cannot mean “so senior that it crowds out the rest of the capital structure.”

If the trust is given too much priority, we simply recreate the same distress in a different form. That would be a bad deal for everyone, including retirees, because it would threaten the company’s survival.

So yes to a durable substitute. No to an instrument that makes the restructured company unfinanceable.

### 2) Equity alone is probably too weak, but full secured priority is too strong
The most workable path is likely a **hybrid**:
- a **material haircut** to the legacy health obligation,
- plus **preferred equity or a limited secured/quasi-secured note**,
- plus possibly **contingent upside rights** if performance improves.

That is consistent with the specialist’s advice and with viability. What we cannot accept is either extreme:
- no real replacement value for the trust, or
- a priority package that effectively sits ahead of the business’s recovery.

### 3) Bondholders cannot demand “comparable upside” if the trust gets protected value
I want to respond directly to the bondholder committee. Your claims are legitimate, but they do not entitle you to parity with a restructured retiree obligation simply because you are dispersed and politically difficult.

If the trust receives a real instrument, bondholders will take a corresponding haircut. That is not unfair; it is how a capital reset works. What is not acceptable is a structure where bondholders preserve too much while labour and retirees carry the main burden.

### 4) Domestic footprint protection has to be tied to operational reality
To the neighbouring governments: we hear the importance of your plants and jobs. But we cannot lock in capacity that the market cannot support simply because it is politically sensitive.

We can support domestic production commitments where the economics justify them. We cannot support a plan that substitutes regional protectionism for viability. That would jeopardize the whole rescue.

## What management believes is necessary

A credible package should include:

1. **Substantial but targeted plant, brand, and dealer reductions**
   - enough to restore competitiveness,
   - not so broad that the company destroys the strongest parts of its franchise.

2. **Meaningful labour concessions**
   - wage, work-rule, and flexibility changes.

3. **A reduced and converted retiree trust obligation**
   - preferably a hybrid of preferred equity and limited senior or quasi-senior paper,
   - with contingent upside, but no open-ended cash drain.

4. **Material unsecured creditor haircuts**
   - enough to produce a viable balance sheet.

5. **Bridge financing with a clear exit**
   - no open-ended public support.

6. **Ownership allocated by sacrifice**
   - no constituency should emerge as if nothing happened.

## Process
We are not attached to consensual form over substance. If consensus can deliver the necessary cuts fast, good. If not, the court-supervised route is an acceptable fallback. The only unacceptable outcome is delay that burns through the last financing and leaves us with no business left to reorganize.

## Bottom line
Management will support a deal that:
- preserves the company as a going concern,
- protects the strongest viable brands and distribution network,
- converts the retiree obligation into a durable but affordable instrument,
- imposes real creditor losses,
- and gets us to finality inside the deadline.

We will not support:
- a trust structure that makes the company unfinanceable,
- creditor recoveries that leave the balance sheet unrepaired,
- or domestic plant commitments that override viability.

If the next round wants to move toward a concrete package, we need actual numbers on:
- the haircut to the retiree obligation,
- the form and size of the replacement instrument,
- the creditor recovery range,
- and the plant/brand footprint that survives.
