We are prepared to keep co-funding an orderly restructuring, but only if the package actually protects the domestic production base we are being asked to help finance.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a **smaller, viable footprint**.
- There must be **real sacrifice from labour and creditors**.
- The old balance sheet cannot be preserved intact.
- A **court-supervised route** is acceptable if it is the only way to reach finality on time.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) Domestic plants cannot be the easy cuts
To the Task Force and management: we do **not** accept a plan where domestic plants are closed while other capacity is protected simply because those closures are politically easier.

If our governments are contributing capital, then **domestic production and employment must be part of the restructuring’s protected core**. We are not asking to freeze every plant forever. We are saying that closures must be justified by real operating necessity, not by shifting pain onto our jurisdictions.

That is not a side condition; it is the basis for our participation.

#### 2) We need a proportionate ownership position
If public money from our side is helping fund the rescue, then we need an equity position that reflects that contribution. We are not prepared to be treated as a passive cheque-writing constituency while others receive governance and upside.

So, to be explicit: **co-funding without a proportionate ownership stake is not acceptable**.

#### 3) The restructuring must be orderly, not simply imposed at speed
We understand the deadline. But speed cannot become a cover for an outcome that strips domestic industrial capacity and leaves us holding the cost with no durable say.

If the consensual path cannot produce:
- binding production commitments,
- employment protection where operations justify it,
- and a proportional equity/governance role for co-funders,

then we will have serious reservations about supporting the package, whether consensual or court-supervised.

### On the labour/creditor dispute
We are not taking sides in the fight over relative recovery between the trust and bondholders. What matters to us is that:
- the company survives,
- the footprint remains viable,
- and domestic production is not sacrificed to clean up the capital structure.

But we are clear on one point: **no funding without commitments protecting domestic production and employment**.

### Our bottom line
We can support an orderly rescue if it includes:
1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
2. **Employment protection where operations justify it**
3. **A proportionate equity stake for co-funders**
4. **A viable operating footprint that does not hollow out our jurisdictions**
5. **Finality within the deadline**

If those conditions are not met, then we should not be expected to fund the restructuring.

If helpful, we can next set out what we would regard as a minimum acceptable domestic footprint and the governance rights attached to our contribution.
