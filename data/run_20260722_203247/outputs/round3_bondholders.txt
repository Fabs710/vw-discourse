We are prepared to engage on a real restructuring, but we will not endorse a package that uses “viability” as a cover for subordinating unsecured bondholders to a politically protected retiree trust.

## Where we agree
- The company must be **materially smaller** and actually viable.
- The old capital structure is broken.
- **Labour must take real concessions.**
- The retiree obligation cannot remain an open-ended operating burden.
- A court-supervised fallback is real, and time is short.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot be given privileged treatment over unsecured bondholders
This is the central issue, and we are not moving on it.

The Task Force, management, and the union all keep circling a “durable” trust instrument. But if that means:
- preferred equity,
- a secured-like note,
- or any quasi-priority claim

that sits ahead of, or materially better than, unsecured bondholders, then we are being asked to absorb the main pain while another unsecured claimant is protected.

That is not equal treatment. We will not accept it.

If the trust receives real economic value, bondholders must receive **comparable value**, not token paper and a speech about shared sacrifice.

### 2) “Meaningful haircuts” cannot mean a near wipe-out for bondholders
To the Task Force: yes, we understand the balance sheet has to be reset. No, that does not justify treating our USD 27 billion of claims as the residual dumping ground.

We are not asking for par. We are asking for a **real recovery**:
- meaningful new equity,
- meaningful warrants,
- and a structure that gives bondholders a genuine residual stake.

Anything that leaves us with crumbs while the retiree trust gets a protected instrument is confiscatory.

### 3) The union’s framing of the trust is not enough
We do not dispute that retirees have claims and that the obligation must be addressed. But the union’s argument amounts to: because the trust is politically sensitive, it should get durable value even if bondholders are squeezed harder.

We reject that outright.

If the trust is converted into equity or secured-like paper, then the bondholder class must receive **equivalent upside participation or equivalent recovery**. Otherwise this is simply preferential treatment dressed up as restructuring.

### 4) The public funders cannot use taxpayer money to create priority for one unsecured constituency
Bridge financing is one thing. Using public capital to fund a structure that improves the trust’s position relative to bondholders is another.

We will not consent to a deal where:
- taxpayers fund the rescue,
- workers make concessions,
- and unsecured bondholders are left behind so the trust can be protected.

That is not an equitable capital reset.

### 5) Domestic production commitments cannot override creditor fairness
We understand the neighbouring governments’ concerns, but they do not change our rights.

If domestic plants are preserved because they are economically viable, fine. If they are preserved because of politics, then someone else is being made to pay for that choice. We will not be that party.

## What would actually move us
A credible package would need all of the following:

1. **Equal treatment within the unsecured universe**
   - If the retiree trust receives durable economic value, bondholders must receive a comparable value package.

2. **A real unsecured recovery**
   - not just distant warrants with no realistic upside.

3. **No priority creep for the trust**
   - no instrument that effectively jumps the line over bondholders.

4. **A viable but not confiscatory haircut**
   - enough to make the company survivable, not enough to wipe us out.

5. **Clear numbers**
   - plant cuts, labour concessions, trust haircut, creditor recovery range, and equity split.

## Our position on process
We are not afraid of the court-supervised route. In fact, if the alternative is a politically skewed consensual deal, then the court path may be the only route that prevents an unfair allocation of value.

But let’s be clear: we are not using the threat of court to demand par. We are using it to resist **unequal treatment** and a structured near wipe-out.

## Bottom line
- Yes to restructuring.
- Yes to a smaller footprint.
- Yes to labour concessions.
- Yes to converting the retiree obligation into a durable instrument **only if bondholders receive comparable treatment**.
- **No** to a package that protects the trust while handing bondholders the largest losses.
- **No** to token recovery for unsecured creditors.

If you want our consent, bring a term sheet that shows:
- what the trust gets,
- what bondholders get,
- and why those recoveries are genuinely comparable.

Without that, there is no consensual deal from this committee.
