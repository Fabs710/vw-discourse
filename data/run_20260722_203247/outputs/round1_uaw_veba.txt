We agree on the central point: the company has to survive as a viable manufacturer, not as a museum of the old cost structure. We are not here to defend every historical arrangement unchanged.

But let me be equally clear about where we will not go.

### What we agree on
- **There must be real restructuring.** Plants, brands, and dealer capacity can be rationalized if that is truly necessary for survival.
- **There must be shared sacrifice.** Workers and retirees are not the only parties who must take losses.
- **The public purse cannot be the permanent backstop.** We understand the bridge-financing logic and the need for a credible path to viability.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) The retiree health obligation is not a throwaway liability
The retiree health trust is not just another unsecured claim to be shaved for convenience. It is a negotiated earned obligation tied to decades of work and sacrifice. We will not accept a plan that simply destroys it or leaves retirees holding an empty promise while other parties preserve value for themselves.

If the obligation must be restructured, then it must be **converted into something durable and real** — not written off into oblivion. That means:
- a meaningful equity or security position for the trust,
- a claim on the restructured company with enforceable value,
- and a structure that gives retirees actual protection, not symbolic treatment.

#### 2) Workers cannot be asked to carry disproportionate pain
We have already accepted concessions in earlier rounds. We are prepared to discuss:
- wage adjustments,
- work-rule changes,
- and operational flexibility.

But we will not accept a plan where labour and retirees absorb the deepest losses while unsecured creditors are treated more generously or while equity control is arranged to protect finance capital.

#### 3) Plant and job cuts must be targeted, not a wholesale destruction of industrial communities
We understand the need for a smaller footprint, but we will oppose a restructuring that sacrifices whole regions and tens of thousands of jobs simply to make balance-sheet math easier for creditors. Cuts must be justified by actual operating necessity, not by a desire to maximize recoveries elsewhere.

### Our bottom line
If the Task Force wants our consent, the restructuring must include:
- **real but limited labour concessions,**
- **a durable instrument for the retiree trust — preferably equity or equivalent secured value,**
- **shared losses by unsecured creditors,**
- **and preservation of as many jobs and plants as the business can genuinely support.**

What we will **not** support:
- liquidation by another name,
- destroying the retiree trust,
- or any arrangement that puts creditors ahead of the workforce and retirees as if our claims were secondary moral obligations.

We are prepared to negotiate hard and move responsibly. But if the proposal is “workers and retirees give most, creditors get protected, and the public bridges the gap,” then we will say no.
