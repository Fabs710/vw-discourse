### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company must emerge as a **viable, smaller business**, not a preservation of the current cost structure.
- There is broad agreement that **real restructuring is unavoidable**, including reductions in plants/brands/dealer capacity.
- All sides accept that **labour, creditors, and public funders must share sacrifice**.
- There is general acceptance that the **sixty-day timetable is real** and that a **court-supervised process** may be needed if consensus fails.
- There is shared recognition that **the retiree health obligation must be addressed in a durable way**, not simply ignored.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health trust treatment:**  
  - Union wants durable protection/value, possibly equity or a secured-like instrument.  
  - Bondholders reject giving the trust preferential treatment over unsecured creditors.  
  - Management and the Task Force say the current obligation cannot remain intact if it breaks viability.
- **Creditor recoveries:**  
  - Bondholders want meaningful recovery and comparable treatment, not a near wipeout.  
  - Task Force and management insist on significant haircuts and no taxpayer-funded makewhole.
- **Labour and plant reductions:**  
  - Task Force and management want substantial cuts tied to viability.  
  - Union wants limited, targeted cuts to avoid disproportionate job losses.  
  - Neighbouring governments want domestic plants and employment protected.
- **Public funding conditions and control:**  
  - Task Force wants taxpayer protection and no open-ended support.  
  - Neighbouring governments want binding production commitments, jobs protection, and an equity stake.  
  - Governance/ownership allocation remains unresolved.
- **Priority between stakeholders:**  
  - Bondholders object to being treated worse than other unsecured claims.  
  - Union objects to any structure that protects creditors while workers/retirees absorb most losses.  
  - Co-funders want assurance that domestic industrial interests are not subordinated.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much of the retiree obligation is converted, reduced, or secured**, and what value replaces it.
- **What level of creditor haircut is acceptable** while still preserving enough recovery to avoid resistance.
- **How much workforce and capacity reduction is truly necessary** versus what is politically or regionally undesirable.
- **Whether domestic production commitments can be reconciled with a smaller, viable footprint**.
- **How ownership and governance are divided** among the public funders, co-funders, creditors, and possibly the trust.
- **What concessions each party makes in exchange for the others’ sacrifices**, especially on control, upside participation, and employment protections.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to package terms:
- specify a **credible operating footprint**,
- define **numerical ranges for labour and creditor concessions**,
- outline **options for the retiree trust instrument**,
- and test **ownership/governance splits** against funding commitments and domestic production guarantees.

The immediate task is to identify a package that is both **financially viable** and **politically acceptable enough to close within the sixty-day window**.
