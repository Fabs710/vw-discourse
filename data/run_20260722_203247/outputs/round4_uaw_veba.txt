We are still prepared to work toward a rescue, but I need to say plainly: **we will not sign a restructuring that makes workers and retirees the default source of value while bondholders and funders preserve a better position.** If that is the direction, there will be no consensual labour peace from us.

## Where we agree

### On viability
We agree the company must emerge **smaller and viable**. We are not defending the old footprint for its own sake. We accept:
- plant and brand rationalization,
- dealer reductions,
- labour concessions,
- and a durable conversion of the retiree obligation.

That said, “smaller” is not a blank cheque for indiscriminate cuts.

### On the retiree obligation
We also agree the legacy retiree burden cannot remain an open-ended cash drain. It has to be converted into something bounded and durable.

But durable means **real economic value**, not a hollow placeholder.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The trust cannot be treated as a junior nuisance
To the Task Force and management: you keep saying the replacement must be “bounded and financeable,” and we agree with both words. But your use of those words keeps drifting toward a structure that strips the trust down until it is barely worth anything.

That is not acceptable.

If the company is asking retirees to sacrifice, then the replacement must have:
- **enforceable value**,
- **protection against being washed out**,
- and **some real participation in upside** if the turnaround succeeds.

A pure unsecured substitute is too weak. A token claim is not a settlement.

### 2) Bondholders cannot be insulated while the trust is cut deeper
To the bondholders: you do not get to demand “comparable treatment” while rejecting the reality that the trust is a negotiated labour-retiree obligation tied to the company’s future operating stability.

We accept that bondholders will take meaningful haircuts. Good. But your insistence that any meaningful trust instrument is somehow unfair to you is not persuasive unless you are also prepared to accept a genuinely deep loss and a real residual stake.

What we will not accept is a deal where:
- the trust gets pushed into a weak instrument,
- workers absorb the largest job losses,
- and bondholders still receive enough to call the deal balanced.

That is not shared sacrifice.

### 3) Plant cuts must be economically justified, not politically convenient
To management, the Task Force, and the co-funders: we hear the call for a plant-by-plant footprint rationalization. Fine. Let’s do it properly.

But the standard cannot be “close the most politically expendable sites first” or “protect whatever is easiest to defend in a boardroom.” We need real economics:
- utilization potential,
- product relevance,
- logistics,
- retooling needs,
- and workforce productivity.

If a plant is competitive, keep it. If not, show us the numbers. We will not support shutting viable plants while preserving weaker ones for political comfort.

### 4) Domestic production commitments are not optional
To the neighbouring governments: if you are putting money in, we understand why you want binding commitments. We are not opposed to that in principle.

But those commitments must protect a **real domestic industrial core**, not just a headline. If your funding is used to strip out the domestic base while preserving capacity elsewhere, then we will oppose the settlement. Communities, suppliers, and workers are not collateral damage.

## What a workable package must include

If you want our consent, the next draft has to reflect these principles:

### A. A durable retiree trust package
The trust should receive a **hybrid instrument** with real economic substance:
- **preferred equity** with anti-dilution protection,
- and/or a **limited secured or quasi-secured note**,
- plus **contingent value rights** tied to performance.

This is the minimum structure that gives retirees something durable without recreating an open-ended liability.

### B. A shared-loss framework that is actually shared
- Labour will make real concessions.
- Retirees will accept a material haircut.
- Bondholders will take meaningful losses.
- Management and the capital structure must also absorb sacrifice.

We will not accept a package where only workers and retirees are expected to carry the load.

### C. A footprint that is smaller but still industrially coherent
We want a plan that keeps the **strongest plants and the strongest jobs**. That means:
- retaining high-productivity, flexible, strategically located sites,
- closing genuinely weak or duplicative plants,
- and aligning the dealer and supplier network to the lower volume base.

### D. Binding domestic commitments where operations remain viable
If a domestic plant is kept, then the agreement should include:
- production commitments,
- employment protections tied to volume,
- and monitoring so the commitments are real.

### E. Governance/equity that reflects sacrifice
If public funders and creditors receive equity, the workforce/trust cannot be treated as an afterthought. The equity split has to reflect who is taking the real pain and who is keeping the company alive.

## On the Task Force’s proposed direction
We will cooperate on a **term sheet with actual numbers**. But we will not cooperate with a structure that starts from “how little can workers and retirees be made to accept.”

That approach is exactly how you get a deal that is technically financeable and socially explosive.

If the next draft includes:
- a meaningful trust instrument,
- real creditor haircuts,
- a defensible footprint,
- and binding domestic commitments where feasible,

then we can keep moving.

If it does not, then say so honestly and let the court-supervised route be tested. We will not endorse a deal that preserves creditor optics at the expense of the trust and the workforce.

## My bottom line
- **Yes** to restructuring.
- **Yes** to a smaller company.
- **Yes** to real labour and retiree concessions.
- **Yes** to a durable trust replacement with actual value.
- **No** to a package that leaves bondholders too protected while workers and retirees absorb the deepest cuts.
- **No** to closures driven mainly by politics rather than economics.

If you want our signature, bring a term sheet that shows shared sacrifice in fact, not just in language.
