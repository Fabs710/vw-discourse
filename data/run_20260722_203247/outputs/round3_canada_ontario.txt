We are prepared to keep co-funding an orderly rescue, but I need to say plainly that **our support is conditional on protecting the domestic production base and jobs we are financing**. We are not a blank cheque, and we are not interested in funding a restructuring that leaves domestic plants as the default casualty while other constituencies are protected.

## Where we agree
- The company must emerge **smaller and viable**.
- The legacy burden must be reduced; the old structure cannot survive intact.
- Labour and creditors both need to share sacrifice.
- A **durable replacement** for the retiree obligation is necessary.
- Finality within the deadline matters, and the court route is a real fallback.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants cannot be treated as the easiest cuts
To management and the Task Force: we do **not** accept a plan where domestic plants are closed simply because the political cost is lower than closing elsewhere.

If we are contributing public money, then **domestic production and employment must be protected in a binding way**. That does not mean preserving every site. It does mean that closures must be justified by real operating necessity, not by convenience.

If the package protects foreign production while hollowing out our jurisdictions, then we cannot support it.

### 2) Co-funding without a proportionate equity stake is not acceptable
We are not prepared to fund this rescue as a passive bystander. If we are contributing capital and taking political risk to protect domestic industry, then we need an **ownership position proportionate to our contribution**.

That means:
- real equity,
- real governance rights tied to that equity,
- and no structure in which we pay but have no commensurate say.

We are not asking for a veto over every operational decision, but we are not willing to be treated as an afterthought.

### 3) Domestic employment commitments must be binding
To be explicit: **no funding without commitments protecting domestic production and employment**.

That means:
- retained domestic plants identified in the plan,
- clear production allocation commitments,
- employment protections where those plants remain viable,
- and a monitoring/enforcement mechanism.

A vague promise that the company will “value” domestic jobs is not enough. We need contractual commitments.

### 4) “Viability” cannot be used to exclude our interests
We understand that the footprint has to shrink. But viability is not a magic word that overrides every other legitimate interest. A viable company that is built by stripping out domestic capacity and social stability is not an acceptable outcome for us.

If the business case requires some domestic closures, then we need to see the economic logic. But if the plan simply pushes the burden onto our regions while preserving other constituencies, we will object.

## On the trust and creditor dispute
We are not taking sides in the fight over whether the retiree trust or bondholders should receive the better recovery. That is not our primary issue.

What matters to us is that the company survives with:
- a footprint that preserves domestic industrial capacity,
- a financing structure that is workable,
- and an ownership split that reflects who is actually funding the rescue.

But I will say this: if a durable trust instrument is part of the package, it cannot be financed by gutting the domestic footprint that we are helping defend. The capital structure and the operating footprint have to fit together.

## What we require to support the deal
At minimum, the next draft must include:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - identified plants or production volumes to remain in our jurisdictions where economically viable.

2. **Employment protection linked to those commitments**
   - not a guarantee of every job, but real protection where operations continue.

3. **A proportionate equity and governance stake**
   - reflecting our co-funding contribution.

4. **A smaller but viable operating footprint**
   - with closures justified by economics, not politics.

5. **Finality within the deadline**
   - if consensus cannot produce these commitments, we would rather move to the court-supervised route than fund a plan that undermines our core objectives.

## To the Task Force
We hear your insistence on viability and taxpayer protection. We share those goals. But viability must include the domestic industrial base that the public will see as the reason this rescue is worth doing. If the outcome is a smaller company with no meaningful domestic footprint, then we will have funded a policy failure.

## To management
If you want our money and our support, bring us:
- a plant-by-plant plan,
- the domestic sites to be retained,
- the production commitments attached to them,
- and the equity structure for co-funders.

Without that, we are not prepared to sign off.

## To labour
We understand your concern about jobs, and on this point our interests overlap. We are prepared to support a viable package that preserves domestic employment where the operations justify it. But the deal has to be financeable and durable.

## Bottom line
We are open to co-funding the rescue, but only if:
- domestic production and employment are protected in binding form,
- our contribution earns a proportionate ownership stake,
- and the plan is an orderly, successful restructuring rather than a political offload.

If those conditions are not met, then we should not be expected to fund it.
