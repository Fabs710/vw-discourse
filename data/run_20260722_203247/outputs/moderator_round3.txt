### 1) What has been agreed
- The company must emerge **smaller and viable**.
- The current legacy burden structure cannot remain intact.
- **Plant/brand/dealer rationalization** is necessary.
- **Labour concessions** will be required.
- The **retiree health obligation must be restructured** into a bounded replacement instrument rather than left as an open-ended cash drain.
- **Bondholders will take meaningful haircuts**.
- **Public funding**, if used, is to be bridge financing and not an open-ended subsidy.
- A **court-supervised fallback** remains available if consensus cannot be reached quickly.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment vs. bondholders**
  - The union/trust wants a durable, enforceable replacement with real economic value.
  - Bondholders oppose any structure that gives the trust priority-like treatment over unsecured debt.
  - Task Force and management are open to a hybrid trust instrument, but only if it is financeable and bounded.
- **Depth and allocation of sacrifice**
  - Labour says workers/retirees should not bear the deepest cuts.
  - Bondholders reject being the main residual loss bucket and want comparable treatment if the trust receives value.
  - Funders want losses shared, but with enough reduction to restore viability.
- **Operating footprint / plant closures**
  - The restructuring specialist says the company likely needs a much smaller, higher-utilization network.
  - Labour and neighbouring governments want closures limited to what is strictly economically necessary.
  - Governments want domestic plants protected where possible and strongly tied to any funding.
- **Domestic production / co-funding terms**
  - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production and employment commitments plus proportionate equity/governance.
  - Task Force and management will not support commitments that preserve excess capacity or create veto rights over restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much haircut** the retiree trust takes versus what exact replacement value it receives.
- Whether the trust’s replacement is best structured as **preferred equity, subordinated/quasi-secured paper, CVRs, or a mix**.
- The **bondholder recovery range** and whether it is comparable in substance to the trust package.
- Which **specific plants/brands/dealers** are retained or closed, and on what economic basis.
- The **size of workforce reductions** and the scope of wage/work-rule concessions.
- The exact **public co-funding amount**, form, and governance rights.
- How to balance **domestic industrial protection** with the requirement for a smaller, financeable company.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single bracketed term sheet** with actual numbers.
- Require a **plant-by-plant retained/close rationale** tied to utilization, fixed cost, and future product volume.
- Specify:
  - trust haircut and replacement instrument,
  - bondholder recovery range,
  - labour savings target,
  - co-funding amount and form,
  - equity split and governance,
  - and any binding domestic production commitments.
- Keep the discussion focused on **financeability, finality, and viability**, with the court route as the fallback if a package cannot be made coherent.
