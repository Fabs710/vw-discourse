We appreciate the specialist’s clarification, and we agree with one core point: the retiree health obligation cannot remain an open-ended burden if the company is to survive. But let me be equally clear: that does **not** justify loading the bill onto unsecured bondholders while the trust is given protected value and the public money props up the rest.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **materially smaller, viable footprint**.
- There must be **real labour concessions**.
- The old capital structure is broken and must be reset.
- A **conversion** of the retiree obligation is more credible than pretending it survives intact.
- Speed matters.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “Durable” cannot mean “privileged”
The union and management both talk about a durable instrument for the retiree trust. Fine. But if that instrument is more senior, more protected, or more certain than the recovery given to unsecured bondholders, then we are simply being subordinated to a politically favoured unsecured claimant.

We will not accept:
- the retiree trust receiving preferred equity, secured-like paper, or any quasi-priority claim,
- while unsecured bondholders are pushed toward a near wipe-out,
- unless we receive **comparable upside participation or equivalent value**.

Equal standing claims should not be turned into a hierarchy because one constituency is more politically sensitive.

### 2) “Meaningful creditor haircuts” is not a license for confiscation
To the Task Force and management: we understand there must be haircuts. We are not asking for par. But there is a line between restructuring and expropriation.

Our claims are legitimate contractual claims. Many holders are ordinary savers, not hedge funds. We will not accept a deal that leaves us with:
- token paper,
- negligible equity/warrants,
- and a story that “everyone sacrificed” when in reality bondholders absorbed most of the pain.

If the trust gets a real economic instrument, then unsecured creditors need a **real residual stake** too.

### 3) The trust cannot be protected “because retirees”
We do not deny the history behind the retiree obligation. But our claims are not morally inferior because we are bondholders. The suggestion that the trust can be insulated while unsecured creditors are asked to bear the deepest losses is exactly the kind of unfairness we are resisting.

If the trust must take a haircut, so be it. If it receives equity or a secured-style instrument, bondholders must receive a **meaningful equity/warrant package** and not just a small cash recovery.

### 4) Public money should not be used to recreate priority
To the Task Force and neighbouring governments: bridge financing is one thing; engineering a structure that effectively preserves one unsecured constituency at the expense of another is another. We will not consent to a plan where public capital plus labour concessions are used to enhance the trust’s recovery while bondholders are diluted.

## What we require
If you want our voluntary participation, the package needs to include:

1. **A real unsecured creditor recovery**
   - not symbolic crumbs.
   - We need a meaningful combination of new equity, warrants, or other value.

2. **Equal treatment within the unsecured universe**
   - if the retiree trust gets a durable instrument, bondholders must receive comparable upside or equivalent value.

3. **A credible but not confiscatory haircut**
   - enough to make the company viable, not enough to wipe out contractual creditors.

4. **A smaller operating footprint**
   - but cuts must be driven by economics, not by a desire to preserve one claimant at our expense.

5. **No open-ended public subsidy**
   - bridge financing only.

6. **Finality**
   - if the consensual proposal is not fair, we will not volunteer to be sacrificed just to avoid the court process.

## On process
We are not frightened by the court-supervised route. If anything, the threat of an imposed process cuts both ways. It is not a reason for us to accept a deal that gives away our recovery for the sake of speed.

We remain open to a restructuring, but not a confiscatory one. If the next proposal treats the retiree trust as a protected instrument and bondholders as the residual dumping ground, then there will be no consensual deal from us.

## Bottom line
- Yes to restructuring.
- Yes to labour concessions and operational downsizing.
- Yes to converting the retiree claim into a durable instrument.
- **No** to giving the trust better treatment than unsecured bondholders without matching upside for us.
- **No** to a near wipe-out dressed up as shared sacrifice.

Bring numbers, not slogans.
