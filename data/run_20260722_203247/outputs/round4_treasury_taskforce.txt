We are not going to let this drift into a politically comfortable but financially incoherent package.

The discussion has clarified the real issue: **the company can be saved only if the legacy burden is cut deeply enough to fit a much smaller, simpler operating business**. That remains our standard. We will not finance a structure that merely shuffles claims around while leaving the enterprise unviable.

## Where we agree

### To management
You are right that the company cannot survive in its current form. The specialist’s operating analysis is also directionally correct: fewer plants, fewer brands/platforms, a tighter dealer network, and much higher utilization at the plants that remain. That is the only route to a business that can generate cash after the rescue.

But let’s be blunt: we still do not have a **term-sheet-level plan**. We need actual numbers, not broad statements about “smaller” and “viable.” If you cannot put a concrete footprint and capital structure on the table, we are prepared to move to the court-supervised path.

### To the union / retiree trust
You are right on one point: the retiree obligation is real and cannot simply be erased. A durable replacement is necessary.

But we do **not** accept the idea that durability means a quasi-priority claim that crowds out everyone else. If the trust’s replacement instrument is too protected, it will just recreate insolvency and force deeper cuts elsewhere. That is not protecting retirees; that is delaying failure.

### To the bondholders
You are right that unsecured creditors should not be treated as the residual dumping ground for every other constituency’s demands. If the trust receives real economic value, bondholders cannot be left with token recovery.

But you are **not** entitled to a near-full recovery. This company is insolvent in substance. A meaningful haircut is unavoidable, and we will not underwrite your pre-crisis expectations with public money.

### To the neighbouring governments
We understand the political and economic importance of domestic production and jobs in your jurisdictions. If you are contributing capital, you deserve a proportionate ownership stake.

But that does **not** give you a veto over the operating reset. We will not preserve excess capacity simply to satisfy regional politics. Any domestic commitments must fit the smaller viable company, not the other way around.

## Where we disagree, clearly

### 1) On the retiree trust
A “durable” trust replacement is acceptable only if it is **bounded, financeable, and subordinate enough to preserve viability**.

That means we are open to:
- a material haircut to the legacy obligation,
- preferred equity,
- a limited subordinated or quasi-secured note,
- and contingent upside.

We are **not** open to:
- a senior or near-senior claim that weakens the company’s recovery,
- open-ended cash obligations,
- or a structure that makes bondholders worse off than is necessary to restore the business.

### 2) On creditor parity
Bondholders keep saying “comparable treatment.” Comparable in what sense?

The answer is: **comparable in being part of the sacrifice, not identical in instrument**. The trust and the bondholders are not economically identical claims, and we will not let that become a pretext for preserving too much of the unsecured stack.

If the trust gets preferred equity and contingent value, bondholders may also get equity and warrants. That does **not** mean everyone gets the same economics. It means everyone takes pain, and the company survives.

### 3) On the operating footprint
The specialist has made the key point: survival requires concentration in the strongest plants. We agree.

What we do **not** accept is a plan that starts from politics and then pretends it is economics. We need a plant-by-plant rationale:
- utilization potential,
- fixed cost per unit,
- retooling capital,
- logistics,
- and product fit.

If a site cannot support the post-restructuring business, it should close. If it can, then it should be retained. Sentiment does not enter the calculation.

### 4) On domestic commitments
Binding domestic production commitments are possible only where they are compatible with a viable footprint.

We will not finance:
- commitments to preserve every domestic plant,
- control rights that amount to a veto,
- or obligations that freeze the company into excess capacity.

If the neighbouring governments want equity, that equity must reflect cash risk and the actual scale of the co-funding.

## Our direction for the next draft

We are not asking for abstract agreement. We are asking for a **single bracketed term sheet** with actual numbers.

### Required components
1. **Plant-by-plant footprint plan**
   - retain / close / consolidate
   - with a clear economic rationale

2. **Labour savings target**
   - wages, work rules, staffing flexibility, workforce reduction

3. **Retiree trust treatment**
   - haircut range
   - replacement instrument
   - cash burden cap
   - governance and anti-dilution terms if equity is used

4. **Bondholder recovery range**
   - equity / warrants / any note component
   - no token recovery, but no protection from the necessary haircut

5. **Public and co-funder capital**
   - amount
   - form
   - equity split
   - governance rights
   - no veto over operational restructuring

6. **Process**
   - either a consensual deal that closes within the deadline
   - or a court-supervised restructuring with the same viability targets and no open-ended public support

## My bottom line

Let me be explicit:

- **We will support a smaller, viable company.**
- **We will support shared sacrifice.**
- **We will support a bounded trust replacement instrument.**
- **We will support meaningful bondholder haircuts.**
- **We will support co-funding only on terms that protect taxpayers and do not lock in excess capacity.**

What we will **not** support is:
- an unrestructured company,
- a trust instrument that is effectively senior to the extent it breaks financeability,
- a creditor package that preserves too much pre-crisis value,
- or a domestic employment promise that prevents the necessary downsizing.

If the next round does not produce a credible term sheet with real numbers, we should stop pretending consensus is close and move to the court-supervised route.
