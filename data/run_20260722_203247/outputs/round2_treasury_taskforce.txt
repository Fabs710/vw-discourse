We are not here to preserve every constituency’s preferred position. We are here to produce a viable company, quickly, at the lowest credible cost to taxpayers.

A few points, directly:

### To the Union / Retiree Trust
We agree on one thing: the retiree obligation cannot simply be ignored. But we do **not** accept the idea that it must be preserved in a form that keeps the company nonviable.

A “durable” instrument is acceptable only if it is durable **and** affordable. If the trust receives equity, a secured-like claim, or another instrument, that must come from a genuinely reduced legacy burden elsewhere and must not recreate the same insolvency in a different wrapper. We are not funding a restructuring that replaces one unpayable promise with another.

Also, job preservation cannot be the veto over the operating footprint. Plants, brands, and dealers will be cut where economics require it. If you want continued financing, you need to come forward with verifiable concessions, not broad statements about fairness.

### To the Bondholders
You are not being singled out, but you are also not entitled to protection from the restructuring simply because you are dispersed and politically awkward.

We agree that your claims are contractual and that many holders are ordinary savers. That does **not** change the arithmetic. This company cannot emerge with an unrestructured balance sheet and a full or near-full creditor recovery. Meaningful haircuts are necessary.

What we will not accept is an argument that because the trust may receive durable value, bondholders therefore deserve comparable upside as of right. No. If the capital structure is reset, every private claimant absorbs losses. Any equity or warrants you receive must be tied to the size of your sacrifice, not used to preserve your pre-crisis recovery expectations.

### To Management
We agree that viability requires a smaller footprint and lower labor costs. But you do not get to hide behind “market reality” while pushing the pain onto labour or the public purse. We forced leadership change precisely because the prior approach was too timid.

Your task is to bring us a package with:
- substantial plant and brand rationalization,
- real labor cost reductions,
- a genuine reduction or conversion of the retiree obligation,
- and a creditor haircut large enough to restore balance-sheet viability.

If you cannot deliver that quickly, then the court-supervised route remains the fallback. We are prepared to use it.

### To the Neighbouring Governments
We will consider co-funding only if it adds real value to the overall rescue, not if it becomes a way to segment the company into protected and unprotected constituencies.

Your request for domestic plant commitments is understood, but it cannot override viability. We will not underwrite a structure that locks in excess capacity simply to satisfy regional politics. If your money comes with conditions, those conditions must be compatible with a smaller, competitive enterprise. And if you expect an equity position, it must reflect actual funded contribution, not a demand for control or a veto over restructuring.

### Our position for this round
We are prepared to support a package only if it includes all of the following:

1. **A materially smaller operating footprint**
   - plant, brand, and dealer reductions substantial enough to make the business competitive.

2. **Real labor concessions**
   - lower cost structure, greater flexibility, and no assumption that workers are insulated from the sacrifice.

3. **A genuine restructuring of the retiree health obligation**
   - not a full preservation of the legacy claim, but a conversion/reduction that preserves some value without sinking the company.

4. **Meaningful unsecured creditor haircuts**
   - enough to restore solvency and avoid repeat distress.

5. **No open-ended public subsidy**
   - bridge financing only, with a credible exit.

6. **Finality within the deadline**
   - if there is no binding consensual deal, we will move through the court-supervised process.

### What we reject
- Any plan that leaves the company structurally unviable.
- Any proposal that makes taxpayers the permanent absorber of losses.
- Any attempt to protect one stakeholder group at the expense of the capital reset.
- Any delay tactic dressed up as negotiation.

If the parties want to avoid the court route, bring a concrete package with actual numbers and actual cuts. Principles are not enough anymore.
