We are prepared to engage seriously on a viable package, but I want to be clear that “viable” cannot be code for dumping the full burden on workers and retirees while preserving creditor expectations.

### Where we agree
- **The company needs a smaller, sustainable footprint.** We are not defending every plant, brand, or dealer as if the market had not collapsed.
- **Labour costs must be addressed.** We have already shown willingness to discuss wage and work-rule changes.
- **The old structure cannot remain intact.** That includes the retiree health obligation, but only if it is converted into something durable and real.
- **Time is short.** We understand the sixty-day clock and do not intend to use delay as a shield.

### Where we disagree

#### 1) The retiree health trust is not a disposable claim
The Task Force keeps saying the obligation must be “restructured,” and management echoes that. Fine. But restructuring is not destruction.

If the trust is to give up the original promise, then it must receive a **real substitute**:
- equity in the restructured company, or
- a secured-style instrument with enforceable value, or
- another durable claim that is not just paper with no path to recovery.

What we will not accept is a plan that effectively writes the trust out of existence and asks retirees to absorb the loss while others preserve value.

#### 2) Workers cannot be the main adjustment mechanism
We are willing to concede on wages, flexibility, and some work rules if that is what it takes to keep the company alive. But we will not accept:
- disproportionate plant closures,
- wholesale job destruction,
- or a restructuring that treats labour as the first and easiest source of sacrifice while creditors argue for “meaningful recovery.”

The burden must be shared in a way that reflects actual ability to absorb losses.

#### 3) Bondholders cannot be protected more generously than the trust and workforce
To the bondholders: yes, your claims are contractual. So are the obligations to current workers and retirees. We do **not** accept the argument that because the trust may receive durable value, bondholders are therefore entitled to comparable upside or lighter treatment as of right.

You will take a haircut. That is unavoidable. What matters is whether you end up with a fair residual claim in a genuinely viable company, not whether you escape pain altogether.

#### 4) Domestic production commitments matter
To the neighbouring governments: we hear your concern about domestic plants and jobs. That concern is legitimate. But we will not support a solution that simply shifts production risk onto workers in one jurisdiction while preserving others unchallenged.

If public co-funding is on the table, then the commitment must be:
- to preserve as many viable plants as possible,
- to protect jobs where operations justify them,
- and to avoid using regional politics as a cover for cutting productive capacity that could remain.

### What a credible package must include
We are not asking for perfection. We are asking for balance.

A workable deal needs:
1. **A materially reduced but viable operating footprint**
   - no cosmetic cuts, but also no unnecessary destruction of industrial communities.

2. **Real labour concessions**
   - wage, work-rule, and flexibility changes, negotiated responsibly.

3. **A durable instrument for the retiree trust**
   - equity or an equivalent enforceable security, not a symbolic promise.

4. **Meaningful unsecured creditor haircuts**
   - but not a structure that rewards creditors while workers and retirees are crushed.

5. **Ownership that reflects sacrifice**
   - if public funders and stakeholders are contributing to the rescue, the equity split must reflect that reality.

### On process
We are not attached to endless consensus. But we are equally not prepared to sign off on a package that uses the court route as leverage to force workers and retirees to accept a one-sided settlement.

If the next proposal continues to treat the trust as a liability to be neutralized rather than an obligation to be honored in durable form, then we will object. If the plan protects creditor recoveries at the expense of jobs and retiree security, we will object. And if the footprint cuts go beyond what is necessary for survival, we will object.

### What would move us
A serious offer would need to include:
- a defined equity/security allocation for the retiree trust,
- explicit employment and plant-retention commitments where feasible,
- and shared sacrifice from unsecured creditors and public stakeholders.

Without those elements, this is not a restructuring we can support in good faith.
