We are ready to move from principles to a workable structure, but let me be clear: **we will not finance a deal that preserves the current burden structure under a different label**. Viability comes first, and it must come quickly.

## Direct response to the parties

### To the Union / Retiree Trust
We agree with the specialist on one point: the retiree obligation cannot remain an open-ended cash drain on the company. It must be converted into a bounded, durable instrument.

But we do **not** accept a structure that gives the trust effective priority over the rest of the capital stack in a way that makes the company unfinanceable. That would simply recreate distress. “Durable” is not the same as “senior to everyone else.”

What we can consider:
- a **material haircut** to the legacy obligation,
- plus a **hybrid replacement** such as preferred equity and/or a limited secured or quasi-secured note,
- with contingent upside if the company outperforms.

What we will not do:
- preserve the full obligation,
- grant the trust a near-absolute senior claim,
- or leave taxpayers exposed to an unrestructured business.

If the union wants value in the replacement, then the legacy claim must be cut enough to make that value financeable.

### To the Bondholders
You are correct that your claims are contractual and that many holders are ordinary investors. That does not change the arithmetic.

You will take a **meaningful haircut**. The company cannot emerge with a balance sheet that leaves unsecured creditors largely whole. That is not a restructuring; it is delay.

But you are **not** being asked to absorb the losses alone. If the retiree trust receives durable value, then bondholders will also receive a real recovery — likely a combination of new equity and warrants — but at a level consistent with a deep capital reset, not pre-crisis expectations.

What we reject is your demand for parity with the trust as if all unsecured claims were identical in practical effect. They are not, and the rescue cannot be structured around preserving old recoveries.

### To Management
You have correctly identified the core issue: the footprint must shrink, labour costs must fall, and the legacy liabilities must be reduced or converted. That is the baseline, not a concession.

However, we are not interested in another vague “transformation plan.” We need a **term sheet-level proposal** with actual numbers:
- plant/brand/dealer cuts,
- labour concessions,
- trust haircut and replacement instrument,
- creditor haircut,
- and the public funding required to bridge the process.

If management cannot produce that in short order, we will not wait indefinitely. The court-supervised route remains available, and we are prepared to use it.

### To the Neighbouring Governments
We are prepared to include co-funding **only if it supports a viable smaller company**, not a politically frozen footprint.

Your domestic production concerns are legitimate, but they do not create a veto over economic reality. We will not underwrite excess capacity simply to protect local constituencies. If you want an ownership stake, it must reflect actual cash contribution and risk — not control rights that distort restructuring.

We can consider binding production commitments where they are compatible with the smaller viable footprint. We cannot commit to preserving every domestic plant or every job.

## Our proposed framework for this round

We are not asking for final agreement on every detail today. We are asking for a realistic bracketed structure that can close fast.

### 1) Operating footprint
- **Substantial rationalization** of plants, brands, and dealers.
- The company must emerge smaller and cash-flow positive on a normalized basis.
- Cuts must be driven by economics, not politics.

### 2) Labour
- Real concessions on wages, work rules, and flexibility.
- Workforce reduction is likely unavoidable.
- The labour side must share the burden, not merely ask others to do so.

### 3) Retiree trust
- The legacy obligation must be **materially reduced**.
- Replacement value may come through a hybrid of:
  - preferred equity,
  - limited secured/quasi-secured paper,
  - and contingent upside.
- But the package must be **bounded and financeable**.

### 4) Bondholders
- Meaningful principal reduction and a real recovery package.
- Likely a mix of new equity and warrants.
- No argument for near-full recovery will be entertained.

### 5) Public funding
- Bridge financing only.
- No open-ended subsidy.
- Any co-funding must support viability, not block restructuring.

### 6) Governance / ownership
- Equity split must reflect sacrifice.
- No constituency gets to keep its pre-crisis position and also claim governance rights as if nothing happened.
- Co-funders may receive equity, but not a veto over operational restructuring.

## What we want next
Bring us a **single draft term sheet** with brackets, not another round of abstract fairness claims.

Specifically:
- proposed plant closures and retained footprint,
- labour cost reduction target,
- retiree trust haircut and instrument structure,
- bondholder recovery range,
- amount and form of co-funding,
- proposed equity split,
- and whether the plan is consensual or intended as the basis for a court-supported fallback.

## Our bottom line
We are not negotiating toward comfort. We are negotiating toward **viability, finality, and taxpayer protection**.

If the package is deep enough to make the company viable, shared enough to be fair in substance, and fast enough to finish inside the deadline, we will support it.

If it is not, we will not fund an unrestructured company just to avoid hard choices.
