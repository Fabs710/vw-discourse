## Moderator summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
- **The company cannot remain in its current form** and needs a materially smaller, viable operating footprint.
- **Plant, brand, and dealer rationalization will be part of the solution.**
- **Real labour concessions are required** on wages, work rules, and flexibility.
- **The retiree health obligation cannot remain as an open-ended legacy liability** and must be converted or reduced in some durable form.
- **Unsecured bondholders will take meaningful haircuts.**
- **Open-ended public subsidy is rejected by all sides**; bridge financing may be acceptable if it has a clear exit.
- **Finality is needed quickly**, and the court-supervised route remains a fallback if no consensual deal is reached.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment:**  
  The union wants a durable, enforceable replacement with real value; the task force and management will not accept a structure that recreates insolvency or creates excessive priority. Bondholders resist any trust instrument that is better protected than their own recovery.
- **Relative creditor recovery:**  
  Bondholders oppose being the main residual loss bearer if the retiree trust receives stronger protection. The task force insists on major haircuts, but not on preserving pre-crisis recovery levels.
- **Domestic plants and co-funder role:**  
  Neighbouring governments want binding protection for domestic production and employment, plus a proportionate equity/governance stake. The task force and management say those commitments cannot override viability.
- **Extent of footprint cuts:**  
  All agree cuts are necessary, but there is no agreement on how far plant/brand closures should go, or which jurisdictions bear the burden.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to convert the retiree obligation** without either leaving it too weak to be credible or too senior to be financeable.
- **How to allocate losses among labour, retirees, bondholders, and public funders** in a way that is seen as shared but still workable.
- **How much domestic capacity can be preserved** without locking in excess cost or unneeded output.
- **What equity/governance each funding or sacrifice group receives** and whether those allocations are proportional enough to secure assent.
- **Whether the package can be closed consensually within the deadline** or whether the court route becomes unavoidable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to **numbers and structure**:
- proposed size of plant/brand/dealer reductions,
- exact labour concessions,
- haircut range for unsecured creditors,
- proposed haircut and replacement instrument for the retiree trust,
- amount and form of public co-funding,
- equity/governance allocations,
- and which domestic plants, if any, are specifically preserved.

If useful, Round 3 should focus on a single draft term sheet with bracketed options rather than further position statements.
