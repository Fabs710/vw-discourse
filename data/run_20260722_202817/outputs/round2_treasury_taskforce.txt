We are aligned on one point: **this company must be restructured deeply enough to survive without continuous public support**. If that means real plant cuts, brand rationalization, workforce reductions, and a material balance-sheet reset, then so be it. Anything less is not a rescue; it is a delay tactic funded by taxpayers.

Where I disagree is on every attempt to turn “shared sacrifice” into a contest over who can preserve the most of their pre-crisis position.

### To labour and the retiree trust
I hear the point that the trust was negotiated over many years and that workers have already given up a lot. But that does **not** make the obligation untouchable. If the trust is treated as effectively senior while unsecured creditors take deep losses, that is not fair burden-sharing — it is preferential treatment dressed up as industrial policy.

I am open to a **durable instrument** for the trust, including equity or equity-linked value, but only if the overall package is economically credible. That means:
- the company must emerge viable,
- the trust cannot be insulated from sacrifice,
- and the workforce cannot be the primary funding source for everyone else’s preservation.

What I will not accept:
- a plan that preserves too many jobs, plants, and benefits and then asks taxpayers to cover the gap;
- a “trust solution” that is actually a backdoor attempt to avoid real restructuring;
- or a package where labour losses are capped politically while creditors absorb the real pain.

### To bondholders
You are right that you should not be singled out arbitrarily. But the answer is not to insist on near-par treatment or to use “equal treatment” as a shield against the restructuring the company actually needs.

A viable deal will require **real haircuts** on unsecured debt. The amount will depend on the final operating footprint and the financing needed to get there, but token losses are not in scope. If the company needs a debt-for-equity conversion, warrants, or other instruments, then that is the direction. What is not acceptable is demanding a recovery structure that keeps the balance sheet too heavy to support the business.

Also, let me be explicit: claims are not all identical in economic and political reality, and I will not let the need for finality be held hostage by demands for treatment that preserve the old capital structure in substance.

### To management
You have been replaced because the prior plan was insufficient. That should tell you the standard here. We are not funding a restart that merely swaps executives while leaving the underlying economics intact.

I agree with your point that speed and certainty matter. But that only helps if the final result is a company that can actually stand on its own. So yes:
- fewer brands if needed,
- fewer plants if needed,
- smaller dealer footprint if needed,
- and a capital structure that can be financed privately after the bridge.

No open-ended government backstop. No rescue that just postpones failure.

### To neighbouring governments
We are prepared to consider co-funding, but only on **firm, enforceable, and economically sensible terms**. I am not going to accept a framework where domestic production commitments are used to block the very rationalization needed to make the company viable.

If you want to contribute, then the commitments must fit within a restructuring that can survive. I will not subordinate the entire rescue to one country’s industrial footprint demands. Public money must support a viable company, not freeze its footprint in place.

### On process
The 60-day deadline is real. If the parties cannot produce a package that delivers:
- meaningful concessions from labour,
- meaningful losses for unsecured creditors,
- a credible operating footprint,
- and a balance sheet that does not require repeated public rescues,

then we will move to the court-supervised route and impose the terms necessary to complete the restructuring.

That is not a threat for effect; it is the fallback we are already preparing.

### What I need to see next
Bring me actual numbers and actual concessions:
- plant closures / workforce reductions sufficient to restore competitiveness,
- a concrete haircut range for unsecured bondholders,
- a concrete restructuring of the retiree trust obligation,
- and any co-funding proposal tied to enforceable conditions, not political aspirations.

If the proposals coming back are still built around preserving too much and sacrificing too little, I will reject them again. We are not here to subsidize delay. We are here to produce a viable company at minimum credible taxpayer cost.
