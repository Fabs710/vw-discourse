We agree on one essential point: **the company must be made viable, and quickly**. We are not defending the old structure for its own sake, and we are not interested in delay. If the choice is between a serious restructuring and collapse, we know which path protects workers, retirees, suppliers, and the industrial base.

But I need to be just as clear about where I **strongly disagree** with what has been put on the table.

### 1) The retiree health trust is not an ordinary unsecured claim
The task force keeps speaking as if “fair burden-sharing” means treating the retiree trust like just another bondholder class. It is not.

That trust was built through decades of concessions by workers and retirees. It is part of the industrial compact of this company. If restructuring simply wipes it out, or pushes it to the back of the line behind financial claims, then that is not shared sacrifice — that is sacrificing workers and retirees to preserve financial recoveries.

We are open to converting the obligation into a **durable instrument**, including:
- equity in the restructured company,
- equity-linked securities,
- or another claim with real value and enforceability.

But it must be **real**, not symbolic. We will not accept a paper promise that can be diluted into nothing.

### 2) Workers cannot be the main source of savings
I agree that there must be plant rationalization and workforce reduction. But I reject the idea that labour should absorb the bulk of the losses while creditors and public authorities preserve their positions.

If viability requires sacrifice, then sacrifice must be:
- shared by unsecured creditors,
- shared by management,
- shared by labour,
- and not imposed disproportionately on the workforce and retirees.

We have already accepted concessions in earlier rounds. We are not the party that overextended the balance sheet or misread the market. So no, we will not support a package where workers pay twice: once through wages and work rules, and again through destruction of the trust and mass plant closures.

### 3) We do not accept “viability” as a synonym for wholesale deindustrialization
Yes, some cuts are necessary. But there is a line between rationalization and liquidation by another name.

We need:
- a footprint trimmed enough to survive,
- not a footprint gutted to satisfy creditors or to make the restructuring easier to finance at the expense of communities.

The company cannot be viable if it destroys the very industrial network that produces its products and supports its sales. So if the proposed cuts are too deep, we will say so plainly: that is not restructuring, that is dismantling.

### 4) On bondholders: equal treatment does not mean identical treatment, but it does mean no special immunity
I hear the bondholders’ complaint that they should not be singled out. On the narrow point, I agree: they should not be made the only class to carry the restructuring.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion they draw from that.

- If the trust receives a durable equity-linked instrument, that is because it reflects a negotiated industrial obligation, not because we are trying to privilege one class over another.
- If unsecured bondholders are receiving debt-for-equity, warrants, or another recovery, then the trust can receive a different but economically equivalent form.

What I will not accept is a race to the bottom where the trust is stripped so bondholders can argue for a better recovery. The appropriate standard is not “who can force the biggest haircut on everyone else,” but whether the final deal is fair, viable, and durable.

### 5) On public funding and neighbouring governments
We understand the task force’s insistence on no open-ended public support. We do not want that either.

But I will be equally blunt about the neighbouring governments’ position: you cannot demand binding domestic production commitments while also insisting on a restructuring that leaves no room for the retained plants to remain viable. If your funding is conditional on keeping the industrial base, then your conditions have to be compatible with an actual operating company.

We are open to domestic-plant commitments if they are part of a balanced package, but not if they become a veto on necessary restructuring or a tool to push the burden onto workers elsewhere.

### 6) What we can support
If you want our consent, the package must include:
- a **meaningful haircut for unsecured creditors**,
- **management accountability**,
- a **durable, real settlement for the retiree health trust**,
- plant cuts only to the extent necessary for viability,
- and a post-restructuring company that still has a credible industrial future.

We can be flexible on form:
- trust debt can be converted into equity or equity-linked value,
- wages and work rules can be adjusted,
- some plants can close,
- some brands can go,
- and some dealers will be rationalized.

But there are red lines:
- no destruction of the retiree trust,
- no wholesale sacrifice of the workforce,
- no fake “equal treatment” that in practice means bondholders recover while retirees are left exposed,
- and no deal that leaves the company too weak to survive.

### 7) On process
We are prepared to do this either consensually or through court-supervised restructuring if that is the only way to get finality. We are not attached to the route. We are attached to the outcome.

But if the court route is being used to impose a solution that is simply harsher on labour and retirees than on everyone else, then we will resist it. That is not a neutral process; that is a power play.

### 8) What needs to come next
If the parties want progress, bring us concrete proposals:
- exact haircut ranges for unsecured creditors,
- exact form and value of the retiree trust’s recovery,
- concrete plant and workforce reduction numbers,
- and the ownership structure of the restructured company.

If the current direction is “workers and retirees take the deepest cuts so everyone else can claim fairness,” then I am saying now: **we do not accept that framework**.

We are willing to make this company viable. We are not willing to let viability become a cover for stripping the workforce, hollowing out communities, and destroying the trust that was negotiated in good faith.
