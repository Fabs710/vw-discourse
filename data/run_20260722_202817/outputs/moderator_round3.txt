## Core impasse

The dispute has narrowed to **one central conflict**:

**Who bears the economic cost of restoring viability, and how the value of the trust, bondholders, labour, and domestic plant commitments is allocated within a limited recovery pool.**

That impasse has three linked sub-issues:

1. **Retiree trust treatment**
   - Union/trust wants a durable, meaningful recovery reflecting negotiated history.
   - Bondholders and Task Force resist any trust structure that is economically better than other unsecured claims.
   - The question is whether the trust gets a protected floor or only a contingent equity-linked value.

2. **Footprint vs. domestic protection**
   - Management, Task Force, and experts require material plant closures and workforce reduction.
   - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production and employment commitments.
   - The question is whether the “minimum viable footprint” can include protected domestic sites without reintroducing insolvency.

3. **Relative recoveries across constituencies**
   - Bondholders object to being the default loss bucket.
   - Labour rejects being the sole source of savings.
   - Co-funders want equity proportional to their support.
   - The question is whether a comparability framework can produce broadly aligned present-value recoveries without overpromising any one group.

---

## Concrete compromise positions

### Compromise 1: Capped trust floor plus contingent upside
**Structure**
- Retiree trust receives an equity-linked preferred instrument.
- A **modest floor** is provided, but it is:
  - capped,
  - deferred,
  - and payable only if performance milestones are met.
- Additional upside is contingent on post-restructuring enterprise performance.

**Purpose**
- Preserves the trust’s special history without making it senior in substance.
- Gives bondholders room for a larger equity/warrant package.
- Reduces the risk that the trust recovery reintroduces insolvency.

**Use case**
- Best if the trust can accept “durable but not guaranteed” value.

---

### Compromise 2: Domestic core protection in exchange for deeper operational cuts elsewhere
**Structure**
- Neighbouring governments identify a **limited protected domestic core**:
  - a small number of strategic sites,
  - defined production volumes,
  - and time-bound investment commitments.
- In exchange:
  - non-core plants are closed or consolidated,
  - workforce reductions are accelerated,
  - and brand/dealer rationalization is more aggressive.

**Purpose**
- Allows co-funders to justify participation with real domestic commitments.
- Keeps the footprint rationalization economically credible.
- Prevents the domestic protection demand from becoming a veto over the restructuring.

**Use case**
- Best if co-funders need visible domestic safeguards but can accept a smaller footprint overall.

---

### Compromise 3: Value-sharing waterfall with independent valuation and class-specific minimums
**Structure**
- Appoint an independent valuation process to set:
  - enterprise value,
  - liquidation value,
  - and recoverable equity pool.
- Establish a **waterfall**:
  - bondholders receive debt-for-equity and warrants,
  - the trust receives an equity-linked preferred claim with a floor,
  - co-funders receive equity tied to fresh capital,
  - management/labour concessions are treated as operating-enabling conditions.
- Each class gets a **minimum expected-value target** rather than identical instruments.

**Purpose**
- Makes “comparable economics” operational rather than rhetorical.
- Reduces accusations of favoritism.
- Creates a process for deciding whether any floor is financeable.

**Use case**
- Best if parties are deadlocked on fairness definitions but can accept a neutral valuation framework.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Government Task Force
**What specific maximum present-value recovery do you consider tolerable for the retiree trust before you would conclude the deal is no longer financeable?**

### To Management
**What is the smallest plant-and-workforce footprint you can support while still meeting liquidity, capex, and debt-service needs under stressed demand assumptions?**

### To Unsecured Bondholders
**What recovery package, stated in present-value terms, would you accept if the retiree trust receives a capped equity-linked floor rather than pure debt or cash?**

### To Industrial Union & Retiree Trust
**Would you accept a reduced but durable trust recovery if the instrument has a real floor, capped upside, and is paired with verified bondholder losses and protected worker interests?**

### To Neighbouring Governments
**What exact domestic production and employment commitments are the minimum required for your funding, and would you reduce or withdraw support if those commitments make the restructuring insolvent?**
