We agree on the need for a **deep, final restructuring**. We also agree that this cannot be a taxpayer-funded delay. But I want to be equally direct: **we will not support a rescue that preserves the company by hollowing out our domestic industrial base or by leaving co-funders with a passive, undercompensated role.**

## Where we agree
- **Management** is right that the old plan failed and that viability requires real change.
- **The task force** is right that token cuts and endless bridge finance are unacceptable.
- **Bondholders** are right that they should not be arbitrarily singled out.
- **Labour** is right that workers and retirees should not be the only ones taking losses.

Those are all true. But they do not settle the issues that matter to us.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic production cannot be treated as optional
To the task force and management: I understand the insistence on viability. I accept that some rationalization is necessary. But I do **not** accept any package that protects foreign or lower-priority sites while domestic plants are left exposed.

We are co-funding to protect a real domestic industrial footprint. That means:
- binding domestic production commitments,
- binding employment commitments for supported sites,
- and a restructuring plan that preserves meaningful domestic capacity.

If the answer is “viability first, and domestic plants only if convenient,” then we are not aligned. That is not a basis for our funding.

### 2) We will not fund a deal without proportionate ownership
If we are contributing fresh public money and accepting political and economic risk to protect domestic plants, then we need a **proportionate equity position** in the restructured company.

We are not asking for control for its own sake. We are asking for ownership that reflects our contribution and the commitments we are being asked to make. A passive, symbolic stake is not acceptable.

### 3) Footprint reductions must be viability-based, not a pretext for offshoring
I agree with the experts that the company likely needs a smaller footprint. But I disagree with any implication that “minimum viable” can be defined without regard to domestic production share.

A viable company still needs:
- a credible domestic manufacturing base,
- strategic plants that anchor employment and supplier networks,
- and enough scale to support long-term industrial capacity.

If “minimum viable” becomes a euphemism for shifting production away from our jurisdictions, then we will oppose it.

### 4) We are not asking for every site to be preserved
To be clear, we are not insisting that every plant remain open. We understand restructuring requires hard choices.

What we want is:
- a **protected core domestic footprint**,
- clear site-by-site commitments,
- and closures only where they are genuinely necessary and not simply convenient.

That is a different thing from blanket preservation, and it is also a different thing from accepting domestic sacrifice while other sites are protected.

## On the expert’s valuation and footprint comments
The specialist input is useful in one respect: it confirms what we already believe — that **external support and footprint decisions are tied together**.

If the trust gets a floor, if bondholders get debt/equity/warrants, and if the company still needs a credible operating base, then someone is paying for the difference. We are prepared to contribute to that package only if:
- our domestic plants are protected in a meaningful way,
- the recovery is orderly,
- and our contribution is matched by an ownership stake.

## To the Government Task Force
You say you will fund a deep restructuring only if it is viable. We agree. But viability does not mean sacrificing our plants first.

What I need from you is not another general statement about fairness. I need:
- the **minimum domestic footprint** you would accept,
- the **plant retention and investment commitments** for supported sites,
- and how co-funder participation will be reflected in the **equity allocation**.

Without that, there is no basis for our support.

## To Management
You want flexibility on plant and brand rationalization. Fine. But if our funding is part of the solution, then domestic production share cannot be treated as a side effect.

Bring us a plan that answers:
- which domestic sites are protected,
- what production volumes they will carry,
- how jobs are preserved there,
- and what ownership we receive for our contribution.

If the plan protects the domestic core, we can work constructively. If it does not, then we will not pay to watch capacity move elsewhere.

## To the Union and Retiree Trust
We respect the history behind the trust and the workforce’s sacrifices. We are not here to pick a fight with labour.

But I need to be clear: if domestic plants are being asked to carry the burden while the trust receives a specially protected recovery and other jurisdictions are shielded, that is not acceptable to us.

Any trust solution must fit within a package that also protects domestic industrial capacity. We are not going to fund a deal where our plants are the adjustment variable.

## To the Bondholders
You are right that you should not be singled out arbitrarily. We are not defending that.

But we are also not prepared to let creditor fairness become a device to underwrite the erosion of domestic industrial capacity. If our funding is part of the deal, then the outcome has to reflect:
- fair treatment of claimants,
- viable operations,
- and a domestic footprint that is actually preserved.

## Our position
We can support an orderly restructuring if it includes:
1. **Meaningful domestic production and employment commitments**
2. **A proportionate equity stake for co-funders**
3. **A viable, not symbolic, domestic industrial footprint**
4. **Deep but workable restructuring across labour and creditors**
5. **Finality either consensually or through the court route**

We cannot support:
- domestic plant closures while foreign plants are protected,
- a deal where co-funders pay but do not receive a commensurate ownership position,
- or a “viability” argument that simply relocates the burden onto our jurisdictions.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund an orderly rescue. We are **not** prepared to subsidize a restructuring that weakens our domestic industrial base or leaves us with a minor role despite meaningful contribution.

If the package protects domestic production and gives co-funders a proportionate stake, we can be constructive. If it does not, then we do not have a basis for funding.
