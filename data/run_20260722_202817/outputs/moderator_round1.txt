### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company requires a **deep restructuring** to be viable.
- All parties accept that **some sacrifice is unavoidable** and that the old cost/capital structure cannot remain intact.
- There is broad agreement that **management must bear consequences** and that **unsecured creditors cannot be made whole**.
- All parties are open, in principle, to either a **consensual restructuring** or a **court-supervised process** if it produces finality and viability.
- There is general acceptance that the outcome must be **fast**, **credible**, and **final** within the 60-day window.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health trust treatment:**  
  The union wants a durable, meaningful recovery for the trust, potentially via equity or equivalent value. Bondholders insist it must not receive materially better treatment than other unsecured claims.
- **Relative burden-sharing among unsecured claimants:**  
  Bondholders reject being singled out for disproportionate losses; the union and management argue that the trust and workforce should not be the main absorbers of the restructuring burden.
- **Scale of workforce and plant rationalization:**  
  Government and management want deeper cuts for viability; the union wants cuts limited enough to avoid wholesale industrial loss; neighbouring governments want domestic plants protected.
- **Public funding conditions:**  
  The lead funder supports public bridge financing only for a credible restructuring, while neighbouring governments condition funding on binding domestic production and employment commitments.
- **Ownership / control in the restructured entity:**  
  Several parties want an equity position tied to their sacrifice or funding, but the size, form, and priority of those stakes remain unresolved.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Viability vs. preservation of jobs/plants:** how much footprint reduction is needed to make the company self-sustaining.
- **Equal treatment vs. political/industrial priorities:** whether the retiree trust or domestic governments receive treatment that differs from ordinary unsecured claims or other funding parties.
- **Depth of creditor losses vs. chance of agreement:** how severe haircuts can be before bondholders refuse consent and force a court process.
- **Public support vs. enforceable commitments:** what specific conditions must accompany any taxpayer or co-funder support.
- **Short-term bridge financing vs. long-term balance-sheet reset:** how to avoid using public funds simply to delay hard restructuring choices.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific numbers, instruments, and conditions**:
  - proposed haircut ranges,
  - proposed equity/warrant allocations,
  - plant/workforce reduction ranges,
  - and the exact terms tied to public funding.
- Clarify whether the retiree trust can be satisfied through **equity-linked value** without creating a hierarchy that bondholders see as unequal.
- Identify a **minimum viable operating footprint** and the associated funding needed to reach it.
- Ask each party to state its **best alternative if no deal is reached**, so the group can test which issues are truly deal-breakers.
