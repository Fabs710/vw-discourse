We are not here to preserve the status quo, and we are not denying that this company needs a deep restructuring. On that point, we agree with the task force and with management: there must be real cuts, real burden-sharing, and a credible path to viability.

But I need to be equally clear: **unsecured bondholders will not accept being singled out for a confiscatory deal while other unsecured stakeholders are treated better simply because they have political leverage.**

### Where we agree
- **Viability comes first.** If the company is not made viable, then everyone loses more.
- **The old capital structure cannot survive intact.** We understand that.
- **Management must be sacrificed.** New leadership is not a substitute for accountability.
- **Court-supervised restructuring is not off the table.** We care about recovery, not the label on the process.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) Equal treatment matters
The retiree health trust may have a powerful political story, but from our perspective it is still an unsecured obligation. We do **not** accept a framework where the trust receives a materially better recovery than the bondholders unless there is a very clear and compensating reason in the actual economics. If unsecured claims are being cut, then they should be cut **consistently**.

What we will not accept is:
- bondholders taking a deep haircut,
- while the retiree trust is insulated through preferred treatment,
- and then being told that this is “fair burden-sharing.”

That is not fair; it is political prioritization.

#### 2) “Shared sacrifice” cannot mean bondholders fund the rescue
We are being asked to absorb losses on claims that were legally extended in good faith, held in many cases by ordinary savers, pensioners, and institutions. We are not the cause of the collapse. We did not mismanage the company. We did not overpromise the product strategy or blow the balance sheet.

So if the task force wants sacrifice, it must be **proportionate** and **evidence-based**, not a selective wipeout dressed up as restructuring.

#### 3) Viability does not justify zero recovery
We are flexible on form. We can consider:
- debt-for-equity,
- warrants,
- contingent value rights,
- a mix of cash and upside participation.

But we will not accept terms that amount to a near-total wipeout with only token upside. A small equity stub without real participation is not an exchange; it is expropriation.

### On the retiree trust
We understand the trust is a sensitive issue, but it cannot be used to push the unsecured bondholders below comparable unsecured treatment. If the trust is going to receive durable value — equity, warrants, or a secured-like instrument — then bondholders must receive **comparable economic treatment**, not a lesser one.

If there is a reason for differential treatment, it must be real and transparent, not simply “the government prefers labour peace.”

### On the process
We are not afraid of the court route. In fact, the credible threat of court is precisely why we are here. If consensual terms are materially worse than what a court would impose on a fair basis, we will reject them.

That said, we are prepared to move if the package includes:
- a meaningful recovery for unsecured bondholders,
- equal treatment relative to other unsecured claims,
- real upside participation in the restructured company,
- and a settlement that is not obviously designed to privilege one unsecured constituency over another.

### Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- **Yes** to deep restructuring.
- **Yes** to viable capital structure changes.
- **Yes** to exchanging our claims for equity/warrants if the economics are real.
- **No** to a politically driven hierarchy among unsecured creditors.
- **No** to a token recovery that leaves bondholders absorbing the bulk of the pain while others do better.

If the other parties want our consent, they need to show us a package where unsecured creditors are treated even-handedly and where our recovery is not materially worse than comparable claims. Without that, we will not volunteer to be the sacrificial class.
