We are prepared to co-fund this restructuring, but only on terms that protect the domestic production base and give us a proportionate stake in the restructured company.

Let me be direct.

### Where we agree
- **This company must be made viable.**
- **There has to be real sacrifice from management, labour, and creditors.**
- **A deep, orderly restructuring is better than chaotic collapse.**
- **Speed matters.** We understand the 60-day window is not a talking point.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) Domestic plants cannot be the first and easiest casualty
We will not support a package that shuts domestic plants while foreign footprint is protected. If neighbouring governments are asked to put money on the table, then there must be binding commitments on:
- domestic production share,
- domestic plant retention,
- and employment levels at the plants we are supporting.

If the restructuring means our taxpayers help finance a deal that simply migrates production elsewhere, that is not acceptable.

#### 2) No funding without real commitments
We are not interested in writing a cheque for a vague promise. If public co-funding is part of the solution, it must be tied to enforceable commitments on:
- plant allocation,
- investment in domestic facilities,
- and an orderly restructuring plan that preserves a meaningful home-country footprint.

#### 3) We must have a fair ownership position
If we contribute financing and accept the risks of the rescue, we expect an ownership position proportionate to that contribution. We are not asking for control, but we are not prepared to be treated as a passive charity either. Partnership means a real seat at the table.

### On the labour and retiree trust issue
I understand the union’s point that the retiree health trust is not an ordinary commercial claim in the political sense. But I also agree with the bondholders that the treatment has to be economically coherent.

What I cannot accept is a structure where:
- labour gets strong protection,
- bondholders are written down hard,
- and domestic governments fund the rescue,
- while the domestic production footprint is still cut back aggressively.

If the trust is to receive equity or another durable instrument, fine — but then the restructuring must still leave enough room for domestic plants to remain viable. Sacrificing our industrial base to preserve everyone else’s symbolic claims is not a solution.

### On the process
I am open to either route:
- a consensual deal, if it meets the conditions above, or
- a court-supervised process, if that is what is required to get finality.

But I want to be clear: we will not sign onto a process that uses the deadline to pressure us into funding plant closures at home. Our support depends on concrete domestic commitments, not general assurances about viability.

### Bottom line
Our position is:
- **Yes** to co-funding, but only with binding domestic production and employment protections.
- **Yes** to restructuring, but not at the expense of our plants.
- **Yes** to a proportionate equity stake for our contribution.
- **No** to paying for a deal that weakens our industrial base while others get protected.

If the package includes those commitments, we are ready to be constructive. If it does not, then we should be honest now: there is no basis for our funding.
