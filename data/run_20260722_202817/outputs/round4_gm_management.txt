We are aligned on the basic facts: this company needs a **deep, immediate restructuring** to survive, and the old plan is dead. I agree with the task force, bondholders, labour, and the co-funders on one point each:

- **Task force:** token cuts are useless.
- **Bondholders:** they should not be singled out arbitrarily.
- **Union/trust:** labour and retirees should not be the only loss pool.
- **Neighbouring governments:** if they put in fresh money, they deserve enforceable commitments.

But I have to be equally blunt about the limits.

## Where I disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot be treated as senior in substance
I agree the trust has a special history. I do **not** agree that history can be translated into a claim that sits ahead of the company’s viability.

If the trust floor becomes large enough to function like protected debt, then the company stays overlevered. That is unacceptable. We are not going to rebuild a balance sheet that immediately breaks again.

What I can support is:
- an **equity-linked instrument**,
- with a **real but modest floor**,
- capped upside,
- and a valuation that shows it is economically defensible.

What I cannot support is a structure that makes the trust effectively untouchable while bondholders absorb the bulk of the pain and the business remains financially fragile.

### 2) “Comparable economics” must be real, not rhetorical
I agree with the expert and with bondholders that different instruments can still be fair if their **present value** is broadly comparable.

That said, I do not accept the union’s framing that the trust must be insulated because it arose from negotiated concessions. That history matters, but it does not change the arithmetic of insolvency.

If the trust gets a floor, bondholders must also get a package with real value. If the trust’s recovery is materially better in economic terms than the bondholders’, the deal will not hold.

### 3) Domestic plant protection cannot veto restructuring
I understand the neighbouring governments’ position. I do not dismiss it. But I cannot support a rescue that freezes an inefficient footprint simply to preserve domestic optics.

We can support:
- a **protected core domestic footprint**,
- specific strategic sites,
- time-bound production and investment commitments.

We cannot support:
- blanket protection of all domestic plants,
- or commitments that force us to keep excess capacity alive.

If the domestic requirements make the company unfinanceable, then they are not a rescue condition; they are a new problem.

## What I am prepared to support

A viable package needs all of the following:

1. **Material unsecured creditor haircuts**
   - bondholders take real losses,
   - debt-for-equity and warrants are appropriate.

2. **A durable but bounded trust recovery**
   - equity-linked preferred or similar instrument,
   - a modest capped floor,
   - no senior-like guarantee.

3. **Meaningful labour concessions**
   - workforce reduction,
   - work-rule changes,
   - benefit cost reduction.

4. **Material footprint rationalization**
   - close or consolidate excess plants,
   - rationalize brands and dealers,
   - preserve the strongest franchises.

5. **Co-funder support tied to enforceable conditions**
   - not open-ended subsidies,
   - not veto rights over necessary restructuring.

6. **Finality**
   - if consensus is possible fast, fine,
   - if not, the court-supervised route is preferable to delay.

## What I cannot accept

I will not sign onto a package that:
- preserves too much footprint for political comfort,
- gives the trust a recovery that is senior in all but name,
- leaves the company overlevered,
- or uses public money to avoid hard choices.

And I will be explicit: I would rather move to a court-supervised process than accept a deal that looks balanced politically but is not financeable operationally.

## On the parties’ positions

### To the Government Task Force
Your insistence on viability is right. Your insistence that no claimant class be protected at the expense of the company is right.

Where I need clarity is on numbers:
- minimum viable footprint,
- acceptable post-restructuring debt load,
- and the maximum trust recovery you can support.

Without that, we are negotiating in the abstract.

### To the Union and Retiree Trust
I respect the history of the trust. I do not accept that it can be preserved at a level that crowds out the rest of the restructuring.

If you want a durable recovery, help define a floor that is meaningful but financeable. If the floor is too large, it simply reintroduces insolvency.

### To the Bondholders
You are right that you should not be the default sacrifice bucket. But you also cannot demand a package that leaves the company too weak to survive.

If you are prepared for real haircuts and debt/equity conversion, then say so in actual ranges.

### To the Neighbouring Governments
We can discuss protected domestic sites and binding production commitments. We cannot agree to domestic commitments that eliminate the flexibility needed to make the company viable.

If your minimum terms exceed the viable footprint, then we have a problem.

## What I need next
I support moving from principles to numbers:

- bondholder haircut range,
- trust floor size,
- equity allocation,
- plant closure/consolidation list,
- workforce reduction range,
- co-funder equity and domestic production commitments.

If those numbers can be made compatible with viability, we have a deal path. If not, we should stop pretending and move to the court-supervised route.
