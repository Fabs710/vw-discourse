We agree on the need for a **viable company** and on the fact that **everyone has to take pain**. The union and the retiree health trust are not here to defend the status quo. We have already accepted concessions before, and we recognize the company cannot survive with the old cost structure unchanged.

But I need to be equally clear on our core limits:

1. **The retiree health trust cannot be treated as an ordinary unsecured claim.**  
   That obligation was negotiated in return for sacrifices by workers and retirees over decades. It is not acceptable to simply wipe it out or push it to the back of the line while bondholders preserve too much value. If there is restructuring, the trust must receive something durable and real — not just a vague promise, not just an empty IOU.

2. **Workers cannot be asked to bear the overwhelming share of the sacrifice.**  
   We will consider wage, work-rule, and benefit adjustments if they are part of a balanced package. But we will not support a plan that destroys jobs, shuts plants indiscriminately, or uses labour as the main source of savings while protecting creditors.

3. **There must be a serious commitment to jobs and plant retention.**  
   We understand rationalization is inevitable. But “viability” cannot become a slogan for wholesale deindustrialization. We want the footprint trimmed enough to survive, not gutted to satisfy financial claims.

On the question of how the retiree obligation is honoured, we are open to **a conversion into equity or other durable securities in the restructured company**, provided that the trust gets a real claim and not a paper instrument with no control or upside. If creditors are taking cuts, then the trust should not be left behind as the residual casualty.

On the path forward, we do **not** insist on a consensual process as an abstract principle. If the only way to prevent liquidation is a court-supervised restructuring, then we will not cling to procedure for its own sake. But if that route is used to impose disproportionate losses on workers and retirees while others are shielded, we will oppose it.

So our position is:

- **Yes** to deep enough restructuring to make the company viable
- **Yes** to shared sacrifice
- **Yes** to a durable settlement for the retiree trust, including equity or equivalent value
- **Yes** to some plant and cost rationalization
- **No** to destroying the trust
- **No** to a restructuring that sacrifices the workforce wholesale for creditor recoveries

If the task force wants our cooperation, it should present a package where:
- unsecured creditors take real haircuts,
- management is also clearly sacrificed,
- the trust receives a meaningful durable stake,
- and the jobs/plant cuts are severe enough to restore viability but not so severe that the industrial base is simply written off.

That is the line we are drawing.
