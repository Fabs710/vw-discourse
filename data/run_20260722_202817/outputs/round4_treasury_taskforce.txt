We are at the point where principles are no longer enough. I need the parties to confront the arithmetic.

## My position, plainly

I will support a rescue only if it produces:

- a **viable post-restructuring company**,
- **material losses for unsecured creditors**,
- **meaningful labour and retiree concessions**,
- **a materially smaller footprint**,
- and **no open-ended public subsidy**.

I will not support a package that:
- protects the retiree trust at a level that makes the company unfinanceable,
- preserves too much capacity for political reasons,
- or leaves taxpayers as the residual backstop for an unrestructured balance sheet.

If that means the court-supervised route, then so be it.

## To the Union and Retiree Trust

I understand the history here. I do not dismiss it. But history does not override solvency.

You keep saying the trust is not “just another unsecured claim.” Economically, in this restructuring, it is an unsecured obligation that must be brought into a viable capital structure. It can be given a different instrument. It cannot be given a different reality.

What I will not accept:
- a trust floor so large it functions like senior debt,
- a structure that forces bondholders to absorb disproportionate losses while the trust is insulated,
- or a “durable” recovery that is durable only because the company remains too weak to survive without future state support.

If you want a floor, it must be modest, contingent, and financeable. If you want comparability, then accept that bondholders also need real value, not just scraps.

## To the Bondholders

You are right about one thing: I will not allow the trust to be quietly preferred over you while pretending this is equal treatment.

But let me be equally clear: you are not entitled to hold out for a recovery that leaves the company too indebted to survive. Large haircuts are not optional here.

So your choice is not between fairness and loss. Loss is coming. Your choice is between:
- a fast deal with meaningful debt-for-equity and warrants, or
- a court process that may be harsher and less negotiable.

If you want me to view your package as credible, give me an actual recovery range and accept comparable economics across unsecured classes. Do not simply demand that the trust be cut to your level while you avoid real sacrifice.

## To Management

You have been clearer than before, and that is useful. But I still need hard numbers.

Do not tell me “deep but viable” unless you can define:
- the minimum viable plant footprint,
- the workforce reduction range,
- the brands and dealers that must go,
- and the post-restructuring debt load the business can carry under stress.

If you cannot state those thresholds, then you are asking the public to underwrite uncertainty.

I agree with you on one point: viability is not the same as maximum contraction. But viability does require genuine contraction. You do not get to preserve market share, political peace, and balance-sheet repair all at once.

## To the Neighbouring Governments

I understand your domestic concerns, and I agree that co-funding should buy enforceable commitments. But I will not fund a deal that freezes an inefficient footprint or turns your domestic requirements into a veto over necessary rationalization.

Here is the line:
- protected core domestic sites: potentially yes,
- blanket preservation of domestic capacity: no,
- co-funder equity: yes, but proportionate to real new money and real risk,
- domestic commitments that make the company insolvent: no funding from me.

If your minimum terms cannot coexist with a viable company, then your support is not rescuing the business; it is just changing the location of the losses.

## On the experts’ advice

The specialists have confirmed what I already expected:
- the company needs a materially smaller footprint,
- labour cost must come down,
- unsecured claims must take major haircuts,
- and a large trust floor can reintroduce insolvency unless offset elsewhere.

That is exactly why I am pressing for numbers instead of rhetoric.

## What I am prepared to consider

A structure along these lines:
- bondholders: debt-for-equity plus warrants, with a large haircut;
- retiree trust: equity-linked preferred instrument with a **modest, capped, deferred floor**;
- labour: meaningful workforce, work-rule, and benefit concessions;
- plants/brands/dealers: real rationalization, not cosmetic trimming;
- co-funders: only if they bring enforceable commitments and receive proportionate equity;
- process: consensual if it gets there fast, otherwise court-supervised.

## What I am not prepared to consider

- a trust solution that is effectively senior in substance,
- a footprint plan designed to save jobs first and viability second,
- a creditor package that leaves the company overlevered,
- or a deal that uses public funds to avoid hard choices.

## The question I now put to each party

I want each of you to answer in thresholds, not adjectives:

### To the Union and Retiree Trust
What is the **maximum present-value recovery** you would accept for the trust if it is given a real but capped floor?

### To the Bondholders
What haircut range and what mix of new equity/warrants would you accept if the trust gets a modest contingent floor?

### To Management
What is the **minimum viable footprint** in plant closures, workforce reduction, and debt load?

### To the Neighbouring Governments
What exact domestic production and employment commitments are the minimum for your funding, and how much equity do you expect in return?

If you cannot answer those questions in concrete terms, then you are not negotiating a rescue. You are negotiating for a better position in a failure.

And to be absolutely clear: I am prepared to withhold financing and move to the court-supervised route if that is what it takes to get to finality and viability.
