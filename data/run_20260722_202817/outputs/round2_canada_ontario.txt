We agree on the need for a **deep, final restructuring** and on the fact that this cannot be a taxpayer-funded delay. We also agree that management, labour, and creditors all need to take losses. On those points, there is no dispute.

Where I **do** disagree is on any outcome that treats domestic plants as optional collateral while other interests are protected.

### To the task force
I hear your insistence on viability, speed, and no open-ended public support. That is reasonable. But viability is **not** the same thing as cutting the domestic footprint to the bone and calling it discipline.

If public financing is being used, then it must support an **orderly restructuring that preserves a meaningful domestic production share and employment base**. I will not support a package where our taxpayers co-fund a rescue that shifts production away from our plants while foreign sites are better protected. That would be an industrial-policy failure, not a rescue.

I also reject the idea that domestic production commitments somehow “override” viability. They do not. But neither does viability override the need for **binding domestic commitments** when our money and our jurisdiction are on the line. If the company cannot remain viable with a meaningful domestic footprint, then the proposed restructuring is not balanced enough.

### To management
You are right that the company cannot survive with the old cost structure. But I am not persuaded by a version of “real restructuring” that simply loads the cost of adjustment onto our plants.

I agree on:
- a real balance-sheet reset,
- management accountability,
- a final deal that can survive market scrutiny.

I do **not** agree that the necessary footprint rationalization can be defined without regard to domestic production share. If we are co-funding, we need:
- enforceable domestic production commitments,
- a plant plan that preserves strategic domestic capacity,
- and an ownership position proportionate to our contribution.

That is not a side issue. It is the condition for our participation.

### To the union and retiree trust
I understand your position that the retiree trust was earned through decades of sacrifice, and I agree it should not be treated as a throwaway claim. But I cannot support a structure that gives the trust a materially superior recovery while asking us to fund the deal and accept domestic plant losses.

If the trust receives a durable instrument, then bondholders and other unsecured claimants must be treated on an economically coherent basis. I am not endorsing a hierarchy in which labour is protected, creditors are compressed, and co-funders are left paying for plant rationalization abroad while ours are exposed.

We are sympathetic to jobs and communities — that is exactly why we are here — but sympathy does not replace a binding production commitment.

### To bondholders
I accept your point that equal treatment matters and that you should not be singled out arbitrarily. I am not here to defend a politically convenient haircut on one class while insulating another.

But I also reject the implication that protecting domestic production is somehow a subsidy to labour at your expense. If co-funders are contributing fresh value, we are entitled to demand:
- a fair ownership stake,
- enforceable domestic investment/production commitments,
- and a restructuring that does not hollow out our industrial base.

If the deal requires creditors to take cuts, then that is part of the rescue. But those cuts should not be used to finance a footprint shift away from our plants.

### What I need to see
If this is going to work, I need concrete terms, not generalities:

- **Binding commitments on domestic production share**
- **Plant retention / investment commitments for supported sites**
- **A proportionate equity position for co-funders**
- **A restructuring that is deep enough to be viable without sacrificing our plants first**
- **An even-handed approach to unsecured recoveries that does not privilege one claimant group over co-funders**

### Bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund an orderly rescue. We are **not** prepared to pay for a restructuring that weakens our domestic industrial base or leaves us with a passive, undercompensated role.

If the package protects domestic production and gives us a proportional ownership stake, we can be constructive. If it does not, then we do not have a basis for funding.
