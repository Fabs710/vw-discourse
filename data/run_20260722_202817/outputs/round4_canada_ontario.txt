We agree with the broad premise that the company needs a **deep, final restructuring** and that endless bridge financing is not an option. We also agree that labour, creditors, and public funders all need to share the burden.

But I need to be direct: **we will not support a rescue that protects foreign or lower-priority plants while domestic plants are left exposed, or one that asks us to co-fund without a proportionate ownership stake.**

## Where we agree
- **Task force:** viability must come first; token cuts are not enough.
- **Management:** the company needs real operational and balance-sheet reset.
- **Bondholders:** they should not be arbitrarily singled out.
- **Union/trust:** workers and retirees should not be the only loss pool.

Those points are all valid.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic production cannot be treated as optional
To the task force and management: if we are putting in public money, we are not funding a generic corporate rescue. We are funding a restructuring that must **preserve a meaningful domestic industrial base**.

That means:
- binding domestic production commitments,
- binding employment commitments for supported sites,
- and no plan that shifts the burden onto our jurisdictions while protecting other plants for political convenience.

If “minimum viable footprint” means our domestic plants become the adjustment variable, then we do **not** support that deal.

### 2) Co-funding requires proportionate ownership
We are not prepared to be passive cash providers.

If we contribute fresh public capital and take the political risk of protecting domestic jobs, then we need a **proportionate equity position** in the restructured company. That is not a side issue. It is part of the bargain.

A symbolic or diluted stake would be unacceptable.

### 3) Protected domestic core, yes; blanket preservation, no
We are not asking that every site survive. We accept that some rationalization is necessary.

But there must be:
- a clearly identified protected domestic core,
- site-by-site commitments on production and investment,
- and closures only where they are genuinely necessary, not simply convenient.

A “minimum viable” plan that hollowes out our industrial base is not viable in the long run.

## On the trust and bondholder dispute
We do not take sides on labour history versus creditor equality. What we do insist on is this:

- the trust can be given a durable, equity-linked instrument if the economics are real,
- bondholders must take major losses,
- but **neither** of those outcomes can be financed by sacrificing domestic plants.

If the trust floor or creditor package forces deeper cuts in our regions while other sites are protected, then that is not a fair restructuring from our perspective.

## On the experts
The experts keep pointing to the same trade-off: the more value is reserved for the trust or creditors, the less room there is for footprint preservation and domestic commitments.

That is precisely why we need the domestic side protected in the final allocation. We are not going to finance a deal that simply reallocates industrial capacity away from our jurisdictions.

## What we need now
Before we can support this, we need concrete answers:

1. **Minimum domestic footprint**
   - Which domestic plants are protected?
   - What production volumes will they carry?
   - What investment is committed to them?

2. **Ownership**
   - What equity stake do co-funders receive?
   - How does it reflect our contribution and risk?

3. **Compatibility with viability**
   - Are the domestic commitments truly financeable without reintroducing insolvency?
   - If not, then the proposal is not workable.

## Our line
We can support an orderly restructuring with:
- meaningful domestic production and employment commitments,
- a proportionate co-funder ownership stake,
- deep but workable sacrifice across labour and creditors,
- and a finality-oriented process, consensual if possible, court-supervised if necessary.

We cannot support:
- domestic closures while foreign plants are protected,
- co-funding without commensurate equity,
- or a “viability” argument that simply exports the pain to our jurisdictions.

If those conditions are not met, then we do not have a basis to fund the rescue.
