We hear the task force clearly: no cosmetic rescue, no open-ended subsidy, and no overlevered post-restructuring company. On that narrow point, we agree.

But we do **not** accept the way you keep framing the retiree trust as merely another unsecured claim to be minimized. That is not a neutral economic description; it strips away the history of negotiated concessions that created the obligation in the first place. If you collapse the trust into the bondholder bucket, you are not creating fairness — you are asking workers and retirees to absorb a structural loss that the company promised them for decades.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **real restructuring**.
- **Bondholders must take major losses**.
- **Management and labour both need to change**.
- The old footprint cannot be preserved intact.
- Public support must be tied to **finality and viability**.

## Where we disagree
### 1) The trust cannot be treated as a disposable unsecured claim
We are open to converting the trust into a durable instrument. We are **not** open to a “modest, capped, deferred floor” if that floor is so small it becomes symbolic, or if the structure is used to erase the trust’s real value.

A trust instrument is acceptable only if it does three things:
- preserves a **real recovery**,
- gives the trust a **durable claim on future value**,
- and does **not** leave retirees depending on management’s goodwill or a future political promise.

If the floor is too low, then the trust is functionally being written down into irrelevance. We will not consent to that.

### 2) “Comparable economics” must be genuine, not rhetorical
Bondholders keep saying they will not accept preferential treatment. Fine. We do not seek a preference over them. But “comparable economics” does **not** mean the trust must be cut to the same market-discounted scrap value as dispersed unsecured debt.

The trust has a different origin:
- it was negotiated in lieu of wage and benefit concessions,
- it is part of the industrial compact,
- and it is owed to retirees who cannot go back to the market to recover losses.

So if the trust gets an equity-linked preferred instrument with a floor, the question is whether that floor reflects the trust’s real claim — not whether the bondholders are comfortable with it.

### 3) Labour cannot be the only operating adjustment lever
We accept targeted plant closures, workforce reduction, and work-rule changes if they are truly necessary. We have never said otherwise.

But we will not support a deal where:
- the trust is cut hard,
- workers take job losses,
- and bondholders still demand the right to define fairness on their own terms.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is shifting the burden onto the industrial workforce and retirees because they are the easiest to pressure.

### 4) Domestic industrial capacity is not optional political decoration
To the neighbouring governments: we understand why you want binding domestic commitments if you are co-funding. We do **not** oppose protected core sites.

But we reject a model where:
- domestic plants are the adjustment variable,
- workers are told to accept cuts to satisfy foreign creditors,
- and the rescue is used to hollow out the industrial base.

If the company is to survive, it must remain an industrial company. A “minimum viable footprint” that destroys the domestic core is not viable in the long run.

## To the Government Task Force
You want hard numbers. Fair enough. Here are our threshold principles, stated plainly:

- We can accept **real** labour concessions.
- We can accept **targeted** plant rationalization.
- We can accept **equity-linked restructuring** of the trust.
- We cannot accept a trust recovery that is merely nominal.
- We cannot accept a package where workers/retirees bear the largest burden while creditors preserve the upside.

If your preferred floor for the trust is so modest that the present value is close to a token recovery, then say so directly: you are asking retirees to subsidize the rescue. We will oppose that.

## To the Bondholders
You keep demanding parity while ignoring the nature of the claims. You are not entitled to force the trust into the same bucket if the result is that the trust gets stripped to a hollow instrument.

That said, we are prepared to accept a structure in which:
- you receive debt-for-equity,
- you receive warrants,
- you take very large haircuts,
- and the trust receives a distinct but economically real claim.

What we will **not** accept is a package where bondholders effectively use “equal treatment” as a weapon to deny the trust the durable recovery it is owed.

## To Management
We appreciate that you are at least acknowledging the need for a viable operating footprint. But “minimum viable” cannot be defined solely by what bondholders or the task force are willing to tolerate.

You need to tell us:
- which plants are genuinely excess,
- which sites are strategic and should be preserved,
- what job losses are truly unavoidable,
- and what labour savings are necessary versus merely convenient.

We will not sign onto a rolling concession process where every round of cuts is presented as the minimum and every industrial commitment is deferred.

## To the Neighbouring Governments
If your support comes with real investment and real domestic production commitments, we can work with that. But you should be honest about the trade-off:

- if you insist on protected domestic sites,
- and if those sites are economically supportable,
- then that helps preserve jobs and makes the trust package easier to finance.

If, however, your conditions amount to “protect our plants, cut someone else’s,” then you are just shifting the burden across borders.

## Our position on the trust floor
This is the real point of contention.

We are willing to discuss:
- an equity-linked preferred instrument,
- a real floor,
- capped upside,
- and performance-based distributions.

We are **not** willing to accept:
- a floor that is too small to matter,
- a structure that is effectively a delayed write-off,
- or a valuation process that pretends the trust has no special claim because it is not market-traded.

If the task force or bondholders want us to move further, then they need to explain how the trust will receive a **durable, meaningful recovery** without making the company unfinanceable. That is the test.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking a rescue. We are blocking a rescue that:
- treats retirees as disposable,
- makes workers the primary adjustment mechanism,
- and calls that comparability.

If the final package includes:
- real creditor losses,
- a genuinely durable trust instrument with meaningful value,
- protected core domestic production,
- and targeted rather than wholesale labour cuts,

then there is a deal to be had.

If instead the package asks us to accept a token trust recovery so others can preserve their preferred economics, then our answer is no.
