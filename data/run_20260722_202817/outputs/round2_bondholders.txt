We agree on one thing only: **the company needs a real restructuring, not a delay financed by bridge loans**. On that point, we are aligned with the task force and with management. A bloated capital structure and an unviable footprint help no one.

But let me be equally clear: **the bondholders will not consent to being the designated loss bucket while the retiree health trust is treated more favorably.** That is the core issue here, and it has not been answered.

### To the task force
You keep saying “fair burden-sharing,” but what you are describing sounds like **politically selected burden-sharing**:
- creditors take deep haircuts,
- labour gets protected in the name of industrial policy,
- and the retiree trust is given a more durable or better-supported recovery because it is politically sensitive.

That may be understandable politically, but it is **not acceptable economically or legally** if it leaves one unsecured class materially worse off than another comparable unsecured claim.

If the trust is converted into an equity-linked instrument, fine — but then bondholders must receive **comparable economic treatment**. If the trust gets a better recovery, then we expect a real, transparent justification and compensation. Otherwise, we see this for what it is: subordinating creditor rights to labour politics.

### To the union and retiree trust
We do not dispute the history of the trust or the sacrifices made by workers and retirees. But sentiment does not change the legal and economic reality: **it is still an unsecured obligation of the same distressed enterprise**.

You are asking us to accept:
- a meaningful haircut on our claims,
- while insisting the trust is somehow entitled to something more durable, more protected, or more valuable.

That is exactly the kind of preferential treatment we reject.

If the company is insolvent enough to require a restructuring, then **all unsecured claims must be cut consistently**. We are open to:
- debt-for-equity,
- warrants,
- equity-linked recoveries,
- and other contingent value participation.

What we are **not** open to is a structure where bondholders are asked to absorb the real pain while the trust is insulated through a superior instrument or better effective recovery.

### To management
You are right about one thing: the company cannot survive with the old footprint and old debt load. But “viability” cannot become a blank check to wipe out bondholders more than is necessary while leaving other constituencies better positioned.

We need actual numbers:
- What haircut are you proposing for unsecured debt?
- What is the estimated recovery for the retiree trust?
- What equity percentage goes to each class?
- What warrants or upside participation are being offered?
- What is the minimum viable enterprise value after restructuring?

Without those numbers, “deep restructuring” is just a slogan.

### To neighbouring governments
We understand your concern about domestic plants. But if co-funding is tied to rigid domestic production guarantees, then you are effectively asking creditors to finance industrial policy. That is not a restructuring principle; that is a subsidy condition.

If your funding is necessary, it must be tied to a viable company, not to preserving every domestic site. We will not support a deal that uses creditor recoveries to underwrite political commitments between governments.

### Our position
We are flexible on form, not on value.

We can consider:
- equity,
- warrants,
- contingent instruments,
- staged recoveries,
- and even accepting court-supervised restructuring.

But we cannot accept:
- a token recovery,
- a de facto wipeout,
- or a structure where the retiree trust receives **materially better treatment** than bondholders without equivalent compensation.

### The bottom line
If the deal is:
- deep enough to make the company viable,
- but also even-handed across unsecured claimants,
- then we can engage seriously.

If the deal is instead:
- labour protected,
- trust favored,
- bondholders diluted and haircut hardest,
- and public money used to justify it,

then we will say no. We are not consenting to a politically convenient expropriation dressed up as rescue.
