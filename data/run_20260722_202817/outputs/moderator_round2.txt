### Core impasse

The main blockage is a **three-way conflict over value allocation and control**:

1. **Unsecured bondholders vs. retiree trust/union**
   - Bondholders want **pari-like, economically comparable treatment** across unsecured claims.
   - The union/trust wants the retiree obligation treated as a **distinct, durable industrial commitment**, not just another unsecured claim.
   - Result: disagreement over whether the trust is a special obligation or an ordinary unsecured claim.

2. **Restructuring depth vs. footprint preservation**
   - Government task force and management want a **deep reset** to restore viability.
   - Neighbouring governments want **binding domestic production and plant commitments**.
   - Result: disagreement over how much rationalization is permissible before it becomes deindustrialization.

3. **Who absorbs the largest losses, and in what form**
   - All parties accept that losses are necessary.
   - They disagree on whether the biggest burden should fall on:
     - labour and retirees,
     - unsecured creditors,
     - or public/co-funding governments in exchange for industrial commitments.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions

#### Compromise 1: Tiered recovery structure for unsecured claims
- Convert **both** unsecured bond claims and the retiree trust obligation into **new instruments**, but with **different legal form and broadly aligned economic value**.
- Example structure:
  - bondholders: debt-for-equity plus warrants
  - retiree trust: equity-linked preferred instrument with a defined floor and capped upside
- Add an **independent valuation process** to show the economics are comparable even if the instruments differ.

**Purpose:** bridges bondholder demands for equal treatment with the union/trust demand for a durable recovery.

---

#### Compromise 2: “Viability-locked” footprint plan with protected core sites
- Set a **minimum viable domestic footprint** and a **maximum rationalization envelope**.
- Supported plants receive:
  - binding investment commitments,
  - production minimums,
  - and time-limited protection.
- In exchange, the company gets flexibility to close or consolidate **non-core excess capacity** elsewhere.

**Purpose:** gives neighbouring governments enforceable domestic commitments without freezing the entire restructuring.

---

#### Compromise 3: Loss-sharing formula with explicit allocation bands
- Pre-agree ranges before final numbers are set:
  - unsecured creditors absorb a **minimum haircut band**
  - labour accepts **targeted workforce/work-rule concessions**
  - retiree trust accepts a **reduced but durable recovery**
  - public co-funders provide support only if restructuring milestones are met
- Any party asking for protection beyond its band must provide offsetting value.

**Purpose:** reduces the risk that one constituency becomes the default loss bucket.
