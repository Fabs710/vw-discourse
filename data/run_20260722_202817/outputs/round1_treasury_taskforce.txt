The government task force’s position is straightforward:

We will support a restructuring only if it is **deep enough, fast enough, and credible enough to produce a viable company**. That means:

- **No open-ended taxpayer subsidy**
- **No preservation of an unviable cost structure**
- **No plan that simply rolls debt and postpones the problem**
- **No rescue in which labour, creditors, and management escape meaningful sacrifice**

Our priorities are:

1. **Viability**
   - The company must emerge able to survive on its own in normal market conditions.
   - That requires real reductions in fixed costs, a rationalized brand and plant footprint, and a balance sheet that can be financed without repeated government support.

2. **Fair burden-sharing**
   - Management, labour, and unsecured creditors all have to take losses.
   - We will not ask taxpayers to bear the entire cost while private claimants are protected.

3. **Speed and finality**
   - The 60-day window is real.
   - We want a clean, orderly resolution that contains systemic damage and gives the company a restart point.

4. **Minimal credible public cost**
   - Public funds are available only to bridge the company through restructuring, not to finance delay or denial.
   - If voluntary agreement fails, we are prepared to use a court-supervised process to impose terms.

What we will argue against:

- Any proposal that leaves the company too bloated to be viable
- Any creditor or labour position that demands near-par treatment at public expense
- Any attempt to use the deadline to extract concessions from the state while avoiding real restructuring
- Any plan that depends on indefinite co-funding without firm commitments and conditions

What we are open to:

- A consensual deal if it delivers the same economic result as a court process
- Different instruments for the burden-sharing, provided the depth is real
- A viable equity structure for the restructured company, including new ownership for those who contribute fresh value and absorb losses

Bottom line:  
**We are here to force a viable restructuring, not to preserve the status quo.** If the parties can deliver deep concessions quickly, we will support it. If not, we will pursue the court-supervised path.
