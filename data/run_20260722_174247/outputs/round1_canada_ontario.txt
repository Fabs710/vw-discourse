We are prepared to co-fund this rescue, but only on terms that protect the domestic industrial footprint and give us a real partnership position in the restructured company.

### Where we agree
- **Viability has to come first.** A weakly reworked balance sheet is just a delayed collapse.
- **Shared sacrifice is necessary.** Labour, creditors, management, and the public all need to take pain.
- **Speed matters.** We cannot let the 60-day window run into paralysis.
- **A leaner footprint is unavoidable.** Not every plant, brand, or dealer can be preserved.

### Where we disagree
I want to be explicit: **we will not support a rescue that shifts production and jobs out of our jurisdiction while asking us to put in money.** If domestic plants are closed while foreign operations are protected, that is not a partnership and we will not treat it as one.

That means:

- **No co-funding without binding commitments on domestic production and employment.**
- **No arrangement where we pay in and get only vague assurances.**
- **No structure that leaves us with risk but no proportionate ownership or say.**

### On the union’s position
We understand and broadly share the goal of preserving jobs and the retiree trust in a durable form. We support protecting the workforce, and we are sympathetic to the argument that retirees should not be left with a hollow claim. But the trust’s treatment cannot come at the expense of the domestic plant base.

If the restructuring preserves employment and production here, we can be flexible on the instrument used for the trust. If it does not, then the deal is not acceptable to us.

### On management
We agree with the need for a viable company and new leadership discipline. But viability cannot be defined only as financial cleanup. A “successful” restructuring that hollowed out the domestic industrial base would be a failure from our perspective.

### On bondholders
We are not opposed to meaningful creditor sacrifice. That said, if unsecured bondholders demand a better package than the domestic workforce or expect the public to fund the rescue while they defend every dollar of recovery, that is not balanced and not credible.

### What would move us
We need three things in the final package:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - Specific plant and employment protections.
   - A clear minimum domestic production share.
   - Real consequences if the commitments are broken.

2. **A proportionate ownership stake**
   - Our co-funding must be reflected in equity or equivalent governance rights.
   - We are not asking to control the company, but we do require a stake proportionate to our contribution and risk.

3. **An orderly restructuring**
   - We prefer a consensual solution if it delivers these protections.
   - If consensus cannot deliver them, then the court route is less relevant than the substance: we will not endorse any process that strips out domestic capacity.

### Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- **We will co-fund only if domestic plants and jobs are protected in binding terms.**
- **We expect a proportionate ownership position for our contribution.**
- **We support a deep, orderly restructuring, but not one that externalizes the cost onto our workers and regions.**

If the current direction is toward a deal that protects creditors or foreign production while sacrificing domestic capacity, we will not support it.
