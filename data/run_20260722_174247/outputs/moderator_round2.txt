### 1) What has been agreed
- **A real restructuring is necessary**; no one supports a cosmetic fix.
- **The company must emerge leaner and viable**, with a materially improved balance sheet.
- **Some plant, brand, dealer, or capacity reductions are likely necessary** if they are uneconomic.
- **Management change and stronger governance** are broadly accepted.
- **The retiree trust must receive real economic value**, not a token promise.
- **Unsecured bondholders must take meaningful losses** and should receive some form of upside participation.
- **Labour concessions are needed**, including flexibility and some relief on legacy burdens.
- **Public co-funding is possible only with conditions**, and if provided, it should come with ownership/governance rights.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment:**  
  The dispute is whether the trust can receive a more protected/durable instrument without being too rich relative to other unsecured claims.
- **Bondholder recovery:**  
  Bondholders reject a near-wipeout and want treatment comparable to the trust if the trust gets a stronger instrument.
- **Labour cuts:**  
  The task force wants deeper labour and legacy relief; the union accepts concessions but rejects disproportionate sacrifice.
- **Domestic plant/job protection:**  
  Neighbouring governments want binding domestic commitments and oppose any deal that shifts the burden onto domestic plants and jobs.
- **Ownership/governance allocation:**  
  All sides agree it must reflect sacrifice, but they differ sharply on who gets how much and under what conditions.
- **Process leverage:**  
  The task force is willing to use court-supervised restructuring; others see that threat as pressure, not a resolution.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to size the retiree trust instrument** so it is meaningful but does not recreate insolvency.
- **How deep bondholder haircuts must be** relative to the trust’s recovery.
- **How much labour cost relief is required** versus how many jobs/plants can realistically be preserved.
- **Whether domestic production commitments can be binding** without making the restructuring unviable.
- **How much public money is needed** and what ownership/governance rights each public funder would receive.
- **How to balance viability against constituency protection** when those goals conflict.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to a **single integrated term-sheet range**:
- proposed **enterprise value / liquidity target**,
- **recovery ranges** for the trust and unsecured bondholders,
- **specific labour concessions**,
- **defined plant/brand/dealer actions**,
- and **public co-funding + governance terms** tied to measurable commitments.

In short: the next step is to test one coherent package against viability, rather than continue debating each claim in isolation.
