The government task force’s position is straightforward:

We will support a restructuring only if it is deep enough, fast enough, and fair enough to make the company viable without turning the public purse into an open-ended subsidy.

Our priorities are:

1. **Viability first**  
   The company must emerge with a cost structure and balance sheet that can actually survive in the market. We will not finance a weakly reworked version of the same problem.

2. **Shared sacrifice**  
   Management, labour, and unsecured creditors must all absorb real losses. Taxpayers cannot be the only party taking risk while private claimants are protected.

3. **Speed and finality**  
   The company has only a limited runway. We want a clean, credible deal within the deadline, or we will use the court-supervised route to impose one.

4. **Minimum public cost**  
   Any further support must be tightly conditioned and limited to what is necessary to preserve systemic value and orderly restructuring.

5. **No preservation of unsustainable legacy claims**  
   Retiree obligations, labour costs, and unsecured debt all have to be addressed in a way that reflects economic reality. If those claims are left largely intact, the plan is not acceptable.

What we will argue for:

- substantial reductions in labour and legacy obligations,
- meaningful haircuts for unsecured bondholders,
- a leaner footprint in plants, brands, and dealers,
- new leadership and governance discipline,
- and a credible path to profitability that allows the public to exit.

What we will argue against:

- any proposal that simply extends bridge financing without fixing the underlying business,
- any plan that shields creditors or labour from real pain while asking the state to absorb the losses,
- and any delay tactic that consumes the 60-day window without producing a durable restructuring.

We are open to an out-of-court deal, but only if it delivers the same depth and speed we would require in court. If it does not, we are prepared to proceed through the expedited court process.
