The Union and the Retiree Health Trust are prepared to do our part, but we will not sign off on a plan that treats workers and retirees as the primary shock absorber for everyone else’s mistakes.

Let me be clear on where we stand:

### Where we agree
- **Viability matters.** We understand the company cannot survive on wishful thinking.
- **Shared sacrifice is necessary.** We have already accepted concessions in earlier rounds, and we are not pretending the old structure can remain unchanged.
- **Speed matters.** A drawn-out collapse would be catastrophic for workers, retirees, suppliers, and the entire industrial base.

### Where we disagree
- **We reject the idea that labor and retiree obligations can simply be gutted while bondholders and management escape with too much.**
- **We reject any plan that destroys the retiree health trust or reduces it to a hollow promise.**
- **We reject a restructuring that sacrifices the workforce wholesale to protect creditor recoveries.**

The retiree health obligation is not some optional legacy nuisance. It is a real claim, earned over decades, and it has to be honored in a form that is durable. If the answer is just “take less and hope for the best,” that is not acceptable.

### What would be workable
We are open to a serious restructuring if it includes all of the following:

1. **A durable claim for the retiree trust**
   - The trust must receive real value, not merely an unsecured promise.
   - Equity, preferred equity, or another secured/structured instrument that gives the trust an actual stake in the restructured company is the minimum serious option.
   - If cash funding is limited, then the trust’s claim must be converted into something enforceable and insulated from future raids.

2. **Job and plant protections**
   - We are prepared to discuss plant rationalization, but not a mass liquidation of productive capacity.
   - Preserving as many jobs and facilities as possible is essential to any legitimate deal.

3. **Real sacrifice from bondholders and management**
   - Unsecured creditors must take meaningful haircuts.
   - Management must not be insulated from the consequences of failure.

4. **A viable labor framework, not a wage war**
   - We can discuss wage and work-rule flexibility where needed.
   - But the core employment structure cannot be dismantled simply to make the numbers easier for creditors.

5. **A credible ownership structure**
   - The post-restructuring equity cannot be allocated as if labor and retirees were junior afterthoughts.
   - The trust and the workforce need a meaningful position in the future of the company if we are expected to accept sacrifices now.

### On the process
We are not attached to out-of-court for its own sake. But we will not endorse a consensual deal that is just a dressed-up cramdown by another name. If the task force wants a real agreement, it has to reflect shared pain and shared upside.

If creditors insist on demanding deep concessions from workers while preserving too much of their own upside, then yes, the court-supervised route may be the only path left. We do not fear the process; we fear a one-sided outcome.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to bargain hard and quickly. But the line is simple:

- **No destruction of the retiree trust**
- **No wholesale sacrifice of workers for creditor recovery**
- **No plan that leaves labor and retirees holding the bag while others control the future**

If the task force wants our signature, we need a restructuring that preserves jobs, converts the retiree obligation into something durable, and makes creditors and management share the pain in a real way.
