We are ready to move from generalities to a term-sheet discussion, but let me be equally clear: **unsecured bondholders will not sign up to a structure that gives the retiree trust materially better treatment while leaving us with a token recovery.** That is not shared sacrifice; it is selective protection dressed up as restructuring.

## Where we agree

### To the government task force
You are right on one point: the company cannot be left with an unsustainable balance sheet. We accept real losses, and we understand speed matters.

### To management
We agree that this cannot be a cosmetic fix and that the old structure is dead. We also agree that new governance and a leaner footprint are necessary.

### To the union and retiree trust
We agree the retiree obligation is a real claim and cannot simply be ignored. We are not arguing for denial of the trust’s rights.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot be “more durable” than unsecured bondholders without justification and compensation
The advisor’s discussion of equity, preferred equity, and contingent value rights is useful. But let’s be direct: if the trust gets a **protected, senior-like, or richer economic instrument** while dispersed unsecured creditors get a much weaker package, that is unequal treatment of similarly situated unsecured claims.

If the trust is to receive a better instrument than bonds, then:
- the difference must be narrowly justified,
- and there must be corresponding compensation to unsecured creditors.

Absent that, we will regard it as a political preference, not an economically or legally defensible allocation.

### 2) “Durable” cannot mean bondholders bear the real haircut while others are insulated
The task force keeps saying unsecured debt must take “deep” losses. Fine. But deep is not a blank check for confiscation.

We will not accept:
- a near-wipeout of unsecured bonds,
- while the retiree trust is converted into a materially better claim,
- and while management and other constituencies preserve more upside than we do.

If someone is going to receive equity, warrants, or a structured claim with genuine upside, then unsecured bondholders must not be left behind as the residual dumping ground.

### 3) The court threat cannot be used to force an unfair exchange
We hear the repeated warning that a court-supervised process can impose terms. That may be true procedurally, but it does not answer the substantive question: **what is fair and value-maximizing?**

If the proposed out-of-court deal is worse for us than a court-imposed alternative, we will not be stampeded into consenting. We will take our chances. The threat of process is not itself a reason to accept bad economics.

## Our position on the proposed structures

### Retiree trust
We do **not** oppose the trust receiving real value. But if it receives:
- preferred equity,
- a protected structured instrument,
- or anti-dilution features,

then unsecured bondholders must receive **comparable economic participation** in the restructured company.

We are open to:
- common equity,
- warrants,
- contingent value rights,
- or a mix,
but only if the total package produces a **meaningful recovery**, not symbolic participation.

### Bondholder recovery
We need a recovery range that is not a joke. At this stage, we are not naming a percentage, but let me be explicit:
- **token equity is not enough;**
- **a near wipeout is not acceptable;**
- **and parity with the retiree trust, or compensation for any difference, is non-negotiable.**

### Plants, brands, and dealers
We support a leaner footprint if it is genuinely necessary. But do not use operational cuts as a pretext to transfer value away from creditors. If major value is being sacrificed, then all unsecured constituencies should share that sacrifice proportionately.

### Ownership and governance
If we are forced to absorb large losses, then unsecured bondholders must receive a meaningful ownership stake or equivalent upside rights in the reorganized company. We will not fund everyone else’s recovery and walk away empty-handed.

## Minimum acceptable terms from our side

A deal must include:

1. **Meaningful recovery for unsecured bondholders**
   - not a symbolic slice,
   - not a recovery that is materially worse than the retiree trust without compensation.

2. **Equal treatment across unsecured claims, or compensation for any difference**
   - if the trust gets a richer instrument, we get offsetting value.

3. **Real upside participation**
   - equity and/or warrants with actual economic value.

4. **Speed and certainty**
   - because delay destroys value for us as well.

5. **No misuse of the court threat**
   - if the court route is used, it must still respect basic fairness among unsecureds.

## On the “durable trust” idea
We are not opposed in principle to a retiree claim being converted into something more durable than bare unsecured paper. But durability is not a free pass to disadvantage other unsecureds.

The practical middle ground, if you insist on calling it that, is this:
- if the trust gets protected equity or preferred equity,
- then unsecured bondholders must receive a **comparable economic package**, even if the exact form differs.

That is the only way this is not simply a political allocation.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to accept losses, and we are prepared to move quickly. But we will not accept:
- a confiscatory exchange,
- materially worse treatment relative to the retiree trust without compensation,
- or a plan that leaves bondholders as the residual sacrifice zone while other constituencies receive the better instruments.

If you want our consent, bring forward a structure with **real recovery, real upside, and equal treatment**. Otherwise, we will reserve all rights and proceed on the assumption that the court-supervised process may be our better option.
