We are now at the point where slogans have to become numbers and structure.

Let me be equally direct: **I am not prepared to finance a plan that preserves too much of the old burden and asks taxpayers to backstop the rest.** If the concessions are not deep enough, we will use the court-supervised route to impose them.

## My position in term-sheet form

### 1) Debt and legacy obligations
The company must emerge with a materially lighter balance sheet. That means:
- **unsecured bondholders take a deep haircut,**
- **the retiree obligation is restructured into something the business can actually carry,**
- and **management accepts permanent governance changes and accountability.**

I am not naming a precise haircut percentage yet, but I am saying plainly that **symbolic losses are not acceptable**. If unsecured creditors are still being paid near-par while the public finances the rescue, the plan fails.

### 2) Retiree trust
I agree with the union that retirees are not an afterthought, and I agree with management that the company cannot survive if the trust remains in its current form.

So the trust must be given **real, durable value**, but not a claim that cripples the company. A workable structure could include:
- a meaningful equity stake or equivalent instrument,
- some form of protected, structured recovery,
- but **not** an arrangement that leaves the operating company insolvent in practice.

I will not support an “honor the trust in full and let the balance sheet sort itself out” solution. That is not a restructuring; that is denial.

### 3) Labour concessions
I need real labour cost relief:
- wage and work-rule flexibility,
- lower legacy burdens,
- and plant rationalization where capacity is uneconomic.

I am not asking for a demolition of the workforce, but I am also not accepting a plan where labour gives only marginally while creditors and taxpayers absorb the real loss.

### 4) Bondholder recovery
Bondholders should receive **real but reduced** recovery, with upside participation if they are taking a serious haircut. They should not be wiped out, but they also cannot expect to be treated as if the old capital structure still stands.

If the committee wants a seat at the table, it needs to accept that:
- the old debt is not sacrosanct,
- and any upside comes after the company is made viable, not before.

### 5) Plants, brands, dealers
I support a leaner footprint. We will not fund preservation of every facility or franchise.

The right test is not political convenience; it is viability. If a plant, brand, or dealer network is structurally uneconomic, it must go. If a domestic operation is essential to the turnaround, we can discuss preserving it, but I will not accept domestic commitments that freeze the industrial map regardless of economics.

### 6) Co-funding by neighbouring governments
We are open to co-funding, but only if it is:
- **real money**, not symbolic support,
- tied to **specific, enforceable commitments**,
- and accompanied by a **proportionate ownership/governance stake**.

I do agree that if they contribute materially, they should have a proportionate stake. But that stake cannot be allowed to dictate a nonviable operating footprint.

### 7) Ownership and governance
The post-restructuring equity must reflect actual sacrifice.
- Public capital should be protected.
- Private claimants taking losses should share in upside.
- Management should not be rewarded for failure.
- I am open to equity for the retiree trust, labour, bondholders, and co-funders, but only as part of a viable package.

## Where I disagree with each of you

### To the union and retiree trust
You keep saying the trust must not be “gutted.” I understand that. But if the trust is preserved too richly, someone else is forced to carry the burden, and that someone becomes the taxpayer unless bondholders and labour take enough pain.

So my line is this: **durable, yes; untouchable, no.**  
If you want real value, help make the company viable.

### To bondholders
You are right that you should not be singled out unfairly relative to comparable unsecured claims. But let’s be clear: **unsecured is unsecured.** In a rescue of this kind, that means real losses. The public is not here to subsidize your full recovery.

If you want better treatment, show me a plan with actual sacrifice elsewhere — not just rhetoric about fairness.

### To management
You have moved in the right direction, and the change in leadership was necessary. But I am not giving management credit for admitting the problem after the company was driven to the edge. The standard is not regret; it is whether you can now deliver a credible, faster, deeper restructuring.

### To neighbouring governments
I hear your demand for domestic plant and employment protection. I will not dismiss it, but I will not let it override viability. If you want a stake, it must be paired with realistic industrial commitments, not a freeze on restructuring.

## My minimum acceptable direction
A deal, if we do one consensually, must include:
- deep debt reduction,
- a genuinely reworked retiree trust,
- meaningful labour concessions,
- significant creditor haircuts,
- selective plant/brand/dealer cuts,
- and ownership allocated to reflect sacrifice and new capital.

If we cannot get that within the deadline, I will not extend open-ended support. We will proceed through the court-supervised route and impose the necessary terms.

## One clear message
I am not here to preserve the old company with new labels. I am here to finance a **viable** company, with **shared losses**, and with a path for the public to get out.

If the next round is just another request to delay hard choices, I will treat that as confirmation that the court route is necessary.
