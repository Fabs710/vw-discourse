### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad agreement that **speed matters** and that the 60-day window is tight.
- All parties accept some form of **shared sacrifice** is necessary.
- There is general support for **new leadership/governance discipline**.
- Most agree a **leaner footprint** is likely unavoidable, including some reduction in plants, brands, dealers, or other operations.
- There is agreement that a workable outcome must preserve **viability** and avoid outright collapse.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment**
  - Union wants a durable, meaningful claim.
  - Bondholders want no materially better treatment for the trust than for other unsecured claims.
  - Management and government say the obligation must be reworked if needed for viability.
- **Size and distribution of sacrifice**
  - Government wants deep cuts from labour, retirees, and unsecured creditors.
  - Union resists workers/retirees being the primary shock absorber.
  - Bondholders resist being singled out for extreme haircuts.
- **Domestic plant and job protection**
  - Neighbouring governments require binding domestic commitments.
  - Government task force and management prioritize viability and may support closure/rationalization if needed.
- **Ownership and control in the restructured company**
  - Union and neighbouring governments want meaningful stakes/rights.
  - Bondholders want real upside if they take losses.
  - Government wants public support tightly conditioned with eventual exit.
- **Process**
  - All parties say they prefer speed, but there is no agreement on whether an **out-of-court deal** can achieve acceptable depth and fairness, or whether the **court route** is needed.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- How to balance **retiree protection** against **bondholder recovery** and **company viability**.
- How much **labour/benefit reduction** is required versus how much **creditor haircut** is acceptable.
- Which plants/brands/dealers can be cut without violating **domestic employment commitments**.
- How to structure **public and neighbour-government funding** in exchange for equity, governance, or operational guarantees.
- Whether the company can support a **durable trust instrument** for retirees without making the operating business unsustainable.
- What level of **upside participation** each constituency receives in return for concessions.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from general positions to a **term-sheet style exchange**:
  1. target reductions in debt and legacy obligations,
  2. proposed treatment of the retiree trust,
  3. specific labour concessions,
  4. specific creditor recovery range,
  5. domestic plant/job commitments,
  6. ownership/governance allocations,
  7. decision on out-of-court vs. court path.
- Each party should state its **minimum acceptable terms** and the **one or two concessions** it could make to close a deal.
