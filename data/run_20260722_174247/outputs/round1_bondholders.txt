We agree on one core point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic patch.** If the balance sheet is left too heavy, everyone loses more in a collapse. We are not here to defend the old capital structure.

But let me be equally clear: **“shared sacrifice” cannot mean unsecured bondholders are asked to absorb a near-wipeout while other constituencies preserve outsized protection or political favor.**

### Where we agree
- **Viability matters.** A dead company yields no recovery.
- **Speed matters.** Time is destroying value.
- **Management should not be protected.** New leadership and governance discipline are appropriate.
- **Some reduction in footprint is unavoidable.** Plants, brands, and dealers must be rationalized if they are uneconomic.

### Where we disagree
The current framing is too one-sided against unsecured creditors.

#### 1) Bondholders must not be the residual dumping ground
We accept meaningful losses. We do **not** accept being singled out for extreme haircuts while the retiree trust receives proportionally better treatment simply because it is politically sensitive. That is not “fairness,” it is selective priority dressed up as public policy.

If the retiree trust is going to receive a materially stronger package than other unsecured claims, then that requires a real justification and compensation elsewhere. Otherwise, equal unsecured claims are being treated unequally.

#### 2) The retiree trust cannot be insulated by forcing bondholders to fund it
We understand the trust is an important obligation. But if the solution is to convert its claim into a more durable instrument while leaving bondholders with a token recovery, that is not a balanced restructuring. We will not accept a plan where workers and retirees are protected at the expense of ordinary bondholders who are also legitimate creditors, including retail savers.

#### 3) “Court-supervised” cannot become a threat to impose confiscatory terms
The fact that a court process may be available does not justify using it to force a deeply unfair outcome. If the only reason for accepting a deal is fear of a worse outcome in court, then the process has already become coercive. We will not volunteer to be undercompensated simply because the task force has leverage.

### What would be necessary
If you want our consent, the package has to include:
- **meaningful recovery for unsecured bondholders, not a symbolic one;**
- **equal treatment relative to other unsecured claims, especially the retiree trust, unless there is compensating value;**
- **participation in upside** if we are being asked to swallow a large haircut now;
- **speed and certainty**, because delay is harmful to us as well.

We are open to forms other than cash:
- equity,
- warrants,
- a meaningful combination of both,
- or a structured claim that gives real upside if the turnaround works.

But what we will not accept is a “rescue” that effectively wipes out unsecured creditors while preserving more favorable treatment for other unsecured constituencies.

### On process
We are not opposed to an out-of-court deal in principle. In fact, if it preserves value and gives us a defensible recovery, we would prefer it. But if the out-of-court proposal is just a prepackaged transfer of value away from bondholders, we will not bless it.

And if the matter goes to court, then the burden is on the task force and company to show why the distribution they propose is fair and legally sustainable. We are not assuming that a court outcome will automatically be worse for us. It may be, but that depends on the plan.

### Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- **No confiscatory exchange**
- **No materially worse treatment for unsecured bondholders than comparable unsecured claims without compensation**
- **No token equity that only gives the appearance of participation**
- **Yes to real sacrifice, but shared sacrifice means all stakeholders, not just dispersed creditors**

If you want our support, bring forward a proposal with **real recovery, real upside, and equal treatment**. Otherwise, we will reserve all rights and take our chances in the court-supervised process.
