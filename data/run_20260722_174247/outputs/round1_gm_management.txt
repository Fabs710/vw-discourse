We agree with the core premise from both of you: **the company has to emerge viable, quickly, and with real sacrifice from all sides**. We are not defending the old structure, and under the new leadership we are prepared to accept deeper cuts than were previously contemplated.

But I need to be equally clear on where we cannot go.

### Where we agree
- **Viability first:** if the balance sheet and cost base are not fixed, there is no rescue.
- **Shared sacrifice:** management, labour, retirees, creditors, and the public all have to give.
- **Speed matters:** we do not have the luxury of an extended process.
- **A leaner footprint is unavoidable:** some plant, brand, and dealer reduction is likely necessary.

### Where we disagree with the union
I understand the insistence that the retiree trust is a real obligation and that workers should not be the only shock absorber. That is fair in principle. But **we cannot accept a structure that tries to preserve the retiree obligation largely intact in its current form if that makes the company nonviable**.

That is the central issue. If the trust remains too heavy, too rigid, or too senior relative to the company’s actual capacity, then the result is not protection of retirees — it is failure of the enterprise, and then everyone loses more.

So:
- **Yes** to converting the obligation into something durable.
- **No** to treating it as untouchable.
- **Yes** to giving the trust a real stake if that helps preserve value.
- **No** to a structure that leaves the operating company strangled by claims it cannot support.

### On bondholders
We agree bondholders must take meaningful pain. They cannot be insulated while workers and taxpayers absorb everything. But their losses also need to be part of a plan that actually preserves enough enterprise value to keep the business alive. An excessive squeeze on one side that triggers a collapse would be unacceptable.

### On management
We accept that management must bear consequences and that governance has to change. New leadership is already part of that. We are not asking to preserve the old way of doing things.

### On plants, brands, and dealers
We should preserve the strongest consumer-facing assets and the core industrial base **consistent with viability**. That means:
- cutting what is structurally uncompetitive,
- keeping the franchises that still have market value,
- and avoiding closures that destroy the recovery case.

### On process
We would still prefer a consensual deal if it can be done fast enough and with enough depth. But we are not committed to out-of-court as a matter of principle. If a court-supervised process is the only way to impose the necessary sacrifices and keep financing flowing, then we will work within that reality.

### Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- **No uncontrolled liquidation**
- **No plan that leaves the company too weak to survive**
- **No preservation of legacy claims that the business cannot carry**
- **Yes to deep restructuring, including labour, retiree, creditor, and footprint reductions if needed**
- **Yes to protecting the core brands, plants, dealers, and warranties through transition**

If there is a deal, it has to be one that gives the company a real chance to survive, not one that merely delays the inevitable.
