We can work with the direction of travel, but I need to be very clear: **we will not co-fund a restructuring that protects foreign capacity while sacrificing domestic plants and jobs, and we will not put in public money without a proportionate ownership and governance position.**

## Where we agree

### To the task force
- Viability first: yes.
- Shared sacrifice: yes.
- Speed and finality: yes.
- A deep restructuring is necessary: yes.

We also agree that the old structure cannot simply be preserved.

### To management
- A leaner footprint is unavoidable.
- New leadership and governance discipline are necessary.
- The company needs a balance sheet that can actually survive.

### To the union and retiree trust
- We understand the retiree obligation is real.
- We support a durable instrument for the trust.
- We also agree the workforce should not be the only constituency carrying the burden.

### To the bondholders
- We agree unsecured creditors must take real losses.
- We do not support a plan that leaves them near par while the rest of the system absorbs the damage.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants and jobs are not optional side issues
The task force and management keep speaking in general terms about “viability” and “selective cuts.” That is not enough.

If the restructuring results in:
- domestic plant closures,
- job losses in our jurisdictions,
- and protection of foreign operations instead,

then we cannot support co-funding. That is exactly the outcome we were brought in to prevent.

We are not asking to freeze the entire industrial map. But we are saying:

- **binding domestic production commitments are non-negotiable;**
- **domestic employment protections must be written into the deal;**
- **and there must be real consequences if those commitments are broken.**

### 2) We will not pay for a rescue and receive no real say
We are prepared to contribute real capital, but only if we receive a **proportionate ownership stake and governance rights** reflecting that contribution and the risk we take.

A vague promise of “partnership” is not enough. Nor is a minority interest with no meaningful protection. If we are asked to co-fund, then we expect:
- equity or equivalent participation,
- board or governance rights proportionate to our contribution,
- and protections for the domestic footprint.

### 3) “Viability” cannot be used as a blanket justification for offshoring value
I want to respond directly to management’s point that a plant or dealer network cannot be preserved if it is uneconomic. In principle, that is true.

But in this case, viability cannot be defined only by global cost minimization. A restructuring that shifts production out of our jurisdictions while preserving capacity elsewhere is not neutral. It is a decision to redistribute industrial value away from our workers and regions.

That is not acceptable if public money is involved.

### 4) On the retiree trust
We are not here to take sides in the internal distribution among unsecured constituencies. But we will say this: if the trust receives a durable, meaningful instrument and that helps stabilize the deal, fine. What we will not support is a structure where domestic plants and jobs are sacrificed so that any constituency - including bondholders - can claim a cleaner recovery.

The domestic footprint must be protected first as a condition of our funding.

## Our minimum acceptable terms

If we are to participate, the deal must include all of the following:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - minimum domestic output share,
   - specific plant commitments,
   - employment protections,
   - and clawbacks or penalties if commitments are breached.

2. **Proportionate ownership and governance**
   - an equity stake or equivalent commensurate with our contribution,
   - and a real seat at the table, not symbolic consultation.

3. **An orderly restructuring**
   - deep enough to be credible,
   - but not one that strips our industrial base.

4. **No domestic closures while foreign operations are protected**
   - if sacrifices are required, they must not be concentrated on our plants and workers.

## On process
We are open to an out-of-court deal only if it includes these protections in binding form.

If not, then the court-supervised route does not solve our issue. A court process may impose financial terms, but it does not give us the domestic industrial guarantees we require. So let me be explicit:

- **Court or out-of-court is not the real question for us.**
- **The real question is whether the final structure protects domestic production and employment.**

If it does not, we will not co-fund.

## Direct response to the task force
You have made clear you will not finance an open-ended subsidy. We agree. But we will also not finance a restructuring that exports jobs and capacity while asking us to underwrite the cost.

So our line is simple:
- **No funding without binding domestic commitments.**
- **No funding without proportionate ownership.**
- **No funding for a deal that sacrifices domestic plants while preserving others.**

## Direct response to management
If you want our capital and support, bring forward a footprint plan that preserves the domestic industrial core in a meaningful way. We will not endorse a “competitive” plan that treats our plants as the easiest place to cut.

## Direct response to the union
We support your insistence on real value for workers and retirees. We do not want domestic jobs traded away to solve a balance-sheet problem. On this point, our interests are aligned.

## Direct response to bondholders
We are not opposed to your receiving real upside if you take real losses. But that upside cannot come from hollowing out the domestic base that we are helping to protect. If there are concessions to be made, they must be shared fairly across all stakeholders.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund a viable restructuring. But our support is conditional and non-negotiable on two points:

- **binding domestic plant and employment commitments**
- **a proportionate ownership/governance position**

Without those, we will not support the deal, regardless of whether it is out-of-court or court-supervised.
