We agree on one central point: **this cannot be a cosmetic rescue**. The company needs a real restructuring, and all stakeholders must share sacrifice. We are not defending the old cost structure or pretending the balance sheet can be fixed with slogans.

But we need to be equally clear about what **cannot** happen:

1. **The retiree health trust cannot be destroyed or reduced to a hollow promise.**  
   The obligation was negotiated in good faith as part of the industrial bargain. Retirees are not an expendable class of creditors. If the trust is forced into a write-off so deep that it becomes insecure or meaningless, that is not shared sacrifice — that is a transfer of pain onto workers and retirees alone.

2. **Workers cannot be asked to absorb disproportionate losses while unsecured creditors preserve too much value.**  
   We understand that creditors must take a haircut. We are prepared for that. But if the restructuring protects financial claims while hollowing out wages, jobs, and retiree security, we will not treat that as balanced or fair.

3. **Plant and job cuts must be tied to a viable industrial plan, not just financial engineering.**  
   We will discuss closures, but not as an easy shortcut for creditor recoveries. If the company is to survive, it needs an operating model that preserves as many productive plants and jobs as possible while being genuinely competitive.

What would move us:

- **A real equity or securities claim for the retiree health trust** in the restructured company, so the obligation is durable rather than merely postponed.
- **Protective commitments on jobs and plants**, especially where facilities remain viable.
- **Shared sacrifice from unsecured creditors and management**, not just labour.
- **A structure that preserves the wage framework as much as possible**, even if some concessions are necessary for survival.

On the mechanism: we prefer a **consensual deal**, but not at any price. If the only path offered is one that guts the trust or sacrifices workers wholesale, then we will not legitimize it simply to avoid a court process. The administration says speed matters; we agree. But speed cannot be used as a weapon to force an unfair outcome.

So our position is simple: **we are ready to do our part, but only in a restructuring that secures jobs, gives the retiree trust real value, and makes creditors bear a fair share of the burden.**
