We agree on the core premise: **this has to be a real restructuring, not a cosmetic bailout**. The company cannot survive with the old cost base and capital structure, and we are prepared to make deeper cuts than the prior plan contemplated.

That said, I need to be explicit about where your position is too rigid relative to the survival problem.

### On the retiree health trust
We understand the trust was negotiated in the industrial bargain, and we are not looking to “destroy” it. But **it cannot be treated as sacrosanct if that would make the company unviable**. If the alternative is insolvency and uncontrolled liquidation, then preserving the trust on paper while the enterprise collapses helps no retiree, no worker, and no dealer.

What we can support is a structure that gives the trust **real, durable value in the restructured company** — potentially equity or other long-term consideration — but at a level consistent with the company’s survival and with equal sacrifice from other claimants. A hollow promise is unacceptable; so is a claim that consumes too much value before the business is stabilized.

### On labour and jobs
We agree that plant and job cuts cannot be arbitrary. They must be tied to a viable operating plan. But we also need to be direct: **the company cannot keep all plants, all brands, and all jobs** and remain a going concern. That is simply not the position we are in.

If we avoid the necessary closures now, we will just defer the pain until the entire franchise is weaker. So the right question is not whether cuts happen, but **which cuts preserve the strongest brands, plants, and dealer network** and which are nonviable no matter how painful they are.

### On creditors
You are right that unsecured creditors must bear a fair share. We are not looking to protect financial claims at the expense of workers and retirees. But the reverse is also true: **we cannot impose a structure that leaves the balance sheet so burdened that there is no credible path to profitability**.

The task force has been clear, and we agree: all private stakeholders must take sacrifice. That includes creditors, labour, management, and the trust. If any one group is insulated too much, the rescue fails.

### On mechanism
Our preference remains a **consensual restructuring** if it can be reached quickly and credibly. But let me be equally clear: **we are not attached to consensus as an end in itself**. If one class uses its leverage to block the only viable rescue, then a court-supervised process becomes necessary. We do not prefer it, but we will not rule it out if that is what secures financing and imposes a workable result.

### Bottom line
Our conditions are simple:

- **Financing certainty**
- **A materially lower cost base**
- **A repaired balance sheet**
- **Protection of the consumer-facing franchise where feasible**
- **No outcome that risks the going concern**

So yes, we are prepared to do our part. But no, we will not accept a settlement that preserves claims or jobs in a way that makes the company unfinanceable. That would be a slow-motion liquidation, and we cannot support it.
