### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad agreement that the deal must produce:
  - a **lower fixed-cost base**,
  - a **repaired balance sheet**,
  - and **faster path to viability/profitability**.
- There is agreement that **some plant rationalization** is unavoidable.
- There is broad acceptance that **management must change** and that the old regime should not return.
- All sides prefer a **consensual restructuring** if it can be made fast, credible, and enforceable.
- There is general support for **co-funding**, provided the terms are commercially meaningful.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health trust**
  - Task force and bondholders say it cannot be protected at full or near-full value.
  - Union says it is a negotiated earned benefit and must receive real, durable value.
  - Main dispute: whether the trust is treated like a standard unsecured claim or given special industrial significance.

- **Bondholder recovery**
  - Task force and management expect a deep haircut and substantial equity conversion.
  - Bondholders reject being treated as the residual loss bucket and want treatment at least comparable to similarly ranked claims.
  - Main dispute: how much value unsecured bondholders can lose, and whether trust treatment creates unfair discrimination.

- **Labour and workforce concessions**
  - Task force and management want wage, work-rule, and headcount reductions.
  - Union accepts concessions in principle but wants them tied to a clear industrial plan and minimum job/site protections.
  - Main dispute: whether labour sacrifices are proportional and sufficiently linked to plant and job commitments.

- **Plant and footprint rationalization**
  - Task force and management want closures where economics require them.
  - Union and neighbouring governments want closures limited to what is strictly necessary and not geographically lopsided.
  - Main dispute: which plants close, how many jobs are preserved, and whether domestic sites are disproportionately affected.

- **Co-funder rights**
  - Task force and management support proportionate equity and limited governance rights.
  - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production/employment commitments and stronger protections.
  - Main dispute: how much governance and footprint control co-funders receive.

- **Process**
  - All accept a court-supervised fallback exists.
  - Task force is more explicit that it may move to court quickly.
  - Other parties are less willing to accept the threat as leverage.
  - Main dispute: whether the consensual deal will be seen as fair enough to avoid court.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much the trust can be reduced** without making the restructuring politically or industrially unacceptable.
- **How to balance bondholder haircuts against trust recovery** so similarly ranked claims are not seen as unfairly treated.
- **How much labour can concede** in wages, headcount, and work rules in exchange for binding job/site commitments.
- **Which plants are truly noncompetitive** versus which can be retained with investment and reallocation.
- **How to allocate equity and governance** among public co-funders, creditors, and the trust.
- **What specific commitments are binding** versus merely aspirational, especially on domestic production and employment.
- **Whether the final package is faster and better than court**, or whether court would deliver a clearer allocation of losses.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and mechanics**:
  - proposed haircut ranges,
  - equity/conversion shares,
  - trust instrument structure,
  - plant closure list or criteria,
  - workforce reduction targets,
  - and co-funder ownership/governance terms.
- Ask each side to identify:
  - its **minimum acceptable outcome**,
  - its **non-negotiable red line**,
  - and one **concession it could make** if others also move.
- Require a clear answer to one central question:  
  **What specific package makes the company viable while being acceptable enough to avoid the court-supervised fallback?**
