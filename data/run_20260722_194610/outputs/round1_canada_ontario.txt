We are prepared to co-fund this restructuring, but only if it preserves a meaningful domestic industrial footprint and gives the neighbouring governments a proportionate voice and stake relative to what we put in.

### Where we agree
- **This cannot be a cosmetic rescue.** The company needs real balance-sheet repair and a viable operating plan.
- **All stakeholders must share sacrifice.** We do not support a deal that leaves taxpayers and workers carrying the burden while other claimants are insulated.
- **Speed matters.** Delay would put domestic plants and jobs at unnecessary risk.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) Domestic plants cannot be treated as expendable collateral
We are hearing a lot about “viability” in the abstract, but that cannot become a cover for shifting production and employment out of our jurisdictions while preserving foreign facilities. If the restructuring results in **closures here while plants elsewhere are protected**, we will not regard that as a fair rescue.

We are willing to accept rational consolidation, but **not a one-sided footprint reduction that sacrifices domestic capacity to make the numbers work for others**.

#### 2) We will not fund without binding commitments
If neighbouring governments are asked to co-finance, then we need more than goodwill and promises. We need:
- **binding commitments on domestic production share**
- **employment protections or clear transition guarantees**
- **a credible role in governance**
- **equity or other ownership reflecting our contribution**

Paying in without commensurate say is not acceptable. We are not here to write a cheque for a deal that strips our countries of industrial capacity.

#### 3) On the trust and creditors: equality is not the only issue
We understand the bondholders’ demand for fair treatment, and we are not here to prejudge the legal ranking of claims. But the rescue cannot be structured solely around creditor parity if that produces a result that devastates domestic employment and industrial presence.

Likewise, the retiree trust cannot be “protected” in a way that blocks the restructuring or consumes too much value. What matters to us is not defending any single claim class; it is securing a **durable company with a domestic footprint worth preserving**.

### What would move us
We can support co-funding if the deal includes:
- **binding production and employment commitments for our plants**
- **a proportionate equity stake or equivalent participation**
- **clear governance rights tied to our financing**
- **an orderly restructuring that actually restores viability**
- **no closure plan that disproportionately burdens our domestic facilities**

### On the mechanism
We prefer an out-of-court solution if it is real, fast, and enforceable. But if the process becomes a vehicle for approving a deal that protects creditors or foreign capacity at the expense of our plants, we will not endorse it just for the sake of consensus.

So our position is clear: **we are ready to co-fund, but only for a restructuring that protects domestic production and employment, and only in exchange for a stake and a say proportionate to our contribution.**
