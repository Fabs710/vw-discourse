We are prepared to support a hard restructuring, but only if it **protects a meaningful domestic footprint** and gives co-funders a **proportionate stake and voice**. That remains our line.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic bailout.
- The capital structure and cost base are broken.
- **Speed and finality** matter.
- Labour, creditors, management, and the public sector all need to share sacrifice.
- A consensual deal is preferable **if** it is fast, credible, and enforceable.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants cannot be the easy casualty
To the task force and management: we accept that not every plant can survive. But we do **not** accept a restructuring where domestic plants are cut while foreign capacity is protected simply because it is administratively easier or politically less costly elsewhere.

That is a red line for us.

If this rescue requires closures, they must be:
- based on genuine competitiveness,
- not geographically lopsided,
- and not designed to shift production out of our jurisdictions while preserving other footprints.

### 2) Co-funding must come with real rights
We are not prepared to fund the rescue as a junior payor with no meaningful say. If we are contributing capital to preserve the enterprise and jobs, then we need:

- a **proportionate equity position**,
- **governance rights** tied to our contribution,
- and **binding commitments** on domestic production and employment.

A deal that asks us to write a cheque without commensurate ownership or protection is not acceptable.

### 3) We will not bankroll a deal that socializes the pain here while exporting the benefit elsewhere
To bondholders: we hear your concern about fairness among unsecured claims. But your recovery cannot be the only axis of fairness.

If the restructuring delivers a viable company by concentrating plant cuts and job losses in our jurisdictions, then the deal is not fair to us, regardless of the creditor math. Our public mandate is to protect domestic production and employment, and we will not ignore that.

### 4) Binding commitments, not assurances
To management: your language about “binding commitments where commercially sustainable” is too open-ended. We need something specific enough to rely on, not a promise that disappears the moment it becomes inconvenient.

We need:
- minimum domestic production commitments,
- employment protections or transition guarantees,
- consultation rights before footprint changes,
- and equity/governance in line with our funding.

## On the trust and bondholders
We are not here to referee the legal ranking among unsecured claims. But we will say this: the restructuring cannot be structured around creditor outcomes alone while domestic industrial capacity is treated as disposable.

If the trust receives restructured value, bondholders will press for parity. That is their issue with the task force and management. Our issue is different: **we will not support a rescue that protects paper claims while stripping real jobs and production from our countries**.

## On the mechanism
We still prefer an out-of-court settlement if it can be made fast and enforceable. But we are not signing up to consensus for its own sake.

If the term sheet does not include:
- domestic production commitments,
- plant/employment protections,
- and proportional ownership/governance for co-funders,

then we will not endorse it, even if others want to move quickly.

## Our bottom line
We are prepared to co-fund, but only on terms that reflect our contribution and protect the domestic industrial base.

So our position is:

- **yes** to a viable restructuring,
- **yes** to deep sacrifice across stakeholders,
- **no** to domestic plant closures while foreign capacity is insulated,
- **no** to funding without proportionate equity and governance,
- **no** to vague commitments instead of binding footprint protections.

If those conditions are met, we can be constructive. If not, we will not be a passive source of money for a deal that undermines our domestic industry.
