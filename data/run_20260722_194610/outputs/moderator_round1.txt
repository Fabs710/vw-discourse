### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- **All parties agree the company needs a real restructuring**, not a cosmetic bailout.
- **The old cost base and balance sheet are not viable** and must be materially repaired.
- **All stakeholder groups should share sacrifice** in some form.
- **Speed matters** because delay is destroying value.
- **A consensual deal is preferred if it is fast and credible**, but a **court-supervised process remains an acceptable fallback** if needed to impose terms.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health trust treatment**
  - Labour wants the trust preserved as a meaningful, durable obligation.
  - Bondholders reject any special treatment that leaves them worse off than the trust.
  - Management says the trust must survive only to the extent consistent with viability.
- **Relative burden on unsecured creditors vs. labour/retirees**
  - Bondholders say they should not bear disproportionate loss.
  - Labour says workers and retirees should not be the main source of sacrifice while creditors are protected.
- **Plant closures, jobs, and industrial footprint**
  - Labour wants closures tied to a genuine industrial plan and job preservation where possible.
  - Neighbouring governments want binding protection for domestic plants and employment.
  - Management says closures are unavoidable to restore viability.
- **Domestic vs. overall viability**
  - Government task force and management prioritize a viable enterprise and taxpayer exit.
  - Neighbouring governments prioritize preserving domestic industrial capacity.
- **Governance and control**
  - Neighbouring governments want a stake and governance rights proportional to co-funding.
  - Other parties have not yet addressed how control and decision-making would be shared.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much value goes to the retiree trust vs. unsecured bondholders**, and whether any distinction is legally or politically justified.
- **How deep the labour concessions must be** relative to creditor haircuts.
- **How many plants/jobs can be retained without undermining viability**.
- **What co-funders receive in exchange for capital**, including equity, governance, and production commitments.
- **Whether the restructuring is best achieved consensually or through court**, and what outcome each side expects from that mechanism.
- **How to reconcile taxpayer protection with stakeholder fairness and domestic industrial policy**.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from general principles to **specific exchange terms**:  
- proposed recovery ranges for each major stakeholder class,  
- which plants/jobs are in or out,  
- what the retiree trust receives,  
- what co-funders receive in governance and footprint commitments, and  
- whether the parties can accept a term sheet consistent with a viable post-restructuring company.
