## To management
You are right that the bridge must be quantified. You are also right that internal measures may not be enough if the residual gap is large.

But you have not yet earned support for keeping closures and compulsory redundancies as a standing fallback simply because you can’t yet prove the opposite. “Not yet disproven” is not the same as “necessary.”

What we need is exactly what the forensic experts asked for:
- plant-by-plant utilization
- fixed costs by site
- labor-hours per unit
- savings from attrition, early retirement, redeployment, working-time flexibility, productivity, and overhead cuts
- management and owner contribution
- residual recurring gap
- timing to restore margins

If that bridge shows a **material residual gap** that internal measures cannot close in time, then we will not support a no-closure package just because it is politically easier. Competitive margins matter more than optics.

## To the works council and union
We understand and share your opposition to a destructive confrontation. We also accept that workforce contributions can be part of a serious settlement if they are time-limited and reversible.

But we do **not** accept a position that says “no closures, no redundancies” without also proving that the alternative closes the full recurring gap. A no-closure package is only acceptable if it is **economically sufficient**, not merely preferable.

Your insistence on binding guarantees is understandable, but guarantees that freeze an uncompetitive footprint are not value-protective. If you want binding protection, then the burden is on you to support a package that still delivers a credible margin reset.

## To the regional state government
We appreciate your opposition to a reckless closure-first approach. That restraint is valuable. But a blocking minority cannot be used to preserve excess capacity indefinitely.

If the bridge shows that non-destructive measures fully close the gap, then fine — a protected settlement is defensible. If it does not, then using veto power to block hard restructuring would simply shift the cost onto shareholders and weaken the company further. We won’t support that.

## To the controlling family holding
We agree that value-destroying strikes and governance paralysis are unacceptable. But we also agree with management that a permanent guarantee regime is only defensible if the economics work.

So: yes to a serious, quantified bridge; no to closure-first brinkmanship; and no to a settlement that locks in underperformance.

## Our position
We are prepared to support a **no-closure settlement** only if independent verification shows it does all of the following:
1. closes the full recurring cost gap,
2. materially improves capacity utilization,
3. restores a credible margin path,
4. includes enforceable milestones and review gates,
5. does not depend mainly on one-off measures.

If those conditions are met, then binding site and job guarantees through 2030 become supportable, because they would protect a competitively viable asset.

If those conditions are **not** met, then we will not endorse a deal that merely postpones the restructuring problem.
