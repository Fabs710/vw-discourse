I will be direct.

I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one point: **this is a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible**. I also agree with the works council and union that **a destructive conflict would damage the company and the region**.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion management keeps pushing: that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain the normal fallback. For the regional state, that is not a neutral restructuring tool. It is a line that would inflict **lasting damage on jobs, sites, and the regional economic base**. I will not support that as the default path.

## Where I stand

### To Management
You keep saying the burden is on the employee side to prove a no-closure package can work. I disagree with that framing.

If you want the state to accept deep restructuring, you need to show:
- the **full quantified gap**,
- what savings come from **capacity adjustment short of closures**,
- what comes from **management and owner contributions**,
- and why a non-destructive package is insufficient.

So far, I hear a lot of assertions about necessity, but not a convincing demonstration that all serious alternatives have been exhausted.

What I will support is:
- **capacity reduction without German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **natural attrition, early retirement, redeployment, qualification**
- **working-time flexibility and productivity measures**
- **targeted product and investment commitments**
- **a time-limited employee contribution**

What I will not support is:
- a package built on **plant closures**
- **forced layoffs**
- or a settlement that treats co-determination as an obstacle to be worked around

### To the Works Council and Union
Your core red lines remain the right ones:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no dismantling of the collective framework**

I support those red lines.

But I also need to be clear: if we want to defend them successfully, the workforce side must bring a serious industrial contribution, not only resistance. That means a package must be able to show concrete savings through:
- shift and working-time flexibility,
- internal transfers,
- natural attrition,
- early retirement,
- qualification and redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and a time-limited contribution that is truly reversible.

That is the only kind of compromise I can defend politically and economically.

### To the Controlling Family Holding and Investors
You are right that the company needs real savings and a durable solution. You are also right that deadlock would destroy value.

Where I disagree is in treating closures and forced redundancies as acceptable “discipline” if the numbers are not immediately satisfied. In this governance structure, that is not just an efficiency decision. It is a decision with major regional, political, and industrial consequences. If you force that route, you should expect resistance that can make the company worse off, not better.

A plan that is economically neat but socially and politically unimplementable is **not** a good plan. That is why I support a negotiated restructuring, but only one that protects sites and jobs.

## My bottom line
The state is prepared to support a **serious restructuring package** if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job protection**
4. **Real investment and product commitments**
5. **A quantified, shared burden from management, owners, and labour**
6. **A governance process that respects co-determination and our blocking rights**

What we will not do is endorse deindustrialization in the name of competitiveness.

## What I want next
Bring forward a package with numbers, not slogans:
- how much capacity comes out **without closures**
- how much cost is cut through **internal restructuring**
- what the **management and ownership contribution** is
- what the **employee contribution** is
- and how all of that restores competitiveness while protecting the company’s role as a regional anchor

If that can be shown, we can work constructively. If the only answer remains “closures and redundancies must stay on the table,” then my answer remains: **not with our support**.
