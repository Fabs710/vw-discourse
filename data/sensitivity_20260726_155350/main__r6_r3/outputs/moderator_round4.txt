Core impasse identified:

1. **Sequencing and conditionality**
   - Employee-side stakeholders require **binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy guarantees first**.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders require **proof first** that internal measures can close the recurring cost gap without footprint reduction.
   - This is a classic deadlock over **“guarantee before burden” vs. “numbers before guarantees.”**

2. **Scope of acceptable restructuring tools**
   - The conflict is not about whether restructuring is needed; all sides accept that it is.
   - The conflict is about whether **plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain legitimate fallback tools**.
   - Employee/state sides want them removed from the table.
   - Management/investor/family sides want them retained unless and until the quantified bridge proves otherwise.

3. **What counts as sufficient proof**
   - The discussion now hinges on **plant-by-plant, measure-by-measure, time-phased savings**.
   - No side accepts the others’ estimates as sufficient.
   - The expert inputs suggest the decisive variable is the **residual gap after non-destructive measures**.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Phased no-closure agreement with evidence-based review
**Structure**
- Phase 1: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, time-limited employee contribution, redeployment, early retirement, attrition, working-time flexibility, product allocation, and management/owner contributions.
- Independent verification of savings and capacity effects at fixed milestones.
- If targets are missed, the parties do **not** automatically reopen closures; instead they enter a **mandatory renegotiation window** with a requirement to exhaust additional non-destructive measures first.

**Why it may bridge**
- Gives employee/state side immediate protection.
- Gives management/investors a structured mechanism to test whether the bridge is sufficient.
- Replaces the closure fallback with a sequenced escalation path.

---

### Proposal 2: Protection-for-transformation package with quantified residual-gap trigger
**Structure**
- Binding site and job guarantees through 2030.
- In exchange: quantified reductions in labor hours, overhead, shift capacity, and product complexity.
- A jointly agreed **residual-gap threshold** is defined ex ante.
- Only if the independently verified residual gap exceeds that threshold after all agreed measures are implemented do the parties reopen the question of additional structural measures.

**Why it may bridge**
- Satisfies the employee/state demand for protection.
- Satisfies management/investors’ demand for measurable discipline.
- Turns the debate from ideology into a pre-agreed accounting test.

---

### Proposal 3: Joint governance body with real transparency and limited escalation rights
**Structure**
- A joint body with employee, management, owner, state, and independent expert participation.
- Full disclosure of plant-level economics, utilization, and savings assumptions.
- Clear decision rights on product allocation, redeployment, qualification, and process redesign.
- Escalation to closures is not unilateral; it requires a documented finding that agreed non-destructive tools have been exhausted and that the residual gap remains material.

**Why it may bridge**
- Addresses distrust over hidden closure paths.
- Gives investors and management a mechanism for disciplined oversight.
- Preserves co-determination while creating a shared fact base.
