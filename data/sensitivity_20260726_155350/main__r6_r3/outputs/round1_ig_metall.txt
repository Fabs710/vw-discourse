We share the same red lines: no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: if management tries to push a restructuring that makes workers carry the whole burden, we will escalate. The end of the peace obligation means the workforce is no longer expected to stay passive while historic protections are torn up.

Where I agree with the Works Council:
- A durable solution has to protect jobs and sites.
- Any savings must be negotiated, not imposed.
- The workforce can contribute, but only as part of a balanced package.
- There must be binding commitments on investment and future products.

Where I draw the line:
- We will not accept open-ended insecurity in place of real guarantees.
- We will not accept wage cuts, bonus cuts, or working-time sacrifices if they are simply a prelude to layoffs or closures.
- We will not accept a “shared sacrifice” story that in practice means employees pay while owners and management avoid meaningful contribution.

What a viable path looks like from our side:
- No plant closures.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- A negotiated capacity reduction through natural attrition, redeployment, early retirement, and internal transfers.
- Temporary labour-cost measures only if they are strictly time-limited, reversible, and tied to enforceable site and investment guarantees.
- A fair contribution from management and owners as well, not just from the workforce.

If management says the only way forward is closing German plants and cutting thousands of jobs, then we reject that premise. There are alternatives, but they require management to stop treating the workforce as the only adjustment variable. If that is the stance, then warning strikes were only the beginning.
