### Core impasse
The central impasse is this:

- **Employee-side and state-side red lines:** no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of co-determination/job security.
- **Management/capital-side requirement:** a **material, durable, and implementable** reduction in cost base and capacity, with a credible fallback if non-destructive measures do not close the gap.

So the dispute is **not** about whether change is needed. It is about **what counts as sufficient structural change** and **who bears the risk if the non-closure package does not fully work**.

A second fault line is procedural:
- the employee/state side wants **binding protection first, then restructuring**;
- management/investors want **credible adjustment authority first, then protection**.

### Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

#### 1) “No-closure first phase” with hard review gates
A phased agreement in which Phase 1 contains:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- time-limited employee contributions,
- redeployment, attrition, early retirement, qualification, working-time flexibility,
- management/owner contributions and investment commitments.

It would include:
- **pre-agreed quantitative targets** for cost and capacity reduction,
- **independent verification** of progress,
- and a **mandatory reopen clause** if targets are missed.

Key safeguard: the reopen clause would **require renewed negotiation and exhaustion of non-destructive alternatives** before any harder measure could be activated.

#### 2) “Protection-for-transformation” package with binding mutual commitments
A package combining:
- **binding site and job guarantees through 2030**,
- **binding product allocation / investment commitments** to German sites,
- and **measurable internal restructuring** obligations.

In exchange, the workforce side would accept:
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- targeted cost moderation,
- redeployment across sites,
- qualification and mobility measures,
- and a strictly time-limited contribution.

Key safeguard: the agreement would specify that the burden is **shared and quantified** among labour, management, owners, and product planning—not left to employees alone.

#### 3) Joint governance body with decision thresholds
A joint steering body with:
- employee, management, state, and shareholder representation,
- access to full financial/capacity data,
- authority to approve or reject implementation steps within a fixed timetable.

It would have:
- defined milestones,
- a transparent dashboard of savings and capacity changes,
- and a supermajority or unanimity rule for any move beyond agreed non-destructive measures.

Key safeguard: the body cannot be used to bypass co-determination; it can only operate within the supervisory and statutory framework.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
What exact combination of measures—quantified by savings and capacity effects—would you accept as sufficient to meet the company’s restructuring target **without** closures or compulsory redundancies?

#### To the Industrial Union
What is the maximum time-limited employee contribution you are prepared to recommend, and what binding reversibility provisions would have to be written into the agreement?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What are the precise savings and capacity numbers you still need, broken down by category, before you can state that non-destructive measures are insufficient?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage will the state use, through its governance rights, to secure both industrial protection and measurable restructuring delivery?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you support binding site and job guarantees through 2030 if the package includes independently verified, material internal restructuring and quantified owner contributions?

#### To Institutional Investors
What minimum level of verified capacity reduction and cost reduction would make a no-closure package acceptable from your perspective?
