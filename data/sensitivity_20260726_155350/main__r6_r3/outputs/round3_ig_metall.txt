I will be equally direct.

The Works Council is right on the core red lines, and management is still not answering the central question: **why should the workforce be the primary shock absorber for a structural problem it did not create?**

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state, and even parts of the shareholder side on the diagnosis:
- this is structural, not temporary;
- a cosmetic deal is worthless;
- the company needs durable savings, not slogans;
- and a destructive conflict would damage everyone.

I also agree that the workforce must contribute. I have said that before, and I repeat it now. We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- natural attrition,
- productivity improvements,
- qualification,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

But let me be clear: **employee contribution is not the same thing as accepting closures or forced layoffs.**

## Where I disagree

### To Management
You keep saying “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are incompatible with competitiveness. That is still an assertion, not a proven necessity.

If you want a credible mandate for deeper measures, then you need to present the full cost bridge:
- what savings come from capacity measures short of closure,
- what comes from management and owner contributions,
- what comes from labour-cost measures,
- what comes from product allocation and investment decisions,
- and what remains as a real gap after all of that.

So far, what I hear is:
- employees should give up guarantees,
- employees should accept lower pay or fewer protections,
- and if that is not enough, closures stay available.

That is not a shared-sacrifice package. That is a threat structure.

And I’ll say this plainly: **if management’s answer to every shortfall is “then we may need closures,” then management has not yet exhausted the negotiated alternatives.**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
You are right that a fake compromise would be value-destructive. But you are wrong if you think that means closures are the benchmark of seriousness.

A serious package is one that:
- protects the company’s industrial base,
- restores competitiveness,
- and shares the burden fairly.

If the only way to get your approval is to keep plant closures alive as a fallback, then you are asking the workforce to negotiate under permanent blackmail. That is not how you build trust or durable value.

### To Institutional Investors
You want “visible structural change.” Fine. So do we. But visible does not mean destructive.

Structure can change through:
- shift models,
- working-time adaptation,
- internal mobility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- product allocation,
- and hard productivity measures.

If those instruments are not enough, then show the numbers. Not vague references to markets. Not abstract talk of target returns. Numbers.

If you cannot demonstrate that the non-destructive package is insufficient, then your insistence on keeping closures and forced redundancies in play is premature and unnecessary.

### To the Regional State Government
Your position is broadly correct, but it has to be backed by real leverage. Saying “no closures, no redundancies” while allowing management to keep them as bargaining threats is not enough.

If the state wants a settlement, it should help lock in:
- binding site guarantees,
- binding job guarantees,
- investment commitments,
- and a negotiation framework that does not begin with closure as the default instrument.

## On the bridge models

### 1) Protected restructuring with measurable triggers
This is only acceptable if triggers do **not** become a backdoor to plant closures or forced redundancies.

I will not accept a design where “missed targets” automatically reopen the door to layoffs and shutdowns. That would be a conditional promise with a built-in trap.

If there is a review mechanism, it must require:
- renewed negotiation,
- exhaustion of non-destructive alternatives,
- and a fresh test of management’s own contribution before any tougher measure is even discussed.

### 2) Site guarantees in exchange for deeper internal restructuring
This is the strongest direction on the table, and it is close to what we have already signaled.

We can discuss:
- internal transfer,
- redeployment,
- early retirement,
- qualification,
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- and product shifts.

But only if the package includes:
- binding site security,
- binding job security,
- and a real management/owner contribution.

That means no hidden erosion of the collective wage agreement. No “temporary” sacrifice that becomes permanent downgrade.

### 3) Phased restructuring with joint governance
A joint body can be useful, but only if it has real substance and cannot be used to normalize closure logic over time.

If it is just a consultation forum while management keeps the closure weapon in reserve, then it is not joint governance. It is a delaying tactic.

If it is a genuine joint process with:
- employee influence,
- transparent numbers,
- verified milestones,
- and no unilateral escalation to closures,
then it is discussable.

## My position, clearly stated
I am **not** moving on the essentials:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- **No one-sided burden on the workforce.**

What I am prepared to discuss:
- time-limited employee contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement and natural attrition,
- productivity measures,
- qualification,
- and capacity adjustments that do not destroy sites.

## What would actually move me
A real package would need all of the following:
1. **binding site guarantees**
2. **binding job guarantees**
3. **management and owner contributions**
4. **credible investment and product commitments**
5. **quantified non-destructive savings**
6. **no hidden closure fallback**

If management and shareholders want labour peace, then they need to stop treating the workforce as the residual adjustment variable.

## Final word
We are ready to help build a durable settlement.  
We are **not** ready to sign up to closures, forced redundancies, or a hollow “restructuring” that simply shifts the pain onto employees.

If the company wants contribution from labour, it will get it — but only in a fair package where management and owners also give something real, and where jobs and sites are genuinely protected.
