Here is a procedural readout of the plateau and a set of facilitator proposals.

## Core impasse
The main deadlock is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is over **what must be proven before closures/compulsory redundancies can remain on the table**.

The two sides are using different default rules:

- **Labor side:** no-closure/no-forced-redundancy is the default; management must prove that destructive measures are unavoidable.
- **Management / some capital voices:** closures must remain a real option unless the no-closure bridge is shown to close the residual gap.

So the dispute has become a **proof standard dispute**:
1. who carries the burden of proof,
2. what level of residual gap justifies footprint reduction,
3. whether binding guarantees can come before, or only after, economic sufficiency is established.

A second impasse sits behind it:
- labor wants **binding protection first**,
- management/investors want **economic flexibility first**.

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise
These are not endorsements, just structured options that could bridge the main conflicts.

### Proposal 1: Two-stage agreement with conditional protections
A first-stage deal would immediately lock in:
- no compulsory redundancies during Phase 1,
- a temporary no-closure pause,
- time-limited employee contribution,
- management/owner contribution,
- full disclosure of plant-level numbers,
- independent verification.

A second-stage decision would be triggered only after the bridge is audited.

Possible compromise feature:
- closures are not accepted as an automatic fallback,
- but they are not permanently removed before the data test.

Why it may help:
- gives labor immediate protection,
- gives management a real economic test,
- gives investors a disciplined decision point.

### Proposal 2: No-closure settlement with a hard review gate and predefined fallback ladder
This would keep the no-closure path open, but define a **strict escalation ladder** if targets are missed:
1. redeployment
2. attrition / early retirement
3. working-time flexibility
4. productivity and overhead cuts
5. product allocation changes
6. only then renewed bargaining on footprint measures

Possible compromise feature:
- no closure mandate up front,
- but also no indefinite prohibition if the bridge fails.

Why it may help:
- addresses management’s concern about credibility,
- preserves labor’s demand that destructive measures be last, not first.

### Proposal 3: Protection-for-transformation package with partial guarantees
A settlement could include:
- site protection for core German sites,
- job protection for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- but with a narrower review clause for underperforming units,
- plus binding product and investment commitments.

Possible compromise feature:
- protects the bulk of the workforce/sites,
- while allowing a limited, clearly defined review of the most difficult assets.

Why it may help:
- reduces the all-or-nothing confrontation,
- may be more acceptable to investors and the family holding than full blanket guarantees.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact residual recurring gap, after plant-by-plant internal measures and owner/management contributions, would still justify reopening the case for closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the works council
**If independent verification showed that the no-closure package leaves a material but not catastrophic residual gap, what additional non-destructive measures would you accept before any discussion of footprint reduction?**

### To the union
**Would you accept a strictly time-limited employee contribution tied to enforceable milestones, if it came with a formal no-forced-redundancy period and a transparent plant-level review?**

### To the regional state government
**How far is the state prepared to go in supporting a no-closure settlement if the verified numbers show only a moderate residual gap, but not a fully closed one?**

### To the controlling family holding
**What level of verified economic underperformance would make you accept a no-closure solution as sufficient, rather than insisting on the continued availability of closures?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you support binding job and site guarantees through 2030 if the independent bridge shows the company’s recurring gap is fully or materially closed without closures, even if the margin reset is slower than management prefers?**

## Suggested next procedural step
The discussion now needs to move from positions to a common test. A useful next step would be:

- a **single agreed residual-gap model**,
- plant-by-plant,
- independently verified,
- with agreed assumptions,
- and a predefined decision rule for what counts as “sufficient” internal adjustment.
