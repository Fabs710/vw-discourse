1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties agree the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem** and that a merely temporary fix is insufficient.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **negotiated**, **durable**, and include a **credible industrial plan** with **investment commitments**.
- Most non-management stakeholders agree that a destructive conflict would be value-destroying and that a settlement should, if possible, preserve the company’s long-term viability.
- There is shared openness to **alternative instruments** such as working-time flexibility, redeployment, early retirement, natural attrition, productivity measures, and time-limited workforce contributions.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:** employee representatives and the state reject closures; management, family holding, and investors say closures must remain an option if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** employee representatives and the state reject them; management, family holding, and investors consider them possible if alternatives do not deliver sufficient structural savings.
- **Extent of labour contribution:** employees will only consider sacrifices if tied to binding guarantees; management and capital-side actors want material cost reductions and capacity adjustment first, not symbolic concessions.
- **Binding guarantees vs flexibility:** employees and state seek firm site/job/investment guarantees; management and shareholders warn against guarantees that freeze an uncompetitive structure.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- How to achieve **durable savings and capacity reduction** without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- What **specific, enforceable concessions** from labour would be sufficient, and what **binding commitments** from management/owners/state would be credible in return.
- Whether a package can simultaneously satisfy **co-determination/political constraints** and still restore **target margins and competitiveness**.
- How to balance **short-term social protection** against **long-term industrial viability** without creating a deal that is either unimplementable or economically weak.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principle statements to **concrete package options**: exact savings measures, capacity actions, timelines, and enforceable guarantees.
- Test each option against two criteria: **implementation feasibility** under the governance structure and **sufficiency** for restoring competitiveness.
- Ask each side to specify its **minimum acceptable package** and the **specific trade-off** it is prepared to make in exchange.
