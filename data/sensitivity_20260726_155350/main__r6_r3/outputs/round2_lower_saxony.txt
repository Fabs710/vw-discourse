I appreciate the clearer positions, but I need to be equally clear from the state’s side: **a package that keeps plant closures and compulsory redundancies “on the table” as default tools is not one I can support**. That is not because I deny the economic pressure. It is because I do not accept that the only credible restructuring path is to damage the regional industrial base in a way that is politically, socially, and economically irreversible.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on several core points:
- the competitiveness problem is real,
- the current structure is not sustainable unchanged,
- a cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible,
- and the company needs a durable solution that restores margins and funds future investment.

I also agree with the works council and union that:
- this cannot be a one-sided burden on employees,
- there must be a credible industrial plan,
- and any workforce contribution must be tied to binding commitments, not vague promises.

## Where I disagree
### To Management
You keep saying closures and forced redundancies are necessary if alternatives do not “close the gap.” But you have not shown that you have exhausted the serious alternatives in a way that is credible to co-determination and to the state’s role as an anchor shareholder.

I am not interested in a theoretical claim that closures are easier. I am interested in a package that is:
- implementable,
- durable,
- and socially sustainable.

If management wants state support, then it must bring forward a concrete alternative that does not begin with plant closures as the preferred outcome. That means serious numbers on:
- capacity reduction without site destruction,
- working-time and shift flexibility,
- natural attrition and early retirement,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a fair management/owner contribution.

If you cannot show that those tools are insufficient, then your argument for closures is not yet persuasive enough to justify the consequences.

### To the Family Holding and Institutional Investors
I hear your emphasis on competitiveness and value creation. That is legitimate. But I will not accept a framing that treats social and regional stability as secondary constraints to be overridden once a financial model points in that direction.

This company is not a generic asset. It is an industrial anchor with a special role in the region, and that has governance consequences. If the only “value-preserving” solution is one that inflicts lasting damage on the regional economy, then we have a disagreement about what long-term value actually means.

A package that provokes prolonged conflict, undermines trust, and triggers deep political backlash is not durable value creation. It is risk accumulation.

### To the Works Council and Union
I support your core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is the correct starting point.

But I also need to be honest: if we want to defend those lines successfully, the workforce side must be prepared to engage on a serious, structured contribution that is more than a slogan. The state can only help defend sites and jobs if there is a credible alternative plan to show to the board and the capital markets.

That means I expect a negotiated package to include:
- time-limited and reversible labour-cost measures,
- internal flexibility and redeployment,
- meaningful productivity improvements,
- and a binding industrial plan with investment and product commitments.

I will not support any package that weakens co-determination or turns employee participation into a fig leaf. But co-determination also has to be used to shape a viable future, not merely to block every adjustment.

## My position on the actual trade-off
I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** if it meets these conditions:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job guarantees**
4. **A credible investment and product allocation plan**
5. **A real, time-limited workforce contribution**
6. **Management and ownership also sharing the burden**
7. **A route to competitiveness that can survive the governance reality of this company**

What I will not support is a package that:
- starts from closures,
- treats forced layoffs as normal,
- and asks the state to endorse industrial retrenchment as if it were a neutral efficiency measure.

If management wants a durable solution, then it should stop using closures as the benchmark and start testing whether a negotiated industrial transformation can actually work. If that requires a more modest but real capacity adjustment, then that is the path we should explore. If it does not, then the burden is on management to prove why not.

## Clear bottom line
The state is open to restructuring. We are not open to deindustrialization by another name.

Bring forward a concrete package that protects German sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, and we will engage constructively. If the package remains dependent on plant closures or compulsory redundancies, then it will not have our support, and you should expect us to use the rights we have under the statute.
