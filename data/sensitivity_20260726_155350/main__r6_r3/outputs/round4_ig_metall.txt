I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even parts of the shareholder side on one thing: **this is a structural problem, not a temporary one**, and a cosmetic deal would be a waste of time.

I also agree that the workforce should **contribute**. I have never claimed otherwise. A fair settlement can include:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- natural attrition,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a **time-limited employee contribution**.

That is not the issue. The issue is **who carries the pain and whether jobs and the collective framework survive**.

## Where I disagree with management
Management is still not answering the central question:

**Why must the restructuring be built around plant closures and compulsory redundancies?**

You keep saying “material, durable, implementable.” Fine. Then show the **full itemized bridge**:
- savings from product allocation to German sites,
- savings from management and owner contributions,
- savings from working-time flexibility,
- savings from internal transfers and redeployment,
- savings from early retirement and natural attrition,
- savings from productivity and process gains,
- savings from qualification and shift redesign.

Until that bridge is on the table in hard numbers, your closure threat remains just that: a threat.

And let me be clear: **we do not accept closures or forced redundancies as the default fallback**. If you keep those as your standing answer, then you are not bargaining in good faith; you are trying to force the workforce to pre-pay for management’s failure.

## Where I disagree with the controlling family and investors
I understand the demand for durability and competitiveness. I do.

But I do **not** accept the idea that “serious restructuring” means preserving closures and layoffs as normal instruments. That is not seriousness; it is a willingness to solve an industrial problem by destroying trust and jobs.

A no-closure package is not “soft” if it actually delivers:
- verified savings,
- real capacity adjustment,
- and a stable industrial base.

What I reject is a package that shifts the adjustment burden almost entirely onto labor while management, owners, and the strategic side hide behind market discipline.

## To the Regional State Government
Your position is closer to ours, and I recognize that. But support in principle is not enough.

If the state opposes closures and compulsory redundancies, it must use its leverage to make that real:
- insist on quantified savings without closures,
- insist on management and owner contributions,
- insist on product commitments to German sites,
- insist on binding job and site protection,
- and refuse any “solution” that makes workers the residual variable.

If management says the package misses a target, then the answer is **not** to open the door to layoffs. The answer is to find the missing contribution where it belongs: from management, owners, pricing, product allocation, and internal restructuring.

## On the facilitator’s bridge models

### 1) Protected restructuring with measurable triggers
This is only acceptable if the trigger **does not** automatically reopen the door to closures or forced redundancies.

If a target is missed, the response must be:
- renewed negotiation,
- transparent review,
- and exhaustion of non-destructive alternatives.

Anything else is just a backdoor closure clause.

### 2) Site guarantees in exchange for deeper internal restructuring
This is the only direction that comes close to acceptable, because it starts from protection rather than destruction.

But the guarantees must be real:
- binding site guarantees through 2030,
- binding job guarantees through 2030,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- real investment commitments,
- and measurable internal restructuring.

Not promises. Not vague intentions. Not “we’ll see later.”

### 3) Joint governance body
A joint body can be useful only if it has real substance and cannot be used to bypass co-determination or normalize closure logic.

If it includes:
- employee influence,
- full transparency,
- real decision rights,
- and no unilateral escalation to closures,

then it may help.

If it is merely a consultation club, then it is a delay tactic.

Bring a real, quantified, non-destructive plan.  
Bring binding guarantees.

Bring product and investment commitments.

Then we can talk seriously.
