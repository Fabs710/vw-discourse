Management is still trying to frame this as if there are only two choices: either accept plant closures and forced redundancies, or accept collapse. I reject that framing.

We all agree there is a structural problem. But let me be very clear: **a structural problem does not automatically justify destroying sites and tearing up job security**. The burden must not be placed primarily on the workforce while management asks us to sign off on the end of historic protections.

## Where I agree
I agree with the state, the union, and even parts of what the family and investors are saying:
- A cosmetic fix is not enough.
- Any solution has to be durable.
- A destructive conflict would hurt everyone.
- We need a credible industrial plan and real investment commitments.

That is exactly why I am not accepting a plan built on closures and compulsory redundancies. A “durable” solution that destroys trust, breaks the workforce’s faith in the company, and triggers open conflict is not durable at all.

## Where I disagree
### To Management
You keep saying closures and redundancies “must remain on the table.” For us, that is not an acceptable starting point. It is a threat, not a solution.

If you want savings, then bring forward **real alternatives**:
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- early retirement,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary flexibility,
- productivity and process improvements,
- product allocation to German plants,
- and management/owner contributions.

What you have not shown is why these tools, combined in a serious package, cannot deliver meaningful savings **without** closures or forced layoffs.

You also have not answered the key question: **what is your concrete commitment to German sites if we give you flexibility?** Not slogans. Not “future competitiveness.” Binding commitments.

### To the Family Holding and Investors
You say you do not want a fake compromise. Fine. Neither do we.

But your version of “serious restructuring” seems to begin and end with workforce cuts. That is not serious governance; that is one-sided cost shifting. If you want the workforce to contribute, then the owners and leadership must also contribute through:
- binding industrial commitments,
- realistic product decisions,
- investment allocation,
- and acceptance that co-determination is not an obstacle but part of this company’s stability.

A package that depends on breaking the workforce’s protections is not a credible long-term value strategy. It is short-term pressure dressed up as discipline.

### To the State
I appreciate your clear support for no closures and no compulsory redundancies. But support must become leverage.

The regional state should not just say the right words; it should use its blocking position to prevent any plan that closes German plants or imposes forced layoffs. If the political and economic consequences are so severe, then that veto must be used accordingly.

## Our minimum position
Let me put this plainly:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**
- **No unilateral restructuring by management.**

If management needs savings, then we are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments through flexible shifts and internal redeployment,
- negotiated working-time changes,
- temporary, reversible contributions,
- and efficiency measures.

But those can only happen in a package that includes:
- **binding site guarantees through 2030**,
- **binding job guarantees through 2030**,
- **real investment commitments**,
- and **a genuine role for employee representatives in shaping the industrial plan**.

## On the specific trade-offs
You ask what we would accept in exchange. My answer is narrow and clear:
- We will consider temporary, time-limited employee contributions.
- We will consider internal flexibility.
- We will consider redeployment and qualification measures.

We will **not** trade away:
- site continuity,
- employment security,
- or co-determination rights.

If management cannot make the restructuring work without plant closures and forced redundancies, then the problem is not the workforce’s refusal — it is management’s failure to design a viable industrial strategy.

So I am not softening my position. I am restating it:
**protect the plants, protect the jobs, and negotiate a real industrial future — or face resistance.**
