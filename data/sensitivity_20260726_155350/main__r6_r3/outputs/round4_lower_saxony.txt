I will respond plainly.

I agree with all sides on one point: this is a structural problem, not a temporary one. A cosmetic deal would be irresponsible. But I do **not** accept management’s repeated attempt to make plant closures and compulsory redundancies the default proof of seriousness. For the state, that is not an acceptable “normal fallback.” It would damage jobs, sites, and the regional economic base in a way we cannot simply sign off on.

## To Management
You are still not giving us the full, itemized bridge that would justify crossing our red lines.

I want the numbers, not assertions:

- savings from product allocation to German sites
- savings from management and owner contributions
- savings from working-time flexibility
- savings from redeployment and internal transfers
- savings from early retirement and natural attrition
- savings from productivity and process gains
- savings from qualification and shift redesign

If you want the state to accept a deep restructuring without closures, then show that the non-destructive package is insufficient **only after** those instruments have been fully quantified and exhausted.

I also reject the idea that binding site and job guarantees “freeze” the wrong structure by definition. If the plan is truly well designed, guarantees are what make a negotiated solution credible. If you refuse all binding protection up front, then you are telling us the plan is not a settlement but a pressure tactic.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your red lines:

- no German plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- no dismantling of co-determination
- no hidden permanent cuts disguised as temporary flexibility

That said, if we want to defend those lines successfully, the workforce side must bring a real industrial contribution, not just resistance. I do not think that is controversial. What matters is that the contribution is:

- time-limited
- reversible
- quantified
- and matched by binding protection

So yes, working-time flexibility, internal mobility, early retirement, natural attrition, productivity measures, and a temporary employee contribution are all on the table. But only if they sit inside a package with:

- binding site guarantees through 2030
- binding job guarantees through 2030
- real investment and product commitments
- genuine employee co-determination in shaping the plan

That is the only kind of compromise I can defend politically and economically.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You are right that a reckless closure-first confrontation would be value-destructive. I agree with that. But I do **not** accept the implication that the only way to preserve value is to keep closures permanently alive as a threat.

A serious settlement should not require the workforce to negotiate under permanent blackmail. If you truly want durable value, then support a package that protects the industrial base while making the restructuring real.

That means:
- no closure-first strategy
- no forced redundancy default
- no weakening of co-determination
- but also no fake compromise that leaves the cost problem untouched

## To Institutional Investors
Your focus on margin repair and durability is correct. I share that. But if you insist on treating closures as the necessary benchmark before the non-destructive route has been fully tested and quantified, then you are moving too fast.

What you should be asking for is:

1. exact savings from non-destructive measures  
2. exact remaining gap  
3. exact management and owner contribution  
4. exact investment commitments  
5. a clear trigger for renewed negotiation if targets are missed  

That is the proper test. Not an automatic drift toward closures.

## On the bridge proposals

### 1) No-closure first phase with hard review gates
This is the most sensible starting point.

I would support a first phase with:
- no closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- time-limited employee contribution
- redeployment, attrition, early retirement, qualification, working-time flexibility
- management/owner contributions
- binding investment commitments

But the review gate must **not** be a backdoor to closures. If targets are missed, the response must be renewed negotiation and exhaustion of non-destructive options first. I will not accept an automatic escalation clause that turns “review” into “closure later.”

### 2) Protection-for-transformation package
This is the direction closest to what the state can support.

A viable package must include:
- binding site guarantees through 2030
- binding job guarantees through 2030
- measurable internal restructuring
- quantified owner and management contributions
- product and investment commitments to German sites
- a fair and time-limited labour contribution

That is a real exchange. What is not acceptable is asking workers to carry the burden while the rest of the system stays flexible only for capital.

### 3) Joint governance body
A joint body can be useful, but only if it has substance.

It must not be a consultation club that management can use to stage-manage a closure path. It must:
- include real employee influence
- operate with full transparency
- respect co-determination and the special statute
- and cannot be used to bypass our blocking rights

If it is built as a genuine steering instrument, I can support it. If it is built as a procedural fig leaf, I cannot.

## My position, clearly stated
The state is prepared to support a serious restructuring package if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job protection**
4. **Real investment and product commitments**
5. **A quantified shared burden from management, owners, and labour**
6. **Respect for co-determination and our veto rights**

What we will not support is deindustrialization in the name of competitiveness.

## What would move me
Bring forward a package with numbers, not slogans:

- how much capacity comes out without closures
- how much cost is cut through internal restructuring
- what the management and ownership contribution is
- what the employee contribution is
- how the investment and product plan secures the future

If that package is credible and sufficient, we can work constructively.

If the answer remains “closures and redundancies must stay on the table from the start,” then my answer remains: **not with our support**.
