### Core impasse
The debate has converged on one central fault line:

**Whether the company should commit now to a no-closure / no-forced-redundancy settlement, or whether closures and compulsory redundancies must remain live fallback options until a plant-by-plant residual-gap model proves sufficiency.**

More specifically, the impasse has three layers:
1. **Burden of proof** — employee-side actors want management to prove that non-destructive measures are insufficient before closures are kept on the table; management and investors want proof that the no-closure route closes the recurring gap.
2. **Sequencing** — worker/state actors want binding protections first, then verification; management/investors want verification first, then protections.
3. **Fallback design** — the parties disagree on whether any review mechanism can exist without becoming a backdoor to shutdowns.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions

#### 1) **Phased no-closure agreement with locked first phase**
A possible bridge would be:
- **Phase 1:** immediate no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, time-limited employee contribution, management/owner contribution, full transparency, independent verification.
- **Phase 2:** only triggered after independent review, but limited to additional non-destructive measures first; destructive options remain excluded unless a supermajority governance threshold is met after a second verification round.

This may address:
- the employee/state demand for immediate protection,
- the management/investor demand for verification,
- the family’s concern about value-destroying delay.

#### 2) **No-closure settlement with a quantified escape threshold**
A second bridge would be:
- a binding no-closure settlement through 2030,
- paired with a **pre-agreed residual-gap threshold** defined in advance,
- if the threshold is exceeded after all internal measures, the parties must reopen talks on additional burden-sharing before any site action is possible.

This may address:
- management’s need for an economic trigger,
- the union/works council demand that closures not be a standing threat,
- investors’ demand for a defined test rather than open-ended uncertainty.

#### 3) **Hybrid footprint stability plus controlled adjustment package**
A third option would be:
- protect all German sites from closure for a defined period,
- allow internal capacity adjustment, product allocation changes, redeployment, and targeted working-time measures,
- combine this with stronger owner and management contributions,
- include an independent midterm review that can only recommend non-destructive adjustments unless all parties agree otherwise.

This may bridge:
- regional/state and worker concerns about industrial base protection,
- management/investor concerns about untested structural cost burdens,
- family concerns about prolonged instability.
