Management is still framing this as if the only credible route is closures and compulsory redundancies. I do not accept that, and I want to be explicit about why.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state, and even parts of what the family holding and investors are saying:
- This is a **structural** problem, not a temporary dip.
- A cosmetic patch will not be enough.
- The outcome has to be **durable** and **implementable**.
- The company needs a credible industrial plan and investment commitments.

I also agree that employees cannot simply say “no” and leave the economics untouched. We have already signaled willingness to engage on:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- early retirement,
- natural attrition,
- and a fair employee contribution.

That openness is real. But it is not unlimited.

## Where I disagree

### To Management
You still have not shown that plant closures and forced redundancies are unavoidable. You keep presenting them as the only serious answer, but that is not a proof — it is a preference.

If you want the workforce to contribute, then you need to answer a very simple question:  
**What is management and ownership contributing?**

Because from where I stand, the burden must not fall primarily on employees through:
- wage cuts,
- bonus cuts,
- longer hours,
- and then layoffs on top.

That is not a shared-sacrifice package. That is workers paying twice.

If management wants a negotiated settlement, then it must put something concrete on the table:
- binding site security,
- binding product allocation,
- a real investment commitment,
- and a clearly limited, reversible labour-cost contribution.

Without that, your “structural adjustment” simply means job cuts first and promises later. We will not endorse that.

### To the Family Holding and Investors
You are right that the company must regain competitiveness. But you cannot reduce competitiveness to headcount cuts and closure threats.

A package that destroys trust, triggers prolonged strike action, and undermines co-determination is not “discipline”; it is a high-risk gamble. If you insist on treating closures and forced redundancies as routine tools, you are underestimating both the governance structure and the industrial consequences.

You want a package that is durable and value-preserving. Then support a package that:
- avoids closures,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- reduces capacity through non-destructive means,
- and asks management and owners to share the pain visibly.

### To the State
I appreciate your position, but I need to be blunt: support is not enough if it does not translate into leverage. If you say no closures and no forced redundancies are unacceptable politically and industrially, then that position has to be enforced in the governance process, not just stated.

## Our minimum position
Let me restate this clearly:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- **No restructuring where employees bear the bulk of the pain while others remain protected.**

## What we can discuss
We remain open to a serious negotiated package, including:
- time-limited and reversible labour-cost concessions,
- capacity reduction through natural attrition and internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- productivity improvements,
- and a temporary employee contribution if — and only if — it is tied to enforceable guarantees.

But those guarantees must be real:
- protection of jobs,
- protection of sites,
- protection of the collective framework,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

## The test
If the company can produce a package that achieves meaningful savings **without** plant closures and **without** compulsory redundancies, we are prepared to negotiate it seriously.

If the only package management can offer is one that shifts the adjustment onto the workforce, then we will oppose it with the full bargaining power we have. We have already used warning strikes, and we are prepared to escalate if necessary.

So my position remains unchanged:
**no closures, no forced layoffs, and no one-sided sacrifices. Bring a fair package, or expect resistance.**
