From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the discussion still comes down to one question: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins, or does it preserve a structurally impaired cost base under the label of compromise?**

## Where we agree

I agree with management, the family holding, and to a point the state, on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity appears real,
- and a cosmetic settlement will not fix the equity story.

I also agree with the works council and union on one important point:
- a prolonged strike, governance deadlock, or industrial confrontation would destroy value very quickly.

So far, that is the only real common ground.

## Where I disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
I respect the desire to protect jobs and sites, but I do **not** support an outcome that effectively freezes the current structure and leaves the company uncompetitive.

Your position still reads to us as:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of guarantees,
- but also no clear willingness to accept savings that are large enough to restore margins.

That is not credible from a capital markets perspective unless you can show, with numbers, that the alternative package delivers **equivalent durable cost and capacity reduction**. We have not seen that yet.

Temporary measures, attrition, redeployment, and time-limited contributions may help, but they do not automatically add up to a serious restructuring. If they do not close the gap, then they are simply delay.

### To Management
You are right that the problem is structural. But I also see a risk in the way you are framing the solution: if the package becomes too reliant on plant closures and forced redundancies, you may provoke exactly the kind of conflict that wipes out the intended savings.

So I am **not** asking you to be soft. I am asking you to be implementable.

A plan that is economically strong but politically impossible is not a good plan. If your preferred route cannot pass co-determination and triggers prolonged industrial action, then it is not value-maximising either.

### To the State and Family Owners
We understand the political constraints and the long-term ownership logic. But political discomfort is not the same thing as economic impossibility.

If the company’s margins are structurally impaired, then the governance bloc should not hide behind site guarantees that merely postpone the adjustment. At the same time, if you block every hard measure, you should not expect capital markets to give the company credit for discipline.

## Our standard remains unchanged

We will support only a package that is:
1. **material**
   - real cost reduction, not symbolic savings

2. **durable**
   - not temporary deferrals or one-off measures

3. **credible on capacity**
   - overcapacity must come out somehow

4. **implementable**
   - it must survive the governance and labour reality

5. **margin-repairing**
   - there must be a believable path back to target returns

## What would move us
We do not require plant closures as an ideological goal. But we do require **visible structural change**.

So we would look for:
- quantified and enforceable capacity reduction,
- a meaningful and durable labour-cost contribution,
- productivity gains with measurable delivery,
- and a settlement that can actually be implemented without endless recrimination.

If the no-closure alternative can genuinely deliver that, fine. If not, then the market will treat it as a cosmetic compromise.

## Bottom line
We are not attached to closures for their own sake, and we are not demanding confrontation. But we **are** saying that:
- a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive is unacceptable,
- and a settlement that cannot be implemented is also unacceptable.

So the burden is now on the employee side and management together:
- either produce a package that clearly restores competitiveness,
- or acknowledge that the current positions are still too far apart to justify investor support.

If the next round does not show a real bridge to competitive margins, our position will remain: **no support for a deal that preserves the cost problem under a softer name.**
