As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. The workforce has already delivered flexibility, productivity and sacrifices over many years. What we will not accept now is a restructuring that shifts the burden onto employees while management tears up historic job-security commitments.

Our priorities are:

- Keep every German plant open
- Secure jobs for the long term, at least through 2030
- Protect co-determination and meaningful employee influence over any restructuring
- Achieve any necessary adjustments through negotiated, socially responsible measures, not forced layoffs
- Ensure that any contribution from the workforce is matched by binding guarantees on investment, product allocation, and site security

If management wants a sustainable solution, then it must come with:
- binding site guarantees
- binding job guarantees
- a credible industrial plan for the German plants
- real participation for employee representatives in shaping the future model

We are willing to discuss alternative savings measures — working-time adjustments, temporary flexibility, efficiency programs, internal redeployment, and a fair negotiated employee contribution — but only if they explicitly avoid closures and forced job cuts.

If the proposal still relies on closing plants, cutting thousands of jobs, or dismantling the job-security framework, then we will oppose it decisively. We are prepared to resist that with all the means available to us, including industrial action and using our co-determination rights to block any unacceptable plan.
