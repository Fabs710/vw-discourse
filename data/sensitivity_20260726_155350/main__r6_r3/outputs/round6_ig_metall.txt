I will be equally direct.

## To the works council
I agree with your core red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- no settlement that shifts the structural risk onto workers.

I also agree that labour should not be asked to carry the full burden while management, owners, and investors preserve all their options.

Where I sharpen the line is this: we should not let the debate freeze at “management hasn’t proven enough yet.” That is true, but it is not enough. We need a **binding, quantified package** that makes a no-closure settlement economically testable. Otherwise management will keep calling the same uncertainty a reason to retain destructive fallback tools.

So yes: I support your demand for plant-by-plant numbers. But I also insist that those numbers must be tied to a **real no-closure commitment**, not a disguised path back to shutdowns.

## To management
You are right about one thing: this company needs a structural answer, not a cosmetic one.

Where I disagree is your repeated implication that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available unless labour proves they are unnecessary. That is the wrong burden of proof.

If you want the workforce to accept sacrifices, then you must first show:
- the plant-by-plant residual gap,
- the savings from management and owner contribution,
- the savings from product allocation,
- the savings from working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- early retirement,
- attrition,
- productivity and overhead cuts,
- and the timing of all of this.

Until then, closure talk is not “responsible realism”; it is leverage.

And I will be blunt: if your plan still depends on threatening sites and jobs, then it is not a credible transformation plan. It is social pressure dressed up as strategy.

## To the controlling family holding
You say you want long-term value. Then prove it by rejecting the lazy assumption that closures are the natural fallback.

A serious owner should support:
- binding site guarantees through 2030,
- binding job guarantees through 2030,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- and real owner contributions.

If the no-closure bridge later proves insufficient, then we can talk again. But you do not get to keep permanent insecurity on the table while asking workers to make concessions upfront. That is not stewardship.

## To institutional investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value.

But I reject your framing that a no-closure deal is only acceptable if it is “economically sufficient” in the abstract while closures remain live as an ever-present threat. If you want sufficiency, then demand the full bridge from management and insist on real owner and management contributions.

What you should not do is use “verified gap” language as a cover for preserving destructive options before the non-destructive route has been exhausted. That would simply reproduce the same conflict under a more technical label.

## To the regional state government
Your stance is materially closer to ours than management’s.

But your leverage must become concrete. If you oppose closures and forced redundancies, then use that power to secure:
- a binding no-closure settlement,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- and co-determination in shaping the industrial plan.

Do not wait for management to “discover” social responsibility. Make it part of the governance outcome.

## On the expert assessments
The expert input confirmed something important:
- internal measures can materially reduce the gap,
- but the final answer depends on the residual gap and timing.

That is exactly why we need a proper bridge. But the expert did **not** say closures are automatically justified. The expert said the no-closure route can work if the gap is moderate and capacity can be adjusted internally.

That means:
- management has not proven that destructive measures are necessary,
- labour has not been disproven,
- and the default should remain a negotiated no-closure solution.

## What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a serious package based on:
- redeployment,
- early retirement,
- natural attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification,
- productivity and process improvements,
- product allocation,
- and a strictly time-limited employee contribution,

**provided that** the agreement includes:
- binding site guarantees through 2030
- binding job guarantees through 2030
- no compulsory redundancies
- no German plant closures
- real co-determination in the industrial plan
- management and owner contributions
- binding investment and product commitments

That is the only fair basis for burden-sharing.

## What I reject
I reject:
- closures as a standing fallback,
- compulsory redundancies,
- permanent insecurity disguised as “review,”
- a “numbers first” process that leaves jobs exposed while management shops for a mandate,
- and any solution that asks workers to absorb the entire structural adjustment while capital keeps the exit door open.

## My position on the procedural proposals
### Two-stage agreement with conditional protections
I can accept this only if Phase 1 contains real, immediate protection:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- time-limited employee contribution,
- management and owner contribution,
- full disclosure,
- independent verification.

If Phase 2 is merely a trapdoor back to destruction, then it is unacceptable.

### No-closure settlement with a hard review gate
Only acceptable if the review is **not** an automatic escalation mechanism to closures. Any follow-up must first exhaust additional non-destructive measures.

### Partial guarantees
I reject partial guarantees that protect only selected sites or groups. That would split the workforce and undermine the collective wage framework.

## Bottom line
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing restructuring by social demolition.

If management wants a real settlement, bring:
1. the quantified bridge,
2. management and owner contributions,
3. product and investment commitments,
4. binding site and job guarantees,
5. and a genuine no-closure commitment.

Until then, my answer remains unchanged:

**No German plant closures. No compulsory redundancies. No dismantling of job security. No retreat from co-determination.**

If management refuses that basis, we will escalate industrial action strategically.
