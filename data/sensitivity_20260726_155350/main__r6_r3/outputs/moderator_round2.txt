### Core impasse
The central impasse is a **difference in what counts as an acceptable restructuring outcome**:

- **Management / investors** want a package that delivers **clear, durable cost and capacity reduction**, and they want to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies available** if needed.
- **Works council / union / state** want restructuring only if it **rules out German plant closures and compulsory redundancies**, and they want binding site and job protections.
- The dispute is not over whether change is needed, but over **whether structural adjustment must include destructive measures** or can be achieved through **negotiated, non-destructive tools** with binding safeguards.

So the deadlock is:  
**management demands structural certainty; employee-side and state demand structural protection.**  
Neither side yet accepts the other side’s definition of a viable “durable” solution.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three possible bridging positions for further discussion.

#### 1) Protected restructuring with measurable triggers
A compromise package could combine:
- **no immediate closures / no compulsory redundancies**
- **time-limited employee contributions** (working-time flexibility, wage restraint, productivity measures)
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **a quantified cost-reduction target**
- **a review mechanism with objective triggers**

**Bridge logic:**  
This gives employees and the state formal protection up front, while giving management and investors a credible mechanism to verify whether the package is sufficient. If targets are missed, the parties would have to reopen talks rather than jump directly to closure.

---

#### 2) Site guarantees in exchange for deeper internal restructuring
A second option is:
- **site guarantees through 2030**
- **job guarantees through 2030, but with redeployment and qualification obligations**
- **capacity reduction achieved by shifts, internal transfer, natural attrition, early retirement, and product reallocation**
- **shared burden from management, owners, and workforce**

**Bridge logic:**  
This keeps the red lines of the employee side intact while still allowing meaningful restructuring. The trade-off is that the company gets flexibility and savings, but not through plant destruction.

---

#### 3) Phased restructuring with a joint governance body
A third compromise is a **phased model**:
- Phase 1: non-destructive measures only
- Phase 2: independent verification of savings and competitiveness impact
- Joint steering body with management, employee reps, state, and owners
- Any further measures require **joint sign-off** after Phase 1 results are assessed

**Bridge logic:**  
This reduces mutual distrust by making implementation conditional on verified outcomes. It also creates a shared governance process so no side feels the other is imposing a final settlement unilaterally.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
If closures and compulsory redundancies are not yet your first step, **what quantified cost gap remains after you apply redeployment, attrition, working-time measures, productivity gains, and owner contributions — and what exact threshold would make you rule out closures?**

#### To the Works Council
If site and job guarantees are essential, **what specific, measurable internal measures and workforce contributions are you prepared to accept so that management cannot argue the no-closure path is economically insufficient?**

#### To the Union
What is the **maximum time-limited employee contribution** you would support, and under what **binding conditions** would that contribution remain reversible rather than becoming a permanent downgrade?

#### To the Regional State Government
If you oppose closures and compulsory redundancies, **what concrete leverage will the state actually use to enforce a no-closure settlement, and what would you do if management says the no-closure package still misses the cost target?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support **binding site guarantees** if they are paired with verifiable internal restructuring and targeted capacity reduction, even if that means accepting a slower or less dramatic cost improvement than management originally wanted?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What level of **verified structural cost reduction** would you consider sufficient if it is achieved **without closures**, and would you support a phased agreement if it includes enforceable milestones and a fallback review mechanism?
