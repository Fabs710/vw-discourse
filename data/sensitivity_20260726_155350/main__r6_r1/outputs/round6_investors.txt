We agree on the diagnosis: the current German cost base and capacity structure is not competitive, and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value.

But we do **not** accept the employee-side attempt to hard-code:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and full job-security guarantees through 2030

as if those were compatible with a credible return to margin targets **by default**. They may be politically understandable, but they are not yet financially convincing.

## Where we agree
- **Restructuring is necessary.**
- **Temporary fixes are not enough.**
- **A prolonged strike would be value-destructive.**
- **Any deal must be durable and operationally credible.**
- **A site-by-site audit is the right next step.**

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
You keep saying closures are “not proven unavoidable.” We agree with that statement — and it cuts both ways. It means **closures are not yet justified**, but it also means **non-closure alternatives are not yet proven sufficient**.

That is the real issue.

From our perspective, a package that preserves every site and every protection may still fail if it leaves:
- excess capacity in place,
- fixed costs too high,
- and the margin problem unresolved.

We will not back a solution that merely postpones the hard adjustment and lets equity value erode further.

### To Management
We support your insistence on durable savings and capacity reduction, but you still have not made the case with enough precision. “Closures remain on the table” is not enough. You need a proper business case:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- utilization,
- direct and indirect cost burden,
- and the incremental savings from closures versus non-closure measures.

Without that, capital cannot assess whether the proposed restructuring is truly value-preserving or just a bargaining stance.

### To the Regional State and Family Holding
We understand the concerns about jobs, legitimacy, and governance. But preserving influence and preserving the industrial base cannot become a veto on economically necessary change. If the company stays structurally weak, everyone loses.

At the same time, we also do not support reckless restructuring. A strike, brand damage, and execution failure would also destroy value. So the answer has to be economically real, not politically convenient.

## What would move us
We would support a non-closure package if it is:
- **quantified**
- **independently verified**
- **durable**
- and **material enough to restore competitiveness**

That means it must show:
1. recurring savings, not one-off cuts,
2. real capacity reduction in practice,
3. a credible path back to target margins,
4. and a labor peace period long enough to realize the savings.

## What we can support
We are open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- function pooling,
- product reallocation,
- and targeted consolidation,

**if and only if** they close the gap enough to restore competitiveness.

## What we cannot support
We cannot support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves all sites while margins remain weak,
- or a settlement that shifts the entire adjustment burden onto shareholders through continued value destruction.

## On the audit
We support the joint audit, but it has to be decision-useful, not ceremonial.

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with access to plant costs, utilization, product allocation, indirect overhead, and the savings from non-closure versus closure/consolidation options]

The audit should answer one question clearly:
**Can a non-closure package deliver durable, competitive margins without leaving excess cost and capacity in place?**

If yes, we will support that route.  
If no, then deeper structural measures remain necessary.

That is our position.
