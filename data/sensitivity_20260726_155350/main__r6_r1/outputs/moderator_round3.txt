## Procedural readout: core impasse

The central impasse is between two different definitions of a “durable solution”:

- **Employee side, state, and to some extent the expert**: restructuring is acceptable only if it **preserves German plants, avoids compulsory redundancies, and protects co-determination**, while still delivering meaningful savings.
- **Management, investors, and controlling family**: restructuring must **materially reduce fixed cost and capacity**, and they are not willing to rule out closures or deeper structural measures if needed.

So the dispute is not over whether change is needed. It is over **which instruments are permissible to achieve it** and **who bears the burden of proving sufficiency**.

A second, related impasse is about **sequencing and trust**:
- employee side wants binding “no closure / no layoffs” commitments up front,
- management side wants flexibility first, with outcomes verified later.

That sequencing gap is blocking any converging package.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### 1) Jointly verified “non-closure first” package with hard checkpoints
A compromise could be structured as:

- a **time-limited protected period** with **no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**
- in return, the employee side agrees to a defined package of:
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - retraining
  - bonus/remuneration moderation
- an **independent audit** measures whether the package closes the savings gap
- if the gap remains material at the checkpoint, the parties reopen talks on **targeted consolidation options**, but only with a new mandate and verified data

This bridges the demand for proof with the demand for immediate protection.

### 2) Protected consolidation without shutdowns
A second bridge is:

- keep all German sites formally open
- reduce capacity through **internal consolidation**, such as:
  - shift reductions
  - line reallocation
  - function pooling
  - partial specialization by site
  - product transfers
- pair this with:
  - limited employee concessions
  - investment commitments
  - a moratorium on compulsory redundancies
  - co-determination oversight on implementation

This gives management real fixed-cost relief while respecting the employee side’s red line on formal closures.

### 3) Guaranteed site protection in exchange for stronger economic concessions
A third option is:

- a binding **no-closure commitment through a defined horizon**
- no compulsory redundancies
- but a stronger, quantified employee contribution:
  - longer working-time flexibility
  - stronger bonus moderation
  - stricter redeployment obligations
  - narrower legacy benefits for a limited period
- in parallel, management/owner commitments on:
  - product allocation
  - investment
  - management contribution
  - governance transparency

This only works if the combined savings are demonstrably large enough to satisfy capital-side concerns.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If an independent audit shows that the needed savings cannot be fully achieved without reducing capacity at one or more sites, **what specific non-closure measures are you prepared to accept instead, and what quantified savings would make that package sufficient for you?**

### Industrial Union
Would you accept a **time-limited, audited restructuring phase** with no closures and no compulsory redundancies, if it included stronger working-time and remuneration concessions and a clearly defined review point based on jointly verified numbers?

### Group & Brand Management
What exact minimum savings target must be met for you to rule out closures, and are you prepared to publish the site-by-site economic assumptions that define that threshold?

### Independent Expert
What would the audit need to show, quantitatively, for a **no-closure solution** to be economically credible, and what would make closure or consolidation economically more likely?

### Regional State Government
Are you prepared to convert your political opposition to closures into a **formal conditional mandate** for a no-closure restructuring period, backed by concrete leverage if management rejects the audit-based path?

### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a restructuring package that preserves all German sites but requires deeper temporary concessions from owners and management, if the audit shows that this would deliver equivalent durable savings?

### Institutional Investors
If the audit identifies a non-closure package that is slightly more expensive in the short term but materially lowers execution risk and conflict risk, would you support it as value-preserving?
