### 1) Points of agreement
- All sides agree the current cost/capacity situation is not sustainable.
- There is broad support for a negotiated solution rather than prolonged confrontation.
- Most parties accept that temporary or cosmetic measures alone will not be sufficient.
- There is general recognition that any settlement must be durable and operationally credible.
- Several sides indicate willingness to consider alternatives to outright closures if they deliver comparable savings.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works Council, union, and regional state reject them as a baseline; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side rejects them categorically; management and capital-side actors do not accept an absolute ban.
- **Job-security guarantees through 2030:** Employee side wants binding protection; management and capital-side actors say unchanged guarantees are unrealistic.
- **Scope of labor contribution:** Management and investors want meaningful labor-cost relief; employee side wants contributions only as part of a broader, balanced package.
- **Governance control:** The family holding stresses that ownership/governance structures are not up for renegotiation; workforce and state emphasize co-determination and board influence.
- **State/shareholder role:** The regional state favors restructuring but resists solutions that marginalize employment, sites, or its statutory role.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **durable cost reductions** without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- Whether **job and site guarantees** can be limited, conditional, or phased rather than absolute.
- What level of **employee contribution** is acceptable in exchange for security commitments.
- How to combine **capacity reduction, redeployment, qualification, and working-time tools** into a package that meets savings targets.
- What **investment commitments** and future product plans would accompany any concessions.
- How to prevent a deal that is either **socially unacceptable** or **financially insufficient**.

### 4) Brief refocus for next round
Please move from principles to **specific package options**. Each side should identify:
- its non-negotiables,
- one or two possible concessions,
- and the minimum conditions under which it would support a deal.

The next round should focus on **a quantified restructuring package**: expected savings, affected sites or functions, employment impacts, and any binding guarantees or investment commitments.
