I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the basic diagnosis:
- the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable,
- cosmetic measures will not be enough,
- and a durable, negotiated solution is preferable to a long conflict.

I also agree with the expert’s central point: **the record does not yet prove that German plant closures are economically unavoidable**. That matters.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s attempt to make closures and compulsory redundancies the default answer.

You still have not shown, with site-by-site evidence, that the company cannot be restructured without crossing the red lines on jobs and plants. “Durable fixed-cost relief” is not a license to pre-decide closures.

And I do **not** accept the idea that the state’s role is to “help shape” a package that may ultimately hollow out the regional industrial base. My role is to prevent exactly that.

## To management
You say you need:
- durable capacity reduction,
- labor-cost relief,
- lower fixed cost per vehicle,
- and a path back to target margins.

Fine. But you have not proven that these objectives require plant closures in Germany. Not yet.

If you want serious support from the state shareholder, then bring:
- a **site-by-site economic case**
- a **quantified savings bridge**
- and a **clear comparison of non-closure measures versus closure scenarios**

Until then, closures remain a **proposal**, not a necessity.

And I must be clear: I will not support a settlement that gutts regional employment or treats the state’s co-determination and blocking rights as decorative.

## To the Works Council and Union
I agree with your insistence on:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no bypassing of co-determination,
- and no hollowing out of the job-security framework.

That is legitimate and necessary.

But I also need to be candid: an absolute “everything stays exactly as it is” stance will not be enough if the audit shows real fixed-cost and capacity gaps. You are right to demand proof; you are not yet right to assume that all capacity can be preserved at current structure indefinitely.

So: I support your red lines, but I expect a **serious non-closure package** with quantified savings, not only principles.

## To the controlling family and investors
I hear the concerns about competitiveness and enterprise value. They are real.

But value is not protected by forcing through a socially explosive restructuring that weakens the company’s legitimacy in its home region. A company like this is not only a financial asset; it is a regional anchor.

If you want durability, you need:
- labor peace,
- investment,
- product commitment,
- and a restructuring that does not destroy trust.

That is not soft. It is strategic.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a restructuring if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A quantified, audited savings package**
4. **Real capacity and cost reduction through non-closure measures first**
5. **Binding investment and future product commitments**
6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
7. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**

That is the framework I can defend politically and economically.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a package that reduces jobs and sites in the state as the easy way out,
- a restructuring that sidelines the state shareholder,
- or a deal that asks the workforce to carry the pain while ownership and management avoid meaningful contribution.

## What needs to happen next
We need an independent, jointly mandated audit with full access to:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- utilization,
- product allocation,
- indirect costs,
- and the savings potential of non-closure measures versus consolidation or closure.

[SUMMON: Joint independent site-by-site restructuring audit with industrial-economics and execution-risk assessment]

That audit must answer one question clearly:
**Can the company achieve the required durable savings without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies, while preserving the regional industrial base?**

If yes, that is the path I will back.  
If no, then the burden on management to justify any deeper step becomes much higher — and the state will use its blocking rights to protect jobs, sites, and co-determination.

## Bottom line
I am not here to block restructuring.
I am here to block a restructuring that sacrifices the regional economic anchor.

If management wants my support, it must prove that the company can be made competitive **without gutting regional employment or sidelining the state’s role**. Until then, closures are not an accepted solution; they are an unresolved threat.
