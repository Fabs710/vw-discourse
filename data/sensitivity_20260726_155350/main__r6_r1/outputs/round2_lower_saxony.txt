I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **the status quo is not sustainable**.  
The cost base is too high, the market is under pressure, and the company needs a restructuring that is **durable**, not cosmetic.

I also agree with the workforce side that a prolonged confrontation would be damaging. Strikes, deadlock, and public escalation would harm the company, the region, and value creation.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the idea that the only credible restructuring is one that includes **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies**.

That crosses a line for us.

As a regional anchor shareholder, our responsibility is not only to balance the books; it is to protect the company as an industrial base in the region. If restructuring becomes a synonym for hollowing out German employment and weakening the state’s role, then we are not talking about a viable solution — we are talking about a politically and economically destructive one.

## To management
You say the numbers already justify tough measures. I do not dispute the need for change, but I reject the leap from “change is needed” to “closures and forced layoffs are necessary.”

If you want support from us, then you need to show that you have genuinely exhausted alternatives that preserve sites and jobs:
- capacity reductions without closure,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time instruments,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary remuneration adjustments,
- and product and investment commitments that strengthen future utilization.

I am open to **targeted, binding capacity adjustments**. I am **not** open to a package whose core logic is to sacrifice sites and then call that strategy.

## To the Works Council and Union
I agree with your rejection of **forced redundancies** and with your demand that co-determination remain intact. I also agree that the workforce should not carry the burden alone.

But I need to be equally clear: a blanket refusal of any productivity, flexibility, or cost contribution would also be irresponsible. If the company is to remain competitive, there must be a negotiated employee contribution — but it must be part of a broader bargain, not a one-sided extraction.

So yes:
- no forced redundancies,
- no bypassing of co-determination,
- no arbitrary dismantling of site protections.

But also yes to:
- flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary cost relief,
- and serious internal restructuring measures.

## To the family holding and investors
I understand the concern for competitiveness and value. I share it. But a restructuring that destroys social peace, creates lasting political conflict, and weakens the company’s regional base is not a value-maximizing solution.

Durable value depends on execution capacity, labor peace, and legitimacy. If those are lost, then the financial logic collapses too.

## My position
I am prepared to support a restructuring if it contains all of the following:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No blanket plant closures in Germany**
3. **Binding site and employment guarantees where economically possible**
4. **A meaningful but fair employee contribution**
5. **Visible management and owner concessions**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies in the region**
7. **Full respect for co-determination and the state’s statutory role**

## What I can consider
I can consider:
- capacity rationalization,
- voluntary workforce reductions,
- internal transfers,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary wage or bonus moderation,
- and targeted consolidation of functions,

provided these are part of a package that **protects the regional industrial base** and does not make closures or forced layoffs the starting point.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a restructuring that guts regional employment,
- a deal that sidelines the state shareholder,
- a package that breaks the social partnership model,
- or an outcome that treats co-determination as an obstacle rather than part of the solution.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking **the wrong kind** of restructuring.

If management brings a credible package that restores competitiveness **while preserving as many sites and jobs as possible**, we will engage constructively and use our leverage to make that deal possible. If the proposal remains “closures first, labor pays, and the rest of us follow,” then we will not support it.
