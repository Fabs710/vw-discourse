I will respond plainly, because we are now at the point where ambiguity is just another way of delaying decisions.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central fact: **the current structure is not sustainable**.

- The European market is shrinking.
- The cost base in Germany is too high.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- We need durable, not cosmetic, change.
- A prolonged open conflict would damage the company further.

So on diagnosis, there is broad agreement. The disagreement is about whether the company is willing to make the structural changes that diagnosis requires.

## Where I disagree
### To the Works Council and the Union
Your position remains:  
- no plant closures,  
- no compulsory redundancies,  
- no meaningful change to legacy protections,  
- and yet the company should somehow still achieve a fundamentally lower cost base.

That is not a restructuring plan. That is a veto on restructuring.

I respect that you want to protect jobs and sites. But you are still asking us to believe that the existing footprint, existing guarantees, and existing cost structure can somehow produce the competitiveness we need by 2027, 2030, and beyond. I do not accept that.

You keep demanding “proof” that closures are necessary. My answer is: **the burden is not only on management to justify every instrument; the burden is also on you to produce an alternative that actually closes the gap**.

And let me be very direct:  
- working-time flexibility,  
- internal redeployment,  
- voluntary exits,  
- retraining,  
- and temporary moderation  

may help, but they are **not automatically sufficient** to deliver the scale of durable savings we need.

If you want to rule out closures and forced redundancies, then show me a package that delivers:
- real capacity reduction,
- real labor-cost relief,
- and a lasting improvement in margins.

Not slogans. Not principles. Numbers.

### To the Regional State Government
I appreciate your rejection of a pure closure-and-layoff strategy. I do. But I cannot accept a position that effectively says: “restructure, but do not touch the industrial footprint in any serious way.”

If the company is to remain investable, then some hard choices are unavoidable. The state’s role cannot be to freeze the cost base and declare that a solution. That would be managed decline.

You are right that social peace and execution matter. But social peace without competitiveness is not a strategy. It is postponement.

### To the Family Holding
I agree that the long-term value of the enterprise must be protected. But long-term value is not protected by insisting on a level of labor-cost relief that is too weak to change the economics.

You speak about avoiding cosmetic change. I agree. Then we must be honest: if a package leaves all essential structures intact and only trims the edges, it is cosmetic.

### To the Institutional Investors
You are right that value destruction is unacceptable. But value destruction also occurs if we preserve every site and every legacy protection while margins remain structurally weak. Then we are simply managing decline more politely.

## What management requires
I will state our position again, clearly and without apology:

We need a package that delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Meaningful labor-cost relief**
3. **A lower cost per vehicle**
4. **A path back to the brand’s return-on-sales target**
5. **Investment capacity for future products and technology through 2030**

That is the minimum. Anything else fails the business.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am not fixated on closures as the only instrument. I have said that before, and I repeat it.

I am prepared to consider:
- partial site consolidation,
- production reallocation,
- function pooling,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- and negotiated adjustments to legacy arrangements.

But these are acceptable only if they produce **structural**, **durable**, and **quantified** savings at the necessary scale.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a deal that keeps every German plant open by definition,
- a ban on compulsory redundancies in all circumstances,
- legacy guarantees untouched through 2030,
- and then a claim that competitiveness will somehow follow.

That would not be a compromise. It would be a refusal to confront reality.

I also cannot accept the idea that an independent audit is useful only if it confirms your preferred answer in advance. If we do a verification exercise, it must be credible and it must be open to hard conclusions. Otherwise it is just theater.

## [SUMMON: Independent restructuring and industrial-cost expert]
We need an independent, jointly accepted expert team to verify:
- site-by-site cost structures,
- capacity utilization,
- achievable savings from non-closure measures,
- the labor-cost gap versus competitors,
- and the execution risk of different restructuring paths.

That is the only way to end the endless argument over whose narrative is more convenient.

## My minimum conditions for a deal
A deal must include:
- a **durable reduction in capacity**,
- a **meaningful employee contribution**,
- a **real reduction in labor costs**,
- **employment flexibility**,
- and **certainty for investment and product planning to 2030**.

If the employee side brings a package that truly achieves those outcomes without closures, I will evaluate it seriously. But it must be equivalent in effect, not equivalent only in rhetoric.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve the current structure.  
I am here to change it in a way that restores competitiveness.

If the other stakeholders want a negotiated settlement, then the discussion must move from “no closures, no layoffs” to **what exact combination of measures produces the necessary structural savings**.

If that cannot be done, then we will continue to pursue tougher measures, because standing still is not an option.
