### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one central issue:

**Whether the company can close the structural savings gap through protected, non-closure measures alone, or whether durable competitiveness ultimately requires German plant closures and/or compulsory redundancies.**

Everything else is secondary to that.  
In procedural terms, the impasse has three layers:

1. **Burden of proof**
   - Employee side: closures are not proven unavoidable.
   - Management/capital side: non-closure alternatives are not yet proven sufficient.

2. **Definition of “protected consolidation”**
   - Workers and state want it to mean genuine site protection.
   - Management and investors want it to remain compatible with later deeper structural options.

3. **Trust in sequencing**
   - Employees fear a protected phase is a prelude to shutdowns.
   - Management and capital fear a no-closure commitment would freeze necessary restructuring.

### Facilitator compromise proposals
These are not endorsements, only possible bridging formulations for Round 7:

#### Proposal 1: Audit-first, decision-gated path
Agree now on a strictly mandated independent audit and a staged decision process:
- Round 7 focuses only on data and scenario modeling.
- No side pre-commits to closures or no-closures before the audit.
- The audit must produce a site-by-site scenario table with:
  - non-closure measures,
  - protected consolidation,
  - closure/deeper footprint reduction,
  - recurring savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - employment effects,
  - payback periods.

**Bridging function:** gives management and capital a path to prove residual gap, while giving labor and state protection against premature irreversible moves.

#### Proposal 2: Time-limited protected consolidation with explicit non-escalation rule
A negotiated interim package could include:
- no German plant closures during the protected phase,
- no compulsory redundancies during that phase,
- employee contributions limited to time-bound measures,
- management/owner contributions also specified,
- and a written rule that no closure decision can be taken unless the audit shows a defined residual gap and a second negotiation round fails.

**Bridging function:** addresses worker fears of a hidden shutdown path, while preserving a later evidence-based discussion if needed.

#### Proposal 3: Site-specific protection plus conditional restructuring reserve
A differentiated compromise could distinguish between sites:
- sites shown by audit to be economically viable remain protected,
- sites with severe underutilization enter a separate consolidation track,
- any closure/conversion discussion is conditional on objective thresholds,
- relocation, specialization, or function pooling are tested before shutdown.

**Bridging function:** avoids an all-or-nothing package and may make room for partial agreement where the economics differ by site.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If the audit shows that one or more German sites remain structurally uneconomic even after maximum non-closure measures, what specific adjustment path would you accept short of closure or compulsory redundancies?

#### To the Union
What employee contribution package would you consider sufficient to make a protected consolidation deal credible without treating it as a disguised prelude to shutdowns?

#### To Group & Brand Management
Will you commit that no closure recommendation will be advanced before the audit delivers site-specific proof of a residual gap after non-closure measures, and if not, why not?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to enforce a genuine non-escalation period while the audit and negotiation run, beyond general political statements?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
What binding governance constraints are you willing to accept so that the protected phase cannot be used as a sequencing device for later irreversible cuts without renewed stakeholder review?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What minimum quantitative threshold would you require before you would regard a non-closure package as sufficiently value-preserving to support over a closure-based alternative?

#### To the Expert
What exact metrics should be treated as decisive in determining whether the residual gap is “small to moderate” versus “large and persistent” for purposes of the next round?
