### Core impasse
The central deadlock is over **whether structural adjustment must include German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or whether comparable savings can be achieved while fully preserving sites and job-security guarantees**.

More specifically, the parties are split on three linked issues:

1. **Instrument choice**
   - Management, family holding, and investors insist on durable capacity and cost reduction, including openness to closures and deeper legacy changes.
   - Works council, union, and regional state reject closures and compulsory redundancies as a starting point.

2. **Proof standard**
   - Employee-side stakeholders ask for **site-by-site quantified proof** that closures are unavoidable.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders say the burden is on the employee side to produce an **equivalent alternative**.

3. **Risk allocation**
   - Workforce-side stakeholders want adjustment risk shared with owners/management and protected by binding guarantees.
   - Management/capital-side stakeholders want a package that delivers lasting competitiveness even if that requires painful structural change.

---

### Possible compromise positions
Below are three facilitator-style bridge options that could narrow the gap:

#### 1) “No-closure-first, trigger-based restructuring” package
A time-limited settlement in which:
- all German plants remain open for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies are allowed during that period,
- the workforce accepts temporary flexibility, working-time reductions, wage/bonus moderation, and redeployment,
- management gets defined performance and savings targets,
- and if targets are missed, the parties pre-negotiate a second-phase review of more structural options.

**Purpose:** gives labor protection now while giving management a credible fallback if savings do not materialize.

#### 2) “Equivalent-savings test” with independent verification
All sides agree to an independent expert audit that evaluates:
- whether closures are economically necessary,
- whether non-closure alternatives can deliver equivalent durable savings,
- what each site contributes to cost, capacity, and future product allocation.

If the audit finds a non-closure path that meets a defined savings threshold, that path is adopted. If not, the parties return to negotiation with a shared factual basis.

**Purpose:** resolves the dispute over proof and reduces accusations of unilateral storytelling.

#### 3) “Protected consolidation” rather than outright closure
Instead of immediate closures, the parties explore:
- partial site consolidation,
- product-line transfers,
- function pooling,
- voluntary exit programs,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and investment commitments tied to the remaining German footprint.

This would allow real capacity reduction and cost relief while avoiding abrupt plant shutdowns and forced layoffs.

**Purpose:** creates structural change without crossing the most politically and socially explosive red lines.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If an independently verified package showed that **some German capacity must be reduced to achieve equivalent savings**, what specific non-closure measures would you accept first, and what threshold would still make you reject the package?

#### Industrial Union
What is the union’s **quantified alternative savings target**, and which exact combination of working-time, wage, redeployment, and voluntary measures would you commit to if closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table?

#### Group & Brand Management
What minimum set of **measurable savings, capacity reductions, and timeline milestones** would qualify, in your view, as a credible alternative to closures and forced redundancies?

#### Regional State Government
What concrete governance or statutory leverage are you prepared to use to enforce a settlement that protects the regional industrial base, and where exactly is your red line if the economics point toward consolidation?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Under what conditions would you support a settlement that preserves sites but requires larger owner and governance concessions, and what evidence would you require to accept that as value-preserving?

#### Institutional Investors
What level of durability, quantified savings, and margin improvement would be sufficient for you to support a non-closure compromise, and how long must the labor peace period be for that support to hold?
