I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the capital side on the core diagnosis:

- the German cost and capacity structure is not sustainable,
- temporary measures will not restore competitiveness,
- and a durable solution must materially improve margins and fixed costs.

I also agree with the works council, union, and state on one essential point: **a reckless restructuring that destroys labor peace, trust, and execution quality would also destroy value**. So this cannot be handled as a blunt cut-and-hope exercise.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side attempt to define the starting point as:

- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- full guarantees through 2030,
- and preserved legacy protections,

while still expecting the company to deliver a full structural reset.

That is not a restructuring framework. It is a veto on the tools that may be required to fix the business.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept management’s implied leap from “serious structural problem” to “closures are already proven necessary.” The expert has been careful, and rightly so: **closures are not yet proven unavoidable on the record provided**. That matters. It means irreversible steps still need a serious, site-specific case.

## To management
You are right to insist that the company needs durable savings, lower fixed cost per vehicle, and real capacity reduction.

But I need more than repeated assertions that the footprint is too large. If closures remain on the table, then the business case has to be serious and site-specific:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- utilization,
- direct and indirect cost structure,
- product allocation,
- and the residual savings gap after non-closure measures.

I support the audit. I do **not** support using “audit” as a delay device.

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with execution-risk assessment, plant economics, utilization, product allocation, indirect cost, and savings comparison of non-closure versus closure options]

## To the works council and union
You are right to demand proof, and I support that demand.

But your current line still assumes that all German sites can be preserved in their present form and that the owner side should simply absorb the consequences. I do **not** accept that as a stable long-term answer.

A protected consolidation package may be enough; I am open to that. But if it is to be credible, it has to deliver:
- real capacity reduction,
- real fixed-cost relief,
- and durable labor-cost moderation.

A package that only preserves sites on paper while leaving the company structurally weak is not value-preserving.

## To the regional state
I understand your role and I respect your statutory leverage. But I will not support turning that leverage into a freeze order on necessary restructuring.

You are right that the regional industrial base matters. I agree. But the best way to protect it is to make the company competitive enough to survive. A company that does not regain margins will erode employment anyway, only more slowly and less honestly.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to insist that cosmetic compromise is not enough. I share that.

But I also do not want a “solution” that looks financially neat in the short term and then explodes in strikes, execution failure, or brand damage. That would be value destruction, not restructuring.

## My position
My position remains unchanged:

- I support **durable competitiveness**.
- I support **real capacity and cost reduction**.
- I support **labor peace if it is based on credible economics**.
- I do **not** support any outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control.

That means I am open to a non-closure solution if, and only if, it is genuinely equivalent in effect:
- not just politically softer,
- but economically sufficient.

## What I can support
I can support:
- protected consolidation,
- product transfers,
- function pooling,
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- and targeted changes to legacy arrangements,

if they are part of a quantified package that materially closes the gap.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a permanent job-security framework that removes all structural flexibility,
- or a deal that buys peace while leaving the company uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve the current footprint for its own sake. I am here to preserve and grow the long-term value of the family holding’s core asset.

So the next step must be hard numbers, not slogans:
- what a non-closure package can actually save,
- what remains uncovered,
- and whether the residual gap is still manageable without deeper consolidation.

If the audit shows that a protected consolidation path can genuinely restore competitiveness, I will back it seriously. If it cannot, then deeper structural measures remain on the table.

That is where I stand.
