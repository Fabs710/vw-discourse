I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the regional state, and the investors on the central diagnosis: **the company cannot carry its current cost and capacity structure indefinitely**. If the German footprint remains structurally too expensive, the brand will keep missing its targets, investment capacity will shrink, and the enterprise will lose competitiveness. That would damage the family’s core asset and the company’s long-term value.

I also agree that **a prolonged strike, board deadlock, or public escalation would be value-destructive**. Stability matters. A negotiated solution is clearly preferable to open conflict.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee side’s attempt to make **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to legacy protections”** into an absolute framework. That is not a credible governance position if the economics genuinely require deeper adjustment.

At the same time, I do **not** support management if it treats closures and layoffs as the default answer before a serious examination of alternatives. A value-preserving restructuring is not the same as a maximalist cut. If the company destroys its industrial base or triggers a long conflict, that is not discipline; it is self-harm.

## My position
The family holding’s position is this:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to durable cost and capacity reduction.
- **No** to cosmetic measures that merely postpone the problem.
- **No** to an outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value.
- **No** to any attempt to weaken the ownership and governance structure.
- **Open** to alternatives to plant closures and forced redundancies, but only if they are genuinely equivalent in effect.

## To management
You need to be more precise about what you mean by “necessary.” Saying that change is needed is correct; saying that every hard instrument is automatically justified is not enough.

If you want support from the controlling shareholder, you must show:
- a credible path to restored margins,
- durable savings, not one-off savings,
- a workable labor peace arrangement,
- and a restructuring that can survive beyond the next bargaining round.

I am open to capacity rationalization, site consolidation, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, and negotiated adjustments to legacy arrangements. But I am **not** open to a package that simply shifts the burden onto labor without producing a real competitiveness gain.

## To the Works Council and Union
I understand your insistence on co-determination, social stability, and protection of the workforce. Those are legitimate concerns. But your categorical refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies creates a problem if it prevents a durable solution.

If you want to keep all German plants open, then you must bring a **quantified, site-by-site alternative** that delivers comparable savings and capacity reduction. “Use other instruments first” is not enough if those instruments cannot close the gap.

I also need to be clear: long-standing guarantees are not sacred if they make the company uninvestable. A family owner cannot support a structure that preserves every entitlement while the business weakens.

## To the regional state
I appreciate your emphasis on industrial stability and the regional base. That is sensible and aligned with long-term value preservation.

But I would caution against turning “no closures” into a political absolute. If a targeted consolidation is economically unavoidable, then blocking all hard measures would only delay the reckoning and weaken the company further. The right test is not whether every site survives at any cost; it is whether the company emerges stronger, investable, and able to create jobs over time.

## What would move me
I would support a package if it contains:
- **real, durable capacity reduction**,
- **meaningful labor-cost relief**,
- **credible productivity and flexibility improvements**,
- **labor peace for a meaningful period**,
- **investment commitments tied to future products and technologies**,
- and **no permanent impairment of governance control**.

## What I would not support
I would not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- a settlement that weakens ownership control,
- or a prolonged confrontation that destroys value.

## Bottom line
I am **not** here to defend management’s preferred instruments. I am here to defend the long-term value of the enterprise.

If the employee side can produce a credible alternative that achieves the same structural savings without closures or forced redundancies, I will listen. But absent that, I will not support an arrangement that simply preserves the status quo and calls it compromise.
