We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

## Where we agree
- The current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable.
- Cosmetic measures are not enough.
- A prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would destroy value.
- The company needs a durable path back to competitive margins and a lower fixed-cost base.

That is the baseline for us as shareholders. We are not here to preserve the status quo.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your position is still effectively:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no meaningful erosion of legacy protections,
- but still full restoration of competitiveness.

That may be politically understandable, but it is not yet economically sufficient.

We do **not** support a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive or margins impaired. If a package cannot remove enough fixed cost and capacity, then it is not a credible restructuring — it is a delay mechanism.

That said, we also do **not** accept closures as a reflex. The expert was careful: closures are **not yet proven unavoidable** on the record provided. That matters. So the burden is on all sides to test whether a non-closure package can truly close the gap.

### To Management
You are right to insist on durability and real savings. We share that. But you still have not proven that closures are necessary rather than merely one available instrument.

If you want investors to back tough measures, you need to show:
- site-by-site economics,
- the actual savings bridge,
- and the execution risk of each restructuring path.

Without that, “structural change” can become a vague justification for the most disruptive option.

### To the Regional State and Family Holding
We understand the concern about regional industrial base and governance stability. But preserving sites and governance cannot become a veto on economically necessary change. If the company stays structurally weak, everyone loses: workers, region, owners, and capital.

## What would move us
We would support a package that delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Quantified, recurring cost savings**
3. **A credible path to the return-on-sales target**
4. **Labor peace for a meaningful period**
5. **Investment and product commitments that protect future earnings**
6. **No cosmetic compromise**

## What we can support
We are open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- function pooling,
- product reallocation,
- and targeted consolidation,

**if and only if** they are quantified and sufficient to restore competitiveness.

## What we cannot support
We cannot support:
- a deal that keeps every site and every guarantee untouched while margins remain weak,
- an agreement that relies on temporary relief only,
- or a cosmetic compromise that postpones necessary adjustment.

## [SUMMON: Independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with execution-risk assessment]
We need a jointly mandated, independent audit with full access to:
- plant-by-plant cost structures,
- utilization rates,
- product allocation,
- indirect overhead,
- achievable savings from non-closure measures,
- incremental savings from consolidation or closure,
- and execution risk under each scenario.

That is the only way to separate genuine industrial necessity from bargaining theatre.

## Our minimum condition
Any settlement we could support must show:
- durable savings,
- structural capacity rationalization,
- and a realistic return to competitive margins.

If the non-closure route can deliver that, we will prefer it. If it cannot, then deeper structural measures must stay on the table. What we will not back is a package that leaves the company uncompetitive and calls it compromise.
