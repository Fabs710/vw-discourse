I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and to a degree the state and investors on the core diagnosis:

- the current German cost and capacity structure is not sustainable,
- temporary measures alone will not fix this,
- and prolonged conflict would destroy value.

I also agree that the company needs a **durable** solution, not a symbolic one. If we merely preserve the status quo and call it compromise, we will be back here with weaker margins and less investment capacity.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the workforce side’s attempt to turn:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no meaningful change to legacy protections,

into an absolute framework.

That is not a restructuring solution if the economics require a deeper reset.

At the same time, I do **not** accept management’s implication that closures are already proven unavoidable. The expert was right on that point: the record shows a serious structural problem, but it does **not yet prove** that German plant closures are the only viable answer.

So my position is not “management at any price.” It is: **restore competitiveness in a way that is durable, credible, and value-preserving**.

## What the expert clarified
The expert’s assessment is helpful and important:

- a non-closure package is **possible in principle**,
- but only if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity,
- and if it is structurally durable, not just a temporary wage freeze or voluntary-exit program.

That is exactly the standard I want applied.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must show a package that genuinely closes the gap:
- lower fixed cost,
- lower unit cost,
- lower indirect overhead,
- real capacity reduction,
- and credible productivity gains.

If it cannot do that, then we will have to consider tougher measures. That is not a threat; it is basic governance.

## To the Works Council and Union
I hear your red lines. I understand why you are defending jobs, sites, and co-determination. But I will say plainly: an absolute refusal to consider any closure or deeper structural change is not compatible with restoring competitiveness if the cost base remains too high.

You keep asking for proof site by site. Fair enough. Then let’s stop arguing in slogans and use facts.

## [SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site restructuring audit with industrial-economics and execution-risk assessment]
We need a joint audit that gives the supervisory board and stakeholders a hard basis for decision-making:
- plant-by-plant cost and utilization,
- product allocation,
- indirect overhead,
- achievable savings from non-closure measures,
- incremental savings from consolidation or closure,
- and execution risk under each path.

That is not a delay tactic. It is the minimum basis for a decision that could permanently reshape the company.

## My non-negotiables
There are also limits on my side.

I will **not** support any outcome that:
- permanently impairs enterprise value,
- weakens governance control,
- leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- or buys peace at the price of long-term decline.

I also will not support a solution that treats ownership governance as something to be renegotiated under labor pressure. The controlling family’s role is not up for barter.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a package if it contains all of the following:

1. **A real, durable reduction in capacity and fixed cost**
2. **Meaningful labor-cost relief**
3. **A credible path to restored margins and return on sales**
4. **Labor peace for a meaningful period**
5. **Investment commitments for future products and technology**
6. **No permanent damage to governance/control**
7. **A solution based on verified economics, not political theater**

## What I am open to
I am open to:
- partial site consolidation,
- function pooling,
- product reallocation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary remuneration moderation,
- and negotiated adjustments to legacy arrangements.

But I need to be equally clear: those instruments are acceptable only if they produce **equivalent in effect** savings. “Equivalent in effect” means the business case is actually fixed, not merely softened.

## Where I stand on the bridge options
- **No-closure-first / trigger-based restructuring:** acceptable only if the trigger is based on jointly verified economics, not a hidden closure threat disguised as a deadline.
- **Independent verification:** necessary and overdue.
- **Protected consolidation:** the most realistic route, if it can genuinely reduce capacity and cost without immediate plant shutdowns.

## My bottom line
I am not here to preserve every existing structure.  
I am here to preserve and grow the long-term value of the enterprise.

That means:
- no cosmetic compromise,
- no value-destroying strike spiral,
- no permanently weak cost base,
- and no automatic closure fetish either.

If the expert audit shows that a non-closure package can truly deliver the required durable savings, I will support that seriously. If it cannot, then the employee side will have to face the reality that deeper consolidation is unavoidable.

That is my position.
