As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price of a restructuring that management failed to prepare properly.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to carry the full burden. If the company needs a restructuring, then it must be a fair one: with binding site guarantees, protection of jobs, and a real co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping any transformation. That means alternatives must be examined seriously first — capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, working-time solutions, training and qualification, and negotiated contributions that preserve the industrial base.

Our priorities are straightforward:
- keep every German plant open,
- rule out forced layoffs,
- secure binding guarantees through 2030,
- protect co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and ensure that any savings are shared fairly, not imposed one-sidedly on employees.

If management wants cooperation, it has to come with a credible, reversible plan that protects people and locations. If it comes with a fait accompli — closures, mass cuts, or the end of the historic guarantees — then it will meet fierce resistance, including mobilization of the workforce.
