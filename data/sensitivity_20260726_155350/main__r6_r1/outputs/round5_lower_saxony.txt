I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on the diagnosis:
- the current cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- cosmetic fixes will not be enough,
- and a durable settlement is preferable to a long conflict.

I also agree with management, investors, and ownership that the company must become more competitive. If the business remains structurally weak, everyone loses — workers, the region, shareholders, and the company itself.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the attempt to turn “durable restructuring” into a default case for:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or weakening the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not proven necessity; it is one possible instrument set. And on the record so far, closures are **not yet proven unavoidable**.

That distinction matters. If the evidence does not show inevitability, then irreversible measures cannot be treated as the starting point.

## To management
You are right that a temporary truce is not enough. But you still have not demonstrated, site by site, why the needed savings cannot be achieved through a protected consolidation path.

What I need to see is:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- utilization,
- direct and indirect cost structures,
- product allocation,
- and a clear comparison of savings from non-closure measures versus closures.

Until that is on the table, “closures remain on the table” is not a business case — it is a bargaining position.

And I say plainly: I will not support any restructuring that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s statutory role.

## To the works council and union
I agree with you that the burden of proof is not yet met.

I also agree that:
- forced redundancies are highly damaging,
- plant closures would have lasting regional consequences,
- and co-determination is not a decorative feature but part of the company’s legitimacy.

That said, I cannot support a position that assumes all existing structures must remain untouched regardless of the economics. If a protected consolidation can deliver the savings, then we should test it seriously.

But “test” does not mean “prepare the way for shutdowns behind the back of the workforce.” I reject any hidden closure trigger.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right to insist on durability and real savings. I share that.

But you are wrong if you think value is preserved simply by cutting capacity in the abstract and shifting the social costs onto the region. A company like this is not just a financial asset; it is a regional anchor, and trust is part of that value.

A restructuring that destroys labor peace, supplier confidence, and regional legitimacy is not value-preserving in the long run.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a restructuring if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
4. **A quantified, independently verified savings package**
5. **Real capacity and cost reduction through non-closure measures first**
6. **Binding investment and future product commitments**
7. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**

That is the framework I can defend politically and economically.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a package that treats job cuts in the state as the easy solution,
- a restructuring that sidelines the anchor shareholder,
- or a deal that asks workers to carry the pain while management and ownership avoid meaningful responsibility.

## On the audit
I support a jointly mandated independent audit, but it must be explicit in purpose:

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site audit with binding access to plant economics, utilization, product allocation, indirect costs, and the savings potential of non-closure measures versus consolidation or closure]

Its mandate must be:
- to test **non-closure solutions first**,
- to quantify the residual savings gap,
- and to determine whether the company can achieve durable competitiveness **without** plant closures and **without** compulsory redundancies.

If the audit is framed as a closure justification exercise, I will oppose it.

## Bottom line
I am not here to block restructuring.
I am here to block a restructuring that sacrifices the regional industrial base and weakens the company’s social and governance foundations.

If management can prove, with hard numbers, that a non-closure package cannot work, then the burden of justification rises sharply — and I will not pretend that there are no consequences. But until that proof exists, closures are not an accepted solution; they remain an unresolved threat.

That is my position.
