I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council on the diagnosis: the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic pause.

I also agree that:
- overcapacity is real,
- the cost base is too high,
- temporary fixes alone will not solve this,
- and a negotiated solution is preferable to an open industrial conflict.

So yes, the problem is real. We are not disputing that.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that “durable restructuring” is a code word for:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or dismantling the collective wage framework.

That remains **an assumption, not a proven necessity**.

The expert has been clear: **closures are not yet proven unavoidable on the record provided**. That matters. If the evidence does not prove inevitability, then closures cannot be treated as the default instrument and imposed on the workforce as a foregone conclusion.

## To management
You keep saying the company needs “real structural change.” Fine. Then show it.

What you have **not** shown is:
- why every German plant cannot remain open,
- why compulsory redundancies are necessary,
- why internal consolidation, product reallocation, shift changes, and voluntary measures are insufficient,
- and why workers should accept irreversible sacrifices before the company has done the hard work of proving the case.

Right now, “closures remain on the table” is not an argument. It is leverage language.

And let me be very explicit: I do **not** accept a so-called protected phase that is really just the first stage of later shutdowns once workers have already made concessions.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right about one thing: a company with a structurally weak cost base cannot survive by hope.

But you are wrong if you think value is protected by cutting plants and jobs first and asking questions later. Value is also destroyed by:
- strikes,
- broken trust,
- supplier disruption,
- demoralization,
- and long-term damage to the company’s industrial identity.

A “value-preserving” restructuring that shifts all pain onto the workforce is not value-preserving. It is short-term financial relief with long-term industrial damage.

## To the regional state
You are right to defend the industrial base and the state’s role. But if that is more than rhetoric, then use your leverage now.

Do not allow management to build a fait accompli and then call it responsible restructuring. The state should not become a spectator while irreversible site damage is pushed through under pressure.

## On the expert position
The expert is right that non-closure measures must be quantified. I support that.

But let me be equally clear: the audit is **not** a permission slip for later closures.

Its purpose must be:
- to test whether the required savings can be achieved with all German plants open,
- without compulsory redundancies,
- and without breaking the collective wage and job-security framework.

If the audit is framed as a closure-justification exercise, we will oppose it.

## What the union can accept
We are prepared to discuss a serious workforce contribution, but only in a balanced package.

That can include:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- shift flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- and time-limited remuneration restraint.

But that must happen **with**:
- job protection,
- site protection,
- and visible contributions from management and owners.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies,
- a permanent weakening of the collective framework,
- or a deal that makes workers pay for management’s strategic failure.

## On the bridge options
### 1) Time-limited protected consolidation with hard verification gates
This is only acceptable if “protected” means exactly that:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- no backdoor dismantling of site guarantees.

If the gate is a closure trigger, then it is not protection. It is a delay mechanism.

### 2) Site protection in exchange for quantified internal capacity reduction
This is the only direction that is discussable, but only if the guarantees are real and the company-side contribution is visible.

That means:
- investment,
- product allocation,
- management restraint,
- and no erosion of co-determination.

### 3) Two-track settlement with contingent fallback
No. I do **not** support a hidden closure trigger.

If there is a protected phase, then it must be genuinely protected. Not a waiting room for shutdowns.

## On the audit
Yes, we need one. And it must be real, not theater.

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site audit with binding access to plant economics, utilization, product allocation, indirect costs, and the savings potential of non-closure measures]

The mandate must be explicit:
- test **non-closure solutions first**
- quantify the savings gap
- compare that gap with what closures would supposedly add
- and assess whether the company can reach the required durable savings **without** closures and **without** compulsory redundancies

If the audit is framed as a closure justification exercise, we will oppose it.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking a restructuring that defaults to sacrificing workers, sites, and protections while management and ownership avoid meaningful contribution.

If the company can prove, with hard numbers, that a non-closure package cannot work, then it will still have to explain why workers should bear the social cost of that failure. If it cannot prove that, then closures and forced redundancies are not legitimate first choices.

Our position remains:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Preserve the collective wage framework**
- **Preserve co-determination**
- **Negotiate a fair employee contribution only in exchange for binding site and job security**

That is the only basis for a durable settlement we can defend to members.
