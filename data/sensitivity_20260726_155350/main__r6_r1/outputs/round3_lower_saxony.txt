I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with almost everyone on the basic diagnosis: **the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**.  
I also agree that the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic truce that simply postpones the next crisis. And I agree that prolonged conflict would be damaging for the company, the region, and the workforce.

So yes: change is necessary.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only serious path forward is **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies**.

That is where management is overreaching, and where the capital side is at risk of turning a necessary restructuring into a socially and politically destructive one.

### To management
You say the burden is on the employee side to produce an equivalent alternative. I disagree with that framing.

If you want to demand deep cuts, then you also have to show, with credibility, **why the company cannot be restructured without crossing the red lines on jobs and sites**. The expert has already said what I have long suspected: closures may be one option, but they are **not proven to be unavoidable** on the facts we have here.

That matters. Because once a plant is closed, the damage is permanent. Jobs are lost, local supply chains weaken, political trust collapses, and the company’s role as a regional anchor is weakened for years.

I am therefore not prepared to support a package that starts from closure and treats it as the default.

### To the Works Council and the Union
I agree with you on the essential point: **no compulsory redundancies, no bypassing of co-determination, and no hollowing out of the job-security framework**.

That is not sentimental. It is practical. A restructuring that destroys labor peace and the company’s social legitimacy will not be stable.

But I also have to say this plainly: you cannot simply say “no closures” and stop there. The expert has correctly pointed out that the company still needs **real capacity reduction and durable fixed-cost relief**. If all you offer are temporary or reversible instruments, that may protect the workforce in the short run, but it may not solve the structural problem.

So I support your red lines, but I also expect a serious, quantified non-closure package.

### To the family holding and investors
I understand your emphasis on value, competitiveness, and governance stability. Those are legitimate concerns. But value is not preserved by forcing through measures that trigger a long industrial conflict or permanently damage the company’s regional legitimacy.

A restructuring that undermines the workforce, alienates the state shareholder, and weakens the company’s industrial base is not a clean value solution. It is a high-risk bet.

### To the regional state
I agree with your core line: **the state cannot accept a restructuring that hollowes out regional employment and sidelines its statutory role**.

That said, “no closures” cannot remain only a slogan. If we are to use our leverage responsibly, we also need to help shape a package that actually works economically. Protecting the regional base is not the same as freezing the business model. But neither is competitiveness a license to dismantle the company’s social and regional foundation.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a restructuring if it contains all of the following:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures as the starting point**
3. **Binding respect for co-determination and the state’s veto role**
4. **A quantified, site-by-site plan showing how capacity and costs are reduced**
5. **Real company-side concessions: investment, product allocation, and management contribution**
6. **A fair workforce contribution, limited in time and tied to hard guarantees**
7. **A durable commitment to preserve the company as an industrial anchor in the region**

That is the framework I can defend politically and economically.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a package that guts regional employment,
- a restructuring by ultimatum,
- forced layoffs as a substitute for planning,
- or a solution that treats the state shareholder as decorative.

I also will not support a “temporary” agreement that quietly sets up closures later while asking the workforce to surrender now. If there is a second phase, it must be genuinely negotiated and based on verified facts, not a hidden threat.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert has said something important: **a non-closure deal may be feasible, but only if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity.**

That is exactly the point. The debate is not whether change is needed. The debate is whether the company can achieve it through:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- function pooling,
- product reallocation,
- temporary remuneration moderation,
- and targeted capacity reductions **without closing plants**.

That is the route I want tested seriously.

## [SUMMON: jointly mandated independent restructuring and industrial-economics audit team]
We need a joint expert process with access to:
- site-by-site economics,
- capacity utilization,
- product allocation,
- indirect cost structures,
- and the savings potential of non-closure measures versus closures.

But the mandate must be clear: this is **not** an exercise in manufacturing a pretext for closures. It is to determine whether we can achieve durable savings while preserving sites and employment as far as possible.

## My practical minimum conditions
Any deal I could support must include:
- a **moratorium on German plant closures during the restructuring period**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- a **binding site-and-employment package**
- **visible investment commitments for future products**
- **management and owner contributions**
- and **co-determination fully respected**

If those conditions are not met, then I will not help force through a solution that sacrifices the region’s industrial future.

## Bottom line
I am open to restructuring. I am not open to hollowing out the company’s German base.

If management wants my support, it must bring a plan that is:
- economically credible,
- socially fair,
- and politically defensible in the regional interest.

If it does not, then it will not get it.
