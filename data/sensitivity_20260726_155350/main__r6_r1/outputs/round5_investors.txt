We agree on the diagnosis, and that is the only reason this can still be resolved.

## Where we agree
- The current German footprint is too costly for the market reality.
- Temporary fixes, slogans, and “managed decline” will destroy value.
- A prolonged strike would be bad for shareholders and for the business.
- Any settlement must be durable, not cosmetic.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and full job guarantees through 2030** is understandable, but it is **not yet a credible answer to the margin problem**.

From a shareholder perspective, we cannot support a deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive or preserves excess capacity simply because the adjustment is politically hard. If the company cannot permanently lower fixed cost and improve unit economics, then the restructuring has failed, even if it is socially more comfortable.

That said, I also do **not** accept closures as a reflex. The expert has been clear that closures are **not yet proven unavoidable on the record provided**. That matters. So the burden is now on management and the workforce to prove whether a non-closure package can actually close the savings gap.

### To Management
You are right that the company needs durable savings and meaningful capacity reduction. But you still have **not proven** that plant closures are necessary rather than merely available.

If you want capital-market support, you need to show:
- site-by-site economics,
- the actual savings gap,
- which costs are structural versus temporary,
- and what non-closure measures can realistically deliver.

Without that, “closures remain on the table” is not a business case; it is a bargaining posture.

### To the Regional State and Family Holding
I understand the concern about regional legitimacy and governance. But preserving influence and preserving sites cannot become a veto on economically necessary change.

If the company stays too expensive, everybody loses: employees, region, owners, and shareholders. We will not back a settlement that protects the structure while the margin problem persists.

## What would move us
We would support a package if it is **quantified, verified, and durable**, with:
1. clear capacity reduction,
2. recurring cost savings,
3. a credible path back to the return-on-sales target,
4. labor peace for a meaningful period,
5. investment and product commitments that support future earnings.

## What we can support
We are open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- function pooling,
- product reallocation,
- targeted consolidation,

**if and only if** they are sufficient to restore competitiveness.

## What we cannot support
We cannot support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves every site and every guarantee while margins remain weak,
- or a settlement that postpones the hard decision and pushes the cost into future equity value.

## On the audit
We support the joint audit, but it must be real and decision-useful.

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with execution-risk assessment, including plant costs, utilization, product allocation, indirect overhead, savings from non-closure measures, and incremental savings from closure or consolidation]

The audit must answer one question clearly:
**Can a non-closure package deliver durable, competitive margins without leaving excess capacity and fixed cost in place?**

If yes, we will support that path.  
If no, then deeper structural measures remain on the table, including closures.

That is our position.
