## Core impasse

The central impasse is now very clear:

1. **Employee-side stakeholders** require a restructuring that explicitly excludes:
   - German plant closures,
   - compulsory redundancies,
   - and weakening of core protections.

2. **Management, investors, and ownership** require a restructuring that produces:
   - durable fixed-cost reduction,
   - meaningful capacity reduction,
   - and a credible path to restored competitiveness.

3. **The expert position** has narrowed the disagreement:
   - closures are **not yet proven unavoidable** on the current record,
   - but non-closure measures are also **not yet proven sufficient**.

So the dispute is no longer about whether change is needed.  
It is about **what level of structural adjustment is required, and what evidence is sufficient to justify it**.

The unresolved fault line is this:

- Employee-side actors want **binding protection first, economics tested second**.
- Management/capital-side actors want **structural flexibility first, protection conditional on results**.

That sequencing conflict is the plateau.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Time-limited protected consolidation with hard verification gates
A possible bridge is a **protected consolidation phase** that includes:

- no German plant closures during the protected period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- a jointly mandated independent audit with full site-by-site access,
- predefined savings targets,
- and a hard review point after the audit.

At the review point:
- if the audited non-closure package meets the target, it becomes the operating settlement;
- if it does not, the parties reopen negotiations on additional measures.

This would preserve the employee-side demand for a genuine protected phase while giving management and capital a structured test of economic sufficiency.

---

### Proposal 2: Site protection in exchange for quantified internal capacity reduction
A second bridge is a package in which:

- all German sites remain formally open,
- but internal capacity is reduced in a quantified way through:
  - shift cuts,
  - line simplification,
  - product reallocation,
  - function pooling,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - and voluntary exits.

This would require:
- a binding savings target,
- a binding volume-transfer plan,
- and a visible employer-side contribution, such as investment and product commitments.

This option may bridge the gap between “no closures” and “real restructuring” if the audit shows sufficient savings can be generated without shutdowns.

---

### Proposal 3: Two-track settlement with protection plus contingent fallback
A third compromise is a **two-track deal**:

**Track A: Immediate protection**
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- temporary job security through the audit and implementation phase.

**Track B: Conditional escalation mechanism**
- only if the jointly verified savings gap remains material after the protected phase,
- and only after a second joint review,
- further structural measures may be negotiated.

To address employee-side concern, the escalation mechanism would need strict safeguards:
- no automatic closure trigger,
- no unilateral management action,
- no silent backdoor shutdown path.

This is less preferred by employee-side stakeholders than Proposal 1, but it may be the only format that management and investors will view as credible if the audit reveals a residual gap.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
What specific savings gap, by site, remains after you apply all non-closure measures you are willing to put on the table, and what evidence will you provide to show that gap cannot be closed without plant shutdowns?

### To the Works Council
If the audit shows a measurable residual gap that cannot be closed with internal adjustment alone, what additional non-closure measures would you be prepared to discuss before ruling out any further structural change?

### To the Union
What employee-side contribution package would you regard as sufficiently serious to test a no-closure settlement in good faith, while still preserving the collective framework you want protected?

### To the Regional State
What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to keep the process genuinely open to non-closure solutions, beyond public opposition to closures?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a protected consolidation deal if it produces quantified recurring savings, even if that requires visible management restraint and binding product commitments in exchange for labor concessions?

### To the Institutional Investors
What level of verified recurring savings would be enough for you to support a no-closure package, even if it leaves some current inefficiencies in place during the transition?

### To the Expert
What minimum data set and threshold tests would you require in the audit before concluding that non-closure measures are insufficient and deeper consolidation must be considered?
