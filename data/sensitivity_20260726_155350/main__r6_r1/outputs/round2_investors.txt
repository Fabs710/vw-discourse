We agree with the diagnosis, but not with any solution that leaves the company structurally weak.

## Where we agree
- The current cost base and capacity are not sustainable.
- A prolonged strike, board deadlock, or public escalation would destroy value.
- Cosmetic measures alone will not restore competitiveness.
- Any settlement must be durable, not just a temporary truce.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your position still reads as: **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful change to legacy protections**.

That is not enough if the company truly needs a lasting step-change in margins. We do not support a package that preserves every site and every protection while the brand remains below target. If your alternative can deliver **the same magnitude of savings** with lower execution risk, then we will consider it. But you have not yet shown that it can.

### To Management
We do not support a “closures first” mindset either. Plant closures and layoffs are only justified if they are part of a coherent plan that actually restores return on sales and frees up investment capacity. If the restructuring triggers prolonged conflict and execution risk, then it can destroy value just as surely as inaction.

### To the Regional State and Family Holding
We agree that governance stability and social peace matter. But “preserve the industrial base” cannot become a veto on necessary restructuring. Nor can “governance control” be used to block economically necessary change. Value preservation requires hard choices, not just institutional caution.

## Our position
We will support whichever package credibly achieves all of the following:
- durable capacity reduction,
- meaningful labor-cost relief,
- a clear path back to competitive margins,
- no prolonged conflict,
- and investment capacity for future products and technology.

That means we are open to alternatives to closures and compulsory redundancies **only if they are economically equivalent in effect**. If they are not, then they are not a solution.

## Minimum conditions for support
1. Quantified, durable savings that materially improve margins
2. Capacity rationalization that matches demand
3. A real employee contribution
4. No open-ended conflict
5. A credible link to future investment and product competitiveness

## Bottom line
We are not here to protect any side’s preferred instrument. We are here to protect enterprise value.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, it needs to put forward a package that is just as credible on savings and durability. If management wants support for tougher measures, it needs to show that those measures will not become value-destructive overreach.

Absent that, we will judge the company by results — and continued margin erosion will not be acceptable.
