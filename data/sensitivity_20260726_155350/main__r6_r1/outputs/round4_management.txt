I’ll be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the union, the state, the family, and the investors on one point: **the current situation is not sustainable**.

- The cost base is too high.
- The footprint is too large for the market reality.
- The brand is not meeting its return-on-sales target.
- Cosmetic measures will not fix this.
- A long conflict would damage everyone.

So yes, the diagnosis is shared.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the attempt to convert that shared diagnosis into a veto on the instruments needed to fix it.

The employee side keeps repeating:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no real change to legacy protections,
- and then asks us to believe that a structurally uncompetitive cost base can still be repaired.

That is not a restructuring position. It is a refusal to confront the scale of the problem.

## To the Works Council
You say closures are “not proven unavoidable.” Fine. I accept that they are **not yet proven on the record we have**, and that is exactly why we need the audit.

But do not mistake “not yet proven” for “therefore impossible.” The company still has to solve a real overcapacity problem. If the audit shows that some plants are subscale, duplicative, or structurally too expensive, then you will need to confront that reality.

What I cannot accept is your assertion that every German plant must stay open by definition, regardless of economics. That is not a plan; it is a prohibition.

## To the Union
You are right that value is also destroyed by strikes, mistrust, and disruption. But value is also destroyed by preserving an uncompetitive structure and hoping margins magically recover.

You say management has not proven that closures are required. That is fair. We have not claimed proof without evidence. That is precisely why we are calling for a site-by-site audit.

But you also need to understand this: a package that only uses working-time moderation, voluntary exits, and temporary restraint may reduce pain, but it may still fall short of the durable savings we need. Temporary measures do not solve fixed-cost overhang unless they are paired with real capacity reduction and structural simplification.

## To the Regional State
I respect your concern for the industrial base. But I reject the idea that political leverage can be used to freeze the company in its current form.

A state shareholder cannot demand competitiveness on one hand and no serious restructuring on the other. That is managed decline dressed up as protection.

If the audit shows a way to preserve the industrial base without closures, I will take it seriously. But if the numbers show that deeper consolidation is necessary, then the state cannot simply block the decision and pretend the problem goes away.

## To the Controlling Family
You are right on one thing: governance control is not up for barter. Agreed. It is not.

But control also carries responsibility for hard decisions. If the company needs durable savings and capacity cuts, then ownership cannot just insist on results while rejecting the means to achieve them.

## To the Investors
You are right to insist on durable savings and margin repair. You are also right not to support a cosmetic deal.

But I will say this clearly: a deal that preserves all sites and all legacy protections while leaving the company structurally weak is not value-preserving. It simply postpones the next crisis.

## What management needs
Management needs a package that delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Meaningful labor-cost relief**
3. **Lower fixed cost per vehicle**
4. **A credible path back to target returns**
5. **Investment capacity for future products and technology through 2030**

That is the minimum. Anything else is insufficient.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am not fixated on closures as an end in themselves. I am fixed on outcomes.

I am prepared to consider:
- partial site consolidation,
- product reallocation,
- function pooling,
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- bonus moderation,
- and negotiated changes to legacy arrangements.

But those instruments are only acceptable if they produce **equivalent in effect** savings — not equivalent only in rhetoric.

“Equivalent in effect” means:
- a real drop in cost,
- a real drop in excess capacity,
- a real improvement in unit economics,
- and a real return to competitiveness.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a permanent job-security framework that leaves no room for structural adjustment,
- or a package that tells management to fix the cost base while removing the necessary tools.

I also cannot accept the idea that the company should shoulder the burden of future investment while the current structure remains untouched.

## On the expert position
The expert has been careful and, in my view, correct:  
**a non-closure solution is possible in principle, but only if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity.**

That is exactly the standard I am applying.

What the expert did **not** say is that German plant closures are impossible. The expert said they are **not yet proven unavoidable** on the record provided. That is a very different statement.

So the next step is not to declare victory for either side. The next step is to verify the economics properly.

## [SUMMON: Independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with execution-risk assessment]
We need a jointly mandated expert team with full access to:
- plant-by-plant cost structures,
- utilization rates,
- product allocation,
- indirect overhead,
- achievable savings from non-closure measures,
- incremental savings from consolidation or closure,
- and execution risk under each path.

If the employee side is confident that all plants can remain open while the gap is closed, then let the audit show it. If not, then we need to face the implications honestly.

## My bottom line
I am not here to preserve the current footprint for its own sake.  
I am here to restore competitiveness.

That means:
- no cosmetic compromise,
- no endless delay,
- no refusal to make hard choices,
- and no solution that leaves the company structurally weak.

If the audit shows that a no-closure package truly delivers the required durable savings, I will evaluate it seriously. If it does not, then deeper consolidation remains on the table.

That is where I stand.
