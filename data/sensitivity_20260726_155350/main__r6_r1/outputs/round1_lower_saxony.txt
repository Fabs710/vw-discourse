As the regional state shareholder, I want to be very clear: **I share the assessment that the company needs a credible restructuring.** I do **not** share the view that the only credible path is plant closures and broad forced layoffs in Germany.

We have a responsibility to the company’s long-term competitiveness, but also to the regional economy, to the workforce, and to industrial stability. Those are not sentimental add-ons; they are part of what makes this company viable as a German anchor.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on three points:

- **The status quo is not sustainable.**  
  Structural overcapacity and a cost problem cannot simply be ignored.

- **We need durable solutions, not cosmetics.**  
  Temporary measures alone will not be enough.

- **A negotiated settlement is preferable to open confrontation.**  
  A prolonged conflict, especially with strikes and board deadlock, would damage everyone.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with management on the core assumption that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the necessary baseline**.

That may be one possible instrument, but it is not a foregone conclusion, and it is certainly not the only path that deserves serious examination. If the company wants support from a long-standing anchor shareholder with a special statutory role, it must show that it has exhausted alternatives that preserve industrial capacity and employment in Germany.

I also cannot accept the tone that treats long-standing protections as if they were merely obstacles to be removed. Those guarantees and the co-determination framework are not incidental. They are part of the company’s governance and part of the social compact that has supported its success.

### To the workforce and union
I understand and largely support your insistence on:

- no blanket plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of co-determination,
- and a fair sharing of the burden.

That position is legitimate and, in my view, responsible. But I will also be direct: the workforce side will need to engage seriously with **productivity, flexibility, and cost relief** if it wants to avoid harsher measures later. Refusing every adjustment would not be a durable answer either.

### What I would support
A settlement becomes realistic if it contains all of the following:

- **binding site guarantees where economically possible**, especially for strategically important German plants,
- **no forced redundancies**, with reductions handled through natural fluctuation, transfers, qualification, and agreed instruments,
- **capacity adjustments that are targeted and reversible where possible**, not arbitrary cuts,
- **a fair employee contribution**, but one that is matched by visible concessions from management and owners,
- **investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**,
- and **full respect for co-determination and the state’s veto/board role**.

### My bottom line
I am **open to restructuring**. I am **not open to a solution that guts regional employment or sidelines the state and workforce in the process**.

If management can bring forward a package that genuinely restores competitiveness **while preserving as many sites and jobs as possible**, then I will engage constructively. If the proposal remains “closures first, labor pays, and everyone else waits,” then I will not support it.

We should now focus on a package that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable. Any other approach is too risky for the company and unacceptable for the region.
