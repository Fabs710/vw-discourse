Let me be equally direct in round 2:

### Our non-negotiables remain unchanged
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**
- **No bypassing of co-determination.**

Those are not bargaining chips to be traded away because management says the situation is difficult. The workforce is not responsible for management’s failure to prepare this transition properly, and we will not accept a restructuring that turns workers into the shock absorbers of every strategic mistake.

### Where I agree
I do agree with one point from almost everyone here: **the status quo cannot simply continue unchanged**. We know the company faces pressure, and we know that cost and capacity questions have to be addressed. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would hurt everyone.

But that agreement stops at the point where management and investors use “realism” as a code word for closures, layoffs, and the erosion of protections. That is not a serious restructuring plan; it is a transfer of risk onto employees.

### Where I disagree sharply
#### To management
You keep saying that “comparable savings” could justify anything, including closures and forced redundancies. I reject that logic.

If you want structural savings, then bring forward a **quantified alternative package** that preserves all German plants and rules out compulsory redundancies. That means:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment across sites,
- natural attrition,
- qualification and retraining,
- temporary remuneration moderation if truly necessary,
- and a fair, negotiated employee contribution **in exchange for binding guarantees**.

What you have not shown is why closures are necessary when these instruments have not been fully exhausted. And let me be clear: vague claims about competitiveness are not enough. Show the numbers, show the site-by-site impact, and show why your preferred measures cannot be replaced by reversible alternatives.

#### To the family holding and investors
You both talk about “value” and “governance safety.” Fine. Then let’s be honest about what destroys value: social conflict, strikes, loss of trust, and a broken production system after unilateral cuts.

A package that tears up the job-security framework and provokes industrial confrontation is not “discipline.” It is a high-risk move that can backfire for years. If you want durability, then you need labor peace built on security, not fear. That means no forced redundancies and no closure blackmail.

You say alternatives must be “equivalent in effect.” Agreed. But equivalent in effect does **not** mean equivalent in pain. It means equivalent in savings **without** destroying sites and livelihoods. That is exactly what we are demanding.

#### To the regional state
I appreciate your support for jobs and sites, but support has to be real. The state should not tolerate any restructuring model that strips out industrial employment in Germany under the banner of competitiveness. If you have a veto and a governance role, then use it to prevent a fait accompli, not to decorate one.

### Our minimum conditions for any discussion
If management wants even a basis for further talks, then we need:
1. **Binding guarantees that all German plants remain open through 2030.**
2. **A categorical exclusion of compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework.**
4. **A transparent, site-by-site restructuring plan showing how savings will be achieved without closures.**
5. **Real company-side contributions**: investment commitments, product allocation, and management/owner concessions.
6. **A fair workforce contribution only within that protected framework.**

### What we can discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- flexible shift models,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exit programs,
- and narrowly targeted cost adjustments if they are tied to hard guarantees.

What we are **not** prepared to discuss is any plan that starts from the premise that a German plant must be closed or that workers must be threatened with forced layoffs to accept the rest.

### Bottom line
Management says it needs structural change. We say: **yes, but not at the expense of the workforce’s basic security and not through closures or compulsory redundancies**.

If the company genuinely wants a durable solution, then it should stop using threats and start putting a credible, quantified alternative on the table. Until then, the works council will continue to resist with all the means available to us.
