I will be equally clear in Round 6.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with the state on one important point: this must be a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic pause.

I also agree that:
- overcapacity exists,
- the cost base is too high,
- temporary fixes alone are not enough,
- and a negotiated solution is preferable to escalation.

So no one should confuse our position with denial of the problem.

## Where I disagree
I reject the attempt by management, investors, and ownership to turn “durable restructuring” into a code word for:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and the erosion of the historic job-security framework.

That is still not proven unavoidable. The expert has repeated that point: **closures are not yet proven unavoidable on the record provided**. That matters. If the evidence does not prove inevitability, then irreversible measures cannot be the starting point.

## To management
You keep saying the company needs “real structural change.” Fine. Then show it.

What you have **not** shown is:
- why every German plant cannot remain open,
- why compulsory redundancies are necessary,
- why internal consolidation, product reallocation, shift changes, and voluntary measures are insufficient,
- and why workers should accept irreversible sacrifices before the company has done the hard work of proving the case.

Right now, “closures remain on the table” is not an argument. It is a threat dressed up as strategy.

And let me be very explicit: I do **not** accept a so-called protected phase that is really just the first stage of later shutdowns once workers have already made concessions.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right about one thing: a company with a structurally weak cost base cannot survive by hope.

But you are wrong if you think value is protected by cutting plants and jobs first and asking questions later. Value is also destroyed by:
- strikes,
- broken trust,
- public backlash,
- supplier disruption,
- and long-term damage to the company’s industrial identity.

A “value-preserving” restructuring that tears up co-determination and job security is not value-preserving. It is short-term financial relief with long-term industrial damage.

## To the regional state
You are right to defend the industrial base and the state’s role. But if that is more than rhetoric, then use your leverage now.

Do not allow management to build a fait accompli and then call it responsible restructuring. The state should not become a spectator while irreversible site damage is pushed through under pressure.

## On the expert position
The expert is right that non-closure measures must be quantified. I support that.

But let me be equally clear: the audit is **not** a permission slip for later closures.

Its purpose must be to test whether the required savings can be achieved:
- with all German plants open,
- without compulsory redundancies,
- and without breaking the job-security and co-determination framework.

If the audit is framed as a closure-justification exercise, we will oppose it.

## What the works council can accept
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- shift changes,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- product reallocation,
- function pooling,
- and temporary remuneration moderation.

But only within a framework of:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **preserved co-determination**
- **preserved collective protections**

That remains the minimum. It has not changed.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- “protected consolidation” that is just closure by another name,
- “no-closure first” with an invisible escalation path to shutdowns,
- voluntary measures that are really forced redundancies in disguise,
- or a restructuring that treats employees as the default adjustment variable.

## On the proposed bridge options
### 1) Time-limited protected consolidation with hard verification gates
Not acceptable if the gate leads to closures. That is precisely the trap we will not sign.

### 2) Site protection in exchange for quantified internal capacity reduction
This is only discussable if “site protection” means exactly that:
- all German plants remain open,
- no forced layoffs,
- no hidden dismantling of site guarantees.

If the meaning is “open for now, closed later,” then no.

### 3) Two-track settlement with contingent fallback
No. I do not support a hidden closure trigger, even if dressed up with safeguards.

A protected phase must be a protected phase, not a waiting room for shutdowns.

## On the audit
I support a jointly mandated independent audit — but only under a strict mandate:

[SUMMON: jointly mandated independent site-by-site audit with binding access to plant economics, utilization, product allocation, indirect costs, and the savings potential of non-closure measures]

Its purpose must be:
- to test **non-closure solutions first**
- to quantify the savings gap
- to compare that gap with the supposed gains from closures
- and to determine whether the company can achieve durable savings **without** plant closures and **without** compulsory redundancies

If the mandate is broadened to justify closure as the preferred outcome, we will oppose it.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring.
We are blocking a restructuring that defaults to sacrificing workers, sites, and protections while management and ownership avoid meaningful responsibility.

At this stage, no one has proven:
- that closures are unavoidable,
- that compulsory redundancies are necessary,
- or that the company cannot achieve durable savings through protected consolidation and internal adjustment.

So the burden remains where it belongs: on management and capital to prove their case, not on the workforce to surrender first.

My position remains firm:
- **Every German plant stays open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**

- **Preserved co-determination**
- **Real restructuring only through non-closure measures**
