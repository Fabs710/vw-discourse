I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central point: **the current situation is unsustainable**.  
The cost base is too high, the European market is shrinking, and we cannot continue as if the German structure can remain untouched while the brand’s return on sales stays below target. I also agree that a prolonged open conflict would be damaging.

But agreement on the diagnosis does **not** mean agreement on the solution.

## Where I disagree
### To the Works Council and the Union
Your position amounts to:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of long-standing protections,
- and a restructuring burden that is somehow supposed to be absorbed without deep structural change.

That is **not** a credible answer to the scale of the problem.

You keep asking management to prove that closures are necessary. I am telling you the evidence is already clear enough: if we leave the German footprint, labour cost structure, and fixed cost base broadly intact, then we will still be uncompetitive in 2027 and 2030. That is unacceptable.

Let me be equally clear:  
**“No closures, no layoffs, no concessions” is not a restructuring offer. It is a refusal to restructure.**

I am prepared to discuss alternatives to closures, but only if they produce:
- **durable capacity reduction**,  
- **material labor-cost relief**, and  
- **a lasting improvement in competitiveness**.

If your alternative simply preserves every site and every guarantee while relying on voluntary measures, temporary working-time changes, and internal transfers, then it does **not** solve the core problem.

### To the Regional State Government
I appreciate your willingness to support restructuring, but I cannot accept the implication that the company should be forced to preserve every site irrespective of economics. A special shareholder role does not create a right to freeze the cost base.

If the regional position is: “restructure, but do not close anything and do not materially reduce the labor cost base,” then that is not a path to competitiveness. It is a path to managed decline.

### To the Family Holding and Investors
I agree with you on the need for durable value creation and governance stability. But I will also say this: if we do not obtain a serious labor-cost and capacity package, then the value destruction continues. The market will not reward half-measures, and neither will customers.

## The management position
I want to state this directly:

We need a restructuring package with these features:

1. **A permanent reduction in capacity and fixed costs**
2. **A meaningful employee contribution**
3. **A clear reduction in labor cost per vehicle**
4. **A path that restores the brand’s return-on-sales target**
5. **Investment capacity freed up for future products and technology through 2030**

That is the minimum.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am **not** fixated on one instrument. I am open to:
- partial site consolidation,
- production shifts,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- bonus moderation,
- and negotiated adjustments to legacy protections.

If the employee side can bring a package that delivers **comparable, durable savings** without closures, then I will evaluate it seriously.

But I need to be blunt: it must be **comparable in effect**, not just comparable in rhetoric.  
That means:
- real savings,
- not delayed savings;
- structural savings,
- not one-off savings;
- and savings large enough to change the economics.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept a settlement that:
- leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- preserves all current guarantees untouched through 2030,
- and asks management to pretend that this is a solution.

That would be a cosmetic compromise, and it would fail the company.

I also cannot accept the idea that management should disclose every number, site by site, before the employee side even acknowledges the need for significant change. We are past the stage of debating whether there is a problem. There is one. The issue is whether we solve it decisively or postpone it.

## My minimum conditions for a deal
A deal must include:
- a **durable capacity reduction**,
- a **clear labor-cost contribution**,
- **real employment flexibility**,
- and **certainty for investment and product planning to 2030**.

If the employee side wants no closures, then it must bring an alternative that truly delivers these outcomes at the required scale. Otherwise, management will continue to pursue tougher measures, because standing still is not an option.

## Final point
I am not here to negotiate away competitiveness.  
I am here to restore it.

If the other stakeholders are serious about preserving the company’s industrial future, then they need to stop treating structural change as optional. The choice is not between comfort and disruption. The choice is between **controlled restructuring now** or **prolonged decline later**.

I am ready to negotiate the instruments.  
I am **not** ready to negotiate away the need for deep, durable change.
