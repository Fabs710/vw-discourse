## What the audit must establish

A decision-useful restructuring audit should quantify, by site and by function:

1. **Current fixed and variable cost structure**
   - direct labor cost
   - indirect labor / overhead
   - energy and utilities
   - maintenance
   - logistics
   - complexity costs

2. **Capacity and utilization**
   - installed capacity
   - actual output
   - realistic demand forecast
   - utilization by plant and line
   - flexibility constraints

3. **Savings from non-closure measures**
   - working-time reductions or flexibility
   - bonus / remuneration moderation
   - voluntary exits
   - redeployment
   - retraining
   - function pooling
   - product reallocation
   - procurement / overhead reduction

4. **Savings from protected consolidation**
   - specialization by site
   - line transfers
   - partial footprint reduction
   - centralization of functions
   - reduced complexity

5. **Incremental savings from closure**
   - what closure adds beyond consolidation
   - one-time closure and transfer costs
   - severance / social-plan cost
   - logistics penalties
   - ramp-up cost at receiving sites
   - execution risk

## Decision logic

A rational restructuring choice follows this sequence:

### If the residual gap is small to moderate
Then a **protected consolidation** package may be economically sufficient.

### If the residual gap is large and persistent
Then **deeper footprint reduction**, including closure or equivalent structural exit, becomes economically stronger.

So the question is not whether closures are politically acceptable.  
It is whether the **recurring savings from non-closure measures can close the structural gap**.

---

## What would count as a credible no-closure package

A no-closure package is economically credible only if it produces:

- recurring annual savings, not just one-off deferrals
- a materially lower cost per vehicle
- lower fixed cost per unit at the remaining footprint
- enough capacity alignment to avoid chronic underutilization
- a payback period that is acceptable relative to the company’s investment horizon

If those conditions are not met, the package likely postpones rather than solves the problem.

---

## What would count as closure being economically justified

Closure becomes persuasive if the audit shows:

- large and persistent overcapacity,
- structurally weak utilization at one or more sites,
- high indirect cost density,
- non-closure measures closing only a minority of the gap,
- and recurring savings from closure clearly exceeding its one-time and execution costs over a reasonable payback horizon.

- headcount impact
- recurring savings

- risk rating
- payback period

That is the cleanest way to move from confrontation to decision.
