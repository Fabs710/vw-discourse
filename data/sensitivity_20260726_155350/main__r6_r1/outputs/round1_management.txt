I’ll be equally clear from the outset: **the current cost base and overcapacity are not sustainable, and we cannot preserve every structure, every site, and every guarantee unchanged if we are serious about the brand’s future.**

I do agree on one point: **we need a negotiated solution, not chaos.** We do not want a prolonged confrontation for its own sake, and we know the supervisory-board reality. But negotiation only has value if it produces **structural** change.

Where I disagree sharply is this:

- **No plant closures in Germany, as a blanket rule, is not credible.**
- **No compulsory redundancies, as an absolute red line, is not compatible with the scale of the problem.**
- **Preserving long-standing job-security guarantees unchanged through 2030 is not realistic** if they prevent the company from restoring competitiveness.

Management has to act on the actual numbers, not on hopes. German factory costs are too high, the market is shrinking, and our competitors are moving faster and cheaper. If we settle for a cosmetic package — natural attrition, training measures, temporary working-time tweaks, and vague investment promises — then we will still be carrying an uncompetitive cost base in 2027, 2028, and 2030. That would be irresponsible.

So let me state the management position plainly:

1. **We need a durable reduction in capacity and fixed costs.**  
   That can include plant closure, partial site consolidation, or other binding capacity cuts — but it must be real, not symbolic.

2. **We need a meaningful labour-cost contribution.**  
   That means discussing pay moderation, the removal or reduction of legacy protections that no longer fit the cost situation, and additional employee contribution beyond standard flexibility tools.

3. **We need certainty on the path to 2030.**  
   If the employee side wants guarantees, then those guarantees must be earned through a package that restores margin, not block the restructuring.

4. **We are prepared to discuss the form of the adjustment, not its necessity.**  
   If you can bring forward alternatives that achieve comparable, durable savings in capacity and cost — at the same scale, with the same reliability — then we can assess them seriously. But “no closures, no layoffs, no concessions” is not an alternative.

On the union’s point about fairness: I reject the idea that management, owners, and capital markets should somehow be insulated while labor carries the burden. This is a group-wide crisis, and everyone will have to contribute. But contribution does not mean equal pain in abstraction; it means each side takes the measures necessary to save the business.

So my message is simple:

- **We are not negotiating around the need for deep restructuring.**
- **We are willing to negotiate the instruments.**
- **We will not accept a deal that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.**

If the employee side is serious about safeguarding the company’s industrial future, then it should come to the table with a package that delivers real and durable savings — including on labor costs and capacity. Otherwise, we will have to pursue tougher measures.
