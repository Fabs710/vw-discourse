We agree with the central diagnosis: the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable, and cosmetic measures will not restore competitiveness.

Where we differ is on what counts as a credible remedy.

## Where we agree
- **The status quo cannot continue.**
- **Durable savings matter more than short-term peace.**
- **A prolonged strike or board deadlock would destroy value.**
- **The company needs a path back to competitive margins and a lower fixed-cost base.**

That is the baseline. On that, the employee side, state, family, management, and investors are not actually far apart.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your line is still effectively:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no meaningful change to legacy protections,
- but still a structural reset in costs and margins.

That is not enough. If your package cannot remove enough fixed cost and capacity, then it does not solve the business problem. It merely redistributes pain into weaker margins, reduced investment, and delayed action.

We do **not** support a deal that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

At the same time, we also do **not** support closures as a reflex. If non-closure measures can really deliver equivalent durable savings, we will prefer that. But “equivalent” means economically equivalent, not just socially more comfortable.

### To Management
You are right that a serious restructuring is needed, but you still have not proven that closures are unavoidable. The expert assessment was important here: it showed that plant closures may be justified, but not yet proven necessary on the record we have.

That matters. Because if management pushes closures without exhausting credible alternatives, it risks:
- prolonged conflict,
- execution failure,
- and a value-destroying compromise that destroys the very savings it seeks.

So we are not giving management a blank cheque for maximalist restructuring. You need to show the economics, not just the urgency.

### To the Regional State and Family Holding
We understand the concern about social peace and regional industrial strength. But preserving the industrial base cannot become a veto on necessary change. If the company remains structurally weak, everyone loses: shareholders, workers, the region, and future investment capacity.

We also reject any attempt to treat governance control as untouchable while asking capital to accept the downside of a weak operating model.

## Our position
We will support whichever package credibly achieves all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Meaningful and lasting labor-cost relief**
3. **A clear path back to competitive margins**
4. **No prolonged conflict**
5. **Investment capacity for future products and technology**

If that can be done without closures and forced redundancies, good. If it cannot, then the company must consider more structural measures.

## What we need next
We support the idea of an **independent, jointly mandated site-by-site audit** as the next concrete step.

[SUMMON: Independent site-by-site restructuring and industrial-economics audit with execution-risk assessment]

The mandate should be to quantify:
- plant-by-plant cost and utilization,
- direct and indirect overhead,
- product allocation,
- durable savings from non-closure measures,
- incremental savings from consolidation or closure,
- and the execution risk of each path.

## Minimum conditions for capital-market support
Any settlement we could back must show:
- **quantified, durable savings**
- **material improvement in return on sales**
- **credible capacity rationalization**
- **real employee contribution**
- **a clear link to future investment**
- **no open-ended labor conflict**

## Bottom line
We are not here to protect any stakeholder’s preferred instrument. We are here to protect enterprise value.

If the employee side can produce a truly equivalent non-closure package, we will take it seriously. If not, then the company must be allowed to pursue deeper structural change. What we will not support is a cosmetic settlement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive and margins structurally impaired.
