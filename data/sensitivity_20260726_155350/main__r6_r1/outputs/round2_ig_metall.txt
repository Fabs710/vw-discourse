I will be equally direct in round 2.

## Our position
The union remains at the same line:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided burden on the workforce**

That is the minimum basis for any serious settlement. If management expects the union to help stabilize the company, then the company, owners, and capital side must also take real responsibility.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the current structure is not sustainable as it stands**. Overcapacity, weak margins, and a cost problem have to be addressed.

I also agree that **temporary or cosmetic measures alone are not enough**. If all we do is buy time, we will be back here in a year with the same conflict.

And I agree that a prolonged public confrontation would hurt the company. That is exactly why we have been signaling openness to a negotiated package.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You keep saying closures and forced redundancies are “necessary” because the numbers demand it. We are not accepting that as a given.

If you claim there is no alternative, then you need to prove it with a **site-by-site, quantified case**. Show where the savings come from, over what time horizon, and why they cannot be achieved through:
- working-time instruments,
- internal redeployment,
- natural fluctuation,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification and retraining,
- capacity alignment without closures,
- and a fair employee contribution.

Right now, what you are asking for is not “responsibility.” It is shifting the adjustment cost onto employees while preserving management’s freedom to declare jobs expendable. That is not acceptable.

### To the family holding and investors
You speak about value and governance. Fine. Then understand this: **industrial conflict destroys value too**.

A package imposed through threats, closures, and forced layoffs will not be stable. It will trigger resistance, damage trust, and weaken execution. That is not disciplined restructuring; it is a high-risk gamble.

If you want durable value creation, then support a settlement that protects the industrial base and secures labor peace. That means accepting that employees cannot be the only ones paying.

### To the regional state
I appreciate your rejection of a pure closure-and-layoff strategy. But support has to be translated into leverage. If the state wants a socially sustainable solution, then it should insist on real guarantees and not just speak in general terms about balance.

## Our counter-position
We are prepared to discuss a package with real employee contributions, but only under binding protections. That means:

- **site guarantees**
- **no forced layoffs**
- **voluntary and socially cushioned workforce reduction only**
- **working-time adjustments**
- **temporary wage or bonus moderation if necessary**
- **internal mobility and retraining**
- **a clear investment commitment for future models and technologies**
- **management and owner contributions as well**

That is the only kind of shared sacrifice we will discuss.

## What would move us
A genuine compromise would need all of the following:

1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A defined and time-limited employee contribution**
4. **Visible management/owner concessions**
5. **Binding employment and investment guarantees**
6. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
7. **A transparent cost and capacity plan that shows how savings are achieved without destroying jobs**

If those conditions are met, then the union can discuss meaningful labor-cost relief and flexibility. If they are not met, then we have no basis for agreement.

## What we will not accept
Let me be clear:  
- No restructuring by ultimatum  
- No threats that “equivalent savings” justify any measure at all  
- No use of competitiveness as a pretext to break protections  
- No settlement in which workers carry the pain while management and owners keep control and avoid sacrifice

If management refuses a fair package, then we will use the tools we have. The workforce is already mobilized, and we will escalate further if necessary.

## Bottom line
We remain open to a **durable, negotiated transformation**. But it has to be one in which:
- jobs are protected,
- sites remain open,
- the collective wage system is preserved,
- and everyone contributes fairly.

That is the only path to a stable settlement. Anything else will be resisted.
