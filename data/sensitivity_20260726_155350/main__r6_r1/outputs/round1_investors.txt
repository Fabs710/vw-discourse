As dispersed institutional investors, our position is straightforward: **we support a restructuring only if it is credible, durable, and sufficient to restore competitive margins.** We are not aligned with any side for its own sake. We care about risk-adjusted value, and right now that means fixing a structurally too-expensive cost base.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family shareholder, and the regional state on the basic diagnosis: **the current structure is not sustainable**.  
If German operations remain above peer cost levels and the brand misses its return-on-sales target, then equity value will continue to erode. We also agree that **a prolonged strike or board deadlock would destroy value**.

We also agree that **cosmetic adjustments are not enough**. Temporary working-time tweaks, natural attrition, or vague investment promises will not convince capital markets if the underlying cost base remains uncompetitive.

### Where we disagree
We disagree with the Works Council and the Union to the extent that they treat **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies** as absolute starting points. If those red lines make it impossible to achieve durable savings at the required scale, then they are not economically credible. We cannot support a settlement that preserves every site and every legacy guarantee while leaving margins structurally impaired.

We also disagree with management if it assumes that a harsh package automatically creates value. **Plant closures and layoffs are only justified if they are part of a coherent plan that actually restores competitiveness.** If the result is industrial disruption, political blockage, and execution risk without sufficient savings, then value destruction continues.

### What would move us
What would make us supportive is a package with these features:

- **clear, quantified cost reductions** that are durable, not one-off,
- **credible capacity rationalization** aligned with demand,
- **labor-cost relief** that is meaningful enough to matter to margins,
- **no prolonged conflict** that would delay implementation,
- **investment commitments** tied to productivity and future product competitiveness,
- and **a transparent path back to the return-on-sales target**.

### Our bottom line
We are **not** demanding closures for their own sake.  
We are demanding **results**.

If alternatives to closures and forced redundancies can deliver the same magnitude of savings with lower execution risk, we would welcome that. But if the alternative is simply to preserve the current structure and hope for the best, then we will not support it. That would be a failure of capital discipline.

So our message is:
- **We support real restructuring.**
- **We do not support cosmetic compromise.**
- **We will back whichever plan credibly restores competitiveness and margins.**
- **We will not back an outcome that leaves the company overstaffed, underproductive, and permanently below target.**

If management, owners, and labor can produce a binding agreement with real, measurable savings, we will view it positively. If not, we will judge the company by the continuing erosion in value.
