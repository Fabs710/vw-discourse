### Core impasse

The main impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed — all parties accept that it is.  
The impasse is over **whether the required savings and capacity reduction can be achieved without German plant closures and compulsory redundancies**.

More specifically, the sides are split on four linked issues:

1. **Proof threshold**
   - Employee-side actors say closures have **not been proven unavoidable**.
   - Management and capital-side actors say the economics may still require deeper consolidation.
   - The disagreement is about what evidence is sufficient to justify irreversible steps.

2. **Sequence**
   - Employees want **non-closure measures tested first**.
   - Management wants the audit to keep closures available as a real option.
   - The dispute is whether “non-closure first” is a genuine test or a delay mechanism.

3. **Red lines**
   - Employee and state-side actors insist on:
     - no closures,
     - no compulsory redundancies,
     - strong job and site guarantees,
     - preserved co-determination.
   - Management, investors, and ownership will not commit to a package that rules out structural adjustment in advance.

4. **Credibility of the savings package**
   - Everyone agrees the package must be durable.
   - The unresolved question is whether durability requires formal closures, or whether protected consolidation can deliver equivalent savings.

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Protected consolidation with verified savings target
A time-bound agreement in which:
- all German sites remain formally open for a protected period,
- no compulsory redundancies occur during that period,
- the company implements real capacity reduction through:
  - shift cuts,
  - product reallocation,
  - function pooling,
  - working-time changes,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - and remuneration moderation,
- and an independent audit verifies whether the agreed savings target has been met.

**Bridge function:**  
This gives employee-side actors site protection while giving management a real structural test of whether non-closure measures are sufficient.

---

#### Proposal 2: No-closure-first with pre-agreed escalation logic
A staged arrangement:
- Phase 1: no closures, no forced layoffs, immediate non-closure restructuring measures, protected labor peace.
- Phase 2: an independent audit reports on the residual gap.
- Phase 3: if and only if the gap remains material, the parties return to the table with a pre-agreed menu of additional measures.

**Bridge function:**  
This addresses management’s need not to foreclose future options, while giving workers protection against an automatic closure trigger.

---

#### Proposal 3: Site protection in exchange for deeper internal adjustment
A package where the workforce accepts a stronger contribution, such as:
- longer duration of wage moderation,
- more extensive shift flexibility,
- broader redeployment,
- more function pooling,
- more substantial productivity changes,

and in exchange the company grants:
- binding site guarantees for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- investment commitments,
- and product allocation commitments.

**Bridge function:**  
This creates a more balanced burden-sharing structure, aimed at making a no-closure solution more credible economically.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If the audit shows that all German sites can stay open only by accepting materially lower volume, stronger labor flexibility, and longer-term remuneration moderation, would you treat that as an acceptable protected consolidation — or do you require unchanged site structure as well?

#### To the Union
What specific combination of working-time, wage, and redeployment measures would you consider sufficient to produce durable savings without closures, and how would you define the threshold at which you would say the non-closure path has failed?

#### To Management
What is the exact residual savings gap after all non-closure measures are modeled, and what site-by-site evidence would you accept as sufficient to rule closures in or out?

#### To the Regional State
If the audit concludes that no-closure restructuring is viable only with substantial internal consolidation and labor concessions, will you support that path even if it requires politically difficult changes inside the plants?

#### To the Controlling Family
Would you support a protected consolidation deal if it preserves every German plant formally but requires significant margin sacrifice in the short term in exchange for long-term industrial stability?

#### To Institutional Investors
What minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction would make a non-closure package credible enough for you to support, even if it leaves some short-term earnings pressure?

#### To the Expert
What exact metrics should define success for a non-closure restructuring, so that all parties can judge whether the package has genuinely closed the gap rather than merely postponed the decision?
