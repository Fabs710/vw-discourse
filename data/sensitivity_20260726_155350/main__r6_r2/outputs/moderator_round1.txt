### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a temporary fix.
- There is broad agreement that **costs must come down** and **capacity must be adjusted** to market conditions.
- Several parties accept that a workable package may include some combination of:
  - voluntary departures,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time measures,
  - labor-cost contributions,
  - operational flexibility,
  - and investment commitments for future products and technologies.
- There is also shared concern that **prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and state reject closures as a default or acceptable outcome; management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state oppose them; management, family holding, and investors will not rule them out categorically.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees; management says it cannot commit to blanket guarantees without a credible economic package.
- **How savings should be achieved:**  
  Employee and state side prefer savings through alternative measures and shared sacrifice; capital and management emphasize the need for **durable, measurable cost and capacity reduction**, including potentially harsher instruments.
- **Role of governance and co-determination:**  
  State, works council, and union insist on preserving governance rights; management and capital want enough flexibility to execute a deep restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Jobs/sites vs. competitiveness:** how much industrial footprint can be preserved without undermining the cost base.
- **Short-term labor peace vs. long-term restructuring depth:** whether concessions now can avert escalation, or whether insufficient measures just delay the problem.
- **Voluntary instruments vs. enforceable reductions:** whether enough headcount and capacity can be reduced without compulsory measures or closures.
- **Investment commitments vs. immediate savings:** how to balance future product investment in Germany against near-term cost reduction.
- **Regional/political considerations vs. enterprise value:** how far shareholder and state concerns can shape the final package.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from general red lines to **specific package options**:
- What exact combination of measures could deliver the required savings?
- What savings, capacity reductions, and headcount effects would each side consider sufficient?
- What guarantees, if any, could be made conditional on agreed contributions?
- What fallback options exist if voluntary measures do not reach the target?

The next round should focus on **numbers, sequencing, and conditional commitments**, not just principles.
