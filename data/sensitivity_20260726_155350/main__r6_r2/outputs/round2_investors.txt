We appreciate the specialist’s input, and it sharpens the key point: **a non-closure package can work, but only if it is deep enough to produce closure-like economics**. That is exactly the standard we care about.

## Where we agree

### With management, family holding, and the expert
We agree that:
- this cannot be a cosmetic settlement,
- the cost base must come down materially,
- capacity has to be reduced,
- and labor peace matters because prolonged conflict destroys value.

We also agree with the expert that **voluntary exits and wage restraint alone will not be enough**. If the company wants to avoid closures, it still needs a package with real headcount reduction, real capacity reduction, and durable labor-cost relief.

### With the state and workforce side, in part
We agree that a prolonged strike and a pure closure war would be value-destructive. We also agree that a negotiated solution is preferable **if** it actually restores competitiveness.

## Where we disagree

### On the works council and union red lines
Your insistence on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and strong guarantees through 2030

is not something we can support as absolute. If those red lines prevent the company from achieving the savings ranges the expert described, then they are economically unacceptable.

We are not here to preserve every site at any cost. We are here to protect returns. If a package cannot deliver roughly the equivalent of:
- **15% to 20% headcount reduction** in the affected German scope,
- **10% to 25% capacity reduction**,
- and durable cost relief,

then it is not good enough.

### On the state shareholder
We respect the political and regional role, but we do **not** accept a veto posture that blocks economically necessary restructuring. A restructuring that protects every site in its current form while leaving the cost base uncompetitive will not be supported by capital markets.

### On management
We are also not giving management a blank cheque simply because closures are “on the table.” The expert’s framing matters: if management reaches for closures before showing that a deep non-closure package truly fails, that is a bargaining failure and a governance problem.

## What we would support

We would support a package if it credibly includes:
- substantial and recurring labor-cost relief,
- meaningful headcount reduction,
- real capacity consolidation,
- operating simplification,
- and a clear timetable for margin recovery.

That package could be closure-free **only if** it is deep enough to match the economic effect. The expert’s analysis suggests that means a lot more than symbolic concessions.

## What we will not support

We will not support:
- a deal that keeps all sites effectively intact while only trimming around the edges,
- temporary concessions that expire before the savings problem is solved,
- or a “peace” arrangement that protects everyone politically but leaves shareholders with structurally impaired margins.

## Our practical position

So our position is:

1. **No cosmetic compromise.**
2. **No outcome that leaves the cost base materially uncompetitive.**
3. **No agreement that substitutes headlines for economics.**
4. **Yes to a deep negotiated package if it delivers closure-like savings without closures.**
5. **If it does not, then harder measures remain justified.**

## What we want next
We want management to come back with a quantified package that clearly shows:
- how much savings come from labor-cost measures,
- how much from headcount reduction,
- how much from capacity reduction,
- how much from overhead and productivity measures,
- and whether the total is in the range the expert outlined.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it now has a concrete standard to meet. If it cannot meet it, then the market will not reward a compromise that merely delays the restructuring.

In short: **we are open to a non-closure solution, but only if it is financially equivalent in substance, not just in rhetoric.**
