## Core impasse

The dispute has narrowed to one central deadlock:

**Employees, the regional state, and to some extent investors all accept that the restructuring must be deep, but they do not accept closures or compulsory redundancies as the mechanism.**  
**Management, the family holding, and investors want closure and forced-layoff fallback options preserved unless a closure-free plan is proven sufficient.**

So the conflict is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about:

1. **whether a closure-free package can be made genuinely closure-equivalent**, and  
2. **whether management must give up fallback coercive instruments before that proof exists.**

A second, related impasse is over **conditionality**:
- employee and state actors want binding protection,
- management and owners want flexibility if the package underperforms.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-closure test with automatic review
A structured compromise could be:

- A **deep non-closure package** is implemented first,
- with **clear numerical targets** for headcount, capacity, overhead, and savings,
- over a defined **12–18 month test phase**,
- monitored jointly by management, workforce representatives, and an independent expert.

**If targets are met**, closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table for the agreed period.  
**If targets are missed**, all parties return to the table with pre-defined fallback options, but only after a transparent review.

This bridges the gap between:
- the workforce’s demand for no immediate closure threat, and
- management’s insistence on not permanently surrendering flexibility.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-security agreement with differentiated flexibility
A second compromise could separate the sites into categories:

- **Core sites**: protected from closure, with binding investment and job-security commitments.
- **Adjustable sites**: no closure now, but capacity, shifts, and product allocation may be reduced materially.
- **Review sites**: protected against closure for a fixed period, but subject to a jointly governed strategic review if benchmarks fail.

This would allow:
- the state and works council to defend regional stability,
- management and owners to preserve some operational flexibility,
- and investors to see that the restructuring is not merely cosmetic.

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional employee contribution tied to verified owner/management contribution
A third bridging position could be:

- workers contribute through voluntary measures, time, or moderated compensation,
- **only if** management and owners commit to measurable counterpart actions:
  - overhead cuts,
  - procurement savings,
  - executive reduction,
  - investment commitments,
  - and no forced redundancies during the agreed implementation window.

This directly addresses the concern that the workforce should not be the only side paying for the turnaround.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If a closure-free package is accepted only if it achieves the same savings and capacity reduction as a closure scenario, **what specific additional flexibility on shifts, product allocation, or internal redeployment would you accept to make that package workable?**

### To the Industrial Union
If voluntary measures fall short of the target headcount reduction, **would you accept an independently verified extension of the restructuring period, provided closures and compulsory redundancies remain excluded during that extension?**

### To Group & Brand Management
What exact evidence threshold would make you conclude that the deep non-closure route is credible enough to stop treating closures and compulsory redundancies as active fallback instruments?

### To the Regional State Government
Would you support a **time-limited no-closure agreement** if it includes binding milestones, independent monitoring, and a review clause that reopens governance discussions only if the savings bridge misses target?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to support a non-closure restructuring **if** it is independently verified to deliver closure-equivalent economics, even if that requires stronger owner contributions in the short term?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you consider a restructuring acceptable if it is **not a closure plan**, but is independently validated to deliver the same recurring cost and capacity reductions within 2–3 years?

### To the Expert
What is the smallest set of measurable triggers that would let all sides determine, without ambiguity, whether the non-closure package has succeeded or failed?
