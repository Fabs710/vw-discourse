As dispersed capital-market shareholders, we are looking at this through one lens: **risk-adjusted value**. We need a restructuring that credibly restores margins, reduces structural overcapacity, and lowers the cost base in a durable way. Anything less is just value erosion with a different label.

So let me be direct.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and even the state on one point: **this company needs decisive action, not a cosmetic settlement**. The margin problem is real. The competitive pressure is real. The current cost base is not sustainable. If the outcome leaves German operations structurally uncompetitive, the share price will remain under pressure and capital will stay expensive.

We also agree that prolonged industrial conflict is value-destructive. Strikes, uncertainty, and delayed execution hurt production, customer confidence, and investor credibility. That cost is not abstract; it shows up in earnings and valuation.

## Where we disagree
### On the works council and union line
We understand the desire to protect jobs and sites, but **“no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute red lines are not acceptable if they prevent the company from fixing the economics**. Those positions may be politically understandable, but they do not answer the underlying problem: overcapacity and an uncompetitive fixed cost base.

If the only way to achieve equivalent savings is through closures, then closures must remain on the table. If employee-side opposition blocks all meaningful capacity reduction, then the company will continue to bleed margin. We will not support an outcome that preserves every site but structurally impairs returns.

That said, we do not require closures as a matter of principle. What we require is **equivalent, durable savings**. If the workforce and management can deliver the same financial result through a different mix — capacity cuts, work-time flexibility, redeployment, voluntary departures, labour-cost concessions, and site consolidation — we would clearly prefer that to a prolonged strike and legal/political confrontation.

### On the state shareholder
We share the concern about regional employment, but we are not prepared to let political considerations override enterprise value. A blocking posture that prevents economically necessary restructuring is not shareholder-friendly and will ultimately damage the company’s ability to invest, compete, and employ people at all.

Protecting every site at any cost is not a strategy. The state should support a solution that preserves long-term competitiveness, even if that means accepting painful adjustments. Regional stability depends on a viable company, not on freezing the footprint.

### On the family holding
We agree with the family that symbolism is not enough. But we will also say plainly that **long-term value is not protected by insisting on a deal that looks tough but does not actually move the margin needle**. The family should support a restructuring that is deep enough to work, even if it is unpopular. Investors will not reward half-measures.

## What we would support
We would support a package if it credibly delivers:
- durable capacity reduction,
- measurable and recurring labour-cost savings,
- lower fixed costs,
- a realistic path back to competitive return on sales,
- and execution certainty without endless escalation.

We would also support:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- limited but real employee contributions,
- and investment commitments that are tied to the restructuring and not used as a substitute for it.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that preserves structural overcapacity,
- a deal that leaves labour costs materially above peers,
- or a prolonged conflict that destroys operating performance while management and stakeholders argue over optics.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend a particular instrument — closures, layoffs, or wage cuts. We are here to defend **returns**. If management can secure a credible, durable restructuring without closures, fine. If not, then difficult measures must remain on the table. What is unacceptable is a “solution” that reassures everyone politically while leaving the business economically weak.

So our message to all sides is simple:

**Bring forward a package that truly restores competitiveness.**
If you cannot, do not expect capital markets to back an arrangement that merely postpones the problem.
