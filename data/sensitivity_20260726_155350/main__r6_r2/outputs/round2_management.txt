Let me respond plainly.

I hear the repeated “no closures, no layoffs, no erosion of guarantees” message from the works council and the union. I understand why you are saying it. But I need to be just as clear: **if that is the full answer, then it is not a restructuring.** It is an attempt to preserve an overextended footprint while the company’s economics continue to deteriorate.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one,
- savings must be **structural**, not temporary,
- and prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging for everyone.

I also agree that a package can and should include:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time instruments,
- product and investment commitments,
- and some form of negotiated employee contribution.

Those are not being ruled out. But they are only acceptable if they actually deliver the required scale of savings.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No plant closures” is not a viable red line
You keep treating closures as some kind of political provocation. From management’s perspective, they are an instrument of last resort — but still an instrument. If the alternatives do not close the gap, then closures remain on the table. That is not rhetoric; it is the reality of the cost base we are facing.

If you want closures off the table, then you must deliver **equivalent and durable** savings through other means. So far, I am hearing principles, not numbers.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute
I would prefer to avoid compulsory redundancies. But I will not accept a framework that makes headcount reduction impossible if the business requires it. If your answer is “only voluntary departures,” then the question becomes whether that is sufficient at the required pace and scale. If it is not, then you are asking management to accept a solution that does not solve the problem.

### 3) Binding site guarantees through 2030 are not free
You are asking for guarantees first and flexibility later. That reverses the logic of restructuring. If we lock in all sites and all jobs too early, we remove the very tools needed to restore competitiveness. A guarantee is only credible if it is tied to a package that genuinely improves the economics.

## On the numbers and sequencing
The moderator is right to ask for specifics. So here is the core issue:

- We need a **material reduction in labour cost**.
- We need a **durable reduction in capacity**.
- We need a **lower fixed-cost base** fast enough to matter.
- And we need a path to restore the brand’s return on sales and fund future investment.

If you want to argue that this can be done entirely without closures or compulsory redundancies, then bring a package that adds up. Not a list of desirable measures — a package that closes the gap.

### What I would regard as serious
A serious proposal would need to include, at minimum:
- quantifiable labour-cost relief,
- real capacity reduction,
- measurable headcount reduction,
- operational flexibility,
- and a credible implementation timetable.

That can be through a mix of voluntary exits, working-time measures, and negotiated concessions. But if the total effect is too small, then it is not serious.

## To the works council
You say management should first prove the numbers transparently. Fair enough — but transparency does not change the conclusion. The company’s cost base is too high. German operations are materially above target. Market conditions are tightening, not improving. The burden is on all of us to produce a solution that works economically, not just socially.

You also say we have not done the hard work of designing an alternative. That is not correct. We are prepared to discuss alternatives. What we are not prepared to do is pretend that every alternative, regardless of scale, is sufficient.

## To the union
You are right that any employee contribution must be time-limited, fair, and matched by contributions elsewhere. I am open to that principle. But I will not accept the implication that workers can define the boundaries of the restructuring while management is responsible for fixing the financial gap.

If the membership will not accept compulsory redundancies or closures, then the practical question is whether the alternative package can deliver enough savings. If it cannot, then we are back to the same structural problem.

## To the state shareholder
You say you will block a one-sided closure program. I hear you. But I have to say clearly: the state cannot veto reality. If the economics require structural change and the alternative package falls short, then simply blocking closures does not solve the problem. It just postpones it and raises the eventual cost.

If you want to use your influence constructively, help deliver a package that is actually competitive. That means real savings, not political insulation.

## To the family holding and investors
I agree with you on the core point: symbolism is not enough. A deal that “feels balanced” but leaves us uncompetitive is not acceptable. We cannot buy labor peace at the cost of future viability.

At the same time, I need your support for a settlement that is executable. If the only outcome you will back is the maximum closure scenario, then you are setting us up for unnecessary confrontation. We need a workable path that can actually be implemented.

## Bottom line
Here is my position, clearly:

- I am **not** accepting a cosmetic deal.
- I am **not** accepting a package that preserves structural overcapacity.
- I am **not** accepting a settlement that leaves labour costs materially above target.
- I am **not** accepting an arrangement that postpones the crisis to 2028 or 2030.

What I **am** prepared to consider:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary employee contributions,
- product and investment commitments,
- and, if necessary, closures or other hard measures if the alternatives do not deliver sufficient, durable savings.

So the next step is not more red lines. It is a credible package with numbers. If the employee side wants to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, then it must bring a solution that achieves the same structural result. If it cannot, then I will not pretend otherwise.
