Yes. On the industrial-economics question, a **closure-free package can sometimes substitute for closure economics**, but only if it is large enough to remove the same recurring cost and capacity burden that closure would remove.

## Bottom line
A non-closure package is credible only if it delivers, at minimum:

- **headcount reduction:** roughly **15%–20%**
- **capacity reduction:** roughly **10%–25%**
- **time to steady state:** about **2–3 years**
- **durable recurring savings:** not just one-time labour concessions
- **site overhead simplification:** otherwise fixed-cost drag remains

If the package falls materially below that range, it is **not closure-equivalent**; it is just deferral.

## What has to be true for a closure-free package to work
To match closure-like economics without closing a plant, the company would need some combination of:

1. **Large voluntary workforce reduction**
   - voluntary exits
   - phased retirement
   - internal transfers out of the affected network
   - hiring freeze / replacement suppression

2. **Capacity rationalisation without formal closure**
   - shift elimination
   - line consolidation
   - product allocation changes
   - lower operating days
   - lower output at selected plants

3. **Real fixed-cost removal**
   - plant-level overhead reduction
   - management layers cut
   - maintenance / support functions consolidated
   - indirect labour reduced

4. **Recurring labour-cost relief**
   - wage moderation / bonus restraint
   - longer working-time adjustments
   - removal or suspension of certain legacy guarantees
   - higher utilisation from fewer people

## What “closure-equivalent” means in practice
Closure usually removes:
- direct labour,
- indirect labour,
- plant overhead,
- maintenance burden,
- depreciation drag tied to underused assets,
- and future fixed support costs.

A non-closure package must replace those savings with **other structural reductions**. If it only cuts wages temporarily, or relies only on voluntary exits without changing capacity and fixed cost, it will not be durable enough.

## Size of the required adjustment
Without company-specific network data, the best decision-useful estimate is:

- **Headcount:** if the German footprint is materially overstaffed relative to volume, a durable non-closure settlement likely needs a **high-three-digit to low-four-digit reduction per large multi-plant network**, depending on how many plants are affected and how much indirect labour can be removed.
- **Capacity:** a reduction in the **low double digits to mid-20s percent** is the right order of magnitude if the goal is to avoid closure but still remove excess footprint.
- **Timing:** even a well-designed package usually needs **24–36 months** to reach steady state because voluntary exits, product transfers, retraining, and shift reconfiguration do not happen instantly.

## Key constraint
The hardest part is not direct labour. It is **fixed-site overhead**.

If every plant remains open with essentially the same running costs, then the company can cut hours and people but still carry too much structural cost. That is why a closure-free package only works if one or more plants are effectively:
- turned into lower-throughput sites,
- assigned fewer shifts,
- or stripped of enough overhead that they become much cheaper to run.

## Practical test
To determine whether a non-closure package is sufficient, management should produce a **site-by-site bridge** with:

- current and post-restructuring headcount
- direct vs indirect labour savings
- capacity by site, before and after
- shift pattern changes
- fixed-cost reduction by site
- one-time restructuring cost
- annual recurring savings at steady state
- sensitivity if voluntary exits fall short

If that bridge does not show savings comparable to closure, then the non-closure route is not economically equivalent.

## My assessment
So, in neutral terms:

- **Yes**, a non-closure package can work in principle.
- **But only** if it is deep enough to deliver **closure-like recurring savings**.
- In the absence of plant-specific numbers, the threshold for credibility is approximately:
  - **15%–20% workforce reduction**
  - **10%–25% capacity reduction**
  - plus meaningful fixed-cost removal over **2–3 years**
