I hear the argument: if the package must be deep, then maybe the easiest way is to close a plant and cut force.

That is precisely the logic we reject.

The benchmark tells you what the **economic outcome** must be:
- material headcount reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- recurring cost relief,
- and a 2- to 3-year implementation path.

It does **not** tell you that the industrial answer must be closures or forced layoffs.  
That leap is yours, not ours.

### 3) “Conditional guarantees” cannot be a loophole for permanent insecurity
The state shareholder speaks of conditional protection. I understand the intent, but I need to be clear: if “conditional” means the company can later reopen the same closure threat whenever management feels pressure, then that is not a guarantee.

A serious agreement must protect:
- every German site,
- jobs,
- co-determination,
- and the job-security framework through 2030.

Conditions can govern **how** the package is implemented.  
They cannot be a backdoor to keep closures permanently in reserve.

## On the numbers

The experts have repeatedly said a closure-free package may need roughly:
- **15%–20% headcount reduction**
- **10%–25% capacity reduction**
- **2–3 years** to steady state

I accept that as a testing range.  
I do **not** accept it as a justification for closing sites or forcing people out.

Those reductions can be achieved through:
- voluntary departures,
- phased retirement,
- redeployment,
- hiring restraint,
- shift elimination,
- product allocation changes,
- internal transfers,
- qualification measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

That is the route to a serious settlement without destroying trust.
