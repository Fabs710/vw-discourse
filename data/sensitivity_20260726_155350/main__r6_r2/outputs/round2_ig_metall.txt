I’ll be direct.

I agree with the works council on the core line: **no compulsory redundancies, no wage dumping, and no restructuring that pushes all the pain onto the workforce**. I also agree that we need a **durable** solution, not a temporary patch. And yes, if the company needs savings, then those savings must come from a **shared-sacrifice package**, not from workers alone.

But I need to be equally clear about what I do **not** accept.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “Closures are just a fallback” is not good enough
To management, the family, and investors: saying plant closures remain “on the table” is not a neutral technical statement. It is a threat of irreversible damage. In a company with this industrial weight, closures are not a routine efficiency tool. They mean lost skills, lost trust, and escalation.

If you want a negotiated outcome, then stop treating closure as the default bargaining lever.

### 2) “No blank cheque” cuts both ways
We will not give management a blank cheque on labour concessions.  
But you also do not get a blank cheque on plant closures, forced job cuts, or unilateral wage erosion.

If employees are expected to contribute, then the contribution must be:
- **time-limited**
- **fair**
- **clearly quantified**
- and **matched by contributions from management and owners**

That is the only basis on which I can take anything back to the membership.

### 3) Job guarantees cannot be dismissed as unrealistic
Management keeps implying that binding site and job guarantees are somehow irresponsible. That is backwards. If you want workforce cooperation, you need to create the conditions for trust.

Without some form of binding protection, any employee contribution just becomes a prelude to the next round of cuts.

## What a serious package must include

I’ll spell out what I think is negotiable and what is not.

### Non-negotiable
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No German plant closures**
- **No deterioration of the collective wage framework**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or special governance protections**

### Potentially negotiable, if the package is fair
- temporary wage restraint at the margin
- bonus restraint or deferral
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exit programmes
- internal redeployment
- reduced shift patterns
- temporary solidarity contribution
- capacity reduction through operational adjustment, not closures

## What I need to see from the other sides

### To management
Bring a package with:
1. **hard numbers** on the savings gap,
2. a clear explanation of **how much comes from management and overhead cuts**,
3. a concrete plan for **voluntary and internal measures first**,
4. and a binding statement that **forced layoffs are not the primary instrument**.

If your answer is only “we need flexibility,” that is not a plan. That is a demand that workers absorb the risk.

### To the family holding
If you want long-term enterprise value, then support a solution that keeps the workforce committed and the industrial base intact. Long-term value is not created by provoking a labour conflict and then calling the outcome “discipline.”

### To the state shareholder
Your veto power matters. Use it to prevent a one-sided closure strategy. Support a package that protects industrial employment and keeps the company rooted in Germany.

### To investors
You are right that half-measures are not enough. But you are wrong if you think the only credible answer is closure pressure. A company can restore competitiveness through a serious mix of:
- capacity adjustment,
- labour-cost contribution,
- productivity improvements,
- and investment clarity.

That is what a durable settlement looks like.

## The union’s position
From the collective bargaining side, I am prepared to discuss a **temporary and targeted contribution by employees** if, and only if, it is embedded in a binding package that protects jobs and sites.

That could include:
- a temporary moderation of certain wage elements,
- reduced bonus components,
- working-time instruments,
- and a negotiated solidarity contribution.

But I will not sell this to the membership unless it is matched by:
- management cuts,
- owner responsibility,
- site protection,
- and a credible future industrial plan.

## Bottom line
So let me summarize:

- **Yes** to shared sacrifice.
- **Yes** to durable cost and capacity reductions.
- **Yes** to flexibility, redeployment, and voluntary instruments.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies.
- **No** to plant closures.
- **No** to workers carrying the restructuring alone.

If management and the owners want cooperation, bring a balanced package with real reciprocity. If they want confrontation, then they should be honest about that too — because we are already mobilized, and we will defend the collective wage framework and the jobs of our members.
