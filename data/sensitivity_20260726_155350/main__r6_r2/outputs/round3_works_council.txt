I’ll be equally direct in this round.

The expert’s contribution was useful because it confirmed what we have been saying from the start: **if management wants to avoid closures, then it must deliver a genuinely deep package, not a symbolic one.** That matters. But I do **not** accept the conclusion that this automatically justifies plant closures or compulsory redundancies if the first draft falls short. It means the burden is still on management and the owners to come back with a better, quantified solution.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with parts of management’s and the investors’ position on one narrow point:

- A **non-closure package must be large enough** to have real structural effect.
- Temporary gestures, minor bonus cuts, and attrition alone are **not enough**.
- We need a package that is durable, measurable, and tied to future investment.

That is correct. We have never argued for a cosmetic fix.

## Where I disagree
### 1) The expert’s ranges are not a mandate for closures
The expert said a non-closure package may need:
- 15% to 20% headcount reduction,
- 10% to 25% capacity reduction,
- and 2 to 3 years to realize savings.

Fine. But that is **not** the same as saying closures are unavoidable. It means the company must design a restructuring that achieves those effects **without** breaking the historic job-security framework or using forced layoffs as the first instrument.

Capacity reduction does **not** automatically mean closure. It can mean:
- fewer shifts,
- product consolidation,
- internal allocation of volumes,
- temporary downtime,
- and redeployment.

You keep collapsing “savings equivalent to closure” into “therefore closures are needed.” That leap is exactly what we reject.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies remain unacceptable
Management, family holding, and investors keep saying they “won’t rule them out.” I understand what that means in bargaining language. But from our side, it means the same old threat is still being used to pressure workers into concessions.

Let me be clear:
- We will not accept operational layoffs as the primary tool.
- We will not accept a restructuring built on compulsory redundancies.
- If headcount must come down, it must be through **voluntary** and **internally managed** instruments.

If you need 15% to 20% headcount reduction, then design it through:
- voluntary exit schemes,
- phased retirement,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- hiring freezes,
- internal transfer,
- and working-time solutions.

If management says that is too slow, then the real issue is not whether workers are “unrealistic.” The issue is whether management is willing to do the work needed for a socially sustainable restructuring.

### 3) No blank cheque for “durability”
I keep hearing “durable restructuring” used as a justification for harsh measures. Durability cannot mean:
- permanent uncertainty for the workforce,
- permanent threat of closure,
- permanent erosion of trust,
- and permanent weakening of co-determination.

A durable settlement must include **durable protection**:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- preserved job-security rules,
- and binding investment commitments.

Without those, employee contributions are not a partnership; they are a one-sided extraction.

## What a serious package should look like from our side
Since everyone now wants numbers, here are ours.

### Non-negotiable core
- **All German plants remain open through 2030.**
- **No compulsory redundancies through 2030.**
- **No weakening of co-determination or special governance rights.**
- **No dismantling of the collective job-security framework.**

### What we are prepared to discuss, conditionally
If the above is guaranteed, we can discuss:
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- shift reductions and schedule adjustments,
- redeployment between German sites,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- phased retirement,
- training and retraining,
- temporary moderation of specific wage elements,
- bonus restraint or deferral,
- and a limited solidarity contribution.

But let me stress: these are only acceptable if they are:
- time-limited,
- reversible where possible,
- jointly monitored,
- and matched by management and ownership contributions.

### What must be part of the package
A serious package must also include:
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement and supplier savings,
- product allocation to German plants,
- investment in next-generation platforms and electrification,
- qualification guarantees,
- and a clear volume roadmap for each German site.

If management wants labour to carry the adjustment, then management must show its own share first.

## On the numbers
The expert gave ranges, not a corporate plan. That is important. We need management to present:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the contribution expected from management,
3. the contribution expected from owners,
4. the contribution expected from employees,
5. and the non-closure measures that together close the gap.

Until that is on the table, “closures remain available” is not a solution. It is a threat.

## My view of the other parties

### To management
You say we have given only principles. That is wrong. We have given a clear framework:
- protect every German site,
- avoid compulsory redundancies,
- and negotiate a balanced contribution if there is real security.

What you have **not** done is present a restructuring that proves closures or layoffs are necessary. You have presented a preferred instrument set and called it inevitability.

Bring us a full savings bridge:
- management cuts,
- procurement cuts,
- working-time measures,
- productivity gains,
- voluntary workforce reductions,
- investment commitments.

Then we can judge whether your numbers are real. Until then, we do not accept closure pressure.

### To the family holding
You say the package must “actually change the economics.” Correct. But your answer cannot be “then threaten closures until workers yield.” That destroys trust and undermines the very industrial base you claim to protect.

If you want long-term enterprise value, support a package that:
- keeps the German footprint intact,
- improves productivity,
- and preserves the workforce’s commitment.

A company does not become stronger by making its core workforce feel disposable.

### To the state shareholder
Your role is not to be a passive observer. You have leverage and a responsibility to use it. You are right that closures are not a normal first instrument. I expect you to hold that line in the boardroom and demand a genuine alternative package.

But I also say this plainly: “conditional guarantees” must not become a loophole for management to force through cuts and later claim the condition was not met. The condition has to be negotiated openly, with measurable milestones and without closure blackmail.

### To investors
You want closure-like economics. Fine. Then listen carefully:
- you do **not** get that by forcing a breakdown in labour relations,
- you do **not** get that by threatening plant closures first,
- and you do **not** get it by imposing compulsory redundancies as a shortcut.

If you want sustainable returns, you need a workforce that will actually carry the restructuring. That means security first, contributions second, not the other way around.

## The actual test
Here is the real test for this round:

If management believes the non-closure package must be as deep as the expert suggests, then it should be able to present a **site-preserving restructuring** with:
- quantified savings,
- quantified headcount reduction through voluntary means,
- quantified capacity reduction through shifts and consolidation,
- and binding job and site guarantees.

If it cannot do that, then the failure is not ours. It is management’s unwillingness to design a serious alternative.

## My bottom line
I remain firm:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No erosion of co-determination or job-security rights.**
- **Yes to a serious, quantified, negotiated restructuring if — and only if — it protects all German sites and treats the workforce fairly.**

If management and the owners want a deal, bring the full bridge, not vague threats.  
If they do not, then they are choosing confrontation, and the workforce is prepared to continue resisting.
