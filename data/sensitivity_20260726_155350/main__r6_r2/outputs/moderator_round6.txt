Core impasse:

1. **Closure-free vs. closure fallback**
   - Works council, union, and state shareholder want a binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff path.
   - Management, family holding, and investors want closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available if savings fall short.
   - This is the central blocking issue.

2. **Guarantee design**
   - Labour side wants real site and job security through 2030.
   - Capital side wants only conditional protections tied to milestones.
   - The dispute is not just over security, but over whether security is genuine or reversible.

3. **Proof standard**
   - Everyone agrees the restructuring must be deep.
   - The conflict is over who must prove what:
     - labour side says management must prove closures are necessary,
     - management/capital say closure-free plans must prove sufficiency first.
   - There is no shared evidentiary threshold yet.

---

Compromise positions that could bridge the gap:

### Proposal 1: “No closures by default, fallback only after hard trigger”
- All parties commit first to a deep closure-free package.
- Closures and compulsory redundancies are not used immediately.
- A fallback is permitted only if pre-defined trigger points are missed:
  - savings gap remains above a set threshold,
  - capacity reduction falls below target,
  - voluntary uptake is below target after a fixed period,
  - or milestone audits fail.
- This preserves a closure-free starting point while keeping management’s fallback tools conditional rather than open-ended.

### Proposal 2: “Binding site security with milestone-based review”
- German sites receive protection through 2030, but protection is linked to verified implementation milestones.
- Reviews occur at fixed intervals, using an independent auditor.
- If the company meets targets on headcount, capacity, and recurring savings, protections continue.
- If targets are missed, parties reopen the package with a predefined escalation ladder, not an immediate closure decision.
- This could address the labour side’s need for real security and capital’s need for enforceable discipline.

### Proposal 3: “Deep non-closure package with symmetrical burden sharing”
- The package is limited to voluntary exits, phased retirement, redeployment, shift changes, and product allocation changes in phase one.
- Management, owners, and workforce each contribute in parallel:
  - workforce: time, flexibility, voluntary exits,
  - management: overhead cuts, executive restraint,
  - owners: investment and governance commitments.
- A joint implementation board with co-determination and independent monitoring tracks savings.
- Closures remain neither approved nor excluded, but only as a last-resort decision after a structured review.
