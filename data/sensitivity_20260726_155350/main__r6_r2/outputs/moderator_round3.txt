1) **What has been agreed**
- A purely cosmetic settlement is insufficient.
- Any viable restructuring must be **durable** and **economically material**.
- The expert’s benchmark is broadly accepted as the relevant test: a closure-free package must be deep enough to produce **closure-like economics**.
- There is shared acceptance that a serious solution likely requires:
  - meaningful headcount reduction,
  - some capacity reduction,
  - recurring labor-cost relief,
  - management/owner contributions,
  - and a multi-year implementation horizon.
- There is also broad agreement that **voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, productivity gains, and investment commitments** should be part of the toolbox.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Closures:**  
  Management, investors, and the family holding insist closures remain available if needed; employee representatives and the state shareholder reject closures as a default or first instrument.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side representatives reject them; management and capital-side actors will not rule them out as a matter of principle.
- **Guarantees:**  
  Employee and state-side actors want binding site/job protection; capital-side actors say guarantees must be conditional and cannot freeze flexibility upfront.
- **Burden sharing:**  
  Workers insist contributions must be matched by management and owners; capital-side actors agree in principle but say employee-side measures so far may still be too shallow.
- **Scale and mechanism of adjustment:**  
  There is no agreement yet on whether the needed reduction can be achieved through voluntary/internal measures alone, or whether more forceful measures may be required.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- The **exact savings gap** has not been formally presented by management.
- There is no agreed **quantified savings bridge** showing how much comes from:
  - management cuts,
  - owner contributions,
  - labor contributions,
  - procurement/overhead savings,
  - and capacity changes.
- The parties have not resolved the trade-off between:
  - **job and site security**, and
  - **speed/depth of cost reduction**.
- It remains unclear whether a non-closure package can remove enough **site overhead and fixed cost** to truly substitute for closures.
- No consensus exists on the acceptable balance between:
  - **voluntary measures**, and
  - **potential compulsory measures if voluntary tools fall short**.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to a **single quantified comparison** of three scenarios:
  1. closure,
  2. moderate non-closure,
  3. deep non-closure.
- Require management to present, for each scenario:
  - savings gap,
  - headcount impact,
  - capacity impact,
  - one-time restructuring cost,
  - timing to steady state,
  - and which sites/functions are affected.
- Ask each stakeholder to state explicitly:
  - what minimum package they could support,
  - which concessions are possible,
  - and which red lines are absolute.
- Keep the discussion focused on whether a **deep non-closure package** can truly deliver closure-equivalent economics, not on rhetorical repetition of positions.
