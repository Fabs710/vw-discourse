I’ll be clear and specific.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council that the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a temporary patch. I also agree that a **non-closure package can be credible only if it is deep enough** to materially change the economics. The expert’s ranges are useful for that reason.

I also agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **symbolic concessions will not solve this.** If the company’s cost base is structurally too high, then the package must produce real, recurring savings.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Deep does not mean “plant closures first”
Management keeps repeating that closures remain on the table. I do not accept that as a starting principle. Once closures are used as the first bargaining lever, trust is damaged and the whole process becomes coercive.

If the expert says a non-closure solution needs:
- 15% to 20% headcount reduction,
- 10% to 25% capacity reduction,
- and durable labor-cost relief,

then that is a **design challenge**, not a license to close plants.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are still not acceptable
I am not backing away from this. If the package needs significant headcount reduction, then it must come through:
- voluntary exits,
- phased retirement,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- natural attrition,
- working-time measures,
- and negotiated flexibility.

If management cannot make those tools add up, that does **not** automatically justify forced layoffs. It means management has not done enough to build a socially sustainable restructuring.

### 3) Workers cannot be the only ones making the sacrifice
I agree with the works council that employee contributions must be matched by contributions from management and owners. That is not a slogan; it is the basis for any agreement I can sell to the membership.

If workers give up wage elements, bonus components, or time flexibility, then I expect:
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement and supplier savings,
- product and investment commitments,
- and binding site security.

Without reciprocity, there is no fair deal.

## What I take from the expert’s analysis
The expert did **not** say closures are unavoidable. The expert said a closure-free package must be **large and enforceable** to match closure-like economics. That is exactly the standard we should be working to.

So the practical question is:
- can management present a package that reaches that scale **without** closures and **without** compulsory redundancies?

That is the only serious test.

## What a serious union position is
I am prepared to discuss a **temporary, quantified employee contribution** if, and only if, it is embedded in a binding package with real job and site protection.

That could include:
- temporary moderation of certain wage elements,
- bonus restraint or deferral,
- reduced overtime premia,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a solidarity contribution.

But I will not recommend anything to the membership unless it is:
- time-limited,
- fair,
- measurable,
- and matched by management and owner contributions.

## My response to the other parties

### To management
You say we need numbers. Fine. Then give them:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the exact contribution you expect from management cuts,
3. the exact contribution expected from owners,
4. the exact workforce contribution you are asking for,
5. and the exact job/site protections you are prepared to sign.

If you want a workforce agreement, stop speaking in generalities and put the full bridge on the table.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the core line: no compulsory redundancies, no wage dumping, no one-sided pain. Where I want to sharpen things is this: if we are discussing 15% to 20% headcount reduction, then we need to be explicit about **how** that happens. I support the use of voluntary and internal instruments first, but I will not accept a package that quietly relies on pressure to force exits.

### To the family holding
I understand your concern for durable economics. But durability is not created by threatening people into submission. If you want a workforce that carries this restructuring, you need to support a package that makes participation rational, not fearful.

### To the state shareholder
You are right to push for a site-preserving solution. I expect you to use your influence to ensure management does not weaponize closure threats to bypass negotiation. But “conditional guarantees” have to be real guarantees, not a trapdoor.

### To investors
You are right that we cannot accept a cosmetic compromise. But you are wrong if you believe forced layoffs or closures are the only way to reach seriousness. A non-closure package can deliver the needed economics if management and owners are willing to accept a smaller, but still viable, industrial footprint and if all sides contribute.

## The union’s line, plainly
I remain prepared to discuss:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus restraint or deferral,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and capacity adjustments.

I will **not** accept:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures as the bargaining baseline,
- or a restructuring where workers pay first and others pay later, if at all.

## What I want next
I want management to come back with a **quantified savings bridge** and a **non-closure scenario** that shows:
- headcount reduction through voluntary/internal means,
- capacity reduction without plant closures,
- duration of employee contribution,
- and binding commitments from management and owners.

If that package is deep enough, we can discuss it seriously. If it is not, then the company is not offering a solution — it is offering pressure.
