### 1) Points of agreement
- All sides accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that **costs must fall**, **capacity must be adjusted**, and **future investment/product commitment** is necessary.
- Most parties agree that **prolonged industrial conflict would damage value**.
- There is openness, in principle, to a package using **voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, productivity measures, and some employee contribution**.
- The expert clarified a shared factual baseline: a **non-closure package can work only if it is large enough to produce closure-like economic effects**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and state shareholder reject closures as a normal or acceptable instrument.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain available if alternatives do not close the gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side and state shareholder oppose them as the main tool.  
  - Management, family, and investors will not rule them out if needed to achieve the required savings.
- **Job/site guarantees:**  
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management, family, and investors view unconditional guarantees as incompatible with restructuring flexibility.
- **Burden sharing:**  
  - Employee side wants labor concessions only if matched by management/owner/state commitments.  
  - Capital/management side wants the workforce package to be quantified and sufficiently large first.
- **Scale test:**  
  - The main dispute is whether a non-closure package can realistically reach the savings required, not whether concessions are desirable in principle.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Exact savings gap:** no shared, quantified target has been agreed.
- **Distribution of burden:** no clear split yet between management cuts, workforce concessions, procurement/overhead savings, capacity reduction, and possible closures.
- **Size and timing of workforce measures:** the range needed for labor-cost relief is still unresolved.
- **Capacity reduction mechanism:** whether output cuts can be achieved without closures, and at what cost to utilization and fixed overhead, remains open.
- **Binding guarantees vs. flexibility:** no compromise model yet links employee concessions to credible site/job protection while preserving restructuring options.
- **Investment commitments vs. cost reduction:** future product and plant commitments are discussed, but not yet tied to a measurable restructuring package.

### 4) Refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single quantified scenario set**:
  1. closure scenario,
  2. moderate non-closure scenario,
  3. deep non-closure scenario.
- For each scenario, specify:
  - expected savings,
  - headcount effect,
  - capacity effect,
  - duration of employee contributions,
  - and any site/job protections.
- Ask management to present the **full savings bridge** and employee side to state the **maximum acceptable concession package** under defined guarantees.
- Keep the discussion focused on **whether a closure-free package can meet the same economic threshold**, rather than repeating red lines without numbers.
