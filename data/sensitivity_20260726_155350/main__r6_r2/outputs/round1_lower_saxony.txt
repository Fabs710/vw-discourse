I will be equally direct.

I agree with management on one essential point: this company needs a durable restructuring, not slogans. The cost base must come down, capacity must be aligned with demand, and the business has to become competitive again.

But I do **not** agree that the only credible path runs through plant closures in Germany or through a restructuring that shifts the burden disproportionately onto the workforce. That would be economically risky, politically reckless, and unacceptable from the perspective of a shareholder responsible for the regional economy.

Let me state our position clearly:

- **We will not support a restructuring that guts German sites or destroys large numbers of jobs in the region.**
- **We will not accept being sidelined in decisions that affect the industrial base and regional employment.**
- **We will not accept a solution that weakens co-determination or the veto rights attached to the special statute.**

To the works council and the union: your insistence on protecting sites, jobs, and collective safeguards is legitimate. I share the concern that management cannot simply socialize the pain onto employees while preserving strategic flexibility for itself. There must be reciprocity.

That said, I also want to be honest with you: preserving every line in its current form is not automatically a solution either. If we want to protect the company as a regional anchor over the long term, we need a restructuring that is economically credible. I would support a negotiated package if it includes:

- site protection in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- strong preference for voluntary departures, redeployment, and working-time instruments,
- a fair employee contribution that is limited, time-bound, and matched by contributions from management and owners,
- and, crucially, binding investment commitments for future products and technologies at German sites.

What I cannot support is a pure closure logic dressed up as competitiveness. Once a plant is gone, jobs, suppliers, skills, and regional value chains are gone with it. That is not a reversible adjustment; that is a strategic loss. For a company of this importance, that threshold should not be crossed lightly.

To management: if you want our support, you will need to demonstrate that you have seriously exhausted alternatives before invoking closures. Show us a package that actually delivers savings through capacity reduction, product mix changes, working-time flexibility, internal mobility, and negotiated contributions — without tearing out the company’s industrial core.

And let me be explicit: the state shareholder will use its rights to block any outcome that is a one-sided cut-and-close program. We are prepared to back a tough restructuring, but only one that preserves the company’s role as a regional economic anchor and respects the company’s governance balance.

So my message is:
- yes to restructuring,
- yes to competitiveness,
- yes to shared sacrifice,
- **no to plant closures as the default answer,**
- **no to compulsory layoffs as the main instrument,**
- and **no to any deal that removes the state’s co-determination and veto role.**

If a serious negotiated package comes forward that protects sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, we are ready to engage constructively. If not, you should assume we will stand with the workforce and use every lever available to stop an unacceptable outcome.
