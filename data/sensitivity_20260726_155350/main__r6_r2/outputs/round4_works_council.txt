I will speak plainly, because that is what this situation requires.

## First: what I agree with

I agree with the expert and with all sides on one important point: **a cosmetic deal is useless**.  
If the company wants a durable restructuring, then it must be economically material, measurable, and not just a press-release compromise.

I also agree that a serious non-closure package must be **large enough to have closure-like effects**. That is not controversial. The dispute is whether that automatically justifies closures or forced layoffs. It does **not**.

## Where I disagree — strongly

### 1) “Closure-equivalent economics” does **not** mean closures are acceptable
Management, the family holding, and investors keep trying to turn a benchmark into a weapon:  
because a non-closure package must be deep, therefore closures must remain on the table.

That is not a logical necessity; it is a bargaining threat.

The benchmark tells us what the economics must be. It does **not** erase the duty to find a solution that protects:
- every German site,
- the workforce,
- co-determination,
- and the job-security framework.

If a deep non-closure package is required, then management must design one. That is the job of management. Not the works council. Not the union. Not the state shareholder. And certainly not the workforce via blackmail.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies remain unacceptable
Let me be crystal clear: **we will not accept operational layoffs as the primary tool, and we will not accept them as a hidden fallback either.**

If the company needs headcount reduction, then it must be done through:
- voluntary departures,
- phased retirement,
- redeployment,
- hiring restraint,
- training and qualification,
- internal transfers,
- and working-time tools.

If management says that is too slow, then the answer is not “then force people out.”  
The answer is: **then management has not built a serious social restructuring plan.**

### 3) “Flexibility” cannot mean permanent insecurity
I also reject the attempt to frame binding guarantees as somehow unrealistic. They are the whole point of a credible settlement.

A durable agreement must include:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- preserved job-security rules,
- and binding investment commitments through 2030.

Without that, workers are being asked to pay for a restructuring that leaves them exposed to the next round of pressure.

## On the numbers

The expert’s ranges are a reference point, not a mandate for destruction.  
Yes, a deep non-closure package may need roughly:
- **15% to 20% headcount reduction**
- **10% to 25% capacity reduction**
- **2 to 3 years** to realize effects

But the critical question is **how** those effects are achieved.

For us, the answer is:
- fewer shifts,
- product consolidation,
- internal volume redistribution,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- voluntary departures,
- and reallocation across German sites.

What we do **not** accept is treating those ranges as code for:
- closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or the dismantling of the historic job-security framework.

## To management
You have still not answered the central question:  
**What is the exact savings gap, and what is your exact plan to close it without closures and without compulsory redundancies?**

You keep describing the problem. Fine.  
Now present the full bridge:
1. management overhead cuts,
2. procurement and supplier savings,
3. product and volume allocation,
4. voluntary workforce reduction,
5. capacity reduction without closure,
6. investment commitments,
7. and binding site protection.

Until then, “closures remain available” is not a plan. It is pressure.

## To the family holding
You say a real restructuring must materially change the economics. Correct.  
But if your solution is to threaten the workforce until it accepts a one-sided deal, then you are weakening the very industrial base that creates value.

Long-term value does not come from making German plants feel disposable.  
It comes from:
- stable sites,
- committed employees,
- predictable governance,
- and investments that make the German footprint stronger, not weaker.

## To the state shareholder
You have leverage. Use it.  
Do not hide behind “the economics” while management tries to push closure risk into the discussion as a bargaining shortcut.

A regional anchor shareholder has a duty to insist on:
- site preservation,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a genuine negotiated alternative.

But I will also say this clearly: a “conditional” approach must not become a loophole. Conditions must be **measurable, transparent, and jointly governed**. Not invented later as a pretext to reopen the closure threat.

## To the union
We are aligned on the essentials: no compulsory redundancies, no wage dumping, no one-sided sacrifice.  
Where I sharpen the position is this: if you discuss employee contributions, they must be **strictly conditional on binding guarantees**.

I do not want a package where workers give up pay, time, and flexibility first, and then management says the guarantees are “not met” and demands more. That is not partnership; that is escalation by instalment.

## To investors
You say moderate non-closure is too shallow and deep non-closure may be acceptable. Fine.  
But understand this: if you insist on “closure-like economics” while refusing the social conditions that make implementation possible, then you are pushing the company into conflict that will destroy value anyway.

A workforce that sees itself sacrificed will not carry a restructuring.  
If you want sustained returns, you need a settlement people can actually live with.

## My bottom line

I remain firm:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No weakening of co-determination.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**

I am prepared to discuss a serious, quantified restructuring **only if** it protects every German site and relies on voluntary, internal, and reversible measures.

## What I want next
If management wants credibility, bring back a **fully quantified non-closure bridge** with:
- the exact savings gap,
- the exact management contribution,
- the exact owner contribution,
- the exact voluntary workforce contribution,
- the exact capacity reduction by site and function,
- and the exact guarantees through 2030.

If that cannot be done without closures or forced layoffs, then that is not proof that workers are unrealistic.  
It is proof that management has chosen the wrong restructuring model.
