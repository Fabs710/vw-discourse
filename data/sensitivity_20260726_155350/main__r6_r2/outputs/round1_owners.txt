I appreciate the clarity from all sides, and I will be equally clear: the family holding is not indifferent to sites or jobs, but we will not support a solution that preserves the status quo while the company’s competitiveness continues to erode.

Where I agree:
- Management is right that the cost base is too high and that a durable solution is needed.
- The works council and union are right that a purely destructive approach would be self-defeating and would damage labor peace.
- The state is right that this company matters far beyond the balance sheet and that a one-sided closure program would carry heavy economic and political costs.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** accept that “no plant closures” can be a categorical rule if the business case cannot otherwise close the gap.
- I do **not** accept a restructuring that asks only management to be patient while labor costs remain structurally uncompetitive.
- I do **not** accept indefinite guarantees that would freeze the company into an unsustainable footprint.
- I do **not** accept a confrontation that drifts into prolonged industrial conflict and value destruction.

From the family holding’s perspective, the first duty is to protect long-term enterprise value. That means restoring margins, funding future investment, and preserving ownership control through a viable company. A settlement that avoids difficult decisions today but leaves the business weaker tomorrow is not acceptable.

That said, I am not wedded to closures as the only instrument. If there is a package that achieves comparable and durable savings without permanently damaging the industrial base, that is preferable. But it must be real, not rhetorical. It must include:
- meaningful and permanent cost reductions,
- measurable capacity adjustment,
- credible headcount reduction,
- operational flexibility,
- and a clear contribution from the workforce as well as management.

On the employee red lines: I hear them, but I cannot endorse a blanket promise of no closures and no compulsory redundancies if the savings do not materialize otherwise. That would simply defer the problem and increase the eventual damage.

To management: if you want support from the owners, bring us a plan that is credible, executable, and proportionate. A restructuring that is too weak to fix the margin problem will not be backed. At the same time, a plan that triggers avoidable governance conflict, reputational damage, and lasting labor war will also not be acceptable.

So my position is:
- We support a serious competitiveness agenda.
- We support negotiation over the instrument mix.
- We do **not** support symbolic restructuring.
- We do **not** support permanently impairing the company to preserve every structure unchanged.
- We do **not** support a prolonged conflict that destroys value.

If a package can be built that restores competitiveness, protects the company’s future, and avoids unnecessary instability, the family holding is prepared to engage constructively through governance. If not, we will not pretend that an unsustainable compromise is a solution.
