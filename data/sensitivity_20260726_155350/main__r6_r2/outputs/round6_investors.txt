We agree on the diagnosis: this company needs a **deep, durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic settlement. That is the only starting point we care about as capital-market shareholders.

Where we part company is on **execution discipline**.

## Where we agree
- A shallow compromise would be value-destructive.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, product allocation, and overhead simplification should all be used.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would damage margins and the share price.
- The package must be large enough to materially reduce the cost base and excess capacity.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be the answer by itself
The works council and union keep saying “no closures” as if that were a solution. It isn’t.

We are not wedded to closures. But we **will not support a settlement that rules them out in advance** if the deep non-closure route fails to remove enough fixed cost and capacity. If every site stays open and the overhead structure remains too heavy, then the company is just preserving an uncompetitive footprint.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute pre-commitment
We prefer voluntary tools first. But we will not back a blanket promise that excludes compulsory redundancies regardless of whether voluntary measures actually deliver the required reduction. If the uptake is short, management must retain credible fallback tools.

### 3) Conditional guarantees are better than unconditional guarantees
Binding site/job guarantees through 2030 are not acceptable if they freeze the turnaround. We can consider **conditional protections** tied to verified milestones on:
- headcount reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- recurring savings,
- and implementation timing.

But guarantees that cannot adapt to underperformance are not investment-grade governance.

## What we need
The expert is right: this needs a **site-by-site quantified savings bridge**. Not rhetoric, not threat inflation, not slogans.

We want management to put on the table, for each scenario:
1. exact savings gap,
2. management overhead cuts,
3. procurement/supplier savings,
4. product and volume allocation by site,
5. voluntary workforce measures,
6. capacity reduction by site,
7. one-time restructuring cost,
8. timing to steady state,
9. sensitivity if voluntary exits underdeliver.

Until that exists, nobody can credibly claim that a closure-free package is economically equivalent.

## Our position
We are open to a **deep non-closure restructuring**. We are **not** open to:
- a cosmetic deal,
- unconditional no-closure commitments,
- unconditional no-redundancy commitments,
- or an arrangement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

If the non-closure plan truly delivers closure-like economics, we can support it. If it does not, we will not pretend that preserving every site is the same as creating value.
