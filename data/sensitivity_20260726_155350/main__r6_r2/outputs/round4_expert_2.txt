# 1) Decision framework: what closure buys
A closure typically delivers savings from:

1. **Direct labor elimination**
   - wages, benefits, shift premiums, overtime

2. **Site fixed-cost removal**
   - utilities, maintenance, security, local management, site logistics, plant support

3. **Capacity rationalization**
   - less duplicated installed capacity, higher utilization at remaining plants

4. **Structural overhead simplification**
   - fewer local support layers and less duplicated admin/engineering

A non-closure package has to recreate most of this through:
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- shift cuts
- product consolidation
- working-time measures
- labor-cost concessions
- overhead simplification

# 6) What “closure-equivalent economics” means numerically
A closure-free package is economically credible only if it captures most of these effects:

## Must-have elements
- **At least 15% headcount reduction**
- **At least 10% capacity reduction**
- **Recurring cost reset**, not just temporary concessions
- **Site overhead simplification**
- **2–3 year implementation path**

## Why
Because if all sites stay open at near-original scale:
- fixed overhead remains too high
- utilization stays too low
- local support costs persist
- temporary concessions expire
- the cost problem returns
