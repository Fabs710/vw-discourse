We are now beyond principles, and the issue is whether the proposed alternatives are sufficiently concrete and quantitatively credible.

Let me be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the state, and the investors on one central point: this company needs a restructuring that actually changes the economics. A settlement that merely postpones the problem would be irresponsible. The family holding will not back a cosmetic compromise.

I also agree with the works council and the union on one important point: prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value. That is not in anyone’s interest, least of all ours as long-term owners.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” as an absolute is not credible
I understand the concern about irreversible damage. But a categorical ban on closures is not a governance position I can support if the arithmetic does not work otherwise. If alternatives cannot deliver equivalent durable savings, then closures must remain available.

That said, I am **not** asking for closures as a slogan or first instinct. I am asking for a package that makes them unnecessary. But that package has to be real.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute is also not enough
I would prefer voluntary instruments and internal adjustment. But if headcount and fixed costs do not come down at the required scale, then management cannot be boxed into an arrangement that looks humane but fails economically.

The test is not whether a measure is pleasant. The test is whether it produces durable structural savings.

### 3) Site guarantees through 2030 cannot be unconditional
I do not support blanket guarantees that lock in the current footprint regardless of business performance. That would protect structures, not value. Any guarantee must be conditional on a package that genuinely restores competitiveness and creates room for future investment.

## What I think a serious package must include
To be clear, I am open to avoiding closures if the alternative package is sufficiently strong. But it must include all of the following in a measurable way:

1. **Substantial and recurring labour-cost relief**
   - not symbolic,
   - not temporary window dressing,
   - and not dependent on optimistic assumptions.

2. **Real capacity reduction**
   - through shifts, product allocation, and footprint optimization,
   - with a clear effect on fixed costs.

3. **Credible headcount reduction**
   - preferably through voluntary departures, retirement, redeployment, and working-time measures first,
   - but with enough scale to matter.

4. **Operational flexibility**
   - shift and deployment flexibility,
   - more efficient use of sites,
   - and lower unit costs.

5. **Investment commitments tied to restructuring**
   - future products and technologies in Germany,
   - but not as a substitute for cost reduction.

6. **Labor peace for a defined period**
   - because execution matters,
   - but only if the package is genuinely durable.

## On the other parties

### To the works council
You ask for transparency and a sequencing of alternatives before hard measures. That is reasonable in principle. But transparency does not change the commercial reality. If you want closures off the table, then show us a package that closes the savings gap.

What I have not heard yet is a quantified alternative of sufficient scale. “Voluntary,” “flexibility,” and “redeployment” are tools, not an outcome. The outcome has to be a materially lower cost base.

### To the union
I appreciate that you are prepared to discuss a time-limited contribution. That is the right direction. But it must be quantified and significant enough to matter. A small, short-lived concession will not restore competitiveness.

If you want the workforce to contribute, then I expect a package with:
- clear duration,
- clear savings,
- and clear reciprocity from management and owners.

### To the state shareholder
I respect the regional responsibility you carry. But I need to say plainly: protecting every site at any cost is not a strategy. A company that remains structurally uncompetitive will eventually damage the region more severely than a tough restructuring would.

Your leverage is valuable if it helps produce a workable settlement. It is not valuable if it freezes the company into decline.

### To management
You are right that the company needs a decisive solution. But decisive does not mean blunt for its own sake. If you want support from the owners, you need to come back with a package that is both executable and economically sufficient.

That means:
- hard numbers,
- a realistic sequencing plan,
- and a clear explanation of how much savings come from which instrument.

Do not ask for approval of a closure-first strategy unless the alternatives genuinely fail. And do not present vague options as if they were a complete solution.

## My position, clearly
The family holding will support:
- a deep restructuring,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- real cost reduction,
- voluntary and negotiated workforce measures,
- and future investment tied to competitiveness.

We will **not** support:
- a symbolic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the cost base structurally too high,
- or a prolonged conflict that destroys value.

We are open to avoiding plant closures if — and only if — the alternative package is strong enough to produce the same durable economic effect. If it is not, then we cannot pretend otherwise.

## What I want next
I want management and the employee side to come back with a **quantified package**:
- exact savings,
- exact headcount effect,
- exact capacity reduction,
- exact duration of any employee contribution,
- and the conditions under which site protection would be credible.

Without that, we are still debating intentions, not a solution.
