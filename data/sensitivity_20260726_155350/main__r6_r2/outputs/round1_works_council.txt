As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price of a restructuring that management created through its own strategic mistakes.

Our priority is simple and non-negotiable: secure every German site, protect the workforce, and keep co-determination and collective safeguards intact. If the company needs savings, then management must look first to alternative measures that preserve jobs and locations: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, voluntary instruments, smarter investment sequencing, and a fair contribution from all sides — not a one-sided assault on employees.

We are prepared to discuss a serious restructuring, but only under binding conditions:
- no plant closures,
- no operational layoffs,
- clear site and job guarantees through 2030,
- preservation of co-determination rights,
- and a credible industrial plan for future products and investment in Germany.

If management comes here with a fait accompli, we will resist it. We have already shown that the workforce is prepared to act, and we will use every legitimate means — including warning strikes and public pressure — to defend jobs and sites. We want a solution, but it must be a fair one that does not sacrifice the workforce to fix management’s miscalculations.
