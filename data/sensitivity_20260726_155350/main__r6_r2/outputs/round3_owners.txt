I will be direct.

The family holding’s position is unchanged in substance: **we need a restructuring that materially and durably restores competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity**. We are not backing a cosmetic compromise, and we are not backing a settlement that merely defers the problem while preserving structurally uncompetitive costs.

At the same time, we are also not interested in a destructive confrontation that damages the company’s value, reputation, and execution capability. So the test is simple: **does the package produce closure-like economics in a way that is executable in this governance structure and stable enough to hold?**

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the company cannot stay in its current cost and capacity configuration.  
You are also right that any serious solution must be **durable**, not temporary.

The expert’s contribution is useful because it confirms a hard truth: **if we avoid closures, the alternative must still deliver major headcount reduction, capacity reduction, and recurring labor-cost relief**. That is the right benchmark.

### With the union and works council
You are also right that a purely coercive process would be value-destructive.  
A prolonged strike or open-ended labor war would hurt the enterprise, the workforce, and the owners alike.

You are right that temporary concessions alone are not enough if they are not tied to a real restructuring path.

### With the state shareholder
You are right to insist that the German industrial base matters and that co-determination cannot simply be brushed aside.  
A company of this scale cannot be governed as if plants were interchangeable spreadsheet cells.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” as an absolute is not acceptable
I do **not** support a categorical ban on closures.  
If the economics cannot be fixed otherwise, then closures must remain available as a lawful restructuring instrument.

That is not my first preference. It is not my preferred outcome. But I will not remove a necessary instrument before I know whether a closure-free package truly closes the gap.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute is also not acceptable
I would prefer voluntary exits, redeployment, retirement pathways, and working-time instruments.  
But I will not commit the owners to a framework that guarantees no compulsory headcount reduction regardless of business need.

If the company needs a real fixed-cost reset, then the workforce side must accept that the outcome may require more than attrition.

### 3) Unconditional site guarantees through 2030 are not supportable
I understand why the employee side is asking for certainty.  
But unconditional guarantees that freeze the footprint regardless of performance would protect structures, not value.

A guarantee only makes sense if it is tied to a credible package that actually restores competitiveness. Otherwise it is just a delay mechanism.

## What I think the expert’s analysis means

The expert did not say closure is the only route.  
The expert said a closure-free package would need to be **deep enough to behave economically like a closure**.

That is the real standard here.

So let’s be clear: a non-closure solution is possible **only if** it includes enough of the following at once:

- substantial and recurring labor-cost relief,
- meaningful headcount reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- lower fixed costs through simplification and utilization gains,
- and a credible implementation timeline.

If we do not get those elements in sufficient scale, then the package is too small.

## My assessment of the current positions

### To the works council
I hear your insistence on site and job security. But you have not yet shown how the numbers work at the scale the company needs **without** meaningful structural change.

Saying “voluntary exits, redeployment, shifts, and internal allocation” is not yet a savings bridge. It is a list of tools.  
What I need to see is the actual bridge:
- how many jobs come out,
- what capacity comes out,
- what cost comes out,
- and over what time period.

### To the union
I appreciate that you are prepared to discuss a time-limited contribution. That is constructive.  
But again: it must be **quantified, durable, and significant enough to matter**. Small temporary concessions will not restore competitiveness.

If workers contribute, I expect that contribution to be matched by:
- management cost reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and a credible industrial plan.

### To management
You are correct that the company cannot afford a symbolic deal.  
But you still need to bring back a package that is not only tough, but **executable**.

Do not come back with closure language as a bargaining shortcut unless the non-closure options have been fully tested and shown to fail.  
And do not ask the owners to endorse a deal that is economically insufficient.

### To the state shareholder
Your role matters, but your veto power cannot be used to preserve uncompetitive structures indefinitely.  
If you want us to back a closure-free outcome, then help ensure it is deep enough to work. Protection without economics is not stewardship.

## What I would support

I would support a package if it contains all of the following:

1. **A clear savings target**
   - with a full bridge showing management, labor, and operational contributions.

2. **Meaningful headcount reduction**
   - preferably via voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, and hiring restraint first,
   - but sufficient in scale to materially reduce fixed costs.

3. **Real capacity reduction**
   - fewer shifts, product consolidation, operational simplification, and lower utilization where needed.

4. **Temporary employee contributions**
   - only if they are time-limited, measurable, and matched by management and ownership action.

5. **Investment commitments**
   - tied to future products and technologies,
   - but not used as a substitute for cost reduction.

6. **Labor peace for implementation**
   - because prolonged conflict would destroy value.

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- a package that preserves current employment and capacity levels while asking the owners to absorb the losses,
- or a settlement that prevents management from taking the measures needed to restore margins.

## My bottom line

The family holding is open to a **closure-free solution**, but only if it is deep enough to create **closure-like economics** in substance, not just in rhetoric.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, then it must show:
- the exact headcount reduction,
- the exact capacity reduction,
- the exact labor-cost relief,
- and the exact duration of concessions.

If that package adds up, we can support it.  
If it does not, then we should not pretend otherwise.
