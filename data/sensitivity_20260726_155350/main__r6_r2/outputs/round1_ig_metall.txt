I support the core line from the works council: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective framework.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: if management tries to make the workforce carry the full burden of the restructuring, there will be resistance. The peace obligation has ended, and our members are already expecting action. Warning strikes were not symbolic; they were the beginning of a serious bargaining process.

Where I do agree:
- The company clearly needs a durable solution.
- Costs have to come down.
- Capacity has to be adjusted to market reality.
- We should not pretend that nothing must change.

Where I disagree:
- We will not accept a restructuring built on plant closures in Germany.
- We will not accept forced layoffs as the main instrument.
- We will not accept wage dumping or the erosion of collective standards under the pretext of competitiveness.
- We will not accept that employees pay while owners, management, and other stakeholders contribute little or nothing.

What is possible, and what we have already signaled willingness to discuss, is a shared-sacrifice package:
- temporary or negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- reduced capacity and shift adjustments,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary departures and internal redeployment,
- bonus restraint or other employee-side contributions,
- and a credible investment commitment for future products and sites in Germany.

But this only works if there is real reciprocity. If management wants sacrifices from the workforce, then we expect binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and future investment. We need a settlement that is durable, not a short-term cut that leaves workers exposed and the company weaker in the medium term.

So my position is straightforward:
- We are ready to negotiate hard.
- We are ready to contribute fairly.
- We are not ready to accept closures or compulsory redundancies.
- And if those red lines are pushed, industrial action will intensify.

If management and the owners want a viable path forward, they need to come with a package that shares the pain and protects the industrial base in Germany.
