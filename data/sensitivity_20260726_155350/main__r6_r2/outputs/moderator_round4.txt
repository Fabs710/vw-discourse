## Core impasse
Stakeholders have converged on the **need for a deep restructuring**, but remain split on **the permissible instruments**.

The conflict is concentrated in three linked questions:

1. **Can the required economics be achieved without closures?**  
   Employee-side stakeholders say yes, if the package is deep enough and built from voluntary/internal measures.  
   Capital and management-side stakeholders say: maybe, but not yet proven at scale.

2. **Can compulsory redundancies be ruled out in advance?**  
   Employee-side stakeholders say yes, as a non-negotiable principle.  
   Management and investors say no, not before the economics are proven.

3. **Can site/job guarantees through 2030 be binding?**  
   Employee-side and state-side stakeholders want firm guarantees.  
   Management and capital-side stakeholders argue that guarantees must remain conditional on performance and execution.

So the deadlock is not over whether change is needed. It is over **whether the change can be made credible without plant closures or forced layoffs, and whether guarantees can be unconditional**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging positions that could narrow the gap:

### 1) Deep non-closure package with milestone-based fallback
A closure-free package is adopted first, but only if it is **quantified, phased, and independently monitored**.  
If agreed milestones on headcount, capacity, and cost are missed by defined checkpoints, the parties reopen the package for additional measures.

**Why it bridges:**  
- Satisfies employee-side demand for a non-closure starting point.  
- Gives management and investors a credible fallback if the package underperforms.  
- Reduces the binary “either closures now or never” framing.

---

### 2) No plant closure moratorium, but no closure decision before a verified bridge test
All sides agree to a time-limited pause on closure decisions while a specialist team tests whether the deep non-closure package can meet the benchmark.  
During that period, no side precommits to closure, but also no side receives a permanent veto.

**Why it bridges:**  
- Preserves employee trust by suspending immediate closure threats.  
- Preserves management and investor leverage by not foreclosing closure permanently.  
- Creates a disciplined decision window tied to evidence.

---

### 3) Conditional guarantees with stronger burden-sharing
The parties agree on:
- no compulsory redundancies as the **first** instrument,
- site protection for the relevant planning horizon **if** agreed savings are achieved,
- and matched contributions from management, owners, and workforce.

If the package delivers the verified savings, guarantees activate or extend. If not, the parties renegotiate predefined fallback measures.

**Why it bridges:**  
- Gives the workforce real protection, but not unconditional immunity.  
- Gives capital a performance-based structure rather than open-ended commitments.  
- Makes contributions reciprocal instead of one-sided.
