We need to stop talking in abstractions and put the burden where it belongs: on management and the owners to present a real restructuring that does **not** destroy jobs, sites, and co-determination in Germany.

I will respond point by point.

## Where I agree
Yes, the company needs a durable solution.
Yes, costs must come down.
Yes, capacity must be adjusted.
Yes, we need future investment and product commitment in Germany.

We are not denying the business reality. What we reject is the idea that the only “serious” solution is to start with German plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That is not strategy; that is an attack on the workforce to cover for years of mismanagement and strategic delays.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable “fallback”
Management, investors, and the family keep saying closures remain on the table “if needed.” We reject that framing. If you begin by putting closures on the table, you are already threatening irreversible damage to skills, suppliers, regional economies, and trust. This company has never closed a German plant in its history. That threshold should not be crossed now simply because management wants a faster or easier accounting fix.

If you claim alternatives are insufficient, then show the numbers transparently:
- What exact savings are needed?
- What part comes from management cuts?
- What part comes from supplier and procurement measures?
- What part comes from working-time, shift, and capacity measures?
- What part comes from voluntary instruments?

Do not hide closure threats behind vague language about “durability.”

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are off the table
We will not accept operational layoffs. Period.
If headcount must reduce, then it must be through:
- voluntary departure programs,
- retirement and phased retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- natural attrition,
- and working-time solutions.

If management says these are not enough, that is not proof that layoffs are necessary. It is proof that management has not done the hard work of designing an alternative.

### 3) No blank cheque on labour concessions
We hear “labour cost reduction” from management, investors, and the family. Fine — but be specific.
What exactly do you want?
- temporary wage restraint?
- bonus deferral?
- longer working time?
- shift flexibility?
- reduced overtime premia?
- a one-off solidarity contribution?

We are prepared to discuss targeted, time-limited measures **only if** they are tied to binding guarantees:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- site protection through 2030,
- preserved co-determination rights,
- and a real investment plan for German plants.

Without that, any concession from the workforce would just finance the next round of pressure.

## What a serious package must look like
If management wants a negotiated result, here is the only credible direction:

### A. Site and job guarantees
- Binding guarantee that all German plants remain open through 2030.
- No compulsory redundancies through 2030.
- No breaking of existing job-security arrangements.
- Full preservation of co-determination and the special governance protections.

### B. Capacity adjustment without closures
- Reduced shifts where demand is lower.
- Temporary downtime and flexible production allocation.
- Internal reallocation of volumes across German sites.
- Product mix changes to use existing sites better.
- Voluntary capacity reduction, not closure.

### C. Workforce contribution, but only conditional and limited
We are ready to discuss:
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- voluntary wage restraint at the margins,
- reduction of non-essential bonus elements,
- and special solidarity measures,
**but only** if they are:
- limited in time,
- jointly monitored,
- and matched by management, owners, and the state-side governance actors.

### D. Investment and future products
A restructuring without future investment is just managed decline.
We need:
- binding product allocation to German plants,
- battery/electrification and platform commitments,
- training and qualification guarantees,
- and a clear volume roadmap.

## On sequencing
The correct order is not “announce closures, then negotiate pain.”
The correct order is:
1. transparently define the savings need,
2. exhaust alternatives that preserve sites,
3. agree a balanced contribution from all sides,
4. lock in guarantees,
5. then implement.

If management refuses that sequence, it is choosing confrontation.

## On the positions of the others
### To management
Your refusal to commit to job and site guarantees is exactly why trust is low. You ask for flexibility first and promise safety later. We have seen where that leads. If you want our cooperation, bring a plan that starts with preservation, not destruction.

### To the family holding
You speak about long-term enterprise value, but there is no long-term value if you tear out the industrial base. A “credible” restructuring that closes plants is not credible for the workforce, the regions, or the company’s social legitimacy.

### To the state shareholder
We appreciate your support for site protection and veto rights. But support has to be active. Use your leverage to stop closure logic and force management to the table with a genuine alternative package.

### To investors
You keep saying “returns” as if jobs and industrial capability were irrelevant. They are not. A company that wins by hollowing out its domestic base is not creating sustainable value; it is just shifting risk onto workers and regions.

## Bottom line
Let me be unambiguous:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No erosion of co-determination or job-security rights.**
- **Yes to negotiated, temporary, and fair contributions if — and only if — jobs and sites are protected.**

If management cannot deliver a savings package without closures and layoffs, then management has not presented a solution. It has presented a threat.

Bring exact numbers, bring a site-protection framework, and bring a real industrial plan. Until then, the works council will stand firm, and we will continue to mobilise the workforce to defend every German site.
