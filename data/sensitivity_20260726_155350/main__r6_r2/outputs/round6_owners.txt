I will be direct.

## My position
I support a **deep restructuring** because the company’s German cost base and capacity are not competitive enough.  
I do **not** support a cosmetic compromise that preserves overcapacity and merely postpones the problem.

But I also do **not** accept an outcome that permanently traps the company in an uneconomic structure. So if a closure-free package is to be preferred, it must be proven to deliver **closure-like economics**. If it cannot, then it is not a solution.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the investors on the central economic point:

- the package must be **large**,
- it must be **durable**,
- it must produce **recurring savings**,
- and it must materially reduce **headcount, capacity, and fixed cost**.

I also agree with the works council, union, and state that a prolonged conflict would destroy value. That is true. A strike spiral, reputational damage, and prolonged uncertainty would hurt the business, the workforce, and the owners.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be a starting absolute
The works council and union keep repeating that no German plant closures are acceptable under any circumstances.

I understand the political and social logic. I do **not** accept it as a governance principle.

If the non-closure package truly closes the economic gap, fine. But if it does not, then preserving every site at any cost is not stewardship — it is value erosion. I will not endorse a framework that removes the fallback before the economics are proven.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” also cannot be absolute upfront
Voluntary exits and redeployment should absolutely be the first tools. I support that.

But I will not commit the owners to ruling out compulsory redundancies in advance if voluntary measures underdeliver. That would weaken restructuring credibility and invite delay tactics.

### 3) Conditional guarantees must be real, not a disguised veto
The state shareholder and employee side want site/job guarantees through 2030. I can support **conditional protection** tied to milestones. I cannot support a blanket guarantee that freezes flexibility regardless of execution.

A guarantee that can never adapt is not governance; it is a lock-in to weakness.

## On the numbers
The expert and finance specialist have given the right seriousness test:
- **15%–20% headcount reduction**
- **10%–25% capacity reduction**
- **2–3 years** to steady state

That is the correct order of magnitude if the company is to regain competitiveness without closures.

But let me be equally clear: those numbers do **not** prove that closures are unnecessary. They prove only that the non-closure route, if used, must be deep enough to remove the same economic burden.

## What I need to see
Management still owes the board and owners a **full savings bridge**. Not slogans, not pressure. A bridge.

I want, at minimum:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. management overhead cuts,
3. procurement and supplier savings,
4. product and volume allocation by site,
5. voluntary workforce reduction,
6. capacity reduction by site,
7. one-time restructuring cost,
8. timing to steady state,
9. sensitivity if voluntary exits fall short.

Without that, nobody can honestly say the deep non-closure route is proven.

## To management
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. I support that.

But do not turn “closures remain on the table” into an excuse to avoid proving the closure-free case. Show the numbers. If the non-closure route works, I will support it. If it does not, I will not pretend otherwise.

## To the works council
You are right that workers cannot be the only ones paying. I agree.

But you are not yet entitled to assume that a closure-free package will automatically be sufficient simply because it is preferable. Preference is not proof. If the package truly delivers the economics, then it can be supported. If not, then insisting on no closures in advance is not a strategy.

## To the union
I agree that employee contributions must be matched by management and owner contributions. That is non-negotiable.

But temporary concessions are not enough unless they create durable structural change. The company cannot simply postpone the cost problem and call that social partnership.

## To the state shareholder
I respect your regional responsibility. But regional importance does not override economic reality.

I can support a site-preserving solution if it is genuinely deep and credible. I cannot support a political veto on all hard instruments before the evidence is in.

## To institutional investors
You are right to reject a shallow compromise. I share that concern.

Where I differ from the employee side is this: deep does not automatically mean closure, but neither does it automatically mean preservation of every site. The outcome has to be judged by the economics.

## Bottom line
I remain open to a **deep non-closure restructuring** if it is:

- quantified,
- independently tested,
- large enough to deliver closure-like economics,
- and disciplined by milestones.

But I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic package,
- unconditional no-closure commitments,
- unconditional no-redundancy commitments,
- or guarantees that freeze the company’s ability to adapt.

The company needs durable competitiveness and restored margins. That is the standard I will hold.
