We are prepared to keep negotiating, but I want to be very clear about the limits from the workforce and retiree trust side.

## Where we agree with the task force and management
- The company must be viable after restructuring.
- Delay is dangerous; we do not want a slow collapse.
- Every stakeholder has to share sacrifice.
- Public support should be conditional and not open-ended.

We are not denying reality. We have already accepted concessions in earlier rounds, and we know more is needed.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The retiree health trust cannot be treated as just another line item to be erased
You keep saying the trust cannot be “sacrosanct” if the company cannot carry it. We understand that. But there is a huge difference between making the obligation sustainable and destroying it.

What we will not accept:
- a near-wipeout of the trust,
- a token instrument that has no real value,
- or a structure that leaves retirees bearing the deepest cuts while bondholders preserve meaningful upside.

If the trust is converted, it must be converted into something durable and real — equity, preferred equity, secured paper, or a genuinely valuable contingent instrument. Not a paper promise.

### 2) Workers cannot be asked to finance the rescue through wholesale job destruction
You talk about a “minimum viable company,” but for us that cannot mean a disguised liquidation of plants and communities.

We can accept selective closures and brand reductions. We cannot accept:
- broad plant shuttering that sacrifices whole regions,
- a collapse of the wage framework,
- or work-rule changes so severe that the jobs that remain are hollowed out.

Viability is not achieved by gutting the workforce and calling it efficiency.

### 3) The bondholders cannot insist on equal treatment while resisting meaningful losses
We hear the bondholders’ argument on equal treatment among unsecured claims. But let’s be honest: unsecured creditors are already asking to be repaid out of a company that is weeks from failure. They are not entitled to protection from real pain.

That said, we do not support using the retiree trust as a dumping ground to make bondholder losses look smaller. If the trust receives a durable security or equity-linked claim, then bondholders also need a recovery that is real, not symbolic. But there must be shared sacrifice across both unsecured classes.

## On the facilitator’s proposed bridge

### “Minimum viable company + protected domestic core”
This is the right direction, but only if “protected domestic core” means actual plants and jobs, not just brand language.

We would want:
- a binding minimum domestic production footprint,
- commitments on a core set of plants,
- and limits on closures that preserve the industrial base.

If that cannot be written into the deal, then “core” is just rhetoric.

### “Equal unsecured treatment with compensating instruments”
This can work only if the economics are fair.

Our position is:
- the retiree trust should receive a durable, meaningful instrument,
- bondholders should receive their own real recovery package,
- and the combined restructuring should not place disproportionate pain on workers and retirees.

If the bondholders want to object to the form of the trust recovery, then they need to accept equivalent economic value elsewhere. We will not accept a structure where the trust is downgraded merely to make creditor optics easier.

### “Staged public support tied to milestones”
We can support this, provided the milestones are not only about labor concessions and plant closures.

Milestones should also include:
- actual debt reduction,
- credible funding of the retiree obligation in its new form,
- and verified commitments on domestic production and employment.

Public money should not be released unless the rescue protects both viability and the industrial base.

## What would move us
We remain open to serious concessions if the package includes:
- a durable and valuable instrument for the retiree trust,
- binding commitments to preserve a core set of domestic plants and jobs,
- shared pain from creditors and management,
- and labor concessions limited to what is truly necessary.

We can discuss wages, work rules, and benefits. We cannot discuss accepting a plan that destroys the trust or treats the workforce as the easiest place to extract value.

## Bottom line
We want a viable company. We want the company to survive. But survival has to be built on a fair settlement, not on sacrificing retirees and workers first so that creditors and the public can say they achieved “discipline.”

If the task force is serious about a durable rescue, then it should support a restructuring that:
- preserves a real domestic industrial core,
- gives the retiree trust a meaningful lasting claim,
- and imposes genuine losses on unsecured creditors and management as well.

Otherwise, you may get a fast process, but you will not get a settlement we can stand behind.
