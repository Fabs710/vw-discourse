### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company must remain viable after restructuring.
- All parties agree the process should be fast, final, and not open-ended.
- There is broad support in principle for conditional public support tied to milestones.
- There is shared acceptance that sacrifices must be shared across stakeholders.
- There is tentative openness to a “minimum viable company” framework.
- There is some willingness to use different instruments, so long as economic value is treated as real and transparent.
- Neighbouring governments and others accept that co-funding should come with governance rights or equity of some kind.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment:** whether it can be substantially converted or reduced, and whether any alternative instrument is genuinely equivalent in value.
- **Bondholder recovery:** whether unsecured bondholders are being asked to absorb too much of the burden, and whether they are being treated equitably relative to the retiree trust.
- **Labour and plant rationalization:** how deep closures, job cuts, wage/work-rule changes, and brand reduction must go to achieve viability.
- **Domestic production commitments:** how much domestic capacity must be preserved, and whether binding production floors can coexist with a lean enough company.
- **Public/co-funder conditions:** whether funding can be released without locking in an uneconomic footprint, and whether domestic protection can be made contractual without undermining restructuring.
- **Court-supervised fallback:** whether the fallback route is a neutral backstop or an unacceptable threat of imposed terms.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Viability vs. preservation:** how much capacity and employment can be retained without leaving the company overburdened.
- **Equality vs. differentiation:** whether different stakeholder classes can receive different instruments if the economic value is equivalent.
- **Domestic footprint vs. cost reduction:** what minimum domestic presence is compatible with the needed cost base.
- **Speed vs. completeness:** how to finalize the deal quickly without leaving essential concessions unresolved.
- **Public support vs. private losses:** how much public money is justified, and under what verified restructuring steps.
- **Fairness vs. acceptability:** which group bears the deepest losses, and how to avoid any one stakeholder being seen as the primary absorber.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please converge on a single package framework with numbers or ranges for:
- minimum debt reduction,
- minimum viable plant/brand footprint,
- retiree trust recovery instrument and its economic value,
- bondholder recovery range,
- domestic production/employment floor,
- and the milestones required for public/co-funder releases.

The next round should focus on narrowing these variables into one internally consistent restructuring plan rather than restating positions.
