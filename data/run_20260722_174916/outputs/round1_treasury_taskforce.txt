The government task force’s position is straightforward:

We will support a restructuring only if it is deep, fast, and credible enough to produce a viable company without open-ended public subsidy.

Our priorities are:

1. **Viability first**  
   The company must emerge with a cost structure, plant footprint, labor terms, and balance sheet that can actually survive in the market. We are not prepared to finance a delayed collapse.

2. **Shared sacrifice**  
   Management, labour, and creditors must all take real losses. The burden cannot fall primarily on taxpayers.

3. **Speed and finality**  
   We have a 60-day window. We want a definitive agreement or, if necessary, a court-supervised process that can impose one quickly and cleanly.

4. **Taxpayer protection**  
   Any public support must be limited, temporary, and tied to hard restructuring milestones. We are not here to underwrite an unrestructured legacy structure.

What we will argue for:
- A substantial reduction in debt and legacy obligations.
- Meaningful concessions on labor costs and retiree health commitments.
- Plant, brand, and dealer rationalization at a scale that matches demand reality.
- A capital structure that leaves the company investable and solvent.
- Equity terms that reflect the losses imposed on private claimants.

What we will argue against:
- Any plan that merely postpones insolvency.
- Any proposal that preserves too much debt or too much excess capacity.
- Any attempt to shift the burden onto the public purse while shielding creditors or other private stakeholders.
- Delay tactics or demands for better treatment without corresponding concessions.

We are open to a consensual deal, but only if it meets these standards. If it does not, we are prepared to proceed through an expedited court-supervised restructuring.
