We are prepared to co-fund a rescue, but only on terms that **protect domestic production and employment in a concrete, binding way**.

### Where we agree
- **The company has to be viable.** We are not interested in financing a slow-motion collapse.
- **All stakeholders must share sacrifice.** That includes labour, creditors, management, and the public sector.
- **Speed matters.** Sixty days is not long, and delay only destroys more jobs and value.
- **A court-supervised path is acceptable if needed.** We are not attached to process for its own sake.

### Where we disagree
#### 1. Production footprint cannot be treated as a secondary issue
A restructuring that preserves a viable company on paper while shifting production and jobs out of our jurisdiction is not an acceptable rescue from our standpoint.

If foreign plants are protected while domestic plants are closed, or if the domestic workforce is asked to absorb the cuts while production migrates elsewhere, we will not support it. Our participation depends on **binding commitments to maintain a meaningful domestic production share and employment base**.

#### 2. We will not fund without a commensurate say
If the neighbouring governments are co-funding, we cannot be treated as passive cheque-writers. A proportionate contribution requires a **proportionate ownership position and governance voice** in the restructured company.

We are not asking for control. We are asking for **partnership treatment**, with equity reflecting our contribution and the footprint we are protecting.

#### 3. The restructuring cannot be balanced by sacrificing domestic plants
We understand the need for rationalization. But “plant cuts based on utilization” can too easily become a formula for closing domestic facilities while leaving other locations relatively insulated. That is exactly the outcome we will resist.

Any plant rationalization must be tied to:
- clear viability criteria,
- protection of core domestic capacity,
- and explicit commitments on employment and production.

### On the other stakeholders
- **To management:** We appreciate the focus on viability, but viability is not just a balance-sheet exercise. It also means preserving the industrial base that supports long-term competitiveness. We can support a leaner company, not a hollowed-out one.
- **To labour and the retiree trust:** We are sympathetic to the employment and community stakes. But the rescue cannot be built on preserving every legacy cost if the result is failure. The tradeoff has to be real and durable.
- **To bondholders:** You are right that unsecured creditors should be treated fairly. We are not asking for you to carry the whole burden. But fairness cannot come at the expense of domestic production commitments being ignored.

### Our bottom line
We are open to co-funding only if the package includes:
1. **Binding commitments to preserve domestic production and employment**;
2. **A proportionate equity stake for the co-funders**;
3. **A restructuring that is deep enough to be viable** without offshoring the domestic industrial footprint;
4. **A process that reaches finality quickly**, whether consensual or court-supervised.

If those conditions are not met, then we should not be expected to fund a rescue that protects the company everywhere except at home.
