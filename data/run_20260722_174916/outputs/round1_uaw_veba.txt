We agree on one central point: **the company has to emerge viable, not merely propped up for a few more months**. We also agree that every stakeholder has to share sacrifice. The workforce and retirees are not asking to be insulated from reality.

But let me be equally clear: **shared sacrifice is not the same as sacrificing workers and retirees first or disproportionately**.

### Where we align
- **Viability matters.** We understand the company cannot survive with an unsustainable cost base and balance sheet.
- **Speed matters.** We do not want endless delay or a collapse into liquidation.
- **Credibility matters.** Any restructuring has to be durable enough that the company can actually invest, operate, and keep jobs.

### Where we disagree
1. **Retiree health obligations cannot simply be treated as disposable debt.**  
   That trust represents earned obligations to people who already gave a lifetime to this company. If the proposal is to wipe that out or reduce it to a token recovery, that is not a fair restructuring — it is an abandonment. We will not accept destruction of the trust.

2. **Workers cannot be asked to carry the bulk of the rescue while unsecured creditors are protected.**  
   The bondholders took risk in exchange for return. We are prepared for them to take losses. But there is no acceptable settlement in which jobs, wages, work rules, and retiree benefits are cut deeply while creditors are treated too generously.

3. **Plant closures and brand cuts must be selective, not a liquidation by another name.**  
   We recognize the need for rationalization, but the burden on industrial communities cannot be arbitrary or one-sided. We need a plan that preserves as many productive plants and jobs as possible while still making the company viable.

### What would move us
We are open to a settlement if it includes:
- **A real equity or securities claim for the retiree health trust** — something durable, not symbolic.
- **Meaningful job and plant commitments** so the restructuring does not become wholesale workforce destruction.
- **Shared losses from creditors and management**, not just labor.
- **A structure that preserves the wage framework as much as possible**, with concessions only to the extent necessary for survival.

### Our bottom line
We are ready to negotiate serious concessions if they are part of a balanced survival plan. But if the message is that workers and retirees must absorb the deepest cuts while financial creditors are made whole enough to call it “consensual,” then we will oppose it.

If the task force is serious about viability, it should support a restructuring that **converts the retiree obligation into something durable, preserves core employment, and forces creditors to bear genuine pain**. Otherwise, the court-supervised route may be faster, but it will not be legitimate from our standpoint.
