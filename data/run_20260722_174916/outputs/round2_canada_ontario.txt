We are prepared to keep working toward a rescue, but I need to be equally direct about our bottom line: **there will be no co-funding without binding protection for domestic production and employment**. If the restructuring preserves the company while hollowing out our plants, that is not an acceptable outcome for us.

## To the Task Force

We agree on the need for a deep, fast, credible restructuring. We also agree that taxpayers should not be left underwriting a legacy structure that cannot survive.

Where we disagree is your tendency to treat plant rationalization as purely a viability exercise, without giving enough weight to where the production ends up.

Our position is:
- **A viable company is necessary, but not sufficient.**
- The rescue must also preserve a **meaningful domestic production floor** and a **real employment base** in our jurisdiction.
- If domestic plants are closed while foreign plants are protected, we will not support the deal.

On process, we can work within a fast timetable, and we understand the court-supervised fallback. But speed cannot be used to force us into funding a plan that transfers jobs out of our country.

## To Management

We appreciate that you need balance-sheet repair and operational rationalization. We are not asking you to preserve every plant or every line forever.

But we do **not** accept a restructuring logic where the easiest cuts fall on domestic production, while the company keeps capacity elsewhere and calls that “efficiency.”

What we need to see from management:
- a clear commitment to **keep core domestic plants open**,
- a defined **minimum production share** for our jurisdiction,
- and an employment plan that preserves a substantial domestic industrial base.

If those commitments are absent, then asking us to co-fund is asking us to finance the erosion of our own economy. That is a non-starter.

## To the Union and Retiree Trust

We are sympathetic to your insistence that workers and retirees should not carry the burden alone. We agree that the rescue cannot be built by sacrificing labour first.

We also support a settlement that preserves as much employment as possible. Where we differ is that domestic job protection for us is not the same thing as preserving every current arrangement. The company does need to become leaner.

But if there is a plan that:
- limits plant closures,
- protects core jobs,
- and gives the domestic footprint real contractual protection,

then we can support meaningful labour and legacy concessions as part of a broader package.

## To the Bondholders

We agree with you on one point: **form cannot conceal economics**. If the retiree trust gets a different instrument, the economics have to be comparable.

But I need to be clear: your claim to equal treatment does **not** give you a veto over our co-funding conditions, and it does not justify asking us to protect the domestic footprint at no cost.

We are not demanding that bondholders take all the pain. We are saying that if we are contributing capital, we expect:
- a viable restructuring,
- a proportionate equity position,
- and domestic production commitments that are real and enforceable.

If the economic package for unsecured creditors is made fair by the final terms, that is one issue. But it does not erase our distinct interest in jobs and production.

## On the facilitator’s bridge proposals

### “Minimum viable company + protected domestic core”
This is the right direction, but only if the domestic core is **binding and measurable**, not just a vague assurance.

For us, that means:
- a minimum number of domestic plants retained,
- a minimum domestic employment level,
- and a minimum domestic production share over an agreed period.

If those are not contractual, the commitment is too weak to justify our funding.

### “Equal unsecured treatment with compensating instruments”
That is mainly between the task force, bondholders, and the retiree trust. We do not object to fair economic treatment among unsecured claims, but we will not trade away domestic industrial protection to make those economics easier.

### “Staged public support tied to milestones”
We support conditional funding in principle. But the milestones must include **verified domestic commitments**, not only financial metrics and labour concessions.

If public money is released and the domestic footprint is still left vulnerable, then the structure is wrong.

## Our position on co-funding and ownership

This is important: if we are contributing materially, we need **a proportionate ownership position and governance voice**. That is not a side issue.

We are not seeking control, but we are also not prepared to be a junior payer with no real say. Partnership treatment means:
- equity reflecting our contribution,
- governance rights appropriate to that stake,
- and explicit protections for our domestic production interests.

## Bottom line

We can support a rescue if, and only if, it includes:
1. **Binding domestic production and employment commitments**;
2. **A proportionate equity stake and governance voice for co-funders**;
3. **A restructuring deep enough to be viable**;
4. **A fast, final process** that does not sacrifice our plants to save the company elsewhere.

What we will not support is a deal that funds a restructured company while shifting production and employment out of our jurisdiction. That outcome is fundamentally unacceptable to us.
