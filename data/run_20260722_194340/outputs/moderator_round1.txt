### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **fast restructuring** and that the **60-day timeframe is real**.
- There is broad agreement that the **old capital structure is not viable** and that **deep sacrifices** will be required.
- All parties accept some form of **balance-sheet reset** and **operating rationalization**:
  - plant/brand/overhead reductions,
  - meaningful unsecured creditor losses,
  - and changes to labor and legacy obligations.
- There is openness, in principle, to a **court-supervised process** if it is the fastest route to finality.
- There is also general acknowledgment that **labour peace** and **continued operations** are essential to preserving value.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree health obligation / trust treatment**
  - Union: wants the trust preserved in a durable, real-value form.
  - Management and task force: want material restructuring and lower economic burden.
  - Bondholders: object if the trust receives better treatment than unsecured creditors.
- **Relative treatment among unsecured claimants**
  - Bondholders insist on **pari passu / principled treatment** and reject being disadvantaged versus the retiree trust.
  - The union wants creditors to take real losses, but not at the expense of workers and retirees being the primary shock absorber.
- **Extent and targeting of plant/brand rationalization**
  - Task force and management support major rationalization.
  - Union wants closures to be targeted and justified, not indiscriminate.
  - Neighbouring governments want domestic production and jobs protected.
- **Use of public funding / co-funding conditions**
  - Task force wants taxpayer exposure minimized.
  - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production and employment commitments.
  - No final alignment yet on what public support purchases in terms of operational commitments.
- **Ownership and recovery structure**
  - All sides mention equity/upside allocation, but there is no agreement on:
    - who receives what,
    - in what order,
    - or what value each constituency should recover.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much value is allocated to labor/retirees vs. unsecured creditors**
  - This is the central economic dispute.
- **Whether the retiree trust becomes a reduced but durable claim, or is materially converted/written down**
  - No agreed formula or benchmark.
- **How deep creditor haircuts must be to make the company viable**
  - “Substantial” has not been quantified.
- **What public co-funding is contingent upon**
  - Especially whether it requires binding domestic production guarantees.
- **How much capacity should be cut without undermining the viable core business**
  - No shared line yet between needed rationalization and destructive shrinkage.
- **What the restructured company’s ownership should look like**
  - No agreement on control, equity split, warrants, or upside participation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **specific exchange terms**:
  1. quantify proposed haircuts/conversions for unsecured creditors,
  2. define the retiree trust’s replacement structure,
  3. specify labor concessions and any corresponding job/plant commitments,
  4. identify which plants/brands are retained or closed,
  5. clarify what public co-funding requires in enforceable terms.
- The next round should aim to narrow the dispute to **one integrated package** rather than separate side positions.
