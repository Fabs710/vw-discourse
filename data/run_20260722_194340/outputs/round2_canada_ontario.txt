We are prepared to be constructive, but I need to be explicit about the line we will not cross.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **real restructuring quickly**.
- The old capital structure is **not viable**.
- There must be **shared sacrifice** from labour, creditors, and public stakeholders.
- A **court-supervised process** is acceptable if it is the fastest reliable route to finality.

We also accept the valuation advice: this is an insolvent, cash-burning business and the rescue only works if the burden is meaningfully reduced.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic production cannot be treated as a side issue
To the task force and management: we do **not** support a plan that closes domestic plants while foreign capacity is protected, or one that uses our co-funding without binding commitments on domestic production and employment.

That is not a negotiable preference for us. It is the condition of our participation.

If public money is going in from our side, then the restructuring must preserve a **credible domestic industrial footprint**:
- binding production commitments,
- binding employment commitments where feasible,
- and a footprint allocation that protects skilled jobs and supply chains in our jurisdiction.

A plan that simply shrinks our plants to fund viability elsewhere is unacceptable.

### 2) We are not a passive funding source
To the task force: we are not asking for control for the sake of it, but we are also not willing to write a cheque and be told after the fact that our plants are the adjustment variable.

If we are co-funding, we expect a **proportionate ownership position** reflecting that contribution, plus governance rights that match the risk we are taking. Being treated as a junior payer with no meaningful seat at the table is not partnership.

### 3) We support real restructuring, not offshoring by rescue
To management: we understand that the footprint must be rationalized and that not every site can survive. But “rationalization” cannot become a euphemism for shifting production away from our jurisdictions while we help finance the transition.

We can support closures or consolidation **only if** they are part of a broader plan that preserves a viable domestic core and does not hollow out the industrial base we are responsible for.

## On the bondholders’ argument
We hear the parity argument. We are not taking sides in the unsecured creditor fight as such.

But I will say this clearly: **co-funding from neighbouring governments is not available to subsidize an outcome that sacrifices domestic jobs or production while financial claimants argue over relative recoveries.**

If the restructuring needs our participation, then domestic commitments must be embedded in the deal first. That is the basis on which we can support it.

## On the union and retiree trust
We support the principle that workers and retirees cannot be the only shock absorber.

At the same time, our support for the rescue depends on a package that protects the domestic footprint. That means we can accept a durable, reworked legacy obligation if it sits inside a restructuring that preserves the plants and employment base here. We cannot support a deal where labour takes pain and the domestic footprint is cut away anyway.

## What we need to see
A viable package from our perspective must include:

1. **Binding domestic production and employment commitments**
2. **A proportionate equity stake for co-funders**
3. **An orderly plant rationalization plan that protects the domestic core**
4. **No co-funding if domestic plants are disproportionately sacrificed**

If those points are not in the final term sheet, then our answer remains no.

## Bottom line
We are constructive partners, not an open-ended backstop.  
We can support a rescue, but only if it protects the domestic production base and gives us a proportionate ownership position for the capital we are putting at risk.
