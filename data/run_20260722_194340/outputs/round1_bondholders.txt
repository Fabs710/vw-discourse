We are not here to underwrite a rescue by **handing unsecured creditors the short end of the stick while others protect their own economics**.

Let me be direct.

### Where we agree
- The company is in crisis and needs a real reset, fast.
- The old capital structure cannot survive unchanged.
- A viable company requires plant, brand, and overhead rationalization.
- Labour and the retiree trust cannot be treated as the only parties making sacrifice.
- If a court-supervised process is the only way to get finality, then so be it.

### Where we disagree
The current framing still sounds like **creditors are expected to absorb a severe haircut while the retiree trust is asked to be “restructured” in a softer, more protected way**. That is not acceptable.

We are a legitimate unsecured creditor class with a clear contractual claim — including many ordinary savers, not “speculators.” We will not agree to a deal where:
- unsecured bondholders are written down deeply,
- the retiree trust is treated more favorably in economic terms,
- and the result is effectively a political allocation of loss.

If there is shared sacrifice, it must be **real and proportionate** across unsecured claims. The trust cannot be ring-fenced into something superior while our recovery is pushed toward a wipeout.

### On the retiree trust
We do not oppose converting the retiree obligation into a durable instrument. We do oppose **any structure that leaves the trust economically ahead of us** simply because it is politically easier to justify.

If the trust receives equity, securities, or a funding vehicle, then unsecured bondholders must receive **comparable treatment in value terms**, not a token stub. Equal ranking unsecured claims should not be treated as if some are sacred and others disposable.

### On our recovery
You say “substantial haircut.” Fine — but define substantial. Because from our perspective, “substantial” too easily becomes confiscatory.

We will not accept terms materially worse than comparable unsecured claims without compensation. That means:
- if the retiree trust gets a meaningful recovery, so must we;
- if we are forced into equity or warrants, the allocation must reflect our senior legal standing relative to any other unsecured recovery;
- and if the process is court-supervised, the court should not be used to bless political favoritism.

### On the process
We are not blocking the rescue. We are blocking **an unfair rescue**.

If the task force is serious about finality, then it should make clear that:
1. unsecured claims will be treated on a principled, pari-passu basis;
2. no stakeholder gets a stealth preference;
3. any creditor exchange must provide real upside participation, not just diluted paper;
4. and the restructuring cannot be structured so that ordinary bondholders are wiped out while another unsecured constituency is preserved.

### Bottom line
We are open to a fast deal. We are open to equity, warrants, and a court-backed process if necessary. But we are **not** open to:
- a confiscatory exchange,
- inferior treatment versus the retiree trust,
- or a restructuring that uses urgency as a pretext for political subordination of creditor rights.

If you want our support, the terms need to show that unsecured bondholders are being treated as real creditors, not as the easiest class to sacrifice.
