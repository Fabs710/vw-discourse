We agree on one central point: **this company must be made viable quickly, and nobody gets a free ride**. The workforce and the retiree trust understand that the old structure cannot survive intact.

But I need to be equally clear: **a viable restructuring cannot be built by treating workers and retirees as the primary shock absorber while unsecured creditors are protected or while the trust is simply stripped out.** That is not a rescue; it is a transfer of pain onto the people who carried the company for decades.

Our position is this:

1. **The retiree health obligation must be honored in a durable form**
   - We are open to changing the form of the obligation.
   - We are not open to destroying the trust or turning it into a meaningless promise.
   - If the claim is to be reduced or restructured, it must be converted into something real and enforceable — including equity, securities, or another durable funding vehicle with actual value.

2. **Workers will consider labor concessions, but only alongside shared sacrifice**
   - We have already made concessions in earlier rounds.
   - Further wage, work-rule, or benefit changes can be discussed.
   - But those changes must be tied to plant and job commitments, not just abstract promises of competitiveness.

3. **Unsecured creditors must take substantial losses**
   - They cannot be made whole while retirees are cut deeply and workers absorb the rest.
   - If the task force insists on shared sacrifice, then creditor write-downs must be real, not cosmetic.

4. **We will support plant and brand rationalization only if it is targeted and justified**
   - We understand the need for a smaller footprint.
   - But we will resist blanket closures that sacrifice industrial communities unnecessarily.
   - The cuts must be tied to a credible long-term operating plan, not a liquidation by another name.

5. **We are prepared to work through this quickly, but not to be railroaded**
   - If the court-supervised route is used to impose a fair balance, we will assess it on substance.
   - If the process is used to force a disproportionate burden onto labor and retirees, we will not treat that as acceptable just because time is short.

So, to be specific:  
- **Yes** to deep restructuring.  
- **Yes** to shared sacrifice.  
- **Yes** to a durable reformed ownership structure if the trust receives a real claim.  
- **No** to destroying the retiree trust.  
- **No** to a deal where workers and retirees are sacrificed wholesale to protect creditor recoveries.

If the task force wants labour peace and a viable company, then the plan has to recognize that **the workforce and retirees are not junior expendable parties**. We are essential stakeholders, and our support depends on that reality being reflected in the final terms.
