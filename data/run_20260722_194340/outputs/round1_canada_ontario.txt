We are prepared to support this rescue, but only on terms that **actually protect domestic production and employment**. That is the condition under which we are a co-funder, not a passive bystander.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a real restructuring, not bridge financing disguised as a plan.
- The balance sheet and cost base must be reset.
- Unsecured creditors must take meaningful losses.
- The process must move quickly and decisively.
- A court-supervised route is acceptable if it is the only way to get a viable outcome.

### Where we disagree
I need to be very clear: **we will not put public money into a restructuring that closes domestic plants while foreign plants are protected, or that pays for a deal without a commensurate domestic footprint commitment.** That would be politically and economically unacceptable.

To the task force: if co-funding from neighbouring governments is part of the solution, then it must come with **binding commitments on production, employment, and plant allocation** in our jurisdiction. We are not interested in subsidizing a footprint that migrates elsewhere after we absorb the cost.

To management: your point about viability is right, but viability cannot mean simply shrinking our plants while leaving other parts of the network intact. If this rescue is to succeed, the operating plan must include **durable commitments to preserve and rationalize domestic production in a way that protects skilled jobs and regional supply chains**.

To the union and retiree trust: we support labour peace and we recognize that workers and retirees cannot be the sole shock absorber. But our support for co-funding depends on seeing a settlement that protects the domestic industrial base. We can work with changes to the trust, provided they are part of an orderly, durable restructuring and not a mechanism to shift the entire burden onto labour in exchange for plant closures here.

To the bondholders: we hear the concern about pari passu treatment. We are not asking for preferential treatment over legitimate unsecured claims. But we are also not willing to provide public support unless the restructuring includes **real commitments to the domestic plants that our taxpayers and workers depend on**. That is not favoritism; it is the basis on which our participation is justified.

### Our position
We are willing to co-fund if, and only if, the final deal includes:
1. **Binding production and employment commitments for domestic plants**
2. **A proportionate equity position reflecting our contribution**
3. **An orderly restructuring that preserves a viable long-term footprint here**
4. **No funding if domestic production is sacrificed while others are shielded**

If those conditions are not met, we should not be expected to write a check.

So the message from us is simple: **we are constructive partners, but not a financing backstop for offshoring by restructuring.**
