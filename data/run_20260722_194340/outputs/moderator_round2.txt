### Neutral moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- The company is **not viable in its current form** and needs a **rapid balance-sheet reset**.
- There is broad agreement that the restructuring must include:
  - **deep unsecured creditor losses**,
  - **material reduction of the retiree health obligation**,
  - **labor concessions**,
  - **meaningful plant/brand rationalization**,
  - and likely **wiping out old equity**.
- There is also shared acceptance that a **court-supervised process remains a credible fallback** if a quick consensual deal cannot be reached.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Retiree trust treatment**
  - The task force, management, and bondholders want it materially reduced in economic value.
  - The union insists it must remain **durable and real**, not gutted.
  - Bondholders object to any structure that gives the trust better economics than unsecured creditors without clear legal justification.

- **Relative treatment of unsecured claimants**
  - Bondholders are demanding **pari-passu / comparable economic treatment** with the retiree trust.
  - The union rejects being forced into the same recovery logic as bondholders if that undermines the trust’s durability.
  - The task force wants flexibility to allocate value based on viability, not strict symmetry.

- **Domestic production / plant commitments**
  - Neighboring governments will only support the rescue if there are **binding domestic production and employment commitments**.
  - The task force and management insist those commitments cannot veto economically necessary plant cuts.
  - The union also wants specific plant/job protections, but only for the viable core.

- **Ownership and governance**
  - Co-funders want a **proportionate equity stake and governance rights**.
  - The task force is open to co-funding but not to giving it control over restructuring decisions.
  - Final ownership allocation remains unresolved.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much of the burden falls on bondholders versus the retiree trust** if the value pool is limited.
- **Whether the trust gets a distinct, durable replacement instrument** and if so, what its present value should be.
- **Which plants, brands, and jobs are preserved** versus closed, and what criteria determine that list.
- **How domestic production covenants are balanced** against the need to shrink the footprint for viability.
- **What exact equity split** results among new money, public co-funders, impaired creditors, and any labor/retiree participation.
- **What valuation case will anchor the plan**, since recoveries depend on the assumed enterprise value and normalized EBITDA.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single integrated term sheet** with:
  - a valuation range,
  - class-by-class recoveries,
  - a proposed retiree trust conversion,
  - a defined plant rationalization list,
  - domestic production covenants,
  - and a proposed ownership/governance split.
- The next round should aim to answer one question: **what exact package is financeable, legally durable, and politically supportable enough to close quickly?**
