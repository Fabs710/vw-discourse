I’ll be direct.

I **agree** with management, the family holding, and investors on one essential point: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one. The cost and capacity problem is real, and pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

But I **do not** accept the implication that the only credible solution is plant closures and deep cuts that gut German sites. That is not a neutral economic choice for me — it would damage the regional economy, destroy trust, and undermine the company’s role as an industrial anchor. As a blocking shareholder under the special statute, I will not support that path.

### To the Works Council and the union
I support your insistence that the burden cannot be dumped onto employees alone. I also support using:
- working-time adjustments
- voluntary departures
- redeployment
- qualification
- internal transfers
- temporary and negotiated employee contributions

Where I differ is that I need more than a political red line. “No closures, no compulsory redundancies” is only sustainable if you can help produce a package that is **quantified and materially credible**. If the alternative measures do not deliver enough savings and capacity adjustment, then we are not yet at a settlement.

That said, I am **not** prepared to treat closures as the default benchmark and force the workforce to justify its own survival. The right question is not “which plant do we close first?” The right question is: **what package protects the German industrial base while restoring competitiveness?**

### To management
You are right that temporary optics are not enough. But you are still not giving me a convincing answer to the governance and regional consequences of your approach.

If you want my support, then bring a plan that includes:
- **binding investment commitments for German sites**
- **a substantial but negotiated employee contribution**
- **management and owner contribution visible in the package**
- **site-preserving capacity adjustment first**
- **no compulsory redundancies as the default**
- **closures only as a last resort, after alternatives are proven insufficient**

Right now, your argument sounds too much like “prove to us that closures are not needed.” I expect the reverse as well: **prove that you have exhausted non-destructive alternatives before asking for plant closures in the state.**

### To the family holding
I hear your concern about preserving a viable business. I share that concern. But stability is not achieved by pushing through measures that blow up social partnership and political legitimacy. A deal that may look cleaner on paper but triggers industrial conflict is not a durable solution.

### To investors
You are right that we cannot accept a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. But “equivalent economics” cannot mean treating jobs and sites as disposable inputs. For this company, governance, co-determination, and regional anchoring are part of the industrial model, not externalities.

### My position
I am open to a **negotiated restructuring**, but only if it:
- protects a substantial German employment base
- avoids compulsory redundancies
- avoids plant closures unless they are truly unavoidable and demonstrably last-resort
- secures real investment in future products and sites
- shares the burden fairly across management, owners, and workforce
- preserves co-determination and the state’s role in governance

I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that removes jobs and sites in the region as the primary adjustment tool. If that is the direction management insists on, then it will meet a veto from me.

If the employee side can help shape a package that produces real savings without destruction, I will support it. If management insists on closures as the starting point, I will oppose it.
