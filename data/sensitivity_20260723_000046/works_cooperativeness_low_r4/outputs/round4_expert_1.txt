Below is a side-by-side restructuring comparison, staying within industrial restructuring and finance. Because the record does not give hard euros or capacity figures, I use **scenario-based estimates** and clearly separate **verified facts**, **inferences**, and **assumptions**.

---

# Executive view

**Verified from the record**
- The company has a structural competitiveness problem and German overcapacity.
- A non-closure package is being discussed, but no quantified full bridge has been proven.
- Plant closures and compulsory redundancies face strong governance and labor resistance.
- Conflict risk is material; labor peace has ended.
- Management says non-closure measures may be insufficient; employee-side actors say they are sufficient if fairly shared.

**Finance conclusion**
- **Non-closure restructuring** is usually better on **timing, certainty, and execution risk**.
- **Closure-based restructuring** usually offers **higher gross structural savings**, but also much **higher one-time charges, disruption risk, and implementation uncertainty**.
- On **risk-adjusted NPV**, a non-closure package can win **if** it closes most of the cost gap and starts savings quickly.
- Closure-based restructuring wins only if the residual gap after non-closure measures is large and closure execution is relatively clean.

# Side-by-side comparison

## 1) Direct cost savings

### A. Non-closure package
Typical measures:
- wage moderation / bonus restraint
- overtime reduction
- shift flexibility
- voluntary exits / attrition
- redeployment and qualification
- management overhead cuts
- owner contribution via reinvestment / lower distributions

**Estimated annual savings**
- **Direct labor and operating savings:** roughly **40–70% of closure-level savings**
- In many automotive cases, the non-closure package can capture **a substantial but incomplete share** of the footprint-reduction benefit.

**What that means in practice**
- If closure-based restructuring would save 100 on a normalized annual basis, non-closure measures might save **40–70**.
- Savings are often faster to realize because they do not require shutdown sequencing.

### B. Closure-based restructuring
Typical measures:
- plant closure(s)
- capacity reduction
- larger workforce reduction
- elimination of fixed site costs
- deeper labor-cost reset

**Estimated annual savings**
- **100% benchmark** for structural savings, and sometimes more if a site is fully removed and overhead is cut decisively.
- Usually the largest gross savings option.

**Important caveat**
- Gross savings are not the same as net savings. Shutdown costs and disruption can erode a large part of the headline benefit.

---

## 2) Indirect cost savings and value effects

### A. Non-closure package
Indirect advantages:
- less production disruption
- less loss of know-how
- better supplier continuity
- lower probability of warning strikes or prolonged conflict
- lower brand and reputation damage
- less management distraction

**Estimated indirect benefit**
- Often material, but hard to quantify.
- In NPV terms, this can be worth **meaningful low double-digit percentage points** of the gross savings gap versus closure, depending on conflict severity.

### B. Closure-based restructuring
Indirect effects:
- possible long-run simplification of the network
- reduced structural complexity
- better utilization at surviving sites if volumes are consolidated well

But also:
- lost local know-how
- ramp-up risk at receiving plants
- supply-chain and launch disruption
- political and social backlash
- lower morale and retention risk across the network

**Estimated indirect cost**
- Can be large enough to offset a meaningful part of the gross savings, especially if conflict is prolonged.

---

# Employment impact

## A. Non-closure package
**Likely employment effect**
- lower headcount mainly through voluntary exits, attrition, reduced hours, redeployment
- fewer or no forced layoffs
- jobs preserved at site level

**Estimated impact**
- Workforce reduction is usually **partial and slower**.
- Example range: if a closure plan would eliminate 100 jobs, a non-closure package might reduce staffing by **30–60** through voluntary and flexible measures, depending on workforce age profile and transferability.

## B. Closure-based restructuring
**Likely employment effect**
- much larger direct job losses
- stronger concentration of losses at specific sites
- higher risk of compulsory redundancies if voluntary exits are insufficient

**Estimated impact**
- Can deliver the largest absolute reduction in employment
- but with a much sharper local and social shock

---

# Capacity implications

## A. Non-closure package
**Capacity effect**
- capacity is reduced via shift elimination, product allocation, working-time changes, and role differentiation
- network remains intact, but utilization is improved through internal reallocation

**Typical outcome**
- capacity reduction is **real but partial**
- preserves optionality for future demand recovery
- less risk of permanently losing production capability

**Estimate**
- Often sufficient to cut **some** overcapacity, but not always enough to remove a deep structural excess if the footprint is genuinely too large.

## B. Closure-based restructuring
**Capacity effect**
- removes capacity decisively
- can produce a cleaner utilization reset
- can improve fixed-cost absorption at surviving plants if volumes are transferred efficiently

**Typical outcome**
- strongest footprint correction
- best if the core problem is chronic structural overcapacity rather than temporary demand weakness

**Estimate**
- More likely to fully remove excess capacity
- but the realized benefit depends on whether the volume transfer is stable and the surviving plants can absorb it without new bottlenecks

---

# Transition / implementation risk

## A. Non-closure package
**Risk profile**
- lower implementation risk
- lower political and labor confrontation
- easier to phase in
- lower chance of operational interruption

**Main risk**
- savings may be too small or too temporary
- concessions may expire before competitiveness is restored
- renegotiation risk in later years

**Overall risk rating**
- **Low to moderate**

## B. Closure-based restructuring
**Risk profile**
- much higher implementation risk
- risk of strikes, warning strikes, injunction-related delays, transfer bottlenecks, attrition of key talent
- shutdown and relocation risk

**Main risk**
- headline savings are delayed or partially lost
- conflict costs can be large
- one-time restructuring charges may exceed initial estimates

**Overall risk rating**
- **High**

---

# Timing and certainty of outcomes

## A. Non-closure package
**Timing**
- savings can begin **within months**
- early measures like overtime reduction, bonus restraint, and shift changes are fast
- voluntary exits and redeployment take longer, but are still quicker than closures

**Certainty**
- higher certainty of near-term implementation
- lower certainty that the package fully solves the structural problem

**Best use**
- when the company needs immediate relief and wants to avoid operational disruption

## B. Closure-based restructuring
**Timing**
- savings often arrive **later** because of consultation, shutdown sequencing, transfer of production, and ramp-up at receiving sites
- the full benefit may take **12–24 months or longer** to realize

**Certainty**
- lower certainty of clean execution
- higher probability of delay and partial realization

**Best use**
- when management can prove the residual gap is too large for negotiated measures and can sustain the transition cost

# NPV-style assessment

Because we lack hard financials, the right comparison is **risk-adjusted NPV**, not headline savings.

## Non-closure package: likely NPV advantages
- lower one-time charges
- faster savings realization
- lower strike and disruption risk
- less know-how loss
- less supplier disruption

## Closure-based restructuring: likely NPV disadvantages
- high upfront charges
- delayed savings
- higher conflict risk
- higher execution complexity

## Bottom-line finance judgment
- **If non-closure measures can credibly close most of the gap, they likely produce the higher risk-adjusted NPV.**
- **If they only close a minority of the gap, closure-based restructuring may outperform on NPV, but only if execution is relatively clean and disruption is contained.**

# Practical scenario ranges

To make the trade-off concrete, here is a normalized illustration:

## Scenario 1: Non-closure package closes most of the gap
- Gross savings achieved: **60–70% of closure plan**
- One-time charges: **low**
- Disruption risk: **low to moderate**
- Timing: **fast**
- Likely winner: **non-closure package**

## Scenario 2: Non-closure package only partially closes the gap
- Gross savings achieved: **40–50% of closure plan**
- One-time charges: **low**
- Disruption risk: **low**
- Likely winner depends on residual gap size and how urgent margin recovery is

## Scenario 3: Closure plan triggers serious conflict
- Gross savings achieved: **100% on paper**
- Realized savings after delay/disruption: **materially lower**
- One-time charges: **high**
- Likely winner: often still **non-closure**, on NPV grounds

## Scenario 4: Closure plan executes cleanly
- Gross savings achieved: **100%**
- Realized savings close to plan
- One-time charges absorbed
- Likely winner: **closure-based restructuring**
- But this is not the base-case assumption given the governance and labor environment in the record

# Final conclusion

## Non-closure restructuring
**Best on:**
- timing
- certainty
- execution risk
- social and operational continuity

**Weakness:**
- may not fully remove structural overcapacity unless the contribution package is deep and durable

## Closure-based restructuring
**Best on:**
- gross structural savings
- capacity reset
- long-run footprint reduction

**Weakness:**
- high one-time cost
- high conflict risk
- slower realization
- uncertain net NPV

## Overall
From a restructuring-finance perspective, **a well-designed non-closure package is usually superior on risk-adjusted NPV unless management can demonstrate a substantial residual savings gap that non-closure measures cannot close.** Closure-based restructuring should be treated as the stronger but much riskier instrument, not as the default superior option.
