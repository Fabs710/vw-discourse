### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- **All parties accept that the company faces a serious, durable competitiveness problem** and that a cosmetic fix is not sufficient.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should, where possible, use **negotiated instruments first**: working-time adjustments, voluntary departures, redeployment, qualification, and internal transfers.
- There is also broad agreement that a viable package should include **investment commitments** and a **path back to sustainable margins**.
- Most parties accept that **open conflict would be value-destructive** and that a negotiated settlement is preferable.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- The main split is over **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table**.
  - Works Council and union reject both as red lines.
  - Management, family holding, and institutional investors say structural reductions may be necessary if alternatives do not deliver sufficient savings.
  - The state government is opposed to closures as a first choice but not fully closed to negotiated adjustment.
- There is also disagreement over **how large and durable employee contributions must be**.
  - Management and investors want quantified, material savings.
  - Employee side accepts contributions only if they are fair, limited, and matched by management/owner contributions.
- A further divide concerns **the acceptable level of job and site security through 2030**.
  - Employee and state sides seek binding guarantees.
  - Management, family, and investors question whether absolute guarantees are compatible with competitiveness.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Security vs. flexibility:** no common formula yet for protecting jobs and sites while preserving room for capacity adjustment.
- **Voluntary measures vs. structural savings:** no agreement on whether voluntary and working-time tools can produce savings equivalent to closures or deep cuts.
- **Fair burden sharing:** the precise split between employee contributions, management contributions, owner support, and operational adjustments remains undefined.
- **Quantification:** management has asked for concrete savings; employee-side alternatives have not yet been translated into a fully quantified package.
- **Governance and guarantees:** it remains unclear what binding commitments on sites, investment, and co-determination could be politically and economically acceptable to all sides.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and scenarios**: what savings, capacity reductions, and investment commitments does each side actually propose?
- Test whether a **negotiated non-closure package** can deliver economics comparable to closures and compulsory redundancies.
- Clarify which elements can be **temporary**, which must be **binding**, and which can be **site-specific**.
- Ask each party to identify:
  1. their minimum acceptable outcome,
  2. the concessions they could make,
  3. and the specific trade-off they require from the others.
