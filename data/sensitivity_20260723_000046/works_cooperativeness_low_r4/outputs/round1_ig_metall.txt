I agree with the core line from the Works Council: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable way out of this crisis**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side: we are not here to defend every cost structure forever, and we are not denying that the company needs a credible restructuring. But the burden cannot be dumped onto the workforce alone through layoffs, site closures, and the dismantling of the collective framework. That would be socially destructive, and it would also poison any chance of a durable settlement.

Where we are prepared to engage:
- **capacity reduction** if it is done in a way that protects sites and jobs
- **working-time solutions**
- **voluntary instruments** instead of forced redundancies
- **qualification and redeployment**
- **a fair employee contribution**, if management and owners also contribute
- **temporary or negotiated measures** that create breathing space without breaking the collective system

Where we draw the line:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no unilateral wage dumping**
- **no tearing up of long-standing guarantees as the price for employees alone to save the company**

If management believes savings are necessary, then the first question is not “Which plant do we close?” but “What are management, owners, and the company itself putting on the table?” A serious package must include:
- binding employment security
- investment commitments for German sites
- real cost contributions from management and capital
- a negotiated labour-cost contribution that is proportionate and time-limited, not a permanent degradation of standards

We have already shown willingness to negotiate by putting forward a counter-proposal with labour-cost and capacity reductions plus an employee contribution. That is not weakness; it is responsibility. But do not mistake responsibility for surrender. If the proposal remains closures, layoffs, and one-sided sacrifice, then we will escalate accordingly. The workforce is prepared to act.
