The expert analysis is useful, and it confirms two things we have said from the start:

1. **A non-closure package can produce real savings.**
2. **A closure-based package is more destructive, more risky, and not automatically superior once conflict and implementation costs are included.**

That matters. It means management cannot simply declare closures “necessary” and expect the workforce to accept that as economic truth.

## Where I agree with the expert
I agree that:
- non-closure measures may not fully match the maximum savings of closures in every scenario,
- voluntary exits, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, and temporary concessions can still deliver **substantial** savings,
- and the cost of conflict, strikes, and disruption must be counted, not ignored.

That reinforces our position: **the company must first exhaust a negotiated, non-destructive restructuring path.**

## Where I disagree with management, investors, and the family holding
You keep treating the higher headline savings of closure as if they automatically justify the social destruction. They do not.

The expert explicitly says:
- non-closure packages can often deliver **40–70% of closure-level savings**,
- and in some cases that may be enough to stabilize the business,
- while closure carries high execution risk and large one-time charges.

So the burden is on **management** to show, transparently and measure by measure, why the remaining gap cannot be closed through a fair package that avoids plant shutdowns and forced layoffs.

### What I reject
I reject:
- using closures as the first or default benchmark,
- forcing the workforce to “prove” survival before management has shown full comparative arithmetic,
- and presenting the company’s structural problem as if workers alone must absorb it.

## To management
Your argument is still too blunt.

Yes, there is structural overcapacity.  
No, that does **not** automatically mean closures are the only serious answer.

If you want us to discuss a serious restructuring, then bring forward:
- the exact savings gap,
- the exact savings from each non-closure measure,
- the cost of implementation and disruption,
- and the contribution from management, owners, and capital.

Until then, closure remains a threat, not a justified necessity.

And let me be clear: **if your package still relies on compulsory redundancies or plant closure, it remains unacceptable.**

## To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective wage framework.

The expert’s numbers strengthen our hand, but they also mean we must be disciplined. We should be ready to discuss a **real, quantified burden-sharing package**:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- reduced overtime,
- shift flexibility,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- capacity reduction through non-destructive means,
- and binding investment commitments.

But only if the burden is shared fairly and the concessions are **time-limited and reversible**.  
We are not signing up to permanent wage dumping.

## To the state government
Your insistence that closures should not be the default is right. But now you need to act like a blocking shareholder, not a commentator.

If management keeps pushing closures instead of proving necessity, then you should use your position to force the company back to a negotiated, site-preserving plan.

## To the family holding and investors
The expert’s comparison shows what you are still missing: **net economics are not just accounting savings**. They include execution risk, industrial conflict, political backlash, and long-term damage to skills and production capability.

A “cheaper” plan that triggers strikes and destabilizes the company may not be cheaper at all.

So if you want our cooperation, stop hiding behind the language of inevitability and start engaging with a real alternative:
- preserve sites,
- avoid forced layoffs,
- share the burden,
- and secure the future through investment and negotiated flexibility.

## Our position in one sentence
We are prepared to accept a fair, quantified contribution from the workforce, **but only in a package that protects jobs and sites, avoids compulsory redundancies, and preserves the collective bargaining framework**.

If management insists on closures or forced layoffs, then there is no social partnership here — only confrontation. And we are ready for that.
