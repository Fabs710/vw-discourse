We are now at the point where the lines must be drawn clearly.

## My response to the round 3/4 proposals

### To management
I **do not accept** framing the discussion as if “review trigger later” is an adequate substitute for **binding protection now**.

You are asking the workforce to accept concessions today on the promise that closures will only be considered later if targets are missed. That is not a secure settlement. That is a delayed threat.

We already know how these things work:
- first comes “temporary” flexibility,
- then “conditional” guarantees,
- then a review,
- then renewed pressure for closures and compulsory redundancies.

That is not a stable basis for trust, and trust is already damaged.

If you want a negotiated solution, then it must start from:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
- **a real co-determination role in implementation**

Without those, there is nothing for us to sign.

---

### To the expert / moderator proposals
I agree that numbers matter. I agree that differentiated site roles and transparent savings bridges are better than slogans.

But let me be equally clear:  
**I will not accept any model that leaves closures in reserve as an open fallback.**

Why? Because once closures remain “available,” management will keep using that threat to force concessions, and the whole process becomes coercive. That destroys the social partnership the company depends on.

So any review mechanism must be limited to:
- checking whether the negotiated non-closure package is being implemented properly,
- checking whether management and owners have delivered their contribution,
- and checking whether investment commitments have been honored.

It must **not** be a backdoor to reopen closure demands.

---

### To the state government
You have said you reject closures and compulsory redundancies. Good. Then act accordingly.

I want you to use your blocking position not just to say “closures are last resort,” but to insist on:
- a **site-preserving restructuring**
- **binding job protection**
- **no forced layoffs**
- **no unilateral closure mandate**
- and **transparent burden-sharing from management and owners**

If you are serious, help build a legally and politically robust framework that makes closures unnecessary and unacceptable.

---

### To the family holding
You say you do not want a blank cheque for management. Fine. Then use your influence to stop management from treating plant closures as the default answer.

If the controlling owner really wants long-term value, it should understand that:
- destroying trust with the workforce
- breaking job guarantees
- and threatening site closure

is not “discipline”; it is a direct attack on the industrial model that made this company strong.

You cannot talk about long-term value while normalizing measures that fracture the company’s internal social compact.

---

### To investors
You keep saying “risk-adjusted value preservation.” Then count the full risk.

A non-closure settlement with real burden sharing is not a sentimental option. It is a **lower-risk** option if it:
- avoids strikes,
- preserves production know-how,
- keeps labour relations stable,
- protects German supply chains,
- and maintains the company’s reputation as a reliable industrial employer.

Your model seems to overstate the certainty of closure savings and understate the costs of conflict and execution failure.

If you want credibility, show:
- how much value is destroyed by industrial action,
- how much by implementation delay,
- how much by knowledge loss,
- and how much by political backlash.

Until then, I do not accept the claim that closures are the economically superior answer.

## On the proposed “non-closure package”
We are prepared to discuss a **real** package, but it must meet our conditions.

### What we can discuss
- temporary wage moderation
- bonus restraint
- reduced overtime
- voluntary departures
- redeployment
- qualification and retraining
- shift flexibility
- product allocation adjustments
- targeted management overhead cuts
- reinvestment of owner returns into German operations

### What we will not discuss as a basis for agreement
- plant closures
- compulsory redundancies
- dismantling job-security guarantees
- permanent wage dumping
- using review clauses as a trapdoor to later shutdowns

## My answer to the moderator’s question: what savings level would be credible?
I will not commit to a euro figure as a precondition for surrendering plant security. That is the wrong order.

The correct order is:
1. **binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff commitment**
2. **transparent savings model**
3. **fair burden-sharing**
4. **co-determination in implementation**
5. **investment commitments through 2030**

Only then can we discuss the scale of contributions.

Because if you reverse the sequence, management gets the protections and the workforce gets uncertainty.
