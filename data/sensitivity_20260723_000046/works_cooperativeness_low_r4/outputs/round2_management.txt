I’ll respond directly.

The workforce side keeps repeating **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies”** as if that alone were a solution. It is not. It is a boundary statement, not a restructuring plan. And it does not answer the central question: **how do we remove enough structural cost and capacity to restore competitiveness?**

### Where I agree
I agree on three points:
- This must be **durable**, not cosmetic.
- We should exhaust **voluntary, negotiated instruments** first where they truly produce savings.
- A package needs to be **quantified** and tied to investment in future products.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the claim that closures are somehow illegitimate or unnecessary by definition.

If the German footprint remains too large for the market we actually have, then avoiding closures at all costs simply preserves an uncompetitive structure. That is not protecting the company; it is deferring the adjustment until it becomes harsher.

I also do **not** accept the idea that only employees are being asked to contribute. The company, management, and owners are all under pressure to secure the future. But employees cannot demand full site and job security through 2030 while rejecting the scale of cost reduction required to fund that very future.

### To the Works Council
You say the numbers do not “automatically justify closures.” That is true in the abstract. But you are not yet offering a package that delivers **equivalent structural savings**.

A promise of:
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and training

may help, but on its own it does **not** tell me how we close a material cost gap. If those measures do not produce comparable savings, then they are not an alternative to structural capacity reduction; they are only a delay.

I am willing to discuss non-closure options, but only if they are:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **large enough**
- and **credible in operational terms**

If they are not, then closures remain on the table.

### To the union
You keep calling closures “social destruction.” I understand the concern, but I reject the implication that any serious restructuring must be framed that way.

What is truly destructive is:
- an uncompetitive cost base,
- declining margins,
- shrinking investment capacity,
- and a slow erosion of the company’s industrial position.

That is exactly what happens if we protect every existing structure and refuse to make hard decisions now.

If you want to avoid forced redundancies, then help produce a package that materially reduces labour costs and capacity in a way the business can actually carry. I will not sign up to permanent wage dumping either, but I do need a real contribution that is not just temporary optics.

### To the state government
I appreciate your insistence on social stability and the regional role of the company. But “no closures as a first choice” is not enough if the alternative cannot close the economics.

If you want my support — and the support of the shareholder side more broadly — then you need to back a package that is **credible on cost and capacity**, not simply politically comfortable.

### To the family holding
I agree with your core point: we need a serious industrial plan, not theatrics. But the plan must not be trapped by the assumption that every site and every guarantee can remain untouched.

If the economics require structural reductions, then the company must be allowed to make them. Otherwise we will all end up defending a weaker business.

### What I am looking for
I am prepared to back a negotiated settlement if it contains:
- **real capacity reduction**
- **a genuine labour-cost contribution**
- **binding investment commitments**
- **measurable improvement in return on sales**
- **durable savings, not temporary deferrals**
- preference for voluntary measures where they are sufficient

What I cannot accept:
- a “no-closure” settlement that leaves the cost base basically unchanged
- absolute job and site guarantees that prevent necessary adjustment
- a package that shifts the burden entirely away from the workforce
- a cosmetic compromise that postpones the crisis

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then show me an alternative with the same economic effect. If not, we should stop pretending that the numbers are compatible with a blanket veto.
