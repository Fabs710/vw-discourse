### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the company needs a **durable, quantified restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad support for **negotiated, non-destructive tools** where effective: voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, internal transfers, and temporary employee contributions.
- There is also shared recognition that **prolonged conflict would be value-destructive** and should be avoided if possible.
- Most parties agree that any solution must include **management/owner contribution**, **investment commitments**, and a **clear savings/margin-recovery logic**.

### 2) Core disagreements
- The central divide remains over **plant closures in Germany**:
  - Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures as a preferred path and oppose compulsory redundancies.
  - Management and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed to achieve sufficient structural savings.
- There is no agreement on whether **“no closures / no compulsory redundancies”** is compatible with the required economic adjustment.
- There is no shared view on whether **non-destructive measures can deliver savings equivalent to closures/capacity cuts**.
- The role of the state shareholder remains contested: whether it should primarily **block closures** or **enable hard restructuring**.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Job and site security vs. cost/capacity reduction**: how much employment and footprint protection can be maintained without undermining competitiveness?
- **Voluntary measures vs. enforceable structural savings**: can negotiated tools close the gap, and if so, by how much?
- **Temporary burden sharing vs. permanent change**: what employee concessions are time-limited, and what structural changes are unavoidable?
- **Investment commitments vs. immediate savings**: what future investment is conditional on which current sacrifices?
- **Governance stability vs. decisive restructuring**: how to preserve social partnership and co-determination while still making credible economic adjustments?

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **side-by-side quantified options**:
- one package that **avoids closures/redundancies** and shows exactly how much savings it delivers,
- one package that includes **possible structural reductions** and shows why they are necessary.

For each option, specify:
- expected savings by measure,
- timing,
- impact on jobs/sites,
- owner/management contributions,
- investment commitments,
- and risks if the package is not implemented.

The next round should focus on **comparability of economics**, not repeating the same red lines.
