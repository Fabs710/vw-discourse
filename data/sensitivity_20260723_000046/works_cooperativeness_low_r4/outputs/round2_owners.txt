I will be equally direct.

### What I agree with
I agree with the central point from management, investors, and the state: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a symbolic one**. The current cost and capacity position is not competitive enough, and if we protect the status quo regardless of economics, we will damage the enterprise over time.

I also agree with the employee side on one important point: **a prolonged destructive conflict would be value-destructive**. A strike spiral, reputational damage, and operational paralysis help no one, including the owners.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that a blanket refusal of closures or compulsory redundancies is, by itself, a viable long-term strategy.

The works council and union are defending an understandable principle, but a principle is not a restructuring plan. If the savings package does not materially and durably improve the cost base, then it is not enough. That is not hostility to labor; it is basic stewardship.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept management’s implied logic that closures are the first or only serious answer. If the restructuring is framed as “close a plant or the plan fails,” then it is too narrow and too destructive before alternatives are fully tested.

### My position
From the controlling family’s perspective, the acceptable outcome is:

- **material, quantified, durable savings**
- **a credible path back to target margins**
- **stability in governance and industrial relations**
- **preference for negotiated, non-destructive measures first**
- **structural capacity adjustment only if the economics truly require it**

That means I am open to:
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary departures
- internal redeployment
- qualification and retraining
- temporary employee contributions
- site-specific capacity reductions

But I will not support:
- a deal that leaves the German cost base fundamentally uncompetitive
- a settlement that preserves all guarantees while delivering only marginal savings
- endless delay disguised as social partnership
- a conflict that erodes the company’s value and brand

### To the works council and union
If you want me to oppose closures, then you need to present something more than a red line. You need a package with numbers that can genuinely replace the economics of closure or deep capacity reduction.

I am not asking for sacrifice for its own sake. I am asking for a restructuring that actually works. If the alternative cannot close the gap, then your position is not protecting jobs; it is postponing a larger cut later.

### To management
You are right to insist on seriousness, but I will not give you a blank cheque for plant closure. You must show:
- the precise savings required,
- the savings from each measure,
- the timeline to margin recovery,
- the investment commitments afterward,
- and the industrial rationale for any closure you still believe is unavoidable.

If you cannot prove that non-destructive measures are insufficient, then closure is not yet justified.

### To the state government
I welcome your insistence on regional stability and investment. That matters. But stability cannot mean freezing the industrial model in place. If you want my support, help shape a settlement that protects a substantial German footprint while restoring competitiveness. A veto against any hard decision would eventually weaken the company and the region together.

### Bottom line
I will support a negotiated settlement only if it is **credible in economics and stable in governance**. I am not aligned with a closure-first approach, but I am also not prepared to endorse a no-change outcome.

If the parties can produce a package with real, durable savings and a serious investment path, I will back it. If not, then the company will remain trapped between conflict and decline — and I will not support that either.
