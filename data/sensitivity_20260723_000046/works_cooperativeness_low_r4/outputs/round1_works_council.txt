As Works Council, our position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the line. The company’s crisis cannot be solved by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and tearing up the historic job-security framework. Management did not come to us with a fair partnership proposal; it came with a threat. We reject a fait accompli.

Our priorities are:

- Keep every German plant open
- Secure all jobs, especially through 2030
- Preserve co-determination and the role of the workforce in shaping the restructuring
- Use alternatives that avoid closures and forced layoffs: capacity adjustment, working-time solutions, voluntary measures, qualification, internal redeployment, and fair negotiated contributions
- Ensure any savings burden is shared fairly, not pushed one-sidedly onto employees

If management wants a real solution, it must put binding guarantees on the table:
- No plant closures
- No compulsory redundancies
- Site and employment guarantees through 2030
- Binding investment commitments for the German sites
- Full involvement of employee representatives in designing the restructuring

We are prepared to discuss change, but only on the basis of security, fairness, and co-determination. If the proposal relies on closures, layoffs, or breaking past commitments, then we will resist it decisively.
