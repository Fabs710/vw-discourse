As the regional state government and a blocking shareholder, I want to be very clear: **I do not support a restructuring that solves the crisis by gutting German sites, hollowing out jobs, or sidelining co-determination.** That is not a responsible path for the company, and it is not acceptable for the region.

I do agree on one point with management: **the company needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.** The competitive pressure is real, and the cost problem cannot be ignored. But durability does not mean closure as the first instrument. It means a restructuring that preserves the company as a long-term industrial anchor in Germany.

To the Works Council and the union: I share your core concern about **plant closures and compulsory redundancies**. Those would cause lasting damage to the regional economy and destroy trust. I also support the principle that the burden must be shared fairly, not placed one-sidedly on employees. At the same time, I do not want to turn this into a blocking posture that prevents any adjustment at all. If there is a negotiated package that protects sites, avoids forced redundancies, and secures real investment, I am prepared to support it.

To management: if you are asking for state support, political backing, and shareholder approval, then you need to bring forward a restructuring that is **credible without being destructive**. That means:

- **no automatic move to plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies as the default route**
- **a serious industrial plan for capacity adjustment through alternatives first**
- **binding investment commitments for German sites**
- **a fair but temporary employee contribution, negotiated in social partnership**
- **management and capital also contributing visibly to the solution**

I am not persuaded by the argument that closure is the only way to achieve competitiveness. That may be the easiest accounting answer, but it is not necessarily the best strategic answer. A company of this importance should be able to pursue:
- working-time adjustments
- voluntary departure programs
- redeployment and qualification
- product and investment commitments tied to restructuring
- targeted capacity reductions where truly unavoidable, but only after alternatives are exhausted

What I cannot support is a deal that:
- removes jobs on a large scale in the state
- weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor
- marginalizes the state’s co-determination and veto role
- treats employee representatives as an obstacle rather than a governing partner

So my position is this: **I am open to a negotiated restructuring, but only if it protects sites, preserves a substantial employment base, and keeps the company rooted in the region.** If management wants my vote and my political backing, it must come back with a package that proves it can restore competitiveness without dismantling the company’s social and regional foundations.

In short: **I will back reform. I will not back disinvestment disguised as restructuring.**
