I will be just as clear as the Works Council: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. If management, the family holding, or investors continue to treat those as the default answer, then you are not proposing a restructuring in social partnership — you are proposing to shift the crisis cost onto the workforce.

### To management
You ask for “quantified, durable” savings. Fine. We are not refusing numbers. We are refusing a one-sided interpretation of the numbers.

What I agree with:
- The company does need a durable solution.
- Voluntary departures, redeployment, qualification, working-time adjustments, and capacity reduction can all be part of that.
- The package must be financially credible.

What I reject:
- Using “credibility” as code for closures and forced layoffs.
- Pretending that plant shutdowns are the only serious way to achieve savings.
- Demanding employee sacrifice while management and capital contributions remain vague or symbolic.

If you want a real deal, then show us:
- what **management** is giving up,
- what **owners** are contributing,
- what **investment commitments** are binding,
- and what the **employee contribution** is in a way that is fair, time-limited, and reversible.

A valid package can include:
- temporary wage moderation or deferral,
- bonus reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- capacity reductions through non-destructive measures.

But I will not accept:
- permanent wage dumping,
- dismantling the collective-bargaining framework,
- or layoffs disguised as “structural adjustment.”

### To the family holding
You say we need “real, durable cost and capacity relief.” I agree. But if your only test of seriousness is whether we accept closures, then you are ignoring the company’s social model and the legal reality of co-determination.

A durable solution must also be **durable in industrial relations**. A package imposed through threats, strikes, and broken trust will destroy value just as surely as an uncompetitive cost base does.

If you want stability, then support a solution that:
- protects sites,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- shares the burden,
- and preserves the collective wage framework.

### To investors
“Equivalent economics” is not a magic phrase that makes closures acceptable. If alternatives can deliver real savings, then the burden of proof is on those demanding closures to show that social destruction is necessary — not on workers to justify their own survival.

### To the state government
We welcome your clear opposition to closures as a first choice. But let’s be honest: that is not enough if management escalates. The state must not become a passive witness while a company of this importance is pushed toward deindustrialisation.

### Our position, plainly
We are prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package**, but only under these conditions:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no unilateral dismantling of the collective wage agreement**
- **fair burden sharing from management, owners, and employees**
- **binding job and site security**
- **investment commitments**
- **real industrial alternatives instead of closure theatre**

If the other side insists that workers must pay most of the price while management keeps its options and owners keep theirs, then we will not sign that deal. We will escalate — including industrial action — because that is exactly what our members expect when social partnership is replaced by pressure politics.

If you want to avoid that, come back with a quantified package that proves:
1. the company is contributing materially,
2. the owners are contributing materially,
3. jobs and sites are secured,
4. and employee concessions remain proportionate and temporary.

Otherwise, this confrontation will continue.
