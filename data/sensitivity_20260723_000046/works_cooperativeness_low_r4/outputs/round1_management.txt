I hear both of you, but let me be equally clear: **a purely defensive position is not sufficient to solve this crisis**.

We are not in a normal bargaining round. We have a shrinking European market, brutal competition, and a cost base in Germany that is structurally too high. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target, and without a durable reset we will not have the funds to invest in the products and technology we need through 2030. That is the reality.

So where I agree:
- Yes, this must be a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one.
- Yes, **voluntary measures, redeployment, qualification, and working-time adjustments** should be part of the toolbox.
- Yes, any package must be negotiated and must preserve the company’s long-term ability to invest.

Where I disagree, firmly:
- **No plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies** as absolute red lines is not a credible response to the scale of the problem.
- If the German footprint remains too large and too expensive, then we cannot rule out **structural capacity reductions**, including closures, where necessary.
- A promise of “no change until 2030” may feel secure, but if it leaves the cost base uncompetitive, it simply postpones the pain and makes the eventual adjustment worse.

I also need to be direct on labour costs: we need a **genuine employee contribution**. That means more than symbolic savings. It has to be measurable, durable, and large enough to materially improve competitiveness. If there is no serious contribution on the cost side, then the business case does not work.

What I am prepared to discuss:
- a package that combines **capacity reduction**, **labor-cost measures**, and **investment commitments**
- **site-specific solutions** where they deliver real savings without undermining the whole structure
- **voluntary departures**, internal transfers, and retraining where they help reduce headcount without destabilizing operations
- a negotiated framework that gives the company room to invest in future products

What I am not prepared to accept:
- a deal that preserves every structure and every guarantee while only adding temporary, marginal savings
- a settlement that blocks meaningful capacity adjustment but still expects management to restore margins
- a situation where the workforce demands full security while the company carries all the adjustment burden

So let me put the question back to you: **what concrete, quantified savings will you put on the table that match the scale of the crisis?** If you want to avoid closures, then you need to show me an alternative that delivers the same structural effect on cost and capacity. Otherwise, the discussion about closures remains on the table.
