From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the test is simple: **does the plan credibly restore competitive margins on a durable basis?** If yes, we can support a negotiated solution. If not, we will not back a cosmetic settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current German cost structure and capacity look misaligned with market reality.
- **The union, works council, state, and family are right** that a destructive conflict and prolonged strikes would be value-destructive.
- We also agree that this must be **durable**, not a temporary accounting fix.

### Where we disagree
#### To the Works Council and the union
Your insistence on **“no plant closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** is understandable politically, but it is not automatically investable if it prevents the scale of adjustment needed.

If the alternative package cannot deliver:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- materially lower labor costs,
- and a clear path back to target margins,

then it is not a real solution, just a delay. Dispersed investors do **not** support a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive while calling it social peace.

That said, we are not demanding closures as an ideological goal. We are demanding **equivalent economics**. If you can show a negotiated package with voluntary departures, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and durable cost relief that is genuinely comparable in impact, we will take it seriously.

#### To management
We will not support a blunt restructuring narrative that relies on closures and headcount cuts without proving:
- the size of the gap,
- the savings from each measure,
- the operational consequences,
- and the investment case afterward.

If management wants investor support, it needs to present a **credible quantified bridge to competitive margins**, not just a threat. A plan that triggers labor conflict and damages execution would destroy value. We need discipline, not theatrics.

#### To the regional state and family owners
We appreciate the need for governance stability, but blocking any meaningful adjustment would be just as damaging as an indiscriminate closure plan. Your role should be to force a **credible compromise with real economics**, not to protect legacy structures that keep returns below cost of capital.

### Our position
We are prepared to support a restructuring package if it includes:
- **durable, quantified cost reductions**
- **real capacity adjustments**
- **an enforceable path to return on sales target**
- **investment commitments tied to restructuring**
- **preference for negotiated, voluntary measures over conflict**
- **no cosmetic deal that merely postpones the problem**

What we will not support:
- a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base,
- a promise of full preservation with only marginal savings,
- or an open-ended labor conflict that erodes margins further.

### Bottom line
We are not aligned with either extreme. We are aligned with **financial reality**. If the alternative to closures is credible and materially equivalent, fine. If not, then management must be allowed to make the structural changes required to protect long-term equity value.
