We agree with the expert analysis on the central point: **a non-closure package can generate real savings, but it must be large, durable, and credible enough to materially improve margins**. We are not here to defend plant closures as an end in themselves. We are here to defend capital value.

## Where we agree
- **The company needs a durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic deal.
- **Prolonged conflict is value-destructive** and should be avoided.
- **Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, qualification, and temporary employee contributions** should be used where they deliver real savings.
- **Investment commitments matter**, but only if they are matched by a credible cost reset.
- We agree that **management and owners must contribute visibly**; this cannot be framed as a workforce-only correction.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and the union
Your red lines are understandable, but **“no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are not investable by themselves**.

The expert’s comparison does not support your claim that a non-closure package is automatically sufficient. It shows:
- non-closure measures may deliver **roughly 40–70% of closure-level savings**,
- which can be meaningful,
- but not necessarily enough if the structural gap is large.

That is the key issue. We will **not** support a settlement that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving the cost base structurally uncompetitive. That would be a cosmetic compromise, and we have already said that is unacceptable.

If you want our support for a non-closure path, it must be translated into a **quantified package** showing:
- how much savings come from wage moderation,
- how much from shift and working-time changes,
- how much from voluntary exits and attrition,
- how much from overhead reduction,
- and how the package restores return on sales.

Without that, your proposal is not a plan; it is a boundary statement.

### To management
We also do **not** give you a blank cheque for closures. The expert analysis does not justify closure as the default answer. It says closures can deliver larger savings, but also come with:
- high transition risk,
- industrial conflict,
- execution uncertainty,
- and major one-time charges.

So management still has to prove:
- the size of the gap,
- the savings from each measure,
- the timeline to margin recovery,
- and why the remaining gap cannot be closed with negotiated tools.

A closure-first narrative without this proof is not credible.

### To the regional state government and family holding
We understand the political and governance constraints, but **blocking hard choices is not the same as creating a viable compromise**.

If the state or the family wants to prevent closures, then they must support a package that actually closes the economics. That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- real cost reduction,
- real flexibility,
- and enforceable implementation.

We will not support a solution that simply preserves the current structure and hopes for better demand or better execution later.

## Our position
We can support a settlement if it includes:
- **durable, quantified cost reductions**
- **real capacity adjustment**
- **binding investment commitments**
- **visible management and owner contribution**
- **a credible path back to competitive margins**
- **preference for negotiated measures over conflict**

What we cannot support:
- a settlement that leaves the German cost base fundamentally unchanged
- a no-closure outcome with only symbolic savings
- a prolonged strike that destroys margins further
- or a compromise that avoids hard decisions without replacing them with equivalent economics

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated non-closure package **only if it is economically equivalent in substance**. If it is not, then management must retain the ability to make structural adjustments necessary to restore competitiveness.

That is our standard. Not fairness rhetoric, not social symbolism — **risk-adjusted value preservation**.
