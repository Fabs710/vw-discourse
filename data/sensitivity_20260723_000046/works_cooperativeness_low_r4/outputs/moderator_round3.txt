## Core impasse

The central blockage is now narrow and clear:

1. **Employee-side actors (Works Council, union)** hold a hard red line against **plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies**.
2. **Management, investors, and the family holding** consider **structural footprint reduction** potentially necessary if non-closure measures do not fully close the savings gap.
3. **The state government** rejects closures and forced layoffs, but has not yet proposed a quantified substitute that all capital-side actors would deem economically sufficient.

So the impasse is not on the need for restructuring; it is on **whether a non-closure package can be made quantitatively sufficient and durably enforceable**, or whether closures remain available if it cannot.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified non-closure package with a hard review trigger
A time-limited agreement could be structured as follows:
- no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- a quantified workforce contribution package:
  - wage moderation,
  - bonus restraint,
  - overtime reduction,
  - shift flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment and training,
- management and owners commit to visible cost and investment contributions,
- an agreed **review trigger** after 12–18 months based on measurable metrics:
  - cost reduction achieved,
  - utilization improvement,
  - margin recovery,
  - implementation of redeployment/qualification,
  - demand trajectory.

If targets are missed, parties would reopen negotiations with pre-agreed fallback options, rather than immediately resorting to closures.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-preserving restructuring with differentiated plant roles
Instead of defending every site equally, the parties could negotiate:
- keeping all German sites open,
- but assigning each site a **clearly differentiated role**:
  - core production,
  - niche production,
  - assembly,
  - pilot/launch support,
  - component or specialty lines,
- capacity reduction through product allocation and shift changes,
- targeted investment only in sites with confirmed future model allocation,
- voluntary exits and redeployment concentrated where overcapacity is highest.

This would preserve the network while still delivering real structural change.

---

### Proposal 3: Social compact with deeper contributions from all sides
A compromise could combine:
- time-limited employee concessions,
- binding management overhead cuts,
- owner contribution through reduced distributions or reinvestment,
- investment commitments for German plants,
- and a jointly monitored savings bridge.

To address investor concerns, the package would need:
- a clear annual savings target,
- transparent reporting,
- independent verification,
- and a defined path to restore competitiveness without immediate closures.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
What specific savings level, in euros and in percent of German cost base, would you consider sufficient for a non-closure agreement to be credible and durable?

### To the Trade Union
Would you accept a time-limited wage and flexibility package if it were tied to binding investment commitments and independent verification of savings?

### To Management
What exact residual savings gap remains after all non-closure measures are applied, and which of those measures have you already modeled line by line?

### To the Regional State Government
If you reject closures and compulsory redundancies, what enforceable alternative package are you prepared to help secure that meets the company’s competitiveness target?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you commit to using your governance influence to support a quantified non-closure settlement if the workforce accepts a material, time-limited contribution?

### To the Institutional Investors
What level of risk-adjusted savings and implementation certainty would make a non-closure restructuring investable for you?
