We are aligned with one objective only: **a credible, durable return to competitive margins**. Everything else is secondary.

### Where we agree
- The company does need a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one.
- We agree that **prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value**.
- We agree that **voluntary measures, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and negotiated employee contributions** should be used where they can deliver real savings.
- We also agree that any package must be **quantified** and tied to a clear investment path.

### Where we disagree
#### To the Works Council and the union
Your insistence on **“no plant closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** as absolute lines is not investable unless you can show equivalent economics.

We do **not** support a settlement that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving the cost base structurally uncompetitive. That would be a cosmetic deal, and we will not back it.

If you want our support for a non-closure package, it must deliver:
- **material capacity reduction**
- **durable labor-cost reduction**
- **a credible path back to return-on-sales target**
- **operational flexibility**
- **measurable, enforceable savings**

Without that, your proposal is just delay.

#### To management
We are not giving you a blank cheque for closures either. A closure-first narrative is not enough. You need to prove:
- the size of the gap,
- the savings from each measure,
- the operational consequences,
- and the margin-recovery path afterward.

If closures are genuinely necessary, show the numbers. If non-destructive alternatives can do the job, use them. What we will not support is management theatre or a blunt restructuring that triggers a strike without a convincing economic case.

#### To the regional state and family owners
We appreciate the emphasis on governance stability and regional anchoring, but blocking all hard choices is not a strategy. You should be forcing a **credible compromise with real economics**, not protecting legacy structures that keep returns below cost of capital.

### Our position
We can support a package if it includes:
- **durable, quantified cost reductions**
- **real capacity adjustment**
- **binding investment commitments**
- **a clear return to competitive margins**
- **preference for negotiated measures over conflict**

What we cannot support:
- a settlement that leaves the German cost base fundamentally unchanged
- a promise of total site/job preservation with only marginal savings
- a prolonged strike that erodes margins further
- or a deal that avoids hard decisions without replacing them with equivalent economics

### Bottom line
We are not here to choose between closures and social peace as slogans. We are here to protect capital value. If a negotiated non-closure package is truly equivalent in financial effect, fine. If not, then management must be allowed to make the structural adjustments necessary to restore competitiveness.
