I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable competitiveness reset, not a cosmetic patch.** The cost gap, the weak market, and the investment demands of the transition are real. If we do nothing meaningful, we will all end up with a weaker company and fewer options later.

Where I disagree is the idea that the only credible path is to start from plant closures or an open-ended rejection of structural change.

### To the Works Council and the union
I understand and respect the insistence on co-determination, social peace, and avoiding unnecessary destruction of jobs. But I cannot support a line that effectively says **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no fundamental change”** regardless of the numbers.

That is not a strategy; it is a veto without an alternative of equivalent effect.

If the German footprint is structurally too large relative to demand, then the burden of adjustment cannot be pushed entirely onto paper savings or temporary working-time tweaks. The company needs **real, durable cost and capacity relief**. I am open to alternatives, but they must be:
- quantified,
- credible,
- durable,
- and sufficient to restore competitiveness.

If they are not, then refusing to discuss structural reductions is not protecting the company; it is risking a larger erosion later.

### To management
I am not giving you a blank cheque either.

If you want support from the controlling family, you need to demonstrate that this is a **serious industrial plan**, not simply a cost-cutting exercise dressed up as strategy. You need to show:
- exactly what savings are required,
- what each measure contributes,
- what the investment path is,
- and how the plan preserves long-term value rather than triggering a destructive labour conflict.

I will not back a move that permanently damages the enterprise, destabilises governance, or creates an endless confrontation with the workforce. A restructuring that burns trust and produces operational paralysis is value-destructive.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your emphasis on the company’s role as a regional anchor. That matters. But that cannot mean freezing the company in place. If the operating model is uncompetitive, then protecting every existing structure simply delays the problem and increases the eventual damage.

Your support is important, but it should be used to secure a **workable settlement**, not to block any meaningful adjustment. If there is a package that protects a substantial German industrial base while restoring margins, that is the direction we should pursue.

### My position
From the family holding’s perspective, the acceptable outcome is not “closures at any cost,” and it is not “no change at any cost.”

What I can support is:
- a **credible, quantified restructuring**
- **durable savings** that materially improve margins
- **preferably achieved through negotiated and phased measures**
- with **voluntary instruments, redeployment, qualification, and working-time solutions** used first
- but with **structural capacity reductions remaining on the table** if needed to make the numbers work
- alongside **binding investment commitments** and a realistic path for future products

What I cannot support is:
- a settlement that leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged,
- a promise of total job and site security that ignores the business reality,
- or a prolonged conflict that erodes the company’s value and reputation.

So my message is this: bring forward a package that is **credible in economics and stable in governance**. If the employee side can show savings that are truly comparable in effect to closures and deep cuts, I will take that seriously. But if the answer is only “not this, not that,” then the company will not be rescued; it will be trapped.
