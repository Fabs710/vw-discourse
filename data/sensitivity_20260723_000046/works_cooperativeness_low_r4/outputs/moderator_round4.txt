### Core impasse
The central blockage is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **the sequence and enforceability of concessions**:

- **Labor-side stakeholders** require **binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff protection now**.
- **Management and capital-side stakeholders** require **credible, quantified structural savings now**, and do not accept a permanent exclusion of closures as a governing principle.
- The **state government** wants restructuring without closures or forced redundancies, but also wants to remain a credible blocking actor.
- The **review mechanism** is the immediate flashpoint: one side sees it as a neutral monitoring tool; the other sees it as a delayed reopening of closure threats.

So the impasse is:
> **How to secure enough savings and capacity adjustment without leaving plant closure as an open fallback threat, while still giving capital-side actors enough confidence that the deal is economically sufficient.**

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise tracks

## Proposal 1: Binding non-closure phase with quantified review gate
A first compromise could be a **time-limited non-closure phase** through 2030, paired with a tightly defined review gate.

**Key elements**
- No plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies during the initial period.
- Workforce contributes through:
  - temporary wage moderation,
  - bonus restraint,
  - overtime reduction,
  - shift flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment and qualification.
- Management and owners contribute through:
  - overhead cuts,
  - reduced distributions,
  - reinvestment commitments,
  - site-specific productivity measures.
- A joint review after a defined period assesses only:
  - whether agreed measures were implemented,
  - whether management/owners delivered,
  - whether investment commitments were honored,
  - whether the savings bridge is on track.

**Safeguard**
- The review would **not** automatically reopen closure demands.
- Any later structural change would require a **new negotiation**, not a trapdoor clause.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- Labor gains real protection now.
- Management gains measurable performance checkpoints.
- Investors get a monitoring framework tied to economics.

---

## Proposal 2: Site-preserving restructuring with conditional escalation only after shared burden is exhausted
A second compromise could be a **site-preserving default**, but with a clearly staged escalation ladder if the savings target is not met.

**Key elements**
1. Stage 1: immediate non-closure package.
2. Stage 2: additional management/owner burden if targets are missed.
3. Stage 3: only if the agreed burden-sharing measures are objectively exhausted, an extraordinary renegotiation is triggered.

**Safeguard**
- The escalation would not automatically permit closures.
- It would instead require:
  - transparent financial proof,
  - joint review,
  - and a fresh bargaining round with state involvement.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- It gives management a path to insist on adequacy.
- It gives labor and the state a firewall against an automatic closure fallback.
- It may be acceptable to investors if the metrics are transparent and enforceable.

---

## Proposal 3: Differentiated site strategy with binding job and investment guarantees
A third compromise could preserve jobs and prevent abrupt shutdowns by assigning **different roles to different sites** rather than treating all sites identically.

**Key elements**
- No outright closure commitment in the short term, but:
  - one or more sites receive protected core production roles,
  - others receive reconfigured mandates, product shifts, or specialization,
  - capacity reduction is achieved through internal reallocation rather than shutdown.
- Binding investment commitments and retraining plans accompany each site role.
- Any future change in site status requires co-determination and renewed negotiation.

**Safeguard**
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral closure decision during the agreement period.
- A jointly governed implementation committee oversees the site map.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- It addresses overcapacity without immediate closures.
- It gives management a structural reset.
- It gives labor and the state something concrete to defend as site-preserving.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

## To management
**What exact residual savings gap remains after your own best-case non-closure package, and which measures are you willing to put in writing as your contribution before asking for any structural fallback?**

## To the works council
**If closures remain off the table, what quantified combination of wage moderation, working-time changes, and productivity measures can you commit to as a credible savings bridge through 2030?**

## To the industrial union
**What is the maximum duration and scope of employee contribution you would accept if it is matched by binding management and owner contributions and protected by enforceable no-forced-layoff terms?**

## To the regional state government
**Will you support a binding non-closure framework only if it includes measurable savings, or do you require an explicit prohibition on closures regardless of the quantified gap?**

## To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to commit visible owner-side contributions—such as reinvestment, reduced distributions, or capital support—if those materially improve the case for a site-preserving settlement?**

## To institutional investors
**What minimum margin recovery and implementation certainty would make a negotiated non-closure package acceptable to you, even if it delivers less gross savings than closures on paper?**
