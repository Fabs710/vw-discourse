I will respond plainly.

## My position
I **do not support plant closures in Germany** and I **do not support compulsory redundancies**.  
That remains my position after hearing the expert comparison.

The expert analysis does **not** change the political and industrial reality for me:
- closures would damage the regional economy,
- they would undermine trust in social partnership,
- they would weaken the company’s role as a regional anchor,
- and they would risk escalating conflict in a way that destroys more value than it creates.

As a blocking shareholder under the special statute, I will not endorse a restructuring built on that foundation.

---

## Where I agree with others

### With management
I agree that:
- the company has a real competitiveness problem,
- the restructuring must be durable and quantified,
- and cosmetic measures are not enough.

I also agree that non-closure measures alone must be taken seriously **as a real operating plan**, not as a slogan.

### With the family holding and investors
I agree that the company cannot be frozen in place and that the current cost base must come down materially.

### With the works council and union
I agree that:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- shift adjustments,
- and temporary employee contributions

must be central to the package.

And I agree that management and owners must contribute visibly. This cannot be a workforce-only correction.

---

## Where I disagree

### To management
Your latest argument still tries to turn the expert’s “closure can save more” into “therefore closures are necessary.” That is **not proved**.

The expert said something more precise:
- non-closure restructuring can deliver **40–70% of closure-level savings**,
- and conflict/transition costs can be very large.

That is exactly why I am **not** willing to start from closure.  
If a negotiated package can achieve most of the adjustment with far less social damage, then closure is not the default answer.

You have not shown that every non-closure alternative is exhausted or that the remaining gap is so large that destruction of sites is unavoidable. Until you do, I will not support closures.

### To the family holding and investors
You keep invoking “equivalent economics.” I hear that. But you are still framing equivalence too narrowly.

For me, economics includes:
- implementation risk,
- industrial peace,
- supply-chain stability,
- regional employment,
- political legitimacy,
- and the long-term ability of the company to operate as an industrial anchor.

A closure-based package may look stronger on a spreadsheet. It may also be weaker once you count strikes, disruption, knowledge loss, and the political damage to the company’s license to operate.

So no, I do **not** accept that closure is justified simply because it produces higher headline savings.

### To the works council and union
I support your red line against closures and compulsory redundancies, but I also need to be honest: saying “no” is not enough by itself.

If you want me to defend the no-closure path in the supervisory board and politically, then I need a package that is not just principled, but credible in numbers.

That means I want to see:
- quantified labor-cost moderation,
- savings from working-time changes,
- lower overtime and agency use,
- redeployment across the network,
- voluntary exit volumes,
- overhead reduction,
- and a realistic contribution from management and owners.

Not because I want to weaken your position, but because I need something I can defend against the argument that the state is merely blocking hard choices.

---

## What I can support
I can support a negotiated package if it contains all of the following:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
4. **Substantial but time-limited employee contribution**
5. **Visible management and owner contribution**
6. **Capacity reduction through non-destructive measures**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
8. **A credible path back to sustainable margins**

That is the line. Anything outside it is not acceptable to me.

---

## What I want on the table next
I want the parties to stop speaking in abstractions and put forward a real site-preserving package with numbers:

- How much savings come from working-time flexibility?
- How much from reduced overtime and agency labor?
- How much from voluntary exits and natural attrition?
- How much from management overhead cuts?
- What investment is committed to German plants, and when?
- What concrete margin improvement is expected?

If management cannot show that the gap remains after these measures, then closure is not justified.

If management can show a residual gap, then I still need to know why that gap cannot be closed through a stronger burden-sharing package that avoids plant shutdowns.

---

## My bottom line
I am **open to a tough negotiated restructuring**.  
I am **not open to plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

I will use my blocking position if needed to prevent a solution that guts employment in the state or sidelines the co-determination role that protects it.

If the parties want a settlement, bring me a **quantified non-closure plan** with:
- real savings,
- real investment,
- real burden sharing,
- and real protection of sites and jobs.

That is the only path I will back.
