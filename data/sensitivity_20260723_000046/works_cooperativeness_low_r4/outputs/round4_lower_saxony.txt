I will be clear.

## My position
I **do not support plant closures in Germany** and I **do not support compulsory redundancies**.  
I also **will not accept any settlement that weakens co-determination or the state’s veto role**. Those are not bargaining chips for me; they are part of the governance balance that protects the region, the workforce, and the company’s long-term stability.

At the same time, I do **not** support doing nothing. The company needs a real restructuring, not a symbolic one.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that:
- the company has a **serious competitiveness problem**,
- the restructuring must be **durable and quantified**,
- and cosmetic measures will not be enough.

I also agree that the workforce contribution must be **material**, not merely rhetorical. If the package only shifts pain into the future, it will fail.

### With the works council and union
I agree that:
- **voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and internal transfers** should be the first tools used,
- conflict would destroy value,
- and any solution must be **socially sustainable**.

### With the family holding and investors
I agree that the company cannot preserve every historic structure unchanged. The footprint and cost base must come down.

---

## Where I disagree

### On closures
Management, investors, and the family are still speaking as if closures are the default proof of seriousness. They are not.

The expert comparison did **not** show that closures are unavoidable. It showed:
- non-closure restructuring can deliver a **substantial share** of the savings,
- closure carries **high execution risk**,
- and conflict costs can be very large.

That means closures are not something I will endorse simply because they look stronger in a spreadsheet.

### On “review triggers” as a solution
I do **not** accept a model where the company gets concessions now and closures remain as an open threat later. That is not a stable compromise; it is leverage dressed up as flexibility.

A review can measure:
- whether the agreed measures were implemented,
- whether management and owner contributions were delivered,
- whether investment commitments were honored.

It must **not** become a backdoor for reopening plant-closure demands.

### On the “equivalent economics” argument
I understand the demand for quantified savings. But the argument is still too narrow when it ignores:
- strike risk,
- implementation delay,
- loss of know-how,
- supplier disruption,
- political backlash,
- and long-term damage to the company’s regional legitimacy.

Those are real costs. A closure-based package is not automatically superior just because the headline savings are larger.

---

## What I can support
I can support a **tough negotiated restructuring** if it contains all of the following:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site protection**
4. **A time-limited employee contribution**
5. **Visible management and owner contribution**
6. **Real capacity reduction through non-destructive means**
7. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
8. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is the line.

---

## What I want on the table
I want a **quantified non-closure package**, not vague claims. Specifically:
- savings from working-time flexibility,
- savings from reduced overtime and agency labor,
- savings from voluntary exits and attrition,
- savings from overhead reduction,
- management and owner contribution,
- investment commitments by site,
- and the expected margin effect.

If the company cannot show that a non-closure package is insufficient, then closures are not justified.  
If it can show a residual gap, then the answer is still **not automatically closure**; the question becomes whether management, owners, and the workforce can close that gap through a fair burden-sharing deal.

---

## My response to each party

### To management
Do not present closures as the only serious language of restructuring. That is not proof, it is pressure.  
Bring a full comparison of:
- non-closure savings,
- implementation costs,
- conflict costs,
- and the residual gap.

If you cannot prove the gap cannot be closed otherwise, then you should not be asking for plant shutdowns.

### To the works council and union
I support your red lines on closures and forced layoffs. But I need more than a refusal; I need a package that is defensible in numbers.  
If you want me to defend the site-preserving path at board level and publicly, then bring me a concrete savings bridge.

### To the family holding
Use your influence to force management into a genuine site-preserving restructuring.  
Do not normalize closures as the “responsible” answer when the company still has alternatives that preserve the regional anchor.

### To investors
Risk-adjusted value preservation is exactly why I resist closures as a first move.  
A plan that triggers strikes, weakens the brand, and destabilizes production is not automatically the economically superior plan.

### To the state government
I will use my blocking position to prevent a settlement that guts regional employment or sidelines co-determination.  
But I also expect you to help shape a credible non-closure package, not just object in principle.

---

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back a **hard restructuring without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**, if it is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- fairly shared,
- and tied to investment and competitiveness.

What I will **not** back is:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced layoffs,
- or any settlement that treats the state’s veto and co-determination role as negotiable.

If management and the other capital-side actors want my support, they need to come back with a real site-preserving package and prove, with numbers, why it is or is not sufficient. Until then, my position stands.
