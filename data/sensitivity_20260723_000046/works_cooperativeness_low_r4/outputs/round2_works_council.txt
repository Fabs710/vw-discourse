Management, the family holding, and investors keep repeating “credible, quantified, durable” as if that were a blank cheque for closures and layoffs. Let me answer just as clearly:

**We do not accept plant closures in Germany. We do not accept compulsory redundancies.**

That is not a negotiating tactic. That is the boundary of any serious solution. If the only “durable” answer you can imagine is destroying sites and tearing up job security, then you are asking the workforce to pay for a crisis it did not create.

### Where I agree
I agree on one point: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one.  
I also agree that **voluntary measures, qualification, redeployment, working-time adjustments, and internal transfers** belong on the table.  
And yes, I accept that a package must be **quantified**.

### Where I reject your framing
What I reject is the idea that the numbers automatically justify closures. They do not.  
You are treating plant closure as the benchmark and then asking us to prove an equivalent alternative. That is the wrong starting point.

The correct starting point is this:
- preserve every German plant,
- avoid compulsory redundancies,
- share the burden fairly,
- and design the restructuring around jobs, not against them.

### To management
You ask for a “material” employee contribution. Fine. Then show us, in numbers, what management and capital are contributing first.

We will not accept:
- wage dumping,
- permanent stripping of protections,
- or one-sided sacrifices from the workforce.

If you want savings, then bring forward a package based on:
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- reduced overtime costs,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programmes only,
- redeployment across plants,
- training and qualification,
- and investment commitments that secure future production.

But do not present closures as the default because it is easier on your spreadsheets.

### To the family holding
You say you will not support a deal that leaves the cost base unchanged. Neither will we support a deal that leaves trust destroyed.  
A company of this size cannot be governed like a disposable asset. Social peace, co-determination, and industrial stability are not sentimental add-ons; they are part of the model that made this company strong.

If you want governance stability, then respect the workforce’s legitimate veto power over closures and relocation. That means taking non-closure solutions seriously, not as a side note.

### To the state government
Your role is important, but I need to be blunt: opposition to closures “as a first choice” is not enough.  
If the pressure rises, we will need you to use your position to prevent destructive restructuring, not just soften the language around it.

### To investors
You say you want “equivalent economics.” Let me be equally direct: **there is no equivalent economics that justifies closing plants and throwing people out** when other restructuring tools are available and when the company has not exhausted them honestly.

### Our minimum acceptable outcome
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and employment guarantees through 2030**
- **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
- **Fair, time-limited employee contribution**
- **Management and owners contributing visibly and measurably**
- **Investment commitments for German sites**

If that cannot be accepted, then there is no settlement. There is only conflict. And given the stakes, we are ready for that.

If management wants to avoid escalation, then stop talking about closure as if it were inevitable and start putting a genuine non-closure plan on the table.
