I will be equally clear from the regional shareholder side: **we are not prepared to support a restructuring that solves the company’s problems by sacrificing the state’s plants, regional employment, and co-determination rights.**

I understand management’s pressure. The market is tough, costs are too high, and the company needs to regain competitiveness. On that point, I do **not** disagree. But the solution cannot be a one-sided destruction of the industrial base in Germany. This company is not just another cost center; it is a regional economic anchor with long-term responsibilities.

Where I agree:
- The company needs a credible competitiveness strategy.
- Overcapacity and high fixed costs cannot be ignored.
- A purely cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible.
- Transformation will require sacrifice from all sides.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable first resort.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **Breaking or hollowing out existing job-security and site protections is not acceptable.**
- **Any deal that sidelines the state’s blocking minority or co-determination role is not acceptable.**

To management: if you want the regional state to treat this as a serious restructuring discussion, then bring forward an alternative that preserves industrial capacity and jobs here, not just a spreadsheet solution. “Comparable savings” cannot mean simply shifting the burden onto workers and communities while investors and executives remain insulated.

To the Works Council and the union: I share your rejection of closures and forced layoffs, and I support the principle that sacrifices must be fair and reversible. But I also have to be honest: if we want to preserve sites and jobs long term, we cannot refuse every form of structural adjustment. A negotiated contribution from labour may be necessary — but only if it is matched by:
1. **binding site guarantees,**
2. **clear investment commitments,**
3. **real management and owner contributions,**
4. **and a credible industrial plan for future products and technology.**

That is the direction I would support:
- capacity reductions through negotiated, non-destructive means,
- working-time and flexibility arrangements,
- voluntary and socially responsible staffing changes,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and a fair burden-sharing package involving management, owners, and workforce.

What I will not support is a plan that treats closures as inevitable and then asks the state to rubber-stamp them. That would be politically and economically irresponsible, and it would violate the basis on which we have held our stake and board seats: safeguarding the regional economy and acting as a long-term anchor shareholder.

So my position is this:
- **I am open to restructuring.**
- **I am not open to plant closures or mass layoffs in our state as the price of it.**
- **I am prepared to back a negotiated alternative if it preserves jobs, sites, and co-determination while restoring competitiveness.**

If management believes it has no alternative, then it should expect resistance, not consent.
