### Core impasse

The discussion is now blocked at one central point:

- **Management / owners / investors** want to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies** available as real options, because they believe durable competitiveness requires meaningful capacity and fixed-cost reduction.
- **Works council / union** want **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding site/job guarantees**, because they see closure talk as an unacceptable starting point and a trust-breaking threat.
- **State government** is aligned with labor on avoiding closures and forced layoffs, but also accepts the need for structural savings.
- **The expert** has clarified that the economic question is unresolved without site-level data and that **partial consolidation may capture much of the needed savings** in some cases.

So the impasse is not over whether the company has a problem; it is over **which instruments remain legitimate before the plant-level economics are proven**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Conditional no-closure review with hard safeguards
A time-limited framework could be used in which:
- all German sites remain open **for the duration of the review period**,
- **no compulsory redundancies** occur during that period,
- the independent model tests **full closure, partial consolidation, and non-closure restructuring** for each site,
- and any later closure consideration requires **documented failure** of a non-closure package against agreed economic thresholds.

This would preserve labor’s trust condition temporarily while still giving management a structured path to test closure economics.

#### Proposal 2: Tiered restructuring package before any closure decision
The parties could agree on a sequence:
1. voluntary exits,
2. internal redeployment,
3. working-time and shift adjustments,
4. overhead and management-cost reductions,
5. partial consolidation where justified,
6. and only then a final review of whether any site still remains non-viable.

This bridges the gap by requiring that **non-closure measures be exhausted first**, while not permanently ruling out stronger measures if the model shows they are necessary.

#### Proposal 3: Protection package tied to measurable burden-sharing
A possible exchange structure could be:
- the workforce accepts a defined restructuring contribution,
- management and owners commit to visible burden-sharing,
- the state supports a regional stability framework,
- and in return there is a **binding commitment against compulsory redundancies**, with **closure decisions deferred** until after the model and a joint review.

This does not settle the closure question upfront, but it creates a framework in which each side contributes before the hardest instrument is considered.

#### To the Works Council:
