### Neutral Moderator Summary

## 1) What has been agreed
- All sides acknowledge that the company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable solution** is needed, not a symbolic or short-term one.
- There is also agreement that the debate must be based on **quantified site-by-site economics**, not slogans.
- Most participants accept that **some form of restructuring** is necessary, including measures such as redeployment, qualification, capacity adjustments, and cost contributions.
- The independent experts have confirmed that **temporary concessions alone are unlikely to be sufficient** if the cost gap is substantial.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**:  
  - Works Council, union, and state reject closures as a default or acceptable starting point.  
  - Management, owners, and investors view closures as potentially necessary if alternatives cannot match the economics.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee representatives reject them outright.  
  - Management and capital side do not accept a blanket prohibition if restructuring requires workforce reduction.
- **Job and site guarantees**:  
  - Labour wants binding protections.  
  - Management and capital argue such guarantees may block needed restructuring.
- **Burden of proof**:  
  - Labour and state say management must prove closures are necessary.  
  - Management and capital say labour must prove non-closure alternatives can deliver comparable savings.
- **Governance and co-determination**:  
  - Labour insists on real influence before decisions are effectively fixed.  
  - Management wants flexibility to implement structural change without binding constraints that preclude action.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The group has **not yet quantified** the minimum savings needed to restore target margins.
- There is still no shared answer on **how much of the cost gap can be closed through non-closure measures** versus how much requires hard capacity reduction.
- The discussion has not yet resolved the trade-off between:
  - **preserving jobs and sites now**, and
  - **achieving durable competitiveness and investment capacity**.
- There is no agreed framework for comparing:
  1. full closure,
  2. partial consolidation,
  3. non-closure restructuring,
  on the same financial basis.
- The timing question remains open: **which measures produce savings fast enough** to matter, and which only delay the problem?

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from position statements to a **shared comparison table**. Specifically, each side should work from the same assumptions on:
- required savings gap,
- site-by-site utilization,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- annual recurring savings,
- timing to savings,
- headcount impact,
- and probability of meeting the return-on-sales target.

A useful next step would be to ask the expert to present a **single neutral scenario matrix** for each site, so the group can discuss trade-offs against the same numbers rather than competing narratives.
