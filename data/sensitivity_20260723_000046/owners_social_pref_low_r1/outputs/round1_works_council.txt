As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. We will not agree to breaking the historic job-security guarantees that have protected this workforce for years.

If management says the only solution is to shut plants and throw people out, then we fundamentally disagree. That is not a restructuring plan; it is shifting the burden of management mistakes onto the workforce.

Our priorities are:

1. **Every German plant stays open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A real co-determination role in shaping any restructuring.**
5. **Fair burden-sharing** if sacrifices are necessary — through negotiated, reversible measures, not one-sided cuts.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect jobs and sites:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- voluntary departures only if genuinely voluntary and socially responsible,
- and a contribution from management and owners, not just the workforce.

But let me be equally clear: any proposal built on closures, mass layoffs, or the destruction of collective protections will meet our full resistance. We are ready to defend the workforce in the supervisory board, at the negotiating table, and, if necessary, with industrial action.
