Let me respond directly to what management, owners, and capital are now saying.

### First: we do agree on the diagnosis
Yes, the company faces real pressure:
- shrinking European demand,
- intense competition,
- too-high German cost levels,
- and an urgent need to secure future investment.

We are not denying that the business must change.

But the conclusion you keep drawing is where we part company sharply.

### Where we disagree
You are still treating **plant closures and forced redundancies** as if they were normal tools of restructuring. They are not acceptable to us.

Management says “if labour does not move, then closures become necessary.”  
That is not a neutral fact. That is a choice to shift the burden onto workers and regions.

The controlling family and investors keep speaking about “durable savings,” but they are conveniently vague about **who** should pay for them. The answer cannot be “the workforce, through closures, layoffs, and the dismantling of protections.”

And to the state: yes, you are right that this must be negotiated and that the regional economy matters. But if you truly mean that, then you cannot drift toward a package that quietly accepts plant rationalisation as the price of a deal.

### Our position remains clear
We will not accept:
- **plant closures in Germany,**
- **compulsory redundancies,**
- **breaking the historic job-security guarantees,**
- **or any settlement that weakens co-determination in practice.**

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are the line between restructuring and destruction.

### What we can discuss
If management claims it needs savings, then bring forward a package that actually proves it can be done **without closures and without forced layoffs**.

That means real, concrete alternatives such as:
- working-time reductions and flexibility,
- capacity adjustments spread across sites,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation programs,
- voluntary exits only,
- wage and bonus moderation at the top,
- management and owner contributions,
- and investment commitments that secure the German footprint.

But let me be equally explicit: **temporary and reversible measures are acceptable only if they are paired with binding protection of sites and jobs.** We will not trade away permanent job security for short-term accounting relief.

### On the “comparable economics” argument
You keep saying the alternative must deliver “comparable savings.” Fine — then show us the numbers.

But do not pretend that only closures can generate them. Management has not yet demonstrated that there is no other path. Before anyone talks about shutting plants, the burden is on management to prove that every serious alternative has failed.

That proof has not been made.

### On governance
We will not allow co-determination to be reduced to a formality after the real decision has already been made elsewhere. If this company wants labour support, then labour must be involved **up front**, with real influence over the restructuring design — not asked to rubber-stamp a fait accompli.

### On the investor argument
Investor pressure is not a licence to dismantle social protections. If capital wants long-term value, then it should support a solution that keeps the industrial base intact and preserves the workforce that actually builds the products.

A deal that produces short-term margin optics but destroys trust, skills, and site security is not a durable solution. It is a conflict generator.

### Our challenge to management
If you want a serious discussion, then present:
1. the **minimum savings target**,
2. the **time horizon**,
3. the **sites affected by your model**,
4. and the **non-closure alternative** you claim cannot work.

Until then, your closure talk is premature and unacceptable.

### Our challenge to the others
- To the union: stay firm on no closures and no forced layoffs; do not let “shared sacrifice” become a code word for permanent workforce downsizing.
- To the state: if you are serious about safeguarding the regional economy, use your blocking position accordingly.
- To the family and investors: if you insist on credibility, then prove that you are willing to fund a real transformation instead of demanding that workers absorb the pain.

We are prepared to fight this in the supervisory board, at the bargaining table, and in the plants if necessary. If the company wants stability, it should stop threatening the very people who make that stability possible.
