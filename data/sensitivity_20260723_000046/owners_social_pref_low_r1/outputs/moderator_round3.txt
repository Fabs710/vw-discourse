## Core impasse
The parties agree on the diagnosis — the company has a structural competitiveness problem and needs durable savings — but they disagree on the **acceptable method** of achieving that adjustment.

The deepest conflict is between:
- **labor and the state**, who insist that closures and compulsory redundancies must not be the starting point, and
- **management, controlling shareholders, and investors**, who insist that they cannot accept a framework that rules out closures or workforce reduction before the economics are proven.

A second fault line is the **trust problem**:
- labor wants binding job and site guarantees to make any restructuring credible,
- capital and management see those guarantees as potentially freezing the structure and preventing a real reset.

A third issue is the **definition of durability**:
- labor seeks durable savings through shared burden and non-closure measures,
- capital seeks durable savings through permanent fixed-cost and capacity reduction, including the possibility of closure if needed.

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise
Below are three concrete bridge options that could narrow the gap without presuming any side’s position.

### Proposal 1: Conditional no-closure framework with built-in review gates
A negotiated package could state that:
- no German plant closure will occur **unless** an independent site-by-site model shows that a given site remains structurally non-viable after agreed non-closure measures are applied,
- and that closure may only be considered after a defined review point.

This would give labor a meaningful non-closure pathway while preserving an economic off-ramp for management if the numbers fail.

### Proposal 2: Partial consolidation first, closure only as last step
The parties could agree to test a sequence:
1. capacity reduction through shift changes, specialization, and overhead cuts,
2. partial consolidation of overlapping functions,
3. only if the target is still not met, a second-stage review of more severe measures.

This creates a staged restructuring path and may allow parties to achieve savings while postponing the most divisive decision.

### Proposal 3: Binding employment protection paired with quantified restructuring contributions
A compromise could combine:
- a time-limited site and job protection period,
- voluntary exits only during that period,
- management, owner, and top-compensation contributions,
- and an agreed minimum labor-cost and overhead reduction target.

This would ask labor for contributions, but only alongside visible burden-sharing from capital and management.

## Pointed questions for each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
What specific combination of working-time, redeployment, overhead, and voluntary-exit measures would you accept as sufficient to prove that a no-closure package is economically credible?

### To the Trade Union
What minimum permanent labor-cost and capacity reduction would you consider compatible with a settlement that avoids plant closures and compulsory redundancies?

### To Group & Brand Management
What is the exact savings gap by site, and at what point would you formally rule out or confirm closure as necessary?

### To the Regional State Government
If the model shows that one site is structurally non-viable, what is the precise threshold at which you would still support a restructuring package that avoids closure?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a staged framework in which closure is deferred unless an independent model shows that the non-closure package fails to close the gap?

### To Institutional Investors
What level of permanent savings and margin improvement would make a non-closure restructuring acceptable to you, even if it preserves all sites?

### To the Independent Expert
What minimum assumptions about utilization, labor-cost relief, and overhead reduction would a non-closure scenario need to satisfy to be considered economically comparable to closure?
