1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties accept that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem and that the current structure is not sustainable without change.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should be negotiated, not imposed.
- There is shared openness to some combination of:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - working-time / flexibility measures,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification / transformation measures,
  - voluntary and socially managed exits,
  - and some form of contribution from multiple stakeholders, not only labor.
- Several parties accept that a durable settlement must be economically credible, not merely symbolic.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point; management, controlling family, and investors see capacity reduction as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee and state side reject them; management and capital side do not rule out stronger structural measures if needed.
- **Scope of job/site guarantees:** Labor and state seek binding guarantees; management and owners reject blanket guarantees through 2030 if they block restructuring.
- **Size and durability of labor contribution:** Management and capital want a meaningful, durable cost reduction; labor is only open to limited, reversible, fairly shared sacrifices.
- **Who bears the burden:** Labor and state want balanced burden-sharing including management/owners; management, family, and investors insist labor costs must be part of the adjustment in a material way.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- The parties have not yet bridged the gap between **preserving sites/jobs** and **achieving sufficient permanent savings**.
- No one has yet presented a concrete package showing how to reach the required economic improvement **without closures** and with only limited workforce concessions.
- The discussion has not resolved:
  - which sites, if any, are non-negotiable,
  - what level of cost reduction is required,
  - what concessions are temporary vs. permanent,
  - what guarantees or investment commitments would accompany any labor contribution,
  - and how governance roles would be protected while enabling restructuring.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to **numbers and design options**: ask management to present the minimum required savings, timeline, and scenarios with and without closures.
- Ask labor and the state to specify what **alternative, non-closure measures** they would accept, and under what binding conditions.
- Test whether a package can be built around:
  - measurable savings,
  - time-limited or reversible sacrifices,
  - site/job protections tied to investment commitments,
  - and a clear division of contributions across management, owners, and workforce.
- The next round should focus on **comparable economics versus comparable social impact**, rather than restating red lines.
