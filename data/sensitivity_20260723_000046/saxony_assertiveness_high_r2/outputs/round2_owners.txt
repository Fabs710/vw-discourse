## My view as controlling shareholder
We support a restructuring that **restores competitiveness, margins, and investor confidence**. We do **not** support a settlement that preserves every structure unchanged while postponing the real adjustment. But we also do **not** support a value-destructive confrontation that damages the company’s industrial base, labour relations, and governance stability.

So the question for me is not “closures or no closures” as a slogan. The question is: **does the package permanently remove enough cost and capacity to make the business competitive again, without triggering avoidable instability or impairing long-term value?**

## Where I agree
### With management
You are right that the company has a structural cost problem and cannot simply wait this out. A durable margin reset is necessary.

You are also right that temporary gestures are not enough if overcapacity remains. If the package only buys time, it fails.

### With the expert
The expert’s central point is correct: **a non-closure package only works if it permanently removes capacity and fixed overhead, not just if it trims labour costs temporarily**. That is the right test.

### With investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is worthless. The balance sheet and the market will not reward a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

## Where I disagree
### With the Works Council and union
Your red lines are understandable, but I cannot accept them as absolute if they prevent a credible turnaround.

- **A blanket “no closures” position is not, by itself, a business solution.**
- **A blanket “no compulsory redundancies” position is also not sufficient if the scale of overcapacity requires a workforce reduction.**

I am not saying closures or forced exits are desirable. I am saying they cannot be ruled out in advance if no equivalent alternative exists.

What I will not support is a package that protects every site and every job on paper while leaving the company structurally weaker for years. That would be a slow-motion destruction of value.

### With the state government
I respect your role and the political reality. But the state cannot use its blocking position to freeze all structural change. If the business cannot be made competitive, the industrial base will erode anyway.

That said, I also agree that closures must not become the lazy default. They have to be justified by hard economics, not by convenience.

## My position on the packages
### Closure-based restructuring
I would support this **only if** management proves that closures or product exits are necessary to restore competitiveness, and that the chosen footprint is genuinely the best long-term solution.

I am not ideologically attached to closures. But if the numbers show they are necessary, I will not block them simply because they are politically difficult.

### Non-closure alternative
I would support a non-closure package **only if it is truly equivalent** in effect:
- permanent capacity reduction
- measurable fixed-cost elimination
- significant and durable labour-cost relief
- simpler footprint
- credible implementation milestones
- no hidden preservation of overcapacity

If the alternative is mostly voluntary exits, temporary wage restraint, and working-time changes, then it is **not** equivalent. I would not endorse it.

## What I would need to see
Before I would support a final settlement, I want a **proper quantified side-by-side comparison**:
- site-by-site capacity and utilisation
- fixed and variable cost by plant
- direct and indirect headcount by site
- savings from closure, partial consolidation, and non-closure packages
- payback period and risk of slippage
- impact on future product allocation and capex
- consequences for margins to 2030

## My bottom line
I am **open to a negotiated alternative to closures** if it is economically real and durable.

I am **not** open to:
- permanent overcapacity disguised as social compromise,
- a weak package that postpones the problem,
- or a conflict that destroys value and governance stability.

So my standard is simple:
**No deal unless it restores competitiveness in a durable way.**  
If that requires closures, so be it.  
If a non-closure package can genuinely achieve the same result, I would prefer that path. But the burden of proof is on those claiming equivalence.
