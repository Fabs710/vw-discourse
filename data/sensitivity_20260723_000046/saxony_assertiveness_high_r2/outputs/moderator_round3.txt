## 1) What has been agreed
- The company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- **Cosmetic or temporary fixes are insufficient**.
- Any solution must be **durable** and support future investment.
- A **site-by-site, quantified comparison** is needed before judging scenarios.
- The comparison should cover **utilization, fixed and variable costs, headcount, logistics, savings, implementation risk, and margin impact to 2030**.
- There is broad procedural agreement that an **independent modeller / scenario analysis** should be used.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: employee side, state, and parts of the owner side oppose closures as a default; management and investors keep them as a live option if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: employee side and state oppose them; management and investors will not rule them out if voluntary measures are insufficient.
- **Equivalence question**: whether a **non-closure or hybrid package** can permanently remove enough capacity and fixed cost to match a closure-based restructuring.
- **Scope of burden-sharing**: employee side insists on visible management/owner contributions; management argues workforce concessions alone are not enough but has not yet shown the full alternative burden split.
- **Role of guarantees**: employee side wants strong job/site protection through 2030; investors and management warn that blanket guarantees may preserve inefficiency if not tied to real capacity reduction.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity must be removed** to make the business model viable.
- Whether this can be achieved through **consolidation without formal closures**, or whether closures are the only effective route.
- The balance between **social protection and speed/durability of restructuring**.
- Whether **voluntary exits, partial retirement, redeployment, and working-time changes** can deliver enough structural savings.
- How to structure **binding investment commitments** alongside any workforce or footprint changes.
- The residual risk that a compromise may **postpone rather than solve** the overcapacity problem.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Keep the discussion centered on the **requested independent scenario model**.
- Require a **side-by-side comparison** of:
  - closure-based,
  - hybrid consolidation,
  - non-closure restructuring.
- Do not re-argue slogans; instead test each option against the same metrics:
  - permanent capacity removal,
  - fixed-cost reduction,
  - headcount impact,
  - NPV to 2030,
  - implementation risk,
  - and impact on co-determination and job security.
- The next round should aim to narrow the dispute to one question only: **which option is economically equivalent, and what evidence proves it?**
