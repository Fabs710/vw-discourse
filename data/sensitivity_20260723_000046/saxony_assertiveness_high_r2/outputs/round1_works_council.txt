As the Works Council, our position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

The workforce has already made major contributions to the company’s transformation. We understand the pressure on costs and competitiveness, but management cannot solve a strategic problem by sacrificing jobs, undermining co-determination, and breaking long-standing job-security commitments. That is not a responsible restructuring; it is a unilateral transfer of risk onto employees.

What we will argue for instead is a negotiated solution that preserves every German plant, protects jobs through 2030, and secures the company’s future without forcing people out. That means:

- binding site guarantees for all German locations
- no compulsory layoffs
- socially responsible adjustments through voluntary measures only
- investment commitments for the German plants and future products
- a real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping any restructuring
- fair burden-sharing, not one-sided cuts

If management wants support for a turnaround, it must present a credible plan that protects the industrial base in Germany and respects the people who built this company. If the proposal still relies on closures or forced job cuts, then we will oppose it with all the means available to us.
