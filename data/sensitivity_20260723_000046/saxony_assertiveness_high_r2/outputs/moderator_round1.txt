**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a serious competitiveness and cost challenge.
- All parties agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.
- There is broad agreement that investment in future products and technology is necessary.
- There is shared recognition that any restructuring must consider the long-term viability of the company, not just short-term fixes.
- Several parties indicated openness to alternatives to plant closures if they can deliver comparable, durable savings and capacity reductions.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state government reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management and institutional investors say closures may be necessary if equivalent alternatives do not exist.  
  - The family holding is open to closures only as part of a broader restructuring, not as a goal in themselves.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state government reject them.  
  - Management and investors say they may be required depending on the scale of overcapacity.
- **Scope of site protection:**  
  - Employee representatives and the state want binding protection for German sites.  
  - Management and investors oppose unconditional guarantees that freeze the current structure.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  - All sides refer to burden-sharing, but differ sharply on what is “fair” and how much labor versus ownership should contribute.
- **Economic credibility vs. social protection:**  
  - Employees and state emphasize job and site preservation.  
  - Management and investors emphasize structural cost reduction and margin restoration.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- What level of cost reduction is required, and how much must come from labor, capacity changes, management, owners, or investment commitments.
- Whether voluntary measures, working-time changes, redeployment, and qualification can together equal the savings from closures or layoffs.
- Which sites or capacities, if any, are genuinely non-negotiable, and which are still open for adjustment.
- How to balance investment promises with measurable restructuring milestones.
- Whether any agreement can be both socially acceptable and economically strong enough to satisfy governance and capital-market expectations.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to concrete design options:
- define the required savings and capacity targets,
- specify which measures are voluntary, temporary, or permanent,
- identify any site protections that can be credibly committed,
- and test whether a non-closure package can match the economic effect of closures or redundancies.

The next round should focus on a package comparison: **closure-based restructuring vs. alternative restructuring**, with explicit numbers, timelines, and safeguards.
