### Core impasse
The plateau appears to be centered on **one unresolved conditional**:

- **Management, investors, and the family** are treating **formal closures and possibly compulsory redundancies as legitimate tools** unless proven unnecessary.
- **Works council and union** are treating **no closures and no compulsory redundancies as non-negotiable starting conditions**.
- **The state** is trying to preserve the industrial base and block a rushed closure path, but does not want to freeze all structural change.
- **The expert** has reframed the dispute from ideology to economics: the package must deliver **permanent capacity reduction, fixed-cost removal, and acceptable NPV to 2030**.

So the deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **which instruments are allowed before the site-by-site evidence is complete, and what counts as “economically equivalent.”**

---

### Facilitator proposals for bridging positions

#### Proposal 1: Conditional non-closure test with trigger thresholds
Create a **time-bound, site-by-site test period** where closures remain **not pre-approved**, but also **not categorically excluded**.

**Elements**
- Independent modeller produces a plant-level comparison within a fixed deadline.
- The parties pre-agree threshold criteria for viability:
  - minimum capacity utilization,
  - minimum fixed-cost reduction,
  - minimum NPV improvement by 2030,
  - maximum implementation risk.
- If a non-closure or hybrid package meets the thresholds, it becomes the preferred route.
- If it fails, the parties move to the next scenario without re-litigating the model.

**Why this bridges**
- It gives employee side a real chance to prove a non-closure solution.
- It gives management/investors a path to stronger measures if the numbers do not work.
- It gives the state a structured blocking role rather than a political veto.

---

#### Proposal 2: No compulsory redundancies first, with a contingent fallback
Agree on a **phased workforce adjustment ladder**:

1. voluntary exits  
2. early/partial retirement  
3. redeployment and retraining  
4. working-time measures  
5. internal consolidation  
6. only then, if a quantified gap remains, a jointly reviewed fallback mechanism

**Elements**
- No compulsory redundancies in phase one.
- A jointly defined review point if voluntary and internal measures fall short by a measured amount.
- Any fallback would require a second review of alternatives and social mitigation.

**Why this bridges**
- It preserves the employee red line in the immediate negotiation.
- It acknowledges management’s concern that voluntary tools may be insufficient.
- It prevents “voluntary” programs from becoming pressure-based exits.

---

#### Proposal 3: Site guarantees tied to measurable restructuring obligations
Offer **targeted site-and-job guarantees through 2030** for the sites that remain, but only in exchange for **binding structural contributions**.

**Elements**
- No blanket guarantee for every current site.
- Guarantees attached to specified plants or functions that become part of the new footprint.
- In return:
  - management commits to investment,
  - owners contribute capital or governance support,
  - labor accepts bounded flexibility or temporary concessions,
  - and overhead/capacity reductions are codified.
- The agreement includes enforceable milestones and review clauses.

**Why this bridges**
- It gives the employee side tangible protection.
- It gives management durable savings and footprint simplification.
- It gives the state a reason to support the package as an industrial-anchor solution.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
If the independent model shows that a hybrid or deep non-closure package can achieve the required savings and margin path to 2030, **what specific site-level thresholds would you accept as sufficient to rule out closures?**

#### To the Works Council
If the independent model shows that a non-closure package fails to remove enough permanent capacity and fixed cost, **what restructuring instruments short of compulsory redundancies would you allow to close the gap?**

#### To the Industrial Union
What precise combination of voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and internal consolidation would you regard as enough to replace a closure-based plan, **and how would you verify that it is not just temporary labor relief?**

#### To the Regional State Government
Under what quantified conditions would you permit a footprint reduction while still using your blocking role to prevent closure from becoming the default outcome?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
What level of permanent return improvement and governance stability would justify your support for a plan that does **not** preserve all current sites?

#### To the Institutional Investors
Would you accept a non-closure settlement if it meets defined thresholds for permanent capacity reduction, fixed-cost removal, and NPV to 2030, even if it preserves more German footprint than a closure-based plan?

#### To the Expert
What minimum data set and threshold logic would you require so that the parties can determine, in a transparent way, whether the non-closure or hybrid scenario is genuinely economically equivalent to closure?
