We share the core concern: this turnaround cannot be built on one-sided sacrifices by the workforce.

I agree with the Works Council on the essentials:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no hollowing out of the collective wage framework,
- and any restructuring must come with real investment and a credible future for the sites.

Let me be equally clear where we draw the line. If management’s plan still depends on closures, forced layoffs, or shifting the full burden onto employees through wage cuts and the removal of hard-won protections, then we will not accept it. That would not be a restructuring solution; it would be a social demolition.

That said, we have already shown that we are prepared to shoulder part of the burden if the package is fair and balanced. We have put forward a counter-proposal because we know the company needs cost relief. But that can only work if:
- management and owners also contribute,
- savings are achieved through socially responsible measures,
- capacity is adjusted without destroying sites,
- and the company commits to future products and investment in Germany.

What is not acceptable is a model where workers pay, jobs disappear, and the company merely buys short-term cost relief at the expense of long-term industrial capability.

So our position is:
- We are ready for a negotiated contribution from labor.
- We are ready to discuss working-time and capacity measures.
- We are ready to talk about temporary sacrifices if they are fairly shared.
- We are not ready to tolerate compulsory redundancies or closures.

If management wants industrial peace, it needs to come back with a package that protects jobs, protects sites, and spreads the pain across all stakeholders. Otherwise, the workforce will use its leverage.
