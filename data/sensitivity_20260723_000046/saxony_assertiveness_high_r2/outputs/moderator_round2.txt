## Neutral moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company faces a real structural cost and competitiveness problem;
- a durable, not merely temporary, solution is needed;
- investment in future products, technology, and modernization is necessary;
- a purely cosmetic compromise would not be acceptable;
- any credible path forward should be based on hard numbers and scenario comparison.

There is also shared interest in avoiding prolonged open conflict if a workable negotiated package can be found.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved disputes are:
- **Closures vs. non-closure restructuring:**  
  Management, investors, and the controlling family see closures or equivalent footprint rationalization as potentially necessary; the works council, union, and state government oppose closures as a default or first-resort solution.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee representatives reject them categorically; management and capital-side actors argue that workforce reduction at scale may be needed if alternatives do not deliver equivalent savings.
- **Binding site/job guarantees:**  
  Employee side wants strong guarantees through 2030; management views broad guarantees as too restrictive if they preserve overcapacity.
- **How burden-sharing should work:**  
  Employees are willing to consider temporary contributions if matched by management, owners, and investment commitments; capital-side actors say temporary concessions are insufficient unless they produce permanent structural savings.
- **State role:**  
  The state wants restructuring, but not at the expense of co-determination or industrial-base stability; others question whether its blocking role can be used to prevent necessary footprint changes.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The dialogue has not yet resolved:
- **whether non-closure measures can match closure-based savings** in a durable way;
- **how much permanent capacity reduction is actually required**;
- **which costs are temporary versus structural**;
- **how to balance site protection against competitiveness**;
- **how to ensure employee contributions are matched by ownership/management contributions**;
- **what implementation safeguards are needed** if a voluntary or hybrid package is chosen;
- **how to avoid a “voluntary” program becoming indirect forced exits**;
- **what the residual risk is if savings targets are missed**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of three scenarios:
1. closure-based restructuring,
2. partial consolidation / hybrid restructuring,
3. non-closure restructuring.

The discussion should focus on:
- site-by-site utilization,
- fixed and variable costs,
- headcount impacts,
- permanent savings,
- capex needs,
- implementation risk,
- and margin trajectory to 2030.

A useful next step would be to ask each side to specify, in numbers, what minimum savings and capacity reduction they consider acceptable and what concessions they are prepared to make in return.
