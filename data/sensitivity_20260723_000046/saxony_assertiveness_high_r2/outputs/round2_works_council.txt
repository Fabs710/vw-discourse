We are moving from abstract “competitiveness” rhetoric to concrete choices, and I will be equally concrete:

**A restructuring that requires plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies is not acceptable.**  
That remains our clear red line. If management believes it can force that through, it is ignoring both the legal reality of co-determination and the industrial reality of this workforce.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on three points:

1. **The company has a real cost problem.**  
   Nobody on the employee side is denying that the market has changed.

2. **A durable solution is needed.**  
   We also do not want a short-lived patch that leaves the company vulnerable again in two years.

3. **The future needs investment.**  
   Without new products, technology, and plant modernization, this debate is pointless.

## Where I disagree
But I strongly reject the conclusion management and some investors are trying to force:

- **Closures are not “just one option” if they destroy the social compact and the industrial base.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable tool for fixing management’s strategic mistakes.**
- **Binding site guarantees are not “freezing the wrong structure”; they are the minimum precondition for trust.**

The workforce has already contributed to transformation. What is not acceptable is that employees are now supposed to pay twice: first through concessions, then through the loss of plants and jobs anyway.

## Concrete alternative package
If management wants a real alternative to closures, then the burden-sharing must be real and measurable, not symbolic. We are prepared to discuss a package with the following elements:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **Voluntary-only workforce reduction tools**
  - early retirement
  - partial retirement
  - severance on a voluntary basis only
  - internal redeployment
  - qualification and retraining
- **Working-time flexibility** where justified and temporary
- **Temporary, negotiated employee contribution** only if matched by:
  - management contribution,
  - owner contribution,
  - and hard investment commitments for German sites
- **Clear product and investment commitments** for every German plant

## What we will not accept
Let me be explicit about the limits:

- not a “voluntary” package that in reality becomes forced exits by pressure
- not a relocation of production under another name
- not a restructuring that leaves one or more German plants on a cliff edge
- not wage cuts presented as the price of survival while management keeps the right to close sites anyway
- not a plan where labor gives up guarantees and management gives nothing durable in return

## On the comparison to closure-based restructuring
Management has not yet shown that closures are the only way to get the required savings. That burden of proof is on management, not on the workforce.

If you claim:
- one or more German plants must close,
- jobs must be cut compulsorily,
- and co-determination protections must be weakened,

then you must provide a transparent case with:
- exact savings,
- exact capacity needs,
- exact investment consequences,
- and proof that no alternative mix can achieve the same result.

So far, what I hear is mostly pressure, not proof.

## To the state government
Your position that closures are not a first resort is correct. But “not a first resort” cannot be a polite prelude to later approval. If you are serious about preserving the industrial base, then use your blocking position to prevent closure scenarios being normalized as the default answer.

## To the controlling family
You say you are long-term owners. Then act like long-term owners: protect the industrial base, invest in the plants, and do not treat workforce rights as an obstacle to be removed. Long-term value is not created by tearing out the company’s social constitution.

## To institutional investors
You want durability and credibility. Fine. Then support a solution that is actually durable: protected sites, skilled workforce retention, future products, and negotiated flexibility. A “hard restructuring” that blows up labor relations and triggers permanent conflict is not value creation; it is destruction with a spreadsheet attached.

## Bottom line
Our position in this round is unchanged but now operational:

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No settlement without binding guarantees to 2030**
- **No employee contribution without a fair contribution from management and owners**
- **No agreement that weakens co-determination or the job-security framework**

If management wants a real negotiation, bring a package built on **voluntary measures, internal adjustment, and investment commitments**.  
If it comes back with closures and forced layoffs, then it will face full resistance from the works council and the workforce.
