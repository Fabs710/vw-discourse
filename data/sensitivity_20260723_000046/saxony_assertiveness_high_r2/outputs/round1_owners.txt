I agree with several points that have been made, but I need to be equally clear about the limits.

First, to the management side: I accept the core diagnosis. The cost base is too high, the market is under pressure, and the company cannot simply preserve the status quo and expect competitiveness to recover. If we do nothing, we erode the value of the enterprise, and that is not acceptable. The family holding is not interested in defending inefficiency for its own sake.

Where I disagree is with the implication that closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or inevitable answer. They may be part of a restructuring toolbox, but they are not a goal in themselves, and any solution that destroys the long-term industrial strength of the company would be value-destructive. We are owners for the long term, not for a one-off balance-sheet clean-up.

To the Works Council and the union: I understand the resistance to plant closures and forced layoffs, and I acknowledge the workforce has already carried part of the transformation burden. I also accept that a durable solution needs labour peace and legitimacy. But a blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” position is not enough by itself if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. Protecting every site regardless of economics is not a sustainable ownership policy either. If you want us to defend a negotiated path, it must show how the required savings and flexibility are actually achieved.

To the state government: I appreciate the role you play as anchor shareholder and the importance of the industrial base to the region. But I would caution against turning this into a political exercise where every difficult adjustment is blocked in advance. The company must remain investable and competitive. If governance is used only to freeze the structure, we all lose in the end.

My position is therefore this:

- We support a serious restructuring that restores margins and competitiveness.
- We do not support permanent value destruction through unmanaged conflict or governance paralysis.
- We are open to alternatives to closures if they deliver equivalent, durable cost and capacity relief.
- Any negotiated package must be credible on savings, flexibility, and future investment.
- No solution can be acceptable if it merely postpones the problem or shifts the burden entirely onto ownership while leaving the operating model unchanged.

If the employee side can bring forward a package that genuinely reduces costs and capacity in a lasting way without destabilising the company, we will support that path through governance. If not, then I will not endorse a soft outcome that preserves appearances but weakens the business.
