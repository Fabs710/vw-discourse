## My position remains unchanged
I support a restructuring that **restores competitiveness, margins, and long-term value**. I do **not** support a settlement that merely preserves the existing footprint while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive. And I do **not** support a value-destructive conflict that damages the enterprise, its governance, or labour peace.

So the real question is not “closures versus no closures” as a slogan. The real question is whether the final package **permanently removes enough cost and capacity** to restore the business to acceptable returns **without** triggering avoidable instability.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the company has a structural problem, not a temporary one. The cost base is too high, and temporary fixes alone will not restore margins. You are also right that the package must be durable, not cosmetic.

### With the expert
The expert’s central point is exactly the right one: **a non-closure package only works if it permanently reduces capacity and fixed overhead, not just labor cost for a few years**. That is the proper test.

### With investors
You are right to reject a hollow compromise. A deal that preserves overcapacity and calls itself “balanced” would be bad for value.

### With the state
You are right to insist on a quantified comparison before anyone treats closures as inevitable.

---

## Where I disagree

### With the Works Council and Union
I understand your red lines, but I cannot accept them as absolute if they block a credible turnaround.

A blanket **“no closures”** position is not, by itself, a solution.  
A blanket **“no compulsory redundancies”** position is also not enough if the scale of overcapacity requires workforce reduction.

That does **not** mean I prefer closures or forced layoffs. It means I will not rule them out in advance if no equivalent alternative exists.

What I will not accept is a package that protects every site on paper while leaving the company structurally weaker and less competitive for years.

### With the state government
I respect the blocking role and the political responsibility. But that role cannot become a tool for freezing all structural change. If the business remains uncompetitive, the industrial base erodes anyway — only more slowly and with more damage.

### With management
You are right about the need for hard structural adjustment, but you still have not demonstrated, with numbers, that closures are unavoidable. Until then, closures remain an option, not a conclusion.

## On the expert assessment
The expert has clarified the issue well:

- **Temporary labor concessions alone are not enough**
- **Voluntary exits alone are usually not enough**
- **A credible alternative to closures must remove permanent capacity and fixed cost**

That is exactly the standard I would apply.

What the expert did **not** say is that closures are automatically required. So I reject any attempt to present closures as the only serious option before the side-by-side economics have been shown.

---

## What I would support
I am open to either of the following:

### 1) A closure-based restructuring
Only if management proves that closures or product exits are truly necessary to restore competitiveness and that they are the best long-term footprint solution.

### 2) A non-closure or hybrid restructuring
Only if it is **economically equivalent** in effect, meaning:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- significant fixed-cost removal,
- meaningful workforce reduction,
- simplified footprint,
- durable labor-cost relief,
- and no hidden preservation of overcapacity.

If the non-closure package is mostly voluntary exits, working-time changes, and temporary concessions, then it is not equivalent.

- fixed and variable cost by plant
- direct and indirect headcount
- product allocation

## Bottom line
I am not attached to closures for their own sake. I am attached to outcomes:
- restored competitiveness
- durable savings
- credible margins
- long-term value preservation
- industrial peace

If a non-closure package can truly achieve that, I would prefer it.  
If it cannot, then deeper structural measures, including closures, remain on the table.
