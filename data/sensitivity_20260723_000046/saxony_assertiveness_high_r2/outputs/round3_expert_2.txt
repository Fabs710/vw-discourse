Yes — I can lay out the **quantitative comparison framework** for the three restructuring scenarios. Below is the model structure I would use to compare them site by site.

## 1) Scenario definitions

### A. Closure-based restructuring
Assumes:
- one or more German plants are fully closed,
- production is reassigned to remaining sites or externalized,
- redundant fixed cost and headcount are removed,
- associated capex and restructuring costs are incurred upfront.

### B. Hybrid / partial consolidation
Assumes:
- no full plant closure, or only limited closures,
- one or more sites lose specific lines, shifts, functions, or models,
- capacity is concentrated into fewer remaining lines/sites,
- overhead and indirect cost are reduced structurally.

### C. Non-closure restructuring
Assumes:
- all named German sites remain formally open,
- cost reduction comes from consolidation within plants, working-time changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, functional simplification, and labor-cost measures,
- no full site exit, but installed capacity and fixed cost still decline materially.

## 4) The right comparison logic

The key question is not “closure vs. no closure” in the abstract. It is:

> **How much permanent fixed cost and installed capacity is removed, at what implementation cost, and with what residual risk?**

A non-closure package is only economically comparable if it removes roughly the same:
- fixed overhead,
- indirect headcount,
- complexity,
- and underutilized capacity.

If it only shifts costs around or defers them, it will underperform on NPV and margin recovery.

## 6) Practical benchmark logic

In automotive networks, the least defensible scenario is usually:
- low utilization remains at several plants,
- indirect cost stays largely intact,
- capex is spent to preserve too many sites,
- and savings depend mostly on temporary labor concessions.

The most credible non-closure scenario is one where:
- one or more lines, shifts, or functions are removed permanently,
- duplication across plants is eliminated,
- logistics flows are simplified,
- indirect headcount is materially cut,
- and the remaining sites are brought to healthier utilization.

---

## 7) What I would treat as decisive in the model

The scenario is credible only if it can show all four:

1. **Permanent savings**
   - not just a 2–3 year effect

2. **Sufficient capacity reduction**
   - enough to lift residual utilization materially

3. **Acceptable implementation cost**
   - capex and restructuring charges do not overwhelm NPV

4. **Manageable execution risk**
   - no large quality, ramp, or supply disruption
