As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be equally clear: we recognize the pressure on the company, and we do not deny that the cost base and overcapacity must be addressed. But we will not support a restructuring that is built on plant closures in Germany, mass job losses, or a weakening of the co-determination and veto structures that protect the long-term industrial base.

Where I agree with management:
- The situation is serious.
- The company needs a credible competitiveness path.
- Investment in future products and technology is essential.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to confrontation.

Where I disagree:
- “Structural reduction” cannot become a euphemism for gutting German sites.
- Closing plants is not a first resort and, in our view, not an acceptable default.
- A one-sided labor contribution is not fair and will not be politically or socially sustainable.
- Binding logic that treats every site as expendable ignores the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.

To the works council and union: your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no closures is understandable and legitimate. I share the core objective of protecting jobs and sites. But I also need to be realistic: we will need a package that is economically credible, not just politically desirable. That means we should be open to:
- voluntary measures,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions short of closure,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and a fair, temporary contribution from labor if it is matched by contributions from owners and management.

To management: if you want the state’s support, you need to come back with a plan that preserves the company’s industrial footprint in Germany and respects the governance balance. We are not a passive investor. We have a blocking position, and we will use it if necessary. A plan that requires us to accept closures as a condition of competitiveness will face serious resistance.

What I would consider a viable direction is this:
- no plant closures without exhausting alternatives,
- no compulsory redundancies as the starting point,
- a negotiated savings package shared across all stakeholders,
- clear investment commitments for German operations,
- and a restructuring path that strengthens the company without destroying its regional base.

That is the standard I will judge any proposal against. If the company can deliver competitiveness while preserving sites and jobs through a fair social partnership, we are prepared to work constructively. If not, then I will not endorse a solution that sacrifices the state’s economy for short-term balance-sheet relief.
