From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the only acceptable endpoint is a credible, durable restoration of competitiveness and margins. We are not here to protect any particular plant, political arrangement, or management preference. We are here to protect the value of the business.

Where we agree:
- Management is right that the current cost base is too high.
- The brand needs a structurally stronger margin profile, not a temporary patch.
- “Do nothing” is not an option.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged conflict that destroys value.

Where we disagree:
- We do not accept a cosmetic compromise that preserves the status quo while calling it restructuring.
- We do not accept an outcome that leaves labor costs and capacity fundamentally unchanged.
- We do not accept prolonged strike risk, because it burns value quickly and creates uncertainty without solving the underlying problem.
- We do not accept a settlement that looks socially balanced but is economically ineffective.

To the Works Council and the union:
Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable from your standpoint, but it is not enough for investors. If your counterproposal does not deliver comparable savings and permanent capacity reduction, it simply shifts the burden into weaker margins and a lower share price. That is not an acceptable trade-off.

If you want us to support an alternative to closures, it must contain:
- real and measurable cost reduction,
- durable capacity adjustment,
- meaningful labor-cost contribution,
- and enough flexibility to make the business competitive against lower-cost rivals.

Voluntary measures alone will not convince us unless they are large enough, fast enough, and durable enough. We are not interested in delays dressed up as social responsibility.

To management:
We support a hard restructuring in principle, but you must be precise and credible. “We need savings” is not enough. The market will judge execution, not rhetoric. If closures are on the table, management should be ready to explain the economics clearly and prove that the package actually restores returns on sales. If alternative measures are chosen, they need to be at least as effective as closures, not merely less controversial.

We also need to see discipline in implementation:
- no open-ended negotiations that drag on for months,
- no agreement that front-loads concessions but leaves the cost base intact,
- no investment promises that are not tied to verifiable restructuring milestones.

To the state government and the controlling family:
We recognize your governance power, but power without economic discipline destroys shareholder value. If you block the only credible route to competitiveness, you are not protecting the enterprise; you are impairing it. We would strongly oppose any arrangement that uses control rights to freeze structural inefficiency into the company.

Our position is straightforward:
- We support a durable restructuring.
- We will back plant closures if they are truly necessary and financially justified.
- We will also back a non-closure alternative, but only if it delivers equivalent savings and capacity reduction.
- We will not support a weak compromise that protects everyone except the balance sheet.

If the outcome leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired, we will regard that as a failure.
