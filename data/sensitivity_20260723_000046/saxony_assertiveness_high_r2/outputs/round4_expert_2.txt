## What can be said now, on an industrial-model basis

### 1) Closure-based restructuring
Usually the most reliable route to:
- permanent capacity removal,
- full fixed-cost elimination,
- lower indirect headcount,
- simpler logistics,
- and the strongest NPV case by 2030.

**Typical strengths**
- highest savings certainty
- fastest structural reset if implemented cleanly
- strongest leverage on overhead and complexity

**Typical risks**
- high one-off restructuring cost
- implementation friction
- potential logistics reconfiguration costs
- political and labor resistance, which can delay savings realization

### 2) Hybrid consolidation
This can be economically close to closure-based restructuring **if** it is truly deep:
- one site sharply downsized,
- product lines consolidated,
- indirect functions removed,
- logistics redesigned,
- and excess capacity permanently taken out.

**Typical strengths**
- can preserve some footprint and capability
- may reduce implementation resistance
- can still capture a large share of savings

**Typical risks**
- savings often diluted by retained overhead
- complexity remains if too many functions stay in place
- may underperform on NPV if savings arrive later or are less certain

### 3) Non-closure restructuring
This is only credible if it removes **real structural capacity**, not just labor cost.

It can work if it includes:
- major line rationalization,
- deep overhead reduction,
- product simplification,
- significant indirect headcount cuts,
- and durable output concentration.

**Typical strengths**
- lower social disruption
- lower immediate closure cost
- potentially faster negotiation path if credible

**Typical risks**
- hardest to make equivalent to closure economics
- often leaves too much fixed cost intact
- may depend too heavily on temporary labor measures, which do not solve structural overcapacity

## My technical judgment
Without site data, the most defensible general statement is:

- **Closure-based** scenarios usually deliver the most complete and durable savings.
- **Hybrid** scenarios can often capture a substantial share of closure savings, but not always all.
- **Non-closure** scenarios are the hardest to make economically equivalent unless the company is willing to remove substantial capacity and overhead in ways that are functionally similar to closure.
