Let me be very clear: I hear the concern about closures and compulsory redundancies, but I do not accept the premise that German plants can simply be protected unchanged while the cost base remains structurally uncompetitive.

The situation is serious. Our European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our factory costs are too far above target. The brand is not meeting its return-on-sales target. If we do not take decisive action now, we will end up with a weaker company, less investment capacity, and ultimately fewer jobs in the long run.

So where I agree:
- We need a credible future for German operations.
- We need investment in future products and technology.
- We need a negotiated solution, not a unilateral diktat.
- We need burden-sharing, not just pressure on employees.

Where I disagree strongly:
- A blanket refusal of plant closures is not a workable starting point.
- A blanket refusal of compulsory redundancies, regardless of the scale of overcapacity, is not compatible with the restructuring we need.
- Binding site guarantees for all German locations through 2030, independent of economic realities, would freeze the wrong cost structure into the future.

What management needs is a structural, durable reduction in cost and capacity. That can come through closures, major capacity reductions, workforce reductions, and a genuine labour-cost contribution. If there are alternative measures that deliver equivalent savings sustainably, I am open to them. But they must be comparable in impact, not cosmetic.

To the union: I appreciate that you say you are prepared to discuss working-time and capacity measures. That is constructive. But “temporary sacrifices” will not be enough if they merely delay the problem. We need a package that permanently lowers the cost base and restores competitiveness to 2030.

So my position is:
- We are open on the instrument.
- We are not open on preserving every site at any cost.
- We are not open on keeping a structurally excessive workforce in place.
- We do need a negotiated contribution from labor, and it must be meaningful.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it has to bring a package that delivers the same effect: real capacity reduction, real labor-cost relief, and real flexibility. Otherwise, the restructuring will not be credible, and we will not get investor or owner confidence back.
