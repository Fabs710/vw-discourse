### Where I agree
I agree with the regional government, the family owner, and even the workforce side on one point: **the company needs a durable, negotiated solution**, not an imposed shock that leaves us with years of conflict. We also agree that temporary fixes are useless unless they are structurally durable.

I also agree that management must be specific. So here are the specifics:

- We face **structural overcapacity** in Germany.
- We face **labour costs that are above target** and above competitors.
- We face a **European market that is shrinking**.
- We need to restore the brand’s **return-on-sales** and free cash for future products and technology to 2030.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the argument that because closures are painful, they should be ruled out in advance. They are not a political symbol for us; they are one possible instrument. If there is a credible alternative that delivers the same scale, certainty, and durability of savings, I will consider it. But I have not heard that alternative yet.

What I have heard is:
- “no closures,”
- “no forced layoffs,”
- “no breaking of protections,”
- and “management must first prove the case.”

That is not enough.

### On the works council’s position
You ask why management keeps closure on the table. Because the numbers force us to. If the alternative measures are not big enough, then leaving the option off the table would be irresponsible. I will not pretend the problem can be solved by goodwill, training, or vague productivity promises.

You say the burden of adjustment cannot be shifted onto the workforce. I agree — **it cannot be shifted onto the workforce alone**. But the workforce contribution must still be real, measurable, and durable. Right now, I have not heard a package that closes the gap.

### On the union’s position
You are right to insist on quantified savings. I am not hiding behind vagueness. The question is not whether there is a gap — there is. The question is whether you can close it with negotiated measures that are:
- durable,
- large enough,
- and operationally reliable.

If you want a no-closure track, then show me how it delivers equivalent savings over time. Not slogans. Not generalities. Actual mechanisms.

A “temporary contribution for a limited period” may help, but if it expires before the market pressure does, then it does not solve the problem. We need **structural** cost relief, not a pause button.

### On the regional government
I understand the concern about regional industrial strength and political trust. But protecting the footprint by refusing hard choices can also hollow out the company. I will not accept a situation where the state’s preference for no closures becomes a veto on competitiveness.

If you want to support a non-closure path, then help secure:
- binding labour-cost relief,
- capacity reduction,
- productivity commitments,
- and investment discipline.

Not just job protection language. Real economics.

### On the controlling family holding
I agree with you that value destruction through overreach would be wrong. I also agree that a purely destructive closure-first strategy is not desirable. ### On institutional investors
