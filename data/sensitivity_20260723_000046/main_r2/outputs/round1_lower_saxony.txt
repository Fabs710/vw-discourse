I want to respond very clearly, because this is exactly where the board and the public interest matter.

We agree on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not denial**. The regional government is not defending inefficiency for its own sake. We understand the competitive pressure, the market shift, and the need for a credible cost base.

But I must also be equally clear: **plant closures in Germany and a restructuring built on mass job destruction are not an acceptable starting point**. As a shareholder with a blocking minority and a public mandate, I cannot support a course that hollowes out the company’s regional footprint and treats jobs as the default adjustment variable.

### Where I agree
I agree with management that:
- the cost problem is real,
- savings must be structural, not cosmetic,
- and the company needs a path back to competitiveness.

I also agree with the union and works council that:
- the burden cannot fall one-sidedly on the workforce,
- and any restructuring must be negotiated, not imposed.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the implication that the only credible path is closures and layoffs.

That is too narrow, too risky, and too destructive for a company of this importance. Once you close a plant, you do not just save costs — you destroy skills, regional trust, supplier networks, and future strategic flexibility. For a core industrial employer in our region, that is not a reversible adjustment. It is a long-term weakening.

### What I would consider acceptable
I am open to a package that includes:
- **capacity reductions without plant closures wherever possible**
- **working-time adjustments**
- **voluntary instruments**
- **redeployment and qualification**
- **temporary or time-limited labour-cost contributions**
- **product and investment commitments for the German sites**

But I want to stress: this has to be a **real industrial package**, not a cosmetic one. If employee contributions are asked for, then management and owners must also take responsibility. That includes:
- a credible investment roadmap,
- management contributions,
- and a clear commitment that German sites remain part of the company’s long-term industrial core.

### What I need from management
Before I can support any restructuring, I need answers to three questions:
1. **How much saving is actually required?**
2. **Which parts can be achieved without closures?**
3. **What binding investment and site commitments are offered in return?**

If the answer is simply “closure or nothing,” then I will not support it. We have the governance rights to prevent that, and we will use them if necessary.

### To the workforce side
I also want to be honest with the works council and union: if we want to preserve sites and jobs, there may need to be **painful but negotiated contributions**. I am not asking for sacrifice for its own sake. I am asking for a package that makes the company viable without breaking the social partnership.

So my position is:
- **No automatic endorsement of closures**
- **No acceptance of unilateral management cuts**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it preserves sites, protects as many jobs as possible, and restores competitiveness in a durable way**

If management brings a quantified alternative that achieves the same structural effect without closures, I am prepared to engage constructively. If not, I will stand with the workforce against a one-sided plan.
