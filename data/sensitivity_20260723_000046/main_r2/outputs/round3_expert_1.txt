### Independent verification panel response: what is needed to judge whether a no-closure path is credible

To determine whether non-closure measures can realistically match a closure-based restructuring, the panel needs a **minimum quantified dataset**. Without it, any conclusion would be speculative.

## 1) Core financial baseline
You need, at minimum:

- **Current cost base by plant and function**
  - direct labour
  - indirect labour / overhead
  - logistics / energy / maintenance
  - depreciation and fixed site costs
- **Current production volumes and capacity utilisation**
- **Current return-on-sales / EBIT contribution by site or product family**
- **Target cost base or margin target**
  - ideally expressed as annual recurring improvement required
- **Time horizon**
  - e.g. 2026, 2028, 2030

## 2) Restructuring measure-by-measure savings
For each proposed non-closure instrument, you need:

- **gross annual savings**
- **implementation timing**
- **duration / durability**
- **one-off transition costs**
- **net present effect if relevant**
- **execution risk**

Typical buckets:

- voluntary exits / attrition
- early retirement / partial retirement
- working-time reductions or flexibility changes
- wage moderation / deferred pay / bonus restraint
- overtime reduction
- internal redeployment / multi-skilling
- procurement and process efficiency
- management / SG&A cuts
- plant-specific productivity measures
- capacity shifts between plants

## 3) Labour-cost structure data
For workforce measures, the panel needs:

- headcount by employment group
- average fully loaded labour cost per FTE
- share of fixed vs variable labour cost
- contractual protections and notice periods
- expected attrition rate without intervention
- likely uptake rate for voluntary instruments
- severance / transfer / qualification cost per exit

## 4) Closure comparison data
To compare against closures, you need for each closure scenario:

- site fixed-cost savings
- variable-cost savings
- decommissioning and remediation costs
- severance and transition costs
- supply-chain disruption costs
- relocation or retooling costs
- lost production during transition
- strategic risk from capacity loss

This is important because **closure savings are often overstated if transition and disruption costs are ignored**.

## 5) Governance and implementation certainty
A credible assessment also needs:

- whether measures are **collectively bargained or legally binding**
- whether savings are **guaranteed or contingent**
- whether there are **re-opener clauses**
- whether the package is enforceable at site level
- what happens if targets are missed

## 6) What the panel should compare
The proper comparison is not:

- “closure vs no closure” in the abstract

It is:

1. **closure scenario**
2. **no-closure scenario**
3. **hybrid scenario**

Each measured on:

- recurring annual savings
- transition cost
- timing
- certainty
- labour peace / execution risk
- operational continuity
- net impact on competitiveness

## 7) Minimum conclusion standard
A no-closure solution is only credible if it can show:

- **savings large enough**
- **durable over several years**
- **implemented with high certainty**
- **net of transition costs**
- **without creating a larger operational or labour-relations risk than closure**

## Bottom line
At present, the dispute cannot be resolved responsibly without:

- plant-level cost data,
- quantified non-closure savings,
- closure scenario transition costs,
- and a common definition of the required residual gap.
