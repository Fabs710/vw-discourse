I will be direct.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: the company needs a **durable** restructuring, and a cosmetic compromise will not do. I also accept that the German cost base must come down and that the answer cannot be “wait and hope.”

Where I **do not** agree is your repeated insistence that closures remain the natural fallback unless others prove you wrong. That is not a neutral starting point in a company that is a regional economic anchor and governed with strong co-determination rights. If closure is to be considered at all, it must be a **last resort after verified alternatives**, not the bargaining anchor from the outset.

You still have not shown:
- the exact recurring savings gap,
- what non-closure measures already cover,
- what management itself will contribute,
- what owners will contribute,
- and what the residual gap is **net of transition costs**.

Without that, closure remains an assertion, not a demonstrated necessity.

## To the works council
I support your core red lines: no mass job destruction, no casual plant closures, no turning the workforce into the sole adjustment variable. That is consistent with the social partnership model and with the state’s responsibility as a long-term anchor shareholder.

But I also have to say this plainly: if the response is only “no closures, no redundancies” without a fully quantified alternative, then we will not get a sustainable settlement. The company must be able to recover competitiveness. So I support your resistance, but I need it paired with a credible, measurable package that can actually carry the business forward.

## To the union
I share your insistence that any workforce contribution must be tied to binding guarantees. Temporary contributions without secure conditions would simply postpone the next conflict.

At the same time, I do not support framing every capacity adjustment as an attack on labour rights. We need:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and negotiated labour-cost moderation.

But these must be embedded in a settlement that protects jobs and sites in Germany, not in an open-ended process that keeps the closure sword hanging over everyone.

## To the controlling family holding
I agree that the company cannot preserve every existing structure and still restore competitiveness. But I also caution you against treating closures as an efficient technical tool detached from regional and governance realities. In this company, plant decisions are not just operational choices; they have industrial, political, and social consequences, and the special statute and co-determination make that plain.

If you want a credible settlement, then support a process that:
- verifies the real savings gap,
- tests a no-closure route first,
- and only then considers footprint reduction if the gap remains substantial.

That is not weakness. That is responsible ownership.

## To institutional investors
You are right to demand durability, scale, and certainty. But certainty obtained through industrial conflict, damaged trust, and regional backlash is not value-maximizing. A company like this does not become stronger by breaking the social compact that holds its German footprint together.

I will support a package only if it is:
- financially credible,
- structurally durable,
- and consistent with the company’s role as a regional industrial anchor.

I will not support a settlement that improves next quarter’s optics while hollowing out the company’s long-term position in Germany.

## My position
I remain open to a negotiated restructuring, but only under these conditions:

1. **A quantified, independent verification of the savings gap**
2. **A serious no-closure scenario tested first**
3. **Binding management and owner contributions**
4. **A workforce contribution tied to site and job security**
5. **No compulsory redundancies as the default instrument**
6. **No weakening of co-determination or the state’s veto role**
7. **A credible investment and product roadmap through 2030**

What I will not accept:
- plant closures as the starting assumption,
- mass layoffs as the preferred adjustment tool,
- or a deal that shifts the burden onto the workforce while leaving management and owners insulated.

## [SUMMON: independent verification panel on restructuring economics and site impact]
We need an independent panel to verify:
- the recurring savings target,
- savings from non-closure measures,
- management and owner-side contributions,
- transition costs,
- and the residual gap if closures are excluded.

And importantly, the panel must also assess the **regional economic impact** of closure scenarios, not just the accounting effect.

## Bottom line
The state is not blocking restructuring.  
We are blocking **industrial hollowing-out presented as necessity**.

If management can prove that a non-closure path is insufficient, we will assess that honestly. But until then, closure is not a justified default, and I will not allow this process to be driven there by pressure alone.
