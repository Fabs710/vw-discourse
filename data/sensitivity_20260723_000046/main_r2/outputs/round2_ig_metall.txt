I will respond directly and plainly.

### To management
You still have not answered the central question in a way that is usable for bargaining: **what exactly is the savings gap, and how much of it is truly non-negotiable?**

You keep saying closures may be necessary, but you are not separating:
- structural savings needed,
- savings achievable through labour-cost moderation,
- savings from management and overhead cuts,
- savings from owners’ contribution,
- and the residual gap.

Without that breakdown, “closure” remains a threat, not a quantified necessity. We will not negotiate under a blank cheque for plant destruction.

And let me be very clear: **if your plan depends on compulsory redundancies or breaking the collective wage framework, then it is not a settlement proposal we can accept.** That is shifting the crisis entirely onto the workforce.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of protections,
- no unilateral management diktat.

I also agree that “temporary” contributions must not become permanent wage dumping. A workforce contribution only makes sense if it is tied to:
- binding site security,
- binding employment security,
- and binding investment.

Where I would add one point: the package must be durable enough to survive beyond the next quarter and beyond the next management mood. If we contribute, it has to buy a real future, not just delay the next attack.

### To the regional state government
Your position is constructive insofar as you reject plant closures as the first answer. But “wherever possible” is too soft if it leaves open a backdoor to closures once the pressure rises.

If the state is serious, it should use its blocking power to insist on:
- a **binding no-closure track for German sites**,
- a **2030 employment and investment pact**,
- and a **credible industrial plan** that replaces scare tactics with negotiated adjustment.

### To the controlling family holding
I hear your point that the company needs structural savings and that a symbolic deal is worthless. That is fair. But I reject your attempt to keep closure as the default bargaining anchor while asking the workforce to prove a better path under threat.

If you want credibility, then support a process that starts from:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- quantified labour contributions,
- management and owner contributions,
- and independent verification of the savings.

If that process fails to close the gap, then we can discuss next steps. But we are not accepting closure as the starting assumption.

### To institutional investors
You are right that vague promises and endless conflict destroy value. But the solution is not to force through a destructive cut and call it discipline. A company of this scale does not get stable margins by undermining labour peace and detonating trust.

What you should support is a package that is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **implemented quickly**
- **and socially stable**

That means a real burden-sharing deal, not a one-sided restructuring dressed up as necessity.

### Our position
Let me state it as a bargaining line, not a slogan:

We are prepared to discuss a **binding shared-sacrifice package** that includes:
- labour-cost moderation for a limited and negotiated period,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary instruments instead of forced layoffs,
- capacity reduction through socially responsible means,
- redeployment and qualification,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner-side investment commitments,
- and a clear guarantee that **no German plant is closed and no compulsory redundancies occur**.

What we are **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures,
- forced layoffs,
- unilateral wage-framework destruction,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or employees carrying the full burden while management and owners contribute little or nothing.

If management wants a deal, it needs to come back with numbers and with equal burden-sharing. If not, then it should expect resistance, including industrial action. That is not a threat for effect; it is the reality of the expired peace obligation and the mandate from our members.
