I will be equally direct.

I agree with management on the core diagnosis: **the company’s German cost base is too high, and the restructuring has to be structural, not cosmetic**. I also agree with the regional government that this is not a case for denial or delay. If competitiveness is not restored, the long-term damage will be greater than any short-term pain.

Where I do **not** agree is with any approach that treats **plant closures as the only credible instrument**. That may be one possible lever, but it is not a preferred one, and it is not automatically acceptable just because it is administratively simple. A closure is not just a cost item; it is a permanent loss of industrial capability, political trust, and strategic flexibility. As a controlling owner, I am not prepared to support value destruction in the name of “decisiveness.”

To the works council and union: I hear your red lines, but I cannot accept a position that amounts to **no closures, no forced layoffs, no fundamental changes, yet full competitiveness restored by some unspecified alternative**. That is not a viable economic answer. If we want site security, we need a package that actually reduces costs and capacity in a durable way. That may require:
- meaningful working-time changes,
- wage or bonus moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and a real reduction in overcapacity.

What I am looking for is not a symbolic compromise, but a **credible industrial settlement** that protects long-term value. That means three things:

1. **Structural savings of sufficient scale**  
   I do not want temporary concessions that expire before the market pressure does.

2. **Durable labour peace**  
   A deal that simply postpones conflict is not worth much if it leads to recurring strikes and uncertainty.

3. **Protection of the core industrial base**  
   I am open to painful measures, but not to a course that permanently weakens the company’s ability to earn returns and invest.

So my position is this:

- **I do not support a blanket commitment against closures in all circumstances.**
- **I do support a negotiated solution that avoids closures if it genuinely delivers equivalent structural savings.**
- **I do not support management simply imposing a one-sided restructuring without real negotiation and without a binding future-for-sites component.**

Management now needs to answer the key question with actual numbers: **what is the required savings volume, what can be achieved without closures, and what is the residual gap?** If that gap is truly unavoidable, then we will need to discuss whether closures are justified. If it can be closed through negotiated measures, then that is preferable.

But I will not endorse an arrangement that preserves every existing guarantee while leaving the economic problem unresolved. Nor will I support a plan that damages the company’s long-term value through an overly destructive restructuring.

That is the line.
