Core impasse identified

The dispute has converged on one central blockage:

- **Management, investors, and the controlling owner** are requiring a **quantified, durable savings bridge** and are not willing to exclude closures unless the no-closure route is shown to be economically equivalent.
- **Works council, union, and state** are requiring **binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff guarantees** before accepting workforce contributions.
- As a result, both sides are effectively asking the other to **move first**:
  - labor wants security before concessions,
  - management wants concessions before security.

So the deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **sequencing, verification, and enforceability**.

Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Verified no-closure pilot with hard fallback trigger
A time-limited package could be tested for a defined period, with:
- plant-specific non-closure measures,
- binding job and site protections for that period,
- independent verification of realized savings,
- and a pre-agreed fallback mechanism if the verified residual gap remains above a set threshold.

This would let labor test a protected no-closure path, while giving management/investors a measurable off-ramp if the numbers do not close.

### Proposal 2: Three-layer burden-sharing package
A compromise structure could require simultaneous contributions from all sides:
- **workforce**: temporary labor-cost moderation, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits,
- **management**: overhead cuts, executive restraint, simplified governance,
- **owners/investors**: capital support, investment commitments, patience on returns.

The key is that no single party bears the whole adjustment, and each contribution is tied to a verified savings schedule.

### Proposal 3: Conditional no-closure commitment with protected review point
A binding commitment to keep German plants open could be made **conditional on measurable milestones**:
- milestone 1: verified savings from non-closure measures,
- milestone 2: investment and productivity delivery,
- milestone 3: review of residual gap.

If milestones are met, no-closure continues through the agreement horizon. If they fail, the parties reopen only a narrowly defined set of options under co-determined review.

Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
What specific combination of temporary workforce measures would you accept if it were legally tied to binding site guarantees, binding investment, and an independent residual-gap test?

### To Industrial Union
Would you support a time-limited, enforceable contribution package if it included hard guarantees against compulsory redundancies, plus a pre-agreed independent review of savings delivery?

### To Group & Brand Management
What exact residual gap remains after your preferred non-closure measures, and at what threshold would you commit to ruling out closures for the agreement period?

### To Regional State Government
Would you be prepared to convert your political support for no closures into a formally binding conditional guarantee, contingent on verified savings and investment milestones?

### To Controlling Family Holding
What level of owner-side contribution and governance restraint would you commit to before insisting that footprint reduction remains on the table?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you accept a negotiated no-closure outcome if independent verification showed it delivered the same recurring savings, but with lower execution and reputational risk?

### To Expert Panel
What minimum evidence standard would you require before concluding that non-closure measures are either sufficient or insufficient?
