- workforce: labor-cost moderation, working-time changes, voluntary instruments

- owners: investment commitment and restraint on distributions

   - shift and premium changes
   - benefit/guarantee adjustments
   - voluntary exits and attrition

2. **non-labour measures**
   - management overhead cuts
   - procurement and logistics savings
   - capex deferral or reallocation
   - footprint / capacity rationalisation short of closure

3. **structural measures**
   - plant closure
   - line removal
   - major product or volume shifts

Without that decomposition, “closures are necessary” is not yet a verified conclusion.

---

### 2) What is economically plausible
In large German automotive plants, **non-closure measures can produce meaningful savings**, but there are limits.

Typical constraints are:

- **Payroll savings from working-time or wage concessions are real but finite**
- **Voluntary exits lower headcount gradually, not instantly**
- **Overhead savings are often smaller than plant-level labour savings**
- **Guarantee or protection extensions can reduce flexibility and may offset some savings**
- **If installed capacity is materially excessive, labour measures alone often do not close the gap**

So, if management is facing a **structural overcapacity problem**, then a purely labour-based solution may be **insufficient unless the required gap is moderate** or volume recovery is assumed.

---

The parties need to answer this in numbers:

> After counting all realistic non-closure measures, what annual recurring saving remains unmet?

That residual gap determines whether closures are financially necessary.

If the residual gap is:

- **small to moderate**: a negotiated non-closure settlement may be viable
- **large and persistent**: closures or equivalent footprint reduction become more plausible
- **driven mainly by temporary earnings pressure**: then permanent closures may be economically excessive

### 4) What I would treat as a red flag
I would be cautious if management is using any of the following without verification:

- **headline savings that are gross, not net**
- **one-off severance effects presented as recurring operating savings**
- **capacity reduction claims without showing volume assumptions**
- **labour-cost comparisons that ignore product mix, utilization, and premium structures**
- **closure arguments that do not separate fixed-cost relief from transition costs**

A closure proposal is only economically credible if the company can show that:
- the savings are **recurring**
- the transition costs are **fully included**
- and the remaining footprint is **actually supportable at expected volumes**
