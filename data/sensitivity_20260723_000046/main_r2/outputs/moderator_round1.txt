### Core impasse

The discourse is stuck on one central conflict:

**Management, backed most explicitly by capital-market investors and partly by the controlling owner, is demanding structural savings of a scale it associates with closures or at least major capacity reduction.**  
**Works council, union, and regional government are rejecting closures and compulsory redundancies as the primary adjustment mechanism and insist on negotiated, socially managed alternatives with site and job security.**

So the deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed — **all sides accept that it is** — but over:

1. **the acceptable instruments**  
   - closures / layoffs vs. negotiated capacity reduction

2. **the required scale and certainty of savings**  
   - management and investors want hard, durable numbers  
   - workforce and state want equivalent savings without plant destruction

3. **the trade-off structure**  
   - workforce wants security first, contribution second  
   - management and investors want contribution first, guarantees conditional  
   - state and family want a middle path but need quantified feasibility

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the gap

#### 1) Quantified no-closure restructuring with a verification trigger
A possible bridge would be a **no-closure restructuring package for a fixed negotiation window**, but only if it is tied to:

- a **fully quantified savings target**
- **binding employee contributions**
- **binding management / owner commitments**
- and an **independent verification mechanism**

If the package fails to deliver the agreed savings by a defined checkpoint, then the parties would reopen discussion on more far-reaching measures. This gives the workforce a chance to prove an alternative can work, while giving management and investors a safeguard against open-ended delay.

#### 2) Site-security pact with differentiated treatment
A second compromise would be a **tiered site agreement**:

- core sites receive **guaranteed protection through 2030**
- weaker or overcapacity areas receive **capacity reductions through voluntary exits, working-time changes, reassignment, and temporary reductions**
- management receives **clear operational flexibility and productivity commitments**
- public-side and owner-side actors back **investment commitments for future products**

This would avoid blanket closure logic while acknowledging that not every location may be treated identically.

#### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with explicit burden-sharing ratios
A third option would be a **burden-sharing framework** in which each stakeholder group pre-commits to a defined contribution:

- workforce: labor-cost moderation, working-time changes, voluntary instruments
- management: overhead cuts, leadership cost restraint, governance discipline
- owners: investment commitment and restraint on distributions
- public side: support for qualification, transformation, and transition measures

This approach would not solve the dispute by slogans, but by making the negotiations concrete and comparable.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
**What specific mix of measures would you accept that is strong enough to replace closures in quantified savings terms, while still remaining compatible with your red lines on no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures?**

#### To Industrial Union
**Which labour-cost and flexibility contributions could you commit to in a binding package, and for what duration, so that the savings are durable enough for management and investors to treat them as equivalent to structural cuts?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the exact savings gap you say must be closed, and which portion of that gap cannot be bridged without closures — in other words, what is your quantified residual after all alternative measures are counted?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What specific governance or political support are you prepared to provide to make a non-closure restructuring credible, and what measurable conditions would trigger your support or withdrawal of support?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure negotiation track if it is backed by independent verification and a pre-agreed fallback mechanism, and if so, what minimum safeguards would you require?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What exact financial metrics — savings, margin improvement, timing, and certainty — would be sufficient for you to endorse a negotiated compromise instead of insisting on closure-based restructuring?**
