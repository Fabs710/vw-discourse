I will be direct.

The regional government is **not prepared to endorse a restructuring that relies on plant closures or mass job destruction in Germany** as the primary adjustment mechanism. That is not a reasonable starting point for a company that is a central economic anchor in our state, and it would be politically and economically irresponsible.

At the same time, I am **not defending the status quo**. Management is right that the company needs a durable restructuring, not slogans. The cost base must come down, and it must come down in a way that is structurally credible. But the burden cannot be placed one-sidedly on the workforce, and it cannot be solved by threatening closures first and asking everyone else to prove an alternative under duress.

## Where I agree

### To management
I agree that:
- the competitive pressure is real,
- temporary fixes are not enough,
- and a serious package must produce durable savings.

I also agree that any alternative to closures must be **quantified** and **credible**. The expert is right on that point. We need numbers, not rhetoric.

### To the works council and union
I agree that:
- forced redundancies are not acceptable as a default instrument,
- a negotiated solution is the right framework,
- and the German sites need a real industrial future, not just a managed retreat.

I also agree that any employee contribution must be matched by commitments from management and owners. Workforce sacrifice without reciprocal responsibility would be unfair and unstable.

### To the expert
I welcome the verification logic. It is exactly the right next step. We need to distinguish:
- recurring savings from one-off effects,
- labour-cost relief from capacity effects,
- and genuine structural improvement from temporary postponement.

## Where I disagree

### Against management’s implicit closure-first logic
What I do **not** accept is the notion that because savings must be structural, closures are therefore the default answer. That leap is not justified yet.

If non-closure measures can close the gap, then closures would be a self-inflicted loss of skills, trust, and regional economic capacity. Once a plant is gone, it is gone. That is not a reversible line item.

### Against any attempt to make jobs the only adjustment variable
I also do **not** accept a restructuring that preserves management freedom, ownership comfort, and investor expectations while treating the workforce as the sole source of adjustment. That would fail on fairness and would likely fail in implementation as well.

## What I expect now

Management must put forward a **fully quantified restructuring package** that shows:

1. **the exact savings gap**
2. **what share can be achieved without closures**
3. **what burden management itself will carry**
4. **what owners are prepared to commit**
5. **what investment roadmap secures the German sites through 2030**

If you cannot demonstrate that closures are the only remaining option after all alternatives are counted, then you do **not** have a case for closures.

## My position on the possible path forward

I am prepared to support a package that includes:
- capacity reduction through socially responsible measures,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary but meaningful labour-cost contributions,
- management overhead restraint,
- and binding investment commitments.

But that package must also include:
- **site security**
- **job security**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no unilateral dismantling of co-determination rights**
- and a clear commitment that German plants remain part of the company’s long-term industrial core.

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- closure as the primary restructuring tool,
- mass layoffs,
- a one-sided cost cut imposed on employees,
- or a deal that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are not acceptable outcomes, and I will say so openly.

## [SUMMON: independent financial and industrial restructuring verification panel]
We now need a joint verification step that does not rely on management’s assertions alone. I propose a **small independent panel** to verify:
- required annual savings,
- savings achievable through non-closure measures,
- net impact of labour contributions,
- and the residual gap after all alternatives.

Only then can we responsibly assess whether closures are genuinely unavoidable or just being used as leverage.

## Bottom line
The state is open to a serious restructuring.  
We are **not** open to industrial hollowing-out in the name of competitiveness.

If there is a credible non-closure solution, we will back it. If there is not, management must prove that fact with numbers, not threats.
