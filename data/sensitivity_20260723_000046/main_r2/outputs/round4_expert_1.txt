## Independent verification view

From a restructuring and labour-cost verification standpoint, the debate is now correctly focused on **quantification**, not slogans.

### What is already clear
- All parties accept that restructuring is needed.
- The dispute is about **instrument choice**: closures/layoffs versus negotiated non-closure measures.
- The real test is whether a **non-closure package** can deliver:
  - durable recurring savings,
  - enough capacity reduction,
  - acceptable transition costs,
  - and implementation certainty comparable to a closure-based plan.

### What cannot be verified yet from the information given
No one has provided the minimum financial data needed to determine whether closures are economically unavoidable. In particular, we need:

1. **Current labour cost base**
   - total annual personnel cost
   - wage/salary bill
   - variable pay
   - social charges
   - pension/accrual effects
   - temporary labour and contractor cost

2. **Restructuring target**
   - exact annual recurring savings required
   - by when the target must be reached
   - whether the target is gross or net of inflation and volume effects

3. **Measure-by-measure savings**
   - voluntary exits / early retirement
   - working-time reductions
   - wage moderation or temporary concessions
   - redeployment and qualification effects
   - management overhead reductions
   - procurement/process efficiencies
   - plant-specific fixed-cost reductions

4. **Transition and exit costs**
   - severance
   - social plan cost
   - transfer company / bridging payments
   - retraining
   - write-downs
   - decommissioning / site shutdown cost
   - working-capital and logistics impacts

5. **Capacity and utilisation data**
   - plant-by-plant output
   - utilisation rates
   - break-even volumes
   - model allocation
   - fixed-cost absorption effects

6. **Scenario comparison**
   - no-closure scenario
   - hybrid scenario
   - closure scenario
   - each with net present cost, timing, and execution risk

### Verification standard
A credible assessment would compare scenarios on a **net recurring basis**, not just gross headcount reduction. That means:

- savings must be **annual and recurring**
- one-off severance must be separated from structural savings
- productivity gains must be evidenced, not assumed
- labour-cost relief must be tested against:
  - inflation
  - volume decline
  - mix change
  - and future pay progression

### Key analytical point
A non-closure package can be economically equivalent to closures **only if** it delivers:

- sufficient fixed-cost reduction,
- enough permanent workforce reduction or hours reduction,
- no large hidden backfill cost,
- and enforcement mechanisms that make the savings durable.

If the savings depend mainly on:
- one-time concessions,
- temporary wage restraint,
- or voluntary measures with weak take-up,
then they are usually **not equivalent** to structural footprint reduction.

### What would settle the question
To determine whether closures are truly necessary, I would need:
- a site-by-site cost model,
- the management business case,
- the union/works council counterproposal,
- and a jointly agreed baseline for recurring savings.

Without that, the best conclusion is only this:

> **It is not yet proven that closures are economically unavoidable, but it is also not yet proven that non-closure measures can close the gap.**
