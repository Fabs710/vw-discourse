We agree with several points made so far, but not with the way some parties are trying to frame the trade-off.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current cost base is not competitive and that the problem is structural, not cosmetic.
- **The expert is right** that we cannot responsibly judge “closures vs. no closures” without a quantified bridge showing the recurring savings gap.
- **The family owner and state are right** that a purely destructive closure-first approach can destroy value if it goes beyond what is economically necessary.
- **The workforce side is right** that prolonged conflict is value-destructive and that any solution has to be durable, not just a short-term patch.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the works council’s and union’s insistence on a blanket no-closure / no-layoff posture as a starting point. From a shareholder perspective, that is only acceptable if it is proven to deliver **equivalent recurring savings** and a credible return to competitive margins.

At present, I do **not** see that proof.

If the package mainly protects existing guarantees while offering only temporary or symbolic concessions, then it is a cosmetic settlement. That would leave margins structurally impaired, and that is unacceptable to us.

We also do **not** support prolonged industrial confrontation as a bargaining strategy. Strikes, uncertainty, and delayed implementation directly destroy equity value and customer confidence.

### What we need
The right question is not “closures or no closures?” in the abstract. The question is:

**What is the recurring annual savings gap, what non-closure measures close it, and what residual gap remains?**

Until that is answered, no one should pretend they have a responsible answer.

### Our position
We are prepared to back a negotiated restructuring **if and only if** it delivers:
1. **durable cost reduction**
2. **material capacity reduction**
3. **a clear path back to competitive margins**
4. **implementation certainty**

If a non-closure package can genuinely deliver the same financial effect, that is preferable. But “preferable” does not mean “less disruptive on paper”; it means **equivalent in scale, durability, and certainty**.

If it cannot, then management cannot be forced into preserving an uncompetitive structure in the name of social peace.

### To the workforce side
If you want site and job security, then the contribution has to be real and measurable. We can accept flexibility, working-time adjustments, voluntary exits, and targeted labour-cost moderation. What we cannot accept is a deal that keeps the economics broken and asks shareholders to absorb the consequence through lower returns and lower valuation.

### To management
You need to present the numbers more clearly:
- required annual recurring savings
- expected savings from labour measures
- expected savings from overhead and process measures
- residual gap after alternatives
- and the effect on return-on-sales

Without that, you are asking the market to trust a restructuring story that is not yet financeable.

### To the expert
Your verification approach is exactly the right one. We support that. Please quantify whether a non-closure route can actually restore competitiveness, not just reduce tension.

### Bottom line
We are not ideologically attached to closures. But we are even less attached to preserving an uncompetitive cost structure.

A negotiated solution is acceptable only if it is financially hard, structurally durable, and capable of restoring margins. Otherwise, it is not a restructuring; it is delay.
