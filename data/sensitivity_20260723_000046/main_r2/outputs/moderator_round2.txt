## Core impasse

The central impasse is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All parties accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the burden of proof and the acceptable adjustment instrument**:

1. **Management / investors / (partly) the controlling family** want a restructuring that produces **structural, recurring savings** at sufficient scale, and do not accept a package that leaves the cost base too high.
2. **Works council / union / state** refuse to start from closures and compulsory redundancies, and want **binding guarantees** before any workforce contribution.
3. The **missing bridge** is a **quantified, independently verified package** showing whether non-closure measures can close the savings gap, and if not, what residual gap remains.

So the real deadlock is:

- **Security first** vs. **economics first**
- **No closures as a premise** vs. **closures as a possible last resort**
- **Trust-based bargaining** vs. **proof-based bargaining**

## Facilitation proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Independent verification package before any final commitment
A neutral expert panel would verify:
- the recurring annual savings gap,
- savings from labour measures,
- savings from management overhead and process measures,
- savings from owners’ contribution,
- and the residual gap under a **no-closure scenario**.

**Purpose:** This allows all sides to argue from the same numbers rather than competing assumptions.

**Possible compromise logic:**  
If the verified residual gap is small enough, the parties pursue a non-closure settlement. If it remains substantial, the parties revisit footprint reduction with clearer evidence.

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional non-closure settlement with review triggers
The parties could agree to a **time-limited, binding package** that includes:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time and flexibility adjustments,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- management overhead reductions,
- investment commitments,
- and a strong site/job security framework.

This would be paired with:
- **objective review triggers** at set milestones,
- a **joint monitoring committee**,
- and a **re-opener clause** if agreed financial targets are missed.

**Purpose:** This gives the workforce security and gives management a credible path to savings.

**Possible compromise logic:**  
No closure is implemented upfront, but the agreement is not open-ended; it is conditional on measurable performance.

---

### Proposal 3: Structural savings sharing model
A negotiated package could allocate burden across all sides:
- workforce contribution,
- management cuts,
- owner support,
- and operational efficiency measures.

It would include:
- explicit percentages or amounts for each side,
- a binding investment roadmap,
- and a defined cap on workforce sacrifice.

**Purpose:** This directly addresses the complaint that the workforce should not carry the full burden.

**Possible compromise logic:**  
If all stakeholders contribute proportionally, then the deal becomes more politically and socially durable.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact residual annual savings gap remains after all non-closure measures, and how have you calculated it net of transition costs?**

### To the works council
**What specific, quantified workforce measures would you accept if they were tied to binding site and job guarantees and independently verified savings targets?**

### To the union
**Would you accept a time-limited labour contribution package if it were legally linked to no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and automatic reversion if management misses its investment commitments?**

### To the regional state government
**Are you prepared to use your shareholder and political leverage to support a binding no-closure track if independent verification shows it can close the savings gap?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Will you support a structured verification process that temporarily removes closure from the bargaining table while the numbers are tested, or do you require closure to remain an active option throughout?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of recurring savings and implementation certainty would make you support a non-closure settlement as financially credible?**

### To the expert
**What minimum data set is required to determine whether non-closure measures can credibly match the savings from plant closures?**
