## To the works council
You are still avoiding the central issue.

I do **not** need a lecture on whether restructuring is “needed.” I agree it is needed. What I have not heard from you is a **credible, quantified alternative** that closes the structural cost gap at the scale and durability required.

You keep saying:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of guarantees,

but that is only a position. It is **not** a solution unless it produces the same recurring savings as footprint reduction.

So here is the blunt truth:  
If you want to rule out closures in advance, then you must show how you will deliver:
- material labor-cost relief,
- material capacity reduction,
- and durable productivity gains,

not just temporary pain-sharing.

I am open to non-closure restructuring. I am **not** open to a package that preserves the current cost base and calls that compromise.

## To the union
I respect the fact that you want binding protections. But you are still asking management to surrender the principal restructuring lever before the economics are proven.

That is not a negotiation; that is a veto request.

You say temporary contributions must not become permanent wage dumping. Fine. Then make the contribution package **measurable, time-limited, and tied to delivery**. But you cannot ask for:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no structural change,
- and full wage security,

while still expecting the business to recover competitiveness.

The market will not wait for us to preserve every legacy feature. If the company does not get a durable cost reset, then the alternative is continued erosion, and that is worse for everyone.

## To the regional state government
I understand your political responsibility. But I reject the idea that the state’s role is to pre-empt hard decisions and freeze the footprint.

You keep saying closures should be a last resort. I agree. They should be a last resort. But “last resort” does not mean “never,” and it certainly does not mean that the burden of proof is impossible to meet.

If you want a serious no-closure path, support one that actually reduces:
- labor cost,
- overhead,
- and capacity.

Do not confuse regional protection with competitiveness. Without competitiveness, there is no industrial base to protect.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. On that we agree.

Where I disagree is your tendency to treat closure as something to be proven only after every other option has been exhausted in practice. That can become a recipe for delay, and delay is exactly what this company cannot afford.

We need a decision that restores earnings power in time to support the 2030 product and technology plan. That means:
- structural savings,
- a smaller cost base,
- and enough operational flexibility to execute.

If non-closure measures can do that, excellent. If they cannot, then you should not insist on preserving every site merely for the appearance of stability.

## To institutional investors
You are right to insist on recurring savings, durability, and certainty. I agree with that fully.

But I will also be direct: a settlement that avoids closures while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive is **not** value protection. It is deferred damage.

I am not asking you to support closures for their own sake. I am asking you to support the **economically credible** route. If non-closure measures are enough, fine. If not, then pretending otherwise is irresponsible.

## My position, clearly
I will support a negotiated settlement if it delivers:
- structurally durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- measurable labor-cost contribution,
- management restraint,
- and a credible path to restore returns by 2030.

I will **not** accept:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a package that rules out compulsory redundancies regardless of the numbers,
- or a deal that preserves high fixed costs and calls that social responsibility.

If the non-closure route proves sufficient, then it can stand on its own merits.  
If it does not, then we must be able to act. That is the reality.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate hard on **how** savings are achieved.  
I am **not** prepared to negotiate on **whether** the savings must be large, recurring, and structurally meaningful.

That is the line.
