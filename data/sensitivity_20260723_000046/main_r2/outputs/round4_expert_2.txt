## Independent verification response

The direct question to my area is: **what minimum evidence is required to determine whether non-closure measures can credibly match the savings from plant closures?**

### Short answer
At present, **it is not verifiable from the information provided whether closures are economically unavoidable**. The debate has been framed correctly, but the necessary cost and capacity data are still missing.

What can be said now is:

- **Verified:** all sides accept restructuring is needed.
- **Verified:** the dispute is about the instrument and sequencing, not the existence of the problem.
- **Not yet verified:** that plant closures are the only way to achieve the required savings.
- **Not yet verified:** that a no-closure package can close the gap at comparable durability and certainty.

---

## Minimum evidence set needed

To judge a non-closure plan against closures, I would need the following, site-by-site and consolidated:

### 1) Current cost base
- annual personnel cost
- wage and salary bill
- employer social charges
- pensions/accrual effects
- temporary labour and contractor spend
- plant-level fixed overheads

### 2) Management’s restructuring target
- exact recurring annual savings required
- timing of achievement
- whether the target is gross or net of inflation, volume decline, and model mix effects

### 3) Measure-by-measure savings table
For each proposed non-closure measure:
- voluntary exits / early retirement
- working-time reduction
- wage moderation or temporary concessions
- redeployment and qualification
- management overhead cuts
- procurement/process improvements
- logistics and footprint rationalization short of closure

Each item should show:
- annual recurring saving
- one-off cost
- implementation timing
- execution risk
- expected take-up rate

### 4) Transition and exit costs
For closures or large cuts:
- severance
- social plan cost
- bridging / transfer payments
- retraining
- decommissioning
- write-downs
- logistics disruption
- working-capital effects

### 5) Plant-level capacity and utilization
- output by plant
- utilization rates
- break-even volume
- model allocation
- fixed-cost absorption
- ability to reassign production elsewhere

### 6) Scenario comparison
At minimum, three scenarios:
1. **No-closure**
2. **Hybrid**
3. **Closure-based**

For each:
- annual recurring savings
- one-off cost
- net present value
- timing
- execution certainty
- industrial-relations risk
- operational disruption risk

---

## Verification standard

A non-closure package is only credibly comparable to closures if it delivers:

- **durable recurring savings**
- **permanent or effectively permanent capacity reduction**
- **enforceable labor-cost relief**
- **low backfill risk**
- **implementation certainty similar to a closure plan**

A package relying mainly on:
- temporary concessions,
- voluntary exits with uncertain take-up,
- or one-off savings,

would usually **not** be economically equivalent to structural footprint reduction.

## Analytical conclusion

The present evidence supports only a limited conclusion:

> **It is not yet proven that closures are necessary, but it is also not yet proven that non-closure measures can fully close the gap.**

That means the right next step is not rhetorical escalation; it is a **jointly agreed, independently verified scenario model**.
