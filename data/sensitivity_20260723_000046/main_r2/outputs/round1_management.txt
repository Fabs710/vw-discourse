I hear the message from works council and union: you want security, no closures, no forced layoffs, and a negotiated path. I’m not dismissing the need for a viable industrial future or the need for investment. On those points, we do share one basic premise: **the company needs a durable solution, not a temporary patch**.

Where I disagree is on the starting assumptions and on what is realistic.

### First: the cost reality cannot be negotiated away
German factory costs are not “a bit above target”; they are structurally too high for the market we are now in. Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our brand is missing its return target. If we leave the cost base fundamentally unchanged, we will not be protecting the industrial base — we will be eroding it over time.

So I need to be blunt: **a package with no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no meaningful labour-cost contribution does not solve the problem**. It preserves the appearance of stability while leaving the company uncompetitive.

### Second: structural change has to be real
I am not insisting on closures as an ideological goal. I am insisting on **durable capacity reduction and structural cost savings**. If there are alternative measures that achieve the same scale and permanence of savings, then I am prepared to examine them seriously.

But I do not accept:
- cosmetic savings,
- short-term givebacks that expire too quickly,
- voluntary-only measures that do not close the gap,
- or promises of future efficiency that are not backed by hard numbers.

### Third: employee contributions must be meaningful
If the workforce is asking for site guarantees and job security through 2030, then there has to be a credible contribution on labour cost, working time, flexibility, or capacity. That contribution cannot be symbolic. It has to move the economics.

That means we need to discuss, concretely:
- lower labour costs,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity increases,
- capacity rationalisation,
- and, where unavoidable, workforce reductions through socially managed instruments.

### Fourth: investment requires a stronger base
I agree that we must invest in future products and technology. But that is exactly why we cannot afford an overextended cost structure. Every euro tied up in excess capacity is a euro not available for the transformation to 2030.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate a **durable restructuring package** that avoids closures **if and only if** it delivers:
1. a **clear, structural reduction in capacity**,
2. a **genuine and measurable labour-cost contribution**,
3. and a **credible path back to competitiveness and margins**.

If the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no guarantees changed, but the company should somehow become competitive anyway,” then that is not a solution I can accept.

So I ask both of you directly:  
**What concrete, quantified package do you put on the table that delivers the same structural savings as closures would?**
