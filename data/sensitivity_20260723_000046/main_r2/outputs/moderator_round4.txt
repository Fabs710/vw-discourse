## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not whether restructuring is needed**. All sides accept that.  
The impasse is **what must be proven before any site closure or compulsory redundancy is considered**.

More specifically, the sides are split on three linked questions:

1. **Reference scenario**
   - Management, investors, and the owner side want a comparison that keeps footprint reduction as a real option.
   - Works council, union, and state want verification to start from a **no-closure baseline**.

2. **Burden of proof**
   - Employee and public actors want management to prove that closures are unavoidable.
   - Management and investors want the workforce side to prove that non-closure measures can close the gap.

3. **Sequencing of concessions**
   - Labor wants binding site/job security first, then contributions.
   - Management wants measurable labor-cost and capacity relief first, then guarantees.

The dispute is therefore a **sequencing and verification impasse**, not a disagreement over the need to restructure.

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

### 1) Scenario-first package with binding decision gates
**Proposal:**  
Create one independent verification process with three formally equal scenarios:
- no-closure
- hybrid
- closure-based

The panel reports on:
- recurring savings
- transition costs
- industrial-relations risk
- implementation certainty

Then the parties pre-agree **decision gates**:
- If the no-closure scenario reaches a defined savings threshold, closures are off the table.
- If it does not, the parties move to a second-stage negotiation on limited footprint adjustment.
- Any closure discussion is therefore contingent, not automatic.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It gives labor a genuine no-closure test, while giving management and investors a path if the gap remains material.

---

### 2) No-closure first, but with quantified fallback triggers
**Proposal:**  
Agree a provisional no-closure restructuring package now, including:
- working-time changes
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- management overhead cuts
- owner contribution

Attach a **fallback clause**:
- If independently verified savings fall short by a specified amount on a specified date, the parties reopen the footprint question under pre-agreed constraints.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It protects employees from immediate closure pressure while avoiding an open-ended promise that the gap can always be closed without structural adjustment.

---

### 3) Guaranteed protection corridor in exchange for measurable sacrifice
**Proposal:**  
Negotiate a time-limited settlement with:
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
- no German plant closures during that period
- employee contribution package
- management/owner contribution package
- investment and qualification commitments
- independent monitoring every 6–12 months

If agreed savings and performance milestones are missed, the parties return to the table with the verification panel’s findings.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It gives the works council, union, and state a real security corridor, while giving management and investors measurable performance conditions.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact recurring annual savings target must be met for you to accept a no-closure restructuring as sufficient, and what portion of that target can you credibly attribute to non-closure measures today?**

### To the works council
**If independent verification shows a residual gap after non-closure measures, what limited form of capacity adjustment would you be prepared to discuss without crossing your no-compulsory-redundancy red line?**

### To the union
**Would you accept a time-limited workforce contribution if it were paired with binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff guarantees through 2030, and if not, what additional safeguard would be required?**

### To the regional state government
**Will you commit to supporting a verified no-closure path even if it requires temporary concessions from workers, provided that management and owners also contribute materially?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to state publicly that closure is not the preferred outcome, and to back a no-closure verification process with a binding willingness to finance owner-side contributions if the numbers support it?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of verified recurring savings would make a no-closure settlement acceptable to you, even if it involves longer implementation and stronger labor protections?**

### To the independent verification expert
**What is the minimum data package and threshold methodology you need to determine, with confidence, whether non-closure measures can match the economics of footprint reduction?**
