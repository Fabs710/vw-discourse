I will be direct.

## My position
I support a restructuring that **restores competitiveness and margins**. I do **not** support a settlement that simply preserves the current cost structure and calls that a solution. That would destroy value over time, which is not acceptable for us as long-term owners.

At the same time, I do **not** want a value-destructive confrontation or a rushed closure-first approach that permanently weakens the company. A plant closure is not a slogan; it is a permanent loss of capability and flexibility. If closures are truly necessary, that must be demonstrated, not assumed.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the cost problem is real and structural. You are also right that temporary or symbolic measures are not enough.

What I agree with:
- the company needs durable cost reduction,
- labour-cost relief must be meaningful,
- the outcome must restore margins and free investment capacity.

What I do **not** yet accept:
- that closures are automatically required,
- or that the workforce must first concede everything while management keeps the right to escalate.

### With the expert
The verification logic is exactly right. We need a quantified bridge from current cost base to target cost base, and we need to separate:
- recurring savings,
- one-off effects,
- capacity reduction,
- and residual gap.

Without that, nobody can honestly claim that closures are unavoidable.

### With the regional government
You are right that this is not just a financial exercise. Industrial capability, trust, and regional stability matter. A destructive move would damage the company’s ecosystem and future resilience.

But I would caution against turning “no closures” into a blanket veto. If the economics do not work, then the company still needs a real answer.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
I understand your red lines politically and socially. But I cannot support a position that effectively says:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no fundamental wage or capacity changes,
- and yet the company must regain competitiveness anyway.

That is not a viable long-term solution. It is a demand for stability without sufficient adjustment. As controlling owners, we cannot sign up to that.

I also do not accept the idea that management is acting in bad faith simply because it insists on hard numbers. Hard numbers are exactly what is required.

### With management, if it is using closures as a negotiating club
I also do not accept a strategy that uses closure threats as the default bargaining lever without first exhausting and quantifying alternatives. That is not how you secure durable labour peace. It may force a short-term reaction, but it risks long-term instability and reputational damage.

## What I need to see
Before I support any course that includes closures or excludes them, I need a proper quantified package.

### [SUMMON: independent restructuring verification and scenario-analysis specialist]
We need an independent verification of:
- required annual recurring savings,
- savings from labour-cost moderation,
- savings from working-time and flexibility measures,
- savings from management overhead and process improvements,
- savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and the residual gap under each scenario, including a no-closure scenario.

This is the only way to determine whether closures are truly unavoidable or whether a negotiated non-closure package is sufficient.

## My stance on the possible paths

### 1) Non-closure restructuring
I am open to this **if** it delivers:
- durable savings at sufficient scale,
- credible capacity reduction,
- measurable labour contribution,
- and a real improvement in competitiveness.

If the non-closure package is strong enough, I would prefer it to closures.

### 2) Closure-based restructuring
I will only consider this if the independent verification shows:
- a substantial residual gap remains,
- non-closure measures cannot close it,
- and the closure is the least destructive way to protect the overall enterprise.

That is a high bar, and it should be.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- open-ended industrial conflict,
- a deal that preserves all guarantees and solves nothing,
- or a closure-first plan that weakens the enterprise more than it helps.

## Bottom line
My line is simple:
- **Competitiveness must be restored.**
- **Savings must be structural and verified.**
- **Instability must be avoided.**
- **Closures are not off the table in principle, but they are not justified by assertion alone.**

Bring the numbers, not just the pressure. Until then, I will not endorse a destructive course in either direction.
