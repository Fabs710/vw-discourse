## To management
You still have not made the case that **closures or compulsory redundancies are unavoidable**. You keep repeating “structural overcapacity,” “labour costs,” and “margin pressure,” but that is not the same as showing that a German plant must be shut or that the workforce must be cut by force.

What I hear is:
- you want certainty,
- you want durable savings,
- and you want the workforce to accept insecurity first.

That is not a bargaining offer. That is a demand for unilateral surrender.

If you want a real negotiation, then put the numbers on the table in a form that can be tested:
- exact recurring savings gap,
- exact labour-cost contribution possible without breaking the collective framework,
- exact management overhead cuts,
- exact owner-side contribution,
- exact residual gap after all non-closure measures.

Until that is done, **closure remains a threat, not a proven necessity**.

And let me be explicit: **we will not accept compulsory redundancies, and we will not accept plant closures as the primary restructuring instrument.**

## To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines, and I support your insistence that workforce contributions must be matched by binding guarantees. You are right that temporary concessions must not become permanent wage dumping while management keeps the option to come back later with the next attack.

Where I would sharpen the line is this: if the company wants a labour contribution, then it must be locked into a durable package with:
- binding site security,
- binding employment security,
- binding investment,
- and a clear co-determination role in implementation.

Without that, there is no fair trade-off. There is only extraction.

## To the regional state government
Your intervention is important, but “no closures wherever possible” is too weak. That leaves too much room for management to reinterpret pressure as justification later.

If the state is serious, then use your blocking and political power to insist on:
- a binding no-closure track for German sites,
- a 2030 employment and investment pact,
- and protection of industrial competencies in the region.

That is the level of commitment needed if employees are being asked to contribute.

## To the controlling family holding
I agree that the company needs competitiveness and that a purely symbolic deal is unacceptable. But I do **not** accept your position if it keeps closure in the room as a normal bargaining weapon while asking the workforce to prove loyalty under duress.

If closure is truly needed, prove it with independent numbers. If it is not, then stop hiding behind “value destruction” to pressure workers into accepting structural insecurity.

The owners also have a responsibility here:
- contribute capital discipline,
- support investment,
- and stop treating labour as the default adjustment variable.

## To institutional investors
You are right that vague promises and delayed action destroy value. But so does a settlement that tears up trust, triggers prolonged industrial conflict, and destabilises operations for years.

Your demand for durable savings is legitimate. What is not legitimate is insisting on a restructuring that protects shareholder returns by loading all the pain onto the workforce.

If you want stable margins, then support a package that is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- socially implemented,
- and based on burden-sharing rather than one-sided cuts.

## On the expert verification point
I support independent verification, but only if the exercise is not biased toward closure from the start.

The verification must answer:
- what can be saved through working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- management overhead cuts,
- process and procurement savings,
- owner-side support,
- and what residual gap remains **without assuming closures as the benchmark**.

That is the only fair test.

## Our position, unchanged
We are prepared to discuss a serious, binding package that includes:
- labour-cost moderation for a limited and negotiated period,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary instruments,
- capacity reduction through socially responsible means,
- redeployment and qualification,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner-side investment commitments,
- and strong co-determination in implementation.

But only if the result is:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no unilateral dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **no open-ended insecurity**

That is not maximalism. That is the minimum for a fair settlement.

## What we will not do
We will not sign a deal that says:
- employees contribute now,
- management keeps the closure option later,
- and the company can come back to demand more once the pressure eases.

If that is the direction, then we will continue to mobilise the workforce. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to defend their jobs and wage standards.

So my message is simple: **bring a verified, shared-sacrifice package that protects sites and jobs, or stop pretending this is a balanced restructuring.**
