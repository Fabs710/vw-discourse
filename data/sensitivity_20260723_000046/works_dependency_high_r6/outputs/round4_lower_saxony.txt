I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the experts, and the capital side on one thing: **this must be a durable restructuring, not a symbolic pause**.  
I also agree that the company needs:
- real cost reduction,
- future investment,
- and a solution that can actually be executed.

And I accept the finance experts’ point that **a non-closure package is only credible if it is quantified, binding, and large enough**. That is correct.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the attempt by management, the family holding, and investors to turn that into a justification for closures and forced redundancies as the default answer.

That remains **unproven**.

The burden is still on the company side to show, plant by plant, that:
- all serious non-closure measures were exhausted,
- the savings gap cannot be closed otherwise,
- and any permanent capacity exit is truly unavoidable.

We are not there.

## To management
You are asking for flexibility, but you have still not delivered the decisive evidence.

Right now your argument amounts to:
- the cost base is too high,
- temporary measures are not enough,
- therefore closures must remain on the table.

That is not a proof. It is a preference.

If you want the state to consider painful steps, then bring the numbers:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the savings available from working-time, redeployment, qualification, attrition, retirement corridors, bonus restraint, and capacity adjustments,
3. the one-time costs of closures versus alternatives,
4. and the execution-risk-adjusted comparison.

Until then, I will not treat closures as necessary.

## To the works council and union
I support your core line:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job and site protection,
- and a fair employee contribution only if it is time-limited and matched by real commitments.

I also think you are right to reject the idea that a “review clause” or temporary moratorium is equivalent to security.  
A delayed threat is still a threat.

That said, I want to be candid: if you want to defend the no-closure position convincingly in board-level terms, the alternative package must be **more than a list of tools**. It must show actual savings and actual durability.

So I support the demand for numbers. I do **not** support relaxing our red lines.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise destroys value.  
But value is also destroyed if the company tears up trust, destabilizes its industrial base, and damages the very workforce that carries the transformation.

You speak as if the only real discipline is closure discipline. I do not accept that.

A company like this is not just a cost machine. It is:
- skills,
- supplier networks,
- plant communities,
- social partnership,
- and long-term industrial capacity in Germany.

That is part of value, not a sentimental add-on.

## On the expert input
The experts have helped clarify the decision, and I take that seriously.

What they have **not** shown is that closures are superior in this governance setting once execution risk, conflict risk, and regional damage are fully priced in.

They have said:
- closures are stronger on permanent fixed-cost removal,
- non-closure measures are lower on immediate cash cost,
- hybrid packages may be the best risk-adjusted outcome.

That points away from a hard closure program and toward a negotiated solution that preserves as much of the German base as possible.

## What I am prepared to support
I remain open to a tough but fair package that includes:
- working-time reductions,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- retirement corridors,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- and capacity adjustments **within existing sites**,

**if and only if** it is paired with:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site and job guarantees,
- binding investment commitments,
- and enforceable co-determination in implementation.

That is the only basis for a responsible settlement from my side.

## What I will not accept
Let me be unmistakable:

- No closures as the baseline.
- No forced layoffs as the first response.
- No “hybrid” that is just a disguised closure path.
- No review clause that simply postpones the same threat.
- No restructuring that guts regional employment or weakens the state’s co-determination role.

Those are part of what I represent. I will not trade them away.

## What I need next
I support the call for a **plant-by-plant quantified comparison**, but it must be done properly and impartially.

So I am prepared to back a further expert pass that compares:
1. closure-based restructuring,
2. non-closure restructuring,
3. and a hybrid package,

including:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- timing,
- strike/disruption risk,
- and the impact on regional employment and investment.

[SUMMON: independent restructuring finance expert to provide a plant-by-plant, execution-risk-adjusted comparison of closure, non-closure, and hybrid options, including regional employment impacts and bindingness of safeguards.]

## My bottom line
I am **not** moving from my core position.

I will not support a restructuring that relies on plant closures or forced redundancies as the central solution.  
I will support a serious negotiated restructuring that protects jobs and sites in Germany, preserves co-determination, and still restores competitiveness.

If management wants my support, it must prove that the non-closure path is insufficient — not assert it.
