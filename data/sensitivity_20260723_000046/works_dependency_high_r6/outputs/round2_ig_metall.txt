I will be equally direct.

I agree with the works council on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**. Those are not bargaining chips for us. If management is still hoping to “normalize” those demands by repeating them often enough, that will not work.

At the same time, I do **not** disagree that the company needs a serious restructuring. The market pressure is real, and we are not here to pretend that nothing has changed. But a serious restructuring is not the same as making the workforce absorb all the pain while ownership and management keep their hands clean.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs durable cost relief,
- future investment in products and technology is essential,
- working-time and redeployment tools should be used,
- qualification and internal mobility should be part of the package,
- and a shared burden is the only defensible way forward.

I also agree that we need **binding numbers**, not slogans. If management claims there is a structural gap, then it should quantify it and show exactly how much savings are needed and over what horizon.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You say the burden must be substantial enough to matter. Fine. But you have not yet shown that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only way to get there.

You keep framing the choice as:
- accept closures and layoffs, or
- accept decline.

That is too narrow and, frankly, too convenient. Before you demand the workforce give up jobs and wage standards, you must exhaust the real alternatives:
- capacity reductions,
- shortened working hours,
- temporary sacrifices,
- bonus waivers,
- wage moderation for a defined period,
- internal redeployment,
- retirement corridors,
- and a negotiated contribution from the company side and owners side.

If those tools are insufficient, prove it. Do not just assert it.

### To the family holding and investors
You both speak about long-term value and competitiveness, and I do not dismiss that. But value is not only a spreadsheet exercise. A company that destroys trust, job security, and collective bargaining power is also destroying a key part of its productive capacity.

We will not accept a model where the workforce is told to “share the burden” while the actual burden falls almost entirely on labor. Shared sacrifice means:
- management contributes,
- owners contribute,
- and employees contribute in a fair, limited, and negotiated way.

Not one-sided austerity.

### To the state government
Your political line on protecting industrial employment is understandable, and in this case it is also economically sensible. But if you want to be credible, you need to support a binding settlement that prevents closures and forced redundancies, not just express concern after the fact.

## What can move us
Let me be precise about what the union can consider:

We are open to a package that includes:
- temporary or phased wage moderation,
- a waiver or reduction of bonuses,
- working-time flexibility,
- a transformation contribution from employees,
- targeted capacity reductions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and restrictions on new hiring or replacement ratios,
**if and only if** this is paired with:
- a binding no-closure commitment,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of the collective bargaining framework,
- and real commitments from management and owners.

That is the only basis on which employees can reasonably be asked to contribute.

## What is not acceptable
I want to be crystal clear:
- **No closure logic disguised as “selective rationalization.”**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No open-ended insecurity.**
- **No erosion of the collective wage agreement as a permanent condition.**
- **No deal where workers pay, but management and owners merely promise to “try harder.”**

If management wants a contribution from the workforce, then the company side must also put something on the table that is tangible and enforceable: investment commitments, site guarantees, and governance that locks the deal in.

## On the numbers and mechanisms
The moderator is right: we need specifics. But specifics must go both ways.

So here is what I want next:
1. the exact savings gap management says must be closed,
2. the exact amount expected from labor,
3. the exact contribution from management and owners,
4. the exact investment commitments for future products and technologies,
5. and the exact legal form of guarantees on jobs and sites.

Without that, “restructuring” is just a word used to justify pressure on workers.

## Bottom line
The union is ready to negotiate a **durable, fair settlement**.

But the settlement has to meet three conditions:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **A fair shared-sacrifice package with binding commitments from management and owners**

If that is not on the table, then we will not be the party that gives the company permission to break its own social contract.

If management persists with closures or forced cuts, then we will respond accordingly — including industrial action.
