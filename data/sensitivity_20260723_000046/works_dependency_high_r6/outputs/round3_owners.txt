I appreciate the finance specialist’s input because it makes the central point harder to evade: **this is a value and execution question, not a slogan contest**.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert, management, and capital side on the core diagnosis:
- the company needs a **durable structural reset**,
- temporary measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient if the savings gap is large,
- and a cosmetic compromise that preserves the current cost base would destroy value over time.

I also agree with labour and the state on one important point:
- **prolonged conflict is value-destructive** and must be avoided if at all possible.

That said, avoiding conflict is not the same as preserving an uncompetitive structure.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept “no closures” as a governing principle
The works council and union continue to treat “no plant closures in Germany” as a red line. I understand why they do that, but I do **not** accept it as a basis for governing a company facing structural overcapacity and cost pressure.

If a site is permanently uneconomic, preserving it indefinitely is not stewardship; it is deferred loss. As controlling owner, I cannot support a framework that rules out necessary industrial rationalisation in advance.

### 2) I do not accept blanket job security to 2030 as an absolute
A broad guarantee has value if it unlocks a credible package. But an unconditional guarantee through 2030 for all jobs and sites can simply freeze the very correction the company needs.

I am open to strong security measures. I am not open to guarantees that make meaningful restructuring impossible.

### 3) I do not accept “shared burden” that is mostly symbolic
The union is right that employees should not carry the whole load. But “shared burden” must mean **real, durable, measurable change** on the cost side. Temporary concessions help, but if they do not change the fixed cost base enough, they are not enough.

## Where the finance expert matters
The specialist has made the right distinction:
- **non-closure measures can work in principle**, but only if they are large, durable, and enforceable,
- whereas closures are financially stronger on certainty, but carry heavy one-time costs and labour risk.

That means the discussion should stop pretending that either side has already proven its case. We have not yet seen a **quantified bridge** showing that non-closure measures close the gap at scale.

So I agree with the expert that we need:
- exact savings targets,
- plant-by-plant economics,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and a risk-adjusted comparison.

But I draw a firmer governance conclusion than the labour side wants to hear:  
**until a non-closure package is shown to match the economics, closures remain a legitimate and necessary option.**

## To management
You now need to move from broad warnings to a disciplined proposal. Bring forward:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the time horizon,
3. what has already been tested,
4. what fixed costs are removable without closures,
5. and the point at which the economics stop working.

I do **not** want theatre. I want decision-grade numbers.

But I also want you to be careful: if the company pushes closure as the first move without exhausting credible alternatives, you risk an avoidable labour confrontation that destroys value in the meantime.

## To the works council
You asked for proof that alternatives cannot work. That is a fair demand. The finance expert has now confirmed the right test: **the burden of proof is economic, not emotional**.

But your position still overreaches when you demand:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a binding guarantee through 2030
**before** the economics are shown.

That is not negotiation; it is pre-judgment.

If you want me to support a non-closure path, it must be:
- quantified,
- structurally durable,
- and credible on execution.

If it is, I am prepared to consider it seriously. If it is not, I will not pretend otherwise.

## To the union
You are correct that temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexibility may help preserve the industrial base. But I agree with the specialist that, on their own, they often do **not** solve a large structural gap.

So I would say this plainly:  
if the workforce wants to avoid closures, then the employee contribution must be significant enough to materially improve the economics, not just symbolically improve the atmosphere.

That still does **not** mean forced layoffs are my preferred outcome. It means I will not support a compromise that leaves the company structurally impaired.

## To the regional state government
I appreciate your role and your willingness to protect the industrial base. But I need to be equally direct: your blocking position cannot become a shield for every site regardless of economics.

You are right to insist on alternatives being properly tested. You are wrong if you imply that closures are politically unacceptable regardless of the financial case.

If you want to preserve employment long term, then support a package that:
- restores competitiveness,
- secures investment,
- and is enforceable.

A blocking minority is a responsibility, not a license to freeze adjustment.

## To the finance specialist
Your assessment is useful, but it still leaves the key governance issue unresolved: **what if the savings gap is large and the non-closure package is too slow or too uncertain?**

In that case, the family holding will not support an outcome that permanently erodes margins simply to avoid difficult decisions. We are long-term owners, not short-term traders. That means we can tolerate pain if it buys a durable future — but not if it merely postpones the bill.

## My position
So the family holding’s position remains:

- **Yes** to a quantified comparison of closure vs. non-closure paths.
- **Yes** to serious non-closure alternatives if they are economically equivalent.
- **No** to blanket no-closure rules.
- **No** to blanket job-security guarantees that freeze structural correction.
- **No** to any deal that leaves the company’s competitiveness unrepaired.

## What I need next
If we are to move, I want a package with:
1. exact savings targets,
2. plant-by-plant and function-by-function economics,
3. quantified non-closure measures,
4. one-time restructuring costs,
5. and binding investment / governance commitments.

Until then, I will not endorse a closure ban, and I will not endorse a compromise that simply postpones the structural problem.

- **closure-based restructuring**,
