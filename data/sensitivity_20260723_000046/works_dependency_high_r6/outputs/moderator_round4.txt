### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **labour-side demands for binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy guarantees** and **capital/management demands for retained flexibility to close sites or impose permanent capacity exits if needed**.

In procedural terms, the parties are not mainly split on whether restructuring is needed. They are split on:
1. **whether closures are permitted at all**,  
2. **whether protections must be absolute or conditional**, and  
3. **what level of quantified savings is sufficient to justify any permanent capacity exit**.

A second, related impasse is that **everyone is demanding “decision-grade numbers,” but no one has yet accepted a common comparison method or data set**. So the debate is stuck at the level of principle rather than moving into verifiable trade-offs.

### Facilitator proposals

#### Proposal 1: Conditional no-closure package with hard triggers
A possible bridge is a **time-limited no-closure commitment** paired with **quantified savings triggers**.

- The company would commit not to close German plants during the initial phase.
- Labour would accept temporary concessions: working-time flexibility, wage moderation, bonus restraint, retirement corridors, redeployment, and qualification.
- An independent expert would test whether the package reaches an agreed savings target.
- If the target is not met by a specified date, the parties would reopen the discussion on additional structural measures.

This does not decide the closure question now; it postpones it behind a quantified test.

#### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with narrowly defined permanent capacity adjustment
A second bridge is a **hybrid settlement** that avoids blanket closures but allows **narrowly defined reductions in permanent capacity**.

- No broad closure program.
- No compulsory redundancies as the first response.
- A limited list of functions, lines, or modules could be rationalized where economics are weakest.
- The package would require binding investment commitments and enforceable job/site safeguards for the remaining base.

This is designed to meet management’s durability concern without accepting an all-sites-closed or all-sites-protected binary.

#### Proposal 3: Binding exchange package with governance safeguards
A third bridge is a **structured exchange**:

- Labour contributions: temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, flexibility, and redeployment.
- Capital/management contributions: binding investment, site protection, and no compulsory redundancies during the agreement term.
- State and shareholder side: defined oversight and a review mechanism with clear veto/approval rules.
- A joint implementation committee would monitor savings, compliance, and reallocation of capacity.

This aims to reduce mutual mistrust by making each side’s concession conditional, measurable, and enforceable.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If an independently verified package shows that a **hybrid solution can close the savings gap without broad plant closures**, would you accept a narrowly defined permanent capacity adjustment under strict protections, or is **any** permanent site reduction unacceptable regardless of the numbers?

#### Industrial Union
What specific combination of **temporary concessions and binding guarantees** would you regard as sufficient to support a negotiated deal, and what exact savings threshold must be demonstrated before you would recommend acceptance?

#### Group & Brand Management
What is the company’s **minimum quantified savings requirement** for a viable restructuring, and which parts of that requirement are truly non-negotiable versus open to being met through non-closure measures?

#### Regional State Government
Under what precise conditions would you exercise or withhold your blocking power: what data, safeguards, and governance commitments must be in place before you would consider any package that includes selective capacity rationalization?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a package that preserves the German site base but accepts a **measurable, limited reduction in capacity** if the risk-adjusted NPV is superior to full closures—and if not, what level of economic underperformance would trigger your support for closures?

#### Institutional Investors
Would you support a hybrid package if it produces a clearly positive **risk-adjusted NPV** but leaves some excess capacity in place, and what minimum margin or return threshold would you require before endorsing it?

#### Restructuring Finance Expert
Can you provide a **common decision template** with one agreed methodology for comparing non-closure, hybrid, and closure options, including the same discount rate, same strike-risk assumptions, and the same treatment of one-time costs?
