Let me respond plainly.

We agree on one thing: this company needs a durable solution, not a temporary patch. But **durable** does not mean **destroying sites, jobs, and the historic protections that have made this company strong**.

## What I agree with
I agree with the need for:
- real cost reduction,
- investment in future products and technology,
- and a shared burden rather than putting everything on the workforce.

I also agree that we should be serious about operational alternatives:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- capacity adjustments,
- and a fair, negotiated employee contribution.

## What I reject
I reject the idea that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are somehow the “credible default.”

That is not a neutral economic fact; it is a management choice.

And I reject the claim that a binding job and site guarantee to 2030 is impossible. If management and owners are serious about transformation, they must be serious about protecting the workforce that makes transformation possible. You cannot ask employees to finance the future while stripping away their security.

## To management
You say the company needs structural savings. Fine. Then show us a **non-closure package with real numbers**, not vague threats.

If you want the workforce to consider sacrifices, then the package must include:
- **binding guarantees that all German plants remain open**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **job security through 2030**,
- **clear investment commitments** for future models and technologies,
- and **co-determination in shaping the transformation**, not just being informed after the fact.

Without those conditions, your proposal is not a restructuring plan; it is a demand that workers pay for a strategy they did not create.

## To the family holding and investors
You speak about value and competitiveness. I speak about industrial reality.

A company does not create value by hollowing out its German base. Once plants are shut, skills, supplier networks, and trust are lost. That is not “efficiency”; it is irreversible damage.

If you want long-term value, then support a path that preserves the industrial base instead of gambling it away for short-term margin improvements.

## To the state government
Your concern about regional employment is right. But concern alone is not enough. Use your blocking position to ensure that no agreement is forced through that breaks the job-security framework or turns co-determination into window dressing.

## The central point
We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity reductions,
- temporary flexibility,
- wage moderation,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and other negotiated measures.

But there is a clear line:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**

If management wants a settlement, it must prove that savings can be achieved **without** crossing those lines.

## What I need to hear next
If anyone wants this to move forward, bring:
1. exact savings required,
2. exact alternatives to closures,
3. exact investment commitments,
4. exact legal and contractual guarantees,
5. and a binding role for co-determination in implementation.

Until then, the works council will remain opposed to any restructuring built on closures or forced job cuts. And if management tries to impose such a plan, it will meet the resistance it deserves — in the plant, on the shop floor, and in the supervisory board.
