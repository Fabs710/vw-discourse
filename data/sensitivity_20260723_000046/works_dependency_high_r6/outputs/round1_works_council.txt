The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines.

Management cannot simply present a restructuring plan that sacrifices sites, jobs, and long-standing protections and expect workforce consent. If the company wants a viable path forward, it must be built on:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job security through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- serious investment commitments for future products and technologies,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the transformation.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives to closures and layoffs: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification, temporary sacrifices, and negotiated contributions. But any contribution from employees must be matched by a credible commitment from management and owners to preserve the industrial base in Germany.

What we reject is a fait accompli: using crisis language to break the historic job-security framework and shift the burden entirely onto the workforce. If management wants peace, it needs to negotiate in good faith. If it insists on closures or forced cuts, it will face fierce resistance, including industrial action and a blockade in the supervisory board.

Our priority is simple: protect every German plant, protect every job, and secure the company’s future without destroying the workforce that built it.
