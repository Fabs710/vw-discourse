### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs:
  - **real cost reduction**,
  - **future investment in products and technology**,
  - and a **shared burden** rather than employees bearing all the adjustment.
- Most parties also accept, at least in principle, that **alternatives to immediate closures or compulsory redundancies** should be explored, including:
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - working-time solutions,
  - capacity adjustments,
  - and targeted wage/flexibility measures.
- There is also shared concern about avoiding a prolonged conflict that would damage the company further.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state government reject closures as a baseline.
  - Management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour side and state government reject them.
  - Management, family holding, and investors do not rule them out if alternatives fail.
- **Job security to 2030**
  - Labour side wants strong guarantees.
  - Management and capital-side actors say a blanket guarantee is incompatible with the scale of restructuring.
- **How much labor contribution is required**
  - Management and capital want a substantial, durable cost contribution.
  - Labour and state insist any employee concessions must be matched by binding commitments from management and owners.
- **Whether non-closure alternatives can achieve sufficient savings**
  - This is the central unresolved factual and economic dispute.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Scale of savings vs. job/site protection**: no common baseline yet on how much cost reduction is required and what level of employment/site protection is still feasible.
- **Temporary measures vs. structural measures**: parties have not agreed on which tools can deliver lasting effects without closures.
- **Investment commitments vs. cost cuts**: there is no package yet showing how savings will be earmarked for future products and technology.
- **Governance and enforceability**: it is unclear how any compromise would be made binding and monitored over time.
- **Fair burden-sharing**: no shared formula yet for how costs, risks, and sacrifices should be distributed among labour, management, owners, and the state.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and mechanisms**:
  - what exact savings are required,
  - what alternative measures can deliver them,
  - and what binding guarantees can accompany them.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - which concessions it can make,
  - which red lines remain non-negotiable,
  - and what it would need in return.
- Focus the next round on a **single comparison**:
  - **closure/redundancy-based restructuring** vs.
  - a **non-closure package with equivalent savings**.
- Require proposals to address both:
  - **economic credibility**, and
  - **social protections**.
