- write-downs / impairment charges,
- decommissioning and remediation,

- labor conflict and production loss,
- execution risk during transition,
- and potential long-term reputational damage to labor relations.

- Higher near-term cash outflow
- Higher one-time charges
- Faster and more certain structural savings
- Lower execution risk if legally and operationally feasible

#### Option B: non-closure restructuring
- Lower immediate cash cost
- Usually slower realization
- Greater dependence on labor cooperation
- Risk that savings fall short or arrive too late
- May preserve industrial capability and avoid disruption costs

The better option is the one with the higher **risk-adjusted net present value** and acceptable liquidity profile.

The decision turns on five variables:

   - Over how many years?

2. **Durability of savings**
   - Temporary wage moderation has lower NPV value than permanent capacity exit.

3. **One-time cash costs**

4. **Execution risk**
   - In this case, governance vetoes and likely industrial action materially increase the risk that a closure plan is delayed, diluted, or legally/politically constrained.

5. **Business demand outlook**
   - If volume recovery is weak, preserving excess capacity becomes expensive.
