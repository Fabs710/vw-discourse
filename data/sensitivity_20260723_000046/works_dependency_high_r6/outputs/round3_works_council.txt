We have now heard enough generalities. So let me be equally direct.

## What I agree with
Yes, the company needs a durable restructuring.
Yes, we need real cost reduction.
Yes, the future of the German sites depends on investment in new products and technology.
Yes, conflict has costs and nobody should welcome a long confrontation for its own sake.

On that level, there is no dispute.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the attempt by management, the family holding, and investors to keep plant closures and compulsory redundancies as routine instruments while calling that “responsible restructuring.”

That is not a neutral economic truth. It is a choice to put the burden of adjustment onto workers and industrial communities.

And I **do not** accept the idea that a binding job and site guarantee to 2030 is somehow incompatible with serious transformation. If the company wants employees to make sacrifices, it must first give them something real in return: security, enforceability, and a future for the German industrial base.

## To management
You keep saying alternatives must be “equivalent.” Fine. Then put the actual numbers on the table.

If you claim the savings gap requires closures, then show:
- the exact gap,
- the exact savings from each plant,
- the exact savings from each alternative measure,
- and the exact reason why a package of working-time, redeployment, qualification, attrition, temporary wage moderation, and capacity adjustment cannot close that gap.

Right now you are still asserting inevitability where you have not proven necessity.

And let me be clear: **a non-closure package is not a cosmetic option if it is properly designed and binding**. It can include:
- capacity reductions,
- shorter working time,
- internal transfers,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement corridors,
- temporary concessions,
- and adjustments in bonuses or allowances.

But it must come with:
- **no closures in Germany**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **binding site guarantees**,
- **investment commitments**,
- and **co-determination in implementation**.

Without that, you are asking the workforce to finance uncertainty.

## To the family holding and investors
You talk about value and competitiveness. I talk about industrial reality.

A company does not create long-term value by threatening its own workforce into accepting closures. It creates value by maintaining skills, trust, supplier networks, and the capacity to produce in Germany.

You say an absolute no-closure rule is not acceptable. I say the opposite: an absolute closure threat is not acceptable either.

And your claim that any guarantee of job security through 2030 “freezes a broken structure” is too convenient. A guarantee does not freeze transformation. It creates the trust needed for transformation to happen without destroying the people who must carry it out.

## To the regional state government
I appreciate your clearer line against closures as a baseline. But I need more than political caution.

If you want to use your blocking position responsibly, then use it to protect:
- all German plants,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and the job-security framework.

Do not leave open the back door for management to impose closures later under the label of “last resort” after the workforce has already conceded.

A last resort without a binding alternative is just a delayed threat.

## To the union
I agree with you on the red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of collective protections.

Your willingness to discuss temporary concessions is understandable, but only if they are truly temporary and paired with hard guarantees. The workforce cannot be expected to accept open-ended austerity in exchange for vague promises.

## On the numbers and mechanisms
The moderator is right that this round needs specificity. So here is what I insist on:

1. **Exact savings target and timeline**
   - management must present a quantified gap,
   - and it must do so plant by plant, not in abstractions.

2. **Exact non-closure savings package**
   - we need to see what working-time cuts, redeployment, qualification, attrition, and capacity measures actually deliver.

3. **Exact employee contribution**
   - temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, or other measures can only be discussed if they are time-limited and reversible.

4. **Exact management and owner contribution**
   - no more pretending that workers alone must absorb the adjustment.
   - management and owners must commit to investment, governance, and financial responsibility.

5. **Exact legal guarantees**
   - binding no-closure commitment for German plants,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - job security through 2030,
   - and enforceable co-determination in implementation.

## The line I will not cross
Let me make this unmistakable:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No deal that breaks the job-security framework.**
- **No “restructuring” that is really just a controlled dismantling of German industrial capacity.**

If management cannot make the numbers work without crossing those lines, then the problem is not employee resistance. The problem is the restructuring model.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am prepared to negotiate hard on:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- limited and time-bound wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal mobility,
- retirement corridors,
- and capacity adjustments.

But only if those measures are matched by:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closures,
- real investment commitments,
- and meaningful co-determination in how the transformation is carried out.

That is not “special treatment.” That is the minimum required for a fair settlement.

## Final point
So far, capital has asked workers to prove that alternatives can work. Fair enough.

Now management, the family holding, and investors must prove the opposite: that they are not simply using structural pressure to force through a closure strategy that has already been decided in principle.

Bring the numbers. Bring binding guarantees. Bring a real non-closure plan.

Until then, the works council will remain opposed to any restructuring built on closures or forced layoffs.
