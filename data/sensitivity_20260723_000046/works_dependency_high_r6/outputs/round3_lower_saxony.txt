I will be direct.

I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: this company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a symbolic truce. I also agree that the status quo is not good enough and that the German cost base has to come down.

But I **do not** accept the premise that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain the default instruments of this restructuring. For this state and for this company, that is not a neutral business choice — it is a decision with lasting damage to jobs, suppliers, skills, and regional stability. And I will not support that as a starting point.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- real savings are necessary,
- future investment must be secured,
- alternatives to closures should be examined seriously,
- and a prolonged conflict would hurt the company.

I also agree that employee contributions have to be part of the package. A fair settlement cannot mean that only capital and management define the burden.

## Where I disagree
### On closures
I reject the idea that closures should remain the routine fallback unless the workforce proves a perfect alternative first. The burden of justification sits with management. If you want to close a German plant, you must show that:
- all serious alternatives were exhausted,
- the savings gap cannot be closed otherwise,
- and the regional damage is truly unavoidable.

We are not there.

### On compulsory redundancies
I also reject forced layoffs as a first response. A serious restructuring must first use:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- retirement corridors,
- working-time solutions,
- attrition,
- and voluntary instruments.

If those are not enough, then we can talk further. But you have not yet shown that they fail at scale.

### On blanket job guarantees
I understand why labour wants strong security through 2030. And I am sympathetic to that demand. But I will not support an unconditional guarantee if it simply freezes every adjustment and blocks any serious restructuring.

What I can support is a **binding social-partnership package** that protects:
- the German industrial base,
- as many sites and jobs as possible,
- and the state’s co-determination and veto role,
while still allowing a measurable, negotiated contribution from employees and owners.

## What I want to hear next
The discussion is still missing the key thing: **numbers with mechanisms**.

I want management to put forward:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the exact contribution expected from labour,
3. the exact contribution from management and owners,
4. the exact investment commitments for future products and technologies,
5. and the exact governance form that makes the deal enforceable.

If the company wants the workforce and the state to consider sacrifices, then the company side must put something concrete on the table:
- binding investment,
- binding site protection,
- and a credible implementation framework.

## My position as anchor shareholder
Let me make our line clear:

- I will **not** support any restructuring that guts regional employment.
- I will **not** accept a deal that sidelines co-determination or weakens the state’s veto role in practice.
- I will **not** endorse closures as the baseline strategy for German sites.

At the same time:
- I **am** prepared to support a negotiated restructuring,
- I **am** prepared to accept serious employee contributions if they are limited, time-bound, and matched by commitments from management and owners,
- and I **am** prepared to back capacity adjustments and other non-terminal measures if they genuinely preserve sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness.

## What would move me
What could move me is a package that:
- avoids plant closures,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- includes a fair but bounded employee contribution,
- secures investment in future products and technologies,
- and is backed by enforceable governance and site commitments.

That is the only basis on which I would see this as responsible restructuring rather than industrial retreat.

If management wants my support, bring:
- quantified savings,
- a credible non-closure package,
- binding investment,
- and enforceable protection for jobs, sites, and co-determination.

Until then, my position stands: **no baseline closures, no forced redundancies, and no deal that damages the regional anchor role of this company.**
