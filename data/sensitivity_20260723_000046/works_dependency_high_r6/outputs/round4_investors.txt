We agree with the finance experts on the core issue: **this is a value-and-execution decision, not a symbolic compromise exercise**. If the restructuring does not deliver a credible and durable return to competitive margins, then shareholders are simply financing delay.

## Where we agree
- The company needs **durable structural savings**, not a temporary truce.
- Future investment in products and technology is essential.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive.
- A **non-closure package can work in principle** if it is large, durable, and enforceable.
- A **hybrid package** may well be the best risk-adjusted route in this governance setup.

## Where we disagree
### 1) We do not accept a blanket “no closures” rule
The works council and union continue to treat “no plant closures in Germany” as a principle. We do **not** accept that.

If closures are needed to restore competitiveness and remove excess capacity, they must remain on the table. Shareholders care about the economics, not the symbolism. A deal that protects every site while leaving the cost base uncompetitive is not acceptable.

### 2) We do not accept a blanket “no compulsory redundancies” rule
We prefer voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and retirement corridors. But if those tools do not close the gap, management must retain the ability to act. Otherwise restructuring becomes aspirational rather than real.

### 3) We do not accept a 2030 guarantee that freezes the problem
A broad job and site guarantee only has value if it unlocks a credible savings package. If it simply locks in overcapacity and defers adjustment, it destroys shareholder value.

## On the expert view
The restructuring finance experts have basically confirmed what matters:
- closures are stronger on permanent fixed-cost removal,
- non-closure measures are weaker on certainty and speed,
- and hybrid solutions often offer the best risk-adjusted outcome in a veto-heavy governance environment.

That is broadly our view as well.

But we want to be clear: **hybrid is acceptable only if it is not a disguised way of preserving too much of the current structure**. A “hybrid” that delivers insufficient savings while avoiding hard decisions would be a cosmetic compromise, and we would not support it.

## What would move us
We need a package with:
1. a **quantified savings target**,
2. a **plant-by-plant and function-by-function bridge** to those savings,
3. an explicit split between:
   - temporary measures,
   - structural measures,
   - and any permanent capacity exit,
4. a **clear employee contribution** that is large enough to matter,
5. binding commitments from management and owners on investment and execution.

Without that, there is no basis for confidence in margins or valuation recovery.

## Our position on alternatives
We are open to a non-closure package **only if** it demonstrably closes the savings gap in durable terms. That means:
- working-time flexibility,
- wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- retirement corridors,
- and capacity reductions

must add up to something real, not just politically convenient.

If they do not, then some permanent site rationalization remains economically rational and should not be ruled out in advance.

## Bottom line
Our position is simple:
- **Yes** to a disciplined, quantified restructuring that restores competitiveness.
- **Yes** to a hybrid if it genuinely maximizes risk-adjusted value.
- **No** to absolute closure bans.
- **No** to blanket job-security guarantees that freeze an uncompetitive structure.
- **No** to a cosmetic settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the parties want investor support, bring the numbers and show how the package gets to sustainable margins. If not, we will treat any weak compromise as value destruction, regardless of how it is packaged.
