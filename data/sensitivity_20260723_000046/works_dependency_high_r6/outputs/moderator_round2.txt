### 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- All sides accept that **cost reduction** and **future investment** in products/technology are necessary.
- There is broad support for examining **non-terminal tools** first, including:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - attrition and retirement corridors,
  - temporary or negotiated employee contributions.
- There is agreement that any solution should be based on **binding numbers, concrete mechanisms, and enforceable commitments**, not slogans.
- All sides recognize that a prolonged conflict would be **damaging** to the company and its future.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Employee side and state government: closures should not be the baseline and should be avoided.
  - Management, family holding, and investors: closures must remain an available option if alternatives do not deliver enough savings.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee side and state government: reject compulsory redundancies as a first response.
  - Management, family holding, and investors: do not want forced layoffs if avoidable, but insist they remain available if needed to achieve the required restructuring.

- **Job/security guarantees to 2030**
  - Employee side: wants binding job and site guarantees.
  - Capital side: views blanket guarantees as incompatible with a serious structural reset if overcapacity remains.

- **Scope of employee contributions**
  - Employee side: open to concessions only if matched by binding site, job, and investment commitments.
  - Capital side: wants contributions large and durable enough to materially change the economics.

- **Role of co-determination / state blocking rights**
  - Employee side and state government: want these rights to be substantively protective.
  - Capital side: warns these mechanisms must not prevent necessary restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How large the savings gap actually is** and over what time horizon.
- **How much of the required adjustment can realistically come from non-closure measures**.
- **What exact employee concessions** are on the table, and whether they are temporary or structural.
- **What binding commitments** management and owners are prepared to make on:
  - investment,
  - site protection,
  - governance,
  - implementation rules.
- **How to balance competitiveness and job protection** without simply deferring the problem.
- **What level of flexibility the company retains** if the non-closure package falls short.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should be limited to **specific proposals with numbers**:
1. exact savings target and timeline,
2. quantified non-closure alternatives,
3. exact employee contribution,
4. exact management/owner contribution,
5. exact legal and governance form of any guarantees.

A useful next step would be to ask each side to submit a **single, quantified package** showing how its preferred option closes the savings gap while addressing jobs, sites, and investment.
