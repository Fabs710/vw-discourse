### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **absolute protection claims** and **economic flexibility claims**:

- **Labour side / state:** insists on **no plant closures in Germany**, **no compulsory redundancies**, and strong **job/site security through 2030**.
- **Company / owners / investors:** insist those red lines may prevent a **large enough, durable, and timely savings package** and therefore must remain negotiable.
- **Expert input:** says both sides may be partially right, but the decisive issue is still **quantified savings, one-time costs, and execution risk**. No party has yet shown a complete, decision-grade bridge from current economics to target economics.

So the impasse is not over the need to restructure. It is over **who bears adjustment risk, whether permanent capacity exit is allowed, and whether a non-closure package can be made large enough and enforceable enough to substitute for closures**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure moratorium with review triggers
A possible bridge would be:
- **No plant closures in Germany for a defined initial period**,
- paired with a **hard review trigger** at a specified date or when agreed financial thresholds are missed,
- and a pre-agreed fallback mechanism if the savings gap is not closed.

This could give labour and the state the security they seek in the near term, while preserving an economic off-ramp if the alternative package underdelivers.

**Key feature:** closure is not ruled out forever, but is deferred under enforceable conditions.

---

#### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with targeted capacity rationalization, not blanket closures
A second compromise would be:
- **No blanket closure program**,
- but **limited, targeted permanent capacity reduction** at the least efficient sites or functions,
- combined with:
  - redeployment,
  - attrition,
  - qualification,
  - retirement corridors,
  - temporary wage moderation,
  - bonus restraint,
  - and investment commitments.

This would align with the expert’s view that a hybrid may offer the best risk-adjusted outcome.

**Key feature:** preserves most sites and jobs while allowing some permanent structural correction.

---

#### Proposal 3: Binding labour-contribution package matched by binding investment and protection package
A third compromise would be a fully linked exchange:
- employees provide **time-limited concessions**,
- management/owners provide **binding investment commitments**,
- and the company provides **enforceable site and employment protections** for the German network.

The missing element would be a **quantified savings bridge**, plant by plant, with each side’s contribution explicitly priced.

**Key feature:** makes the deal reciprocal and measurable, rather than relying on general assurances.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
**What exact combination of measures do you accept as sufficient to close the savings gap without closures, and what objective trigger would cause you to revisit that position if the package underperforms?**

#### To Industrial Union
**How much temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexibility can you credibly commit to in quantified terms, and for how long, in exchange for binding job and site security?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the exact annual savings target by plant/function, and which specific non-closure measures have you already modeled as insufficient or sufficient to meet it?**

#### To Regional State Government
**Will you support a conditional no-closure framework if it includes a review clause and measurable performance triggers, and if so, what enforcement mechanism would you insist on?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a hybrid solution that preserves most German sites but includes targeted permanent capacity exit, provided the NPV and execution-risk analysis shows it is superior to a full closure program?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified savings and margin recovery would make you support a non-closure or hybrid package, and what level of execution risk would still be tolerable?**

#### To the Restructuring Finance / Turnaround Expert
**Can you provide a side-by-side model of closure, non-closure, and hybrid options using assumed but explicit figures for savings, one-time costs, and delay risk?**

#### To the Independent Valuation Expert
**What would be the key sensitivity breakpoints at which a non-closure package becomes financially inferior to a hybrid or closure-based solution?**
