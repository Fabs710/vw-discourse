## Core impasse

The plateau appears to be centered on a single unresolved issue:

**Whether restructuring must be allowed to include plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or whether savings must be achieved entirely through negotiated alternatives with site protection.**

Everything else — investment commitments, labor-cost contributions, co-determination, and state support — depends on that threshold question.  
At present:

- **Management, investors, and the family holding** say durable savings require that closures and workforce reduction remain available.
- **Works Council, union, and state government** say closures and compulsory redundancies are red lines.
- The **expert input** indicates that both sides may be partly right economically, but without site-level numbers no side can prove its case.

So the impasse is not merely about trust; it is about **the acceptable restructuring instrument set** and **who bears the adjustment burden**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional site protection
A possible bridge is a **phased package** in which:

- German sites receive **temporary protection from closure** for a defined period,
- in exchange for **measurable and immediate cost and capacity reductions**,
- with a **pre-agreed review gate** tied to objective financial and operational triggers,
- and with the understanding that if those targets are not met, a second-stage restructuring menu is opened.

This would not settle the closure question permanently, but it could move the parties from principle to performance.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies first” combined with a quantified fallback
A second compromise position is:

- the employee side accepts **voluntary departures, retirement corridors, short-time/work-time reductions, redeployment, and qualification** as the first instruments,
- management agrees to **exclude compulsory redundancies for an initial period**,
- but both sides pre-negotiate a **quantified fallback mechanism** if voluntary tools fall short.

That fallback could be defined in advance as a mix of site rationalization, further capacity cuts, or negotiated severance options, rather than leaving the question open-ended and escalatory.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding mutual commitments
A third bridge is a package with **balanced obligations**:

- Employees: temporary wage moderation, bonus waivers, or working-time flexibility.
- Management and owners: binding investment commitments, product allocation guarantees, and support for German sites.
- State: support for qualification, transition funding, and a governance framework that preserves co-determination.
- Investors: acceptance of a staged margin recovery path rather than immediate maximum returns.

This would shift the discussion from “who concedes first” to “what each side contributes in a measurable way.”

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact savings gap must be closed site by site, and what part of that gap can you credibly close without closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Works Council
**If plant closures remain off the table, what quantified combination of capacity reduction, redeployment, and employee contribution would you accept as sufficient to protect long-term employment?**

### To the Union
**What specific, temporary labor-cost concessions are you prepared to negotiate, and what safeguards would you require to ensure they do not become permanent wage erosion?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you commit to a defined negotiating framework that trades governance support for concrete site, investment, and employment guarantees, rather than relying only on a blocking posture?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Under what measurable conditions would you accept a negotiated no-closure solution, and what evidence would convince you that closures are not necessary?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**What minimum NPV improvement and margin trajectory would make a non-closure package acceptable to you, even if it is slower and more complex to implement?**

### To the Expert
**Can you provide a simple comparison model showing which combination of measures produces the best 5-year NPV under three scenarios: low, medium, and high overcapacity?**

---

## Suggested next step
To break the plateau, the next round should be structured around **one shared quantitative table** with:

- required savings,
- savings by measure,
- implementation risk,
- timing,
- and impact on employment and sites.

That would let the parties argue from the same factual basis rather than from repeated red lines.
