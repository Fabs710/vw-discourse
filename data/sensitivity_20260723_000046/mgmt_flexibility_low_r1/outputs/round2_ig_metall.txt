I will respond very clearly.

## To the Works Council
I agree with your core line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That remains the minimum basis for any meaningful settlement. You are right that the workforce cannot again be treated as the adjustment variable for strategic mistakes.

I also agree that the company needs:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary instruments,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: **a collective-bargaining solution must also protect the wage framework**. If “contribution” becomes a permanent erosion of pay, bonuses, or guarantees, then that is not shared sacrifice — it is one-sided wage dumping.

## To management
You keep saying “credible restructuring,” but you still have not shown that closures or forced redundancies are the only path. Saying something is necessary does not make it so.

If you want the union to talk seriously, then put **numbers and alternatives** on the table:
- What exact savings are required?
- What savings come from capacity reduction alone?
- What savings come from working-time flexibility?
- What savings come from voluntary departures and retirement corridors?
- What investment is committed to Germany in return?

Without that, your argument is incomplete. You are asking the workforce to accept pain first and trust later. That is not how bargaining works, especially after the peace obligation has ended.

And let me be equally clear: **if management insists that the only credible solution is closures and job destruction, then we will use the instruments available to us.** The workforce is already mobilised. Warning strikes were not a gesture; they were the first signal.

## To the family holding
I hear your concern about long-term value. That concern is legitimate. But I reject the idea that long-term value requires making closures and workforce reduction the default answer.

A company like this loses value not only through cost, but also through:
- industrial conflict,
- broken trust,
- loss of skills,
- and damage to its employer reputation.

If you want value protection, then back a settlement that preserves sites, retains qualified workers, and makes the burden fairer. A deal that “solves” the balance sheet by hollowing out the workforce is not a durable ownership strategy.

## To the state government
We appreciate your support for jobs, sites, and co-determination. But I want to be direct: the state must not just “prefer” no closures; it must **use its blocking position to prevent them**. If this company can be restructured through alternatives, then that is exactly what should happen.

At the same time, the workforce is prepared to discuss a **fair and limited contribution** if it is linked to binding guarantees. That means:
- no forced layoffs,
- no closures,
- protection of the collective bargaining framework,
- and real investment commitments.

## To the investors
You keep saying “margins” and “competitiveness,” but you are underestimating the economics of conflict. A prolonged confrontation, lost production, and weak social legitimacy are not efficiency gains.

If you want a deal that can actually be implemented, then you need to accept that a restructuring built on **social peace** may be less brutal on paper but more reliable in practice. We are not here to defend inefficiency; we are here to prevent a restructuring that destroys the company’s workforce base while pretending that is the only rational choice.

## Our position
The union is prepared to discuss a **shared-sacrifice package**, but only under these conditions:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
4. **A fair employee contribution that is temporary, limited, and matched by real concessions from management and owners**
5. **Binding commitments on investment, product allocation, and future work in Germany**
6. **A restructuring path that relies on capacity adjustment, flexibility, and voluntary instruments rather than job destruction**

## What we can discuss
We can discuss:
- temporary bonus waivers,
- moderation in wage growth,
- a solidarity contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- and productivity-related adjustments.

We cannot accept:
- wage cuts that permanently weaken collective standards,
- forced layoffs,
- plant closures,
- or open-ended insecurity dressed up as flexibility.

## Bottom line
Our position is not a refusal to help. It is a refusal to let employees pay all the costs while others keep all the options.

If management brings a serious counterproposal that preserves jobs, sites, and the wage framework while delivering real savings through shared sacrifice, we will bargain. If not, then industrial pressure will continue.
