Here is the procedural readout as facilitator.

## 1) Core impasse
The plateau is centered on one unresolved issue:

**Whether German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded in advance, or remain available if the site-by-site numbers justify them.**

That single question blocks convergence because:

- **Works Council, union, and state government** want a no-closure basis with no compulsory redundancies and strong job/security guarantees.
- **Management, family holding, and investors** want the option set kept open, arguing that real restructuring may require closures, deeper capacity cuts, and workforce reduction.
- **All sides** say they want a durable restructuring, but they disagree on the permissible instruments and on who must carry the burden first.

A second, related impasse is:

**What counts as “proof” that alternatives are insufficient.**
- Labor side wants management to prove closures are unavoidable after exhausting internal tools.
- Management/owners want labor to prove a no-closure path can still deliver the required savings and margin target.

So the discourse is stuck in a burden-of-proof loop.

---

## 2) Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the conflict

### Compromise A: Phased restructuring with a conditional closure review
**Proposal:** Adopt a first-phase package that excludes immediate closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period, while authorizing a time-bound site review against agreed financial triggers.

**Elements:**
- No immediate plant closures.
- Voluntary exits, retirement corridors, redeployment, shift changes, and temporary workforce contributions begin immediately.
- A jointly agreed site-by-site model is completed within a fixed deadline.
- If agreed financial thresholds are still missed after Phase 1, specific sites enter a second-stage review with pre-agreed criteria.

**Why it may bridge sides:**
- Gives labor a no-closure starting point.
- Gives management/owners a credible escalation path if savings do not materialize.
- Reduces the feeling that either side is being forced to concede everything upfront.

---

### Compromise B: No-closure commitment with hard financial backstop
**Proposal:** Keep all German plants open for now, but attach a binding backstop mechanism if the savings target is not met.

**Elements:**
- Explicit no-closure commitment for the restructuring period.
- Strong internal measures and temporary contributions.
- Quarterly monitoring against quantified savings and utilization milestones.
- If milestones are missed, a pre-defined fallback package is triggered automatically, which may include deeper consolidation measures short of immediate closure.

**Why it may bridge sides:**
- Preserves the workforce red line.
- Addresses management’s concern that a purely voluntary package may be too weak.
- Converts the dispute from a principle fight into a performance-based process.

---

### Compromise C: Partial rationalization without full closures
**Proposal:** Replace the binary closure/no-closure debate with a middle category: site shrinkage, line consolidation, and product reallocation without full plant closure.

**Elements:**
- No full closures in the first settlement.
- Some sites may lose lines, shifts, or functions.
- Internal redeployment and qualification absorb part of the headcount reduction.
- Product allocation through 2030 is negotiated site by site.
- A limited number of compulsory redundancies remains off the table initially, but a separate process is created if voluntary tools are exhausted.

**Why it may bridge sides:**
- Gives management and investors material cost and capacity reduction.
- Preserves the core labor demand of avoiding outright closure.
- Fits the expert’s observation that partial rationalization may be the most realistic compromise.

## 3) Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
**Question:**  
If a joint site-by-site model shows that one or more plants still miss the required savings target after redeployment, voluntary exits, shift changes, and temporary contributions, what specific trigger condition would make you prepared to discuss deeper rationalization without treating that as a hidden closure mandate?

### To Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What exact, measurable temporary contribution package would the union accept if it were explicitly tied to binding no-closure guarantees, and what duration and review mechanism would make that package credible?

### To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
What precise internal measures have already been quantified by site, and by when will you provide a side-by-side comparison showing how far those measures close the gap before any closure is put forward?

### To Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Under what quantified conditions would you use your blocking power to support a restructuring package that includes site shrinkage or consolidation but still excludes full closures?

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to support a time-limited no-closure phase if it is paired with hard savings milestones and a defined fallback review, or do you require closures to remain available from the outset regardless of the model outcome?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum 5-year savings, capacity reduction, and implementation certainty thresholds would you need to see before you would back a no-closure or partial-rationalization package over a closure-led one?

### To Expert
**Question:**  
What single indicator in the site-by-site model would most strongly distinguish a viable no-closure restructuring from one that is likely to fail financially within five years?
