From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, I will be very direct:

We are **not** here to preserve legacy structures or to defend any one stakeholder’s comfort zone. We are here to protect the risk-adjusted value of our holdings. That means one thing above all else: **a credible return to competitive margins and a durable lower cost base**.

### Where we agree
We agree with management on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- capacity is misaligned with demand,
- and a cosmetic fix will not restore competitiveness.

We also agree with the family holding that the company cannot drift and that structural savings must be real, not symbolic.

And we agree with labor and the state on one important point: **prolonged open conflict destroys value**. Strikes, uncertainty, and drawn-out confrontation hurt margins, output, and investor confidence. We do not welcome that.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept a settlement that simply preserves every site, every guarantee, and the current cost structure while calling it restructuring.

That would be unacceptable because:
- it leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- it risks structurally impaired margins,
- and it pushes the burden into the future while shareholders absorb the valuation hit today.

Equally, we do **not** need a theatrical fight over closures if a credible alternative can deliver the same economic effect. The issue is not ideology; it is outcome.

### Our threshold
What matters to us is whether the final package clearly delivers:
1. **Durable cost reduction**
2. **Meaningful capacity adjustment**
3. **Margin improvement on a credible timeline**
4. **Deal certainty and operational stability**
5. **A defendable path to return on sales target**

If the answer is yes, we can support a negotiated solution even if it avoids the most disruptive measures. If the answer is no, then we will not back a cosmetic compromise.

### On plant closures and compulsory redundancies
We are not demanding closures as a principle. But we will not support a deal that rules out all hard choices in advance if those choices are necessary to restore competitiveness.

At the same time, we are also not interested in unnecessary destruction of value through prolonged industrial action or rushed plant shutdowns that trigger political backlash and execution risk. The best outcome is a **credible, enforceable restructuring package** that reduces fixed costs and capacity without leaving the company half-finished.

On compulsory redundancies: they should be avoided if possible, but not at the price of preserving an overstaffed structure. Voluntary instruments are preferable, but they must be sufficient in scale and speed. If they are not, then management and the owners need to be honest about that.

### On labor concessions
We would accept a workforce contribution only if it is:
- **real**
- **durable**
- **measurable**
- and part of a broader package that actually restores competitiveness.

We will not support a “contribution” that is just a one-time gesture while the structural problem remains untouched.

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on jobs and sites is understandable, but a categorical “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is only sustainable if you can show an alternative that genuinely fixes the economics. If you can’t, then you are asking capital to tolerate structurally impaired returns indefinitely. That is not acceptable.

### To Management
Do not present this as a choice between total war and a vague savings promise. Bring forward a package with:
- quantified savings,
- a clear implementation path,
- verifiable labor-cost relief,
- and a realistic impact on margins.

If you want investor support, the plan must be credible enough that we can defend it to our own clients and analysts.

### To the State and Family
We recognize your influence, but influence is not the same as economic justification. A politically palatable outcome that leaves the company underperforming will still destroy value. We need a solution that can survive not just the supervisory board, but also the capital markets.

### Bottom line
We are open to a compromise, but only a **competitively credible** one.

Not acceptable:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a prolonged strike without economic resolution,
- or a package that leaves margins structurally impaired.

Acceptable:
- a durable restructuring with real savings,
- whatever mix of capacity reduction, workforce adjustment, and labor-cost change is necessary,
- and a stable path back to competitive returns.

If the parties can deliver that, we will back it. If not, we will not pretend otherwise.
