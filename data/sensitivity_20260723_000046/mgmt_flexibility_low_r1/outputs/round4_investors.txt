We are still focused on one thing: **risk-adjusted value creation through a credible return to competitive margins**. On that basis, our position is unchanged, but it is not rigid.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert on the key diagnosis:

- this is a **structural** problem, not a temporary dip;
- **temporary measures alone are not enough**;
- the German cost base must come down **durably**;
- and a package that is impossible to implement is worthless.

We also agree with labor and the state on one practical point: **industrial conflict destroys value**. Prolonged strikes, delay, and governance paralysis are all shareholder-negative.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the labor/state position if it means:

- no plant closures under any circumstances,
- no compulsory redundancies regardless of economics,
- and meaningful guarantees on top.

That combination risks leaving the company with an **uncompetitive fixed cost base** and only partial restructuring. For us, that is not acceptable.

But we also do **not** accept management’s implied answer if it means closures and layoffs are simply asserted as necessary without a quantified business case. Urgency is not proof.

## Our threshold
We can support a difficult compromise only if it delivers all of the following:

1. **material and durable cost reduction**
2. **meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **implementation certainty**
4. **a credible path to competitive margins and ROS by 2027/2030**
5. **no cosmetic settlement that merely postpones the problem**

If those conditions can be met **without closures**, fine. If not, closures cannot be ruled out in principle.

## On closures
We are **not ideologically attached** to closures. But we are also **not willing to preclude them in advance**.

A no-closure package is acceptable only if it closes most of the gap in a way that is:
- measurable,
- durable,
- and executable.

If “no closures” really means “same footprint plus temporary concessions,” then we reject it. That would leave margins structurally impaired.

## On compulsory redundancies
Our preference is to avoid compulsory redundancies because they raise execution risk and can trigger prolonged conflict. But they cannot be excluded automatically if voluntary tools do not close the gap.

The sensible sequence remains:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement corridors,
- redeployment,
- working-time and pay moderation,
- productivity measures,
- and only then a hard look at whether the residual gap still requires stronger measures.

## On labor’s contribution
We are open to a **real, measurable, time-bound employee contribution**. But it must be:
- material enough to matter to the P&L,
- durable enough to count,
- and not become permanent wage erosion.

A symbolic gesture is not a restructuring.

## On management’s role
Management needs to stop arguing only at the level of principle and bring the numbers.

Specifically, we want a **site-by-site model** showing:
- current capacity and utilization,
- fixed-cost burden by plant,
- savings from partial rationalization,
- savings from closures,
- savings from voluntary exits and working-time changes,
- strike/delay risk,
- and the 5-year NPV of each option.

Without that, the market is being asked to endorse a conclusion without evidence.

## On the state and the family
We respect the governance reality, but governance is not a substitute for economics.

- The **state** should not use its blocking role to freeze all adjustment if the company remains structurally uncompetitive.
- The **family holding** should not use control to preserve legacy structures that impair long-term value.

Both should focus on the same thing we do: a credible route to sustainable margins.

## What we would not support
We will not support:
- cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive footprint,
- or a “socially acceptable” deal that leaves shareholders with structurally impaired returns.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to back a tough restructuring if it is real, quantified, and durable. We are not prepared to back a settlement that protects every site at the expense of competitiveness.

If management wants support, bring the numbers. If labor wants to avoid closures, show that the alternative truly closes the gap. Otherwise, the market will assume the company is choosing delay over value creation.
