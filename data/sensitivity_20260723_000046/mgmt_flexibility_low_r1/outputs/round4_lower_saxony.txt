I will be equally direct.

## My position
As anchor shareholder and regional government, I support **real restructuring**.  
I do **not** support a restructuring that relies on **German plant closures** or **compulsory redundancies** as its normal route.

That is not a symbolic line. It is a core political and economic position grounded in my responsibility for:
- regional employment,
- the industrial base,
- social stability,
- and the company’s long-term legitimacy in Germany.

If a package breaks those foundations, it is not a solution. It is a transfer of the crisis onto the region and the workforce.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that this must be **durable** and not cosmetic. Temporary fixes alone will not solve:
- overcapacity,
- the uncompetitive cost base,
- and the brand’s weak return-on-sales performance.

I also agree that implementation risk matters. A plan that cannot be executed is worthless.

### With the expert
I agree with the expert that the economics depend on:
- one-off restructuring costs,
- implementation delay,
- strike risk,
- and the ability to use internal tools first.

That is exactly why closures are **not** automatically the best answer in this governance setting.

### With labor
I agree that:
- temporary measures alone are insufficient,
- the workforce should not be the only adjustment variable,
- and any contribution from employees must be **limited, fair, and time-bound**.

I also agree that co-determination is not an obstacle. It is part of the governance reality and part of the company’s strength.

---

## Where I disagree

### With management
Management keeps repeating that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available because the cost gap is structural.

That is not yet a justification. It is a claim.

You have still not shown, site by site, that the alternatives are exhausted. Before you speak about closures, I want the full comparison of:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- retirement corridors,
- voluntary departures,
- shift reductions,
- working-time tools,
- temporary labour-cost contribution,
- and productivity measures.

If those measures cannot close most of the gap, prove it with numbers. Until then, closure remains a choice, not a necessity.

And let me be clear: using the possibility of closures as leverage before proving necessity is not responsible restructuring. It is pressure.

### With the works council
I understand your red line on closures and compulsory redundancies. I share that red line in substance.

Where I press you is on the need for a **credible, quantified alternative**. If you want to keep every German site open, then the package must show how that is financially and operationally sustainable through 2030.

That means hard numbers:
- capacity utilization,
- fixed cost by plant,
- savings from working-time changes,
- savings from redeployment,
- savings from voluntary exits,
- savings from temporary contributions,
- and the product allocation path.

I support the no-closure objective. But it cannot rest on assertion alone.

### With the union
I agree that temporary contributions must not become permanent wage dumping.

But I also need to be candid: a contribution that is too small will not solve a structural problem. If the workforce is asked to help, the contribution must be:
- material enough to matter,
- limited in duration,
- and matched by binding commitments from management and owners.

### With the family holding
You say you do not want unnecessary destruction, but you keep closures open as an option.

That may sound balanced from an ownership perspective. From the region’s perspective, it means the pressure remains on workers and local sites while the owners preserve the option to sacrifice them.

I do not accept that as the default logic in this company.

If Germany is truly central to long-term value, then the ownership side should back a settlement that preserves plants and jobs where possible, rather than normalizing closure as a restructuring tool.

### With investors
You are right to demand credibility and margin improvement. But credibility is not only a spreadsheet issue.

A settlement that triggers prolonged conflict, skills loss, industrial distrust, and political resistance may look “clean” on paper and still destroy value in practice.

So yes, margins matter. But the value of industrial peace, trust, and regional legitimacy also matters here — materially.

---

## On the central false choice
What I reject is the false framing that we only have two options:

1. closures and forced redundancies, or  
2. preserving the status quo.

That is wrong.

There is a third path:
- keep German sites open,
- reduce overcapacity through internal measures,
- use voluntary exits and retirement corridors,
- reshape working time,
- make a fair temporary workforce contribution,
- and bind the company to investment and future product allocation in Germany.

That is a real restructuring path. It is not a soft one.

---

## What I am prepared to support
I can support a negotiated package if it includes all of the following:

- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **real capacity reduction through internal measures**
- **temporary, limited employee contribution**
- **binding investment commitments for German sites**
- **binding product allocation through 2030**
- **preservation of co-determination**
- **a credible industrial future for the state’s sites**

That is a serious package. It is not a veto on change. It is a veto on social demolition.

---

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures dressed up as “rationalization,”
- forced layoffs,
- dismantling the job-security framework,
- a deal that treats co-determination as a nuisance,
- or a settlement where workers and the region absorb the damage first and ownership keeps the upside.

If that is the path chosen, then I will use the state’s blocking position accordingly.

---

## What I need next
I support the call for a **site-by-site quantified model**. But it must not become a stealth closure roadmap. The purpose must be to test whether a no-closure package is viable, not to pre-select plants for sacrifice.

So I support this specialist request:

[SUMMON: restructuring finance and automotive operations specialist to provide a site-by-site 5-year comparison of no-closure, partial rationalization, and closure scenarios, including strike risk, delay, social-plan costs, product allocation through 2030, and regional employment impact]

That is the right basis for judging whether the company can be restructured without breaking its regional responsibility.

---

## Bottom line
My position remains firm:

- **Real restructuring: yes**
- **Plant closures in Germany: no**
- **Compulsory redundancies: no**
- **Temporary, fair workforce contribution: yes**
- **Binding investment and product commitments: yes**
- **Co-determination and the state’s veto role: preserved**

If management and ownership want my support, they must bring a package that genuinely protects jobs, sites, and the company’s industrial future. If they cannot, then the problem is not lack of willingness on my side. It is the failure to produce a restructuring plan that respects the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.
