The union’s position is very clear:

We agree with the Works Council on the core line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiating detail; it is the minimum basis for any serious settlement. We will not accept that workers pay for all the structural problems while management and owners preserve their options.

We also agree that there are **alternatives to blunt cuts**:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary instruments,
- and a **fair employee contribution**.

But let me be equally clear: a contribution from the workforce is only acceptable if it is **balanced by real concessions from management, owners, and the company overall**. If the entire burden lands on employees through wage cuts, longer hours, or erosion of guarantees, then that is not a restructuring deal — it is one-sided social dumping.

What we need is a package with three non-negotiable elements:

1. **Job and site security**
   - No compulsory layoffs.
   - No closures of German plants.
   - Clear protection against open-ended insecurity.

2. **Shared sacrifice**
   - The workforce can discuss temporary or limited labor-cost relief, bonus waivers, or a solidarity contribution.
   - But management, shareholders, and the company must also contribute materially.
   - We will not accept employees carrying the full adjustment.

3. **A durable industrial future**
   - Binding commitments on investment, product allocation, and future technologies in Germany.
   - A restructuring that creates a viable path forward instead of hollowing out the workforce and then expecting loyalty afterward.

If management insists that closures and forced job cuts are the only route, then we reject that premise. It is not credible to present the workforce as the problem while the company’s strategic and market choices are treated as untouchable. We are ready to negotiate hard, and we are ready to use industrial action strategically if necessary. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to defend their interests.

So our message is simple: **we are open to a fair, shared-sacrifice solution; we are not open to a restructuring built on plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or one-sided wage destruction.**
