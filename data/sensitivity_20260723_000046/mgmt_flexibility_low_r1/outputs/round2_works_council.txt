Management, the family holding, and the capital market investors keep repeating “credible restructuring,” but they are still avoiding the central question: **credible for whom, and at what social cost?**

Let me be equally direct in Round 2.

## Where I agree
I agree that the company needs a real restructuring and that cosmetic measures are not enough. I also agree that:
- capacity has to be adjusted,
- qualification and redeployment must be used,
- working-time solutions can be part of the answer,
- and any agreement must be durable, not a short-lived patch.

I also agree that open conflict is bad for the company. But do not confuse that with consent to plant closures or forced layoffs. The workforce did not create this crisis, and it will not accept being treated as the sole adjustment variable.

## Where I disagree
I strongly reject the idea that **plant closures in Germany are a normal or necessary restructuring tool**. That is exactly the line we cannot cross.

I also reject any plan that includes **compulsory redundancies**. If management wants to talk about restructuring, then it must first exhaust:
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary departures,
- retirement corridors,
- reduced hours where socially acceptable,
- qualification for new tasks,
- and a fair redistribution of work.

Only after that can anyone credibly claim there is no alternative. And right now, that claim has not been proven. It is being asserted.

## To management
You say the company needs “structural” savings. Fine. Then bring **specific numbers**:
- Which capacity is supposedly surplus?
- What savings come from plant closures versus from alternative tools?
- What investment volume is committed to Germany?
- What future model allocation is guaranteed?
- What exactly is the 2030 employment picture, site by site?

Without that, “necessary” is just a slogan used to pressure workers into concessions.

And let me be clear: **you do not get a blank cheque to restructure by threatening jobs and then demanding trust from the very people whose trust you broke.**

## To the family holding
You say closures may be necessary if sites are uncompetitive. That may sound hard-headed, but from our perspective it is a dangerous shortcut. Once you start treating closure as an acceptable management tool, the burden of strategic failure falls on workers and regions first.

If the family really cares about long-term enterprise value, then it should back a solution that preserves industrial capability in Germany instead of sacrificing sites for a quick accounting fix.

## To the investors
You keep talking about margins and return on sales, but you ignore the cost of destroying trust, industrial know-how, and labour peace. A “solution” that triggers lasting conflict, weakens co-determination, and tears out the company’s social foundations is not a value-preserving solution. It is a risk transfer onto the workforce and the public.

If you want a stable return, then support a stable industrial base. Otherwise you are just asking employees to underwrite shareholder impatience.

## To the state government
I appreciate that you are standing up for regional employment and co-determination. But I need to be blunt: **your blocking position must not become a negotiation about how much pain workers should absorb before you object in principle.** We need you to use that leverage now to prevent closures and forced redundancies, not after damage has already been done.

## What would actually move us
There is only one kind of package that could move the Works Council, and it must contain all of the following:

1. **Binding guarantees that every German plant remains open**
2. **A hard exclusion of compulsory redundancies**
3. **Protection of the job-security framework through 2030**
4. **A genuine industrial plan with investment and product commitments in Germany**
5. **A fair, limited workforce contribution only if management and owners also contribute materially**
6. **Real co-determination in shaping the implementation, not just symbolic consultation**

That is the minimum for serious talks.

## What is not acceptable
Not acceptable are:
- plant closures,
- forced layoffs,
- dismantling the historic job-security framework,
- “flexibility” used as a euphemism for insecurity,
- and restructuring by ultimatum.

If management believes it can secure a two-thirds majority for closures against the workforce and co-determination framework, it is underestimating the legal and political reality of this company.

## Bottom line
We are ready to discuss **how** to secure competitiveness **without breaking the social contract**. We are **not** ready to accept closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of doing business.

So the next step is not more pressure. It is a concrete counterproposal from management that proves savings can be achieved **without crossing our red lines**. If that cannot be done, then the real problem is not the workers’ position — it is management’s failure to develop a viable plan.
