## Where I disagree

Before you even mention closures, you need to show that all of the following have been genuinely exhausted and quantified:
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- retirement corridors,
- voluntary departures,
- working-time reductions,
- shift reductions,
- productivity measures,
- temporary wage moderation,

I do not accept that. If the family truly values Germany as the industrial core, then it must commit to **keeping every German plant open** and to building a long-term product and investment base here. Not merely “considering” it.

### With investors

A no-closure settlement is not “soft” if it delivers:
- durable savings,
- capacity adjustments,
- workforce flexibility,
- and real productivity gains,
while preserving skills, trust, and industrial peace.

That is value creation too. In fact, it is often the more intelligent form of value creation in a co-determined company.

## The central false assumption I reject
The capital side keeps acting as if there are only two options:

1. closures and forced layoffs, or  
2. preserving the status quo.

That is false.

There is a third path:
- keep every German plant open,
- eliminate overcapacity through internal measures,
- secure voluntary reductions,
- reshape working time,
- use temporary contribution measures,
- and bind management to investment and future product allocation through 2030.

That is the path we should be pursuing — because it protects jobs and still delivers restructuring.

## On the “site-by-site” argument
I hear the request for a site-by-site review. Fine. But let me be very clear: a site-by-site review is **not** a gateway to plant closures by stealth.

It must start from this principle:
- every German site is to be kept open unless management can prove, with hard numbers, that no other measure can make it viable.

That is the correct burden of proof in a co-determined company with a historic job-security framework.

If management wants to claim a site is “non-viable,” then it must show:
- the actual capacity utilization,
- the exact fixed-cost burden,
- the savings from shift and working-time changes,
- the gains from redeployment,
- the savings from voluntary departures,
- the effect of temporary cost contributions,
- and the future product allocation through 2030.

If the numbers are not there, then closure is not justified. Full stop.
