Let me be equally direct.

I hear the rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand why you are drawing that line. But I need to be clear: **the company’s current cost and capacity structure is not competitive, and pretending otherwise will not save jobs in the long run.** We are facing a shrinking European market, brutal competition, and factory costs in Germany that are materially above target. That is not a communication problem; it is a structural problem.

So where do I agree?

- Yes, we need a **durable, socially managed restructuring**, not chaos.
- Yes, there should be **redeployment, qualification, voluntary instruments, and working-time solutions** where they actually deliver savings.
- Yes, we need a **credible industrial future in Germany**, including investment in future products and technology.

Where I disagree is the central point:

- **A blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” position is not compatible with restoring competitiveness.**
- **A deal that preserves all sites and all guarantees while leaving the cost base largely intact is not acceptable.**
- **A cosmetic package of temporary measures, bonus waivers, or vague promises will not meet the company’s needs.**

We need a package that delivers **real, structural, and durable** reductions in cost and capacity. That means all of the following must be on the table:

1. **Capacity reduction**
   - If plants remain open, they still must contribute meaningfully to a lower footprint.
   - Overcapacity has to come out of the system one way or another.

2. **A genuine labour-cost contribution**
   - Not symbolic. Not one-time optics.
   - We need sustained relief on labour cost to bring Germany back into line.

3. **Job reductions**
   - I am not going to pretend the workforce can be fully insulated from restructuring.
   - There will be fewer jobs in the future than today if we are serious about competitiveness.

4. **Freedom to consider plant closures**
   - I am not announcing closures as a slogan.
   - But I am not taking them off the table either, because removing that option would handcuff the restructuring and make it impossible to reach the required savings.

What I am prepared to discuss is **how** the savings are achieved, not whether they are achieved. If you can bring forward a package that delivers a comparable structural effect through other means — substantial capacity cuts, durable labour-cost reductions, and a credible path to lower fixed costs — then that is discussable. But it has to be real, measurable, and sustainable through 2030.

On your demand for binding guarantees: I will not trade away the company’s ability to restructure in exchange for promises that freeze the current cost base. We can discuss investment commitments, product allocation, and transition support — but only if they go together with an adjustment package that actually fixes the economics.

And I need to say this plainly: if the response is strikes and blockades, that will damage output, reputation, and trust — but it will not change the underlying math. It will only increase pressure to act more sharply. I would rather reach an agreement, but it must be an agreement that restores competitiveness.

So my position is simple:
- **No cosmetic deal.**
- **No preservation of the status quo.**
- **Yes to a durable restructuring package that materially reduces costs and capacity.**

If you want, bring me a counterproposal that achieves that outcome without plant closures. But if it does not deliver the necessary structural savings, it will not be acceptable.
