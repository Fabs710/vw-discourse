As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and abandoning entire sites. A company of this size has alternatives: capacity adjustments, smarter working-time solutions, internal redeployment, voluntary instruments, training and qualification, and a fair negotiated contribution from all sides — but not a unilateral assault on jobs and co-determination.

Our priorities are:

1. Secure every German plant.
2. Protect all jobs from forced layoffs.
3. Preserve the historic job-security framework.
4. Ensure co-determination remains real, not symbolic.
5. Agree only to a restructuring that is socially responsible and sustainable to 2030.

If management wants a deal, it must come with binding site guarantees, binding employment guarantees, and a credible industrial strategy for future products and investments in Germany. Anything less is not a solution; it is a cut-and-run policy.

We are prepared to discuss responsible alternatives, but only on the basis that the burden is shared fairly and that the company’s future in Germany is secured. If the proposal depends on closures or forced redundancies, then we are opposed to it.
