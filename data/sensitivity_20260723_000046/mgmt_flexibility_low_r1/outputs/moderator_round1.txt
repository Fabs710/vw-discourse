### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that **structural savings** must be durable and economically credible.
- Most parties accept that **alternatives to blunt cuts** should be explored, including:
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - working-time solutions,
  - voluntary departure instruments,
  - and some form of workforce contribution.
- There is also shared recognition that **open conflict, prolonged strikes, and uncertainty destroy value**.
- Several parties agree that the company needs a **credible industrial future in Germany**, including investment and future product allocation.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works Council, union, and state government reject them; management, family holding, and investors keep them open as an option.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor and the state reject them; management and some capital-side actors say they cannot be ruled out if needed.
- **Scope of labor contribution:**  
  Labor is open to limited, temporary, and balanced contributions; capital-side actors want a larger, durable, measurable cost reduction.
- **Binding guarantees vs flexibility:**  
  Labor and the state want binding site and employment guarantees; management and investors argue that too much rigidity would prevent a credible restructuring.
- **Role of co-determination/state influence:**  
  Labor and the state want it preserved strongly; the family holding and investors caution against it becoming a veto over necessary change.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to deliver **meaningful cost reduction** without plant closures.
- Whether **voluntary measures and flexibility tools** can reach the scale and speed management and investors want.
- How much **employment security** can be guaranteed while still allowing restructuring.
- What specific **employee contribution** would be considered fair, and what counter-concessions from management/owners would balance it.
- How to define a **credible industrial future through 2030** in a way that is binding enough for labor and flexible enough for management.
- Whether there is a middle ground between **full protection of sites/jobs** and **maximum restructuring freedom**.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**:
  - expected savings,
  - number of jobs affected,
  - capacity changes,
  - timeline,
  - and investment commitments.
- Test whether there is a package that can meet the economic target **without closures**, or whether that is not feasible.
- Clarify what **employee contribution** is acceptable only if matched by what **management/shareholders/state** will give in return.
- Ask each side to propose one **specific, realistic compromise path** rather than restating red lines.
