I will be direct.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company does need durable competitiveness, and temporary gestures alone will not fix the problem.

But I **do not** accept the leap you keep making from “durable restructuring” to “therefore closures and compulsory redundancies must remain fully available.” That is exactly where we part ways.

You have still not shown that plant closures are the only credible route. You have asserted urgency, not proven necessity. Before you ask for a mandate to break sites and jobs in Germany, you need to present:
- site-by-site capacity data,
- the fixed-cost burden by plant,
- the savings from each alternative,
- the investment plan for Germany,
- and a 2030 employment path.

Without that, your argument is incomplete.

I am open to capacity reduction, working-time tools, redeployment, voluntary exits, and a serious employee contribution. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices plants and regional employment as the first answer. That would be a political and economic mistake, and I will not support it.

## To the Works Council
I agree with your central line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not an emotional reflex; it is a legitimate red line for a company that is a regional anchor and a co-determined enterprise.

I also agree that there are alternatives that must be exhausted first:
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary departures,
- retirement corridors,
- working-time flexibility,
- and site specialization.

Where I would press you is this: if you are asking others to accept a negotiated alternative, it must be concrete and credible. “No closures” is a position; it is not yet a complete restructuring concept. I would like to see a package that shows how much capacity can be reduced, how many jobs can move voluntarily, and what investment commitments secure the remaining sites.

That said, your core demand is justified, and I will back it.

## To the union
I agree that any workforce contribution must be **fair, limited, and matched by real concessions from management and owners**. I also agree that the wage framework cannot simply be hollowed out under the label of “contribution.”

Where I disagree with management and some capital-side voices is on the idea that a “real” contribution must automatically mean deeper wage cuts or permanent erosion of protections. That is not social partnership; that is one-sided adjustment.

A serious contribution could include:
- temporary moderation,
- bonus waivers,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity-linked measures,
- and voluntary instruments.

But it must come with:
- binding site protection,
- no forced redundancies,
- and a real investment commitment in Germany.

## To the family holding
I respect the ownership responsibility to protect long-term value. But I caution you against treating closures as a normal management tool in a company like this.

Yes, the company must not drift. But a restructuring that damages trust, regional capability, and industrial depth in Germany may “clean up” the spreadsheet while weakening the enterprise’s long-term position. That is not responsible value preservation from where I stand.

If you want long-term value, then support a solution that keeps the German industrial base intact and uses the company’s flexibility intelligently rather than destructively.

## To the institutional investors
You are right that prolonged conflict destroys value. I do not dispute that. But you are underestimating the value destruction from closing plants, breaking co-determination trust, and hollowing out a regional anchor company.

A package that keeps industrial peace, protects key sites, and still delivers real savings through negotiated adjustment may look less dramatic, but it can be more durable and less risky. That is the kind of compromise worth supporting. What I will not support is using market pressure to force a short-term fix that leaves lasting social and political damage.

## Where I stand
My position remains clear:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of the state’s co-determination and blocking role**
- **Yes to real restructuring**
- **Yes to capacity adjustment**
- **Yes to a negotiated, fair workforce contribution**
- **Yes to binding investment and future-product commitments in Germany**

I am willing to help broker a solution that protects jobs, preserves sites, and restores competitiveness. I am **not** willing to be part of a process that turns the regional economy into the adjustment buffer for management mistakes.

## What I would support
A viable compromise would need to include:
1. site guarantees for German plants,
2. exclusion of compulsory redundancies,
3. a measurable but limited workforce contribution,
4. management and owner concessions as part of the package,
5. binding investment in future products and technology in Germany,
6. and real co-determination in implementation.

If management can show that this package still delivers the required savings, I will engage seriously. If it cannot, then the answer is not to force closures through pressure; it is to revisit the restructuring concept.

## Bottom line
I support a serious restructuring.  
I do **not** support closures or forced layoffs in Germany.  
I will use my blocking position to prevent an outcome that guts regional employment or sidelines co-determination.

If management wants a path forward, bring a quantified alternative that protects sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness. That is the standard I will judge by.
