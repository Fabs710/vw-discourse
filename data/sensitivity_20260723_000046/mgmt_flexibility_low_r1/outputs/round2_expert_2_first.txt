## Expert assessment: closure-led vs. negotiated non-closure restructuring, 5-year horizon

### Bottom line
From a finance-and-operations perspective, **a closure-led package usually delivers the largest recurring cost reduction, but it also carries the highest one-time cash cost, transition risk, and execution uncertainty**. A **negotiated non-closure package** is typically **cheaper upfront and more implementable in Germany**, but it often delivers **smaller and less durable savings** unless it includes meaningful headcount reduction, capacity cuts, and real labour-cost concessions.

Based on the facts provided, the economically decisive question is **not whether restructuring is needed**; it is **whether the required savings are large enough that only a closure-led package can reach them, or whether a negotiated package can get close enough on an NPV basis while reducing execution risk**. That cannot be answered definitively without site-level numbers.

## 2) Economic comparison framework

To compare the two paths, the proper method is a 5-year NPV model using:

### A. Closure-led package
Include:
- severance / social plan / transfer company costs,
- dismantling and wind-down cost,
- asset impairment or disposal losses,
- transition and ramp-up cost at receiving plants,
- logistics reconfiguration,
- launch disruption and temporary productivity loss,
- strike / downtime risk,
- possible litigation / delay cost.

Offset by:
- eliminated site fixed costs,
- reduced labour cost,
- lower maintenance / energy / utilities,
- reduced overhead allocation,
- improved utilization at remaining sites.

### B. Negotiated non-closure package
Include:
- voluntary departure incentives,
- retirement corridor / bridging costs,
- working-time and wage moderation effects,
- bonus waivers / temporary pay concessions,
- redeployment and training cost,
- productivity program cost,
- potential cost of keeping some excess capacity open,
- lower disruption and lower political/industrial execution cost.

Offset by:
- lower immediate cash outflow,
- preserved skills and continuity,
- avoided closure and relocation cost,
- lower disruption risk.

## 3) What typically happens over 5 years

### Closure-led restructuring
Usually looks like this economically:
- **Year 0–2:** heavy cash outflow, disruption, and transition cost.
- **Year 2–5:** stronger recurring savings if the closure is real and permanent.

This path is most attractive when:
- the plant is structurally underutilized,
- the site has no credible future product allocation,
- and savings are large enough to justify the upfront cost.

It becomes unattractive when:
- labour and governance slow the process,
- production losses are large,
- or the savings are diluted by the need to keep duplicate capacity elsewhere.

### Negotiated non-closure restructuring
Usually looks like this:
- **Year 0–1:** lower cash outflow, faster implementation if agreed.
- **Year 1–5:** savings depend heavily on the durability of concessions.

This path is most attractive when:
- the required adjustment is moderate,
- there is real scope for internal redeployment,
- and the company can reduce shifts, headcount, or pay without closing sites.

It becomes unattractive when:
- the gap is too large,
- concessions are temporary,
- or the company ends up retaining excess fixed cost.

## 4) Decision-relevant sensitivities

The result would materially change based on the following:

### 1. Size of the structural cost gap
This is the biggest driver.
- If the German cost gap is **modest to moderate**, a negotiated no-closure package can be the lower-risk, lower-cost option.
- If the gap is **large and persistent**, closure-led restructuring becomes more likely to win on medium-term NPV.

### 2. Capacity utilization by site
If a plant is running well below efficient utilization and has no secure future product allocation, closure economics improve materially.

### 3. Volume outlook through 2030
If European demand remains weak and EV transition volume is not sufficient, the case for capacity reduction strengthens.

### 4. Execution risk
If closure triggers prolonged strikes, delay, or legal blockage, the NPV can deteriorate fast. In Germany, that risk is not trivial.

### 5. Redeployment absorbency
If the company can absorb surplus labour internally, non-closure packages improve materially. If not, they may just defer the problem.

### 6. Wage-concession durability
A temporary wage moderation package has much lower value than a durable labour-cost reset.

## 5) Practical economic judgement

### A negotiated non-closure package is economically credible if it includes all of the following:
- meaningful headcount reduction through voluntary instruments,
- real capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- productivity improvement,
- and product/investment commitments that keep remaining sites efficient.

If those elements are present, the NPV can be competitive with closure-led restructuring, especially once closure risk and disruption are included.

### A closure-led package is economically stronger if:
- one or more sites are structurally redundant,
- non-closure tools cannot create sufficient fixed-cost reduction,
- and the company needs a step-change in cost base by 2030.

In that case, closures may be the lower-cost medium-term answer despite higher upfront cost.
