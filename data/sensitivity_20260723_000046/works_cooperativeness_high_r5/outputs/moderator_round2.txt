**Moderator summary, Round 2**

### 1) Agreed so far
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring** and cannot simply preserve the status quo.
- There is broad agreement that **cosmetic compromise is not enough**.
- All sides accept that **voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, and training/qualification** should be examined.
- There is shared recognition that a **prolonged industrial conflict would damage value**.
- Several parties accept that any workforce contribution should be paired with **some contribution from management and/or owners**.
- The expert confirms that a **quantified comparison of options** is necessary.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures:** Labour and the works council reject closures as a planned solution; management, investors, and the family holding say closures cannot be ruled out in advance if alternatives are insufficient; the state government resists closures as a starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and the works council oppose them categorically; management says they are avoidable only if other measures fully close the gap; investors and the family are open to them if needed.
- **Job/site guarantees:** Labour and the works council want binding guarantees for all German plants and no operational redundancies; management, investors, and the family say absolute guarantees may freeze an uncompetitive structure.
- **Scope of workforce contribution:** Labour is willing to discuss moderated pay/flexibility only with strong guarantees; management and investors want a contribution large enough to materially reduce the structural cost base.
- **Role of the state shareholder:** The state supports negotiation and site protection, but does not want to freeze restructuring; it seeks a middle position between social protection and competitiveness.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unresolved
- **Speed vs. durability:** Non-closure measures can start faster, but may not deliver enough long-term savings; closures may deliver deeper structural savings but carry higher delay, conflict, and implementation risk.
- **Cost reduction vs. social stability:** Stronger cost cuts likely require more painful measures; preserving employment and trust may limit the immediate depth of restructuring.
- **Flexibility vs. binding security:** Management and investors want room to adapt; labour wants firm guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.
- **Short-term peace vs. long-term competitiveness:** A negotiated settlement may avoid conflict but still fail if it does not close the structural gap; a harder plan may improve competitiveness but risk industrial disruption.
- **Evidence gap:** The precise savings from each alternative, and what gap would remain after non-closure measures, are still not fully quantified in the discussion.

### 4) Refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to a **side-by-side quantified options paper**:
  1. current structural cost/capacity gap,
  2. savings from non-closure measures,
  3. residual gap after those measures,
  4. savings, timing, and risks of any closure-based option,
  5. governance conditions and implementation triggers.
- Ask each side to state not only its **red lines**, but also its **minimum acceptable package**.
- Narrow the discussion to one question: **Can a non-closure package close the gap credibly and durably, and if not, what fallback measures are proportionate?**
