### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company faces a serious structural competitiveness problem and that some form of restructuring is necessary.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should be negotiated rather than imposed.
- Most parties support a package that includes future investment, new products/technology, and measures to improve long-term viability.
- There is shared interest in using softer adjustment tools where possible, such as voluntary exits, retirement, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and internal adjustments.
- There is general recognition that a one-sided burden on employees would not be durable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Employee representatives, the state shareholder, and to a degree the family holding oppose closures as an early or default option; management and institutional investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Strong resistance from labour and the state side; management and investors will only accept avoidance if the overall package still delivers sufficient structural savings.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour seeks binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment; management and investors view blanket guarantees through 2030 as too rigid.
- **Cost reduction approach:** Management and investors want material, verifiable, durable reductions in fixed costs and capacity; labour wants those linked to protections and burden-sharing.
- **Governance/co-determination:** Labour and the state insist on a real role in restructuring decisions; management and investors want enough freedom to execute meaningful change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve the required cost and capacity reduction without plant closures or layoffs**, if that is possible.
- **What management, owners, and capital will contribute**, beyond asking employees for concessions.
- **Which guarantees can be made binding** without removing all flexibility from restructuring.
- **Whether any workforce contribution will be temporary or permanent**, and what concrete protections would accompany it.
- **What constitutes “last resort” for closures**, and what evidence would prove alternatives have been exhausted.
- **How future investment commitments will be tied to site-specific restructuring outcomes.**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Ask the parties to table **a side-by-side comparison of options**: closures/layoffs versus alternative measures, with quantified savings, timing, and risks.
- Require each side to specify **non-negotiables and possible flexibilities** in concrete terms.
- Invite management to present **a detailed industrial and investment plan** that shows where future work will be placed.
- Ask labour and the state side to identify **what level of cost/capacity change they could accept** if paired with binding protections.
- Ask the owner/shareholder and investors to clarify **what burden-sharing and governance conditions** they would support for a durable deal.
