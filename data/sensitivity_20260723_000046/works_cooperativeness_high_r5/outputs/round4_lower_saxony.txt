I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one essential point: **this cannot be solved by slogans**. The company has a real structural problem, and a durable solution must be **quantified, plant-by-plant, and burden-shared**.

I also agree that:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement pathways,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- training and qualification,
- overhead cuts,
- management restraint,
- and real owner contributions

must all be part of the package. Those are not optional. They are the first test of whether management is serious.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept closures as the starting point
Management keeps speaking as if plant closures are the natural endpoint once “the numbers” are reviewed. That is not acceptable in this governance and regional context.

We are not dealing with a normal industrial footprint adjustment. We are dealing with a company that is:
- a major employer in our state,
- embedded in a special statute with a blocking minority,
- and subject to co-determination with an effective workforce veto.

In that setting, closures are not a technical default. They are a **last-resort outcome only if everything else has genuinely failed**.

The burden is on management and capital to prove that the non-closure route, combined with their own contributions and product allocation shifts, cannot close the gap. Until then, closures are **unproven**, not justified.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies
That remains a hard red line.

If the restructuring plan depends on forced layoffs, then it is not a social-partnership solution, and it is not one I can support. We can discuss workforce contribution. We cannot discuss discarding workers as the primary adjustment tool.

### 3) I do not accept a trigger mechanism that normalizes closure
I understand the moderator’s “alternatives-first with a trigger” idea. But I have to be explicit: a trigger can easily become a **standing threat** that pre-judges the outcome.

I can support a sequenced process to test alternatives. I cannot support a process that quietly bakes in closure as the assumed fallback before the non-closure package has been fairly exhausted.

## What the matrix must prove
The expert and the moderator are right about one thing: we need a **real plant-by-plant matrix**. But it must answer the question properly.

That means for each site:
1. current structural gap,
2. savings from non-closure measures,
3. management contribution,
4. owner contribution,
5. product and volume allocation,
6. residual gap,
7. and only then the question of whether any capacity exit remains necessary.

And I want a **strict separation** between:
- internal capacity reduction,
- partial line exit,
- and outright plant closure.

Those are not the same thing, and I will not let them be blurred together.

Value is not only short-term margin. It is also:

- retirement windows,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- training,

What I will **not** support:
- plant closures as a default assumption,
- forced layoffs,
- or any “solution” that shifts strategic failure onto employees and the region.
