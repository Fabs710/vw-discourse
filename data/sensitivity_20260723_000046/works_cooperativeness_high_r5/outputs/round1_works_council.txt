We are prepared to discuss a restructuring, but not at the expense of our people and our sites.

Our position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No breaking of the historic job-security framework.
- Any contribution from the workforce must be tied to binding guarantees for jobs, sites, and investment.
- The works council must have a real co-determination role in shaping any restructuring path.

We recognise the company faces serious competitive pressure. We do not deny the need for action. But the burden cannot be placed mainly on the workforce while management asks employees to pay for strategic mistakes. A solution that destroys jobs, closes plants, or turns long-standing guarantees into scraps of paper is unacceptable.

If management wants a durable settlement, then we need:

1. A binding guarantee that all German plants remain open through 2030.
2. No operational layoffs; departures only through natural attrition, retirement, and agreed voluntary schemes.
3. A credible industrial plan with future products and investments for German sites.
4. A fair burden-sharing approach, including management’s own contribution, before asking employees for sacrifices.
5. A genuine say for employee representatives in any restructuring decisions.

If those conditions are not on the table, then I will oppose the proposal clearly and forcefully. We have the means to do that, and we will use them.
