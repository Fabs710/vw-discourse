## To management
You asked for numbers, and the expert confirms the real issue: **a non-closure package must be quantified, or your closure threat is just a threat**. That is exactly why I have been insisting on a plant-by-plant comparison.

But let me be equally clear: I do **not** accept your conclusion that because non-closure measures may not close 100% of the gap on paper, therefore German plant closures become the default. That is a leap, not a necessity.

What the expert actually said is:
- non-closure measures can be meaningful,
- they may cover a substantial part of the gap,
- and the remainder might still be bridged by a **shared-sacrifice package** if management, owners, and product allocation also contribute.

So before you talk about closures, put your own numbers on the table:
- What is the exact structural gap?
- What do you contribute from management overhead, executive restraint, procurement, and product allocation?
- What fixed costs are you personally prepared to remove at the top?
- What volumes will be placed in German plants to improve utilization?

If you cannot show that non-closure measures fail **after** those contributions, then closure is not a proven necessity. It is management choosing the bluntest instrument first.

And I will say this again because it remains the core line:
**no compulsory redundancies**.  
If your plan depends on forced layoffs, then you are asking workers to pay for strategic failure they did not cause.

## To the works council
I agree with you on the essentials:
- no forced layoffs,
- no unilateral site destruction,
- real co-determination,
- and no one-sided sacrifice.

Where I need to sharpen the joint position is this: we should not just say “no” to closures. We should force management into a credible alternative that meets the social and industrial requirements of the company.

The expert’s matrix is useful here. It confirms that there are intermediate options between “everything stays as is” and “close a plant”:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement windows,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- partial footprint reduction,
- line transfers,
- overhead cuts.

That is the negotiating space. I support using it.

1. **no compulsory redundancies,**
2. **no closures as part of the package,**
3. **binding investment and future-product commitments,**
4. **preservation of the collective bargaining framework,**
5. **and a fair management/owner contribution.**

That remains my position.

And I will repeat for the record: investors do not get to demand that employees carry the pain while capital and management remain protected from the consequences of their own decisions.
