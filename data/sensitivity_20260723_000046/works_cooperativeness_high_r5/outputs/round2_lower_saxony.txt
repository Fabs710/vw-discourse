I will be direct.

I agree with management on one point: the company does need a durable restructuring, and simply preserving the status quo would be irresponsible. I also agree that any solution must be economically credible and capable of restoring competitiveness for the long term.

But I do **not** agree that plant closures in Germany are the starting point, or that the workforce should be asked to carry the main burden before management, owners, and the business have exhausted other options.

## To management
You have still not demonstrated that closures and forced redundancies are unavoidable. You say the current structure is too expensive and the capacity footprint too large. I do not dispute the pressure. What I dispute is the leap from “there is a problem” to “therefore we must close German plants.”

That is not yet proven.

If you want support from the state shareholder, then bring a **quantified comparison**:
- what savings come from voluntary exits,
- what comes from working-time tools,
- what comes from internal redeployment,
- what comes from capacity reductions short of closure,
- what management itself contributes,
- and what the investment consequences are.

Until that is on the table, I will not treat closures as a legitimate first option.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce cannot be the sole shock absorber. You are right that a settlement must be tied to binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment. You are also right that compulsory redundancies would be socially and politically destructive.

Where I will be frank is this: if your position is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no change,” then that is not enough either. I do not support freezing the company in place. We need a restructuring that protects the regional industrial base **and** improves competitiveness.

So I can support:
- voluntary schemes,
- retirement pathways,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal transfers,
- temporary and carefully bounded workforce contributions,
- and a serious industrial plan for future products.

But I will only support these if they are linked to:
- binding site protection,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- and a clear commitment that German plants remain part of the company’s future.

## To the controlling family
You are right that cosmetic compromises are not enough. I share that concern. But I also reject the implication that the only serious stewardship is to keep closure on the table as a routine instrument.

This company is not just a balance sheet. It is a regional economic anchor and a social partnership system. If restructuring destroys trust, jobs, and industrial legitimacy, then the “solution” will create a larger strategic problem than the one it is meant to solve.

So yes, I want durable competitiveness. But I want it achieved in a way that preserves the company’s long-term franchise in Germany.

## To the institutional investors
I understand your focus on margins, returns, and verifiable savings. That is legitimate. But a purely financial reading of this situation is too narrow. A settlement that triggers prolonged conflict, destroys trust, or weakens the company’s industrial base will also destroy value.

You say closures should not be used as theatre. I agree. But neither should worker guarantees be treated as empty obstacles. This is an industrial company with a social structure that has value in itself.

## My line
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring if it meets these conditions:

1. **No compulsory redundancies.**
2. **No plant closures as an opening or default instrument.**
3. **A serious, quantified alternative package is tested first.**
4. **Any workforce contribution is matched by management and owner contribution.**
5. **Binding commitments on investment and future products for German sites.**
6. **Co-determination and the state’s veto role remain intact.**

What I will **not** support is a settlement that:
- uses competitiveness as a blanket justification for closures,
- strips the workforce of security,
- or weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor.

If management wants my support, then show that the required savings can be achieved without gutting German employment and without turning the state shareholder into a bystander. If that is not possible, then I will use the leverage we have.
