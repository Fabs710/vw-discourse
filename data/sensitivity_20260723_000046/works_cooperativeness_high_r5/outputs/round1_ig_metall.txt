The union’s position is very clear: we are not prepared to accept a restructuring that turns into a one-sided attack on jobs, sites, and the collective bargaining system.

I agree with the works council on the core point that the company needs a credible industrial future. We are not denying the economic pressure. But a strategy built on plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and permanent wage dumping is not a turnaround — it is social demolition.

What we can discuss is a fair, negotiated contribution from the workforce **if and only if** the company, owners, and management also take responsibility. That means:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- A real employment and site security package.
- A future product and investment commitment for German plants.
- Management and owners making visible contributions before workers are asked to give up pay, time, or guarantees.

We have already shown that we are willing to negotiate. We have put forward a counter-proposal that includes labour-cost relief and capacity adjustments, because we understand the need to improve competitiveness. But we will not accept a model where employees alone absorb the pain while management preserves all strategic options and the workforce carries the bill.

If the company wants savings, then it must come with a settlement that is balanced and durable:
- use voluntary exits, retirement, working-time tools, and internal flexibility;
- discuss temporary or moderated wage components only in exchange for binding guarantees;
- commit to investment, new products, and site continuity;
- share the burden across management, capital, and labour.

If instead the message is “accept closures and layoffs or else,” then we are in conflict, not settlement. We have the strike weapon, and members are already demanding action. I would rather reach a fair agreement, but not at the price of abandoning jobs, sites, and collective standards.
