The core impasse is now clear:

1. **Labour-side stakeholders** insist that closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table unless every non-closure alternative is proven insufficient plant by plant.  
2. **Management and capital-side stakeholders** insist that a residual structural gap may still require capacity reduction, including closure, if the numbers remain material after alternatives.  
3. **The public shareholder/state** and **controlling owner** are trying to preserve governance legitimacy and regional stability, but are split over how far “last resort” can go.  
4. Everyone agrees on the need for a **plant-by-plant matrix**, but there is no shared rule yet for what happens if the matrix still shows a residual gap.

So the real conflict is not about whether to analyze alternatives. It is about **what counts as a permissible endpoint after the analysis**.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal A: Sequenced restructuring with a hard alternatives-first gate
- Phase 1: management, owners, and product planning must deliver quantified contributions and non-closure savings for each site.
- Phase 2: only if a residual gap remains material after that phase, the parties move to a second-stage discussion.
- Phase 3: second-stage discussion is limited to internal footprint reduction and voluntary measures first, before any closure discussion.

This would preserve the labour side’s demand that closure is not premature, while giving management and investors a defined path if the gap persists.

### Proposal B: No forced redundancies, but site-specific footprint adjustments
- All parties commit that compulsory redundancies are excluded.
- Sites may still adjust through voluntary exits, retirement windows, redeployment, shift consolidation, and product reallocation.
- Closure is not precluded in principle, but it can only be considered after a jointly verified residual-gap review and a social-impact test.

This directly addresses the strongest labour red line while leaving management some restructuring flexibility.

### Proposal C: Jobs-and-investment pact with conditional review clause
- Labour accepts temporary contributions only if management and owners make binding, measurable contributions first.
- In return, management and owners accept a multi-year investment and product allocation commitment.
- A review clause is built in: if targets are not met, the parties revisit the footprint, but only through the agreed matrix and not by unilateral action.

This may be the most viable bridge if the parties want a durable settlement rather than a one-off truce.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council / Betriebsrat
If the matrix shows a material residual gap after all non-closure measures, what specific additional internal adjustment would you accept before you would still say the site must remain open?

### To the Industrial Union
Would you accept a conditional agreement that excludes compulsory redundancies but allows site-specific footprint reduction after a jointly verified residual-gap test?

### To Management
What exact management, overhead, owner, and product-allocation contributions are you prepared to commit to before asking for any site-level capacity reduction?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you state clearly whether you would oppose closures in the supervisory board unless the matrix demonstrates that every non-closure option has truly failed?

### To the Controlling Family
Are you prepared to accept a binding non-closure phase first, even if it delays the option of closure, provided the process is time-limited and audited?

### To Institutional Investors
What residual-gap threshold, if any, would you regard as sufficient to justify stopping short of closure and instead choosing internal capacity reduction?

### To the Expert
What minimum information must be in the matrix before the parties can credibly distinguish between internal reduction, partial line exit, and full closure?
