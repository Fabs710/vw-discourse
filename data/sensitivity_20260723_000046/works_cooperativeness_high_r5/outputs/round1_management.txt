Let me be equally clear: I acknowledge the workforce’s willingness to talk, but your opening position does **not** yet solve the problem we are facing.

We are in a structural cost crisis, not a temporary rough patch. European demand is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our German cost base is materially above target. If we do nothing more than preserve every site, every guarantee, and every existing cost pattern, then we are not restructuring — we are postponing the next crisis.

Where I agree:
- Yes, we need a durable industrial future for Germany.
- Yes, any solution must include investment in future products and technology.
- Yes, a settlement must be negotiated, not simply imposed.
- Yes, voluntary tools, working-time flexibility, and internal redeployment should be part of the toolkit.

Where I disagree strongly:
- A blanket **“no plant closures”** line is not credible in the current situation.
- **No compulsory redundancies** sounds acceptable in principle, but only if the overall package still delivers the required capacity and cost reduction.
- A guarantee to keep **all German plants open through 2030** would effectively freeze the structure even if it remains uncompetitive. That is not a serious answer to the competitive gap we face.
- “Management’s own contribution” is not a substitute for a genuine labour-cost and capacity correction. We are already taking strategic responsibility by confronting the issue head-on; that alone will not close the gap.

What I am prepared to say is this:
If the employee side can bring forward a **credible, durable package** that materially reduces labour costs and overcapacity — not cosmetically, but structurally — then I am prepared to discuss how that is achieved, including alternatives to closures where they deliver the same economic effect.

But I need to be explicit about my non-negotiable core:
- The final outcome must materially reduce capacity and fixed cost.
- It must improve competitiveness sustainably, not just defer pain.
- It must create room for investment in future products and technology.

So if your counterproposal means “some flexibility, but no real structural change,” then I cannot accept it. If it means “a serious labour contribution, verifiable capacity reduction, and a path to lower structural cost,” then we have a basis for further talks.

I am not here to preserve every historic arrangement. I am here to restore the company’s competitiveness for 2030 and beyond.
