### Core impasse
The central blockage is this:

1. **Management / owners / investors** want a restructuring that demonstrably restores competitiveness, and they are not accepting “no closures” as a sufficient answer unless the numbers prove equivalence.
2. **Works council / union / state** are treating **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as red lines**, and they want the burden-sharing test to include meaningful management/owner/product-allocation contributions first.
3. Both sides now agree on the need for a **plant-by-plant quantified matrix**, but they disagree on **what counts as a credible endpoint** if a residual gap remains.

So the impasse is not about whether to quantify; it is about **what restructuring tools remain legitimate after quantification**.

---

### Three concrete compromise positions to explore

#### 1) “Alternatives-first with a trigger mechanism”
- Commit to a fully quantified plant-by-plant alternatives package first:
  - voluntary exits
  - retirement windows
  - redeployment
  - working-time flexibility
  - management overhead cuts
  - owner contribution
  - binding product/volume allocation
- If a residual gap remains above a pre-agreed threshold, a second phase begins with a **jointly reviewed capacity-adjustment menu**.
- This keeps closures from being the starting assumption, while avoiding an open-ended no-closure commitment.

**Possible bridge:** labor gets genuine alternatives-first sequencing; management gets a pre-agreed path if the gap is still material.

#### 2) “No forced redundancies, but negotiated footprint reduction”
- Reaffirm **no compulsory redundancies**.
- In parallel, allow **partial footprint reduction** measures such as:
  - line transfers
  - shift consolidation
  - site conversion
  - function relocation
- Full plant closure is not pre-agreed, but neither is it excluded if the jointly verified matrix shows no viable role for a site.
  
**Possible bridge:** protects against forced layoffs, while giving management a route to real structural savings short of immediate closure.

#### 3) “Jobs-and-investment pact with measurable burden-sharing”
- Tie workforce flexibility and temporary cost relief to:
  - binding investment
  - future-product commitments for German sites
  - management overhead reductions
  - owner capital contribution
- Establish milestone reviews at fixed intervals, with automatic re-opening of talks if investment or volume commitments are missed.

**Possible bridge:** gives employees concrete protection and gives investors a measurable discipline framework.

---

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact cost gap remains after you deduct management cuts, owner contributions, product/volume allocation changes, and the full non-closure package — by plant and by function?**

#### To the works council
**What specific non-closure measures, if quantified and time-bound, would you consider sufficient to support a negotiated restructuring without compulsory redundancies?**

#### To the union
**Are you prepared to accept temporary workforce contributions if they are paired with binding job, site, and investment guarantees — and if so, what are your minimum conditions?**

#### To the regional state government
**What concrete leverage will you use to secure investment and volume commitments for German sites, and at what point would you intervene if those commitments are not met?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**What level of owner contribution are you prepared to put on the table before asking the workforce to accept temporary sacrifice?**

#### To institutional investors
**Would you support a phased restructuring that preserves sites initially if it is shown to close most of the gap and includes a credible trigger for additional action if targets are missed?**

#### To the expert
**Can you define a threshold residual gap — and the corresponding plant-by-plant decision rules — that would distinguish “acceptable negotiated adjustment” from “capacity exit required”?**

---

### Facilitator framing for the next round
A productive next step would be to ask each stakeholder to respond to two items only:

1. **Their minimum acceptable burden-sharing package**
2. **Their red line if the quantified matrix still leaves a residual gap**

That would convert the discussion from general positions into negotiable parameters.
