## Neutral assessment of the current state

### What is already clear
- The company appears to have a **structural cost and capacity problem**, not just a short-term cyclical issue.
- **Non-closure measures** can likely generate meaningful savings, especially through:
  - voluntary exits / retirement,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary pay moderation,
  - overhead reduction,
  - better product allocation.
- However, if the German footprint is **materially overcapacity**, those measures often **do not fully reset fixed costs** on their own.
- **Closures or line exits** are the strongest lever for deep fixed-cost reduction, but they are also the **highest-risk** in this governance environment because of delay, conflict, and implementation friction.

### My neutral judgment
A **non-closure package should be tested first**, but whether it is sufficient depends on the size of the gap.

In standard automotive restructurings, a serious non-closure package often closes roughly **30–60% of the structural gap** in the first phase. That is material, but it often leaves a **residual gap** if the footprint is too large for demand. If that residual remains material, then some form of **capacity exit** becomes economically rational — not necessarily immediate full closure, but possibly:
- line shutdown,
- footprint consolidation,
- partial site conversion,
- or full closure where no viable future role exists.

## How I would frame the decision
The decision should be structured as:

1. **Current structural gap**
   - fixed-cost gap
   - labor-cost gap
   - capacity-utilization gap
   - overhead gap

2. **Savings from non-closure measures**
   - by plant and function
   - with timing and one-time costs

3. **Residual gap after non-closure measures**
   - what remains unaddressed

4. **Capacity actions required to close the residual**
   - shrink, transfer, mothball, close

5. **Execution risk and governance risk**
   - works council / union resistance
   - board veto points
   - strike risk
   - supply-chain disruption
   - time-to-cash impact

## What the matrix should include
For each German site:
- headcount by function
- output and utilization
- product allocation now and in future programs
- fixed vs variable cost
- required capex to stay competitive
- logistics and supply-chain role
- savings from non-closure measures
- one-time restructuring cost
- implementation timeline
- execution risk
- whether the site is:
  - keep,
  - shrink,
  - transfer,
  - convert,
  - or close

## Key comparative point
- **Non-closure measures**: faster, lower conflict, more socially durable, but often incomplete.
- **Closure-based measures**: deeper and more durable on cost, but slower, riskier, and more exposed to governance blockage.

So the right question is not “closure or no closure,” but:
**Can the company credibly close the structural gap without closures after all non-closure levers are used?**
If not, then some capacity exit is likely unavoidable.
