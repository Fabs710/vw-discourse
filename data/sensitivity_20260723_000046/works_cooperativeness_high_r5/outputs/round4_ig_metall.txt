Blanket guarantees are not the issue here. The issue is whether you are prepared to share the burden fairly.

## On the moderator’s compromise options

### 1) Alternatives-first with a trigger mechanism
I support **alternatives-first**.

I do **not** support a trigger mechanism if it is designed to preserve closures as the inevitable fallback. If there is any trigger at all, it must be narrowly defined and tied to proof that management, owners, and product planning have actually exhausted their levers.

Otherwise it is just a threat structure disguised as a process.

### 2) No forced redundancies, but negotiated footprint reduction
This is the only route on the table that can even be discussed — but only if “footprint reduction” means:
- internal reorganization,
- shift changes,
- redeployment,
- line transfers,
- productivity improvements,
- and better use of German capacity.

It does **not** mean plant closure by another name. If it does, then it is rejected.

### 3) Jobs-and-investment pact with measurable burden-sharing
This is the right framework in principle. But I will be clear about the minimum content:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- binding investment and future-product allocation,
- preservation of co-determination,
- and real management/owner contributions.

A pact without those elements would simply lock workers into sacrifice while the company keeps the same structural insecurity.

## My position on the expert’s assessment

So I support the auditor’s requested matrix, with one important clarification: it must distinguish clearly between
- internal capacity reduction,
- partial line exits,
- and outright plant closure.

That distinction matters. Not every restructuring problem requires destroying a site.
