I will be equally direct.

## To the Works Council
I agree with you on one point: a cosmetic deal is worthless. We also agree that co-determination must be respected and that voluntary instruments, redeployment, retraining, and working-time tools should be part of the package.

But I do **not** accept your core line:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **all German plants open through 2030 as a precondition**

That is not a restructuring strategy. It is a demand that the company preserve a cost and capacity structure the market no longer supports. You keep saying the burden should be solved internally, but you have not presented a quantified alternative that closes the gap at the scale required.

If you want me to move away from closures and forced reductions, then I need a package that produces:
- a **material capacity reduction**,
- a **durable labour-cost contribution**,
- and a **credible improvement in return on sales**.

Without that, your position amounts to protecting every site regardless of economics. I cannot sign up to that.

## To the Trade Union
I agree that burden-sharing cannot fall on labour alone. I am prepared to support visible restraint at the top, including bonus discipline and payout restraint where justified.

But I disagree with your attempt to define the employee contribution as only “temporary, limited, and reversible.” That may help with acceptability, but it does **not** solve a structural competitiveness problem.

We need something durable. If the contribution disappears after a short period while the cost base remains too high, then we are back in the same crisis in a few years. That is not acceptable.

You also say closures and forced reductions are not justified because management has not “proved” necessity. The proof is in the numbers: factory costs are too high, the European market is shrinking, and our margins are below target. We do not have the luxury of waiting until the problem becomes undeniable to everyone.

## To the State Government
I respect your role and I understand your responsibility for regional stability. But I do **not** accept the idea that site protection should be the default and economics the exception.

You are asking us to prove closures are unavoidable. I am telling you the burden also lies on those who want to preserve every plant: show me a credible, executable package that delivers the required savings without structural site changes.

So far, I hear political resolve, but not a full economic substitute. If the state wants to defend the industrial base, it should help secure a **real** restructuring, not a preservation exercise.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
I agree with you more than with the labour side on the core issue: we need structural improvement, not symbolism. We also agree that prolonged conflict would destroy value.

Where I push back is your reluctance to treat site changes as a legitimate option. If the footprint is overbuilt, then some capacity must come out. That can be via closures, but it can also be via other hard restructuring measures if they are genuinely sufficient. What I cannot accept is a package that preserves the current structure and simply shifts costs around.

You say you want quantified competitiveness improvements. So do I. Then help get them. But do not ask management to pretend that all existing sites and all existing employment structures can stay intact while the market does not cooperate.

## To the Institutional Investors
You are correct that execution certainty matters and that a cosmetic compromise destroys value. I agree.

But I do not accept your framing that preserving every German site is financially unserious unless proven otherwise. What is financially unserious is pretending that a business can recover margin while keeping a structurally uncompetitive footprint.

You are right to demand quantified improvement. I am too. That is exactly why I will not accept a deal that leaves the underlying overcapacity untouched.

## What management needs
Let me be clear about the minimum acceptable outcome from my side:

1. **A real, durable reduction in overcapacity**
2. **A meaningful labour-cost contribution**
3. **A material workforce adjustment**
4. **Binding investment capacity for future products and technology**
5. **A settlement that can survive co-determination and industrial peace**

## What I can trade
I remain prepared to discuss:
- voluntary exit schemes,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time measures,
- phased and negotiated employee contributions,
- and management/owner restraint.

I am also prepared to sequence measures if that helps execution.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a blanket guarantee that all German plants remain open,
- an unconditional no-headcount-reduction promise,
- a purely temporary cost measure that leaves the structural gap unchanged,
- or a package that protects employment numbers while the business continues to erode.

## The hard truth
If the employee side insists that no closures and no compulsory redundancies are possible under any circumstances, then you are asking us to abandon the core objective of restructuring. That is not something I can do.

If, however, you are ready to engage on a package that achieves the required savings through negotiated instruments, then I am willing to continue. But the package must be real, material, and durable.

## Bottom line
I am not backing away from the need for:
- **structural capacity reduction**
- **durable cost reduction**
- **workforce adjustment**
- **and a competitiveness reset**

If a site-preserving solution can genuinely deliver that, I will consider it. If not, then closures and deeper restructuring remain on the table.

That is the position.
