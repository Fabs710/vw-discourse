## Where I agree

I agree with management, the union, the works council, the state, and the expert on several important points:

- A cosmetic deal is worthless.
- The company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a postponement.
- Voluntary instruments should be used first:
  - redeployment,
  - retraining,
  - early retirement,
  - voluntary exits,
  - working-time tools,
  - internal transfers.

## Where I disagree

I do **not** accept the labour and state position that **all German plants must remain open** and that **no compulsory redundancies** can be treated as fixed preconditions.

That is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand that the company preserve its current footprint regardless of whether the economics support it.

I also do **not** accept management’s tendency to present closures and compulsory reductions as if they were the only serious route. That is too blunt, too risky, and too likely to trigger the kind of conflict that destroys value instead of restoring it.

The expert’s assessment matters here: a no-closure, no-forced-layoff package is **plausible** if the gap is moderate and the measures are durable. That means the answer is **not** automatically “close plants.” It means we need quantified options.

## My position on the package test

The moderator asked for two packages. That is the right approach.

### 1) No-closure / no-forced-layoff package
I am prepared to test this seriously.

But it must be real, not symbolic. It would need to include:

- all German plants remaining open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a **material and durable** employee contribution,
- management and owner restraint,
- binding investment in Germany,
- internal capacity adjustment,
- and quantified savings large enough to matter.

If that package genuinely closes the competitiveness gap, I would prefer it. It is the more stable and value-preserving route.

### 2) Structural-adjustment package
If the numbers show that the gap is too large, then structural adjustment becomes more credible.

But I will say this carefully: if such a package requires plant closures or forced layoffs, then it is not something I will endorse lightly. It would have to be proven, quantified, and clearly necessary. We are **not** there yet.

At this stage, nobody has shown that every site-preserving option has been exhausted. Assertion is not proof.
