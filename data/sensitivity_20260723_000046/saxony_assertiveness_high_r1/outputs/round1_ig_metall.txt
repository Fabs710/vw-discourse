I share the core line that has just been drawn: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, and no unilateral tearing up of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: if management wants the workforce to carry the burden of restructuring, then that burden must be **shared**, **limited**, and **matched by hard guarantees**. We will not accept a model where workers pay through wage concessions, give up guarantees, and absorb capacity cuts while ownership, management, and capital remain largely untouched.

Where I agree:
- **Capacity adjustments** can be discussed if they are reversible and socially responsible.
- **Working-time instruments**, voluntary retirement, internal transfer, and qualification measures are legitimate tools.
- A **temporary employee contribution** is conceivable, but only as part of a broader package with secured jobs and sites.
- The company does need a durable solution, not a short-term patch.

Where I disagree:
- **Closures as a bargaining instrument are unacceptable.** They are not a neutral “option”; they are a threat to the workforce and to the company’s industrial base.
- **Forced layoffs are not on the table.** If management has overcapacity, then it must solve that through negotiated, socially responsible means.
- **Cost reduction cannot come only from labour.** If the business case depends on workers taking all the pain, then that is not a restructuring plan — it is a transfer of the crisis onto employees.

So what would move us?
A serious package with these elements:
1. **Site security for all German plants**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A negotiated reduction in labour costs or wage-related burdens, if necessary**
4. **A fair contribution from owners and management** — including restraint on dividends, bonuses, and other top-level payouts
5. **Binding investment commitments** for future products and technologies in Germany
6. **Qualified redeployment, retraining, and voluntary instruments** to address overcapacity

If management is serious about securing the future, then it should stop using closure threats as leverage and come forward with a shared-sacrifice settlement. If not, then it should expect the workforce to defend its interests — and we have the means to do that.
