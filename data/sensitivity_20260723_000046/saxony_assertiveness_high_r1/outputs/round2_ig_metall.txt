I’ll respond directly.

### To the works council
I agree with your core line:
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **no plant closures as the starting point**,
- **real co-determination**, not a fait accompli.

I also agree that a restructuring which relies on workers carrying the full burden is unacceptable. The workforce has already contributed for years, and a one-sided “sacrifice package” would destroy trust and trigger resistance. That is not a durable settlement.

Where I want to be precise is this: I can support **temporary and limited** employee contributions, but only if they are part of a package that is truly balanced and legally/operationally credible. I will not sign off on a permanent erosion of the collective wage framework or on hidden layoffs via attrition pressure.

### To management
I disagree sharply with your framing that closures and a “material, durable labour-cost contribution” are simply the necessary baseline.

You have not made the case that the only viable path is to put plant closures and forced headcount reduction on the table from the outset. That is an aggressive bargaining position, not proof of necessity.

If you want the union to help secure competitiveness, then you need to stop presenting labour as the first and main adjustment variable. A restructuring that asks employees to give up pay, guarantees, and security while management asks for flexibility on its own terms will not be accepted.

That said, I do hear one point: **the package must be real and durable**, not symbolic. I am prepared to discuss:
- working-time measures,
- temporary wage-related concessions,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and productivity commitments,

but only if the savings are matched by **binding site security**, **no forced layoffs**, and **a fair contribution from management and owners**.

### To the regional state government
I support your insistence that the solution must be negotiated and socially responsible.

Where I caution is that “site protection” must not become a way to delay hard decisions indefinitely while workers are expected to absorb uncertainty. If the state wants credibility in this process, it should support a package that secures:
- all German sites,
- employment through negotiated means,
- and investment in future products,

rather than accepting closure logic as the default.

### To the controlling family and institutional investors
I understand your focus on long-term value and competitiveness. But your definition of value cannot stop at short-term margin repair. If you push a package that tears up trust, triggers prolonged strikes, and undermines the collective bargaining system, you will destroy more value than you create.

I agree with you that the company needs structural improvement. I do **not** agree that the necessary adjustment must come through closures or through a one-sided labour contribution.

If you want burden-sharing, then make it real:
- **executive restraint**,
- **bonus restraint**,
- **dividend discipline**,
- and **binding reinvestment commitments** in Germany.

### The union’s minimum package
Here is what would move us:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **A binding site-security agreement for all German plants**
4. **Temporary, negotiated employee contributions only**
5. **Management and owner contributions that are visible and material**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**
7. **Voluntary, internal, and reversible workforce adjustment tools**
8. **Transparency on the restructuring numbers so the burden can be judged fairly**

### What we will not accept
Let me state the hard boundary clearly:
- No deal that makes workers pay while owners are insulated.
- No deal that uses “competitiveness” as a cover for plant closures.
- No deal that leaves job security dependent on management discretion.
- No deal that weakens the collective wage framework permanently.

### A possible structure
If there is serious willingness on the other side, then the integrated package could look like this:
- time-limited wage restraint or special contribution,
- reduced or redesigned working time,
- voluntary retirement and internal transfers,
- capacity reductions through shifts and utilization management,
- executive and shareholder restraint,
- and binding investment plus job guarantees.

That is the only kind of restructuring I would call fair.

If management is not prepared to move away from closure-first thinking, then we will not be “compromising”; we will be defending the workforce with the means available to us, including industrial action.
