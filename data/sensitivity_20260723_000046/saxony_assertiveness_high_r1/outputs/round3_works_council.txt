Management, the family holding, and the investors keep repeating the same word — “durable” — but they are still trying to make labour carry the structural burden while keeping plant closure and compulsory redundancy as the default threat. I reject that.

Let me be precise for round 3.

## What I agree with
I agree that:
- a cosmetic fix is useless,
- the solution must be executable,
- the company needs investment in future products and technology,
- management, owners, and capital must contribute,
- and voluntary tools should be used first.

I also agree that if we end up in open conflict, everyone loses value. That is exactly why I am insisting on a settlement that protects jobs and sites instead of gambling with closures.

## What I disagree with
I disagree with the core assumption from management, family holding, and investors that a “credible” restructuring must include plant closures or an unconditional option for compulsory redundancies.

That is not a law of economics. It is a management choice.

You have not proven that every German plant must stay exactly as it is — but that is not the question. The question is whether the required adjustment can be achieved **without closures and without forced layoffs**. So far, no one has demonstrated that this cannot be done. You keep asserting it.

And I will say it plainly: if you cannot make the numbers work without breaking the historic job-security framework, then your plan is not a restructuring plan; it is a demand that workers pay for prior strategic errors.

## My position on the supposed trade-offs

### 1) Site security vs. economic flexibility
We are not trading away site security as a “flexibility” issue.

All German plants must remain open. That is not an emotional preference. It is the only basis on which the works council can responsibly engage in a settlement.

If management insists on keeping closure threats alive, then there is no trust and no basis for labour peace.

### 2) Job protection vs. structural cost reduction
No compulsory redundancies. Full stop.

If headcount must come down, then use:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- internal transfers,
- working-time reductions,
- shift redesign,
- and temporary capacity measures.

If that is not enough, then management must come back with a better plan. The answer is not to threaten dismissals.

### 3) Temporary employee concessions vs. lasting competitiveness
I am open to **time-limited** employee contributions only if they are tied to binding guarantees.

That means:
- no operational layoffs,
- no plant closures,
- investment commitments in Germany,
- and a genuine co-determination role in implementation.

Without those, “temporary contribution” is just a down payment on permanent insecurity.

### 4) Management/owner restraint vs. labour sacrifice
I support visible restraint at the top, but let’s not pretend that bonus restraint alone substitutes for fairness.

If labour is asked for anything at all, then top management and owners must give more than speeches:
- real bonus discipline,
- payout restraint,
- reinvestment priority,
- and transparency on where the money goes.

### 5) Regional stability vs. shareholder return
I do not accept the framing that protecting German plants is just political comfort while “real” value sits with investors.

A company does not create value by destroying trust in its own workforce, breaking co-determination, and provoking industrial war. That would be value destruction, not discipline.

## What I will not accept
Let me make the limits unmistakable:

- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no operational dismissals,
- no hidden layoff programme through pressure tactics,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- no permanent erosion of job-security guarantees.

If that is what the “realistic” package requires, then the package is unacceptable.

## What a serious package would have to look like
If the other side wants a deal, then it has to be built on these elements:

1. **All German plants remain open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Job-security framework preserved through 2030**
4. **Temporary, limited employee contribution only**
5. **Management and owner contributions that are visible and material**
6. **Binding investment commitments in Germany**
7. **Capacity adjustments through reversible internal instruments only**
8. **Early and substantive co-determination over implementation**
9. **Transparent numbers, so burden-sharing can be assessed fairly**

That is the minimum basis for discussion.

## Direct reply to management
You say there is a structural cost and capacity problem. Fine — then solve it in a way that does not sacrifice the workforce.

What you have not done is show that all non-destructive options are exhausted. You keep reaching for closures because they are the fastest way to force an accounting improvement. That is not the same as a sustainable solution.

If you want our cooperation, then stop treating the threat of closure as a bargaining weapon and come back with a plant-preserving package.

## Direct reply to the family holding and investors
You say closures are not a trophy and not a first resort. Good. Then stop insisting they remain on the table as if that were a neutral technical necessity.

The only responsible route is to agree on a package that preserves the German base, reduces cost through internal and voluntary means, and protects the company’s long-term industrial capability.

If you want execution certainty, then give the workforce certainty first.

## Direct reply to the state government
You are right to insist on site protection. But I need you to be even clearer: the state should not be used to pressure labour into accepting a closure logic.

If the state has a blocking position, then it should use it to defend:
- all German plants,
- no forced redundancies,
- and binding investment in the region.

That is where your leverage belongs.

## What I am prepared to discuss now
I am prepared to discuss:
- a time-limited employee contribution,
- shift and working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary phased exits,
- and a sequenced implementation with review points.

I am **not** prepared to discuss:
- closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or any settlement that makes job security conditional on management’s future discretion.

## Bottom line
If the integrated package is built on:
- keeping every German plant open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job and investment guarantees through 2030,
- and shared sacrifice from management and owners,

then the works council is ready to work on implementation.

If not, then there is no agreement from us — and I will not pretend otherwise.
