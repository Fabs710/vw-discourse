I will respond directly.

The family holding’s position remains unchanged in its essentials: **we support a restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins, but we will not support a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive or traps it in prolonged, value-destroying conflict**.

I hear the force of the labour side’s red lines. I also hear the state’s concern for the regional base. But I must be equally clear: a package that simply promises “all plants open, no compulsory redundancies, no lasting cost change” is **not** a credible restructuring. It is a delay of the problem, and delay has already become expensive.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the cost base and capacity footprint are misaligned with the market. You are also right that a cosmetic settlement will not be enough. If the company cannot restore margins, it will not have the investment capacity needed for electrification, software, and future products.

### With labour representatives
You are right that no agreement can be executed if it ignores co-determination and industrial relations reality. A forced, unilateral approach would likely destroy more value than it creates.

You are also right that the burden cannot fall on labour alone. Management, ownership, and capital must contribute too.

### With the state
You are right that industrial stability matters. A prolonged conflict would harm the region and the company alike.

## Where I disagree

### 1) On “all German plants remain open”
I cannot support that as a blanket commitment.

From an ownership perspective, it is not responsible to rule out site changes in advance if the current footprint is structurally overbuilt. That would amount to accepting permanent inefficiency and weaker investment capacity. I understand why this is politically attractive, but it is not a viable ownership position.

That said, I also do **not** support a closure-first posture as a reflex. Closures are not a trophy; they are a last-resort instrument if other measures cannot credibly close the gap.

### 2) On “no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I prefer to avoid compulsory redundancies. But I cannot endorse an unconditional guarantee that removes all management flexibility if overcapacity persists.

If headcount must come down, then the first tools should be voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, and working-time measures. But I will not support a structure that makes real adjustment impossible.

### 3) On the state’s insistence that closures are off the table unless proven otherwise
I respect the state’s role. I do **not** accept that the state can demand site security first and economics second as a governance principle.

The burden of proof cuts both ways. If the employee side and the state want to prevent closures, then they need to show a **credible, quantified alternative** that produces durable savings and capacity reduction without destroying competitiveness. So far, I hear commitment, but not yet a full substitute.

## What would move us

The family holding would support a package if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified competitiveness plan**
   - not general assurances, but numbers that close the margin gap

2. **Material structural savings**
   - durable, not symbolic

3. **Capacity reduction through negotiated instruments first**
   - voluntary exits, redeployment, shift redesign, working-time flexibility

4. **A real labour-cost contribution**
   - temporary or phased is fine only if it is meaningful enough to matter

5. **Management and owner restraint**
   - bonus restraint, payout discipline, reinvestment priority

6. **Binding investment commitments in Germany**
   - future products, technology, and industrial capability

7. **Execution certainty**
   - a package that can survive co-determination without becoming a permanent conflict

## What is not enough

I must say explicitly that the following would **not** be acceptable:

- a deal that preserves every site regardless of economics,
- a purely temporary labour contribution that leaves the structural gap untouched,
- a package that shifts all pain onto capital or all pain onto labour,
- or a political compromise that is socially comforting but economically hollow.

## To the works council and union
I am not asking you to accept a dismantling of the German base for its own sake. I am asking you to confront the possibility that some structural adjustment may be unavoidable.

If you can deliver the needed savings through reversible, voluntary, and negotiated means, then that is preferable. But if the answer is “no closures, no forced reductions, and no meaningful structural change,” then we are not negotiating a solution; we are defending the status quo until the company pays the price later.

That is not acceptable to us as long-term owners.

## To management
You do not have a blank check either.

If you want support from governance, then you need to come forward with a **precise, defensible package**:
- what savings are required,
- which measures generate them,
- what is truly non-negotiable,
- and what can be traded.

I will not support vague closure threats used as leverage if they are not backed by a serious executable plan. Prolonged brinkmanship would also destroy value.

## Bottom line

The family holding will support a settlement only if it **restores competitiveness in a durable way** and avoids a destructive labor conflict. We are not aligned with a blanket site-closure policy, but we are also not prepared to endorse a settlement that leaves the company weak, overcosted, and unable to invest.

So my message to all sides is simple:

- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to investment in Germany.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to permanent governance paralysis.
- **No** to an outcome that protects every existing structure while the business continues to erode.

If a serious package exists that materially improves competitiveness without triggering instability, we will back it through governance. If not, we will not support a settlement that merely postpones the unavoidable.
