### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring** rather than a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad support for **negotiated solutions** within the co-determination framework; no one is advocating a unilateral decree.
- There is common ground on using **voluntary measures**, including:
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - working-time instruments,
  - voluntary exits / early retirement,
  - and possibly temporary employee contributions.
- There is also shared recognition that **management, owners, and capital should contribute** in some form, not only labour.
- Several parties agree that any settlement must include **investment commitments for future products/technologies in Germany**.
- There is general concern that a deal must be **credible, executable, and stable enough** to avoid prolonged conflict.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and state government reject closures as a starting point and seek site security.  
  - Management, institutional investors, and the family holding say closures cannot be ruled out if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies / headcount reduction:**  
  - Labour representatives want no compulsory redundancies and strong job security.  
  - Management and investors insist some meaningful workforce reduction may be necessary if overcapacity is real.
- **Labour contribution:**  
  - The union and works council are open to temporary or conditional sacrifices only if matched by binding guarantees.  
  - Management and investors want a **material, durable, quantified** cost contribution, not merely symbolic or reversible measures.
- **Scope of protection for German sites:**  
  - Labour, state, and partly the family holding want all German plants protected or at least strongly safeguarded.  
  - Management and investors want flexibility to restructure sites if needed for competitiveness.
- **Decision framing:**  
  - Labour and state emphasize social and regional continuity.  
  - Management and capital emphasize structural competitiveness, margin recovery, and execution certainty.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much restructuring is necessary** versus what can be achieved through voluntary, internal, and time-limited measures.
- **Which sites, if any, are genuinely non-viable**, and what evidence would justify closure versus alternative adjustments.
- **How to balance job security against competitiveness**, including whether any employment guarantees can coexist with the savings management says it needs.
- **What “fair burden-sharing” means in measurable terms** across workers, management, owners, and capital.
- **What binding investment commitments** are feasible, and whether they can be made contingent on labour concessions.
- **How to avoid industrial conflict** while still delivering the scale of change management and investors say is required.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **package design**:
- define the **minimum acceptable restructuring outcome** for each side,
- identify which measures are **non-negotiable** and which are **tradeable**,
- quantify the expected contribution from **labour, management, and owners**,
- specify what **site/security guarantees** can be offered in exchange for what **cost/competitiveness measures**,
- and test whether there is a **sequenced compromise**: immediate relief measures now, with binding investment and review mechanisms later.

The next round should aim to identify a **single integrated package** rather than restating red lines.
