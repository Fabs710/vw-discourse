I will be just as clear in round 2 as I was in round 1.

### Our minimum acceptable outcome has not changed
We will **not** accept:
- any German plant closure,
- any compulsory redundancies,
- any stripping of the historic job-security framework,
- or any restructuring that turns co-determination into a rubber stamp after the fact.

If those are the assumptions, then there is no basis for an agreement from the works council side.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one. I also agree that voluntary instruments, internal redeployment, qualification, and working-time tools belong in the package.

I also accept that management needs a credible cost plan. But “credible” does **not** mean “closures and forced layoffs”. It means a solution that restores competitiveness **without destroying the workforce and the German industrial base**.

### Where I disagree — sharply
Management says closures cannot be ruled out. We reject that. A plant closure is not an abstract efficiency measure; it is the end of a site, the destruction of livelihoods, and a break with the company’s own history and commitments. Once you open that door, you do not merely “adjust capacity” — you start dismantling the foundation of trust that makes any restructuring possible.

The same applies to compulsory redundancies. If management is saying that some headcount reduction is necessary, then it must be done through **voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, and reduced working time**, not through forced dismissals. If those instruments are not enough, then management must explain why its own planning errors should be paid for by sending people home against their will.

### On the demand for “durable” labour contribution
I reject the attempt to turn “durable” into a one-way street where labour gives permanent concessions while ownership and management offer only promises. We are not willing to discuss wage or flexibility sacrifices unless they are tied to:

1. **Binding site guarantees for all German plants**
2. **No operational layoffs**
3. **Real investment commitments in Germany**
4. **Concrete burden-sharing from management and owners**
5. **A guaranteed co-determination role in shaping implementation**

Without those conditions, any labour contribution would simply subsidize restructuring risk for capital while workers carry the damage.

### What a real package would need to contain
If management truly wants a solution, then the package must be built around these principles:

#### 1. Site security first
All German plants must remain open. That is the starting point, not the reward at the end.

#### 2. Job security through 2030
No compulsory redundancies, no operational dismissals, and no hidden layoff programme disguised as “natural attrition” plus pressure tactics.

#### 3. Reversible capacity adjustment
If capacity needs to come down, then do it through:
- temporary line adjustments,
- shift redesign,
- working-time reductions,
- maintenance windows,
- internal transfers,
- and voluntary phased exits.

That is how you restructure without breaking the company’s social contract.

#### 4. Fair contribution from the top
If management wants workers to make sacrifices, then the top must do its part in a visible and measurable way:
- bonus restraint,
- dividend restraint where appropriate,
- no executive insulation from the consequences,
- and a clear reinvestment priority for German sites.

#### 5. Binding investment and product commitments
No one should accept labour concessions unless there are **hard commitments** on future products, technology allocation, and capital expenditure in Germany.

#### 6. Co-determination with substance
The works council and employee side must be part of designing the restructuring, not merely informed after decisions are already fixed. That means early involvement, transparent data, and binding joint review mechanisms.

### To the union
I agree with you that labour cannot be the only side to contribute. But let us be precise: a contribution from labour is acceptable only if it is **temporary, limited, and matched by guarantees**. We should not be pushed into giving away core protections in exchange for vague promises about competitiveness.

### To the state government
Your insistence on site protection is correct. But that protection cannot be conditional on accepting a closure logic in principle. If the state wants to support a serious settlement, it should insist on investment, training, and continuity — not on accepting the premise that German plants are disposable.

### To the family holding and investors
You keep saying closures and deep restructuring are needed for value. I say the opposite: **destroying trust, breaking co-determination, and triggering industrial conflict destroys value faster than keeping sites open and negotiating responsibly**.

If you want long-term value, then support a settlement that preserves the plants, the workforce, and the capability to produce in Germany. A company does not become more competitive by amputating itself.

### What I am prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary capacity reductions,
- shift and working-time adjustments,
- redeployment across sites,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary retirement and severance on a strictly voluntary basis,
- productivity improvements,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

We are **not** prepared to discuss:
- plant closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or any bargaining model that treats job security as negotiable in exchange for management’s vague promise of future competitiveness.

### Bottom line
If the integrated package starts from **all plants open, no compulsory redundancies, and binding job and investment security through 2030**, then we can talk about the operational tools needed to make that workable.

If it starts from closures or forced layoffs, then the works council will oppose it — in the supervisory board, in the workplace, and if necessary through escalation.

We are ready for a serious restructuring. But it must be a restructuring **with** the workforce, not **against** it.
