I will be equally direct.

## To management
I accept your diagnosis that the company needs a **durable restructuring** and that a cosmetic fix would be irresponsible. I also accept that voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, working-time tools, and some form of contribution from all sides should be part of the package.

But I **do not accept** your insistence that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain normal bargaining instruments.

For us, that is not a technical detail. It is a political and industrial red line. Once closures are treated as a baseline option, the entire negotiation becomes a threat exercise rather than a restructuring discussion. That may be useful in a spreadsheet, but it is not a responsible path for a company that is an anchor of the regional economy.

You keep saying the numbers prove the need. I say the burden of proof has **not** been met for closures in the state, and certainly not for a settlement that would gut regional employment. Show us a serious, quantified alternative first — one that preserves sites and uses internal, reversible, negotiated measures to close the gap.

## To the works council
I agree with you on the essentials:
- no plant closures as a starting point,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- co-determination must be substantive, not decorative,
- and any labour contribution must be **temporary, limited, and matched by binding guarantees**.

That is the correct frame.

Where I want to add one point is this: I do not want us to hide behind principles without a workable package. A site-preserving deal must still be economically credible. If it is credible, I will support it strongly. If it is not, then we have to keep looking — but still without accepting the destruction of regional jobs as the first answer.

## To the trade union
I support your demand for **fair burden-sharing**. Labour cannot be expected to carry the whole restructuring while management, owners, and capital remain insulated.

I also agree that any wage or flexibility contribution must be:
- time-limited,
- proportionate,
- transparent,
- and tied to binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.

That is exactly the kind of social partnership we should defend.

But I will be blunt: if the package turns into a permanent weakening of the workforce in exchange for vague promises of competitiveness, I will not support it. Temporary contribution is acceptable; permanent erosion of job security is not.

## To the controlling family and investors
I understand your focus on margins, execution certainty, and long-term competitiveness. I do not dismiss that. The company must remain viable.

But I reject the idea that the only credible route is to keep closures and hard headcount cuts on the table until labour gives in. That is a very expensive way to negotiate, and it risks exactly what you say you want to avoid: industrial conflict, damaged trust, and a weaker company.

You want a durable improvement? Then support a package that preserves the company’s regional base and gives the workforce a reason to back the change. A settlement that tears up the social foundation will not be durable, no matter how tidy it looks on paper.

## My position
As the state shareholder, I will not support:
- plant closures in the state as the default solution,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or any settlement that removes the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are not negotiable from our side.

What I **will** support is a serious restructuring package if it contains all of the following:
1. **All German plants safeguarded**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Temporary, quantified employee contribution**
4. **Visible management and owner restraint**
5. **Binding investment commitments in Germany**
6. **Internal, reversible capacity adjustment measures**
7. **A genuine role for co-determination in implementation**
8. **Transparent figures and joint monitoring**

That is the minimum basis for our support.

## On the real trade-off
The real trade-off is not “efficiency versus sentiment.” It is:
- a restructuring that preserves the company’s regional anchor and social legitimacy,  
versus
- a restructuring that may look cleaner in the short term but destroys trust, triggers conflict, and weakens the company’s long-term embeddedness.

I am not prepared to trade away the first for the second.

## What would move me
A package would need to show, concretely:
- how the savings are achieved **without closures**,
- how workforce reduction, if any, is managed **without compulsory dismissals**,
- how management and owners share the burden,
- and how investment in Germany is secured in a binding way.

If that is presented, I am ready to broker and defend it.

If not, then let there be no misunderstanding: I will use the leverage the statute gives me, because protecting regional jobs and the state’s co-determination role is not a side issue — it is the core of my mandate.
