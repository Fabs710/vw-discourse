We agree with the diagnosis from management, the family holding, and to a large extent even the labour side: **a cosmetic settlement is worthless**. If this company does not restore competitive margins and lower its structurally too-high cost base, then shareholder value will keep eroding.

We also agree with the union, works council, and state on one critical execution point: **a deal that cannot survive co-determination, labor relations, and governance constraints is not a real deal**. Prolonged conflict, strikes, or board deadlock would destroy value just as surely as weak restructuring.

Where we part company is on the idea that **site security and job security can be treated as hard preconditions without a fully quantified substitute for the savings management is asking for**.

## What we agree with
- **Voluntary instruments first**: redeployment, retraining, early retirement, phased exits, working-time tools.
- **Burden-sharing must be broader than labour alone**: management restraint, bonus discipline, and where appropriate owner/capital restraint.
- **Investment in Germany matters**: future products, technology, and industrial capability are essential to any credible turnaround.
- **A durable solution is required**: temporary optics or symbolic sacrifices will not restore value.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “All plants open” is not, by itself, a value-preserving plan
The works council and state keep making site security the starting point. We do **not** accept that as sufficient.

If all German plants are to remain open, then someone needs to show — with numbers — how the company still gets:
- a materially lower cost base,
- lower capacity,
- and a credible return to target margins.

So far, we have heard assertions of alternatives, but not a fully quantified alternative that closes the competitiveness gap at scale.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an unconditional guarantee
We understand why this is demanded. But from an investor perspective, an absolute no-layoff guarantee is only acceptable if the required workforce adjustment is demonstrably achievable through other means.

If it is not achievable, then such a guarantee simply preserves overcapacity and pushes the correction into future losses. That would be value-destructive.

### 3) Temporary concessions are not enough if the structural gap remains
The union’s position that employee contributions must be temporary and limited is understandable, but we do **not** accept a package where labour gives up something short-term while the business remains structurally overcosted.

We need **durable** improvement, not a pause before the next crisis.

## What would move us
We would support a settlement if it includes all of the following:

1. **Quantified savings targets**
   - not general language, but explicit cost and margin improvement numbers

2. **Durable cost reduction**
   - including labour cost and capacity reduction, not just top-level restraint

3. **A credible workforce adjustment plan**
   - preferably voluntary and internal first, but effective enough to matter

4. **Visible management/owner contribution**
   - bonus restraint, payout discipline, reinvestment priority

5. **Binding investment commitments in Germany**
   - future products, technology, and plant modernization where viable

6. **Execution certainty**
   - a package that can actually survive the governance structure and avoid prolonged industrial conflict

## What would not move us
We will not support:
- a purely political compromise,
- a “site security” package that leaves the company overcosted,
- symbolic or reversible measures that do not materially change the cost base,
- or a deal that protects every existing structure while margins remain impaired.

## Our direct response to each party
### To management
You are right that the economics must improve. But your credibility depends on presenting a **precise, defensible package**, not just keeping closures and redundancies as open-ended pressure points.

If you want support from capital, show:
- what the gap is,
- what closes it,
- what is negotiable,
- and what is indispensable.

### To the works council and union
We hear the insistence on no forced layoffs and no closures. But you cannot ask capital to back a solution that merely preserves employment structures and hope that competitiveness will magically return.

If you want us to back a site-preserving alternative, it must be proven in the numbers. Otherwise, it is not an alternative; it is deferral.

### To the state government
Your role as anchor shareholder matters. But a veto culture that blocks every hard adjustment would be a mistake. Regional stability is important, but only if the business remains investable and viable.

### To the family holding
We agree with you that long-term value matters more than optics. But that also means you need to be disciplined about what “structural change” means. It should be **quantified, executable, and not reduced to symbolic brinkmanship**.

## Bottom line
Our position remains unchanged:

- **Yes** to negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to investment in future capability.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.
- **No** to indefinite protection of the current footprint without quantified replacement savings.

If there is a site-preserving package that truly restores margins and competitiveness, we will back it. But until someone puts that package on the table in hard numbers, we cannot support unconditional guarantees that would lock in underperformance.

If the parties want a constructive next step, I would strongly suggest a **quantified joint financial scenario** showing:
- required margin improvement,
- labor-cost contribution,
- capacity reduction options,
- and the value impact of each alternative.
