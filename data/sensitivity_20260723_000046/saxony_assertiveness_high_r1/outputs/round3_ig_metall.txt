I’ll respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even parts of management and capital on one point: **a cosmetic deal is worthless**. This restructuring must be durable, executable, and backed by investment.

I also agree that:
- voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, and working-time tools should be used first,
- management, owners, and capital must contribute,
- and the solution must be able to survive co-determination and industrial relations reality.

That is all correct.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to make **closures** and **compulsory redundancies** the default or even the “necessary” baseline.

Management says the footprint must shrink and labour costs must come down durably. Fine — but that does **not** automatically justify plant closures or forced layoffs. The burden is on management to prove that all non-destructive measures have been exhausted and that a site-preserving, no-forced-layoff package cannot work.

So far, I have heard assertions, not proof.

I also do **not** accept the family holding’s and investors’ framing that preserving all German sites is somehow financially unserious. A deal that destroys trust, triggers strikes, and breaks the collective bargaining framework is not value creation; it is self-inflicted damage.

## Our position
The union’s position is still this:

- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures as a starting point**
- **no permanent erosion of the collective wage framework**
- **real burden-sharing**
- **binding investment in Germany**
- **job security and site security as the basis for any labour contribution**

If those conditions are met, then yes: we are prepared to discuss a meaningful employee contribution that is **temporary, limited, and fair**.

That can include:
- a temporary wage-related contribution,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary exit packages,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and internal flexibility.

But it cannot include:
- workers paying while owners are insulated,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or a hidden restructuring through pressure and attrition.

## Direct reply to management
You say a durable labour-cost contribution is needed. I’m not arguing against durability. I’m arguing against **one-sided durability**.

If you want labour to contribute durably, then the company must also deliver durable guarantees:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- binding investment,
- and visible restraint at the top.

Without that, your demand is simply a permanent transfer of risk onto employees.

## Direct reply to the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no hollowing out of co-determination.

Where I want to sharpen the point is this: a temporary labour contribution is only acceptable if it is **strictly time-limited, proportionate, and matched by binding commitments**. It must not become a back door to permanent wage erosion.

## Direct reply to the state government
Your blocking position matters, and you are right to insist on site protection. But that protection has to be turned into a **real package**, not just a political line.

Use your leverage to secure:
- all German plants,
- employment protection,
- and investment commitments.

Do **not** let “competitiveness” become a code word for shifting the pain entirely onto workers.

## Direct reply to the family holding and investors
I understand your concern for margins and execution. But if you insist on keeping closures and forced layoffs on the table, you are making the industrial conflict more likely, not less.

If you want execution certainty, then support a package that workers can actually accept. That means:
- credible investment,
- shared sacrifice,
- management restraint,
- and no forced redundancies.

Otherwise you will get the opposite of certainty: warning strikes, escalation, and a deal that collapses under its own unfairness.

## What would move us
I can support a package if it contains all of the following:

1. **Binding job security**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No plant closures**
4. **Temporary employee contribution only**
5. **Material contribution from management and owners**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**
7. **Voluntary, internal, reversible capacity adjustment**
8. **Transparent numbers and joint monitoring**

That is the minimum fair basis.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a deal where workers give up pay and security while closures remain possible,
- a “durable” contribution that is only demanded from labour,
- a settlement that weakens collective bargaining,
- or a package that uses industrial pressure to force acceptance of plant closures.

If the other side wants an agreement, then it must be a **shared-sacrifice package with site and job security**. If not, the union will not sell a bad deal to the workforce.

## Bottom line
I am ready for a serious settlement. But it must be built on:
- **no closures**,
- **no forced layoffs**,
- **fair burden-sharing**,
- **binding investment**,
- and **durable industrial peace**.

If management and owners can accept that framework, we can work on the details. If they cannot, then they should not expect labour to pay for the restructuring alone.
