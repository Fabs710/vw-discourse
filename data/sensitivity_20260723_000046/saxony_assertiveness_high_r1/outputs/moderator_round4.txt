Below is a facilitator’s procedural synthesis of the plateau and a set of bridge options.

## 1) Where the core impasse lies

The central impasse is **not** whether the company needs restructuring. Most parties now accept that it does.

The impasse is over **the acceptable means of adjustment**:

- **Labour / state / works council** insist that:
  - all German plants remain open,
  - no compulsory redundancies occur,
  - job-security and co-determination protections remain intact.

- **Management / investors / family holding** insist that:
  - the savings gap must be closed durably,
  - capacity must be reduced if needed,
  - closure and forced-reduction options cannot be ruled out in advance.

So the deadlock is:

> **site and job preservation as a precondition**  
> versus  
> **capacity reduction as a possible necessity**

A second, related impasse is about **burden-sharing and proof**:
- labour and the state ask for plant-by-plant justification before any closure logic is accepted;
- management and capital ask for a quantified no-closure package that actually closes the gap.

In short: **one side demands proof that closures are unavoidable; the other demands proof that they are avoidable.**

---

## 2) Two or three concrete compromise positions

### Compromise Position A: “No closures for the first phase, with an automatic review trigger”
**Structure**
- All German plants remain open through an initial phase, e.g. 18–24 months.
- No compulsory redundancies in that period.
- A quantified savings and productivity program is implemented immediately.
- A joint review trigger is defined: if agreed financial milestones are missed, the parties reopen the question of more structural measures.

**Why this bridges the gap**
- Gives labour and the state a real site-security window.
- Gives management and investors a credible enforcement mechanism if the no-closure path underperforms.

**Facilitator framing**
- This is not a permanent closure ban.
- It is a time-bounded site-preservation test with pre-agreed metrics.

---

### Compromise Position B: “Capacity reduction without plant closures, if the numbers permit”
**Structure**
- Parties commit first to internal measures:
  - voluntary exits,
  - phased retirement,
  - retraining,
  - working-time reductions,
  - redeployment,
  - management and owner contributions.
- Additional capacity reduction is achieved via:
  - shift cuts,
  - line consolidation,
  - product reallocation,
  - partial site specialization,
  - footprint simplification inside existing sites.

**Why this bridges the gap**
- Addresses management’s demand for a real cost reset.
- Preserves the “no plant closure” red line for labour and the state, at least initially.

**Facilitator framing**
- This is a “maximal internal adjustment” option.
- It tests whether the gap can be closed without site shutdowns.

---

### Compromise Position C: “Protected sites + conditional structural option”
**Structure**
- A list of protected German sites is agreed.
- Those sites are shielded from closure for the duration of the agreement.
- A narrow exception process is created only for sites that fail defined viability criteria.
- Any exception requires:
  - joint review,
  - transparent economics,
  - mitigation package,
  - and a higher decision threshold.

**Why this bridges the gap**
- Gives labour and the state protection against arbitrary closure threats.
- Gives management and investors a defined, non-symbolic route if a site is genuinely unsustainable.

**Facilitator framing**
- This does not predetermine closures.
- It creates a rule-based exception process instead of open-ended confrontation.

## 3) Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
**Question:**  
What quantified combination of voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and productivity gains would you regard as sufficient to keep all German plants open while still closing the savings gap?

---

### To Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What specific, time-limited employee contribution would you be prepared to recommend if it were matched by binding investment, management restraint, and no compulsory redundancies?

---

### To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
What plant-by-plant and function-by-function evidence can you provide that shows why internal measures alone cannot close the gap before any closure or forced-reduction option is activated?

---

### To Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What concrete leverage will you use to secure a no-closure path, and what measurable conditions would trigger you to accept a more structural option if the no-closure package fails?

---

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What is your minimum acceptable durability standard for savings and capacity reduction, and would you accept a time-bounded no-closure test before structural measures are considered?

---

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What quantified margin improvement, by when, would be enough for you to back a site-preserving package instead of insisting on immediate structural adjustment?

---

### To Independent Expert / Advisor
**Question:**  
What specific data would you need, within two weeks, to distinguish a genuinely sufficient no-closure package from one that only delays an unavoidable footprint correction?
