## Moderator summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement on several points:

- A **purely cosmetic** solution is not acceptable.
- Any package must be **durable**, **credible**, and **executable**.
- **Co-determination** and meaningful employee-side involvement are necessary for implementation.
- **Management, owners, and capital** should also contribute; burden-sharing cannot fall on labour alone.
- Preferred tools include **voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, retraining/qualification, working-time measures, and internal transfers**.
- All sides accept that **investment in future products, technology, and Germany-based capability** is part of any viable settlement.
- There is shared concern that a deal must avoid a **prolonged conflict** that could destroy value and damage industrial stability.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:

- **Plant closures**:  
  - Labour side, works council, and state oppose closures as a starting point and want site security.  
  - Management, family holding, and institutional investors say closures cannot be ruled out and that some site change may be necessary.

- **Compulsory redundancies / operational dismissals**:  
  - Labour side and state reject them.  
  - Management and capital will not give an unconditional no-layoff guarantee.

- **Scope of job security**:  
  - Employee and state representatives want binding guarantees, ideally through 2030.  
  - Management and investors say unconditional guarantees may block necessary restructuring.

- **Whether the footprint must shrink**:  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say structural capacity reduction is required.  
  - Labour and state say the case for closures and hard headcount cuts has not been proven and that alternatives must be exhausted first.

- **How “durable labour contribution” is defined**:  
  - Management and capital want meaningful, lasting cost relief.  
  - Labour accepts only temporary, limited contributions tied to firm guarantees.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several trade-offs remain unresolved:

- **Site security vs. economic flexibility**
- **Job protection vs. structural cost reduction**
- **Temporary employee concessions vs. lasting competitiveness**
- **Voluntary/internal adjustment vs. compulsory workforce reduction**
- **Investment commitments vs. the scale of immediate savings sought**
- **Management/owner restraint vs. the amount of labour sacrifice expected**
- **Regional industrial stability vs. shareholder return and margin repair**

In short: no side has yet presented a package that clearly balances **credible cost reduction**, **site protection**, **job security**, and **execution certainty** at the same time.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
For Round 3, the discussion should be narrowed to a concrete package test:

- What exact **savings target** must be achieved?
- Which measures produce those savings **without closures or compulsory redundancies**, if possible?
- If that is not possible, what is the **minimum acceptable fallback** on site and workforce adjustment?
- What **binding commitments** on investment, management restraint, and co-determination would accompany any labour contribution?
- Which elements are **time-limited**, which are **permanent**, and what **review points** would apply?

A useful next step would be for each side to state, in one short list, its **non-negotiables**, its **acceptable concessions**, and the **minimum conditions** under which it would sign a deal.
