We appreciate the clearer positions. Our view remains straightforward:

## Where we agree
- **A durable restructuring is necessary.**
- **Cosmetic delay is value-destructive.**
- **The current cost base is not competitive.**
- **Numbers, execution risk, and 2030 margin impact must drive the decision.**

On those points, we are aligned with management, the family, and the analyst.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
We respect the rejection of forced burden shifting. But your position still goes too far if it means:

- **no closures under any circumstances**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no fallback at all**
- **and full job security regardless of economics**

That is not yet a financially credible answer. If every German site is preserved no matter what, then the burden of overcapacity simply remains embedded in the business. We will not support a solution that leaves the company structurally weak and asks capital markets to absorb the downside.

We are **not ideologically pro-closure**. But we are also **not prepared to fund a permanent competitiveness gap**.

### To Management
We agree that a freeze is not a restructuring. But we also do not accept closures as theatre or as a bluff. If footprint reduction is on the table, the case must be proven with a **fully quantified, side-by-side comparison**.

What is still missing is a credible, transparent bridge from today’s cost base to a competitive one:
- permanent annual savings
- one-time costs
- payback
- execution risk under co-determination
- strike/veto risk
- 2030 margin effect

Without that, “closures are available” is just leverage, not strategy.

### To the State
We agree that political and regional damage matter. But “no closures” as a default principle is not acceptable if it prevents the company from restoring competitiveness. A state-backed solution still has to be economically real.

### To the Controlling Family
We agree on discipline and long-term value creation. But we need you to stop treating the choice as either full-site preservation or blunt closure. There may be a middle path — but only if it produces **durable structural savings**, not just temporary relief.

## Our position on the bridge proposals
### 1) Conditional moratorium
This is only acceptable if it is:
- **time-limited**
- **tied to hard savings targets**
- **independently verified**
- and explicitly designed to test whether a non-closure package is actually sufficient

We can support a moratorium on closures during the test period, **provided the test is real and the benchmark is serious**.

### 2) Non-closure first with contingency measures
This is preferable to a vague closure threat, but only if the contingencies are **still economically meaningful**.

Acceptable:
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits
- retraining and redeployment
- productivity improvements
- overhead reduction
- product and volume reallocation
- management and owner contributions

Not acceptable:
- hidden closure clauses
- automatic site rationalization
- forced redundancies as a back door

### 3) Differentiated site strategy
This is the most realistic structure. Not every site needs the same treatment. But differentiation must not become a code word for sacrifice zones.

It should be used to:
- protect strategic sites
- reduce duplication
- adjust weaker parts of the network
- and improve the overall footprint

## What would move us
We would support a non-closure package over a closure-led one if it credibly delivers:

1. **Durable run-rate savings**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **Lower fixed-cost structure**
4. **Execution certainty under co-determination**
5. **A clear path back to competitive margins by 2030**

If it does not, then we will not pretend it is equivalent just because it is politically easier.

## What we want next
We support the call for a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of:

1. **Non-closure package**
2. **Differentiated-site package**
3. **Partial-closure / footprint reduction package**

For each, we need:
- permanent annual savings
- one-time restructuring costs
- payback period
- strike / veto / implementation risk
- impact on 2030 return on sales and margins

## Bottom line
We will back the solution that **credibly restores competitiveness, protects long-term equity value, and delivers durable margin improvement**.

We will **not** back a cosmetic settlement that preserves every site while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.
