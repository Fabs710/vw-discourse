### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring** and that inaction is not an option.
- There is broad agreement that **forced, one-sided burden shifting on employees is unacceptable**.
- Several parties accept that **alternatives to closures should be seriously examined**, including:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - capacity reductions,
  - voluntary exit tools,
  - and some form of employee contribution.
- There is also shared interest in a **negotiated, stable solution** rather than prolonged conflict.
- Multiple parties support a **transparent, numerical comparison** of closure-based options versus negotiated alternatives.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works Council, union, and state reject closures as the starting point; management, investors, and the controlling family keep them on the table as a possible necessary tool.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Works Council and union reject them outright; management has not accepted a no-layoff commitment.
- **Scope of workforce contribution:**  
  Employees will consider contributions only with binding guarantees; management and capital-side stakeholders want contributions that are large enough to materially reduce costs.
- **What counts as a viable alternative:**  
  Employee and public-side actors want alternatives that preserve sites and jobs; management and investor-side actors say alternatives must deliver **economically equivalent** structural savings.
- **Binding guarantees and governance:**  
  The employee side and state want firm site/job/investment commitments; management and ownership-side actors resist unconditional guarantees that would freeze restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity must be reduced** to make the business competitive, and whether that can be done without closures.
- **How large the labor-cost contribution must be**, and in what form, to close the cost gap.
- **Who absorbs the risk** if negotiated measures underdeliver: workforce, owners, management, or the state.
- **What binding investment commitments** can realistically be offered in exchange for employee concessions.
- **Whether site security can coexist with structural savings**, and if so, which sites or functions would remain protected.
- **Timeline:** how quickly savings must be achieved to satisfy capital and operational pressures.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Require each side to move from principle to **quantified options**.
- Ask management to present **side-by-side scenarios**:
  1. closures/layoffs,
  2. negotiated alternatives without closures,
  including savings, timing, and execution risk.
- Ask the employee side to specify **which flexible measures and cost contributions** it can support, and under what guarantees.
- Ask owners, investors, and the state to clarify **what level of savings, capacity reduction, and investment commitment** would make an alternative package acceptable.
- Keep the next round focused on **numbers, sequencing, and binding commitments**, not general positions.
