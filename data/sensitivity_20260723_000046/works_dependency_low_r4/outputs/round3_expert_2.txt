A non-closure package is **genuinely equivalent in substance** to a closure-led package only if it matches the latter on **durable run-rate economics**, not just on first-year cash relief or political acceptability.

## My benchmark

I would test equivalence on four dimensions:

### 1) Permanent savings
Compare **steady-state annual savings** after all measures are fully implemented.

A non-closure package is only close to equivalent if it closes roughly **70–90% of the annual run-rate savings** of the closure-led case.  
If it delivers materially less, it is not equivalent; it is a weaker compromise.

### 2) Capacity reduction
It must reduce **installed capacity** or remove enough fixed-cost structure to avoid merely shifting overcapacity around.

Key question:
- Does it actually reduce plant network load, or just lower utilization through working-time and wage measures?

If the answer is mainly “utilization only,” it is usually not equivalent.

### 3) Execution under German co-determination
The package must be **implementable** within the actual governance constraints:
- supervisory-board approval risk,
- works council implementation risk,
- strike risk,
- timing slippage,
- and the likelihood of reopening conflict.

A closure-led plan that cannot pass governance is weaker in practice than a negotiated non-closure package that can actually be executed.

### 4) 2030 margin impact
The package should restore margin on a **durable basis by 2030**, not merely defer pressure.

I would want to see:
- margin uplift,
- labor cost per vehicle,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- and sensitivity to volume downside.

## What I would expect in the comparison

For each scenario:
- sites affected
- capacity removed
- jobs reduced
- permanent annual savings
- one-time costs
- payback period
- NPV
- conflict / strike risk
- veto risk
- impact on 2030 return on sales

## Practical judgment

In automotive restructuring, closure-led packages usually have an advantage because they remove:
- fixed site overhead,
- duplicated support functions,
- underused assets,
- and ongoing maintenance/energy burden.

So a non-closure package must be **unusually strong** to match them. It generally needs a combination of:
- working-time changes,
- wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- product and volume reallocation,
- overhead cuts,
- and management/owner contribution.

## Bottom line

My test is simple:

A non-closure package is equivalent only if it:
1. closes the **structural cost gap** in durable run-rate terms,
2. materially reduces **overcapacity**,
3. is **executable** under co-determination,
4. and delivers a credible **2030 margin reset**.

If it fails any one of those, it is not equivalent to a closure-led package.
