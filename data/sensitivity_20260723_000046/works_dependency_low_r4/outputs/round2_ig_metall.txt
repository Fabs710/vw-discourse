We agree with the Works Council on the central red lines:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No one-sided burden shifting onto the workforce**
- **No dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**

And I will be equally clear: the union will not be pushed into accepting layoffs or closures just because management repeats “competitiveness” often enough.

## To management
You say you need structural savings. We do not dispute that the company needs a durable solution.

What we **do** dispute is the idea that closures and job cuts are the necessary starting point. That is not proven. It is a choice. And it is a choice we will resist.

If management wants a serious negotiation, then it must answer three concrete questions:

1. **What exact cost gap must be closed, and by when?**
2. **What management and owner contributions are you prepared to make?**
3. **What binding guarantees will you offer in return for employee concessions?**

So far, I hear a lot about what workers should give up, but not enough about what the other side is giving up.

## To the controlling family and investors
You both say you want an economically equivalent alternative to closures. Fine. Then say what “equivalent” means in practice.

For us, a viable deal must include:
- a **real labour-cost contribution**, but not a permanent wage dismantling,
- **capacity reduction**, but not plant closures,
- **working-time flexibility**, redeployment, and retraining,
- and **binding investment commitments** for the German footprint.

If you expect employees to contribute, then the owners and management must contribute too. That means:
- accepting lower returns in the short term,
- putting capital behind the future of the sites,
- and abandoning the idea that the workforce alone must absorb the restructuring shock.

## To the regional state government
Your insistence on a negotiated solution is right. But it needs to be matched by pressure on management not to use “transparency” as a pretext for closure plans dressed up as inevitability.

We support a side-by-side comparison of:
- closure-based restructuring,
- and negotiated alternatives without closures.

But the comparison must be honest and must include:
- job losses,
- social costs,
- loss of know-how,
- conflict risk,
- and long-term industrial damage.

A narrow spreadsheet comparison is not enough.

## Where we are prepared to move
We have already shown willingness to discuss:
- temporary or structural working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- capacity adjustments,
- deferred or limited wage components,
- and an employee contribution.

That remains our position.

But this only works if there are binding safeguards:
- **site security**
- **job security**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no closures**
- and **respect for the collective wage framework**

If those conditions are met, we can talk seriously about a fair contribution from the workforce.

## Where we will not move
We will not accept:
- closure-led restructuring,
- forced layoffs,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or a deal that turns the crisis into a one-way wage cut for employees while management and owners preserve their position.

If that is the proposal, then it will meet resistance, including industrial action.

## Bottom line
We are ready for a **durable, negotiated restructuring**.

But the price of that is not the destruction of jobs and sites in Germany. The price is a **shared-sacrifice package** in which:
- employees contribute fairly,
- management contributes credibly,
- owners accept responsibility,
- and the company commits to its German industrial base.

If the other side wants a settlement, it must come with numbers and guarantees that reflect that reality. Otherwise, the conflict will continue.
