We appreciate the move from slogans to quantified trade-offs. That is the right direction.

Our position remains straightforward: **we support the package that credibly restores competitive margins with the best risk-adjusted value for shareholders**. We are not wedded to closures as an ideology, but we are equally not prepared to back a “no closures” outcome if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
- With management, the family, and the analyst: the cost base is too high and the status quo is not acceptable.
- With the state, union, and works council: a prolonged conflict would destroy value and should be avoided.
- With everyone: cosmetic solutions are worthless.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and union
Your red line of **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no unconditional guarantees** is understandable, but it is not yet an economically sufficient answer.

You keep saying alternatives should be tried first. Fine — but they must be **large enough and durable enough** to close the structural cost gap. If the package just preserves every site while trimming around the edges, that is not restructuring; it is deferral.

We do **not** accept a settlement that:
- protects all sites regardless of economics,
- preserves an uncompetitive cost base,
- and then asks capital markets to pretend that margins will recover anyway.

If a non-closure package can match the economics, we will support it. But the burden is on that package to prove it.

### To the Regional State Government
We agree that closures should not be a casual default. But “closure as last resort” cannot mean “closure never, even if the numbers fail.” Investor capital cannot subsidize permanent underperformance because it is politically uncomfortable to rationalize capacity.

A comparison that includes social and regional costs is useful, but it must still answer the central financial question: **does the package restore durable competitive returns or not?**

### To management
We agree with the diagnosis, but not with any temptation to use closures as a negotiating bluff unless you are prepared to execute. Investors will not reward theatre.

If you believe closures are necessary, show the numbers. If you believe a negotiated alternative can do the same job, show that too. But do not present a half-measure that leaves the company in the same structural position in two years.

## What we need to see
For any package to win our support, it must demonstrate:
1. **A real and durable reduction in fixed and labour costs**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **Execution certainty under co-determination**
4. **A credible path back to margins and ROS target**
5. **No reliance on temporary concessions that revert later**

## Our bottom line
- We will support **non-closure** only if it is economically equivalent in substance, not just in intention.
- We will support **closures** only if they are truly necessary and part of a coherent plan that restores competitiveness.
- We will **not** support a cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.

If the current round cannot yet show quantified equivalence, then management should bring the side-by-side numbers back. On the present record, we remain skeptical that a pure no-closure solution can fully deliver what is needed.
