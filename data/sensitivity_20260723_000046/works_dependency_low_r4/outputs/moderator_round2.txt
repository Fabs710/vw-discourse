### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one central fault line:

- **Employee side / state:** restructuring is acceptable only if it **preserves all German plants, avoids compulsory redundancies, and protects co-determination**.
- **Management / owners / investors:** restructuring must produce a **durable structural cost reset**, and they do **not** accept an approach that keeps all sites open regardless of economics.

So the impasse is not about whether change is needed. It is about **whether the necessary savings can be achieved without plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and if not, what level of site rationalization is permissible**.

A second, related impasse concerns **proof**:
- employee-side parties say closures have not been demonstrated as necessary;
- management and capital-side parties say non-closure alternatives have not been demonstrated as sufficient.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options for further negotiation. They are proposals for discussion, not endorsements.

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a time-limited no-closure moratorium
- **For a defined period**: no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.
- In exchange, employees accept:
  - temporary working-time reductions,
  - wage moderation or deferral,
  - voluntary exit instruments,
  - redeployment and retraining,
  - capacity reductions through shift and volume changes.
- Management, owners, and state agree to a **joint review milestone** with quantified targets.
- If targets are not met by the review date, the parties reopen discussion on site rationalization.

**Purpose:** gives the employee side immediate protection while giving management a structured path to test whether savings are sufficient without closures.

#### 2) “No closure first” package with a residual fallback mechanism
- Parties jointly design a **non-closure restructuring package** with quantified savings.
- The package is tested against a required savings target.
- If the target is missed, the parties pre-agree that the residual gap will be addressed through:
  - partial site rationalization,
  - line-level consolidation,
  - or other limited footprint adjustments,
  rather than immediate full closures.

**Purpose:** preserves the employee side’s core red line as far as possible, while giving management a fallback if the numbers do not close.

#### 3) Differentiated site strategy
- Not every German site is treated identically.
- Parties classify plants into categories:
  - **core strategic sites**: protected with binding investment and job guarantees;
  - **transition sites**: eligible for capacity reduction, redeployment, or working-time measures;
  - **at-risk sites**: subject to a jointly reviewed rationalization process if economics cannot be repaired.
- In return, the employee side receives stronger guarantees for the core sites, and management receives a path to address the weakest parts of the network.

**Purpose:** breaks the all-or-nothing deadlock by separating strategic protection from economic rationalization.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If management submits a quantified package that closes most, but not all, of the savings gap without closures, what level of residual gap would you still consider negotiable before you would accept that site rationalization must be discussed?

#### Industrial Union
Would you accept a time-limited restructuring agreement with voluntary exits, working-time measures, and wage moderation if it includes binding job security and a jointly verified savings test, even if that package requires a later reopening of the closure issue if targets are missed?

#### Group & Brand Management
What is the minimum quantified permanent savings and capacity reduction you require for a non-closure package to be considered economically sufficient, and what specific measures would you accept in lieu of closures to reach it?

#### Regional State Government
What binding conditions would the state attach to any restructuring support or political backing so that it can credibly defend job security while still allowing a serious competitiveness reset?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a phased compromise that delays closures but includes a pre-agreed trigger for partial rationalization if the non-closure package fails to meet defined savings targets?

#### Independent industrial restructuring analyst
What benchmark would you use to determine whether a non-closure package is genuinely equivalent in substance to a closure-led package under German co-determination constraints?

#### Institutional Investors
What specific evidence or threshold would persuade you that a non-closure package is sufficiently durable and value-creating to merit support over a closure-based alternative?
