Here is the procedural readout from the discussion.

## Core impasse
The central deadlock is **not** about whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is about **what counts as a legitimate restructuring instrument**:

- **Employee side / state:** restructuring must happen **without German plant closures, without compulsory redundancies, and without a hidden closure fallback**.
- **Management / controlling family / investors:** restructuring must deliver a **durable structural cost reset**, and they will not commit unless **footprint reduction remains available** if needed.

So the dispute is over whether:
1. **site closure is a last-resort safeguard**, or
2. **site closure is already a forbidden outcome**.

A second layer of the impasse is **evidentiary**:
- Employee/state side says: prove that non-closure measures cannot work.
- Capital/management side says: prove that non-closure measures can fully close the gap.

In other words, both sides are asking the other to prove the case first.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Conditional moratorium with independent trigger review
**Structure:**  
A time-limited moratorium on German plant closures, paired with a jointly accepted independent benchmark review after a fixed period.

**Key features:**
- No German plant closures during the moratorium.
- A quantified savings and capacity target is set at the start.
- An independent expert verifies whether the non-closure package closes the gap.
- The review measures not just financial savings, but also:
  - capacity reduction,
  - implementation progress,
  - conflict risk,
  - and 2030 margin effect.
- If targets are missed, the parties do **not** automatically accept closure; instead they enter a second-stage escalation procedure with predefined negotiation steps.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Gives employee and state side immediate site protection.
- Gives management/capital side a serious test of whether a non-closure package works.
- Avoids an automatic trapdoor while preserving a structured fallback process.

---

### Proposal 2: Non-closure first package with pre-agreed contingency measures short of closure
**Structure:**  
A primary restructuring package that excludes closures and compulsory redundancies, paired with a menu of **non-closure contingency measures** if targets are missed.

**Possible contingency tools:**
- deeper working-time reductions,
- temporary pay deferrals,
- stronger voluntary exit incentives,
- product and volume reallocation,
- internal redeployment,
- additional management/owner contributions,
- temporary site specialization.

**What is excluded:**  
- automatic plant closures,
- hidden site rationalization clauses,
- compulsory redundancies.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Meets the employee/state demand to avoid closure logic.
- Gives management/capital a mechanism to recover additional savings if the first package underperforms.
- Keeps the discussion within the German footprint rather than turning immediately to shutdowns.

---

### Proposal 3: Differentiated site package with protected core and negotiated adjustment sites
**Structure:**  
A network-wide package in which all German sites remain open, but sites are treated differently in terms of volumes, shifts, investment, and product allocation.

**Key features:**
- No site is designated for closure.
- Some plants are given strategic investment and protected product lines.
- Other plants accept deeper operational adjustments:
  - shift reductions,
  - line changes,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - temporary labor-cost contributions.
- The company obtains structural savings through **footprint optimization without closure**.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Recognizes that not every site has the same economics.
- Avoids the politically explosive label of “at-risk plant.”
- Gives management/capital a route to capacity reduction.
- Preserves the employee/state red line that no German site is closed.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**Question:**  
If an independent review shows that a no-closure package achieves, for example, 80–90% of the structural savings of a closure-led plan, what additional conditions would you still require before agreeing to the package?

### To the Union
**Question:**  
Would you accept a time-limited package with stronger labor contributions and voluntary exits if it is paired with binding site security and an independent verification that no closure fallback is triggered automatically?

### To Management
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to commit to a fully quantified non-closure scenario as the first test case, with closure options suspended during the review period unless an independent benchmark demonstrates a material shortfall?

### To the Controlling Family
**Question:**  
What specific financial threshold or operational failure would lead you to conclude that a non-closure solution is not just politically difficult, but economically insufficient?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
Would you support a non-closure package if it delivers durable margin improvement, even if it requires more time and lower short-term cash savings than a closure-led plan?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What concrete support or governance commitment are you prepared to provide if the parties agree to a no-closure restructuring with binding performance checks?

### To the Independent Restructuring Expert
**Question:**  
What minimum set of metrics should be used to determine whether a non-closure package is “economically equivalent in substance” to a partial-closure package in this governance environment?

---

## Facilitator summary
The discussion can only move forward if the parties separate two questions:

1. **Can the gap be closed without closures?**
2. **If not, what is the least conflict-intensive and most governable alternative?**

At present, each side is treating its preferred answer as the starting assumption. The next productive step is to test a **fully quantified no-closure package against a partial-closure benchmark** using the same metrics and the same timeline.
