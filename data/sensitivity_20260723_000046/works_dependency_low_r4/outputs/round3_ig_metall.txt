I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree with the Works Council
I agree with the core goals:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no one-sided burden shifting**
- **no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**
- **a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**

I also agree that employees should not be asked to pay the full price while management, owners, and capital keep all their options open.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that a credible restructuring must start from closures and layoffs, or that a “residual fallback” to closure is a neutral technicality. It is not. It is leverage against the workforce, and it poisons any serious negotiation from the outset.

I also do **not** accept that the only meaningful protection is a permanent freeze with no adjustment whatsoever. The company does need structural savings. But those savings must be achieved through a **shared-sacrifice package**, not through forced job destruction.

## To management
You still have not met the central burden:
- Show the **exact cost gap**
- Show the **exact savings target**
- Show what **management and owner contributions** are on the table
- Show why a negotiated package without closures cannot close the gap

Right now, you are still treating closures as if they were the default proof of seriousness. They are not. They are a last resort, and only if every negotiated route truly fails.

If you want union cooperation, then bring forward a package that includes:
- working-time adjustments
- internal redeployment
- retraining and qualification
- voluntary exit tools only
- temporary or structural wage moderation
- productivity measures
- and binding investment commitments

But I will say it again: **no compulsory redundancies, no German plant closures, no one-sided wage dismantling**.

## To the controlling family and investors
You both keep asking for “economic equivalence.” Fine. Then show what that means in a credible industrial plan, not just in financial language.

A real equivalent package must include:
- permanent cost relief from management and ownership too,
- not just employee concessions
- a multi-year commitment to German sites
- and protection of the collective wage framework

If your definition of equivalence is “workers give up enough that profit recovers, but the capital side keeps all flexibility,” then we will reject it.

## To the state
Your role is important here, and I agree with you that closures should not be normalized. But I want to be blunt: we need stronger political pressure on management not to hide behind “transparency” while using closure as the hidden baseline.

A serious comparison must include:
- industrial conflict costs
- loss of know-how
- regional damage
- and the damage to trust and productivity

That is not a soft issue. It is part of the economics.

## On the compromise options
### 1) Time-limited no-closure moratorium
This is the only option that begins in the right place: protect jobs and sites first.

But it must not become a disguised countdown to closure. If the company wants a review, then the review must be about:
- whether the negotiated savings have been achieved,
- whether the employee contribution is matched by management and owner contributions,
- and whether the company is regaining competitiveness

It must **not** be a trigger mechanism for reopening closure plans.

### 2) No-closure first with residual fallback
I do **not** support this as written. A fallback to site rationalization is closure by another name. It makes workforce concessions conditional on future threats. That is not a fair bargain.

### 3) Differentiated site strategy
I can see the logic of looking at sites differently, but I will not accept “at-risk” labels that become a roadmap for sacrifice. If differentiation is used, it must be to strengthen the whole footprint, not to create planned losers.

## What the union can support
We remain prepared to negotiate a fair package involving:
- temporary working-time reductions
- capacity adjustments
- redeployment
- retraining
- voluntary exits
- deferred or limited wage components
- and a real employee contribution

But only if the following are in place:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No German plant closures**
3. **Binding site and job security through 2030**
4. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
5. **Management and owner contributions that are material, not symbolic**

## The key question
The question is **not** whether every last euro of the gap can be closed on paper before any discussion can happen.

The question is whether there is a **serious, durable, socially fair alternative** to closures and layoffs.

I believe there is. But it requires management to stop using plant closures as the implied benchmark and to engage honestly on:
- capacity reduction without closure
- real cost-sharing from the top
- and binding guarantees for workers

## Final position
So let me be unambiguous:

- We will not accept compulsory redundancies.
- We will not accept German plant closures.
- We will not accept a one-sided restructuring that makes workers absorb all the pain.
- We are prepared to contribute, but only in a package with fairness, security, and durability.

If management wants a settlement, it must come back with a fully quantified shared-sacrifice package that respects those conditions. If it does not, then conflict remains on the table.
