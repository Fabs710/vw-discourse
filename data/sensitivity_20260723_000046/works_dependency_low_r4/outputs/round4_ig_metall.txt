I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, state, and even the analyst on the basic point:

- the company needs a **durable restructuring**
- cosmetic delay is useless
- employee contributions can be part of a solution
- numbers must be transparent
- a deal that simply shifts the pain onto workers is unacceptable

I also agree that a serious package must be judged on **run-rate economics**, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the growing attempt by management, the family, and investors to make **closures and compulsory redundancies the default benchmark**.

That is not neutrality. It is pressure.

And I do **not** accept the idea that the workforce must first “prove” a perfect non-closure solution while the capital side keeps closure as an implied threat. If there is to be a fair test, then **closure pressure must be removed during the test period**. Otherwise you are not negotiating; you are staging an ultimatum.

## To management
You still have not answered the central question in a way that respects the workforce:

- What is the **exact structural gap**?
- What is the **exact contribution expected from employees**?
- What are the **management and owner contributions**?
- What is the **full cost** of closure, including industrial conflict, loss of know-how, supplier disruption, and execution risk under co-determination?

You keep saying a “real reset” is needed. Fine. But a real reset cannot mean:

- plant closures first,
- layoffs next,
- and wage pressure on top.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is one-sided burden shifting.

## To the controlling family
You say you need economic reality. So do we.

But economic reality also includes:
- strike risk
- loss of trust
- lower productivity after forced layoffs
- recruitment damage
- regional and political backlash
- and the long-term cost of destroying collective bargaining standards

A plan that fractures industrial peace is not automatically value-creating just because it looks cleaner in a spreadsheet.

If you want union cooperation, then accept this as the basis:
- no compulsory redundancies
- no German plant closures
- no fallback closure logic
- protection of the collective agreement framework

If that is impossible for you, then say so openly. But do not call that “discipline” and expect us to applaud.

## To investors
You are right that cosmetics are not enough. But you are wrong if you think the only alternative to closures is weakness.

There is a serious alternative:
- working-time measures
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- retraining
- productivity improvements
- temporary wage moderation or deferral
- management/owner contributions
- and binding investment commitments

That is a real restructuring package if it is properly designed. It is not a fantasy.

## To the state
I appreciate your defense of industrial employment, but I want to be equally clear: we need you to hold the line more firmly against forced redundancies and any plan that normalizes closure threats. Social partnership means more than asking workers to be reasonable.

If the state supports restructuring, it should support:
- no compulsory redundancies
- no closure as the starting assumption
- site and job security
- negotiated adjustment measures
- and investment commitments

## On the analyst’s benchmark
The analyst is right that a non-closure package must be unusually strong to match closure economics.

That does **not** mean closure is therefore justified. It means management must stop pretending the choice is easy.

If the company wants us to accept deep labor-side contributions, then the package must include **real counter-contributions** from management and owners, and it must preserve jobs and sites.

## My line on the bridge proposals
### Conditional moratorium
This is the only sensible starting point, but only if it is real:
- no closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- no hidden trigger mechanism
- no automatic fall-through to rationalization

A review can measure whether the negotiated package works. It cannot be used to smuggle closure back in.

### Non-closure package with contingency measures
This is acceptable only if contingency measures remain **within the non-closure framework**:
- stronger voluntary exits
- more working-time flexibility
- additional temporary contributions
- further internal redeployment
- more product and volume reallocation

Not:
- site rationalization
- partial closure
- compulsory redundancies

### Differentiated site strategy
I remain skeptical if it is used to mark some plants for sacrifice. We will not support dividing the workforce into protected and expendable groups.

## What we can support
The union is prepared to negotiate a serious package with:

- working-time adjustments
- redeployment
- qualification and retraining
- voluntary exit tools only
- temporary or deferred wage components
- productivity measures
- management and owner contributions
- binding investment commitments for German sites

But only if the following are guaranteed:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No German plant closures**
3. **No closure fallback or hidden trigger**
4. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
5. **A durable settlement through 2030**

## What we will not accept
Let me be explicit:

- We will not accept a restructuring that relies on forced layoffs.
- We will not accept plant closures as the hidden benchmark.
- We will not accept a package where workers contribute and capital keeps all its exit options.
- We will not accept a “review” that is really a countdown to closure.

## Final position
We are ready to contribute to a fair, durable restructuring. But the burden must be shared fairly.

If management wants agreement, it must come back with a fully quantified **non-closure** package that:
- protects all German plants,
- rules out compulsory redundancies,
- preserves collective bargaining standards,
- and includes material contributions from management and owners.

If it cannot or will not do that, then the conflict remains. And we will use the strike weapon, alongside our co-determination rights, to defend jobs, wages, and the German industrial footprint.
