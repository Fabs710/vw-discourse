We have now reached the point where positions must be translated into an executable package, not just defended in principle.

I will be equally direct with each side.

## To the Works Council
I respect the clear defense of jobs, skills, and co-determination. I also agree with you on one important point: **a purely one-sided restructuring would be value-destructive**. A prolonged conflict, broken trust, and loss of know-how would damage the company badly.

But I do **not** accept your premise that every German plant can necessarily remain open unchanged and that no compulsory redundancies can coexist with a serious cost reset **without any hard trade-off**.

That is not a governance answer. It is a red line.

If all German plants stay open, if there are no compulsory redundancies, and if the current cost structure is essentially preserved, then the business will continue to bleed competitiveness. That would eventually harm even more jobs and weaken the industrial base you want to protect.

So I can support:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time measures,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- and a negotiated employee contribution,

but only if they are **material enough** to restore margins and competitiveness. Symbolic measures will not do.

## To the Union
I agree that the workforce should not carry the full burden. I also agree that management and owners must show their own contribution.

But let us be honest: **the numbers must close.** A promise of “shared sacrifice” is not enough unless it produces a durable cost reset.

I would support a package that includes:
- wage moderation or deferral,
- flexibility in working time,
- a meaningful voluntary exit program,
- and capacity reduction,

if, and only if, it delivers a **credible structural improvement** in the cost base and protects the future of the remaining German footprint.

What I cannot support is a deal that preserves the old structure and merely postpones the next crisis.

## To the Regional State Government
Your insistence on social partnership is legitimate. I also value stability and the regional role of the company.

But I have to say clearly: **the state cannot insist on preserving every site if that means permanently impairing the company’s value.** That would not protect the region in the long run; it would weaken the industrial anchor itself.

You asked for a transparent comparison. That is right. But the comparison must be judged by one standard: **which package restores competitiveness with the least long-term damage?**

If a non-closure package can do that, I will prefer it. If it cannot, then blocking necessary rationalization would be irresponsible.

## To Management
You have made the case that the current model is not sustainable. I agree with that diagnosis.

Where I need more from you is not rhetoric about urgency, but **a quantified and executable plan**. I want to see:
- how much capacity must actually come out,
- what labor-cost contribution is required,
- what investment is committed to the remaining footprint,
- and how execution risk is controlled under co-determination.

If closures are truly necessary, then prove it with hard numbers and a clear implementation path. If alternatives can deliver the same economics, then use them. But do not come back with a half-measure.

## To the Investor Side
You are right that the market will not reward cosmetic compromise. I support discipline.

But I also want to be clear: **a settlement that destroys labour peace or triggers a prolonged industrial war will also destroy value.** The best outcome is not simply the most severe cut. It is the one that is durable, executable, and restores margins without causing permanent instability.

## Where I stand
The controlling family holding will support a solution only if it meets all of the following:
1. **Structural competitiveness is restored**
2. **Margins are improved durably**
3. **The company’s industrial franchise in Germany is not permanently damaged**
4. **The deal is executable under the governance and co-determination constraints**
5. **Industrial peace is secured**

That means I am open to avoiding closures — but only if the alternative is economically equivalent in substance, not in slogans.

## My current test
Before I can support any package, I need a side-by-side answer to this question:

- What is the savings gap under a closure-led scenario?
- What is the savings gap under a negotiated non-closure scenario?
- How much of that gap is closed permanently, and how much is temporary?
- What is the implementation risk in each case?

If the non-closure package closes the gap in full, I will prefer it.
If it does not, then I will not endorse a solution that preserves every site while leaving the business structurally weak.
