We agree on one central point: the company needs a durable restructuring, and it cannot be done by denial or delay alone.

But we do **not** accept the premise that the only serious path is plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That is a line we will oppose.

Our position is clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- **No one-sided burden on employees.**

If management says the cost problem is real, then the response must be shared sacrifice, not unilateral workforce sacrifice. Employees have already carried a great deal through flexibility, productivity, and years of uncertainty. It is not acceptable that management and owners ask the workforce to pay the full price while preserving their own room to maneuver.

We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary or structural working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- capacity adjustments,
- wage moderation or deferred components,
- and an employee contribution,

**but only if** these measures are tied to binding guarantees for jobs and sites, and only if management and ownership also contribute materially.

What we need to hear from the other side is not just “competitiveness,” but a concrete answer to these questions:

1. Why should employees accept pain while plant closures remain on the table?
2. What is management’s own contribution?
3. What binding commitments on future investment, product allocation, and site security are being offered in return?
4. How does this plan protect the collective bargaining framework instead of using the crisis to tear it apart?

If the proposal is truly about a sustainable future, then it must preserve industrial capacity in Germany and protect the workforce from forced layoffs. Otherwise it is not restructuring; it is shifting the crisis onto employees.

We are ready to negotiate seriously. But if closures and compulsory redundancies remain the starting point, then the conflict will escalate.
