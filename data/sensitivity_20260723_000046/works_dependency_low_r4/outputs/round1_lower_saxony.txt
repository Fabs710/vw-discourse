I appreciate that management is finally acknowledging that alternatives to closures must be assessed seriously, and I also note the union’s willingness to engage on working-time, redeployment, and a negotiated employee contribution. That is the right starting point for a social-partnership solution.

But let me be equally clear: **the state will not support a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany as the default answer, nor one that shifts the burden mainly onto employees.** That is not a sustainable industrial strategy for a company that remains central to our regional economy.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: the current cost and capacity structure cannot simply be frozen in place. We do need a durable restructuring, and we do need a package that is economically credible over the long term.

I also agree with the union and works council that this cannot be imposed one-sidedly. A solution that destroys trust, ignores co-determination, and creates lasting damage to sites and skills will weaken the company, not strengthen it.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the implication that plant closures are somehow the necessary benchmark for seriousness. They are not. They are the most damaging option, and in our view they should be a last resort only after all viable alternatives have been exhausted and credibly shown insufficient.

I also disagree with the idea that “substantial” employee contribution can mean an open-ended demand for wage cuts or the dismantling of guarantees, while ownership and management contributions remain vague. If the workforce is asked to help, then management and owners must also put forward binding commitments and measurable sacrifices.

### What the state expects
If management wants our support, then it must bring forward a package with these elements:

- **No plant closures as the starting point**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **A credible reduction in costs through negotiated alternatives**
- **Capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, qualification, and internal redeployment**
- **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
- **Protection of co-determination and the existing governance role of anchor shareholders**
- **A fair distribution of the burden across management, owners, and workforce**

That means we are open to:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- targeted capacity trimming,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and a limited employee contribution,

**but only if** jobs, sites, and the company’s industrial footprint in Germany are secured in return.

### The state’s red lines
Let me be direct about what will not fly from our side:

- a package that guts regional employment,
- a strategy that turns German plants into mere cost items to be cut,
- any attempt to sideline the state’s co-determination and veto role,
- and any “solution” that is really just a transfer of risk from management to workers and the region.

This company is not just a financial asset. It is a major industrial anchor. Decisions must reflect that reality.

### What I want next
I want management to put forward a revised restructuring concept that compares, transparently and in numbers, the savings from:
1. closures and layoffs,
2. versus negotiated alternatives that preserve sites.

If alternatives can deliver a comparable result, then we should pursue them first. If management cannot show that, then it must explain precisely why, and not merely assert it.

So my message is straightforward: **we are open to restructuring, but not to a shortcut through closure and social damage.** Bring a package that protects the company’s future **and** preserves its industrial base in Germany, and we can work with it. Anything less will meet resistance from us.
