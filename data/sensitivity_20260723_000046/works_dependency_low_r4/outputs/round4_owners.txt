I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree

I agree with all sides on one essential point: **the company needs a durable restructuring now**.  
The status quo is not acceptable, and cosmetic delay would destroy value.

I also agree that:
- temporary fixes alone are insufficient,
- a prolonged conflict would be damaging,
- and any solution has to be judged on **real economics**, not slogans.

So far, the discussion has correctly moved toward the right standard: **durable run-rate economics, execution under co-determination, and 2030 margin impact**.

## Where I disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
I understand your red lines, but I do **not** accept them as the basis for a viable solution.

A package that says:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closure fallback,
- and full job security through 2030 regardless of economics

is not, by itself, a restructuring plan. It is a boundary statement.

And I need to be equally clear: I do **not** accept any framework that permanently preserves every site if that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. That would be value-destructive, and as controlling owners we cannot endorse that.

You keep asking management to prove closures are necessary. Fair. But the burden is not one-sided. A non-closure package must also prove that it can close the structural gap in a durable way. So far, I do not see that proof.

### To Management
I agree with you that the current cost base must come down, and I agree that footprint adjustment cannot be excluded by principle.

But I do **not** accept vague references to “discipline” without a hard comparison. If you want support for a difficult restructuring, then you must present:
- the structural cost gap,
- the permanent savings by measure,
- the one-off costs,
- the conflict and veto risks,
- and the impact on 2030 margins.

If closures are needed, prove it with numbers. If a non-closure package can do the job, then that is preferable. But I will not support a broad, open-ended restructuring mandate based on general pressure alone.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect your role and your concern for industrial stability. I agree that a bad restructuring would be worse than no restructuring.

But I do **not** accept the idea that closures are somehow disqualified as a matter of principle. If the economics require footprint reduction, then the state cannot simply rule it out in advance. That would ignore industrial reality.

At the same time, I agree with you that conflict, trust loss, and regional damage are real costs. Those costs must be in the model, not treated as externalities.

### To Institutional Investors
You are right to insist on credible value creation and not ideology. I agree with that.

But I do not accept a posture that treats every attempt to avoid closures as weakness. A negotiated non-closure package is only acceptable if it is genuinely equivalent in substance. If it is not, then it should not be supported simply because it is politically easier.

### To the independent specialist
Your benchmark is useful. I accept the core point:
- a non-closure package must be judged on **steady-state run-rate savings**,
- it must reduce real capacity and fixed cost,
- and it must be executable under the actual governance constraints.

That is the right test.

## My position on the bridge proposals

### 1) Conditional moratorium with independent trigger review
This is only acceptable if it is truly time-limited and serious.

I can support a moratorium on German plant closures **during a defined test period**, provided the parties commit to:
- a fully quantified non-closure package,
- hard savings targets,
- independent verification,
- and a clear comparison against footprint-reduction alternatives.

But I will not support a moratorium that becomes a permanent shield against hard decisions.

### 2) Non-closure first with non-closure contingency measures
This is the most promising concept, but only if the contingency tools remain economically real.

Acceptable contingency tools could include:
- deeper working-time flexibility,
- temporary or structural wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- product and volume reallocation,
- overhead reduction,
- and management/owner contributions.

What I do **not** support as contingency are:
- hidden closure clauses,
- automatic site rationalization,
- or compulsory redundancies as a back door.

### 3) Differentiated site strategy
This is the most realistic structure if we are serious.

Not every site will contribute in the same way. A differentiated strategy can:
- protect strategic, competitive sites,
- reduce duplication,
- adjust weaker parts of the network,
- and still avoid a blunt all-or-nothing confrontation.

That is not sacrifice logic. It is industrial reality.

## What I need next

Before I can support any final package, I want a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of:

1. **Non-closure package**
2. **Differentiated-site package**
3. **Partial-closure / footprint reduction package**

For each, I need:
- permanent annual savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- payback period,
- conflict and strike risk,
- supervisory-board and veto risk,
- and impact on 2030 margin / return on sales.

## Bottom line

I am not here to preserve every site at any price.

I am also not here to approve a destructive restructuring without proof.

My standard remains:
- **restore competitiveness and margins**
- **protect long-term enterprise value**
- **avoid value-destructive instability**
- **support the most effective and executable solution**

If the numbers show that a non-closure package can genuinely achieve that, I will consider it.  
If not, then footprint adjustment must remain on the table.
