## Where we agree
- With management, the family, and the analyst: the current cost base is not competitive, and the status quo is unacceptable.
- With the state, union, and works council: a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.
- With everyone: cosmetic compromises are useless.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and union
Your repeated red lines — **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no fallback closure mechanism** — are understandable politically, but they are **not yet a sufficient financial answer**.

We do **not** support a solution that:
- keeps every site open regardless of economics,
- leaves structural overcapacity in place,
- and asks capital markets to accept a weaker margin trajectory on faith.

If a non-closure package can truly close the gap in full, we would support it. But the burden is on you to show that it can do so **permanently**, not just delay the problem for the next bargaining round.

### To management
We agree that a freeze is not a solution. But we do **not** support using closures as theatre or as a bluff. If closures are on the table, the numbers must justify them, and the implementation plan must be credible under co-determination constraints.

A threat that cannot be executed is not discipline; it is noise.

### To the state
We agree that closures should not be treated casually. But “last resort” cannot become “never, regardless of the economics.” The company’s value cannot be subordinated indefinitely to preserving every site.

### To the controlling family
We agree that durable competitiveness matters more than short-term optics. But the solution cannot be an open-ended stalemate in which the workforce is pressured while the governance bloc avoids making hard trade-offs.

## What we need to see
Before we support any final package, we need a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of:
1. **non-closure package**
2. **partial-closure / footprint rationalization package**
3. **full closure-led package**

For each, we need:
- permanent annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- payback period,
- implementation risk,
- strike/veto risk,
- and impact on 2030 margins / return-on-sales.

- **We are not ideologically anti-closure.**
- We are pro-**credible economics**.

If a negotiated non-closure package is truly equivalent in substance, we can support it.
