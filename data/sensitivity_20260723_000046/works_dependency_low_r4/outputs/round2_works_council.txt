We are not moving off the core issue: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies.**

I appreciate that some parties now accept that the debate must be numerical and not just rhetorical. That is overdue. But let me be equally clear: numbers do not magically justify breaking historic job guarantees or shutting German sites. If management wants a solution, it must come back with a plan that **preserves every German plant, protects employment, and respects co-determination**.

### Where I agree
I agree with the union, the state, and even parts of what the owners and investors have said:
- the company needs a durable restructuring,
- a cosmetic solution is useless,
- and purely verbal promises are not enough.

I also agree that we need transparent scenarios. Fine. Put the figures on the table.

### Where I disagree
I fundamentally reject the framing that closures and layoffs are the “benchmark” for seriousness.

That is backwards.

A serious plan is one that:
- keeps all German plants open,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- preserves industrial capacity in Germany,
- and still delivers savings through negotiated, socially responsible means.

If management says the current structure is too expensive, then the burden of proof is on management to show why **the same result cannot be achieved without destroying sites and jobs**. Not asserted, demonstrated.

### To management
You say you want structural savings and execution certainty. So do we. But your language keeps returning to closures as if they were the default answer. They are not acceptable to us.

If you want our support, bring forward a package built on:
- working-time reduction,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exit instruments only,
- temporary or staggered labour-cost adjustments,
- and binding investment commitments for German plants.

What you do **not** get from us:
- a blank cheque for closures,
- a mandate for compulsory layoffs,
- or a deal that uses the restructuring as a pretext to tear up job security.

### To the controlling family and investors
You keep saying “economically equivalent.” I will be direct: economically equivalent **for whom**?

If equivalence means protecting shareholder margins by sacrificing thousands of workers and closing plants, then we reject that premise. The company is not a spreadsheet. It is a productive industrial system with skills, communities, and long-term capacity.

You speak about value destruction. Let me remind you: **closing plants, breaking trust, and forcing redundancies also destroys value** — through conflict, lost know-how, damaged commitment, and permanent social damage. That risk is not theoretical. We have already mobilised warning strikes, and we will escalate if necessary.

### To the regional state government
Your insistence on alternatives is welcome, but it must become firmer. A “transparent comparison” is not enough if management uses it to stage-manage closure as inevitable.

The state should make clear that:
- no German closure can be treated as the starting assumption,
- any restructuring must respect the social and industrial fabric of the region,
- and the governance rights of labour and the state are not decorative.

### What we can discuss
We are prepared to discuss real adjustments, including:
- temporary capacity reductions,
- working-time solutions,
- redeployment across sites,
- training and requalification,
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- and production reallocations **within Germany**.

But all of this requires the following binding conditions:
1. **No closures of German plants**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **No dismantling of co-determination or the existing job-security framework**
5. **Material investment commitments to German sites**

Without those guarantees, there is nothing to negotiate. There is only resistance.

### Bottom line
Management has not yet produced a justified case for closing any German plant. Until it does — and I doubt it can — the Works Council will hold the line.

If necessary, we will continue to use all our rights under co-determination, and we will support further industrial action. We are not here to manage the orderly dismantling of German industry. We are here to defend the workforce and the future of our sites.
