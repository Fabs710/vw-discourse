We agree with the core diagnosis from management and the controlling family: **the current structure cannot remain as it is if the company is to recover competitive margins and protect long-term value.** A return to acceptable return-on-sales and a lower, more durable cost base is essential.

Where we differ is on the route.

## Our position
As capital-market shareholders, we will support **the solution that credibly restores competitiveness with the highest risk-adjusted value**. That means:

- durable cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- execution certainty,
- and a clear path back to margin improvement.

What we **will not** support is a package that looks socially neat but leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## To the Works Council and the Union
We hear your objections to plant closures and compulsory redundancies. We understand why you are drawing that line. But from our perspective, **a categorical “no closures, no layoffs” position is not enough unless the economics are equally credible**.

If the alternative package truly delivers the same savings, then fine — we are open to it. But it must be more than:
- temporary optics,
- deferred pain,
- or savings that disappear after the first bargaining cycle.

We would need to see:
- quantified, durable cost reductions,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- structural labour-cost relief,
- and no hidden reversion to the old cost base.

If that cannot be delivered, then insisting on preserving every site becomes a value-destructive stance, however understandable socially.

## To management
We support your insistence that this cannot be cosmetic. But you need to be careful not to overstate closures as a solution before proving that negotiated alternatives cannot close the gap.

From an investor point of view, the key question is not whether closures are politically difficult. It is whether management can show, in numbers, that the final package will:
- restore competitiveness,
- protect investment capacity,
- and stop margin erosion.

If closures are truly required, then make the case transparently. If alternatives can achieve equivalent savings, then use them. But do not return with a half-measure that preserves excess structure and merely postpones the issue.

## To the Regional State Government
We agree with your demand for a transparent comparison of options. That is exactly what the market will look for as well.

But transparency alone is not enough. The comparison must show:
- the cost gap closed in full,
- the timeline to realization,
- and the durability of the savings.

If the alternative package depends on ongoing concessions that can be reversed, or on political guarantees that weaken competitiveness, then it will not be enough to support valuation.

## To the Controlling Family
We share your concern about permanent value destruction. A prolonged conflict that erodes margins and delays restructuring would be very negative for shareholders.

But support from capital markets depends on discipline. If the outcome becomes a compromise that protects every constituency except the balance sheet, then equity holders will continue to discount the company accordingly.

## Our bottom line
We are prepared to support **either**:
1. a closure-based restructuring, **if** it is necessary and genuinely restores margins, or
2. a negotiated alternative, **if** it delivers the same structural economics with execution certainty.

What we cannot support is:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a prolonged standoff,
- or a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

So our ask is simple: bring forward a package with hard numbers showing how the company gets back to a durable competitive margin. Without that, the market will not reward rhetoric, and neither will we.
