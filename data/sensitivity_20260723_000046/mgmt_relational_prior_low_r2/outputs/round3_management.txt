## To the Works Council
I agree with you on one point: this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. Temporary fixes alone will not restore this company’s competitiveness.

But I **do not** accept your attempt to turn:
- **“all German plants remain open,”**
- **“no compulsory redundancies,”**
- and **“binding guarantees to 2030”**

into absolute preconditions.

That would freeze the cost base while the market keeps moving. It is not a restructuring; it is a demand that management carry structural overcapacity indefinitely.

You say closures are a choice, not a necessity. I disagree. In a shrinking market with a cost base far above target, the burden of proof is on anyone claiming that we can preserve the full footprint and still regain margin. So far, I have not seen a package from labour that does that.

If you want to rule out closures, then bring a package that is:
- **large enough**,
- **durable enough**,
- **fast enough**,
- and **enforceable enough**
to close the structural gap.

Not slogans. Not political declarations. Numbers.

## To the Union
I appreciate that you acknowledge the need for real restructuring and a time-limited employee contribution. That is at least reality-based.

But I do **not** accept your line that closures can only be discussed after every alternative has been exhausted in some abstract sense. We are already in a crisis. The company does not have the luxury of endless probation periods for half-measures.

You say you will not accept compulsory redundancies or plant closures. I hear that. But then you need to explain how you intend to remove enough cost and capacity to make the business viable.

A package of:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- limited wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- and productivity promises

may help at the margins. It does **not** yet solve the structural overcapacity problem.

So let me be blunt: if the alternative does not materially change the economics, it is not a solution.

## To the State
I respect your governance role and your concern for the regional industrial base. But the state cannot veto the economics.

I agree that a disorderly restructuring would be destructive. I do **not** agree that the answer is to rule out closures as a default and hope the numbers somehow work.

If you want closures avoided, then support a package that truly closes the gap. If you want job and site protection, fine — but not at the price of permanent uncompetitiveness.

## To the Family Owners and Investors
I agree with your core point: we need durable, measurable savings, not a symbolic compromise.

And I share your concern that a deal which preserves every site, keeps all old guarantees, and delivers only minor concessions would simply postpone the problem. That is unacceptable.

But I also want to be clear: I am not asking for a purely closure-driven route as ideology. I am asking for a package that restores competitiveness. If labour can deliver a **functionally equivalent** outcome without formal closures, I will examine it seriously.

So far, I have not heard that package.

## On the expert assessment
The analyst’s input confirms what we already know:
- non-closure measures can create savings,
- but they usually do **not** fully match closure-level savings unless they include deep and permanent capacity removal plus a real labor-cost reset.

That is exactly why I am not accepting weak alternatives.

If the workforce wants no closures, then the alternative must be economically near-equivalent. Not “somewhat better than nothing.” Near-equivalent.

## My position, plainly
I remain prepared to negotiate, but only on the basis of a package that delivers:

1. **A durable reduction in overcapacity**
2. **A meaningful reduction in labour cost**
3. **A measurable contribution from employees**
4. **A credible investment and product roadmap**
5. **A path back to the brand’s return-on-sales target**

What I will **not** accept:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a short-term truce,
- or a package that preserves the current cost structure and calls it social responsibility.

## What would move me
If labour wants closures off the table, I need to see a concrete alternative with:
- quantified annual savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- enforceable labor-cost relief,
- and a timetable that affects 2030 margins, not just next quarter.

Without that, closures remain on the table.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve every legacy structure. I am here to restore competitiveness.

That means:
- no blanket protection of all sites,
- no absolute veto on structural headcount reduction,
- no agreement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

If you bring me a package that genuinely closes the structural gap, I will consider it. If you do not, then the harder measures remain necessary.
