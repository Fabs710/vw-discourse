I hear the red lines you are drawing, but I need to be equally direct: **a cosmetic restructuring that leaves our German cost base structurally uncompetitive is not an option**.

We are facing a real crisis, not a theoretical debate. Demand in Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our factory costs are materially above target. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target, and if we do not restore competitiveness now, we will not have the funds to invest in future products, software, and technology through 2030.

So where I agree with you:
- We need a **negotiated solution**, not chaos.
- We need **socially responsible implementation**.
- We need **voluntary instruments, redeployment, productivity gains, and investment commitments** as part of the package.

Where I disagree sharply:
- **“Every German plant must remain open” is not a sustainable starting point.**
- **“No compulsory redundancies” cannot automatically mean no structural headcount reduction.**
- **Binding guarantees through 2030, regardless of economics, are not compatible with the scale of adjustment we need.**
- A deal that simply preserves all sites and simply spreads pain thinly across the workforce would be a delay, not a solution.

Let me be very clear on the core issue: **we need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.** That may include plant closures, but if there are other measures that achieve the same structural effect with comparable certainty, I am prepared to examine them. What I am not prepared to accept is a package that relies on temporary savings, deferred pain, or vague promises while the cost base remains above competitive levels.

To the union specifically: I do not reject burden-sharing. In fact, I expect it. But it must be real and measurable. That means:
- a genuine labour-cost contribution,
- lower structural complexity,
- fewer excess capacity costs,
- and a credible path back to target margins.

If the workforce is asking management, owners, and the state to contribute, then I say plainly: we will discuss the full package. But there will be no agreement if the result is merely protecting every current site and every current arrangement while expecting the company to absorb the entire burden.

So my position is this:
1. **I am open to alternatives to closures if they deliver equivalent, durable savings.**
2. **I am not open to a settlement that preserves today’s footprint at tomorrow’s expense.**
3. **I will not accept a deal that fails to materially reduce overcapacity and labor costs.**

If you want a negotiated outcome, bring me a proposal that actually fixes the structure of the business. If not, then we are simply postponing a confrontation that the company cannot afford to lose.
