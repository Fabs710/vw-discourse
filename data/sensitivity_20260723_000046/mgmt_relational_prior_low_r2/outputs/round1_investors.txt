From the capital markets side, our position is straightforward: **we care first about a credible, durable return to competitive margins and value creation.** We are not attached to any particular instrument, site, or political preference. If a negotiated solution can deliver the same structural savings as closures, we will support that. If it cannot, we will not back a cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

Where we agree:
- **Management is right** that temporary fixes alone are not enough.
- **The workforce-side stakeholders are right** that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- **The state and the family are right** that a disorderly or purely confrontational approach would be damaging.
- Everyone seems to recognize that the current cost base and capacity are not sustainable.

Where we disagree:
- We do **not** support red lines that effectively rule out any meaningful structural adjustment before the economics have been fixed.
- We also do **not** support management using “competitiveness” as a blanket justification for a weak or poorly specified plan.
- A package that preserves all sites, avoids hard capacity decisions, and relies mainly on temporary concessions would be unacceptable to us.

Our test is simple:
1. **Does the package materially reduce structural costs?**
2. **Does it restore margins on a durable basis, not just for one budget cycle?**
3. **Is the savings profile credible and enforceable?**
4. **Does it avoid destructive delay and ongoing strike risk?**

On that basis:
- We are open to **plant closures, capacity reductions, or labour-cost reductions** if they are genuinely required and clearly quantified.
- We are also open to **alternative measures** — working-time flexibility, redeployment, voluntary exits, targeted pay moderation, productivity gains — but only if they produce **comparable, durable savings**.
- We are **not** open to vague “shared sacrifice” language unless it translates into measurable margin improvement.

To labour representatives: if you want to avoid closures, you need to bring something that closes the cost gap in hard numbers. “No closures” is not a financial plan.
To management: if you want investor support, show us the numbers, the timeline, and the downside protection. We need to see that this is a real restructuring, not a deferred problem.
To the family and the state: we understand the governance constraints, but those constraints cannot become an excuse for preserving an uncompetitive structure indefinitely.

So our message is:
**We will support the option that most credibly restores competitiveness, even if that means tough decisions.**
But we will **not** support a settlement that protects everyone politically while impairing shareholder value structurally.
