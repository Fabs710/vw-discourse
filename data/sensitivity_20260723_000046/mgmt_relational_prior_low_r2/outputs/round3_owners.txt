I will speak plainly.

## My position
I support a restructuring that restores competitiveness, margins, and long-term value. I do **not** support a cosmetic compromise that preserves the current cost base and merely postpones the problem.

I also do **not** support a value-destructive confrontation. A prolonged strike, political deadlock, or a disorderly restructuring would harm the company and the family holding’s core asset. That is unacceptable.

So the question is not whether change is needed. It is whether the package is **structurally credible**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the analyst on one central point:  
**temporary fixes alone are insufficient.**

I also agree with labour and the state that:
- the burden cannot fall on workers alone,
- a negotiated solution is preferable to escalation,
- and any package must be quantified and enforceable.

That is the common ground. But common ground does not mean I accept the current labour red lines.

## Where I disagree

### 1) On “all German plants remain open”
I do **not** accept that as a governing principle.

If the numbers do not work, then preserving every site by declaration is not stewardship. It is denial. The family holding cannot back a solution that protects every legacy structure regardless of economics.

### 2) On “no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I understand why the workforce says this. I do **not** accept it as an absolute constraint if the structural gap cannot be closed otherwise.

If permanent capacity has to come out, then some form of structural headcount reduction may be unavoidable. I would strongly prefer voluntary and negotiated instruments, but I will not rule out hard measures in principle if that is what the economics require.

### 3) On 2030 guarantees
A rigid blanket guarantee through 2030 is too inflexible if it prevents adaptation to market, technology, or demand shifts.

I can support time-limited security, conditional commitments, and strong protections tied to investment and volume assumptions. I cannot support a lock-up that disables future governance.

### 4) On “no closures unless proven unavoidable”
The analyst’s input makes the burden of proof clearer: non-closure measures can help, but they usually do **not** match closure-level savings unless they effectively remove capacity and cost in a way that is economically similar to closure.

That means a genuine no-closure package must be **near-equivalent** in effect. Not symbolic. Not partial. Not delayed.

## To the Works Council
You are right to insist on real burden sharing and real numbers. I support that.

But I do **not** accept your current formulation that all plants remain open and job security is binding regardless of economics. That would effectively transfer the adjustment burden to future margins and future owners, which is irresponsible.

If you want closures off the table, then the alternative must be strong enough to close the structural gap. That means:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- productivity gains,
- and enforceable implementation.

If those elements are absent, then your position is not an alternative. It is a refusal to confront the problem.

## To the Union
I agree that any employee contribution must be time-limited, fair, and matched by contributions from management and ownership.

I do **not** agree that labour can contribute only in a narrow and easily reversible way while the company is expected to regain competitiveness. The economics have to improve materially.

If the union is offering only symbolic moderation, that will not be enough. If it is prepared to accept a real, measurable contribution that helps close the gap, then that is a different conversation.

## To Management
You have correctly identified the cost problem. I support a hard restructuring goal.

But I want to be clear: you do **not** have a blank cheque to force closures just because they are administratively neat. If you want ownership support, you need to show:
1. the exact structural cost gap,
2. the savings from non-closure measures,
3. the remaining shortfall,
4. and why that shortfall cannot be closed without a closure or an economically equivalent measure.

If you can show that, then I will support decisive action. If you cannot, then you need to accept that labour-protection and site-preservation arguments have real weight.

## To the State
I respect your governance role and your concern for regional stability. But the state cannot veto economics indefinitely.

I do agree that a rushed closure could create value destruction through strikes and political escalation. That is why I am not advocating rash action.

But I do **not** accept the idea that a political commitment to preserve sites can substitute for a viable business case. If the package fails economically, then it is not stewardship to block all structural adjustment.

## To Investors
I agree with your insistence on durable, measurable savings. That is the right standard.

I also share your concern that a weak compromise would only postpone pain and erode value further. We should not back that.

But I caution against treating closures as a default cure. If closures trigger labour conflict and reputational damage, then they may destroy more value than they save. The right outcome is the one that restores competitiveness with the least overall damage.

## My bottom line
The family holding’s position is this:

- We support a **real** restructuring.
- We do **not** support a cosmetic deal.
- We are open to avoiding closures **only if** the alternative is economically near-equivalent, durable, and enforceable.
- We will not back an outcome that permanently weakens the enterprise or the owners’ governance capacity.
- We will not back a prolonged conflict that destroys value.

## What would move me
A credible alternative to closures would need to include:
- a quantified and material cost reduction,
- permanent capacity removal,
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- strong productivity and complexity cuts,
- a time-limited but significant employee contribution,
- and a binding investment/product roadmap.

If that package exists, I will seriously consider it.

If it does not, then closures or functionally equivalent measures remain on the table.

## Final word
I am not here to preserve every site as a matter of principle.  
I am here to protect the long-term value of the company.

So let us stop arguing over labels. If the non-closure package produces closure-level economics, fine. If it does not, then it is not sufficient.

I would welcome a **quantified side-by-side package comparison** from management: one scenario with closures, one without, showing:
- annual savings,
- transition costs,
- implementation timing,
- labour impact,
- and remaining risk.

That is the level of seriousness this situation requires.
