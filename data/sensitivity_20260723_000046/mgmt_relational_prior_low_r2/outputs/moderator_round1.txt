**Moderator summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious competitiveness and cost problem**.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated solution** rather than an imposed or chaotic confrontation.
- Most parties endorse some mix of:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment and qualification,
  - productivity/process improvements,
  - targeted cost measures,
  - and investment/product commitments.
- There is general recognition that **temporary fixes alone are insufficient** and that any solution must be **durable**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: labour-side and state-side actors reject them as a starting point; management, family ownership, and investors keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: works council and union oppose them; management, ownership, and investors do not rule out structural headcount reduction if required.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**: labour and state want strong site/job security; management, ownership, and investors say they cannot accept rigid guarantees that block adaptation.
- **Burden sharing**: labour-side actors want contributions from management and shareholders; management and investors seek measurable labour-cost relief and structural savings, not just temporary concessions.
- **Governance limits**: the state and the controlling family emphasize their co-determination / oversight role and reject unilateral action; management wants room to make structural changes.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to achieve durable savings without closures**: no side has yet presented a quantified alternative that clearly matches closure-level savings.
- **How much labor sacrifice is acceptable, and for how long**: wage moderation, working-time reductions, and flexibility are discussed, but the scale and duration are unresolved.
- **What management/owners contribute concretely**: there is no agreed package on top-level restraint, capital commitments, or governance concessions.
- **What guarantees are feasible**: there is no consensus on whether site/job security can be binding, conditional, or time-limited.
- **How to avoid strike escalation while still reaching a structural deal**: the conflict risk is acknowledged, but no de-escalation mechanism has been agreed.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from position statements to **specific package design**. A useful next step would be for each side to name:
1. the **minimum conditions** for agreement,
2. the **quantified contributions** it is prepared to make,
3. the **non-negotiable limits** it still holds,
4. and one **credible alternative** to plant closures, if it wants closures off the table.

The next round should aim to test whether there is a **numerically credible, socially acceptable, and governable restructuring package**.
