Let me answer directly, because we are now at the point where slogans need to give way to arithmetic.

### First: where I agree
I agree with everyone on one basic point: **this company needs a durable, negotiated restructuring**.  
I also agree that:
- a prolonged strike would destroy value,
- temporary fixes alone are not enough,
- and any solution has to be governable and socially implementable.

I am also prepared to discuss:
- working-time reductions,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

### But I need to be equally clear: where I disagree
I do **not** accept the Works Council’s attempt to turn “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute principles while also demanding 2030 guarantees and no strategic flexibility. That is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand that the company carry its current cost base forward and hope the market improves. It won’t.

I also do **not** accept the union’s framing that closures can only be discussed after every alternative has been proven impossible. We are already past the point where abstract promises are enough. The company needs a **measurable structural reduction** in cost and capacity now.

### The core issue
We have a German cost base that is too high, a shrinking European market, and a brand that is missing its return-on-sales target. That means we need **permanent** savings, not a temporary truce.

So let me state the non-negotiable business requirement:

- **We need a durable reduction in overcapacity.**
- **We need a durable reduction in labour cost.**
- **We need a credible path back to margin.**

If a package can deliver that without closures, I am open to it. But it must be **equivalent in scale, certainty, and timing**.  
Not “some savings.” Not “later savings.” Not “promises of flexibility.” Real, hard, enforceable savings.

### On the Works Council’s proposal
You asked for numbers. Fair enough.

Then here is the reality: if you want to rule out closures, you need to present a package that closes the gap in a way that is:
1. **large enough**,
2. **durable enough**,
3. **enforceable enough**,
4. and **fast enough** to matter.

At present, what I hear is:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and calls for management restraint.

Those are all useful, but by themselves they do **not** yet add up to a structural solution. They may help at the margins. They do not yet solve the overcapacity problem.

So no, I will not accept “all German plants remain open” as a starting assumption. That is a conclusion that would have to be earned through numbers, not declared as a political wish.

### On the union’s position
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss moderated wage development and temporary measures. That is at least a recognition that labour must contribute.

But let me be blunt: a **time-limited employee contribution** only works if it is substantial enough to change the economics. If it is simply a symbolic wage pause while the company keeps carrying excess capacity, then it is not a restructuring contribution — it is a delay tactic.

And I will not accept a package that preserves the collective wage framework in form while making the workforce absorb the full adjustment in practice through hidden flexibility and endless insecurity.

### On the state and the family
I hear the political and governance constraints. I also respect that neither of you wants a destructive conflict. But governance does not change the economic facts.

The state cannot veto reality.  
The family cannot preserve value by protecting every legacy structure.  
And the company cannot regain competitiveness if every difficult measure is ruled out in advance.

If you want to prevent closures, then help deliver a package that is **structurally equivalent** to closures in savings. Not by rhetoric, but by numbers.

### What I am prepared to put on the table
I am prepared to work on a package built around:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity and process improvements,
- a limited employee contribution,
- and investment commitments for future products and technology.

But that package must include:
- a **real** reduction in cost,
- a **real** reduction in capacity,
- and a **real** contribution from labour.

And I am saying plainly: if those elements cannot be achieved without closures, then closures remain on the table.

### My minimum condition
I need to see a proposal that closes the structural gap.  
If you want closures off the table, bring me a **quantified alternative** that does the same job. Until then, I am not moving off the principle that the company must reduce footprint, cost, and complexity.

### Final point
I am not interested in a deal that protects every current site and postpones the problem onto the next balance sheet. That would be irresponsible to the company, to the workforce in the long run, and to the capital providers.

So let’s be honest with each other:
- If you can deliver durable savings without closures, I will examine it seriously.
- If you cannot, then we will have to confront the hard decisions that management has already put on the table.

If you want, I can next specify the **exact package architecture** management would consider acceptable, including the scale of labour contribution and the threshold for avoiding closures.
