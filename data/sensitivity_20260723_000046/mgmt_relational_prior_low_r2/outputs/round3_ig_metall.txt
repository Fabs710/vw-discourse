I will answer directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the core diagnosis: the company has a **real structural cost and competitiveness problem**, and **temporary fixes alone are not enough**. I also agree that a prolonged strike would damage the company, the workforce, and everyone’s long-term interests. So I am not arguing for denial or delay.

I also agree that a serious package may include:
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification and reskilling,
- productivity improvements,
- and a **time-limited employee contribution**.

That is exactly why we have already put counter-proposals on the table.

## Where I disagree — and strongly
I do **not** accept the idea that the only serious benchmark is a closure-driven restructuring.  
And I do **not** accept that the workforce must prove a “near-equivalent” to closure savings before closures can be ruled out.

That framing is backwards.

Management and ownership want to turn plant closures and compulsory redundancies into the default solution, and then require labour to disprove it. But if the company wants workers to accept sacrifices, then the company must first show that it has exhausted fair alternatives and that those alternatives are shared fairly.

## To management
You keep saying you need “durable” and “equivalent” savings. Fine. Then show your arithmetic transparently.

But I reject the leap from “non-closure measures are harder” to “therefore closures are justified.”  
Harder is not the same as necessary.

And I will be very clear: a package that shifts the burden onto workers through wage restraint, working-time cuts, and permanent insecurity while keeping plant closure threats on the table is **not** a fair restructuring. It is pressure by another name.

If management wants labour peace, it must stop treating the workforce as the primary adjustment variable.

### My position on closures
- **No plant closures as part of the agreement.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No hidden closures through staged hollowing-out of sites.**

If the company says it needs structural savings, then it must pursue them through a negotiated package that preserves sites and jobs. I am not prepared to move off that.

## To the works council
I support your insistence that German plants cannot simply be sacrificed.  
I also support your rejection of compulsory redundancies.

But I need to be equally direct: “all plants remain open” is only defensible if we are prepared to back it with a serious alternative package. That means not just principles, but enforceable measures.

Where I would sharpen the position is this:
- plant openness is the goal,
- but the labour contribution must be **limited, time-bound, and matched**,
- and management/top-level restraint must be real, not symbolic.

We should not allow a situation where workers give up wage development and flexibility while management keeps all strategic options open and calls that a deal.

## To the state
I appreciate your rejection of closures as a default and your willingness to support a negotiated outcome. But a “conditional” stance is not enough if it leaves the workforce exposed to future pressure.

If you want to help preserve the industrial base, then use your governance role to back:
- binding site security,
- no forced layoffs,
- serious investment commitments,
- and a settlement that does not undermine co-determination.

But I will also say this: the state cannot be asked to block closures while accepting a deal that leaves workers paying most of the bill. That would not be legitimate protection; it would be managed decline.

## To the family and investors
You are right about one thing: we need hard numbers.  
But your reading of the analyst goes too far if you use it to imply that closures are automatically justified.

The analyst said non-closure measures can reduce costs meaningfully, and that only under aggressive assumptions do they approach closure economics. That does **not** mean closures are the only rational choice. It means the package must be serious. We are prepared to be serious. We are **not** prepared to be blackmailed into accepting plant closures because they are administratively convenient.

And let me be blunt: a strike-heavy conflict, destroyed trust, and political escalation also destroy value. So if you choose confrontation over compromise, do not pretend that is the financially disciplined route.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We remain open to a package that includes:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- moderate wage restraint for a limited period,
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity and process changes,
- and a contribution from employees **matched by management and ownership**.

But that package must include:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
4. **Binding job and site security**
5. **Meaningful contributions from management and owners**
6. **A credible investment and product roadmap**

## What we will not accept
Let me remove any ambiguity:
- no closure-by-stealth,
- no “functional closure” hidden behind network rationalization,
- no permanent wage reset,
- no one-sided flexibility,
- no socializing the adjustment onto workers while ownership keeps strategic freedom untouched.

Those are not bargaining positions I can move on.

## On the expert assessment
The analyst’s point is useful, but it does not change the basic bargaining reality. If closure economics can only be matched by something that is effectively a disguised closure, then that is exactly why the workforce rejects the whole logic. A legitimate restructuring cannot be built by renaming the same outcome.

We are prepared to contribute to a real alternative.  
We are not prepared to sign up for a semantic workaround that empties sites in all but name.

## Bottom line
Our position is unchanged:
- We will negotiate.
- We will accept shared sacrifice.
- We will consider limited, time-bound employee contributions.
- But we will **not** accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

If management and owners want a deal, bring a real burden-sharing package that protects jobs, preserves sites, and respects the collective bargaining framework.  
If they refuse, then they should expect resistance, not consent.
