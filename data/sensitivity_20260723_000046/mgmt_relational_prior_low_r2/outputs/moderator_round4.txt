The discussion has now converged on a clear **core impasse**:

## Core impasse
The parties disagree on whether the restructuring problem can be solved **without permanent site closure or closure-equivalent capacity removal**.

- **Labour / state / works council**: insist that German sites, co-determination, and job security must be preserved, and that closures or disguised closures are unacceptable.
- **Management / investors / controlling family**: accept that non-closure measures may help, but only if they produce **deep, durable, near-equivalent savings**; otherwise they view closures as necessary.
- **Expert**: confirms that a no-closure deal can work only if it removes real fixed cost and installed capacity, not just wages temporarily.

So the unresolved issue is not “whether restructuring is needed,” but **what level of permanent capacity reduction is acceptable, and who bears what burden to achieve it**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three concrete bridging positions framed as procedural options for further negotiation.

### Proposal 1: “No formal closure, but guaranteed capacity reduction”
A package could prohibit plant closures while still allowing:
- line shutdowns,
- shift reductions,
- product reallocation,
- and organized workforce reductions through voluntary exits and redeployment.

**Safeguards**
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job/security rules for a defined term,
- transparent reporting on whether capacity reduction is actually being achieved.

**Purpose**
This tries to separate the question of **site identity** from the question of **capacity and cost reduction**.

---

### Proposal 2: “Time-limited protection with automatic reopener only if jointly triggered”
A restructuring agreement could run for a fixed period, during which:
- closures and compulsory redundancies are barred,
- labour contributes through temporary measures,
- management and owners contribute through overhead, procurement, and capital restraint.

A reopener would be permitted only if **pre-defined, jointly verified financial thresholds** are missed.

**Safeguards**
- independent monitoring,
- jointly agreed trigger metrics,
- no unilateral reopening by management.

**Purpose**
This addresses management’s demand for enforceability while reducing the risk of a one-sided closure threat.

---

### Proposal 3: “Site guarantee plus negotiated work transfer framework”
Sites remain formally open, but the parties agree in advance on:
- which products stay,
- which volumes can move,
- which investments are committed,
- and what minimum employment/production level must remain at each site.

**Safeguards**
- prohibition on hollowing out sites below an agreed threshold,
- joint approval for major reallocations,
- co-determination embedded in implementation.

**Purpose**
This creates a middle path between “all sites frozen” and “closure by stealth.”

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Can you specify the minimum non-closure package that you would accept as economically sufficient, including the exact savings threshold and the capacity reduction required?**

### To the works council
**What level of permanent capacity reduction, if any, could you accept without considering it a disguised closure, provided that sites remain open and no compulsory redundancies are used?**

### To the union
**Would you accept a time-limited labour contribution if it were paired with binding site guarantees, transparent savings reporting, and prohibition of closures during the term?**

### To the state
**Are you prepared to support a restructuring package that preserves sites but still includes significant workforce and capacity adjustment, if it is jointly governed and does not involve formal closures?**

### To the controlling family
**Would you back a no-closure package if independent verification showed it delivers only slightly less savings than closures but materially lowers strike and political risk?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of residual gap through 2030 would still be acceptable to you if a non-closure package demonstrably reduces execution and conflict risk?**
