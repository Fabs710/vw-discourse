As Works Council, I will be very clear:

We reject any plan that includes plant closures in Germany or compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines. We also reject any “restructuring” that simply transfers the burden onto the workforce while management preserves all strategic freedom.

Our priorities are:

1. **Every German plant must remain open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A real co-determination role for labour in shaping the restructuring and investment plan.**
5. **A fair and reversible contribution from all sides, including management and shareholders, not just workers.**

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect jobs and sites:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exit options only,
- productivity and process improvements,
- targeted cost measures negotiated with the workforce,
- and a credible product and investment commitment for German sites.

But let me be equally clear: if management comes with closures, outsourcing, or mass job cuts as a fait accompli, we will resist it with full force. We have both the legitimacy and the leverage to do that, including industrial action and use of our rights in the supervisory board.

This company’s future cannot be built by sacrificing the workforce and breaking historic guarantees. If there is to be restructuring, it must be **socially responsible, negotiated, and secure for the German plants and jobs**.
