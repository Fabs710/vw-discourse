## Core impasse
The stakeholders are not only disputing **how much cost must come out**. They are disputing **what counts as a legitimate restructuring instrument**.

- **Management, family owners, and investors** want a package that is economically near-equivalent to closures and do not want to rule out plant closures or structural headcount reduction.
- **Works council and union** reject closures, compulsory redundancies, and any outcome that looks like phased hollowing-out of German sites.
- **State actors** want to preserve sites and jobs, but also need a package that is financially credible.
- The **analyst** has clarified that a no-closure package can help materially, but usually only matches closure economics if it includes deep and durable capacity reduction.

So the deadlock is not “numbers vs no numbers.”  
It is **site preservation and employment security vs economically equivalent structural adjustment**.

## Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete compromise directions that could bridge the main conflicts.

### 1) “No-closure first, review-triggered fallback” package
**Structure:**
- Immediate no-closure agreement for a defined period.
- Includes working-time reductions, voluntary exits, redeployment, wage moderation, management restraint, procurement/overhead cuts, and investment commitments.
- Contains a **pre-agreed review trigger**: if defined savings milestones are missed by specific dates, parties reopen talks on additional measures.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Gives labour a real chance to keep plants open.
- Gives management/investors measurable safeguards if savings fail to materialize.
- Gives the state a politically defensible path to preserve sites while retaining discipline.

### 2) “Site guarantee plus network adjustment” package
**Structure:**
- Formal guarantee that **German sites remain open**, paired with **capacity reduction elsewhere in the network** through process consolidation, product reallocation, shorter shifts, or gradual line resizing.
- No compulsory redundancies; only voluntary exits and redeployment.
- Binding productivity, cost, and investment commitments.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Preserves the symbolic and social commitment to plant continuity.
- Still allows management and investors to obtain real structural savings.
- May be more acceptable than direct closures if some capacity is removed without site shutdown.

### 3) “Time-limited burden-sharing with hard governance controls”
**Structure:**
- Employees accept a **temporary, quantified contribution**.
- Management and owners accept **matching restraint** and binding investment obligations.
- In exchange, there are stronger governance controls: joint monitoring, transparency on savings, and co-determination over implementation.
- The package runs to a fixed date, after which terms automatically expire unless renewed.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Creates reciprocity and avoids one-sided burden transfer.
- Addresses the concern that workers sacrifice while owners keep all strategic options.
- Gives capital markets and management a defined time path to assess whether competitiveness improves.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If a no-closure package were to deliver quantified, binding savings with joint monitoring, what specific additional safeguards would be sufficient for you to accept a time-limited employee contribution without plant closures?

### To the Union
What is the maximum employee contribution in wages, working time, and duration that you would consider acceptable if it were matched by binding management and ownership contributions?

### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap remains after the currently proposed non-closure measures, and which parts of that gap are non-negotiable versus which can be covered by labor, overhead, or management restraint?

### To the Regional State Government
What governance mechanism would you be willing to back so that site protection is not only politically stated but also enforceable if savings targets are missed?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a no-closure package if it produced verified medium-term savings that are slightly below closure economics but materially reduced strike risk, political damage, and long-term value destruction?

### To Institutional Investors
What minimum level of recurring savings and implementation certainty would make you support a negotiated no-closure solution instead of insisting on closure-equivalent economics?

### To the Expert Analyst
What combination of measures would, in your view, move a non-closure package from “helpful but insufficient” to “economically credible,” and what timeline would be required for that to be believable?
