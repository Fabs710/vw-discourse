### 1) What has been agreed
- The company faces a **serious structural competitiveness and cost problem**.
- **Temporary fixes alone are insufficient**; a durable restructuring is needed.
- A **prolonged strike would destroy value** and is undesirable for all sides.
- There is openness, in principle, to a package involving:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/reskilling,
  - productivity and process improvements,
  - and some form of time-limited employee contribution.
- All sides accept that any solution must be **quantified, enforceable, and more than slogans**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Labour-side stakeholders and the state strongly resist closures as a first resort.  
  - Management, ownership, and investors keep closures on the table unless a near-equivalent alternative is proven.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour rejects them; capital-side actors say they cannot be ruled out absolutely if the economics require permanent adjustment.
- **Guarantees and flexibility:**  
  - Works council wants binding site/job guarantees and no erosion of co-determination.  
  - Management/ownership/investors argue that rigid guarantees could block necessary adaptation.
- **Depth of employee contribution:**  
  - Labour wants limited, reversible, and matched contributions.  
  - Management/ownership/investors want a contribution large enough to materially change the economics.
- **Whether a no-closure package can be “equivalent” to closure economics:**  
  - Labour and the state say this remains unproven.  
  - Management/ownership/investors say it must be proven if closures are to be avoided.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required in total, and from which sources**:
  - labour,
  - management overhead,
  - procurement,
  - capital allocation,
  - and capacity reduction.
- **Whether non-closure measures can actually close the structural gap** or only delay it.
- **How to balance job/site security against strategic flexibility** if market conditions worsen.
- **Whether “no closures” can be maintained without creating a disguised closure through deep capacity cuts elsewhere**.
- **How to distribute burden fairly** without making the package so weak that it fails financially, or so harsh that it becomes politically/socially unworkable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to **side-by-side numbers**. A useful next step would be:
- one quantified scenario with **closures**,
- one quantified scenario with **no closures but deep alternatives**,
- including:
  - savings by category,
  - implementation timing,
  - transition costs,
  - enforceability,
  - and remaining risk.

The discussion should then focus on **which package closes the structural gap with the least overall damage**, rather than restating red lines.
