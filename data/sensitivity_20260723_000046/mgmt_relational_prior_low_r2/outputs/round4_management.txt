I will be equally direct.

## To the Works Council
You keep repeating “all German plants remain open” as if stating it often enough makes the economics disappear. It does not.

I agree with you on one point: this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. A weak, temporary compromise would be unacceptable. But I **do not** accept your claim that closures are merely a management preference. They are on the table because the company has a structural cost problem and a structural capacity problem. If you want to rule them out, you need to produce a package that is economically durable, not politically comforting.

You still have not done that.

You keep asking for the full breakdown. Fine — and I repeat what I have said from the start: the burden is not only on management to show the gap; it is also on labour to show a credible way to close it. Temporary working-time cuts, voluntary exits, productivity promises, and general appeals to fairness do **not** yet solve the problem.

And I will say this bluntly: a no-closure package that merely postpones the day of reckoning while leaving the cost base uncompetitive is not a real alternative. It is delay.

## To the Union
I appreciate that you at least acknowledge the need for real restructuring and time-limited employee contribution. That is more realistic than blanket denial.

But I do **not** accept your insistence that closures can only be discussed after all other options are exhausted in some abstract sense. We are already in a crisis. We do not have the luxury of endless rounds of partial measures that sound fair but fail economically.

You are correct that labour should not carry the full burden. I agree. But you are wrong if you think that means the company can recover competitiveness through limited wage moderation, working-time adjustments, and voluntary exits alone.

That is simply not enough to restore margin at the required pace.

And let me be very clear: if you insist on “all plants open,” “no compulsory redundancies,” and “no closure logic of any kind,” then you are not offering a restructuring. You are offering a constraint on restructuring.

## To the State
You are right to warn against a disorderly conflict. I do not want that either. But I do **not** accept the idea that political protection of sites can substitute for a viable business model.

You say closures are not automatically justified. Correct — but they are not automatically excluded either. The business case matters.

If you want to help preserve German industry, then support a package that actually changes the economics. Do not just block the instruments and hope margins recover by force of goodwill.

## To the Controlling Family and Investors
I agree with you more than I agree with the labour side on the core issue: this cannot be a cosmetic compromise.

You are right that the company needs a durable and measurable improvement in costs and capacity. You are also right that rigid guarantees and symbolic deals would simply push the problem into the future.

But I would caution you against treating every non-closure route as inherently superior only if it mimics closure exactly. The right standard is not “what sounds nicer politically,” but “what restores competitiveness with the least overall damage.”

That said, if the no-closure package does not materially close the gap, then I will not support it. Full stop.

## On the analyst’s assessment
The analyst has actually clarified the issue rather well: non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings, but unless they include deep, durable capacity reduction, they usually fall short of closure-level economics.

That is precisely why I am not moving away from structural measures.

The expert did **not** say: “therefore closures are wrong.”  
The expert said: if you want to avoid closures, you need something economically comparable in effect.

I agree. And so far, I have not heard such a package from labour.

## What would move me
Let me be absolutely clear about the conditions under which I would reconsider the closure route:

I would need to see a package that is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- enforceable,
- fast enough to affect 2030 margins,
- and large enough to close the structural cost gap.

That means:
1. real labour-cost contribution,
2. meaningful capacity reduction,
3. lower structural overhead,
4. measurable productivity gains,
5. and a binding path back to the return-on-sales target.

If that package exists, I will examine it seriously. If it does not, then closures remain a necessary option.

## What I reject
I reject all of the following:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a “peace” package that leaves the cost base intact,
- a solution that preserves every legacy structure and calls that competitiveness,
- and any attempt to make management permanently absorb overcapacity.

I also reject the idea that the workforce can veto all structural change simply by declaring it unacceptable. That is not co-determination. That is deadlock.

## The hard truth
The company does not have unlimited time. The European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and the current German cost base is too high. Every month of delay burns value.

So let me say this plainly: I am not here to preserve the footprint for sentiment’s sake. I am here to restore competitiveness. If that requires closures or economically equivalent capacity removal, then that is what we must pursue.

## My position after round 4
- I am open to a serious negotiated package.
- I am **not** open to a settlement that rules out closures and forced structural adjustment in advance.
- I am **not** open to a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.
- I am open to non-closure measures only if they are deep enough to be economically near-equivalent.

## Next step
If labour wants to keep closures off the table, then bring the numbers:
- annual recurring savings,
- timing,
- transition costs,
- residual gap,
- and enforceable delivery.

If you cannot do that, then the default assumption remains: we need deeper structural measures, including closures if necessary.

That is my position.
