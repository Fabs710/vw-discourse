I will respond directly, because the discussion has now moved beyond generalities.

## My position
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I **do not support plant closures in Germany as the default solution**. I also **do not accept a restructuring that shifts the burden overwhelmingly onto the workforce while weakening the company’s role as a regional economic anchor**.

At the same time, I am not pretending the cost problem does not exist. The company clearly needs a **durable, credible restructuring**. But that restructuring must protect the industrial base in this state and preserve the company’s long-term viability and governance balance.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and investors on one point: **temporary fixes alone are not enough**. If the company keeps its current cost base unchanged, it will not regain competitiveness.

I also agree with the works council and the union that:
- the burden cannot fall on workers alone,
- a prolonged strike would damage everyone,
- and any package must be negotiated and enforceable.

So yes: we need a real package, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
### 1) On plant closures
I do **not** accept closures as the starting point.  
A plant closure in Germany is not just a technical cost measure; it has major consequences for:
- jobs,
- regional supply chains,
- the company’s social legitimacy,
- and the political viability of the whole restructuring.

If management wants me to consider any closure at all, it must first demonstrate, in hard numbers, that **all credible alternatives have failed**. At present, that has not been established.

### 2) On “no flexibility” from labour
I understand the works council’s red lines, but I cannot support a position that treats the current footprint as untouchable regardless of economics. That is not credible.

What I can support is:
- no forced redundancies as a first resort,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time measures,
- qualification and reskilling,
- and time-limited employee contributions.

But those must be part of a real restructuring, not a way to postpone decisions indefinitely.

### 3) On rigid guarantees
I am also not prepared to endorse a blanket guarantee through 2030 if that guarantee removes all ability to adapt. The company needs room to respond to market and technology developments.

That said, I do believe strong site and employment protections are legitimate and necessary. The right approach is **binding protection where feasible, conditional review where necessary, and negotiated adjustment instead of unilateral closure threats**.

## What I want to see
If closures are to stay off the table, then the alternative package must be **quantified and enforceable**. That means at minimum:

1. **A clear cost gap to be closed**
2. **A quantified labour contribution**
3. **Meaningful management restraint at the top**
4. **Concrete productivity and process savings**
5. **A binding investment and product roadmap for German sites**
6. **A governance arrangement that respects co-determination and the state’s veto role**

If management cannot show that such a package closes the structural gap, then it is not serious to demand that labour alone absorb the adjustment.

## My challenge to management
You say closures remain on the table unless an equivalent alternative is found. Fine. Then prove that closures are truly necessary.

Bring forward:
- the exact savings target,
- the exact shortfall after non-closure measures,
- and the exact reason why that shortfall cannot be closed by a shared package.

Until then, closures are a threat, not a justified conclusion.

## My challenge to labour
To the works council and union: I support your insistence that workers must not pay for management’s mistakes alone. But if you want my full backing against closures, then you must also accept that some contribution from the workforce will likely be necessary.

That contribution can be:
- temporary and reversible,
- negotiated,
- and matched by visible contributions from management and ownership.

But it cannot be zero if the company is to regain competitiveness.

## My challenge to ownership and investors
To the family and institutional investors: I hear the demand for hard numbers. That is fair. But you also need to understand that **a disorderly or overly aggressive restructuring would destroy value faster than a negotiated one**.

Regional stability, labour peace, and credible co-determination are not obstacles to value creation. They are conditions for it.

## Bottom line
My stance is:
- **No automatic plant closures**
- **No forced dismantling of the regional industrial base**
- **Yes to a serious negotiated restructuring**
- **Yes to fair burden sharing**
- **Yes to preserving jobs and sites wherever economically possible**
- **No to a solution that weakens the state’s legitimate co-determination and veto role**

If management wants support for structural change, it must bring a package that protects the company’s future **without sacrificing its role in this region**. If it cannot, then I will not help legitimize closures as a first resort.
