As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, I am not prepared to sign up to a restructuring that solves the company’s cost problem by damaging the regional economic base that has carried this group for decades.

Let me be clear where I agree:
- The company does need a credible restructuring.
- We cannot pretend that all current structures are sustainable.
- Productivity, flexibility, and a serious contribution from all sides will be necessary.
- A negotiated solution is better than an escalating conflict.

Where I disagree is just as important:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable starting point.**
- **Compulsory redundancies at scale are not an acceptable answer.**
- **A solution that strips out jobs first and asks questions later is not socially or politically responsible.**
- **Any restructuring that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role is unacceptable.**

The management side is right that temporary measures alone will not be enough. But that does **not** mean the only durable answer is to close sites and cut deeply into employment. There are other ways to achieve structural savings:
- capacity adjustments across the network,
- internal reallocations and qualification,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary exits,
- process and procurement savings,
- and a real commitment to future products and investment in German sites.

What I would consider acceptable is a **negotiated restructuring package** that:
1. Preserves the key German sites and avoids closures,
2. Avoids forced layoffs wherever possible,
3. Includes a fair contribution from labour, management, and owners,
4. Secures binding investment and product commitments for German plants,
5. Protects the company’s long-term competitiveness without breaking social partnership.

To management: if you come to the table treating closures as the only serious option, you are underestimating the governance reality here. You do not have a free hand. There is a blocking minority, there are co-determination rights, and there is a legitimate public interest in keeping this company anchored in the region.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that the workforce must not carry the burden alone. But I also want to be honest that we need a package that is economically credible, not just politically satisfying. If we want to avoid closures, then the alternatives must deliver genuine and durable savings, not just postpone the problem.

So my position is:
- **No blank cheque for closures.**
- **No unilateral burden shift onto workers.**
- **Yes to a serious negotiated restructuring that protects jobs, sites, and the regional industrial base.**

If management wants my support, it needs to bring forward a plan that proves it can restore competitiveness **without sacrificing the company’s role as a regional economic anchor**.
