I will respond directly.

### Where I agree with the Works Council
I agree that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the wrong starting point** and, in fact, remain unacceptable unless every credible alternative has been exhausted. I also agree that the burden cannot fall on the workforce alone. If the company wants labour to contribute, then management, owners, and—where relevant—the state side must also contribute visibly and measurably.

I also agree that a restructuring cannot be built on vague promises. We need a package that is **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.

### Where I disagree or need to qualify
I do **not** accept that “all German plants remain open” can be treated as an absolute regardless of what the numbers say. What I can accept is this:

- no closures as long as there is a credible alternative package,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and capacity adjustments handled through negotiated, reversible instruments.

But I will not sign up to a formulation that gives management nothing but a symbolically softened version of closures while leaving the real burden on workers through wage cuts, frozen conditions, and indefinite insecurity.

### My minimum conditions for agreement
These remain unchanged:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures as part of the agreed package**
3. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
4. **Binding job and site security**
5. **A fair contribution from management and owners**
6. **A time-limited, reversible employee contribution, not a permanent wage reset**

### What I am prepared to discuss
I remain open to a serious burden-sharing package, but only if it is structured fairly. That could include:

- temporary working-time reductions,
- moderated wage developments for a limited period,
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity and process improvements,
- and a solidarity contribution from employees **matched** by restraint and contribution at the top.

But let me be explicit: an “employee contribution” is only acceptable if it is not simply code for wage suppression plus future flexibility for management.

### On management’s challenge
Management has asked for numbers and credible alternatives to closures. Fair enough. Then here is the standard I expect:

- show the exact cost gap,
- specify how much can be closed through executive restraint, procurement, overhead, and investment discipline,
- quantify the savings from working-time and productivity measures,
- and only then compare that package against any closure scenario.

If management cannot prove that closures are unavoidable, then it has no legitimate basis to demand them.

### On investors and owners
To the family owners and institutional investors: I hear the call for hard numbers and durable savings. But you do not get durability by starting a labour conflict that destroys trust and provokes strikes. If you want value preservation, then support a negotiated deal that keeps the company socially stable. A strike-heavy route is not a sign of strength; it is evidence that management has failed to secure legitimacy.

### My challenge to management
If you want labour peace, bring forward a package with these elements:

- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- a limited employee contribution,
- a meaningful management/top-level contribution,
- and a clear investment and product roadmap for German sites.

Without that, you are asking the workforce to pay for a restructuring they did not design and cannot control.

### Bottom line
We are still prepared to negotiate, but only on the basis of **shared sacrifice without broken promises**.  
What we will **not** accept is:
- closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or a permanent erosion of the collective wage framework.

If management refuses to move into that space, then it should expect resistance rather than accommodation.
