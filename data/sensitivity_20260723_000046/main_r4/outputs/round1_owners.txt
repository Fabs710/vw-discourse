I will be equally direct from the perspective of the controlling shareholders.

We are not interested in a symbolic restructuring that preserves headline comfort while the enterprise keeps eroding underneath. The family holding’s responsibility is to protect the long-term value of the company, and that means the business must become competitive again. On that point, I align with management: the current cost and capacity structure cannot simply be carried forward.

That said, I do **not** support a solution that destroys industrial peace, permanently damages the company’s reputation, or creates a governance crisis. A value-destructive confrontation would be a failure of stewardship, not strength.

### Where I agree with management
- The company needs **durable structural savings**, not temporary patches.
- The competitiveness gap is real.
- If the cost base stays where it is, the company will continue to underperform, and that ultimately harms everyone, including employees and the region.

### Where I disagree with management
- I will not endorse a strategy that treats **plant closures as the default answer** unless there is no credible alternative that achieves the same permanent effect.
- I do not want a restructuring that creates a prolonged public conflict, undermines trust in governance, or weakens the company’s license to operate in Germany.
- If the proposed path cannot pass the supervisory board realities and the co-determination constraints, then it is not a serious plan; it is a confrontation.

### Where I agree with labor and the state
- Workers should not be asked to absorb the entire burden.
- A settlement should include **shared sacrifice**, not one-sided demands.
- Binding commitments on investment, future products, and site security matter if employees are being asked to contribute.
- Capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and negotiated employee contributions are legitimate tools.

### Where I disagree with labor and the state
- A blanket refusal of **any** plant closure, no matter the economics, is not a credible long-term position.
- “No compulsory redundancies” may be acceptable only if there is a serious, enforceable package that delivers equivalent structural savings.
- Site guarantees through 2030 are not acceptable if they freeze inefficiency and make the company uninvestable.

### The family holding’s bottom line
We support a restructuring that:
- restores margins and competitiveness,
- preserves the core industrial base where possible,
- avoids unnecessary instability,
- and can actually be executed within the governance structure of this company.

We will **not** support:
- permanent impairment of enterprise value,
- a deal that merely postpones the problem,
- or any outcome that compromises ownership control or the company’s ability to make necessary strategic decisions.

### What would be required for support
If labor and management want our backing, the package must show:
1. **Real structural savings** at scale.
2. **A credible reduction in overcapacity**.
3. **A durable labour contribution** that improves competitiveness.
4. **A stable industrial peace agreement**, not just a short pause.
5. **A future investment and product commitment** that justifies the concessions.

If that can be achieved without closures, I am open to it. If it cannot, then I will not pretend otherwise: the company must be allowed to take stronger measures.

So my position is not “closures at any cost,” and it is not “status quo at any cost.” It is this: **the company must emerge stronger, more competitive, and governable.** Any proposal that cannot deliver that will not have our support.
