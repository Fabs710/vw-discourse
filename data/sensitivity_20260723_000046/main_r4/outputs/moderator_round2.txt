## Moderator Summary

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge that the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a symbolic or temporary fix.
- There is broad agreement that the current cost structure is **not competitive enough** and that **recurring savings** are required.
- There is shared openness to discussing:
  - capacity adjustment,
  - productivity improvements,
  - working-time changes,
  - redeployment/retraining,
  - voluntary exits,
  - and a **temporary employee contribution**, if tied to conditions.
- There is also agreement that any solution must be **operationally executable** and not simply politically desirable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Labor and the state reject closures as a solution.
  - Management, investors, and the family holding say closures may be necessary unless an alternative can deliver equivalent savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labor and the state oppose them.
  - Management and investors do not rule out stronger measures if alternatives fail.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030**
  - Labor and the state want binding guarantees.
  - Management and investors view such guarantees as potentially incompatible with the required restructuring flexibility.
- **Burden sharing**
  - Labor insists workers cannot carry the restructuring alone.
  - Management and investors want a larger and more durable labor-cost contribution.
- **What counts as an equivalent alternative**
  - The main dispute is whether non-closure measures can generate savings equal to a closure-based restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Social protection vs. financial effectiveness**
  - Preserving sites and jobs may reduce conflict, but may not deliver enough structural savings.
- **Speed vs. negotiability**
  - Management wants swift action; labor and the state want negotiated protections and guarantees.
- **Durable savings vs. implementation risk**
  - A more aggressive restructuring may save more, but could be harder to implement under the current governance setup.
- **Trust and industrial peace vs. cost reduction**
  - Labor argues closures would damage trust and future cooperation.
  - Management and investors argue delay or half-measures would prolong uncompetitiveness.
- **No-closure scenario not yet quantified**
  - No one has yet shown, with numbers, whether a no-closure package can match the savings of structural consolidation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principles to **site-by-site numbers**.
- Require a comparative model covering:
  - current capacity and utilization by site,
  - recurring savings by scenario,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - employment impact,
  - and margin effect through 2030.
- Test at least three scenarios:
  1. no closures / no compulsory redundancies,
  2. selective consolidation,
  3. hybrid negotiated package.
- Use the next round to identify:
  - which sites are structurally underutilized,
  - what savings can be achieved without closures,
  - and what guarantees or contributions would be needed for any deal to be acceptable.
