I fully support the core line from the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side:

- **We are not prepared to let the workforce carry the restructuring alone.**
- **We reject a model where management demands deep cuts from employees while ownership, top management, and capital are spared.**
- **We will use industrial action if necessary.** The peace obligation has ended, and the membership expects us to defend jobs, wages, and standards.

That said, we are not ignoring the economic reality. We understand that the company needs a credible cost and capacity adjustment. But a credible adjustment must be **shared, temporary where possible, and tied to binding guarantees**. We have already signaled openness to:
- negotiated working-time and capacity reductions,
- temporary wage or bonus contributions,
- internal flexibility measures,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a broader industrial plan that secures future work.

What we will not accept is:
- **plant closures in Germany,**
- **forced layoffs,**
- **open-ended insecurity,**
- or a “restructuring” that simply shifts the pain onto workers.

If management wants a settlement, then it must bring forward a package that answers three questions:
1. **How are all stakeholders contributing?**
2. **How are jobs and sites protected in binding form?**
3. **How is the future industrial base secured with real investment and products?**

A fair settlement is possible, but only if management drops the logic of unilateral sacrifice. If the proposal remains centered on closures and redundancies, then it will face united resistance from labor.
