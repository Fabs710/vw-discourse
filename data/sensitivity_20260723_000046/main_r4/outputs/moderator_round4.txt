## 1) What has been agreed
- All parties now accept that the restructuring question must be examined **site by site** with a transparent model.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs **durable recurring savings**, not cosmetic measures.
- There is also broad agreement that the model should test at least three scenarios:
  1. **no closures / no compulsory redundancies**
  2. **selective consolidation**
  3. **hybrid restructuring**
- Most parties agree that the model should include:
  - utilization,
  - overhead and fixed costs,
  - recurring savings,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - employment impact,
  - and the residual gap to target.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Labor-side actors and the state reject closures as a restructuring instrument; management, controlling family, and institutional investors keep closures open as an option if the gap cannot otherwise be closed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor-side actors and the state reject them; management and capital-side actors do not rule them out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Guarantees and job security:**  
  Labor and the state want binding site and employment guarantees; management and investors view long guarantees as potentially incompatible with restructuring flexibility.
- **Burden of proof:**  
  Labor and the state say management must prove that internal cuts and non-closure measures are exhausted; management and investors say labor must prove a no-closure package can close the savings gap.
- **Role of the state / co-determination:**  
  The state wants industrial stability and open sites; capital-side actors accept governance constraints but not at the expense of competitiveness.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **size of the residual savings gap** after all non-closure measures.
- How much savings can realistically come from:
  - overhead reduction,
  - management-layer cuts,
  - productivity gains,
  - shift redesign,
  - and volume reallocation.
- Whether preserving all German sites would leave too much **fixed-cost burden** or low utilization.
- The balance between:
  - **social stability and job security**, and
  - **execution certainty and margin recovery**.
- The scope and duration of any **temporary labor contribution**, and what binding commitments would accompany it.
- Whether a negotiated no-closure package can be both **economically sufficient** and **governable** under the existing ownership/statutory structure.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Keep the discussion tightly centered on the **independent site-by-site model**.
- Require clear, comparable data for each site on:
  - utilization,
  - fixed and variable costs,
  - recurring savings by measure,
  - one-time costs,
  - employment impact,
  - and residual gap under each scenario.
- Separate **verified numbers** from **negotiating positions**.
- Use the model to answer one question first:  
  **Can non-closure measures close the full savings gap, or is selective consolidation still required?**
