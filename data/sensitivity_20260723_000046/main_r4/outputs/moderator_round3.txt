### Moderator Summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs **durable recurring savings** and a **credible restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that the discussion should now be based on an **independent site-by-site model**.
- Most participants accept that savings can come from a mix of:
  - overhead reduction,
  - shift and capacity changes,
  - volume/model reallocation,
  - voluntary exits,
  - productivity measures,
  - and, in some views, temporary labor contributions.
- There is also general agreement that a **purely cosmetic settlement** would not be acceptable.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works council, union, and state reject closures as the default; management, investors, and the controlling family keep them on the table if alternatives do not close the gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor representatives and the state reject them; management and shareholders will not rule them out if required for competitiveness.
- **Guarantees through 2030:**  
  Labor and the state want binding site and employment guarantees; management, investors, and the family consider such guarantees too restrictive unless matched by equivalent savings and flexibility.
- **Feasibility of no-closure restructuring:**  
  Labor and the state believe it is still viable; management and capital-side participants say it must be proven in numbers and may be insufficient if the gap is large.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  Labor wants management and ownership to contribute meaningfully; management and shareholders want labor to show that its package is economically equivalent.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- How much of the savings gap can realistically be closed **without** closures or forced layoffs.
- Whether overhead cuts and capacity/utilization changes are sufficient to offset the fixed-cost burden of keeping all sites open.
- How to balance:
  - employment security,
  - investment capacity,
  - competitiveness,
  - and execution certainty.
- What level of **temporary labor contribution** is acceptable, and what binding safeguards would accompany it.
- Whether preserving all sites is compatible with the required margin improvement by 2030.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Focus the discussion on the **independent site-by-site model**.
- Require it to compare, for each German site:
  - current utilization,
  - recurring savings under non-closure measures,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - employment impact,
  - and residual gap to target.
- Keep the next round narrowly centered on one question:  
  **Can a no-closure package deliver equivalent recurring savings, or does the remaining gap require selective consolidation or stronger measures?**
