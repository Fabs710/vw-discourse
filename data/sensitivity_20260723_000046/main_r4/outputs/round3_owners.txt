## My reading of the round so far

### Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the expert on the **diagnosis**:
- the current German cost base is too high,
- the company needs **durable recurring savings**,
- and a cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.

I also agree with the state and labor on one point:
- a prolonged open conflict would destroy value and damage the company’s industrial position.

So the problem is not whether restructuring is needed. It is how much can be done **without** damaging governability, margins, and the company’s long-term value.

## Where I disagree

### With the Works Council and Union
I do **not** accept your position that:
- every German plant must remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies can ever be considered,
- and binding guarantees through 2030 are a minimum floor.

That is not a restructuring. That is an attempt to freeze the structure while the market keeps eroding. If that were adopted, the company would simply preserve the problem and weaken the core asset over time.

I also reject the idea that management or shareholders must somehow “prove” closures before the workforce engages seriously on the economics. The burden is not one-sided. If closures are to be avoided, labor must help produce a package that is **economically equivalent in recurring effect**. That has still not been shown.

### With the State
I respect the state’s role and your concern for regional stability. But I do not accept the implied veto over any meaningful footprint adjustment. The company’s long-term viability is part of regional stability. Protecting every existing site, regardless of economics, is not sustainable industrial policy.

### With management
I agree with your hard-nosed insistence on structural savings. But I caution against treating closures as the default public posture. In this governance setting, that approach risks triggering exactly the value-destructive instability we all want to avoid.

You need to stay focused on **execution certainty**, not symbolic toughness.

## My position as controlling shareholder
I want a package that:
1. restores competitiveness,
2. cuts structural overcapacity materially,
3. improves margins durably,
4. preserves labour peace if possible,
5. and does not permanently impair enterprise value or governance control.

That means I am **open to a non-closure solution only if it is truly equivalent** in hard numbers. Not rhetorically equivalent. Not politically convenient. Economically equivalent.

At present, I have not seen that proof.

## What I support next
The expert has already pointed to the right next step: an independent **site-by-site model**.

That model must show, for each German site:
- utilization,
- fixed and variable cost,
- overhead burden,
- recurring savings under non-closure measures,
- the residual gap to target,
- and the employment impact.

It should compare:
1. no closures / no compulsory redundancies,
2. selective consolidation,
3. a hybrid negotiated package.

[SUMMON: independent site-by-site restructuring and financial model to quantify recurring savings, capacity reduction, and margin impact across all German plants under no-closure, selective consolidation, and hybrid scenarios]

## My bottom line
I am **not** backing a deal that simply preserves every site and calls it a compromise.

I am also **not** backing a reckless showdown that destroys value and industrial peace.

I will back the package that can actually be executed and that restores the company’s competitiveness. If that turns out to be a non-closure solution, it must prove itself in the numbers. Until then, closures and stronger measures remain on the table.
