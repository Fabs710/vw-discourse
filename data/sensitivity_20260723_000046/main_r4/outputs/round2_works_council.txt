Let me respond clearly to each side.

## To management
You say “all options remain on the table,” including closures and redundancies, because the numbers require a structural correction. I do **not** accept that framing as a starting point. It is exactly the kind of unilateral pressure that triggered this confrontation in the first place.

Yes, we acknowledge the need for a durable industrial plan. Yes, we know the market is difficult. But **closing German plants and forcing layoffs are not the only routes to structural savings**, and you have not yet demonstrated that they are unavoidable. Saying “the cost base is too high” is not the same as proving that jobs and sites must be destroyed.

If you want a serious response from labor, then bring concrete alternatives with numbers:
- capacity reductions through internal redistribution,
- shorter working time,
- shift and utilization adjustments,
- qualification and redeployment,
- temporary, negotiated employee contributions,
- and management-side concessions as well.

What you cannot do is demand that the workforce accept closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of your strategic errors.

## To the family holding and investors
You both talk about “durable savings,” “governability,” and “enterprise value.” I understand those concerns. But I reject the idea that the only credible way to protect value is to threaten German plants and jobs.

A settlement that tears up trust, breaks the job-security framework, and triggers industrial conflict is not value-preserving — it is value-destructive. And once you destroy trust, you don’t get it back with rhetoric about competitiveness.

If you want the company stronger, then **you must accept that competitiveness cannot be built by sacrificing the workforce first**. Workers are not the buffer for every management mistake and every market cycle. That logic is exactly what we oppose.

## To the state
I appreciate your support for regional stability and co-determination. On that, we are aligned. But I want to be equally clear: “no closures as a first resort” is not enough for us. It is not a compromise point; it is the minimum floor.

The state should use its influence to insist on:
- preservation of all German sites,
- no forced redundancies,
- binding employment guarantees through 2030,
- investment commitments,
- and a real industrial plan, not a threat-based restructuring.

Anything less leaves workers exposed.

## On the package design
You were asked for specifics, so here is our line:

### 1) Capacity and savings
We are prepared to discuss **capacity adjustment**, but only through measures that do not close plants or destroy permanent jobs. That means:
- smoother volume reduction across sites,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary programs,
- internal transfers,
- retraining,
- and targeted efficiency measures.

What we will not accept is a package whose “savings” are based on shutting down sites or permanent workforce destruction.

### 2) Labor contribution
We have said this before: a **temporary employee contribution** can be discussed, but only if it is:
- time-limited,
- fair,
- shared across the company,
- and tied to binding guarantees on sites and employment.

It cannot become a permanent wage cut disguised as solidarity.

### 3) Guarantees
We want:
- **every German plant kept open,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **binding employment and site guarantees through 2030,**
- and protection of the co-determination framework.

These are not decorative wishes. They are the condition for any serious discussion.

### 4) Investment and future work
If management wants flexibility, then it must commit to:
- future product allocation,
- technology investment,
- training,
- and securing follow-on work for the German sites.

No one will vote for “restructuring” if the only visible outcome is shrinkage and insecurity.

## Bottom line
I am not shifting from my position.

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of job security.**
- **No acceptance of a fait accompli.**

If management cannot design a restructuring that meets those conditions, then it will face resistance — in the supervisory board, in bargaining, and in the plants. The workforce is already mobilized, and we are prepared to keep using that leverage.

So let’s be honest: the burden of proof is on management and the shareholders. Show us a credible plan that protects jobs and sites, or stop pretending that closures and layoffs are the only serious option.
