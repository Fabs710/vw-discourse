### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **credible, durable restructuring** rather than a short-term fix.
- There is broad agreement that **future investment, new products, and long-term industrial viability** are necessary.
- Several parties accept that **capacity adjustment, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and internal efficiency measures** are legitimate tools.
- There is also shared recognition that **a prolonged open conflict would be harmful** to the company, workforce, and investors.
- Most parties indicate openness to a **negotiated package** if it is economically credible and socially responsible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** labor, the state, and in part the family reject closures as a first resort; management and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** labor rejects them; management and investors do not rule them out if needed for structural savings.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030:** labor wants strong site and employment guarantees; management, family, and investors consider long guarantees problematic if they freeze uncompetitive structures.
- **Scale and nature of labor contribution:** labor is open to temporary contributions only with guarantees; management, family, and investors want a more substantial and durable labor-cost contribution.
- **Governance leverage:** labor and the state insist on co-determination and statutory governance constraints; management and shareholders want room to execute a decisive turnaround.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much restructuring can be achieved without closures or layoffs, and whether that is enough.**
- **What specific recurring savings are required, and who bears them.**
- **What binding commitments on investment, product allocation, and site security can be offered in return for workforce concessions.**
- **How to balance competitiveness with regional employment and industrial peace.**
- **Whether a settlement can be both politically acceptable and financially sufficient.**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **specific package design**:
- quantify the required savings and capacity reduction,
- identify which sites, functions, or lines are affected,
- specify what labor contributions are temporary vs. structural,
- define what guarantees or investment commitments are feasible,
- and test whether an alternative to closures and compulsory redundancies can still deliver the needed competitiveness gains.
