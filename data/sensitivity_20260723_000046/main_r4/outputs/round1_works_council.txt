As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the dismantling of the job-security framework that has protected the workforce for decades.

Management did not arrive at this point by surprise, and the workforce should not be made to pay the full price for strategic mistakes, underinvestment, and years of short-term pressure. If the company needs restructuring, then it must be done through alternatives that protect sites and jobs: capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, negotiated internal efficiency measures, retraining, and a fair, temporary employee contribution only if it is tied to binding guarantees.

Our priorities are straightforward:
- every German plant must stay open,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding site and employment guarantees through 2030,
- real co-determination in shaping any restructuring,
- and a credible industrial plan that secures future products, investment, and work for the long term.

We are prepared to discuss measures that are reversible, fair, and socially responsible. But if the proposal is based on closures, mass layoffs, or unilateral cost-cutting at the expense of workers, then we will oppose it with all the leverage co-determination gives us.

This is not a symbolic warning. The workforce is mobilized, and we will defend our plants and jobs. The company needs a solution that preserves its industrial base in Germany — not a restructuring that destroys it.
