I will respond plainly.

I agree on one point: this must be a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic patch. On everything else, I need to be equally clear: the package the employee side is demanding does **not** yet solve the competitiveness problem we are actually facing.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs investment and future product commitments,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and productivity gains should be part of the answer,
- and short-term conflict would be damaging for everyone.

I also agree that management, owners, and the workforce must all make contributions. That is not in dispute.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket ban on plant closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a guarantee to preserve every site and every job through 2030 regardless of the economics.

Those are not “starting points” if they eliminate the only tools capable of restoring the cost base. They are vetoes on restructuring.

### Why this matters
We are not dealing with a normal cycle. We are dealing with:
- structurally too high German factory costs,
- shrinking European demand,
- intense low-cost competition,
- and a brand that is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we keep every structure intact and only adjust at the margins, we will not reach competitiveness. We will simply defer the pain and weaken the business further.

## To the Works Council
You say there are other instruments besides closures. In principle, yes. But the question is not whether alternatives exist in theory; it is whether they are sufficient in practice.

If you want me to rule out closures, then you need to show me a package that delivers:
- a **material and durable** capacity reduction,
- a **real labour-cost contribution**,
- and a cost base that is competitive by 2030.

So far, what I hear is a list of protections, not a credible savings equivalent.

I am not prepared to sign up to:
- voluntary measures only,
- temporary restraint only,
- or internal redeployment only,
if those measures do not add up to the scale of savings required.

## To the Union
You speak about fair burden-sharing. I agree with the principle. But fair burden-sharing means real burden-sharing, not symbolic gestures.

If the workforce wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then the employee contribution must be substantial enough to replace those instruments economically. That means:
- meaningful wage restraint,
- meaningful flexibility,
- measurable productivity improvements,
- and structural changes that reduce fixed costs.

If the contribution is too small, then it is not a solution. It is a delay tactic.

## To the State
I respect the regional responsibility you carry, but I have to say this directly: blocking structural adjustment because it is politically difficult is not a sustainable strategy.

I understand the concern about jobs, regional suppliers, and industrial capacity. But preserving the appearance of stability while allowing the company to remain uncompetitive is not helping the region in the long run.

A restructuring that does not materially change the cost and capacity base is not one I can support.

## To the Family Holding and Investors
I agree with the demand for credibility and measurable savings. I also agree that market confidence cannot be built on vague promises.

But we will not restore investor confidence by pretending that the current footprint can simply be managed around. The economics must change.

## My position on the package
Here is where I stand:

### Acceptable
- negotiated productivity measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and retraining,
- voluntary exit instruments where they are sufficient,
- labor-cost contributions,
- product and investment commitments linked to restructuring,
- and capacity reductions that are real and enforceable.

### Not acceptable
- a deal that bans closures in advance,
- a deal that forbids compulsory redundancies under all circumstances,
- a deal that leaves the cost base structurally above target,
- or a deal that protects every site while asking management to pretend the numbers work.

## What I need to see
If the employee side wants to avoid deeper restructuring measures, then bring forward a **quantified alternative** that closes the gap. Not general principles. Not slogans. Numbers.

Specifically:
1. How much cost reduction is delivered?
2. How much capacity comes out?
3. By when do the savings arrive?
4. What is the binding employee contribution?
5. What is the enforceable investment and product commitment in return?

If that package is economically equivalent, I will consider it. If it is not, then it is not a viable alternative.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve the status quo under another name. I am here to restore competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity through 2030.

So let me be explicit:
- I welcome serious negotiation.
- I reject categorical taboos on restructuring tools.
- I reject a settlement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.
- And I will not endorse a package that substitutes political comfort for industrial reality.

Bring me a package with real, durable savings. If you cannot, then we have not solved the problem.
