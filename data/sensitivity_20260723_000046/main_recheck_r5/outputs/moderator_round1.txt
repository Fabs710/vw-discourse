### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties recognize that the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem**, not just a short-term downturn.
- There is broad support for a **durable solution**, not a cosmetic or purely temporary package.
- Most parties accept that any settlement should include some mix of:
  - productivity improvements,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - investment and product/technology commitments.
- There is also shared recognition that **prolonged conflict or brinkmanship would be harmful**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works Council, union, and state reject them.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say they cannot be ruled out in principle.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Works Council, union, and state reject them.  
  - Management does not fully exclude workforce reductions if needed.
- **Scope of restructuring**:  
  - Employee side and state want restructuring without damaging the German footprint.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors insist the cost base and capacity must be materially reduced.
- **Governance / veto power**:  
  - State and workforce emphasize co-determination and blocking rights.  
  - Capital and management actors warn against a settlement constrained primarily by political or governance limits.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus **how much site/job protection can be preserved**.
- Whether savings can be achieved mainly through **voluntary, negotiated measures** or whether **site closures / stronger capacity cuts** are economically necessary.
- What level of **employee sacrifice** is acceptable if management and owners also contribute visibly.
- How to convert broad references to **investment commitments** and **future product promises** into binding, measurable obligations.
- Whether a deal can simultaneously deliver:
  - competitiveness,
  - employment security,
  - regional stability,
  - and acceptable shareholder returns.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Please move from positions to **specific package elements**:
  1. required savings target,
  2. acceptable instruments,
  3. binding job/site protections,
  4. management/owner contributions,
  5. investment and product commitments,
  6. timeline and enforcement mechanism.
- The next round should test whether there is a **quantified compromise zone** where economic objectives and employment protections can coexist.
