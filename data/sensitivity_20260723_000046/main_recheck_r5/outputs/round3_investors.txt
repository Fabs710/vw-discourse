We appreciate the expert input because it sharpens the issue: the question is not whether restructuring is needed, but whether the package delivers **durable, quantified savings**.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **structural** competitiveness problem.
- A cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would also be value-destructive.
- The solution must be **measurable, enforceable, and fast enough** to restore margins.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your “no closures / no compulsory redundancies” line is understandable politically, but it is **not acceptable to us as an absolute taboo** unless you can show a savings package that is economically equivalent.

Right now, you are asking for:
- binding job and site security,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary restraint,
- and top-side contributions,

but you are **not yet showing a quantified savings bridge** that closes the gap to competitiveness. That matters more than the rhetoric.

A guarantee that every German plant stays open through 2030 is only valuable to shareholders if it does **not** leave the cost base structurally above target. If it does, it is not protection of value; it is deferral of loss.

### To Management
You are right that the company cannot remain uncompetitive. But you still have to prove that the chosen instruments are **necessary and proportionate**, not just the most confrontational available.

The expert is right that plant closures are plausible, but not yet proven as unavoidable on the facts presented. So we want **hard numbers**, not just strategic language:
- required annual fixed-cost reduction,
- capacity reduction by site,
- timing of savings,
- labor-side contribution,
- management/owner contribution,
- and fallback if targets are missed.

If the package is vague, capital markets will treat it as unfinished and discount the equity accordingly.

## Our position
We could support a package if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified savings target**
   - Enough to restore a credible margin trajectory.

2. **Real capacity reduction**
   - Not just accounting changes or wishful productivity assumptions.

3. **A meaningful employee contribution**
   - Working-time flexibility, wage or bonus restraint, redeployment, retraining, and productivity improvements.

4. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - Specific future models, technology, and capital allocation to German sites.

5. **A credible enforcement mechanism**
   - Milestones, independent review, and automatic escalation if delivery fails.

6. **No open-ended conflict**
   - A strike cycle or prolonged standoff is not an acceptable substitute for a plan.

## What we will not support
- A cosmetic compromise.
- A settlement that preserves all structures and hopes margins recover anyway.
- A package where the workforce bears the burden and owners/managers do not.
- A deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.
- A permanent veto on all restructuring tools if the economics do not support it.

## On the expert’s three options
- **Deep cuts with closures/forced redundancies:** economically credible if the gap is large, but conflict-heavy.
- **No closures/no forced layoffs:** only credible if the gap is moderate and voluntary/internal measures are sufficient.
- **Differentiated site-by-site strategy:** this is the **most plausible compromise** if it is genuinely quantified and enforceable.

That last point is important. We do **not** need a blanket closure program. But we do need a **real capacity adjustment** somewhere. If some sites are weaker, then pretending all sites can be frozen indefinitely is not a solution.

## Our bottom line
We are not here to protect the status quo. We are here to protect risk-adjusted capital value.

So our line is:
- **Yes** to a durable, quantified restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing if it is real.
- **Yes** to differentiated site solutions if they deliver savings.
- **No** to cosmetic peace.
- **No** to a deal that structurally impairs margins.
- **No** to a package where closures are forbidden in advance if that prevents equivalent economics.

If the next proposal can show a verified path back to competitive margins without open-ended conflict, we can support it. If not, we will continue to press for stronger restructuring through market and governance pressure.
