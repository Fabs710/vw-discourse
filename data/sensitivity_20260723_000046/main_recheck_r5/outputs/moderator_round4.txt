## Core impasse

The central blockage is now clear and specific:

1. **Employee-side stakeholders** insist that any settlement must start with:
   - no plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - no hidden closure path,
   - and binding protection through 2030.

2. **Management, the family holding, and investors** insist that the company needs:
   - a large, durable savings bridge,
   - real capacity reduction,
   - and enforceable restructuring tools.

3. **The expert’s assessment** suggests that a credible turnaround likely requires roughly **€1bn/year recurring structural savings**, which makes a purely symbolic deal insufficient but does **not** by itself prove that formal closures are the only route.

So the impasse is not “whether restructuring is needed.”  
It is **which instruments are permissible to achieve it**, and **whether capacity reduction can be done without formal closures or forced redundancies**.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure package with hard savings milestones
**Structure**
- All German plants remain open for a defined first phase.
- No compulsory redundancies during that phase.
- In return, employee-side flexibility, wage moderation, redeployment, retraining, and productivity measures are implemented immediately.
- A jointly monitored savings target is set for the phase.
- If the target is not met, the parties must enter a second-round review with pre-agreed, tightly bounded options.

**Why this might bridge the gap**
- It gives the workforce a real no-closure period.
- It gives management and owners an enforceable savings path.
- It avoids an open-ended freeze by tying protection to measurable performance.

---

### Proposal 2: No formal closures, but real capacity reduction through conversion and consolidation
**Structure**
- No plant is formally closed.
- But weaker sites may lose volumes, shift patterns, or vehicle assembly functions.
- Some sites may be converted to other industrial roles, specialized production, logistics, battery-related work, or component functions.
- Stronger sites receive investment and future model allocation.
- Workforce reductions are handled primarily through voluntary and redeployment-based means.

**Why this might bridge the gap**
- It preserves the political red line against closure.
- It still allows the company to remove fixed cost and overcapacity.
- It gives investors and management real structural savings without a blunt closure program.

---

### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package with fixed contribution shares and a binding safeguard
**Structure**
- Savings are split across four buckets:
  - labor contribution,
  - management contribution,
  - owner contribution,
  - and operational/capacity contribution.
- The package includes binding investment commitments for German sites.
- A safeguard clause requires that any further footprint change can only be considered after all other agreed burden-sharing measures are proven insufficient.
- Any review would be triggered by independent verification, not unilateral management declaration.

**Why this might bridge the gap**
- It addresses the demand for burden-sharing at the top.
- It gives workers protection against a direct closure trap.
- It still provides management with a path if the savings bridge proves too small.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact savings bridge, by site and by instrument, gets you to the required annual recurring savings without relying on plant closures as the first step?**

### To the Works Council
**If formal closures remain off the table, what concrete site-level capacity and cost adjustments would you accept as economically meaningful restructuring?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which labor-side measures can you commit to at scale immediately, and what volume of recurring savings do you believe they can realistically deliver?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Would you support a differentiated no-closure package if it included verified capacity reduction, binding investment commitments, and no compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to back a package that preserves no formal closures initially, provided that it delivers verified savings and real capacity adjustment through conversion and consolidation?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you regard a no-closure restructuring as credible if it achieved the expert’s savings benchmark through measurable capacity reduction, labor contribution, and enforceable milestones?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum combination of labor cost reduction, overhead simplification, and capacity shedding would you regard as sufficient to make a no-closure package economically credible?**

## Facilitator next step
A practical next round could be structured around one question only:

**Can the parties agree on a quantified savings bridge that reaches the expert’s minimum benchmark without formal plant closures in phase 1?**

If not, the discussion will remain stuck between:
- a **no-closure principle** on one side, and
- a **capacity-reduction necessity** on the other.
