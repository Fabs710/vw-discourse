I will be direct.

## My position
The state **does not accept** a restructuring logic that treats German plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the default solution. That remains my line.

At the same time, I do **not** deny the seriousness of the competitiveness problem. The expert has made clear that a real turnaround likely needs **very substantial recurring savings**, probably around the **€1bn annual order of magnitude** if the cost gap is truly as large as management claims. So I am not asking for cosmetic adjustment.

What I am saying is: if the company wants the state to consider any restructuring package, it must be **economically credible without sacrificing the regional industrial base**.

---

## Where I agree

### To management
I agree with you on three points:
- this is structural, not cyclical;
- a cosmetic peace would be harmful;
- the package must be measurable and enforceable.

I also accept the expert’s point that **capacity and fixed cost must come out somewhere** if the company is to regain competitiveness.

### To the union and works council
I agree that workers should not be the sole shock absorber. I also agree that burden-sharing must be real:
- labor flexibility,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity gains,
- and some labor-cost contribution.

That is fair and necessary.

### To investors and the family holding
I agree that the outcome must be durable and not just delay the problem to 2031.

---

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept closures as the starting point
Management, family holding, and investors are still speaking as if closures are the economically obvious route. The expert did **not** say that closures are proven unavoidable. The expert said the company likely needs **material capacity reduction**, and that this can sometimes be achieved through differentiated site conversion, line consolidation, and selective volume shifts without immediate formal closure.

That matters.

So the burden is still on management and owners to show:
- why the needed savings cannot be achieved by a differentiated site strategy,
- why internal redeployment and labor measures are insufficient,
- and why a more damaging instrument is truly necessary.

### 2) I do not accept “review sites” as a back door to closure
To the facilitator’s proposals: I am skeptical of any design that creates a protected core and then a separate review category that is effectively pre-closure politics by another name.

That is exactly the sort of framework that erodes trust in the workforce and in the region. If there is a differentiated site strategy, it must be framed as:
- **conversion**,
- **specialization**,
- **volume transfer**,
- and **long-term investment**,

not as a coded closure list.

### 3) I do not accept that co-determination is negotiable
The state’s veto role and the co-determination architecture are not bargaining chips to be eroded in the name of speed. Any package that tries to sideline those structures would be unacceptable.

---

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a **serious quantified compromise** only if it contains all of the following:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - workforce reduction only through voluntary and socially responsible means.

2. **No immediate plant closure program**
   - no closure mandate as the starting instrument.

3. **A differentiated industrial plan**
   - but based on transparent economics, not political labeling.
   - stronger sites protected and invested in.
   - weaker sites converted, specialized, or reduced through agreed measures.

4. **A binding labor contribution**
   - working-time flexibility,
   - bonus moderation,
   - productivity gains,
   - redeployment,
   - retraining,
   - and a temporary labor-cost contribution.

5. **Visible management and owner contributions**
   - executive restraint,
   - capital commitment,
   - investment allocation to German sites.

6. **Binding product and investment commitments**
   - future models and technologies anchored in Germany.

7. **An enforcement mechanism**
   - milestones, independent verification, and pre-agreed escalation that prioritizes further top-side and labor-side contributions before any footprint change.

---

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a plan that begins by normalizing plant closures;
- a plan that uses forced redundancies as the quick fix;
- a plan that turns the German footprint into a hierarchy of safe and sacrificial sites;
- or a plan that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

And I will say openly: if the only way management can “win” is by securing the right to close German plants or cut people by force, then that is **not** a sustainable settlement for this company in this region.

---

## My response to the expert’s numbers
The expert’s estimate is important. If the turnaround really needs around **€1bn recurring savings**, then a trivial package will not do.

But that does **not** automatically validate closures. It validates **serious restructuring**. There is still a difference.

What I need to see is a **savings bridge** showing:
- how much comes from labor measures,
- how much from management/owner contribution,
- how much from product and overhead simplification,
- and how much from capacity reduction **without** immediate plant closure.

If the company can prove that a differentiated, no-forced-layoff package can get close to the required savings, then I am willing to engage constructively.

If not, then the proposal is incomplete — but even then I would not automatically concede closures. I would first insist on a further round of burden-sharing and industrial redesign.

---

## My challenge to each side

### To management
Bring a quantified plan that shows:
- the annual savings target,
- the share from labor,
- the share from overhead and management,
- the share from product/mix improvement,
- and the share from capacity adjustment **without closure first**.

If you cannot show that, then you have not proven the necessity of the harsher tools.

### To the union and works council
Your red lines are politically and socially understandable. But “no closures” by itself is not a savings plan. If you want the state to defend site protection credibly, then you need to help define what labor and operational measures you can actually deliver at scale.

### To the family holding
If you want the state to take a harder line toward labor, you must be prepared to show real top-side sacrifice:
- management restraint,
- capital support,
- and a genuine investment commitment.

### To investors
You are right to want durability. But durability is not only about cutting fast; it is about avoiding a destructive conflict that weakens the enterprise’s domestic base and political legitimacy.

---

## My practical bottom line
I will support a restructuring only if it:
- preserves the company as a regional anchor,
- protects jobs as far as economically possible,
- keeps co-determination intact,
- and delivers a credible competitive turnaround.

That means:
- **no forced layoffs**,
- **no closure-first strategy**,
- **real burden-sharing from all sides**,
- and **a differentiated industrial plan with binding investment**.

If the next proposal cannot meet those conditions, then the state will not treat it as a compromise worth endorsing.
