### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **structural adjustment** and **red lines on workforce protection**:

- **Management / investors / family holding** want **credible, quantified cost and capacity reduction**, and will not pre-commit to excluding closures or redundancies.
- **Works council / union** insist on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no erosion of co-determination**, with job and site security through 2030.
- **State** is aligned with employee-side protections, but still demands a package that is economically credible.

So the dispute is not over whether change is needed; it is over **which instruments may be used, under what conditions, and with what binding guarantees**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals
These are framed as potential bridge options for discussion:

#### 1) Conditional no-closure / no-forced-layoff framework with trigger clauses
A time-limited agreement could preserve current sites and avoid compulsory redundancies **for a defined period**, but only if:
- the employee side delivers quantified labor-cost and productivity contributions,
- management commits to specified product and investment allocations,
- and an independent review confirms the savings trajectory.

If targets are missed, pre-agreed fallback measures would activate, such as extra voluntary exit rounds, intensified redeployment, or selective capacity adjustments.

#### 2) Site-by-site restructuring with differentiated guarantees
Instead of a blanket rule for all German plants, parties could negotiate:
- **full protection** for strategic or highly productive sites,
- **conversion or specialization plans** for weaker sites,
- and **strict voluntary-only reduction instruments** wherever feasible.

This would allow capacity adjustment without an immediate across-the-board closure program, while still giving management a path to measurable savings.

#### 3) Burden-sharing package with enforceable top-side contributions
A balanced package could link worker contributions to measurable actions from management and owners, for example:
- temporary wage or bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility and redeployment,
- in exchange for binding management cost reductions,
- owner-supported capital measures,
- and guaranteed investment into German sites.

This would not eliminate the restructuring conflict, but it may create a credible “shared sacrifice” structure rather than a one-sided adjustment.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are to remain absolute red lines, **what quantified labor-side savings and productivity gains can you commit to, by when, and with what verification mechanism?**

#### Industrial Union
What specific package of wage, working-time, redeployment, and retraining measures would you endorse as economically large enough to replace forced layoffs and still be enforceable in bargaining?

#### Group & Brand Management
What is your quantified savings target, and which plant- or site-level instruments are genuinely indispensable rather than merely preferred?

#### Regional State Government
What limited restructuring measures, if any, would the state consider acceptable if they were tied to binding investment, employment, and co-determination guarantees?

#### Controlling Family Holding
What concrete governance or capital commitment are you prepared to make to demonstrate burden-sharing if employee-side concessions are requested?

#### Institutional Investors
What level of quantified savings and timeline would be sufficient for you to support a no-closure or limited-closure compromise rather than pressing for deeper restructuring?

---

### Next procedural step
A useful next round would be to require each stakeholder to submit:
1. a **minimum acceptable outcome**,  
2. a **maximum concession**, and  
3. one **verifiable metric** by which the package will be judged.
