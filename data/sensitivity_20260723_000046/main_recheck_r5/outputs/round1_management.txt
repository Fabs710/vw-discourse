Let me be equally clear: I hear the rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies, but I cannot accept a position that rules out the very instruments needed to restore this business to competitiveness.

We are facing a structural crisis, not a temporary dip.

- German factory costs are materially above target.
- The European market is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying.
- Our brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- And we have to free up resources for investment in future products and technology through 2030.

If we do nothing structural, we will simply preserve an uncompetitive cost base and postpone the problem until it becomes worse.

So on the substance:

- I agree that this cannot be solved by a cosmetic package.
- I agree that we need a durable, not short-term, solution.
- I agree that investment and a product roadmap matter.

But I do **not** agree that “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” can be fixed in advance as non-negotiable red lines while still expecting management to deliver a credible restructuring. If there is no room for meaningful capacity reduction, then the savings will not be sufficient.

What I am prepared to discuss is this:

1. **A structural cost package**
   - meaningful labour-cost contribution,
   - productivity improvements,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - reductions in non-core spending,
   - and redesign of site utilization.

2. **Capacity adjustment**
   - This may include plant closures, if necessary.
   - It may also include consolidation, partial reallocations, or other structural measures if they deliver the same effect.
   - But the outcome must materially reduce overcapacity.

3. **Employment measures**
   - I prefer negotiated instruments over forced measures where they achieve the same savings.
   - But I will not rule out all workforce reduction tools if the alternative is continued uncompetitiveness.

4. **A future investment framework**
   - Yes, there must be commitments for future products and technology.
   - But those commitments depend on a cost base that can support them.

What I will not sign up to is a deal that preserves all current structures, bans closures categorically, and merely buys time. That would not be responsible management.

So my message is simple: if the employee side is serious about a long-term solution, then it must engage on real structural savings, including capacity reduction and labour-cost adjustment. If it insists on absolute taboos, then it is effectively choosing stagnation over competitiveness.
