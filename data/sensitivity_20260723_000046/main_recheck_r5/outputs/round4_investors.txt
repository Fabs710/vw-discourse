We agree with the expert and management on one central point: **the company needs a durable, quantified reset, not a cosmetic truce**. If the package does not credibly restore margins, it will be discounted by the market and continue destroying value.

## Where we agree
- The problem is **structural**, not cyclical.
- Prolonged conflict is value-destructive.
- Worker, management, owner, and state contributions all matter.
- Any solution must be **measurable, enforceable, and fast enough** to matter for 2030.

## Where we disagree with the employee side and state
Your insistence on **absolute no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no review sites, and no trigger-based fallback** is not acceptable to us as a shareholder position **unless** you can show a savings package that is economically equivalent.

Right now, you are asserting protection, but not yet showing a **validated savings bridge**. That matters more than the framing. A guarantee that every German plant stays open is only valuable if it does **not** leave the cost base structurally uncompetitive. If it does, it is simply delayed impairment.

We also reject the idea that closures or redundancies are automatically “destructive” in every case. If a site is structurally overcapacity or misaligned with future product allocation, then some footprint adjustment may be economically necessary.

## Where we disagree with management and the family holding
At the same time, management and the family holding have **not yet proven** that closures or forced redundancies are unavoidable. The expert was clear: a no-closure package may be insufficient, but that is not the same as proving closure is the only path.

So we do **not** support a closure-first stance without numbers.

What we need is a **management-side savings bridge** with site-level detail:
1. annual recurring savings target,
2. capacity and utilization by site,
3. labor contribution by instrument,
4. management/owner contribution,
5. investment and product commitments,
6. fallback if milestones are missed.

Without that, the market will treat the restructuring as unresolved and the equity will stay under pressure.

## Our position
We could support a package if it has all of the following:

- **A quantified savings target**, likely in the order the expert described for a credible turnaround.
- **Real capacity reduction** somewhere, not just accounting exercises.
- **Meaningful labor contributions**: working-time flexibility, wage/bonus restraint, redeployment, retraining, productivity gains.
- **Visible top-side contributions** from management and owners.
- **Binding investment and product commitments** to German sites.
- **A credible enforcement mechanism** with milestones and escalation.

## On the compromise proposals
### Two-stage settlement
We are open to sequencing only if phase 1 already delivers real structural savings. A phase 1 that merely postpones hard decisions is not enough. If there are triggers, they must be objective, independent, and tied to economics — not political convenience.

### Differentiated site strategy
This is the most plausible route. We do not need a blanket closure program. But we do need a real answer to overcapacity. If stronger sites are protected and weaker sites are converted, specialized, or downsized, that can be acceptable **if** the savings are real and durable.

What we will not support is a “protect everything forever” design that leaves the company structurally above target.

### Burden-sharing package
Yes — but burden-sharing must be genuine. Workers cannot be the sole adjustment reserve, and owners cannot just talk about stewardship while avoiding hard operational change.

## Our bottom line
We are not here to defend the status quo. We are here to protect risk-adjusted capital value.

So our line is:

- **Yes** to a durable, quantified restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing if it is real.
- **Yes** to differentiated site solutions if they deliver savings.
- **No** to cosmetic peace.
- **No** to a package that structurally impairs margins.
- **No** to an absolute ban on restructuring tools if the economics do not support it.

If the next proposal shows a verified path back to competitive margins without open-ended conflict, we can support it. If not, we will continue to press for stronger restructuring through market and governance pressure.
