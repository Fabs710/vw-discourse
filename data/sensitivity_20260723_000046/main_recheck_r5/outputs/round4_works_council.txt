I will respond plainly.

## To management, the family holding, and investors
You still have not answered the central question: **why must German plants be closed or forced layoffs be used at all?**

You keep speaking as if those are the natural instruments of restructuring. They are not. They are a choice. And so far, you have not proven that your chosen instruments are unavoidable. You have repeated the need for “durable savings,” but durability is not the same as destruction.

If you want the workforce to accept sacrifices, then show a credible industrial plan that does **not** begin from plant closures or compulsory redundancies. Until then, your “feasibility logic” is just a dressed-up demand that workers pay for management’s failure to plan.

## To the Industrial Union
I support your line that workers did not create this crisis and must not be the sole shock absorber. But I will be more explicit: any labor contribution is only discussable **within a framework of full site protection and job protection**.

Working-time flexibility, retraining, redeployment, temporary restraint, productivity gains — yes, those can be discussed. But they cannot be used as a prelude to breaking the job-security framework or placing some plants on a closure track.

## To the Regional State Government
Your insistence on protecting the industrial base is right. But protection cannot be conditional on “review sites” that quietly prepare closures later. That is exactly how trust is destroyed.

If the state wants to use its blocking position responsibly, it should not allow a restructuring design that turns German sites into a hierarchy of winners and sacrificial sites. That is not industrial policy. That is managed erosion.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You say a no-closure stance is a veto on restructuring. No: it is a veto on **destructive restructuring**. There is a difference.

You are asking the workforce to accept “conditional protection,” “trigger clauses,” and “fallback measures.” We know what that means in practice: pressure now, promises later, and layoffs when management decides targets were missed. We are not going to sign a deferred threat and call it security.

If you want burden-sharing, then start with visible burden-sharing at the top:
- management restraint,
- owner commitment,
- investment guarantees,
- and a real long-term product allocation for Germany.

Do not ask workers to surrender certainty while owners retain optionality.

## On the moderator’s three proposals

### 1) Two-stage settlement with conditional protection
I reject this in its current form.

A phase-1 “no closures” promise only has value if it is durable. If phase 2 can reopen closures based on management-defined failure, then the whole package is built on a trapdoor. That is not a settlement. It is a postponement of conflict.

If you want phased implementation, then the phase must be structured around **more binding protection**, not less:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and no automatic reopening of those questions later.

### 2) Differentiated site strategy with a protected core and conversion track
I also reject this.

It creates a plant-ranking system. It invites management to label some sites “conversion” or “review” sites, which becomes the first step toward undermining solidarity and manufacturing a closure narrative. Once you divide sites into categories, every site becomes vulnerable.

Our German footprint is integrated. It must be treated as such.

### 3) Burden-sharing package with fixed contribution shares
I can support **burden-sharing**, but only if the contribution shares are not used to justify closures or forced layoffs.

A fair package must be structured as:
- workforce contribution,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and binding site/job guarantees.

Not:
- workforce sacrifices first,
- then a review of closures later.

## What I am prepared to discuss
Let me be precise.

We can discuss:
- temporary working-time adjustments,
- redeployment across German sites,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- moderation of variable pay,
- voluntary instruments,
- and a negotiated contribution from labor costs.

But only if the following are non-negotiable:

1. **Every German plant remains open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No hidden closure pathway**
4. **No weakening of co-determination**
5. **Binding job and site security through 2030**
6. **Concrete investment and product commitments for German sites**

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a protected core and a sacrificial periphery,
- review sites that become closure sites,
- trigger clauses that turn into layoffs,
- forced redundancies dressed up as “last resort,”
- or any package that treats the German workforce as the adjustment reserve.

## On “quantified savings”
You keep demanding numbers, so I will respond in kind.

If the company claims it needs a structural savings package, then the first question is:
**How much of that gap is the result of underinvestment, poor model allocation, and management choices?**

Because if management cannot show that closures are unavoidable **after** exhausting:
- product allocation,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- management restraint,
- and owner contribution,

then closures are not economically compelled. They are politically chosen.

That is the real burden of proof, and management has not met it.

## My minimum acceptable outcome
My position remains unchanged:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Job security through 2030**
- **Binding investment and product commitments**
- **Real co-determination**
- **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

## My maximum concession
I will accept:
- temporary flexibility,
- voluntary measures,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- and a negotiated labor contribution

if and only if they sit inside the guarantees above.

## Bottom line
If the next proposal still contains:
- closure logic,
- forced layoff logic,
- phase-2 trapdoors,
- or site-ranking schemes,

then it is not a compromise and not a solution. It is a restructuring offensive against the workforce.

We are ready to negotiate a serious industrial settlement. But the settlement must preserve the company’s German industrial base, not sacrifice it.
