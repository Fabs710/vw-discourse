I will respond directly.

To management, the family holding, and the investors: you are all repeating the same mistake—treating German plants and German jobs as a cost item to be cut first, instead of as the industrial backbone of this company. I do not accept that logic.

### What I agree with
Yes, the company has a structural competitiveness problem.
Yes, we need a durable solution, not a cosmetic patch.
Yes, productivity, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and investment commitments all belong on the table.

### What I do not accept
I do **not** accept:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- the dismantling of the job-security framework,
- or a restructuring that shifts the burden one-sidedly onto workers.

That is the line. If management wants a serious agreement, it must stay within those boundaries.

## On the claimed need for closures
Management says capacity must come out “somewhere.” That may be true in the abstract, but it does **not** follow that German plants must be closed or that workers must be forced out. There are other instruments:
- internal redeployment across sites,
- shorter working time,
- voluntary departures only,
- qualification and re-skilling,
- temporary capacity reduction,
- shifting production mixes,
- and management and shareholder contributions.

If the business case only works by destroying sites and violating the social contract, then the management case is not yet credible enough to impose on the workforce.

## On the cost base
You all speak about labour costs as though workers alone created this problem. They did not. The company invested heavily in transition, management strategy has been inconsistent, and the burden cannot now be dumped onto the workforce to “restore margins” while ownership and leadership remain protected.

If there is to be any workforce contribution, then it must be matched by:
- real management restraint,
- visible owner contribution,
- and binding future investment in German sites.

That means not vague statements, but enforceable commitments.

## Our package position
If you want a serious route forward, then the package must include:

1. **No plant closures**
   - Binding commitment to keep every German plant open.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - Workforce reduction, if any, only through voluntary and socially responsible instruments.

3. **Job security through 2030**
   - A binding site and employment guarantee.

4. **Co-determination preserved**
   - No bypassing of the works council or erosion of the existing participation rights.

5. **Investment and product commitments**
   - Concrete allocation of future models, technology, and capital spending to German sites.

6. **Fair burden-sharing**
   - If the workforce contributes through flexibility or restraint, management and owners must also contribute measurably.

## On enforcement
We have already made clear that we will not accept a fait accompli. The company has the legal structure and governance reality to know that a unilateral closure agenda will not pass cleanly. If this is pushed further in that direction, conflict will escalate. We are prepared to defend these sites with the full means available to us, including industrial action and supervisory-board resistance.

So my answer is simple:

- **No to closures**
- **No to forced layoffs**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring only if it guarantees every German site and every job through 2030**
- **Yes to real transformation, but not at the expense of the workforce’s existence**

If management and the owners want a credible compromise, bring back a plan that fits those conditions. If not, then you are not negotiating restructuring—you are trying to force through industrial retrenchment, and we will resist it.
