As the regional state shareholder, I will be equally clear: **we are not prepared to support a restructuring that destroys German sites, guts regional employment, or sidelines the co-determination and veto rights that are part of this company’s governance**.

I recognize the management case that the company faces real competitive pressure. I do not dismiss the need for a durable cost and capacity solution. On that point, we are not blind to reality. But there is a fundamental difference between **serious restructuring** and **industrial self-destruction**.

Where I agree:
- The cost base must be addressed.
- The company needs a credible long-term investment and product plan.
- Short-term cosmetics will not solve a structural problem.
- Productivity, flexibility, and internal adjustments should be on the table.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable default instrument.**
- **Mass layoffs or forced redundancies are politically and socially unacceptable.**
- **Any attempt to push through a deal by sidelining the state, the works council, or the workforce would be a grave mistake.**

This company is not just another portfolio asset. It is a regional economic anchor. The jobs, supplier networks, training pipelines, and industrial know-how are part of the public interest. Once lost, they are not easily rebuilt. That is why I will not treat closure as merely one option among others in a neutral spreadsheet exercise.

To management: if you want our support, you need to show a restructuring that is **credible without wrecking the German footprint**. That means:
- negotiated capacity reduction where unavoidable, but not site destruction as the first resort,
- working-time and organizational flexibility,
- serious internal redeployment and qualification,
- investment commitments that sustain future production here,
- and a fair burden-sharing package that is not one-sidedly loaded onto labor.

To the workforce representatives: I support your insistence that this cannot be a unilateral cut-and-close plan. At the same time, I would urge you to remain open to a negotiated package that protects jobs through transformation, not just by freezing the status quo. If there is a way to secure sites and preserve employment through a serious contribution from all sides, that is preferable to a prolonged confrontation.

But let me set a clear boundary: **if the price of “competitiveness” is the hollowing out of German plants and the weakening of our co-determination role, I will not go along.** We have blocking rights for a reason, and I am prepared to use them.

So my position is this:
- **No blanket mandate for closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred route**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring**
- **Yes to productivity and flexibility measures**
- **Yes to investment and future-product commitments**
- **No to any outcome that shifts the burden onto regions and workers alone**

If management wants a durable settlement, it must come back with something that preserves the company’s long-term health **and** its role as a regional anchor. Otherwise, there will be no majority for it.
