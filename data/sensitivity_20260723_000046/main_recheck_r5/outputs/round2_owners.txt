I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: this is a structural problem, and a cosmetic package will not be acceptable. The company needs a durable restoration of competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity. If that does not happen, long-term value will continue to erode, and that is not in anyone’s interest.

Where I differ is on the implied assumption that the German footprint must be preserved essentially unchanged and that the restructuring tools needed to fix the economics can be ruled out in advance.

## To the Works Council and Union
I understand and respect the insistence on avoiding industrial chaos, and I agree that prolonged conflict would destroy value. I also agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to forced measures.

But I do **not** accept:
- a blanket ban on plant closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a guarantee that every existing site and guarantee survives untouched through 2030 regardless of economics.

That is not a credible restructuring framework; it is a veto on structural adjustment.

If you want me to support a no-closure or no-forced-redundancy outcome, then the alternative must be **economically equivalent** and must close the savings gap in a durable way. So far, I have not heard a quantified package that convincingly does that.

## To management
I agree that the company cannot keep carrying an uncompetitive cost base. I also agree that the plan must be more than rhetoric.

But I need to be equally clear: you have not yet made the case that closures are the only route, and I will not support a blunt, destabilizing approach if a negotiated alternative can achieve similar savings with less damage to the enterprise.

So management must come back with hard numbers:
- how much fixed cost reduction,
- how much capacity reduction,
- by when,
- and with what certainty.

“Capacity adjustment” is not enough as a phrase. It must be translated into a quantified, enforceable package.

## To the State
I recognize your regional responsibility, but I cannot support a position that treats German sites as untouchable regardless of competitiveness. Protecting the region in the long run requires a viable company, not just preserving every current structure.

That said, I also agree with you that the solution must not hollow out the industrial base or destroy co-determination. A value-destructive showdown is unacceptable. So if less damaging instruments can credibly deliver the savings, I would prefer them. But they must be real, not symbolic.

## What I would support
I would support a package with these characteristics:

1. **Material, durable savings**
   - The savings must be large enough to restore margin trajectory and competitiveness.

2. **Negotiated workforce contribution**
   - Working-time flexibility, productivity improvements, redeployment, and temporary restraint can be part of the solution.

3. **No unnecessary instability**
   - Voluntary instruments should be used where they are sufficient and credible.

4. **Binding commitments on investment and future products**
   - If the workforce is asked to contribute, the company must commit to future product allocation and investment in the German footprint.

5. **A clear enforcement mechanism**
   - No vague promises. The package must be measurable, time-bound, and reviewable.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that preserves all legacy structures and hopes for the best,
- a deal that makes the workforce carry the burden alone,
- a deal that weakens governance control,
- or a deal that destroys enterprise value through prolonged conflict or half-measures.

## The core test
The real question is simple:

Can you produce a package that restores competitiveness **without** imposing unnecessary value destruction?

If yes, I am open to it.
If no, then I will not back a settlement that merely postpones the adjustment and leaves the company weaker.

## On what I need next
Before I can move, I need a quantified alternative, not just principles:
- required savings target,
- exact instruments,
- timing,
- binding investment commitments,
- and how the package delivers equivalent economics without simply shifting the pain to owners and the future.

If no such package exists, then we should stop pretending that categorical taboos are a viable strategy.
