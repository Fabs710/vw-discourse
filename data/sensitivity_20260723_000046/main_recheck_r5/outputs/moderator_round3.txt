## Core impasse

The dispute is no longer about whether adjustment is needed. The core impasse is:

1. **Employee side and state demand binding protection first**
   - no closures
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - no erosion of co-determination
   - secure jobs/sites as the precondition for any labor contribution

2. **Management, controlling family, and investors demand quantified structural savings first**
   - they view closures/capacity cuts as potentially necessary instruments
   - they reject absolute vetoes on restructuring tools
   - they want enforceable savings, not just assurances

3. **The missing bridge is a shared, verifiable restructuring math**
   - no agreed target for savings
   - no agreed split between labor measures, management/owner contributions, and capacity adjustment
   - no agreed fallback if targets are missed

In procedural terms: **the parties are stuck between an absolute protection logic and an absolute feasibility logic, without a mutually accepted measurement framework.**

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Two-stage settlement with conditional protection and verified milestones
**Structure:**
- Immediate agreement on:
  - no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
  - no plant closures during phase 1
  - labor contribution package begins immediately
- In exchange:
  - management receives a pre-agreed review mechanism after fixed milestones
  - any later escalation can only occur if independent verification shows the savings gap is not being closed
- Fallback measures are **sequenced**, not automatic:
  1. additional management/owner contribution
  2. additional labor flexibility/voluntary measures
  3. only then consideration of footprint changes

**Purpose:**  
This may bridge those who want security now and those who need an enforceable path if the economics fail.

---

### Proposal 2: Differentiated site strategy with a protected core and a conversion track
**Structure:**
- Sites are sorted into three categories by jointly agreed criteria:
  1. **Protected core sites**: guaranteed continuation, investment, and product allocation
  2. **Conversion sites**: guaranteed no closure, but allowed to change role, volume, or product mix
  3. **Review sites**: no immediate closure, but subject to joint turnaround plans and measurable milestones
- No compulsory redundancies across all categories in the initial period
- If a site is in the review category, any changes must prioritize:
  - redeployment
  - retraining
  - voluntary departures
  - then only residual structural measures

**Purpose:**  
This avoids an all-or-nothing plant freeze while also avoiding an open closure map.

---

### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package with fixed contribution shares
**Structure:**
A quantified package divided into three lanes:

- **Labor lane**
  - working-time flexibility
  - temporary wage/bonus restraint
  - redeployment and retraining
  - voluntary exit instruments

- **Management/owner lane**
  - executive restraint
  - bonus deferral
  - governance-level commitment
  - visible capital/investment commitment

- **Industrial lane**
  - targeted capacity adjustments only where supported by jointly verified site economics
  - no blanket closure mandate
  - no forced redundancies in the initial settlement window

**Purpose:**  
This converts the debate from “who sacrifices first” into “who contributes how much.”

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What exact combination of labor measures, redeployment capacity, and productivity gains would you accept as sufficient to justify a binding no-forced-redundancy agreement through 2030?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which specific worker contributions are you prepared to put into a quantified package, and what measurable management/owner contributions must be matched in return?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum annual savings target, and how much of it must come from labor measures versus footprint or capacity changes for you to consider the plan workable?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What binding conditions would make you support a restructuring package that includes targeted site changes but still avoids compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to commit to a settlement that forbids closures in phase 1 if an independent review and a phased fallback mechanism are built in?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a time-bound package without immediate closures if it contained verified savings milestones, a differentiated site strategy, and automatic escalation steps if targets are missed?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum data set is required within the next round to determine whether a no-closure package is materially credible or structurally insufficient?**
