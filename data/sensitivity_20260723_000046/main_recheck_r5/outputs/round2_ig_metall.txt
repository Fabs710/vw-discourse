I will respond directly and plainly.

## To management
You are right about one thing: this is a structural crisis, not a temporary hiccup. And yes, we need a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.

But I reject your conclusion that this automatically justifies plant closures or forced layoffs. Those are not “necessary instruments” in the abstract; they are the most aggressive options, and they would shift the burden onto the workforce while destabilizing the company’s social and industrial base.

If you want my serious engagement, then you must prove that savings can be achieved **without** compulsory redundancies and **without** closing German plants. If your only model is to cut jobs and sites first, then you are not presenting a negotiated restructuring — you are presenting a unilateral downsizing plan.

## To the Works Council
I agree with your core line:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the job-security framework,
- and no one-sided burden on workers.

That is the correct starting point.

Where I would sharpen it is this: we should not just say “no” and stop there. We need a **counter-model** that is economically serious and politically enforceable. The company needs savings, but those savings can come from a package of:
- reduced working time or flexible shifts,
- voluntary measures only,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity commitments,
- and a negotiated labor-cost contribution.

That is the bargaining path. If management refuses it, the responsibility for escalation lies with them.

## To the family holding and investors
I hear your demand for real savings and competitiveness. That is legitimate. But you are overreaching when you imply that the only credible route is structural site closures.

There is a difference between:
- reducing overcapacity,
- and dismantling the German footprint.

There is also a difference between:
- asking the workforce for a fair contribution,
- and making employees absorb the full adjustment while owners and top management remain protected.

If you want support for a restructuring, then you must accept that **burden-sharing has to run in all directions**. That means:
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and worker contribution.

A “shared sacrifice” package is possible. A one-sided austerity package is not.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to discuss a package with the following elements:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - Workforce reduction only through voluntary and socially responsible instruments.

2. **No plant closures in Germany**
   - Site security must be part of the deal.

3. **A fair employee contribution**
   - Temporary wage restraint, bonus restraint, or other collective contributions may be negotiable.

4. **Capacity adjustment without site destruction**
   - Better utilization, internal reallocations, product-mix changes, and working-time flexibility.

5. **Binding investment commitments**
   - Concrete future products and technology allocations to the German sites.

6. **A time-bound security framework**
   - A durable agreement, not vague promises.

## What I will not accept
Let me be explicit:

- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no hollowing out of the collective wage framework,
- no open-ended insecurity,
- and no “competitiveness” argument that simply means employees bear the pain alone.

## On enforcement
If management and the owners want industrial peace, they need to come back with a package that reflects reality on both sides:
- the company gets cost relief,
- the workforce gets binding protection,
- and the German sites get a future.

If that is refused, then we will use the means available to us. The peace obligation has ended, and we are not here to be pressured into accepting a destructive solution.

## Bottom line
I remain open to a serious settlement — but only one based on **fair burden-sharing, job security, and site security**. If the proposal requires closures or forced layoffs, then it crosses the line. If it requires the workforce to contribute while management and owners also contribute, then we can negotiate.

That is the position.
