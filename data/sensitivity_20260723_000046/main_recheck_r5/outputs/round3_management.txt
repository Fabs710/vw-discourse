I’ll be equally direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: this must be a **durable industrial solution**, not a cosmetic truce. But let’s not pretend that durability can be achieved by freezing the current footprint and adding a few flexibility measures on top. That would leave the cost base uncompetitive and fail by 2030.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs investment and product commitments,
- productivity, redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility all have a role,
- management, owners, and the workforce all must contribute,
- and prolonged conflict would be damaging.

That is the easy part. We all know it.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket ban on plant closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a guarantee that every site and every job can be preserved unchanged through 2030.

Those are not bargaining positions. They are vetoes on restructuring.

And I need to be clear: if those red lines remain absolute, then the package is very unlikely to deliver the structural savings required to restore competitiveness.

## To the Works Council
You say the burden of proof is on management. Fine — and I accept that I must show the economics. But you are still not answering the central question:

**How do you close a structural cost gap of this size without any possibility of closures or compulsory redundancies?**

Voluntary exits, redeployment, and flexibility may help, but they do not automatically add up to enough. If you want to rule out the instruments that remove fixed cost fastest, then you need an alternative that is quantitatively equivalent, not just morally more comfortable.

And I do not accept “no closure” as a stand-alone metric. A site can remain formally open and still be economically hollowed out. That does not solve the problem.

## To the Union
I appreciate that you are trying to move from protest to package. That is the right instinct. But a package only works if it materially changes the cost base.

Temporary wage restraint, bonus restraint, and working-time flexibility may be part of it — but I will say plainly: **that is not enough on its own** unless it is substantial and durable.

If the employee contribution is only symbolic, then it is not a restructuring solution. It is delay.

## To the State
I understand your regional responsibility. But I must reject the idea that any serious restructuring is somehow a threat to the region by definition.

What threatens the region long-term is a company that cannot compete, cannot fund new products, and keeps bleeding margin.

I am open to binding job, site, and investment commitments — but not to a settlement that preserves the appearance of stability while leaving the economics broken.

## To the Family Holding
I agree that value destruction through inaction would be unacceptable. But I also agree with the union on one point: a blunt, destabilizing approach would be counterproductive if a negotiated, economically equivalent alternative exists.

So the standard is not “closure at any cost.” The standard is: **show me a durable package that closes the gap.**

## To Investors
You are right to demand credible, durable savings. But I do not accept the notion that management has not made the case for restructuring simply because we have not yet enumerated every plant decision in public.

The core issue is simple:
- German factory costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand must hit return-on-sales targets.

If a package cannot materially reduce fixed cost and capacity, it is not credible.

## What I need to see
To move, I need a **quantified package**, not slogans. Specifically:

1. **Required savings target**
   - What exact annual fixed-cost reduction are you prepared to commit to?

2. **Capacity reduction**
   - How much capacity comes out, and by when?

3. **Employee contribution**
   - What is the concrete labor-cost contribution?
   - Temporary wage restraint alone is not enough unless it is large and durable.

4. **Management and owner contribution**
   - What visible burden-sharing comes from the top?

5. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - What models, technologies, and capital are allocated to Germany?

6. **Enforcement**
   - What happens if targets are missed?

## My position on the facilitator’s bridge ideas
### 1) Conditional no-closure / no-forced-layoff framework
I could consider a **time-limited** protection framework **only if** it is paired with hard, measurable savings and a credible fallback. But I will not sign up to a structure that locks in protection and leaves us no remedy if the economics do not work.

If the workforce side wants certainty, then the economic delivery must be equally certain.

### 2) Site-by-site differentiation
This is the most realistic route if we are serious about preserving as much German industrial capacity as possible.

A blanket promise to keep every site exactly as is is not credible. But neither is a crude across-the-board closure plan. We need differentiated solutions:
- stronger sites protected and expanded,
- weaker sites converted, specialized, or reduced,
- and where needed, capacity removed in a targeted and enforceable way.

That is not “destroying the footprint.” It is preserving a viable footprint.

### 3) Burden-sharing package
Yes — if it is real.

That means:
- labor contribution,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and measurable capacity and cost reduction.

Not slogans about fairness without numbers.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a package with these elements:

- negotiated productivity measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and retraining,
- voluntary exits where sufficient,
- meaningful labor-cost contribution,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- and targeted capacity reduction.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that bans restructuring tools in advance,
- a deal that leaves the cost base structurally above target,
- a deal that relies only on temporary restraint,
- or a deal that preserves every structure and calls it transformation.

## Bottom line
We have a choice:
- either we deliver a **structural, durable cost and capacity reset**, or
- we drift into a compromise that protects appearances and weakens the company further.

I am not here to accept the second option.

If the next step is to negotiate numbers, then bring numbers. If the next step is to defend absolute taboos, then be honest that you are choosing to block the restructuring, not improve it.

If useful, I can now set out the management-side minimum package in one page: savings, capacity, labour contribution, and investment terms.
