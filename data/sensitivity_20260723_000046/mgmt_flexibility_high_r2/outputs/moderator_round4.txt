### Facilitator assessment: where the impasse lies

The core impasse is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. Most parties accept that it is.  
The impasse is over **the permissible instruments and the sequencing of decisions**:

1. **Labour side** insists that closures, compulsory redundancies, and any weakening of job-security guarantees are off the table unless a no-closure package has first been fully tested and shown insufficient.
2. **Management, family holding, and investors** insist that closures and forced measures must remain available if the alternative package does not close the economic gap.
3. **State government** wants to protect regional employment and co-determination, but has not yet secured a credible economic alternative that management would accept.
4. **Expert** has clarified the technical test: any no-closure solution must be **large, durable, and enforceable** to be economically equivalent.

So the deadlock is a **proof and sequencing dispute**:
- Labour says: “Prove closures are unavoidable.”
- Management says: “Prove a no-closure package is economically equivalent.”
- Neither side accepts the other’s threshold for proof.

---

### Facilitator proposals to bridge the conflict

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure test phase with binding metrics
A possible compromise is a **fixed-duration no-closure implementation phase** with:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- jointly governed KPIs,
- and pre-agreed contributions from management, owners, and capital.

At the end of the period, an independent review would assess:
- actual savings achieved,
- capacity reduction achieved,
- margin improvement,
- and remaining gap.

**Purpose:** gives labour the genuine test it demands, while giving management a structured review rather than an open-ended ban.

---

#### Proposal 2: Staged escalation ladder with hard stop before closure
A second compromise is a **sequenced escalation model** in which the first phase must exhaust:
- management compensation restraint,
- overhead cuts,
- owner capital support,
- procurement savings,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining.

Only if the jointly measured residual gap remains material after those steps would the parties enter a second-stage negotiation on harsher instruments.

**Purpose:** preserves management’s insistence on economic discipline, while preventing closures from becoming the default first response.

---

#### Proposal 3: Site-security package tied to owner and management commitments
A third compromise is a **site-security package** for defined German plants, linked to:
- binding investment commitments,
- capital support from owners,
- management restraint,
- measurable productivity targets,
- and a moratorium on unilateral escalation.

This could be paired with:
- temporary concessions from labour,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment commitments,
- and a review clause based on transparent financial thresholds.

**Purpose:** creates a credible middle ground between unconditional job protection and open-ended closure threats.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To management:
**What exact residual savings gap remains after management restraint, owner support, overhead cuts, productivity measures, and voluntary exits are fully counted? Please specify the number before any closure option is discussed.**

#### To the Works Council:
**What level of quantified, multi-year, enforceable labour and flexibility contribution would you consider sufficient to test a genuine no-closure package, without regard to whether the result is politically easy?**

#### To the Industrial Union:
**Which specific instruments are you prepared to trade for binding job-security and site-security commitments, and which instruments remain absolutely non-negotiable?**

#### To the Regional State Government:
**Will you use your governance role to support a binding no-closure test phase with measurable obligations on all sides, or only express political preference against closures?**

#### To the Controlling Family:
**What concrete capital or return-sacrifice commitment are you prepared to make to demonstrate stewardship before closure-based options are considered?**

#### To Institutional Investors:
**Would you support a no-closure package if it demonstrably meets the expert’s standard of being large, durable, and enforceable—even if it preserves more sites than you would otherwise prefer?**

#### To the Expert:
**What minimum combination of savings, capacity reduction, and duration would you regard as sufficient for a no-closure package to be economically comparable to closure-based restructuring?**
