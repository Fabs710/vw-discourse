Let me be equally direct in return.

## Where I agree

I agree with all parties on three things:

1. **A cosmetic deal is useless.**  
   A temporary patch that leaves the German cost base structurally too high is not acceptable.

2. **Burden-sharing must be real.**  
   Management, owners, and capital must contribute. I have said that consistently.

3. **We need quantified alternatives.**  
   We are past slogans. We need numbers, timelines, and binding conditions.

So far, we are aligned on the need for a serious package.

---

## Where I strongly disagree

### 1) “No closures” as an absolute line
I cannot accept that.

You keep repeating that closures are a choice, not a necessity. That is only true if you can demonstrate an alternative package that closes the gap in a durable way. So far, you have not done that.

We face:
- structural overcapacity,
- a shrinking European market,
- intense price pressure,
- and a German cost base above target.

If that overcapacity cannot be removed by other means at the required scale, then plant rationalization remains necessary. Not desirable. Necessary.

I will not pre-commit to preserving every site regardless of economics.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as a blanket commitment
I cannot give that as an unconditional guarantee.

If there is a negotiated, voluntary path that avoids forced exits, I will support it. But if the alternative is to freeze the structure and pretend the cost problem has disappeared, then we are not restructuring. We are postponing the crisis.

### 3) Binding job-security through 2030 regardless of the package
That is not credible if the package does not restore competitiveness.

A job-security guarantee is only acceptable if it is tied to a materially improved cost and capacity structure. Otherwise it becomes a promise to preserve an uncompetitive model.

---

## Where I think your arguments are still incomplete

### To the Works Council
You say there are many alternatives to closures. Fine. Then show me the numbers.

I want a side-by-side comparison of:
- exact annual savings required,
- exact capacity reduction required,
- how much comes from working-time reductions,
- how much from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- how much from wage restraint and bonus waivers,
- how much from management restraint and overhead cuts,
- how much from owner contribution,
- and what gap remains.

Until that is produced, “no closures” is a preference, not a restructuring solution.

And let me be clear: I am not dismissing co-determination. But co-determination is not a veto on necessary change.

### To the Union
You say industrial peace is a value factor. I agree. But industrial peace does not create competitiveness by itself.

If you want to avoid closures and forced exits, then labour contributions must be **material**. Not symbolic. Material.

That means discussing:
- wage restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- bonus reductions or waivers,
- productivity commitments,
- and structural labour-cost reduction.

If those are ruled out in practice, then there is no serious alternative to sharper restructuring.

### To the State Government
I respect your concern for regional stability. But the state cannot ask us to remain competitive while simultaneously ruling out the tools needed to achieve competitiveness.

If the state wants to avoid closures, then it must support a package that is economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring. That means:
- real cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- and real flexibility.

Not a political shield for the status quo.

### To the Controlling Family
You are right that a closure-led confrontation would be value-destructive. I do not want conflict for its own sake.

But stewardship also means accepting that some sites or structures may no longer fit the business model. If we preserve overcapacity to avoid friction, we will destroy more value later.

I need owners to be prepared for a hard solution if the alternatives do not close the gap.

### To Institutional Investors
You are right to focus on value and certainty. I share that.

But certainty built on an uncompetitive base is false certainty. Investors do not benefit if we preserve every legacy structure and then face another crisis in two years.

I want a package that restores margins, not one that merely delays pain.

---

## What I am prepared to accept

I remain open on **instruments**. That is important.

I am prepared to support a package that includes:
- management compensation restraint,
- overhead reductions,
- procurement savings,
- owner support,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- working-time adjustments,
- and negotiated labour-cost measures.

If that package delivers durable, measurable savings and capacity alignment, I do not care whether every site remains untouched in form. What I care about is whether the company becomes competitively viable.

But if the answer is:
- no closures,
- no forced exits,
- no meaningful labour-cost reduction,
- and no structural capacity change,

then that is not a package. That is avoidance.

---

## What I need next

I am not asking for rhetoric. I am asking for a **quantified counter-package**.

Bring me:
1. the savings target,
2. the capacity reduction target,
3. the labour contribution,
4. the management contribution,
5. the owner contribution,
6. the timeline,
7. and the remaining gap.

If labour can show a credible, durable, economically equivalent alternative to closures, I will take it seriously.

If it cannot, then I will continue to insist that all structural options remain open — including plant closures and compulsory redundancies if necessary.

---

## Bottom line

Let me state this without ambiguity:

- I do **not** accept a no-closure promise as a starting principle.
- I do **not** accept a blanket no-layoff guarantee.
- I do **not** accept job security through 2030 if the economics do not support it.
- I do accept a negotiated solution if it produces real, durable, structural savings.

The company needs to restore competitiveness now, not after years of delay.

So either bring a quantified alternative that matches the economics, or stop pretending closures and labour-cost reduction can be ruled out by declaration alone.
