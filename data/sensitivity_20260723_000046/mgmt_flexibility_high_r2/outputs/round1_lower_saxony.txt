As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys regional employment, sidelines co-determination, or treats plant closures as the default answer.**

At the same time, I do not dismiss the competitiveness problem. The company does need a durable solution. A denial of structural challenges would be irresponsible. So let me respond directly to each side.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: **cosmetic measures will not be enough**. The company needs a credible path to lower costs and restore competitiveness.

Where I disagree is your framing that plant closures and broad layoffs must remain permanently “on the table” as if they were the normal starting point. That is not acceptable to us. Why?

- This company is a central part of the regional economy.
- German sites are not just cost centers; they are industrial assets, skills bases, and local anchors.
- A solution that simply shifts the burden onto workers and regions may look neat in a spreadsheet but creates long-term damage to the business and to social trust.

We also reject any attempt to use the crisis to weaken co-determination or to render the special shareholder protections meaningless. **Our veto and board role are not decorative.** They exist precisely to prevent one-sided, socially destructive decisions.

So: yes to restructuring, **no to a closure-led logic**.

### To the works council and union
I agree with you that the workforce did not create this crisis and cannot be asked to pay for it alone. I also agree that **forced redundancies should be avoided** wherever possible, and that any adjustment must be socially fair.

But I also need to say this plainly: if we simply declare all German sites untouchable under every circumstance, we risk making it impossible to secure the company’s future. A durable solution will require:
- real capacity adjustment,
- genuine productivity gains,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

That contribution must be fair, temporary where possible, and matched by contributions from management and owners. But it cannot be zero. If we insist that no structural change is possible anywhere, then we are not protecting jobs in the long run — we are postponing a worse outcome.

### What I can support
I would support a negotiated package built around these principles:

1. **No plant closure as a first resort**
   - Closures should not be the starting point.
   - They would only be imaginable if all other options have been exhausted and if there were no credible alternative to avoid severe damage to the enterprise.

2. **No compulsory redundancies if avoidable**
   - Through natural attrition, redeployment, working-time adjustments, and voluntary instruments, we should minimize involuntary job losses.

3. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - Any restructuring must come with a clear investment plan for future technologies and products in Germany.
   - We are not interested in shrinking for its own sake.

4. **A fair burden-sharing agreement**
   - Management compensation, overhead, procurement, capital contributions, and employee measures must all be part of the package.
   - If workers contribute, so must management and owners.

5. **Protect co-determination and regional interests**
   - No attempt to bypass the supervisory board or use the crisis to weaken the state’s role as anchor shareholder.
   - Major site decisions require genuine agreement, not pressure tactics.

### What I cannot accept
I cannot support:
- a restructuring that begins with closure decisions,
- mass layoffs as the primary instrument,
- or an outcome that leaves the state and workforce with the costs while capital remains insulated.

### Bottom line
My position is not “no change.” It is: **change must be negotiated, balanced, and socially responsible**. The company’s long-term health depends not just on cost cuts, but on preserving industrial capability, trust, and regional stability.

So I am asking management to bring forward a **credible alternative package** that delivers real savings **without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies as the starting logic**. And I am asking the workforce representatives to stay open to a meaningful, but fair, contribution if it is part of a broader settlement that protects sites, jobs, and the company’s future.

If that kind of package is on the table, we can work with it. If not, then you will find the state government using every legal and political instrument available to block a socially destructive outcome.
