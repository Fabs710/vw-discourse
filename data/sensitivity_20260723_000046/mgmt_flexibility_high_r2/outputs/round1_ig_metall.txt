We share the core line that was just stated by the Works Council: **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points**. If management wants a serious dialogue, it has to stop treating the workforce as the only adjustment variable.

Let me be equally clear about the union’s position:

- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No plant closures as the price of restructuring.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework as if workers alone must pay for management mistakes.**

If the company has structural overcapacity and cost problems, then the answer must be a **shared-sacrifice package**, not a one-sided cutback program. That means we are prepared to discuss:
- temporary or partial working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments through negotiated instruments,
- voluntary arrangements,
- forgoing bonuses or other temporary employee contributions,
- and productivity measures that are jointly agreed.

But this only becomes realistic if **management, owners, and capital also contribute visibly**. Workers cannot be asked to absorb wage losses, insecurity, and reduced standards while the company, its leadership, and its shareholders are insulated from the pain.

So where do we stand?

### Where we agree
- The company needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.
- Competitiveness matters.
- A negotiated restructuring is preferable to open conflict.
- Job security and site security must be central.

### Where we disagree
- If management’s “solution” still relies on **closing plants or forcing people out**, then that is not restructuring — it is destruction.
- If labour is asked to carry the burden alone, that will not be accepted.
- If the collective agreement is to be overridden permanently, then we are not talking about a fair settlement, but about breaking standards that protect the whole workforce.

### Our test for any acceptable package
We need three things, at minimum:
1. **Binding protection of sites and no compulsory redundancies**
2. **A fair employee contribution matched by comparable contributions from management and owners**
3. **A durable industrial settlement, not an open-ended period of uncertainty**

If those conditions are met, we can negotiate seriously. If not, then the workforce will defend its interests with the instruments available to it, including industrial action.

We are ready for a hard negotiation. We are not ready for a unilateral sacrifice imposed on employees.
