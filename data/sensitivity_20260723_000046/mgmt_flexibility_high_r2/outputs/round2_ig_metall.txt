I will respond directly and plainly.

### To the Works Council
I agree with your core line: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures as the first answer, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**. That remains the union’s baseline as well.

Where I would sharpen it is this: we should not only say “not this.” We need to show the company a **credible alternative package** that makes “not this” economically workable. If management keeps claiming there is no alternative, we need to force that claim into the open with numbers and commitments.

So I support your stance, but I want us to be careful not to let the discussion drift into a symbolic “sites forever” formula unless the package is actually financed and balanced.

### To Management
Your answer is still not sufficient.

You keep saying “real structural adjustment,” but you have not shown that **closures or forced layoffs are necessary**, only that they are convenient for your leverage. That is not the same thing.

If you want the union to consider sacrifice, then you need to answer four concrete questions:

1. **What exact savings gap must be closed?**
2. **How much of that gap can be covered by management, owners, overhead, procurement, and productivity measures?**
3. **What capacity reduction is really needed, and can it be achieved without closing plants?**
4. **What binding guarantees do workers get in return for any labour contribution?**

Until that is answered, “the plants must stay on the table” is not a restructuring plan. It is a threat.

And let me be equally clear:  
- **No compulsory redundancies** remains my position.  
- **No plant closures** remains my position.  
- **No permanent weakening of the collective agreement** remains my position.

If management cannot live with that, then it is not yet offering a fair bargaining basis.

### To the State Government
I appreciate the insistence that the workforce cannot carry this alone. That is right. I also appreciate your refusal to normalize closures as the default answer.

But I will say this clearly: if the state wants social peace, then it has to back a package that is real, not just “balanced” in language. Balance means:
- measurable management restraint,
- real owner contribution,
- investment in German sites,
- and binding protection for jobs and skills.

If those elements are absent, then the burden will still fall mainly on workers, whatever the press release says.

### To the Controlling Family
You are right that the company needs a durable solution and that empty symbolism will not do. But stewardship also means **not using the crisis to shift all adjustment onto labour**.

You say closures are not your default. Good. Then help make them unnecessary.

That means:
- visible capital support,
- acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- investment commitments,
- and willingness to back a negotiated labour-cost and capacity package that preserves sites and avoids forced exits.

If owners want long-term enterprise value, then they must help finance the transition rather than demanding that workers pay first and last.

### To Institutional Investors
You are right about one thing: half-measures are no solution. But you are too quick to reduce the issue to “competitive margins” without acknowledging what destroys value in the real world: **open conflict, broken trust, and industrial instability**.

A bargain that forces the workforce into permanent insecurity may look efficient on a slide deck and still fail in implementation. We have seen that before.

If you want our support for a negotiated package, then it must include:
- real and measurable savings,
- but also a **credible no-closure and no-forced-layoff architecture**,
- plus burden-sharing by management and owners.

Without that, you are not asking for a solution; you are asking workers to underwrite risk they did not create.

## The union’s package logic
Let me put our position in package terms, because that is what this discussion needs.

### We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- short-term wage restraint or deferred components,
- forfeiting bonuses or similar monetary elements,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- productivity and organizational improvements,
- and a jointly negotiated capacity adjustment.

### In return, we require:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and employment protection through 2030**
- **real management restraint**
- **owner and capital contributions**
- **a durable collective settlement**

That is not unreasonable. That is shared sacrifice.

### What is not acceptable
What is not acceptable is a model where:
- workers give up wages, security, and guarantees,
- while management preserves full discretion,
- owners preserve capital returns,
- and the company still keeps closure options as a disciplinary threat.

That would not be a fair restructuring. It would be a transfer of pain downward.

---

## Where the discussion now has to go
If management is serious, then bring a quantified proposal:
- savings target,
- capacity reduction target,
- labor contribution,
- management and owner contribution,
- and the exact job and site guarantees that accompany it.

If that package respects the red lines I have stated, we can negotiate it.

If it does not — if it still depends on plant closures, forced layoffs, or the weakening of the collective wage framework — then the union will not endorse it, and we will use our instruments accordingly.
