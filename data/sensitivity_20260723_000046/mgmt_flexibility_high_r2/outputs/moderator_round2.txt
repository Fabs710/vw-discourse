### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the situation requires a **real restructuring**, not cosmetic measures.
- There is broad agreement that **burden-sharing must be real** and cannot fall only on employees.
- There is also agreement that any package should be **quantified**: savings target, capacity adjustment, contribution by each side, and implementation timeline.
- Most parties accept that **management restraint, owner/capital contribution, and operational improvements** must be part of any solution.
- Several actors agree that **open conflict would destroy value** and that a negotiated outcome is preferable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Management, investors, and to some extent the family side keep closures as an option if needed. Works council, union, and state government reject closures as the starting point and want them avoided.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour and the state oppose them; management and investors will not rule them out if the gap cannot otherwise be closed.
- **Job-security guarantees through 2030:**  
  Labour wants binding protection; management and investors say such guarantees are only acceptable if the economics work.
- **Extent of labour concessions:**  
  Labour is prepared to discuss working-time changes, voluntary exits, wage restraint, and productivity measures, but not if these are used to justify job destruction. Management and investors want contributions that are explicitly material and durable.
- **Role of co-determination / governance vetoes:**  
  Labour and the state emphasize these as substantive constraints; management and investors emphasize that governance cannot block economically necessary restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No side has yet provided a **side-by-side numerical comparison** of:
  - required savings,
  - capacity reduction,
  - labour contribution,
  - management contribution,
  - owner/capital contribution,
  - and the remaining gap.
- It remains unresolved whether a **no-closure package** can deliver savings and capacity reduction that are truly equivalent to a closure-based restructuring.
- It is still unclear how much **labour flexibility** is acceptable in exchange for binding employment/site protection.
- The level and form of **owner and capital support** remain unspecified.
- There is no agreed answer on whether **site guarantees** should be unconditional, conditional, or performance-linked.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and conditionality**.
- Ask each side to submit a **quantified package** with:
  - target savings,
  - capacity adjustment,
  - specific contributions by management, owners, and employees,
  - and the exact conditions for any job/site protections.
- Focus the next round on whether a **credible no-closure alternative** exists that is economically equivalent, or whether the gap remains too large without closures.
