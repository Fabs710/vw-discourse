Let me be equally direct.

I hear the insistence on protecting sites and avoiding compulsory redundancies. I understand why you are drawing that line. But I cannot accept the premise that **German plants are automatically untouchable** or that the current cost and capacity structure can be defended unchanged.

We are in an acute competitiveness crisis. The facts are not optional:
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our German cost base is above target,
- and the brand is not meeting its return-on-sales target.

If we do nothing structural, we do not preserve the workforce — we simply delay a much larger decline.

### Where I agree
I agree that a purely cosmetic deal is worthless.
I agree that a negotiated solution is better than an uncontrolled conflict.
I agree that we need durable industrial peace, not permanent uncertainty.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** agree that the only acceptable outcome is binding protection for all German sites through 2030, with no closures and no forced reductions, regardless of economics.

That would preserve today’s structures, not restore competitiveness. It would leave us with:
- too much capacity,
- too high a fixed-cost base,
- and too little room to invest in the products and technology we need for 2030.

That is not a sustainable solution.

### What management needs
We need a package that produces **real, structural, and durable savings**. That means:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a substantial labour-cost contribution,
- and measures that materially improve the cost base, not just temporarily defer it.

I am open on the exact instruments. I am **not** fixed on closures as the only route. If there is a credible alternative package that delivers equivalent savings and capacity adjustment, then we should look at it seriously. But it must be equivalent in effect, not merely in rhetoric.

### On your conditions
- **No compulsory redundancies:** I am prepared to discuss alternatives, but I will not rule out headcount reduction as a result of restructuring. If you want to avoid forced exits, then you need to bring forward a package that actually makes that possible.
- **No plant closures:** I cannot accept a blanket exclusion. Plant decisions must remain on the table because the company must have the option to reduce structural overcapacity.
- **Job security through 2030:** I cannot commit to that if it locks in an uncompetitive structure. Any guarantees have to be conditional on a credible cost and capacity solution.
- **Co-determination role:** yes, but co-determination is not a veto on necessary change. It is a framework for reaching a workable settlement.

### On burden sharing
You are right that employees cannot be the only ones asked to contribute. Management and owners must also support the restructuring. But that does not change the fact that the labour-cost base has to come down materially. Without that, we do not close the gap.

### My message
I am not here to negotiate away the restructuring. I am here to achieve it in a form that is durable and legally workable.

So the real question is this:
- Can the workforce and union bring forward a package that delivers **substantial, lasting savings and capacity reduction** without plant closures?
- Or are you simply asking management to give up the only instruments that can restore competitiveness?

If it is the latter, then we are not yet at a serious solution.
