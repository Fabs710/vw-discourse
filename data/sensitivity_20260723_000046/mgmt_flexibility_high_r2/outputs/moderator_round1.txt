### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties say a **durable, negotiated solution** is preferable to open conflict.
- There is broad agreement that **cosmetic or purely temporary measures are insufficient**.
- Most parties accept that the company faces a **real competitiveness and capacity problem**.
- There is shared willingness to discuss **alternative restructuring tools** such as working-time adjustments, redeployment, voluntary exits, productivity measures, and broader burden-sharing.
- There is general agreement that **management, owners, and capital should also contribute**, not only employees.
- Most parties value **industrial peace, trust, and long-term enterprise viability**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state government reject closures as a starting point; management, family owners, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if no equivalent alternative exists.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour side wants them excluded; management and owners will not give a blanket exclusion and want flexibility if needed.
- **Job security guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Labour side wants binding protection; management and investors see that as too rigid if it blocks restructuring.
- **Scope of co-determination:**  
  - Labour and state want a strong shaping role; management says co-determination is not a veto on necessary change.
- **How much adjustment is needed:**  
  - Management, owners, and investors want **real structural capacity and cost reduction**; labour and state want that achieved **without site destruction or forced layoffs**.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to deliver equivalent savings without closures or forced layoffs**, if that remains the preferred social outcome.
- **How much labour contribution is acceptable** and what form it should take:
  - wage restraint, working-time reductions, bonus waivers, temporary concessions, etc.
- **What management, owners, and capital will concretely contribute** beyond general statements.
- **How to define “equivalent” restructuring outcomes** in measurable terms:
  - savings, capacity reduction, margin improvement, timeline, and certainty.
- **Whether any site-specific differentiation** could replace a blanket commitment or blanket closure threat.
- **What safeguards would make a negotiated package credible** enough for both industrial and financial stakeholders.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please shift from positions to **specific package design**:
- quantify the required savings and capacity reduction;
- identify which measures each side is prepared to put on the table;
- test whether a no-closure / no-forced-layoff package can still meet the company’s competitiveness target;
- and define what binding commitments each stakeholder would need in order to support a deal.
