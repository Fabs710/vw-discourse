Let me respond directly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree on three points, and I want that stated clearly:

1. **Cosmetic restructuring is not enough.**  
   A temporary patch that leaves our German cost base above target is worthless.

2. **Burden-sharing must be real.**  
   Management, owners, and capital must contribute. I have said that from the start.

3. **We need quantified options, not slogans.**  
   I accept the call for numbers, timelines, and implementation conditions.

So yes: we are aligned on the need for a serious, measurable package.

---

## Where I fundamentally disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a starting principle
I cannot accept an absolute no-closure line.

Why? Because management is responsible for restoring competitiveness, not preserving every site regardless of economics. We face:
- structural overcapacity,
- a shrinking European market,
- severe price pressure,
- and a German cost base that is clearly above target.

If those facts cannot be corrected by alternatives at sufficient scale, then plant rationalization remains necessary. Not desirable — necessary.

You can call that harsh. I call it reality.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be guaranteed in advance
I am open to a negotiated, voluntary path. I prefer it. But I will not promise in advance that the company can be restructured while ruling out every forced adjustment.

If the package truly closes the gap, then fine — forced redundancies may not be needed.  
But if the answer is simply “no closures, no forced exits, and no meaningful labour-cost reduction,” then the company remains structurally uncompetitive.

That is not a deal. That is a delay.

### 3) Binding job security through 2030 only works if the economics work
A job-security guarantee is not something you can demand independently of the business model.

If the package materially reduces costs and capacity, then employment protection may be possible.  
If it does not, then a 2030 guarantee simply locks in a weak structure and pushes the crisis forward.

That is not responsible restructuring.

- reduces capacity sufficiently,

- and restores margin to the required level.

- management restraint,
- overhead cuts,
- owner support,
- productivity measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and labour-cost contributions.

If that remaining gap is still material, then closures remain on the table. That is not extortion. It is arithmetic.

And let me be very clear: co-determination is important, but it is not a veto on necessary change.

- structural permanence,
- capacity alignment,
- and implementation certainty.

That is the benchmark.
