## Core impasse
The parties are no longer mainly disputing the need for restructuring in the abstract. The deadlock is over **the means of restructuring**:

- **Management / investors / family owners** want the option of **closures and, if necessary, compulsory redundancies** to ensure a durable cost and capacity reset.
- **Works council / union** reject closures and forced redundancies as a starting point and want a **no-closure, no-forced-layoff** model.
- **State government** is trying to hold the line against closures while still demanding a restructuring that is economically credible.
- The real unresolved issue is whether a **no-closure package can be shown, in numbers, to match the savings, capacity reduction, and certainty of a closure-based package**.

So the plateau is not about values alone; it is about **equivalence, proof, and enforceability**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Conditional no-closure package with a hard review trigger
A possible bridge would be a **time-limited no-closure moratorium** paired with a quantified restructuring package and a pre-agreed review point.

**Elements:**
- No plant closures during the initial phase.
- No compulsory redundancies during that same period.
- Employee contributions through working-time flexibility, wage restraint, voluntary exits, and productivity measures.
- Management, owners, and investors commit to visible contributions.
- A jointly agreed review date with clear KPIs:
  - savings achieved,
  - capacity reduction achieved,
  - margin improvement,
  - implementation progress.
- If targets are missed, the parties reopen the question of structural options under pre-agreed rules.

**Why this could bridge the gap:**  
It gives labour a genuine chance to prove the no-closure path, while giving management and capital an enforceable fallback if the package fails.

---

### Proposal 2: “No forced layoffs first” with a staged escalation ladder
A second compromise would be to agree on a **sequenced restructuring ladder**:

1. management and overhead cuts,
2. owner/capital contribution,
3. voluntary exits and redeployment,
4. working-time and productivity measures,
5. only then, if the agreed savings gap remains, discussion of harsher structural measures.

**Elements:**
- A binding order of instruments.
- Closure and compulsory redundancies are not first-line tools.
- They remain available only after documented exhaustion of the earlier steps.
- An independent financial benchmark determines whether the earlier steps were sufficient.

**Why this could bridge the gap:**  
It preserves the management side’s insistence on economic discipline while addressing the employee side’s demand that closures and forced exits not be used as the initial lever.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-security package tied to measurable owner and management commitments
A third route would be a **site-security package** for selected German plants, but only in exchange for strong counter-commitments.

**Elements:**
- Protection for designated sites through 2030.
- No compulsory redundancies at those sites.
- In return:
  - management compensation restraint,
  - deeper overhead cuts,
  - owner capital injection or support,
  - investment commitments,
  - productivity and flexibility measures,
  - and possibly selective capacity shifts elsewhere in the network.
- A clear clause that site protection depends on delivery of agreed savings.

**Why this could bridge the gap:**  
It gives the works council, union, and state a defensible employment anchor while still requiring enough financial and operational adjustment to satisfy management and capital.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact quantified gap remains after management restraint, owner support, overhead cuts, productivity gains, and voluntary measures — and what evidence shows that this gap cannot be closed without closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Works Council
**What is your quantified alternative package, including savings, capacity reduction, and duration of concessions, that proves a no-closure solution can match the economics of closure-based restructuring?**

### To the Union
**Which concrete labor-side measures are you prepared to put on the table immediately, and for how long, so that a no-closure package is not just principled but financially credible?**

### To the State Government
**What governance or shareholder action are you prepared to use to make a no-closure settlement enforceable, rather than merely aspirational?**

### To the Controlling Family
**What specific capital, return, or investment commitment are you prepared to make so that a no-closure outcome becomes economically equivalent rather than politically symbolic?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum financial metrics would make you support a no-closure package, and would you accept a conditional moratorium if those metrics were independently verified?**
