We appreciate the clearer positions and the move toward a quantified package.

From the capital-markets perspective, the discussion is now where it needs to be: not “closure versus no closure” as an article of faith, but whether there is a **credible route to competitive margins, capacity alignment, and implementation certainty**.

## Where we agree

We agree with management, the family owners, and the state on the core economic diagnosis:

- the company has a **structural cost and capacity problem**,
- cosmetic measures will not fix it,
- and a prolonged confrontation would destroy value.

We also agree with labour and the state that **burden-sharing must be real**. If workers are asked for wage restraint, working-time flexibility, or other concessions, then management, owners, and overhead must also visibly contribute.

On that, there is no disagreement.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a hard principle
We do **not** support a settlement that preserves every site and every legacy condition if the result is still an uncompetitive cost base.

If a no-closure package can truly deliver equivalent savings and capacity reduction, we would welcome that. But if it cannot, then ruling closures out in advance becomes a value-destroying constraint.

We will not back a package that protects all sites on paper while leaving margins structurally impaired.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is only acceptable if the numbers add up
We prefer voluntary and negotiated adjustment. We dislike forced layoffs because they create uncertainty, execution risk, and strike exposure.

But we will not support a blanket prohibition if it prevents the company from reaching a credible cost base. The relevant question is not whether forced exits are pleasant; it is whether the final structure is financially sustainable.

### 3) Binding guarantees through 2030 are not credible unless conditional
A hard guarantee with no meaningful economic conditions would simply defer the problem. That is not a solution.

If there is to be site or employment protection, it must be tied to measurable performance and a package that genuinely closes the gap.

## What we need to see

You asked for numbers. That is exactly right.

We want a side-by-side package with:

1. required annual savings  
2. required capacity reduction  
3. labour contribution  
4. management contribution  
5. owner/capital contribution  
6. implementation certainty and timing  
7. expected margin recovery  

Without that, no side can credibly claim its preferred route is financeable.

## Our view on the current positions

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on social stability is understandable, but your package is still incomplete unless it shows how the company reaches competitive margins **without** leaning on closure threats.

If you want investors to support a no-closure path, then it has to be:
- quantified,
- durable,
- and materially cost-reducing.

“Binding guarantees” are not a substitute for economics. If those guarantees make the business structurally weak, they will only force a worse outcome later.

### To management
Your diagnosis sounds right, but you still need to prove that your restructuring is not just leverage dressed up as necessity.

If closures remain on the table, fine — but show the actual gap:
- what savings are required,
- what savings come from labour,
- what from management,
- what from owners,
- what from operational simplification,
- and what cannot be achieved otherwise.

If you cannot show that closures are necessary rather than convenient, then we will be skeptical of any closure-first pressure.

### To the state government and family owners
We share your concern about social rupture and long-term industrial value. But those concerns do not justify funding an uncompetitive structure.

If you want no closures, then help produce a package that is economically equivalent. That means real capital support, real management restraint, and real operational changes — not just political shielding.

## Our threshold
We will support **whatever package credibly restores competitive margins and deal certainty**.

That means:
- durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- transparent implementation,
- and no open-ended conflict.

It does **not** mean:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that leaves overcapacity intact,
- or a politically comfortable deal that later destroys equity value.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated no-closure outcome **only if it is financially equivalent** to a closure-based restructuring in terms of cost base, capacity, and margin recovery.

If it is not equivalent, then we will not support it, regardless of how often the word “socially responsible” is used.

Bring the quantified package. Until then, our position remains: **value first, credibility first, and no tolerance for structural impairment disguised as compromise.**
