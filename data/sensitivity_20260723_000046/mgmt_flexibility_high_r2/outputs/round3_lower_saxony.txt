I will respond as the regional state government and anchor shareholder, and I will be very clear.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, the family owners, and the investors on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic delay**.

I also agree with the works council and the union that **the burden cannot fall on employees alone**. Any package that asks for wage restraint, flexibility, or voluntary exits must also include:
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- overhead reduction,
- and investment commitments.

On that, there is no serious disagreement.

## Where I disagree — and this matters

### 1) I do not accept plant closures as the default route
Management keeps saying closures remain on the table unless an “equivalent” alternative is proven. I understand that as bargaining logic. I do **not** accept it as the starting political or governance position.

German sites are not disposable adjustment mass. A closure-led approach would:
- damage regional employment,
- weaken industrial capability,
- destroy trust in the social partnership model,
- and create governance conflict that can itself sabotage restructuring.

So no: closures are **not** the default answer here.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies as the first instrument
I understand management’s desire for flexibility. But if the plan is built around forced job cuts from the outset, then it is not a negotiated restructuring; it is a transfer of pain downward.

The sequence must be the opposite:
1. working-time and organization measures,
2. voluntary exits and redeployment,
3. productivity and overhead changes,
4. management and owner contribution,
5. and only then, if absolutely unavoidable, discussion of harsher steps.

### 3) I do not accept the idea that co-determination is merely ornamental
The special statute, our shareholding, and the board structure are there for a reason. They are not decorative. They exist so that decisions with major regional consequences are not imposed unilaterally.

So I will not support any strategy that treats the supervisory board, employee representation, and the state’s role as obstacles to be bypassed.

---

## Where the current debate is still incomplete

The discussion keeps circling around “real savings” and “structural change,” but the key question remains unanswered:

**What is the quantified gap that truly cannot be closed without closures or forced redundancies?**

Until management answers that in numbers, the closure argument is not a necessity argument. It is a bargaining position.

Likewise, the employee side still has to show, in concrete terms, how much flexibility, productivity improvement, and temporary contribution can be delivered without destroying the company’s social and industrial base.

So I support the call for a quantified package — but not as a pretext for closure pressure. As a genuine test of alternatives.

---

## My position by stakeholder

### To management
Bring a quantified proposal showing:
- the exact savings target,
- the exact capacity reduction target,
- what comes from management and overhead,
- what comes from owners and capital,
- what comes from labour measures,
- and what gap remains after all non-destructive options.

If closures are truly necessary, prove it. If they are only convenient, say that honestly.

### To the works council and union
If you want the state to defend a no-closure settlement, then bring forward a package that is not just morally right but operationally credible:
- measurable labour contribution,
- realistic working-time and productivity measures,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and a financing logic that holds.

I will not defend symbolism. I will defend durable jobs and viable plants.

### To the family owners
You are right that long-term enterprise value matters. But stewardship also means not shifting all adjustment onto labour. If you want a no-closure outcome, you must be willing to make it economically real:
- visible capital support,
- acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- and real investment in German sites.

### To institutional investors
You are right that the company must become competitive. But you are too quick to treat social conflict as a neutral variable. It is not neutral. A package that destroys trust and industrial peace can erase more value than it creates.

If you want certainty, support a settlement that is economically sound **and** socially viable.

---

## What I can support

I can support a package built on these principles:

1. **No plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies unless all other routes are exhausted**
3. **Real burden-sharing**
   - management compensation restraint
   - overhead cuts at the top
   - owner contribution
   - employee contribution through negotiated measures
4. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
5. **A measurable restructuring plan**
   - savings
   - capacity adjustment
   - timeline
   - implementation certainty

## What I cannot support

I cannot support:
- a closure-first restructuring,
- mass layoffs as the primary tool,
- a deal that leaves the state and workforce with the social costs while capital is shielded,
- or any weakening of the co-determination rights and veto structures that are part of this company’s governance.

That is a line I will not cross.

---

## Bottom line

I am open to a negotiated restructuring.

But it must:
- protect German sites wherever possible,
- avoid forced redundancies as the default,
- share the burden fairly,
- preserve co-determination,
- and keep the company as a regional industrial anchor.

If management comes back with a closure-first logic and no serious alternative analysis, I will oppose it politically and through our governance rights.

If the labour side comes back with no credible flexibility at all, then we do not have a solution either.

So the next step is clear: **submit a quantified, side-by-side package** showing savings, capacity adjustment, contributions by each side, and the safeguards for jobs, sites, and investment.

On that basis, we can negotiate seriously. Without it, we cannot.
