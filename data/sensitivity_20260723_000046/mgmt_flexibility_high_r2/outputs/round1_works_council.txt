As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

The workforce did not create this crisis, and we will not accept that its solution is paid for by destroying jobs, closing sites, and breaking trust. The company has a responsibility to secure its future **without sacrificing German plants and thousands of livelihoods**.

What we are prepared to discuss are **alternative restructuring measures** that genuinely preserve sites and jobs, such as:
- capacity adjustments,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- negotiated efficiency measures,
- internal redeployment,
- and a fair contribution from all sides, including management and capital.

But if management comes here with a fait accompli, with closures or forced layoffs already decided, then there is no basis for trust. In that case, we will resist this politically, socially, and through the supervisory board and works-council instruments available to us.

Any acceptable solution must include:
1. **Binding guarantees for all German sites**
2. **Job security through 2030**
3. **A real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the restructuring**
4. **A fair burden-sharing approach that does not put the whole adjustment on employees**

We are ready to negotiate seriously. But we will not negotiate away the existence of plants or the jobs of the people who built this company.
