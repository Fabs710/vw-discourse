## On the facilitator’s three proposals

### 1) Conditional no-closure moratorium
I would support this **only** if it is a genuine moratorium and not a countdown to closure.

That means:
- no plant closures during the agreed period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a jointly governed process where management, owners, and capital must first deliver their contributions before any further discussion.

If the “hard review trigger” is just a hidden closure clause, then no — that is not acceptable.

### 2) No forced layoffs first, staged escalation ladder
This is closer to a serious approach, but only if the ladder truly starts with:
- management cuts,
- owner contribution,
- overhead cuts,
- productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits.

And I stress: “escalation” cannot mean that once the first few measures are uncomfortable, closure suddenly becomes the path of least resistance. It must remain a last resort only after every alternative has been exhausted and proven insufficient.

### 3) Site-security package tied to commitments
This is the only route that could become genuinely serious, because it recognizes what matters: **binding site and job security** in exchange for real contributions.

But let me be precise:
- site security must be more than selective PR protection,
- no compulsory redundancies must remain excluded,
- and no closure should be used as a bargaining threat against other sites.

If the package protects only some plants and abandons others, then it does not solve the core issue. It just redistributes fear.
