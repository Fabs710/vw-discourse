I will be equally direct.

## My position has not changed
A restructuring that depends on:
- **plant closures,**
- **compulsory redundancies,**
- or the **dismantling of the job-security framework**

is not an acceptable starting point for us. It remains a hard line.

If management, owners, or investors want a serious agreement, they must first prove why these destructive tools are allegedly unavoidable. So far, that proof has **not** been provided.

---

## Where I agree

### 1) A cosmetic deal is useless
Yes. I agree with everyone on that. We do need:
- measurable savings,
- durable changes,
- and a package that actually restores competitiveness.

### 2) Burden-sharing must be real
Yes. It cannot be workers alone. If employees are asked for concessions, then management, owners, and capital must also contribute visibly and materially.

### 3) We need numbers
Yes. I welcome the move to quantified package design. But numbers must be used to test alternatives, not to dress up a closure agenda as “inevitable.”

---

## Where I strongly disagree

### 1) Management has not shown that closures are necessary
You keep saying “overcapacity” and “structural adjustment,” but you still have **not** demonstrated that a no-closure solution cannot close the gap.

If closures are truly necessary, then show:
- the exact savings target,
- the exact capacity reduction target,
- how much comes from labour,
- how much from management,
- how much from owners,
- how much from procurement, overhead, and productivity,
- and what gap remains after all non-destructive measures.

Until that is done, closure is not necessity. It is a bargaining weapon.

### 2) Investors are treating social destruction as a neutral financial variable
I disagree with the idea that a “financially equivalent” package can be assessed without properly counting the costs of:
- industrial conflict,
- broken trust,
- strike escalation,
- loss of skills,
- reputational damage,
- and long-term erosion of co-determination-based stability.

You all talk about valuation, but a forced restructuring that blows up labour relations can destroy more value than it creates.

### 3) The state is right to reject closures, but not yet forcefully enough
I appreciate the state’s position, but let me be clear: the state should not merely say closures are undesirable. It should use its governance power to **prevent** a closure-led solution unless there is absolutely no alternative.

And there is still no evidence there is no alternative.

---

## What a real alternative must contain

If management wants us even to consider an adjustment package, then it must be built on these principles:

### Non-negotiable for us
- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Security of employment through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination rights**
- **Protection of the German industrial footprint**

### Possible instruments we are prepared to discuss
- temporary working-time reductions,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- bonus restraint,
- deferred compensation,
- management compensation restraint,
- overhead cuts,
- owner contributions,
- investment commitments in German sites.

But let me stress: these are not a license to destroy jobs. They are the means by which jobs are protected.

---

## To management

You say you are open on instruments but not on outcomes that leave the company uncompetitive. Fine. Then prove that **competitiveness and site security are not in conflict**.

What I expect from you is not a threat, but a credible calculation:
- How much can be saved without closures?
- What capacity reduction can be achieved without closing plants?
- What is the real cost of each site compared with the cost of destroying it?
- How much of your own top-line structure are you cutting?
- What are owners prepared to inject?
- What are investors prepared to absorb in lower near-term returns?

If you cannot show that a no-closure package is insufficient, then you are not making a case — you are making an ultimatum.

---

## To investors

You say you support whatever restores margins and deal certainty. But certainty built on coercion is fragile.

A package that humiliates the workforce and signals that historic guarantees can be broken whenever pressure rises will not create sustainable certainty. It will create future conflict.

If you want credibility, then support a package that:
- avoids closures,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- and includes binding commitments from management and owners.

Otherwise, you are simply asking labour to underwrite all the downside while capital stays protected.

---

## To the family owners

You say you are open to a no-closure outcome if it is genuinely equivalent. Good. Then help make it equivalent by putting real weight behind it:
- capital support,
- acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- investment in German sites,
- and a refusal to use closure threats as leverage against the workforce.

If owners want stewardship, then stewardship means protecting the productive base, not squeezing it until it breaks.

---

## To the state government

You are right that regional stability matters. But I need more than caution. I need political force behind the no-closure line.

This is not a small site issue. This is about:
- tens of thousands of livelihoods,
- the future of industrial skills,
- and the credibility of co-determination in Germany.

If closures are normalized here, then every guarantee becomes conditional on the next crisis. That we cannot accept.

---

## The real test
You keep asking for a side-by-side numerical package. Fine.

Here is our test:

A package is only worth discussing if it shows all of the following:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job security through 2030**
4. **Real management restraint**
5. **Real owner contribution**
6. **Measurable productivity and working-time measures**
7. **A credible investment plan for German sites**

If the numbers do not work under those conditions, then the correct answer is not “close plants anyway.” The correct answer is: **find another restructuring model**.

---

## My conclusion
So I will not soften this.

- I do **not** accept closures as a normal restructuring tool.
- I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as a necessary instrument.
- I do **not** accept that job security can simply be traded away because management wants faster leverage.
- I do **not** accept a deal that shifts structural risk entirely onto the workforce.

If management wants a settlement, it must come with a **credible no-closure, no-forced-layoff, co-determined restructuring package** that is properly financed and that protects German plants to 2030.

Until then, our answer remains: **no closures, no forced layoffs, no dismantling of worker protections.**
