## Core impasse
The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **the mechanism and the guarantee**:

1. **Management / investors / family** want a package that is **economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring** and backed by a **credible, executable fallback**.
2. **Works council / union** want **no closures and no compulsory redundancies** as binding protections, plus job and site security.
3. **State government** wants **site preservation and co-determination preserved**, and resists any solution where closures become the default benchmark.

So the conflict sits at one point:  
**Can the parties agree on a package that removes enough cost and capacity, while preserving sites and avoiding forced layoffs, with a fallback everyone accepts as real?**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals
These are procedural bridge options, not judgments.

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure trial with automatic numeric trigger
A jointly agreed **12–18 month no-closure phase** with:
- quantified savings targets,
- site-by-site capacity reductions,
- a defined headcount reduction path through voluntary exits/attrition,
- and a pre-agreed trigger that automatically reopens structural options if targets are missed.

This may bridge:
- labour/state demands for site preservation,
- investor/family demands for enforceable economics.

### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with partitioned risk
A **hybrid settlement** that combines:
- immediate non-closure measures,
- hard recurring savings,
- shift/line rationalization,
- investment commitments,
- and a legally defined fallback that is limited to pre-specified sites or functions if milestones fail.

This may bridge:
- management/investor demands for credibility,
- works council/state demands to avoid blanket closure logic.

### Proposal 3: No-closure package plus independent verification and staged escalation
A **no-closure package** subject to:
- independent monitoring by the restructuring expert,
- quarterly public progress reviews,
- and a staged escalation ladder if performance misses targets:
  1. more labour-cost relief,
  2. deeper capacity reductions,
  3. reopening of structural measures.

This may bridge:
- the desire to avoid immediate closures,
- the demand for enforceable discipline and measurable delivery.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact recurring annual EBITDA improvement, by site and by measure, do you require for the package to be acceptable without closures?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a no-closure or hybrid package if it meets a pre-agreed NPV and margin threshold, even if the mechanism is not closure-based?**

### To the Controlling Family
**What fallback would you personally regard as politically and governance-wise executable if the no-closure trial misses its targets?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What level of capacity reduction and labour-cost relief would you accept as sufficient if no plant closes, and would you support automatic consequences if those numbers are missed?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What quantified combination of wage restraint, flexibility, voluntary exits, and capacity reduction are you prepared to table if closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table initially?**

### To the Works Council
**Which site- and employment-protection guarantees would make you prepared to support measurable capacity reductions without demanding a blanket no-closure promise?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum threshold of recurring savings and capacity reduction would you define as the line between a genuine restructuring and a delayed compromise?**
