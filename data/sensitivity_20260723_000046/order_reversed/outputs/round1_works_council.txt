I’ll be equally direct.

We are not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that destroys sites, tears up job security, and then asks the works council to call that “responsible transformation.” It is not.

### Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one point: **this company needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one**.  
If the German footprint is too expensive and too fragmented, then the answer cannot be endless postponement. I also agree that prolonged conflict is bad for everyone.

### Where I fundamentally disagree
I reject the idea that the only credible solution is **plant closures and compulsory redundancies**.

That is exactly the line management is trying to normalize: first declare the current model “uncompetitive,” then make jobs and sites the adjustment variable, then present that as inevitability. We have seen this logic before, and we do not accept it.

The workforce did not create the strategic errors, market shifts, or investment choices. It is unacceptable to treat workers as the easiest line item to cut.

### To management
You say closures are just a tool. In practice, they are a threat against the workforce to force concessions. Let me be clear:  
**No German plant closure and no forced layoffs.**  
That is not a bargaining tactic; it is the line you are running into.

If you want a serious negotiation, then bring a package that shows how you achieve the needed savings through:
- capacity reductions without site closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- genuine management contributions,
- reductions in temporary and agency use first,
- and a fair employee contribution only within a framework that protects jobs.

You cannot demand “durability” from labour while offering only insecurity in return.

### To the investors
Your insistence on real savings is understandable. But “equivalent economics” cannot mean that you get the same financial result by simply destroying the industrial base. If a negotiated alternative delivers comparable savings without closures, that is not a cosmetic deal — that is a better deal.

What you should not support is a short-term cost-cutting programme that permanently damages trust, skills, and production capability.

### To the controlling family
You say closures are not an end in themselves. Good. Then stop treating them as the default instrument. If ownership wants a durable future, then ownership must help secure that future through:
- binding job guarantees to 2030,
- binding site guarantees,
- investment commitments,
- and a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring.

Without that, you are asking the workforce to accept all the risk and none of the security.

### To the regional state government
We appreciate that you are not simply endorsing mass job destruction. But let us be precise: “balanced” cannot mean that the workforce loses job security while everyone else keeps optionality.

If the state wants to protect the industrial base, then it should back a settlement that explicitly rules out:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures,
- and a one-sided cost shift onto labour.

### Our position
We are prepared to negotiate seriously on a package that includes:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time flexibility,
- a temporary and negotiated employee contribution,
- productivity measures,
- and investment commitments.

But these are only acceptable if the settlement also contains:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job guarantees to 2030**
- **a real co-determination role**
- and protection of the collective bargaining and social partnership framework

That is the only basis on which the works council can even consider a settlement.

If management insists on keeping closures and forced layoffs on the table, then do not expect cooperation. You will get resistance — at the plants, in the boardroom, and publicly. We are already prepared to mobilize the workforce, and we will do so again if necessary.

We want a future for this company. But not at the price of sacrificing the people who built it.
