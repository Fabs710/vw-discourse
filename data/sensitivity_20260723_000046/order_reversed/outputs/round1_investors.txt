Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable return to competitive margins** and a **material reduction in the cost base**.

What matters to us:

- **No cosmetic settlement.** We will not support an outcome that merely postpones the problem while preserving structurally uncompetitive German costs.
- **Decisive action on capacity and labour costs.** If the current footprint is too large, then capacity must come down. If labour costs are above peers, they must be reduced meaningfully.
- **Deal certainty over prolonged conflict.** Extended strikes or a drawn-out confrontation destroy value quickly. We prefer a negotiated solution, but only if it is strong enough to restore competitiveness.
- **Durability.** Any agreement must be credible beyond the next quarter or bargaining cycle. Temporary savings are not enough.

We are open on the exact mechanism. We do not insist on plant closures as a matter of principle, but we do insist on **equivalent economics**: if closures are avoided, the alternative must still produce the same scale of savings, the same capacity adjustment, and the same improvement in returns.

What we would argue for:
- A restructuring package with **clear quantified savings**
- **Capacity reduction** aligned with demand
- **Lower structural labour costs**
- A **credible implementation timetable**
- Protection of future investment only if it is tied to real cost discipline

What we would argue against:
- Any deal that preserves all sites and most jobs while claiming victory
- Any agreement that relies on vague productivity promises without enforceable savings
- Any prolonged strike strategy that erodes cash, margins, and market confidence

In short: we back the path that most convincingly restores competitiveness and shareholder value. If management’s proposal is the only route that achieves that, we support it. If there is a negotiated alternative with the same financial impact and better certainty, we would accept that instead.
