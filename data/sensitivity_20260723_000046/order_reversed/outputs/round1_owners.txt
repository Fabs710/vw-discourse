We broadly agree with the capital-markets view on the **objective**: this company needs a durable restoration of competitiveness, not a cosmetic truce.

Where I align:
- **Margins and cost base matter.** The current German cost structure cannot remain untouched if it leaves the core brand below target.
- **Durability matters.** A deal that only buys a few months of calm while preserving structural overcapacity would be value-destructive.
- **Prolonged conflict is unacceptable.** A drawn-out strike or public escalation would damage the enterprise, the workforce, and the owners.

Where I am not prepared to simply endorse management’s deepest-cut version:
- **Plant closures are not an end in themselves.** They may be justified if there is no other way to achieve equivalent economics, but I will not support closure as a symbolic demonstration of toughness.
- **Value destruction through instability is not acceptable.** If the restructuring becomes a confrontation that undermines the brand, disrupts supply, or damages investment capacity, that is not a success.
- **Governance constraints are real.** Any outcome must be legally and politically executable within the company’s co-determination and special-law structure. A paper plan that cannot pass the board or survive labour resistance is not credible.

So our test is simple: **show us a package that materially and durably reduces cost and capacity, with implementation certainty**. If that requires closures, then those must be justified by the economics. If the same result can be achieved through alternative measures — capacity cuts, working-time changes, labour-cost concessions, and enforceable productivity gains — that is preferable **only if the savings are equivalent and durable**.

What we will not support:
- a settlement that protects every site and most jobs while pretending the problem is solved;
- vague promises of future efficiency without hard numbers and enforcement;
- a strategy that relies on endless confrontation to force concessions.

In short: we back a **serious restructuring**, not a symbolic one. If management’s plan is the only credible route to the required savings, then it deserves support. If labour and management can deliver the same financial effect without closures, we are open — but the burden of proof is high.
