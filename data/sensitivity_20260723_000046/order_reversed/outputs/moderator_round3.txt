## Core impasse
The dispute is no longer about whether the company needs restructuring. The impasse is about **what counts as a credible restructuring instrument**:

- **Management / investors / family** want a package that proves, in hard numbers, it can restore margins and cost competitiveness quickly and durably, with a real fallback if targets are missed.
- **Union / works council** want that reset to be achieved **without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**, with binding job and site security.
- **State government** wants competitiveness restored **without dismantling the regional industrial base or weakening co-determination**.

So the core block is this:  
**Can the required savings and capacity reduction be delivered through non-closure measures that are credible enough for capital, yet protective enough for labour and the state?**

A second layer of impasse is **enforcement**: even where stakeholders accept a no-closure or hybrid path in principle, they do not agree on what happens if delivery fails.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled as procedural compromises.

### Compromise 1: Quantified hybrid with a hard backstop
A time-limited package that:
- preserves current sites for a defined period,
- delivers specified savings through shift reduction, working-time changes, voluntary departures, bonus restraint, and productivity measures,
- includes site-specific capacity targets,
- and has a **pre-agreed review date**.

If targets are missed, the parties automatically move to a defined fallback menu, which could include deeper site rationalization or other structural measures.

**Why it could bridge:**  
It gives investors a credible enforcement mechanism while giving labour and the state a chance to prove that non-closure measures can deliver.

---

### Compromise 2: No-closure first phase with measurable equivalence test
An initial phase with:
- no immediate closures,
- no compulsory redundancies for a fixed period,
- and a strictly quantified “equivalence test” against closure economics.

At the review point, the parties compare:
- recurring savings achieved,
- capacity removed,
- labour-cost reductions,
- and implementation certainty.

If the package is short of the agreed threshold, the parties reopen the structural options.

**Why it could bridge:**  
It avoids an immediate closure decision while not pretending that a weak package is acceptable.

---

### Compromise 3: Site-preserving restructuring with variable governance triggers
A deal that keeps all German plants open for now, but:
- assigns each site a required contribution,
- links future investment to delivery milestones,
- and creates automatic governance triggers if performance slips.

For example:
- if a site misses savings or utilization targets, additional shift reductions or product reallocation are triggered;
- if the company overperforms, the job/security commitments stay in force.

**Why it could bridge:**  
It gives the state and works council site protection, while giving management and investors a mechanism to prevent open-ended underdelivery.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact annual recurring savings and capacity reduction do you require, by date, for a non-closure package to be acceptable in your view?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a no-closure package if it met a pre-agreed quantified threshold for savings, capacity reduction, and enforceability, even if it did not include closures?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Are you willing to back a hybrid or no-closure solution with a real backstop, even if that means accepting a later structural trigger that could reopen site decisions?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Would you support a package that preserves all sites for now but includes measurable capacity cuts, enforceable milestones, and automatic consequences if delivery fails?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What specific combination of wage, time, flexibility, voluntary departure, and productivity measures would you table that you believe can realistically match the required savings without closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Works Council
**Which site-preserving adjustments are you prepared to bind into a measurable package, and what would you accept as an objective test that the package has delivered enough?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum mix of recurring savings, capacity reduction, and implementation certainty would you set as the threshold for calling a no-closure package economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring?**
