### Core impasse
The discourse has narrowed to a single central conflict:

**Whether the required cost and capacity reset can be achieved without plant closures and compulsory redundancies.**

- **Investors / family / management** are saying: if the savings are not structurally equivalent, closures remain on the table.
- **Union / works council / state** are saying: closures and forced layoffs are not acceptable starting assumptions; any restructuring must preserve sites, jobs, and co-determination as far as possible.
- **The expert** has clarified that a no-closure package is possible in principle, but only if it is deep, quantified, enforceable, and fast enough.

So the plateau is not about whether change is needed. It is about **which instruments are legitimate and credible enough to deliver it**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridging positions that could move the talks forward:

#### 1) **Hybrid restructuring with conditional closure deferral**
- No immediate plant closure decision.
- A quantified package of capacity reduction, shift elimination, and labor-cost concessions is implemented first.
- A jointly agreed review point is set at a fixed date.
- If savings targets are not met, a pre-agreed fallback restructuring path, including site rationalization, automatically reopens.

**Bridge function:** Gives labour/state a chance to avoid closures now, while giving investors/management an enforceable backstop.

---

#### 2) **Site-preserving adjustment package with hard economics**
- One or more plants remain open, but each is assigned a binding reduction in shifts, volumes, or product lines.
- Employee-side contributions include wage moderation, working-time flexibility, and removal of selected legacy protections.
- Management/owners add investment commitments and governance safeguards.
- The package includes milestones, verification, and explicit remedies for underdelivery.

**Bridge function:** Preserves sites while still forcing real, measurable capacity and cost reduction.

---

#### 3) **Phased settlement with voluntary workforce reduction first**
- Immediate use of voluntary departures, early retirement, redeployment, and retirement bridge programs.
- Temporary labor-cost moderation and flexibility measures.
- Closure remains formally deferred while the company tests whether the cost base can be reset sufficiently without it.
- If voluntary and structural measures fall short, a second-phase structural review is triggered.

**Bridge function:** Reduces confrontation now and creates a measurable test of whether non-closure measures are sufficient.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To **Management**
What exact cost and capacity target must be achieved for you to say a no-closure package is sufficient, and which elements of that target are non-negotiable?

#### To **Institutional Investors**
If a package delivers the same quantified savings and capacity reduction through non-closure means, would you commit not to insist on closures as a matter of principle?

#### To **Controlling Family Holding**
What concrete governance support are you prepared to provide for a no-closure package if it includes binding savings, milestone triggers, and a fallback mechanism?

#### To **Regional State Government**
If a settlement preserves sites and jobs but requires substantial wage and flexibility concessions, what level of restructuring would you consider politically and legally supportable?

#### To **Industrial Union**
Which specific non-closure measures are you prepared to put on the table now, and what measurable savings would you accept as the threshold for calling the package credible?

#### To **Works Council**
What site-level capacity reductions, redeployments, or working-time changes are you willing to negotiate if they are tied to binding no-closure guarantees?

#### To **Expert**
What minimum package components would you require to judge a no-closure alternative as genuinely economically equivalent rather than merely delaying restructuring?
