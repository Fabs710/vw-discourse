I agree on one point: **this cannot be a cosmetic settlement**. If we leave the German cost base structurally uncompetitive, then we will simply defer the crisis and weaken the brand further.

But I need to be very clear where I disagree.

### On the core issue
Management’s diagnosis is not a talking point; it is a financial reality. We have:
- **excess capacity**
- **labour costs above target**
- a **shrinking European market**
- and a brand that is **missing its return-on-sales target**

That combination is not survivable with incremental tweaks. If we do not make a **durable structural correction**, we will not have the funds to invest in future products, battery tech, software, and the 2030 portfolio.

### To the investors
You are right to demand **equivalent economics** and not accept vague productivity promises. I share that view. But “equivalent” has to mean real, measurable, and enforceable savings — not aspirational language about flexibility or future efficiency. If the package does not reduce cost and capacity in a way that materially changes the P&L, it is not enough.

### To the controlling family
You are right that plant closures are not an end in themselves. I am not treating them as symbolic. They are a tool. If the economics and implementation certainty require closures, then they have to stay on the table. What I will not accept is a political preference for preserving every site if that leaves the business structurally uncompetitive.

### To the regional state government
I understand the concern about regional employment and the industrial base. But we cannot pretend that protecting all locations at all costs is compatible with long-term job security. If we fail to act now, the consequence will be slower decline, weaker investment, and eventually more damage to the workforce and the region. That is not a responsible alternative.

### Where I stand
My position remains this:

- **We need a deep, durable reduction in cost and capacity**
- **We need a genuine labour-cost contribution**
- **We need implementation certainty**
- **We need a result that restores medium-term margins**

I am open on the mechanism:
- closures,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- pay adjustments,
- removal of legacy guarantees,
- or a combination.

But the package must achieve the required scale. If it does not, I will say plainly: **it is not sufficient**.

### What I will not accept
- a deal that preserves the current overcapacity and calls it a restructuring
- temporary savings that vanish at the next bargaining round
- commitments without enforceability
- a settlement that protects the status quo while draining funds from future investment

We are under time pressure. Prolonged strikes are bad, yes — but a weak compromise is worse. If labour and co-determination stakeholders want to avoid closures, then they must bring forward a package that delivers the same structural effect. Otherwise, management will continue to press for closures and substantial job reductions, because that is what the company’s competitiveness requires.
