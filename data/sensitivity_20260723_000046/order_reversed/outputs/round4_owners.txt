We are at the stage where the discussion must be judged against one criterion only: **does it produce a durable, enforceable, value-preserving reset?**

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the expert on the core economic point:
- this cannot be a cosmetic deal,
- the current cost base is not competitive,
- and a prolonged strike would destroy value.

I also agree with the state and the works council that a deal which is not governable in practice is worthless. Any package has to fit the legal and co-determination reality.

## Where I disagree
### With labour and works council
I do **not** accept “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as fixed premises.  
If those constraints make it impossible to reach the required savings and capacity reduction, then they are not protective principles — they are a veto on restructuring.

I also do not accept the claim that because a no-closure package is possible in principle, it is therefore sufficient. The test is not theoretical possibility. The test is whether it is **fast, deep, and durable enough** to restore margins.

### With the state government
I understand the political and regional concern. But I do **not** accept any framing in which preserving every site becomes more important than preserving the enterprise as a competitive industrial asset.

A company that cannot fund its future will not remain a regional anchor. So I support site sensitivity, not site protection at any price.

## My position
The family holding supports a **serious restructuring that restores competitiveness, protects long-term value, and avoids unnecessary instability**.

That means:
- no cosmetic compromise,
- no open-ended conflict,
- no protection of excess capacity for its own sake,
- and no settlement that permanently weakens the company’s governance or economics.

I am open on the instrument, but not on the outcome.

## On the current bridge options
### 1) Quantified hybrid with a hard backstop
This is the most plausible route **if and only if** the fallback is real.

I would support a deferral only if the package contains:
- hard quantified savings targets,
- binding capacity reduction,
- a fixed review date,
- and a consequence if delivery fails.

If the fallback is politically impossible to execute, then this is just delay.

### 2) No-closure first phase with equivalence test
This can be acceptable only as a **test**, not as a permanent exemption from hard decisions.

If the equivalence threshold is pre-agreed, the time horizon is short, and failure reopens structural options automatically, then it is worth trying.

If not, it becomes postponement.

### 3) Site-preserving restructuring with governance triggers
This is acceptable only if the triggers are automatic and meaningful.

If the triggers merely create another round of bargaining later, they are not safeguards. They are delay.

## What would move me
I would support a package that shows, in hard numbers:
- annual recurring savings,
- capacity reduction by site and line,
- labor-cost contribution,
- headcount reduction path,
- implementation timetable,
- and a binding backstop if targets are missed.

And I would insist on:
- no indefinite renegotiation,
- no vague productivity promises,
- and no settlement that leaves the company trapped in its current cost structure.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a settlement that preserves the current footprint while calling it transformation,
- temporary concessions that simply defer the problem,
- a deal that burdens only workers while management and owners make no visible contribution,
- or any arrangement that undermines the company’s long-term investment capacity.

## On the balance between economics and stability
Stability matters, but it cannot be used to protect an uncompetitive structure. The right objective is not avoiding all pain; it is avoiding **value-destructive instability while restoring competitiveness**.

That is why I would support:
- a real hybrid,
- a real no-closure package if it truly matches the economics,
- or, if necessary, a closure-based plan if no equivalent alternative can be made credible.

But I will not support a weak compromise that preserves every site, preserves every guarantee, and leaves the business structurally impaired.

## Bottom line
The family holding’s position is unchanged, but now it is precise:

- **We want a durable competitiveness reset.**
- **We will support a negotiated solution if it is economically equivalent and enforceable.**
- **We will not block necessary restructuring just to preserve appearances.**
- **We will also not accept a weak compromise that sacrifices long-term value.**

So the next step should be a quantified comparison of:
1. closure,
2. hybrid,
3. and true no-closure,

including:
- recurring savings,
- one-off costs,
- implementation risk,
- and impact on margins and future investment.
