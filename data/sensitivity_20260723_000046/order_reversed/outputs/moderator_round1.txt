### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the current situation cannot be resolved by a cosmetic or short-term settlement.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs a **durable competitiveness reset** with real cost reduction and some form of capacity adjustment.
- All sides agree that a **prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would be damaging**.
- There is shared acceptance that any solution must be **credible, enforceable, and legally executable** within the company’s governance framework.
- Several parties are open to **non-closure mechanisms** if they achieve equivalent financial and operational effects.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Management keeps closures on the table; labour, works council, and the state government oppose them as a default tool; investors and the family will accept them only if economically necessary.
- **Job security vs. cost reduction:** Union and works council insist on no compulsory redundancies and strong site/job guarantees; management and investors insist on meaningful structural change that may require deeper labour adjustment.
- **Distribution of burden:** Labour wants shared sacrifice across management, owners, and workers; management and investors focus on the scale of required savings; the state government wants social and regional protection.
- **Meaning of “equivalent economics”:** Investors and management mean savings and capacity cuts that are real and durable; labour and works council mean equivalent savings achieved without closures or forced layoffs.
- **Governance constraints:** The state government and works council emphasize co-determination and political/legal feasibility, while management emphasizes urgency and implementation certainty.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The exact balance between **site preservation** and **capacity reduction**.
- Whether the required savings can be delivered through **working-time changes, flexibility, productivity, and pay concessions** instead of closures.
- What level of **employee contribution** would be acceptable, and whether management/owners will make visible counter-contributions.
- Whether any package can include **binding job and site guarantees** while still delivering the scale of cost reduction demanded by investors and management.
- How to reconcile **speed and certainty** with **social acceptability and governance constraints**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single quantified options set**: each side should table what savings, capacity reduction, and implementation guarantees they consider acceptable.
- Test **two or three concrete restructuring models** side by side: closure-based, no-closure, and hybrid.
- Clarify the **minimum non-negotiables** on each side, especially on closures, redundancies, and job/site guarantees.
- Ask management to specify the **required financial target** in measurable terms, and ask labour/state representatives to specify the **alternative measures** that could credibly meet that target.
- Keep the discussion focused on **durable economics plus executable governance**, not general statements of intent.
