We agree with the investors on the central test: **this must restore margins and competitiveness in a durable, measurable way**. A cosmetic peace that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive would destroy value, not protect it.

Where I also agree with labour and the state:
- **A prolonged strike is bad for the company, the workforce, and the owners.**
- **Any solution must be legally and governance-feasible.**
- **A negotiated alternative is preferable if it truly delivers equivalent economics.**

But I need to be clear about the limit of our flexibility.

### On “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies”
Those cannot be treated as absolute starting assumptions if they block a real restructuring. If the company’s economics require capacity to come out, then someone has to explain how that happens without closures or job reductions. We will not support a package that preserves the footprint and calls that transformation.

### On job and site guarantees
Binding guarantees to 2030 may be useful only if they are compatible with a credible cost reset. If they become a blanket veto on change, then they are not a stability tool — they are a mechanism for preserving overcapacity. That would be unacceptable.

### On the burden of proof
The burden is now on labour and the state to show a **quantified, enforceable alternative** if they want to avoid closures. General references to flexibility, productivity, and shared sacrifice are not enough. We need:
- concrete capacity reductions,
- measurable labour-cost relief,
- enforceable productivity commitments,
- and a timetable that delivers savings soon enough to matter.

If that package exists, we can support it through governance. If it does not, then management’s restructuring logic remains the benchmark.

### What we will not support
- a settlement that protects every site and most jobs while pretending the economics are fixed;
- temporary concessions that expire before the next cycle;
- vague promises without enforcement;
- or a prolonged confrontation that erodes value and damages the brand.

So my position is unchanged: **we support a serious restructuring, not a symbolic one**. If closures are not needed, prove it with equivalent economics. If they are needed, then we will not block necessary action just to preserve appearances.
