We share the basic diagnosis: this company needs a durable competitive reset, not a cosmetic pause. A structurally weak cost base, excess capacity, and prolonged uncertainty would damage the brand, the balance sheet, and ultimately the jobs we all want to preserve.

Where I agree with both of you:
- **Durability matters.** A short-lived truce that just kicks the can down the road is not acceptable.
- **Competitiveness must be restored.** There has to be a real contribution to cost reduction and capacity adjustment.
- **A prolonged conflict is harmful.** A strike spiral would hurt workers, suppliers, and the company’s investment capacity.

Where I draw a clear line:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not a default instrument.** They may only be discussed if there is no credible alternative. We will not accept closures simply to signal toughness to the markets.
- **No settlement that guts regional employment.** The company is a regional economic anchor. A restructuring that leaves lasting industrial devastation in the state is not a responsible outcome.
- **No sidelining of the state’s role or co-determination.** The special-law blocking minority and our supervisory-board role are not decorative. Any path that tries to bypass these governance realities will fail politically and likely legally as well.

What would move us:
- A **negotiated restructuring** that preserves core sites and most jobs while delivering verifiable savings.
- A package with **capacity reduction**, **working-time and flexibility adjustments**, **targeted labour-cost contributions**, and **binding productivity measures**.
- **Clear investment commitments** tied to the restructuring, so we are not simply cutting the present to lose the future.
- A **social-partnership solution** with enforceable milestones and review points, rather than a one-shot confrontation.

What we would resist:
- A plan that relies primarily on closures and mass layoffs.
- Any “equivalent economics” argument that treats job destruction as interchangeable with negotiated savings without showing social and regional costs.
- Any attempt to force through a deal by escalation while ignoring the governance veto points that exist in this company.

So my position is this: I am prepared to support a hard restructuring, but only if it is **balanced, legally executable, and socially responsible**. If management comes back with a package that preserves the company’s long-term health **without sacrificing the regional industrial base**, we can work with that. If the only answer offered is closure and large-scale job destruction, then I will not support it.
