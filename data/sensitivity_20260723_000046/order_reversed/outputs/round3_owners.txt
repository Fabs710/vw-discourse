## My position

The family holding will support a **serious restructuring that restores margins, preserves long-term value, and avoids unnecessary instability**. That means:

- no cosmetic deal,
- no open-ended conflict,
- no protection of overcapacity for its own sake,
- and no settlement that permanently weakens the company’s governance or economics.

I am open on the instrument, but not on the outcome.

## On the three bridge options

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with conditional closure deferral
This is the most plausible route **if and only if** the fallback is real.

A deferral can be supported only if the package contains:
- hard quantified savings targets,
- binding capacity reduction,
- clear milestones,
- a fixed review date,
- and a consequence if delivery fails.

If the “fallback” is politically impossible to execute, then this is just a delay mechanism. I will not support that.

### 2) Site-preserving adjustment package with hard economics
This can be acceptable if the numbers are genuinely equivalent. That means:
- material fixed-cost reduction,
- real capacity removal,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- and enforceable productivity gains.

If the package merely reshuffles pain without changing the economics, I will not back it.

### 3) Phased settlement with voluntary workforce reduction first
This is useful as a first step, not as the final answer. Voluntary measures alone rarely deliver enough structural change. They can be part of a package, but they cannot be the package.

## What I would need to support a no-closure or hybrid solution

I would need a package that shows, in hard numbers:

- annual recurring savings,
- capacity reduction by site and line,
- labor-cost contribution,
- headcount reduction path,
- implementation timetable,
- and a binding backstop if targets are missed.

And I would insist on:
- no indefinite renegotiation,
- no vague productivity promises,
- and no settlement that leaves the company trapped in its current cost structure.

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- a settlement that preserves the current footprint while calling it transformation,
- temporary concessions that simply defer the problem,
- a deal that burdens only workers while management and owners make no visible contribution,
- or any arrangement that undermines the company’s long-term investment capacity.
