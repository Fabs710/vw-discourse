From the capital markets’ perspective, the discussion is moving in the right direction on diagnosis, but not yet on execution.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and to a degree the state that:

- the cost base is structurally too high,
- temporary patches are not enough,
- overcapacity has to be addressed,
- and the company needs a durable path back to competitive margins.

We also agree that a prolonged, open-ended conflict would destroy value. A strike-heavy confrontation, with uncertainty dragging on for months, would hurt margins, confidence, and the equity story.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that is mainly designed to preserve peace while leaving the economics unimproved.

That means:

- no cosmetic compromise,
- no package that looks balanced but leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- no outcome that substitutes vague promises for measurable savings.

We also do **not** accept the premise that every German site must be preserved if that prevents the company from restoring returns. If the economics require consolidation, then management should not rule it out just to avoid difficult headlines.

At the same time, we are also not persuaded by a strategy that uses the threat of closures as a negotiating posture without delivering a clear, implementable margin reset. Markets will not reward brinkmanship; they will punish indecision.

## Our core test
We will back the path that credibly delivers:

1. **durable, quantified cost savings**
2. **a clear return to competitive margins**
3. **implementation certainty**
4. **limited disruption**
5. **a believable investment case for future products**

If the employee side wants to block closures and forced redundancies, then it must put forward a package that is economically equivalent, not just politically preferable.

Temporary wage moderation, deferred bonuses, or soft flexibility measures are not enough on their own. They do not convince us unless they materially and durably change the cost base.

## To the Works Council and union
We respect the rejection of a one-sided burden on labour. But the position “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to legacy guarantees” is only acceptable if it comes with hard numbers that close the gap.

So far, I have not heard a package that gives us confidence that the margin problem is actually solved. If the answer is simply “preserve everything and hope,” that is not acceptable to shareholders.

## To management
You have our support only as long as you pursue a restructuring that is strong enough to matter.

But credibility is now on you. If you return with a plan that is too weak, too slow, or too dependent on industrial peace without real savings, then you will lose capital-market support.

## To the family holding and the state
We recognize your governance power. But governance does not change the underlying economics. If the outcome leaves the company structurally impaired, the market will re-rate the equity accordingly.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution, but only if it is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **implementable**
- and **sufficient to restore competitiveness**

We will **not** support a settlement that protects appearances while impairing margins. If there is a credible no-closure alternative that achieves the same savings, we will consider it. If not, then management should not be prevented from taking structurally necessary measures.
