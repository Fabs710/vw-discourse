### 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **durable industrial and competitive future**.
- **Temporary fixes alone are insufficient**; any solution must be structurally credible.
- A **negotiated settlement** is preferable to an open confrontation.
- There is openness, in principle, to **investment commitments**, **working-time flexibility**, **capacity adjustments**, and **some form of workforce contribution** if it is fair and time-limited.
- All sides acknowledge that the issue involves not just economics, but also **governance, co-determination, and regional responsibility**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: employee side, state, and investors differ sharply with management/owners, who do not rule them out.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: strongly rejected by employee side and state; management/owners will not exclude workforce reductions if needed.
- **Scope of site/job guarantees**: employee side seeks binding protection; management/owners say open-ended guarantees are not acceptable.
- **Nature of workforce contributions**: employees prefer temporary, fair, negotiated measures; management/investors want durable, quantified savings.
- **Whether all German sites can be preserved**: employee side and state say yes in principle; management/owners/investors say economics may require consolidation.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **competitive long-term cost reductions** without closures or forced layoffs.
- Whether **temporary employee concessions** can be made sufficiently durable and verifiable to satisfy management, owners, and investors.
- What **binding commitments** on investment, product pipeline, and site modernization would accompany any workforce contribution.
- How to balance **social legitimacy and co-determination** against the demand for rapid structural adjustment.
- What level of **capacity reduction** is acceptable if all sides want to avoid escalation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from stated red lines to **concrete package design**: quantify savings, specify measures, and compare alternatives.
- Ask each side to propose:
  1. the **minimum acceptable safeguards**,
  2. the **specific cost-saving measures** they can accept,
  3. the **binding guarantees or commitments** they would offer in return.
- Focus on a **side-by-side comparison** of at least two scenarios:
  - a no-closure / no-forced-layoff package,
  - and a structurally tougher alternative if the first cannot deliver sufficient savings.
- Keep the discussion centered on **verifiable numbers, implementation certainty, and governance conditions**.
