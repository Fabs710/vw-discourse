From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the discussion has clarified the problem — but not yet produced a package that is clearly sufficient on **risk-adjusted value creation**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, the state, and the analysts on several core points:

- The company has a **structural cost and capacity problem**.
- **Temporary fixes alone are not enough**.
- The business needs a **durable** return to competitive margins.
- A **light** no-closure package is very unlikely to be sufficient.
- Prolonged conflict or a long strike would destroy value.

I also agree that a negotiated settlement is preferable to open confrontation, but only if it actually restores competitiveness.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position as currently stated that:

- all German sites must remain open regardless of economics,
- compulsory redundancies can be ruled out in every circumstance,
- and binding job guarantees through 2030 should be a precondition no matter what savings are achieved.

That is too rigid for a company with an uncompetitive cost base and excess capacity. If those demands freeze the structure, they are not a solution — they are a way of deferring the problem while margins continue to erode.

At the same time, I also do **not** support management using closure threats as a substitute for a quantified plan. Threats are not value creation.

## On the analyst and modeler input
The technical contributions were useful, and they point to the right standard:

- A **light** no-closure package is not enough.
- A **deep hybrid** package **might** be enough.
- It must remove enough **fixed cost, capacity, overhead, and labor cost** to come close to consolidation economics.
- It must be judged on **risk-adjusted NPV**, not headlines.

That is exactly the right framework.

What matters to us is not whether the restructuring looks socially neat. What matters is whether it **restores margins**, **reduces overcapacity**, and does so with enough certainty that the market can believe it.

## What would move us
We could support a no-closure or hybrid package if it includes all of the following:

1. **Material permanent capacity reduction**
2. **Substantial voluntary workforce reduction**
3. **Durable labor-cost relief**
4. **Overhead and support-function cuts**
5. **High implementation certainty**
6. **Binding investment commitments**
7. **A credible product roadmap**
8. **A measurable path back to competitive margins**

If a package can do that, then it deserves serious consideration.

## What will not move us
We will not support:

- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that mainly preserves labor peace while leaving the economics weak,
- open-ended guarantees that lock in the current structure,
- or a settlement that relies on temporary concessions only.

We also will not support an outcome that leaves the company structurally impaired just to avoid difficult decisions.

## On the “hybrid” idea
A hybrid is only acceptable if it is truly hybrid in substance, not just in language.

That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- real cost reduction,
- real headcount reduction, even if voluntary,
- and a durable change to the economics.

If “hybrid” just means postponing closures while keeping the cost base basically intact, then it is not a solution.

## My position on closures and redundancies
I am **not** demanding closures as the first choice. But I am also **not** prepared to rule them out in advance.

If the no-closure path can prove equivalence on a risk-adjusted basis, fine — we support the stronger value outcome. If it cannot, then management must retain the ability to take structural measures.

The same applies to compulsory redundancies: they are not our preferred mechanism, but neither can they be excluded if the alternative fails to deliver the necessary savings.

## Bottom line
Our test is simple:

**Can the proposed package restore competitive margins with credible, durable, and enforceable savings?**

- If yes, we are open to a deep hybrid or no-closure solution.
- If no, then preserving every site and ruling out every hard measure is not responsible stewardship of capital.

If the parties want to claim that a no-closure package is economically equivalent, then they should bring a fully quantified comparison showing:

- expected savings,
- NPV,
- implementation timing,
- strike and execution risk,
- and the resulting margin path.
