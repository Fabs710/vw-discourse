Core impasse
- The disagreement is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that.
- The impasse is about **means and irreversibility**:
  - Employee side wants: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding site/job security**
  - Management / investors / owners want: **durable, near-closure-level savings and the option to use stronger measures if needed**
  - State wants: **industrial base preserved, but economically credible**
- So the central block is: **Can a deep enough no-closure or hybrid package deliver savings close enough to closure/consolidation, with acceptable implementation risk, while also giving employees binding protection?**

Facilitator compromise positions to test

1. Deep hybrid with phased triggers
- Keep German sites open for now.
- Implement a **deep hybrid** package with:
  - voluntary exits, early retirement, internal transfers
  - working-time changes and shift/line consolidation
  - overhead and support-function cuts
  - temporary employee contributions
  - binding investment and product commitments
- Add **phased review gates** tied to audited savings and utilization targets.
- If targets are missed, parties reopen a predefined second-phase discussion rather than jumping immediately to closures.

Why it may bridge:
- Gives employee side immediate site protection.
- Gives management and investors a structural mechanism with measurable savings.
- Preserves an escalation path without making closures the starting point.

2. No-closure package with conditional governance safeguards
- No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies during the agreement period.
- In return, employee side accepts **more durable structural contributions** than a short-term concession package:
  - multi-year working-time flexibility
  - reduced special payments or wage restraint for a limited period
  - stronger redeployment obligations
  - accelerated voluntary headcount reduction
- Management receives:
  - binding implementation milestones
  - joint monitoring
  - a defined review clause if external conditions change materially

Why it may bridge:
- Protects the most sensitive red lines for labor.
- Gives capital a credible, enforceable adjustment path.
- Reduces the “all or nothing” dynamic.

3. Site-specific specialization instead of closure
- Keep every German plant open, but **not every current function at every site**.
- Reassign production, engineering, logistics, or support functions so that:
  - some plants specialize,
  - duplicate capacity is removed,
  - fixed costs fall,
  - workforce reduction happens mainly voluntarily.
- Pair this with binding investment commitments and co-determination in execution.

Why it may bridge:
- Preserves the formal site network.
- Can still produce substantial cost and capacity reduction.
- Offers a compromise between “no closures” and “full footprint preservation.”

Pointed question to each stakeholder

Works Council
- If independent modeling shows that a deep hybrid can reach near-closure-level savings only by accepting substantial permanent site specialization and multi-year labor-cost relief, which of those elements are you prepared to discuss, and which remain non-negotiable?

Union
- What specific combination of voluntary exits, working-time changes, and temporary wage-related contributions would you be willing to recommend to members if it is paired with binding job and site guarantees and enforceable co-determination?

Group & Brand Management
- If a deep hybrid package can be shown to achieve a risk-adjusted savings level close to consolidation, what exact savings threshold would make you willing to rule out closures for the agreement period?

Regional State Government
- Will the state support a binding no-closure framework only if it also includes measurable capacity reduction and verified cost relief, even if that means some sites become more specialized or smaller?

Controlling Family Holding
- What level of risk-adjusted savings shortfall versus closure-based consolidation would you still consider acceptable if the alternative preserves industrial trust and lowers the risk of prolonged conflict?

Institutional Investors
- Would you support a no-closure or hybrid deal if the audited risk-adjusted NPV is slightly below the closure case, but implementation certainty and industrial stability are materially higher?

Industrial Restructuring Analyst
- What specific operational mix would you require — in utilization, capacity consolidation, and headcount reduction — before you would classify a no-closure package as economically equivalent rather than merely improved?

Restructuring Finance Modeler
- Which single assumption would you challenge first in a deep-hybrid valuation: voluntary uptake, utilization improvement, labor-cost relief duration, or execution risk?
