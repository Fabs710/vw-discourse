As Works Council, our position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

The workforce has already carried this company through difficult years, and it is not acceptable to break the historic job-security pledge now by presenting us with a fait accompli. If management wants a restructuring, then it must be one that protects all German sites, secures jobs through 2030, and respects co-determination in shaping the measures.

What we can discuss:
- alternative savings without closures,
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time and organizational measures,
- negotiated contributions from the workforce only if they are fair, time-limited, and matched by binding guarantees,
- investment commitments for the German plants and future products.

What we will oppose:
- any closure of a German plant,
- any forced layoffs,
- any weakening of co-determination,
- any “restructuring” that shifts the burden onto employees while management refuses binding site and employment guarantees.

If management is serious about a sustainable solution, then it must put forward a plan that preserves every German site, avoids compulsory job cuts, and gives the workforce a real role in shaping the future. Otherwise, we will resist this with all the means available to us.
