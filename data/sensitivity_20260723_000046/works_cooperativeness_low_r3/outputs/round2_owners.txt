I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me speak plainly from the controlling family holding’s governance perspective.

## Where I agree

I agree with the core diagnosis:

- the company needs **durable** competitiveness, not a postponement;
- the German cost base is too high relative to the market reality;
- temporary fixes alone will not restore margins;
- and any solution must preserve the company’s ability to invest in future products and technology.

I also agree that a prolonged open conflict would be destructive. It would damage value, distract management, and weaken confidence in the business. That is not in the interest of the owners, the workforce, or the company’s long-term industrial role.

## Where I disagree

I do **not** accept the position that all German sites are untouchable regardless of economics.

I also do **not** accept open-ended job guarantees through 2030 as a precondition for every change. That would freeze the structure while the competitive environment keeps moving, and it would simply shift the problem forward.

And I do **not** accept the idea that a “fair” solution is one that preserves the current footprint but fails to restore competitiveness. That would be value-destructive, even if it is politically comfortable.

## To the Works Council and Union

I understand your insistence on no closures and no compulsory redundancies. I also recognize the workforce’s history of contribution. But you are asking the owners to accept a package that may not solve the structural problem.

A no-closure, no-forced-layoff solution is only acceptable if it delivers **comparable, durable, verifiable savings**. Not rhetorical savings. Not temporary relief. Real improvements in the cost base.

So if you want to exclude closures and forced redundancies, then the burden is on you to present a package that actually closes the gap. That means:

- meaningful capacity reductions,
- working-time and organizational flexibility,
- durable labour-cost contributions,
- removal or adjustment of legacy cost elements,
- and a structure that is enforceable, not aspirational.

If the package does not materially change the economics, then it is not a serious alternative.

## To management

Management is right to insist that the current structure is too expensive. But you still need to prove that your preferred path is the **best** path, not simply the hardest one.

I am not endorsing closures as an end in themselves. I am saying that you cannot rule them out in advance if they are required to restore competitiveness. At the same time, if there is a credible alternative that achieves the same savings without closures or forced layoffs, then that deserves serious consideration.

That is the standard: **economically equivalent, durable, and implementable**.

## To the regional state government

I respect the state’s role and its responsibility for regional stability. But stability is not preserved by insulating the company from necessary restructuring.

If we protect every structure unchanged, we risk weakening the company, which in the end would be worse for the region as well. A stronger company is the best safeguard for jobs and legitimacy over time.

So I agree with you on the need for balance and social responsibility. I disagree where balance becomes a veto on all structural change.

## To the institutional investors

You are right to focus on margin recovery, implementation certainty, and long-term value. I share that objective.

But I also caution against treating plant closures as the only credible signal of seriousness. What matters is not whether the plan is brutal on paper; it is whether it is **credible in execution** and actually improves the business.

If a negotiated solution can achieve the same durable savings with less instability, that is preferable from a stewardship perspective. But again, it must be real, not symbolic.

## What would move us

A package could gain our support if it contains:

1. **Quantified durable savings** sufficient to restore competitiveness  
2. **A credible reduction in overcapacity**  
3. **A meaningful labour contribution** that is not merely temporary optics  
4. **Implementation certainty** with clear governance and milestones  
5. **A believable investment path** for future products and technology  
6. **Maximum avoidance of unnecessary instability**

## What will not move us

We will not support:

- a blanket refusal to address overcapacity;
- permanent guarantees that lock in the existing structure;
- or a settlement that preserves peace in the short term while leaving the company weaker.

That would be a bad outcome for the family holding and for the enterprise.

## My bottom line

We are prepared to support a negotiated restructuring, but only if it restores competitiveness in a durable way.

If the employee side can bring a **credible no-closure package** that produces comparable long-term savings, we will engage seriously. If not, then we will not back a settlement that rules out necessary structural measures in advance.

The company’s long-term value comes first. Social peace matters, but not at the cost of permanent economic weakness.
