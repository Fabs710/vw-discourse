### 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **durable long-term solution**, not temporary patchwork.
- All sides accept that the **cost base, capacity situation, and future investment needs** must be addressed.
- There is broad support for **negotiated settlement** over prolonged conflict.
- There is partial common ground around **working-time flexibility, capacity adjustments, voluntary instruments, and investment/product commitments**.
- All sides acknowledge that **implementation credibility** and **verifiable outcomes** matter.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: employee and state-side actors reject them; management, owners, and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: employee and state-side actors reject them; management and capital-side actors will not rule them out in advance.
- **Scope of job/site guarantees**: employees and state push for strong, binding guarantees; management, owners, and investors resist open-ended or blanket commitments.
- **Nature of workforce contribution**: labour wants temporary, fair, reciprocal contributions tied to guarantees; capital wants a contribution large enough to produce durable savings.
- **Standard for an acceptable plan**: labour and state want a credible **no-closure** solution; management, owners, and investors require evidence that such a plan is economically equivalent to site consolidation.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- How to achieve **durable savings** without closures or forced redundancies.
- Whether **capacity reductions** can be sufficient without site shutdowns.
- What level of **workforce contribution** is acceptable, and what specific guarantees would make it reciprocal.
- How to balance **social legitimacy/co-determination** against **competitiveness and margin recovery**.
- Whether the parties can agree on a package that is **quantified, enforceable, and time-limited** while still meeting the different red lines.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **side-by-side package design**.
- Ask each side to submit a **concrete, quantified proposal** showing:
  - expected savings,
  - capacity changes,
  - staffing impact,
  - investment commitments,
  - and the guarantees attached.
- Use a common test: **Can the plan restore competitiveness without closures and forced redundancies, and if so, under what exact conditions?**
- Keep the discussion focused on **measurable equivalence**, not repeated restatement of red lines.
