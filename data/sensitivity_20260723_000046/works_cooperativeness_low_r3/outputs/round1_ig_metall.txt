We fully support the core line from the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no restructuring that loads the crisis onto employees alone**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: the peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to defend jobs and the collective wage framework. We have already shown willingness to discuss **shared-sacrifice measures** — but only under conditions that make sense and are fair.

What we can discuss:
- **Temporary and negotiated employee contributions** instead of permanent wage erosion
- **Capacity reductions, working-time adjustments, and operational flexibility**
- **Waiving or deferring bonuses / special payments**
- **A jointly negotiated contribution fund** tied to investment and future product commitments
- **Measures that preserve all German sites and avoid forced job losses**

What we cannot accept:
- **Plant closures in Germany**
- **Compulsory redundancies**
- **Open-ended job insecurity**
- **Cuts that are permanent while management and owners avoid their share of the burden**
- **Any weakening of the collective bargaining framework**

So the burden of proof is on management and the owners. If they want the workforce to contribute, then they must also contribute in a tangible way:
- binding guarantees for sites and employment,
- a credible industrial plan for the German plants,
- and a fair distribution of sacrifices across capital, management, and labour.

A restructuring that simply says “employees pay, jobs disappear, and the company hopes for competitiveness later” is not acceptable and will be met with resistance. If management wants a durable solution, it must come with a plan that protects the workforce and the sites first — not after the fact.
