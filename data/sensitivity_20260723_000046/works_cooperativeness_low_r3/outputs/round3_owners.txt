I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the analysts, and partly with the state on the core diagnosis:

- the German cost base is structurally too high,
- the company needs durable, not cosmetic, change,
- temporary measures alone will not restore competitiveness,
- and prolonged conflict would destroy value for everyone.

I also agree that a negotiated settlement is preferable to an open-ended strike confrontation. From a governance perspective, instability is not a strategy.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side premise that all German sites are untouchable as a matter of principle.

I also do **not** accept open-ended job guarantees through 2030 as a precondition for any restructuring. That would freeze the structure and simply push the competitiveness problem into the future.

And I do **not** accept a package that preserves today’s footprint while leaving the company with an uncompetitive cost and capacity structure. That would be value-destructive, even if politically comfortable.

## On the works council and union position
You have been clear: no closures, no forced redundancies, strong guarantees, and temporary workforce contributions only.

I respect the clarity, but I do not think that position yet solves the actual problem.

The restructuring finance modeler has made the key point: a light no-closure deal is unlikely to be enough. If you want to rule out closures and compulsory redundancies, then the burden is on you to show how the remaining package closes the gap **economically**, not just morally.

At this stage, I have not seen a fully credible package that does that.

## On the analyst’s findings
The analysis is useful because it narrows the real question.

It does **not** say that no-closure is impossible.  
It says:
- a **light** no-closure package probably fails,
- a **deep** or **hybrid** package might work,
- but only if it removes enough fixed cost, capacity, and overhead, and does so with high execution certainty.

That is the right framework.

So I am **not** endorsing closures as the first resort. But I am also **not** accepting a blanket exclusion of closures if the alternative does not produce near-equivalent savings.

## On management
Management is right that “temporary” measures are not enough. But management has not yet fully demonstrated that every non-closure path has been exhausted.

So my position is:

- I am open to a serious hybrid or deep no-closure package,
- but it must be quantified, durable, and credible,
- and it must materially change the economics.

If it cannot, then site decisions remain on the table.

## On the state government
I understand the state’s political and regional responsibility. That matters.

But regional stability is not served by protecting an overextended structure indefinitely. If the company weakens, the region loses jobs anyway — only later, and with more damage.

So I support a solution that preserves industrial presence where possible. I do **not** support a solution that treats every current arrangement as untouchable.

## What would move me
A package could gain support from the controlling family holding if it includes all of the following:

1. **Quantified durable savings**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **Substantial voluntary workforce reduction**
4. **Durable labor-cost relief**
5. **Overhead and support-function cuts**
6. **Investment commitments for the German footprint**
7. **A credible product and technology roadmap**
8. **Implementation certainty with governance discipline**

That is the standard for a responsible stewardship decision.

## What will not move me
I will not support:
- a purely temporary concession package,
- a settlement that avoids all structural change,
- permanent guarantees that lock in the current footprint,
- or a deal that looks socially neat but leaves the business structurally weak.

## My bottom line
The right answer is **not** “closures at any cost.”  
But it is also **not** “no closures, no forced redundancies, no structural pain.”

The right answer is a package that actually restores competitiveness. If a deep hybrid or no-closure solution can do that, I will support serious consideration. If it cannot, then the company must retain the ability to take stronger structural measures.

If the parties want the family holding to back a no-closure path, then bring a **fully quantified hybrid package** showing:
- recurring savings,
- headcount impact,
- capacity changes,
- overhead reductions,
- and investment commitments.

If that cannot be done, then I will not pretend that preserving the current structure is responsible governance.
