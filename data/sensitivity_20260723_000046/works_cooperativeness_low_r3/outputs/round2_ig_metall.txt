I’ll be direct.

We agree with the Works Council on the non-negotiables: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no restructuring that turns workers into the sole shock absorber**. That is not just a slogan for us — it is the minimum condition for any settlement we can bring to our members.

We also agree that the company needs a **durable** solution, not a delay. Temporary patches alone will not stabilize the business. But durability does **not** automatically mean closures. It can also mean a negotiated package of capacity adjustment, working-time flexibility, and shared contributions — if, and only if, it is fair and balanced.

## Where we disagree with management, owners, and investors
You keep treating **plant closures** and **forced redundancies** as if they were ordinary management tools. For us, they are not acceptable starting points. If you insist on making them your default leverage, then you are choosing confrontation, not restructuring.

And I have to say plainly: the repeated argument that “the economics require it” is not sufficient by itself. Economics matter, yes. But so do:
- the binding collective-bargaining framework,
- the workforce’s contribution over many years,
- the co-determination structure,
- and the company’s social responsibility as a major industrial employer.

A solution that improves margins by destroying trust, breaking job security, and provoking a prolonged labour conflict is not a good solution. It is a risky one.

## On the demand for durable savings
We hear the call for “durable” and “quantified” savings. Fine. But then the burden is not only on labour. It is on management and owners too.

If you want a workforce contribution, we can discuss:
- temporary waiver or deferral of bonuses and special payments,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions without closures,
- voluntary instruments instead of forced layoffs,
- a jointly governed contribution fund,
- and flexibility in operations.

But that has to be matched by:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a clear investment path for German plants,
- and commitments on future products.

A wage contribution without security is just a wage cut. We will not sell that to our members.

## On the state and family owners
The state government is right to reject closures and forced redundancies as a default response. That is the correct framework for a company of this significance.

To the family owners: if you are serious about long-term stewardship, then show it by backing a settlement that keeps the industrial base intact. Long-term ownership is not proved by forcing through a “clean-up” at the expense of employees. It is proved by preserving the company’s industrial capability and workforce trust.

## What we can accept
Let me be concrete. We are prepared to discuss a package that includes:
1. **Temporary employee contribution**, not permanent wage erosion
2. **Capacity reductions** through organizational and working-time measures
3. **Voluntary** instruments for workforce adjustment
4. **Investment commitments** for German sites
5. **Binding protection of the collective wage framework**
6. **No closures and no forced redundancies**

That is a serious offer. It is not a surrender. It is a shared-sacrifice path.

## What we will not accept
- plant closures in Germany
- compulsory redundancies
- open-ended insecurity
- permanent labour-cost cuts without reciprocal contributions
- any weakening of collective bargaining or co-determination

If management comes back with the same message — “accept closures or accept layoffs” — then there is no basis for agreement, and our members will understand exactly where the responsibility lies.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking a solution. We are blocking a **one-sided** solution.

If there is a real package that:
- protects all German sites,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- delivers verifiable savings,
- and distributes sacrifices fairly,

then we will negotiate it seriously.

If not, then we will defend the workforce with all tools available to us.
