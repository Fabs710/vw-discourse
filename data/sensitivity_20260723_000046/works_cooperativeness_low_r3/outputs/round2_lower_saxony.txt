I will be equally direct.

## My position remains unchanged on the fundamentals

I **agree** that the company needs a durable industrial future, real competitiveness, and a restructuring that is more than a delay tactic. I also agree that temporary fixes alone are not enough.

But I **do not accept** the repeated attempt to present **plant closures in Germany** and **compulsory redundancies** as if they were the only serious options. They are not an acceptable default for a company of this regional significance, and they are not compatible with the state’s role as anchor shareholder, nor with the social partnership model that has underpinned this company for decades.

## Where I agree with management, owners, and investors
I agree on four points:
- the cost base must improve,
- capacity issues must be addressed,
- savings must be durable and verifiable,
- and the future product and investment path matters.

That is the common ground.

## Where I disagree sharply
I disagree with the conclusion that this necessarily requires:
- closures of German plants,
- open-ended threats of forced layoffs,
- or treating German sites as disposable if the numbers are difficult.

That is not “discipline”; it is a high-risk strategy that could damage the company’s legitimacy, labor relations, and regional economic base more than it helps.

The state will not simply endorse a restructuring that shifts the burden onto workers while preserving all pressure points for capital. If you want us to support tough measures, then the package must be balanced, credible, and socially sustainable.

## To management
You keep saying that the economics may require site decisions. I hear that. But you have not yet shown that a no-closure alternative has been genuinely exhausted or properly designed.

So I am not ready to accept your framing that the only credible answer is consolidation. Before even considering closures, I would expect a package that demonstrably combines:
- capacity reduction without site shutdowns,
- working-time and organizational flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary workforce adjustment,
- and a meaningful labor-cost contribution.

If that package cannot be made to work, then the burden is on management to prove why not — not simply to declare closures inevitable.

## To the works council and union
I support your red lines on **no compulsory redundancies** and strong protection of jobs and sites. Those are not just bargaining positions; they are core to the regional and social responsibility of this company.

That said, I would also ask you to stay focused on a package that is not just defensive, but implementable. A solution needs to be economically serious enough that it can actually survive the boardroom and the markets.

What I would support from the state side is:
- negotiated, time-limited workforce contributions,
- capacity adjustments without plant closures,
- investment commitments for German plants,
- future product guarantees,
- and binding co-determination in shaping the measures.

What I will not support is a deal that quietly becomes a wage cut with no security in return.

## To the controlling family owners and investors
You are right that the company needs durable savings and credible implementation. But long-term stewardship is not measured only by cost discipline. It is also measured by whether you preserve industrial capability, trust, and the regional anchor function of this company.

If you push for a “clean” restructuring that damages the social compact, you may win a short-term margin argument and lose the long-term industrial foundation. That is a bad trade for everyone.

So I am prepared to engage with seriousness, but I will not back a solution that sacrifices the company’s regional legitimacy for the sake of a faster accounting improvement.

## What would move me
A proposal could gain my support if it includes:
1. **No closure of German sites**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A real, quantified workforce contribution that is time-limited and fair**
4. **Capacity and working-time adjustments**
5. **Binding investment commitments for German plants**
6. **A credible product and technology roadmap**
7. **Full respect for co-determination and the state’s governance role**

That is the standard I would consider responsible.

## What will not move me
I will not be moved by:
- “there is no alternative” arguments that have not been demonstrated,
- a blanket insistence on site closures,
- open-ended insecurity for workers,
- or a restructuring that erodes the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are red lines for us.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated restructuring. But it must be one that:
- protects German jobs and plants,
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- respects co-determination,
- and still delivers real competitiveness.

If management, owners, and investors want our support, bring a **credible no-closure package** first. If you cannot, then do not expect the state to assist in dismantling the industrial base we are responsible for protecting.
