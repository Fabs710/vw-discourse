Let me respond directly, because the positions on the table are still not good enough from a restructuring and competitiveness standpoint.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one thing: **the company needs a durable future, not a cosmetic fix**.  
I also agree that temporary patches alone will not solve this. We need something that materially improves the cost base and funds investment into future products and technology to 2030.

I also acknowledge the governance reality:
- co-determination matters,
- a negotiated settlement is preferable to a prolonged confrontation,
- and any sustainable outcome must be implementable in practice.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee side’s core line that all German sites must be preserved regardless of economics.

I do **not** accept that compulsory redundancies can simply be ruled out in advance if the business case demands structural adjustment.

And I do **not** accept that a “fair” solution is one that protects the current structure while leaving the company with an uncompetitive cost base.

That is exactly the kind of outcome that would fail by 2030.

## To the Works Council
You keep saying “no closures, no forced redundancies, no job cuts.” I understand the stance, but it does not solve the problem.

If you want to rule out closures, then bring me a **credible no-closure package** that produces comparable, durable savings. Not slogans. Not generalized commitments. Not only temporary measures.

What would need to be on the table:
- real capacity reductions,
- meaningful labour-cost reductions,
- organizational simplification,
- flexibility on working time and deployment,
- and a package that is quantifiable and sustainable.

A plan that only shifts pain into the future, or only uses voluntary instruments that do not move the economics enough, will not be acceptable.

## To the union
I hear your willingness to discuss temporary employee contributions, working-time flexibility, and voluntary instruments. That is better than a flat refusal. But it is still not enough unless it delivers a **durable and verifiable** improvement.

A temporary waiver of bonuses or a deferred payment does not fix a structurally too-high cost base.  
A joint contribution fund is interesting only if it leads to measurable long-term competitiveness.  
Capacity reductions without the possibility of site decisions may still leave us with too much cost and too much idle capacity.

So I need to be blunt: if your proposal cannot close the gap structurally, then it does not meet the needs of the business.

## To the state government
I understand your concern about regional stability and social legitimacy. But regional stability is not protected by pretending there is no overcapacity.

If the company keeps carrying excess cost and excess capacity, we weaken the business, the investment case, and ultimately the regional base as well.

So yes, I am open to a negotiated package. But no, I will not endorse a framework that treats German sites as untouchable no matter what the numbers say.

## To the controlling family owners and investors
I agree with your core diagnosis: we need **durable, quantified savings**, not symbolic peace.

But let me also say this clearly: if the governance majority wants competitiveness, then it must back a restructuring that is strong enough to achieve it. A compromise that looks balanced but leaves the company structurally impaired is not a solution.

## What management is asking for
Management is not asking for disruption for its own sake. We are asking for a restructuring that:
1. restores competitiveness,
2. reduces overcapacity,
3. lowers labour costs in a durable way,
4. protects the return-on-sales target,
5. and frees funds for investment in future products and technology.

That requires real sacrifice. It may require site decisions. It may require workforce reductions. It definitely requires a labour-cost contribution that is more than symbolic.

## What would move me
I remain open to alternatives **if** they achieve all of the following:
- durable savings,
- verifiable capacity reduction,
- a genuine labour-cost reset,
- and a credible path back to competitive margins.

If the employee side can bring forward a package that truly delivers that, then we can discuss how to implement it. If not, then I will continue to argue for deeper structural measures, including plant consolidation.

## What will not move me
I will not be persuaded by:
- open-ended job guarantees,
- a blanket ban on closures,
- permanent protection of every legacy arrangement,
- or temporary concessions that do not change the underlying cost structure.

That is not a sustainable answer.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate seriously, but only around a restructuring that **materially** improves competitiveness.

If the others want to avoid harsher site decisions, then they need to bring a package that is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- and economically equivalent.

Otherwise, management’s position remains: **the company cannot carry its current German cost and capacity structure into the future.**
