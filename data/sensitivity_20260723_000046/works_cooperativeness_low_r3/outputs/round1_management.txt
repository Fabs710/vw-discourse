Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

We agree on one point: this company needs a **durable industrial future**, not a symbolic gesture. But we do **not** agree that this can be achieved while treating every German site, every legacy guarantee, and every cost level as untouchable.

The reality is simple: **our German cost base is structurally too high**, our European market is shrinking, and competitors are moving faster and cheaper. If we do not cut **capacity** and **labour costs** in a lasting way, then we will not restore margins, and we will not have the funds to invest in the products and technology this company needs by 2030.

So, directly:

- **No**, we cannot accept a plan that preserves all German sites regardless of economics.
- **No**, we cannot accept open-ended job guarantees that freeze the current structure.
- **No**, we cannot accept a cosmetic compromise that delivers temporary relief but leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

I hear the resistance to plant closures and compulsory redundancies. But management cannot be asked to shoulder the entire burden of a transition while the workforce contribution is limited to temporary measures and hopes about future demand. That will not fix the structural problem.

What we are willing to discuss is a **serious package** that delivers real savings:
- capacity reductions,
- binding labour-cost contribution,
- flexibility on working time and organization,
- and a restructuring that is durable, not just deferred.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must help produce an alternative that achieves **comparable, verifiable, long-term savings**. That means more than temporary bonuses or soft promises. It means a credible contribution that moves the cost base now and sustainably.

So my position is this:  
**We are prepared to negotiate, but only around a restructuring that materially reduces overcapacity and labour costs.** If that requires difficult site decisions, then we will not rule them out. If the proposal does not deliver structural competitiveness, then it is not acceptable.
