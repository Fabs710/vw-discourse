I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the analysts, the state, and in part the workforce representatives on one essential point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a delay disguised as compromise**.

I also agree that:
- temporary fixes are not enough,
- the current German cost and capacity structure is not competitive enough,
- and a prolonged conflict would destroy value for everyone.

From a governance perspective, I also accept the technical conclusion that a **light** no-closure package is unlikely to be sufficient.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position that:
- all German sites are untouchable,
- no compulsory redundancies can ever be considered under any circumstances,
- and binding job guarantees through 2030 should be a precondition regardless of economics.

That is not a restructuring framework. It is a veto on change.

I also do **not** accept a solution that preserves the full current footprint while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive. If the business remains too expensive and too overcapacity-heavy, then we are simply postponing the problem and eroding value.

## To the works council and union
You are right to insist that workers should not be the only shock absorber. I agree with that.

But your current position still does not answer the hard question: **how do you get near-closure-level savings without closure-level decisions?**

A serious no-closure or deep hybrid package would have to show, clearly and credibly:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- substantial voluntary workforce reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- overhead and support-function cuts,
- and a real improvement in utilization and unit cost.

If that can be done, I will take it seriously. If it cannot, then your position is not an alternative; it is just a refusal.

And let me be clear: I am not moved by the argument that closures are always worse because they are unpopular. Value destruction can happen through inaction just as surely as through restructuring.

## To management
I agree with you that temporary concessions alone do not solve a structural problem.

But I also think management still owes the board and owners a more disciplined comparison of the actual options, including:
1. consolidation / closure,
2. a deep hybrid package,
3. and a no-closure package with real structural adjustment.

You have argued the case for urgency. That is fair. But urgency is not the same as proof. If the non-closure path can genuinely get close enough on a risk-adjusted basis, I am open to it. If not, then management must retain the ability to take stronger structural measures.

## To the state government
I understand the regional and political responsibility. I do not dismiss it.

But I will not support a position that protects every current site structure regardless of economics. Regional stability is not served by preserving an uncompetitive cost base until the entire business weakens.

What the state needs to support is not a slogan, but a **credible industrial plan** that preserves long-term jobs by restoring competitiveness. If the deep hybrid can do that, it deserves consideration. If it cannot, then we should not pretend that site preservation alone is responsible stewardship.

## To institutional investors
You are right to demand quantified, durable savings and implementation certainty. That is the proper test.

I would only caution against assuming that the harshest answer is automatically the best one. The best answer is the one that:
- restores margins,
- reduces overcapacity,
- funds future investment,
- and can actually be executed without creating a longer, more damaging conflict.

If a deep hybrid package can achieve near-closure-level savings with lower instability, that may be preferable. But only if the numbers are real.

## What would move me
A package could win support from the controlling family holding if it includes all of the following:
1. **Quantified durable savings**
2. **Material capacity reduction**
3. **Substantial voluntary workforce reduction**
4. **Durable labour-cost relief**
5. **Overhead and support-function cuts**
6. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
7. **A credible product and technology roadmap**
8. **Implementation certainty with governance discipline**

That is the minimum standard for a responsible stewardship decision.

## What will not move me
I will not support:
- a cosmetic no-closure deal that does not materially improve the economics,
- a temporary concession package dressed up as restructuring,
- permanent guarantees that freeze the structure,
- or an arrangement that leaves the company dependent on hope instead of competitiveness.

## My bottom line
I am **not** insisting on closures as the first resort.  
But I am also **not** accepting a blanket exclusion of closures if the alternative cannot prove comparable savings and durability.

So the burden is now on the parties pushing a no-closure or deep hybrid solution to show, with numbers, that it can truly close the gap.

If a specialist model is needed to test whether the proposed deep hybrid is actually near-equivalent to consolidation on a risk-adjusted basis, then I support that.
