Management is asking the workforce to pay for a crisis we did not create, and it is doing so by threatening the very foundations of this company: German plants, secure jobs, and the historic social partnership that has made this group successful for decades.

My position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral stripping of long-standing job-security and co-determination protections.
- Any restructuring must be negotiated, fair, and reversible where possible.
- If savings are needed, they must come through alternatives that protect sites and employment: capacity adjustments, temporary working-time measures, internal redeployment, qualification, and a credible product and investment plan.

I will not accept a fait accompli. Announcing closures and job cuts first, then asking the workforce to “understand” later, is not a basis for serious negotiation. It is a breach of trust.

If management wants a solution, then it must put real guarantees on the table:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- binding job security through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- a genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring,
- and concrete commitments to investment, future products, and technology in Germany.

We are ready to discuss a fair contribution from the workforce only if those red lines are respected. But I want to be equally clear: if this is about closing plants and pushing through mass layoffs, we will resist it with all the means available to us — in the boardroom, in bargaining, and on the shop floor.
