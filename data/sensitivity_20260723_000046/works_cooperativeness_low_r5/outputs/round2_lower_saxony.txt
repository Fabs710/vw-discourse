I will be frank.

I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: this company does need a credible competitiveness response. A purely temporary fix would be irresponsible. But I do **not** accept the conclusion that this must mean German plant closures or a restructuring that treats jobs and sites as the primary adjustment variable.

## To management
You still have not shown that closures are necessary rather than simply convenient.

You speak of a structural cost and capacity problem, but you have not yet put a transparent, quantified case on the table that proves:
- what the exact savings target is,
- what the footprint reduction must be,
- which alternatives were seriously tested,
- and why those alternatives cannot deliver the same durable effect.

That matters, because plant closures are not just another cost lever. In a company like this, they are a strategic and political rupture with long-term consequences for industrial capability, regional economies, and trust in co-determination. I will not treat them as the default starting point.

If you want the state to consider deeper restructuring, then bring a real comparison:
- closure scenario,
- non-closure scenario,
- quantified savings,
- employment effects,
- investment effects,
- and the consequences for future product allocation in Germany.

Until then, “all options on the table” sounds less like analysis and more like pressure.

## To the works council and union
I agree with you that the workforce cannot be asked to pay for management’s problem with mass layoffs and closures.

I also agree that temporary measures alone are not enough if the company truly needs durable change. But your position has to engage with the reality that the company does need some structural correction. What I cannot support is an answer that simply says “no closures, no layoffs, no change in footprint” without showing how competitiveness is restored in a durable way.

That said, the burden is **not** on labour alone. If the company wants contribution from employees, then it must also commit to:
- investment in German plants,
- concrete future-product allocation,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a real role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring.

A negotiated contribution can be part of a solution. A one-sided demand for sacrifices cannot.

## To the family holding and investors
I understand the concern about durable savings and market credibility. But you should be careful not to confuse “durable” with “irreversible damage.”

A solution that destroys trust, provokes prolonged industrial conflict, and weakens the regional base of the company is not durable. It may look decisive on paper and fail in execution.

I also want to be explicit: a binding job guarantee is not a luxury demand. It is what makes a negotiated contribution politically and industrially possible. If you want flexibility from the workforce, you need to offer security in return. That does not mean freezing the company forever. It means sequencing change in a way that preserves the industrial base and avoids forced redundancies wherever possible.

## My position
The state will support a tough restructuring **only if** it is built around:
- no German plant closures as a first or default resort,
- no forced redundancies,
- a negotiated workforce contribution,
- binding investment and product commitments in Germany,
- and a plan that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor.

I am open to capacity reductions, working-time measures, internal transfers, qualification, and other negotiated adjustments. I am **not** open to using closures as the primary instrument while treating the regional economy as collateral damage.

## What I want next
Before anyone tries to force a false choice between “closure” and “no solution,” management should table the numbers in a way that allows real comparison. And the employee side should be prepared to discuss a credible, quantified contribution package that is structurally meaningful but does not break the company’s social and regional foundation.

If that cannot be done, then the conflict will simply deepen — and I will not support a settlement that gut-punches our plants and jobs in the state.
