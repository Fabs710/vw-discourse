### Core impasse

The central deadlock is **between the employee side and the ownership/management side on the question of structural adjustment**:

- **Works Council + Union**: reject German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as a method of restructuring; insist on binding job and site security, with only temporary or reversible measures.
- **Management + Controlling Family + Investors**: insist that the company needs **durable, structural savings** and will not rule out plant closures or workforce reductions if those are needed to restore competitiveness.
- **State Government**: wants restructuring, but only if it avoids closures as the default path and protects regional industrial capacity.

So the unresolved issue is not whether change is needed; it is **what form of change counts as “equivalent” to closures and workforce cuts, while still delivering permanent savings and credibility**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options

#### 1) Two-track restructuring package
A compromise could be built around a **two-track process**:

- **Track A:** immediate non-structural relief
  - working-time adjustments
  - bonus deferrals / moderated variable pay
  - redeployment
  - qualification and internal transfers
- **Track B:** independently verified structural review
  - plant-by-plant economics
  - quantified savings target
  - demand and capacity forecast to 2030
  - comparison of closure vs. non-closure scenarios

**Bridge function:**  
This allows employee representatives to avoid accepting closures as a preset outcome, while giving management and capital markets a route to test whether equivalent permanent savings are actually achievable without them.

---

#### 2) Employment-security-for-restructuring exchange
A second compromise could pair **time-limited employment security** with **hard restructuring commitments**:

- no forced redundancies for a defined period
- no closure decisions before a jointly reviewed business case is completed
- binding investment and product-allocation commitments for German sites
- in exchange, workforce accepts temporary concessions:
  - wage moderation
  - working-time flexibility
  - lower bonus components
  - productivity measures

**Bridge function:**  
This gives labour something concrete—security and investment—while giving management a structured path to obtain savings without immediately triggering closure conflict.

---

#### 3) Site-specific rescue and transformation model
A third option is a **differentiated footprint approach**:

- not every German site is treated identically
- sites are classified into:
  - core future-production sites,
  - transformation sites,
  - sites needing restructuring support,
  - sites only to be closed if a quantified threshold is met
- closure becomes a **last-step decision**, not a first-step assumption
- affected sites receive:
  - retraining
  - internal placement
  - phased capacity reduction
  - social safeguards

**Bridge function:**  
This may reduce the all-or-nothing conflict by preserving some industrial base while allowing management to argue that not every site must remain in its current form.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target, capacity reduction, and timeline are you willing to publish now so the other parties can test whether a closure-free alternative can truly match it?**

#### To Works Council
**If a verified alternative package delivers the same permanent savings without closures, what specific concessions are you prepared to put on the table beyond temporary working-time measures?**

#### To Industrial Union
**What would you accept as a binding, quantified labor contribution package that is durable enough to satisfy investors while still preserving employment security?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What concrete state-backed conditions—investment, training, product commitments, or governance safeguards—would you require for supporting a restructuring that avoids closures?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Which outcome matters more to you in the next round: a faster restructuring with higher conflict risk, or a slower negotiated settlement with stronger employment guarantees but potentially lower immediate savings?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What level of verified permanent cost reduction would you consider sufficient to support a deal that excludes closures, and would you accept a phased path to that target rather than immediate full implementation?**
