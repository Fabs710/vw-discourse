I will be direct.

I agree with all sides on the diagnosis that this business needs a durable competitiveness reset. I do **not** agree that we can get there by preserving the existing footprint and cost base while hoping for better margins. That would be value-destructive in a slower, more disguised form.

At the same time, I also do **not** support a reckless process that forces closures through confrontation and then leaves us with strikes, damaged trust, and an unstable company. That would destroy value just as surely as inaction.

## To the Works Council
I understand your red lines, but I cannot accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful structural change” as a governing position. Those are not solutions; they are an attempt to pre-decide the outcome before the economics are shown.

Where I agree with you is that management must be specific.  
So yes: management should table the actual savings target, the required capacity reduction, and the timeline. Generic language is not enough.

Where I disagree is your insistence that plant closures are off-limits in principle. They are not off-limits if they are genuinely needed to restore the company’s economics. The controlling owner cannot endorse a structure that preserves every site at the cost of the enterprise’s future.

## To the Union
I agree that labour should not carry the burden alone. A negotiated contribution is fair only if management and owners also commit to future investment and a credible industrial plan.

But I do **not** accept the argument that forced redundancies and closures can simply be excluded from the toolbox. If you want to rule them out, then you need to present an alternative that delivers equivalent **permanent** savings, not temporary relief.

Temporary working-time reductions, bonus deferrals, and short-term flexibility are useful only if they are part of a deeper structural package. On their own, they are not enough.

## To the Regional State Government
I respect the governance role and the need to protect regional employment. But the state should not confuse protecting the industrial base with freezing it in place.

I agree with you that closures are not the starting point. They must be justified, not assumed. That said, “prove it harder” is not a sufficient counterproposal. If plant-specific economics do not work, then the governance duty is to confront that reality, not postpone it.

## To Management
You have support on the need for structural change, but not a blank cheque.

What I need from you is not just “all options on the table,” but a disciplined, quantified plan:
- what is the exact savings gap?
- what footprint change is actually required?
- what measures are truly permanent?
- what would a closure-free alternative need to deliver?
- what investment, product allocation, and execution certainty accompany the restructuring?

If you cannot answer those questions clearly, then you do not yet have a decision-ready proposal.

## My position as controlling shareholder
The family holding’s priority is not to defend any one plant, nor to impose pain for its own sake. The priority is to protect the long-term value of the core asset. That means:

- restoring margins,
- aligning capacity with demand,
- reducing structural cost,
- preserving the ability to invest in future technology,
- and avoiding prolonged conflict that weakens the brand and the balance sheet.

So I will support a serious restructuring that achieves those goals. I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a no-change settlement dressed up as social peace,
- or a process that permanently impairs competitiveness.

## What I think should happen next
We need two things on the table in the next round:

1. **Management:** a quantified restructuring case, including the savings target, capacity reduction needed, and the economic rationale for any closures.
2. **Employee side:** a quantified alternative if it wants to avoid closures — one that shows equivalent durable savings, not just temporary concessions.

If the alternative is real, we should examine it seriously. If it is not, then the company must retain the option of deeper structural measures, including closures and workforce reductions.

I am not here to endorse conflict. I am here to ensure the company remains competitive and valuable over the long term.
