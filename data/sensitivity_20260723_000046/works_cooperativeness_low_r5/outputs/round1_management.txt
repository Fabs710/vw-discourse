I’ll be direct.

I agree with one thing you both said: this cannot be solved by slogans or by shifting the whole burden onto the workforce. We do need a negotiated outcome. But I do **not** agree with the core red lines as stated, because they would leave the company with a cost and capacity base that is still structurally uncompetitive.

Management’s position is not a bluff, and it is not a desire to tear up the social model for its own sake. We are facing a shrinking European market, severe price pressure, and domestic factory costs that are materially above what this business can sustain. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target. If we do nothing durable now, we will simply be back here in a worse position later — with fewer options, weaker finances, and more jobs at risk overall.

So let me be equally clear on what is and is not acceptable from our side:

### What is non-negotiable
- We need a **structural, durable reduction in cost and capacity**.
- We need a **genuine labour-cost contribution**.
- We need measures that restore competitiveness through **2030**, not a temporary pause that preserves excess capacity.
- We need the freedom to make the portfolio and footprint fit the market reality.

### What I am not prepared to accept
- A purely cosmetic package that preserves every site and every pattern of production while assuming the financial problem somehow disappears.
- A deal built only on temporary working-time measures, deferred pain, or “future promises” without hard cost savings.
- A settlement that guarantees the status quo and still expects management to fund the transition, new products, and technology out of an unchanged cost base.

### On plant closures and jobs
I am not treating closures as an end in themselves. I am saying plainly that **all options must be on the table**, including plant closures, capacity reductions, and job reductions, because the current structure is too expensive. If you want to avoid closures, then you need to bring forward an alternative that produces **equivalent structural savings**. That means more than short-term concessions. It means a package that changes the economics for good.

### On guarantees
You are asking for binding site guarantees and job security through 2030. I understand why you want certainty. But I will not lock the company into guarantees that prevent necessary restructuring and leave us unable to respond to market reality. Any guarantees have to be conditional on a credible savings package and must not block realignment.

### On workforce contribution
A contribution from labour is not optional if we are serious. It can take different forms — pay, working time, flexibility, productivity, bonus structures — but it has to be real and measurable. The same applies to management and ownership in terms of commitment to the future investment agenda. I do not dispute that burden-sharing is needed. I dispute the idea that labour can contribute nothing structural while expecting management to carry the full adjustment.

### On strikes and confrontation
If this turns into prolonged industrial action, that will damage output, reputation, and confidence. But I need to be equally blunt: a prolonged strike is not a substitute for an economically sound plan, and it will not change the underlying pressure we face. It can delay decisions; it cannot repeal market reality.

So here is the central point:
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it must come with a **credible, quantified, durable alternative** that cuts cost and capacity enough to put the business back on a viable path. If that alternative is real, we can discuss how it is implemented. If it is not, then the company will have to proceed with more drastic structural measures.

I am open on the **method**, but not on the **need** for deep change.
