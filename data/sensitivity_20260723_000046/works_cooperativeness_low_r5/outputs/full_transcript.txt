## What matters economically

### 1) Plant closures save fixed costs, not just labor costs
A closure is powerful because it can remove:
- direct labor,
- indirect labor,
- energy and utilities,
- maintenance,
- logistics and intra-site overhead,
- depreciation on underused assets,
- and a large share of site-level fixed cost.

That matters because in car manufacturing, when volumes fall, **fixed cost absorption deteriorates quickly**. If a plant is running well below efficient utilization, the cost per vehicle rises sharply.

### 2) Closure-free alternatives can save money, but usually less reliably
Measures like:
- working-time reductions,
- bonus moderation,
- redeployment,
- internal transfers,
- temporary wage restraint,
- productivity improvements,
- and product reallocation

can reduce cost, but they often have three limitations:

1. **They are partly variable or temporary**, not fully structural.  
2. **They may not remove site-level fixed cost** if plants remain open.  
3. **They depend on sustained labor cooperation and high execution discipline**.

That means they can narrow a cost gap, but if the company has genuine excess footprint, they often do **not** fully eliminate the economic burden.

- current production volume by German plant,

#### 3) Hybrid package with protected sites and capped workforce adjustment

- site protection for a defined list of plants,

- product and investment commitments,

This would not remove the restructuring burden, but it could reduce conflict by making the adjustment measurable and bounded.
