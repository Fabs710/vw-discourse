### Core impasse
The central deadlock is this:

- **Management / capital** want restructuring that can include closures and workforce reductions unless a closure-free package can prove **equivalent permanent savings**.
- **Works Council / Union** reject any negotiation under a closure threat and want **no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and security through 2030**.
- **State government** wants restructuring, but only if it preserves the industrial base and avoids closures as the default path.
- The **independent expert** says a structural problem is plausible, but **closures are not yet numerically proven as the only necessary option**.

So the impasse is not over whether change is needed. It is over:
1. **what counts as equivalent savings**,  
2. **whether closures may remain on the table during bargaining**, and  
3. **what binding guarantees can accompany any labour contribution**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### 1) Quantified hybrid package with conditional site protection
A possible bridge is a **hybrid restructuring package** that combines:
- working-time moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity measures,
- and product allocation changes,

with a **conditional no-closure moratorium during the negotiation period**.

In return, labour would accept a **time-limited contribution package**, but only if management commits to:
- publishing the savings gap,
- site-by-site economics,
- and a clear timetable to 2030.

This would preserve the possibility of structural adjustment while suspending closure decisions long enough to test the alternatives.

---

#### 2) Two-stage agreement: immediate relief now, structural decision later
A second option is a **two-stage deal**:

**Stage 1: Immediate stabilization**
- temporary working-time measures,
- bonus moderation,
- internal transfers,
- and a freeze on compulsory redundancies.

**Stage 2: Independent structural review**
- a verified comparison of closure, partial consolidation, and closure-free options,
- using agreed economic criteria,
- with the result binding on all sides.

This approach separates short-term peace from long-term footprint decisions.

---

#### 3) Site-by-site rescue framework with binding investment and redeployment
A third option is a **site-specific rescue model**:
- each German plant is assessed individually,
- sites with viable future products receive binding investment and volume commitments,
- weaker sites receive redeployment, retraining, or conversion plans,
- and any potential closure must first pass an agreed review threshold.

This could allow differentiated treatment rather than an all-or-nothing closure debate.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**Will you publish, by plant, the quantified savings gap, required capacity reduction, and the cost of each alternative scenario so that a closure-free option can be tested against the same standard as closures?**

#### To Works Council
**If an independently verified package delivers equivalent permanent savings without closures, what limited, time-bound employee contributions are you prepared to accept to secure it?**

#### To the Union
**Would you support a negotiated package that includes temporary labour concessions if it is paired with binding employment, investment, and product commitments through 2030?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to condition any public or political support on a binding industrial plan that includes site protection, future-product allocation, and no forced redundancies during the review period?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you accept a negotiated review process in which closures remain suspended until the independent comparison of alternatives is complete, provided the review is time-bound and economically rigorous?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**If a closure-free hybrid package produces the required NPV savings and margin recovery, will you support it even if it preserves more German capacity than a closure-first plan?**

---

### Facilitator closing formulation
The next productive step is not another exchange of positions. It is a **jointly defined decision framework**:
- agreed numbers,
- agreed comparison criteria,
- agreed time limit,
- and agreed interim protections.

If the parties want, the next round can be structured around a single question:  
**What combination of guarantees, labour contribution, and footprint adjustment is sufficient to satisfy both economic viability and employment security?**
