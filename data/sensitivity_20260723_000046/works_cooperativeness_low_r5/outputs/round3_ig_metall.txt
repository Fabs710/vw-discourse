I’ll be equally direct.

## To management
I agree with one thing: we need **numbers**, not slogans. But you still have not made the case that plant closures or forced redundancies are necessary rather than preferred.

If you want this union to consider any meaningful contribution, then put the full case on the table now:
- the exact savings gap,
- the capacity reduction needed,
- the timeline to 2030,
- the plant-by-plant economics,
- and what alternatives were genuinely tested before closure was brought in.

Until then, “closures may be needed” is not a business case. It is pressure.

And I reject your framing that a binding job guarantee automatically “locks in excess cost.” A job guarantee is not a luxury. It is the minimum condition for a credible bargain after the peace obligation has ended and members are being asked to give up money and flexibility.

## To the Works Council
I support the core of your position: no compulsory redundancies, no unilateral wage cuts, and no settlement that turns the workforce into the sole adjustment variable.

Where I want to sharpen it is this: if management finally puts a verified alternative on the table that delivers **equivalent permanent savings without closures**, then we must be ready to negotiate a real labour contribution. Not unlimited, not open-ended, but real.

That can include:
- temporary working-time measures,
- moderated variable pay,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

But only if it is tied to hard guarantees on sites, jobs, and future investment.

## To the controlling family holding and investors
You keep repeating “durable savings” as if that automatically means closures and layoffs. It does not.

A durable solution can also come from:
- structural capacity balancing,
- internal transfers,
- product allocation shifts,
- working-time arrangements,
- productivity gains agreed with labour,
- and a fair contribution from owners and management.

What you have not shown is why those tools are insufficient. If you believe they are insufficient, then show the numbers. Do not ask the workforce to accept irreversible damage on trust.

And let me be explicit: if you push plant closures, you should expect conflict. Not because we are being difficult, but because you are crossing a line that would destroy trust for years.

## To the state government
I agree that a purely cosmetic fix is not acceptable. But I need you to be clearer: “closures are not the first resort” is not enough.

If the state wants to protect the industrial base, then it should insist on:
- binding investment commitments in Germany,
- concrete future-product allocation,
- qualification and internal placement,
- and a genuine no-forced-layoffs framework.

That is how you preserve regional industry while allowing restructuring to happen in a controlled way.

## On the moderator’s compromise options
The two-track approach is acceptable only if Track A is not just a delay tactic and Track B is not a disguised closure roadmap.

What would make it serious?

### Track A
Immediate measures we can discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- bonus moderation,
- voluntary flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- and productivity measures negotiated with the workforce.

### Track B
A real review, but with clear rules:
- plant-by-plant transparency,
- independent verification,
- comparison of closure and non-closure options,
- and a binding requirement that any alternative be judged against **permanent** savings, not just temporary relief.

I will not accept a “review” whose real purpose is to postpone shutdown decisions until the pressure is lower.

## What we are prepared to put on the table
The union is prepared to discuss a **fair burden-sharing package**, but only under conditions that protect jobs and the collective bargaining framework.

That package can include:
- time-limited wage moderation,
- deferred or reduced bonuses,
- temporary working-time measures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and upskilling,
- and productivity improvements.

What it cannot include:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures as a default restructuring method,
- unilateral pay cuts,
- or dismantling of the collective wage agreement.

## What must be true for any labour contribution
If employees contribute, then management and owners must also contribute in a visible and binding way.

That means:
- investment in German plants,
- future-product commitments,
- no one-sided burden shifting,
- and security for the workforce in return.

If you want sacrifice, it must be shared. If you want flexibility, it must come with protection.

## Bottom line
I am not softening on the core red lines:
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No closure-first restructuring.**
- **No unilateral erosion of wage standards.**

But I am keeping the door open to a negotiated, quantified package if — and only if — management shows that a closure-free route can deliver the same durable effect and if capital and owners also make a fair contribution.

So the next step is not more general debate. It is numbers, verification, and binding commitments.

If that cannot be provided, then there is no basis for agreement.
