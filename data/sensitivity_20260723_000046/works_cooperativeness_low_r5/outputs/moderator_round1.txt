### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides acknowledge that the company faces a real competitiveness and cost problem.
- There is broad agreement that a purely cosmetic or short-term solution is insufficient.
- Most parties accept that some form of restructuring is needed and that labour, management, and owners may all need to contribute.
- There is general openness to negotiation rather than an immediate imposed settlement.
- Several actors accept that future investment, products, and technology commitments matter for any durable deal.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  The Works Council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point; management, the family holding, and investors keep closures on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour and the state oppose forced layoffs; management and capital do not rule out job reductions if needed.
- **Scope of workforce concessions:**  
  Labour is prepared to discuss temporary or negotiated measures; capital and management want durable, structural labour-cost and capacity reductions.
- **Guarantees vs flexibility:**  
  Labour and the state want binding site/job guarantees and protections; management, owners, and investors argue that hard guarantees could block necessary realignment.
- **Nature of the restructuring:**  
  Labour emphasizes negotiated, reversible adjustment; management and shareholders want permanent changes to cost and footprint.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No common definition yet of what counts as an “equivalent alternative” to plant closures.
- No agreed savings target, cost gap, or footprint reduction number has been put on the table.
- No concrete package yet links workforce contributions to binding investment, product, and employment commitments.
- The balance between temporary measures and permanent structural changes remains unresolved.
- There is still no shared framework for how co-determination, governance constraints, and management discretion will operate in the process.
- The parties have not reconciled regional/political employment protection with shareholder demands for durable competitiveness.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to numbers: ask management to specify the required savings, capacity reductions, and timeline.
- Ask labour and state representatives to define what concrete package could deliver comparable durable savings without closures.
- Clarify which commitments could be made binding on sites, jobs, investment, and product allocation, and under what conditions.
- Separate **temporary relief measures** from **structural measures** so the parties can compare them explicitly.
- Use the next round to test whether a negotiated alternative is economically credible, not just politically acceptable.
