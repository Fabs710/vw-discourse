### Core impasse
The dispute is now concentrated on **one central fault line**:

- **Employee side / state:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no closure threat during bargaining; they want binding security and a tested closure-free route.
- **Management / owners / investors:** they accept the need for restructuring, but insist they need to preserve the option of closures and workforce reductions if the numbers show a large structural gap.

So the impasse is not about whether the company has a problem. It is about **whether structural adjustment must include closure as an available tool, or whether a verified closure-free package can be made sufficiently durable and equivalent**.

A second, related impasse is **sequencing**:
- labour and the state want a moratorium first, then testing;
- management and capital want no pre-commitment that removes closure leverage before the numbers are settled.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridge proposals that could be put to the table.

#### 1) Time-limited, data-bound moratorium with a hard expiry
A **short closure moratorium** during a strictly defined review window, paired with:
- immediate publication of plant-by-plant economics,
- a fixed deadline for the independent review,
- preservation of investment planning,
- and explicit confirmation that the moratorium does **not** preclude later action if the review shows the gap cannot be closed.

This may address labour/state concerns about bargaining under threat, while preserving management’s right to act after verification.

#### 2) Conditional no-closure pathway with trigger points
A **provisional no-closure arrangement** that remains in force only if the closure-free package hits pre-agreed milestones, such as:
- minimum savings levels,
- utilization improvements,
- product allocation commitments,
- and staffing reduction through attrition/internal redeployment rather than forced redundancies.

If the triggers are missed, the parties revert to renegotiation and a broader restructuring menu.

#### 3) Hybrid package with protected sites and capped workforce adjustment
A **hybrid settlement** that combines:
- site protection for a defined list of plants,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- time-limited labor contributions,
- product and investment commitments,
- and a capped, quantified workforce adjustment achieved via voluntary exits, redeployment, and qualification measures.

This would not remove the restructuring burden, but it could reduce conflict by making the adjustment measurable and bounded.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
Can you commit to publishing, within a fixed timeframe, the full plant-by-plant business case and to testing a closure-free package against the same economic assumptions as the closure scenario?

#### To the Works Council
If the numbers show a meaningful but not fully closed gap, are you prepared to discuss a quantified, time-limited labor contribution package linked to binding site and job guarantees?

#### To the Industrial Union
What exact conditions would make you accept a short review moratorium without treating it as a hidden pathway to shutdowns later?

#### To the Regional State Government
Would you support a time-limited moratorium only if it is tied to hard deadlines, independent verification, and a published investment-and-product allocation plan?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a conditional no-closure pathway for a defined period if it delivers verified savings and utilization gains, even if it reduces your immediate flexibility?

#### To Institutional Investors
Would you treat a short moratorium as acceptable if it is strictly time-boxed, transparent, and accompanied by a credible path to durable savings and margin recovery?
