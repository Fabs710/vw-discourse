We are aligned with the works council on the core point: restructuring cannot be paid for by the workforce alone.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side:

- No compulsory redundancies.
- No plant closures.
- No dismantling of the collective wage framework.
- No “competitiveness” strategy that simply transfers risk, cuts, and insecurity onto employees.

We have already shown that we are willing to negotiate. Our counter-proposal was not symbolic: we signaled openness to labour-cost contributions, capacity reductions, and employee contributions if — and only if — there is real site and job security in return. That remains our position.

Where we agree:
- The company faces real pressure.
- The transition cannot be ignored.
- Some contribution from labour can be part of a settlement.

Where we disagree:
- Management’s current direction treats closures and forced layoffs as the starting point. That is unacceptable.
- A one-sided savings programme that preserves ownership and managerial freedom while asking workers to absorb the pain is neither fair nor durable.
- “Future investment” is not a justification for hollowing out the workforce today.

If management wants a serious settlement, then it must move on three points:

1. Binding guarantees for German sites and jobs  
   We need protection against plant closures and forced redundancies. If there is to be restructuring, it must come through natural attrition, redeployment, qualification, working-time tools, and voluntary instruments.

2. Shared sacrifice, not worker sacrifice alone  
   We are prepared to discuss temporary labour-cost measures, bonus restraint, working-time adjustments, and other negotiated contributions. But owners, shareholders, and management must also take a visible hit. There must be real burden-sharing.

3. A credible industrial future  
   Any savings package must be tied to investment, product commitments, and a medium-term industrial plan that keeps production and skills in Germany.

If those conditions are not met, then we will not legitimize this restructuring. The peace obligation has ended, and our members are already mobilised. Warning strikes were the first step, not the last.

So our message to management and owners is simple:
If you want agreement, come with guarantees, fairness, and a future for the workforce.  
If you come with closures, layoffs, and unilateral cuts, you will get resistance.
