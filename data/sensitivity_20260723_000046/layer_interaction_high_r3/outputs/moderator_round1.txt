## 1) What has been agreed

- All parties accept that the company faces **real competitive and cost pressure**.
- There is broad agreement that a **purely cosmetic or short-term fix is not sufficient**.
- Most parties accept that **some form of restructuring is necessary**.
- There is also broad openness to a package that includes:
  - **redeployment**
  - **qualification/training**
  - **working-time measures**
  - **voluntary exit instruments**
  - **capacity adjustments**
  - some form of **workforce contribution**
- Several parties acknowledge that **investment and an industrial plan through 2030** are needed for credibility.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain

- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state shareholder reject closures as a starting point and want them excluded if possible.
  - Management, family holding, and investors will not rule them out if needed for competitiveness.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee side and state shareholder reject forced layoffs.
  - Management, family holding, and investors want them kept possible if necessary.

- **Binding site/job guarantees through 2030**
  - Employee side wants firm guarantees.
  - Management and investors say unconditional guarantees may block necessary restructuring.
  - State shareholder is open to guarantees but only within a negotiated restructuring.

- **Burden-sharing**
  - Everyone says burden-sharing is needed, but there is no agreement on **who bears how much**.
  - Employees want owners and management to take visible cuts too.
  - Management and investors insist labour must also make a substantial contribution.

- **Extent of structural change**
  - Employee side wants negotiated adjustment with protection of sites and jobs.
  - Management and investors insist the solution must reduce cost and capacity **durably**, even if that requires major structural change.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed

- **Jobs and sites vs. competitiveness**
  - No shared answer yet on how to secure employment and industrial presence while still achieving the required cost reduction.

- **Voluntary/soft measures vs. hard capacity cuts**
  - It remains unclear whether voluntary exits, reduced hours, redeployment, and qualification can close the full gap.

- **Short-term peace vs. long-term viability**
  - A settlement that avoids conflict may still fail if it does not materially improve margins and capacity.

- **Governance and co-determination vs. speed of restructuring**
  - The process needs employee and state involvement, but management and owners want enough flexibility to act decisively.

- **Scope and timing of savings**
  - No party has yet provided a jointly accepted, quantified path showing exactly how much each measure contributes and by when.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round

- Move from principles to **numbers, scenarios, and sequencing**.
- Ask management to present a **quantified restructuring case**:
  - required savings,
  - excess capacity,
  - impact of each measure,
  - and whether alternatives to closures can fully close the gap.
- Ask employee representatives to specify **which non-closure instruments** they can support and under what conditions.
- Ask owners and the state shareholder to clarify the **limits of acceptable guarantees** and what burden-sharing they are prepared to back.
- The next round should focus on **one concrete package option at a time**, rather than repeating red lines in general terms.
