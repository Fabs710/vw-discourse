### Core impasse
The central deadlock is over **whether restructuring can be confined to “soft” measures** or must retain **closures and forced redundancies as available instruments**.

In practical terms, the split is between:

- **Employee-side/state-side position:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding site guarantees, and restructuring only through negotiated soft tools.
- **Management/owners/investor position:** no absolute red lines; all instruments must remain available if needed to restore competitiveness.

So the negotiation is not yet about the size of the contribution. It is about **the permissible toolset** and **the level of certainty the company can give before the numbers are fully tested**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridging positions that could be tabled without endorsing any side:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a review gate
A package could be structured in **two stages**:
- **Stage 1:** immediate use of soft instruments only: redeployment, qualification, working-time adjustments, voluntary exits, temporary labour-cost measures, and management/owner contributions.
- **Stage 2:** a pre-agreed review point, based on a jointly audited plant-by-plant case, where harder measures would only be considered **if and only if** the savings gap remains demonstrably unclosed.

This would give employee representatives a protected first phase while preserving management’s claim that all options remain available if the numbers require it.

#### 2) Site-specific guarantees with conditional flexibility
Instead of a blanket “all sites or no sites” position, the parties could consider:
- **binding guarantees for core production, employment volume ranges, and investment commitments at each German site through 2030**, and
- a **narrowly defined exception mechanism** only for sites shown by joint audit to be structurally non-viable.

This would replace an absolute closure threat with a high evidentiary threshold and a procedural lock.

#### 3) Burden-sharing ladder tied to measurable triggers
A sequenced burden-sharing framework could be set:
- management and owners contribute first,
- workforce contributions activate next,
- and only if a quantified gap still remains do additional measures become available.

Each step would be linked to **specific numeric triggers**:
- savings achieved,
- capacity reduced,
- margin improvement,
- and investment commitments delivered.

This creates a structured escalation path rather than an open-ended clash over principles.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If closures and forced redundancies remain fully off the table, **which specific combination of soft instruments and workforce contributions do you believe can close the quantified gap by 2030, and what evidence supports that claim plant by plant?**

#### To the Industrial Union
What concrete concession package would you accept as sufficient to suspend escalation while the numbers are reviewed, and **what would make you conclude that the company has actually exhausted non-coercive alternatives?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to a **jointly audited, plant-by-plant restructuring case** with explicit savings assumptions for every measure, and **will you agree not to invoke closures or compulsory redundancies before that audit is completed?**

#### To the Regional State Government
What form of regional or political support are you prepared to provide **if** a negotiated package secures jobs and investment but requires limited flexibility on footprint or staffing, and **what are your non-negotiable conditions for backing the deal?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a **conditional non-closure framework** for German sites if the joint numbers show that soft measures can close most, but not all, of the gap — and if not, **what level of residual gap would justify hard capacity cuts in your view?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
What exact financial thresholds would make you accept a soft-measures-first package as sufficiently value-preserving, and **how much slower margin recovery would you tolerate in exchange for avoiding immediate hard cuts?**
