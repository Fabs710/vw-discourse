We are prepared to discuss a restructuring, but not at the expense of German jobs and sites.

Our position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.
- Any restructuring must be negotiated, socially responsible, and backed by binding guarantees.

If management wants workforce contributions, then the company must first commit to:
- securing all German sites through 2030,
- protecting core production and investment at those sites,
- using alternatives to forced layoffs: internal redeployment, working-time measures, voluntary instruments, qualification, and capacity adjustments,
- involving employee representatives substantively in shaping the transformation, not presenting a fait accompli.

We do not deny the economic pressure. But “competitiveness” cannot mean shifting the burden entirely onto the workforce while management and owners decide on closures from above. That is exactly the kind of betrayal that destroys trust and provokes resistance.

So our baseline is:
- restructuring only with job and site guarantees,
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- no attack on co-determination,
- and a real industrial plan for the future of every German plant.

If those conditions are not met, we will oppose the proposal with all the means available to us.
