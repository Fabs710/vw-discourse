### 1) What has been agreed
- The company faces a real competitiveness and restructuring problem.
- A cosmetic compromise will not be sufficient.
- The discussion must be based on **quantified, plant-by-plant analysis** rather than slogans.
- There is broad support for **soft measures first**, including:
  - redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - working-time adjustments
  - voluntary exits / retirement pathways
  - internal mobility
  - capacity rebalancing
- There is broad agreement that **burden-sharing cannot fall on labour alone**; management and owners should contribute visibly.
- Several parties accept the need for an **independent joint audit/model** to test savings, capacity, and margin impact through 2030.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** employee-side and state actors want closures excluded; management, owners, and investors want closures to remain available if the audit shows structural overcapacity.
- **Forced redundancies:** employee-side and state actors reject them; management, owners, and investors want them retained as a fallback if needed.
- **Guarantees:** employee-side and state actors want binding site/job guarantees through 2030; management, owners, and investors say such guarantees cannot be unconditional if they limit restructuring flexibility.
- **Role of soft measures:** employee-side and state actors believe soft measures and burden-sharing should be exhausted before hard actions; management, owners, and investors believe soft measures may not be sufficient if there is persistent structural overcapacity.
- **Meaning of the audit/review:** employee-side and state actors see it as a test to justify non-destructive restructuring; management, owners, and investors see it as a test that may confirm the need for hard capacity cuts.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **size of the residual gap** after soft measures and ownership/management contributions.
- The **cost and timing** of soft measures versus hard measures.
- Whether preserving sites/jobs now could create **long-term competitiveness risk** if capacity remains excessive.
- Whether closing sites or using compulsory redundancies would create **industrial conflict, reputational damage, and political fallout** that offsets financial gains.
- What level of **investment/product commitment** is needed to make any restructuring credible.
- The practical definition of “conditionality”: what happens if the audit shows the gap is only partly closed.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to a **single shared analytical framework**.
- Ask the expert/auditor to present a **site-by-site savings and capacity model** with:
  - current utilization
  - recurring savings from each measure
  - one-off restructuring costs
  - residual gap through 2030
- Require each side to state:
  1. what minimum outcome it can accept,
  2. what concessions it is prepared to make,
  3. what specific trigger would change its position.
- Keep the next round focused on **tested numbers, sequencing, and conditional next steps**, not on repeating absolute red lines.
