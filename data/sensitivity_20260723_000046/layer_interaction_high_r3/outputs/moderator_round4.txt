## Core impasse

The dispute now hinges on **one central fault line**:

**Whether the restructuring must preserve an absolute non-closure / no-forced-redundancy line, or whether closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available as fallback instruments if the quantified gap persists after soft measures.**

In practice, this means the parties are not mainly divided on whether restructuring is needed. They are divided on:

1. **the acceptable boundary of restructuring tools**
2. **how much certainty the workforce needs up front**
3. **how much flexibility ownership and management need to preserve**
4. **what the joint audit is supposed to test: a non-closure solution or all options**

There is also a secondary impasse:

**No side fully trusts the others’ sequencing.**  
Employees fear that an audit is being used to justify closures later; owners and management fear that a pre-set red line makes the audit meaningless if the numbers require hard measures.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise position 1: “Soft-measures-first, hard measures only by jointly triggered threshold”
A possible bridge is a **sequenced restructuring agreement** in which:

- Phase 1 is limited to:
  - management/owner burden-sharing
  - redeployment
  - qualification
  - working-time measures
  - voluntary exits
  - internal mobility
  - temporary cost relief
- Phase 2 is a **jointly commissioned audit**
- Phase 3 only opens if the audit shows a defined residual gap above a pre-agreed threshold

Under this model, **closures and compulsory redundancies are not removed entirely**, but they become permissible only if:
- the audit is independently validated,
- the residual gap exceeds a clearly defined benchmark,
- and the parties fail to agree on additional non-destructive measures.

This gives labour stronger protection than an open-ended threat, while preserving a conditional fallback for management and owners.

---

### Compromise position 2: “Conditional no-closure pact with sunset and review triggers”
Another bridge is a **temporary no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy commitment**, for example through a defined period such as **until the audit is completed plus a review point in 2027 or 2028**.

In return, the employee side and state side would accept:
- deeper soft measures now,
- more explicit productivity and flexibility commitments,
- measurable burden-sharing from management and owners,
- and a stricter monitoring regime.

If the company misses agreed financial or operational milestones, the parties would reopen the package under a formal review mechanism.

This would not be a permanent veto on hard measures, but it would convert them from an immediate bargaining threat into a **last-resort, milestone-triggered option**.

---

### Compromise position 3: “Site-specific protection instead of blanket protection”
A third bridge is to replace blanket positions with **site-by-site treatment**:

- sites meeting defined viability and utilization thresholds receive stronger protection
- sites below agreed thresholds enter a special transformation track
- in that track, the parties first exhaust soft measures, investment commitments, and capacity rebalancing
- only after that can the company propose harder actions, subject to enhanced consultation and social safeguards

This would allow the employee side to defend viable plants more strongly, while giving management and owners a route to address genuinely subscale sites without treating all locations identically.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**If the audit shows one or more sites remain structurally below viable utilization even after all soft measures, what specific additional non-destructive measures would you accept before you would consider any harder capacity action?**

### Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited, conditional framework in which closures and compulsory redundancies are only available if an independent audit confirms a residual gap above an agreed threshold and all softer measures have been exhausted?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact numerical threshold or decision rule would you accept as sufficient proof that soft measures are no longer enough, so that the hard-capacity question is not left entirely open-ended?**

### Regional State Government
**What governance mechanism are you prepared to use to make your “no closures, no forced redundancies” position operational if the audit identifies one or more genuinely non-viable sites?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a binding interim protection period if it were paired with audited milestones, defined management contributions, and a jointly agreed trigger for reopening the hard-measures question?**

### Institutional Investors
**If the audit shows the residual gap is modest rather than large, would you commit to supporting a non-closure settlement even if it means lower short-term flexibility for shareholders?**

### Expert: Independent Restructuring Finance / Capacity
**What minimum data set and scenario design would you require so that both sides can trust the audit result as a fair test of the non-closure path?**

### Expert: Industrial Capacity / Rest
**What would be your clearest indicator that a plant is subscale enough that “keeping it open” is no longer economically equivalent to “preserving viable capacity”?**
