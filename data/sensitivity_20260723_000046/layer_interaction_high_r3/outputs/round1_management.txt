We hear the position of the works council and the union. Let me respond clearly.

First, I do agree on one point: this restructuring cannot be a cosmetic exercise. A short-term patch that leaves our cost base uncompetitive would fail the company, the workforce, and the owners. We need a durable solution that restores the brand’s competitiveness and funds investment into the next product cycle and technology through 2030.

But I have to be equally clear: the current German cost and capacity structure is not sustainable. We cannot ignore that our European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our domestic factories are materially above target on cost. If we do nothing substantial, we will lose more ground, not less.

So where I disagree is on the idea that “no closures, no forced redundancies, no deep structural change” is a viable baseline. It is not. If we preserve every site and every existing structure, we will simply preserve the problem.

On the specific demands:

- No plant closures in Germany: I cannot accept that as an absolute line. We are not talking about closures for their own sake, but we are also not ruling them out if they are necessary to achieve a sustainable structure.
- No compulsory redundancies: I prefer to minimise forced job cuts, but I will not accept a framework that rules out all structural workforce reduction upfront.
- Binding site guarantees through 2030: I cannot commit to blanket guarantees if they prevent the restructuring needed to make the business viable.
- Shared sacrifice: yes, there must be a labour-cost contribution. That is non-negotiable if we are to regain competitiveness.

That said, I am not coming here only to repeat demands. I am prepared to discuss how the savings are achieved, provided they are real and durable. That means we should look seriously at:

- capacity reductions,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exit and retirement instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and a negotiated contribution on labour costs.

But let me be explicit: these alternatives must add up to structural savings, not merely postpone decisions. If they do not close the gap in a credible way, then they are not enough.

To the union’s point about burden-sharing: management is prepared to do its part, and the owners and the group must also support a credible transformation path. But visible burden-sharing does not eliminate the need for workforce contribution. We need both.

So my position is this:

1. We are prepared to negotiate a serious settlement.
2. We need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.
3. Labour-cost contributions are required.
4. I am open to alternatives to closures if — and only if — they produce equivalent structural savings and restore medium-term margins.
5. If the response is simply “no closures, no cuts, no change,” then there is no basis for agreement.

If you want us to move away from plant closures as the central instrument, then bring a package that actually delivers:
- a measurable and lasting reduction in cost base,
- credible capacity adjustment,
- and a clear path back to competitiveness.

Anything less would be asking management to accept an uncompetitive status quo. We will not do that.
