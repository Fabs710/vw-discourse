As the regional state shareholder, I want to engage directly and clearly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the controlling owners, and the investors on one point: this company does need a **serious, durable restructuring**. A cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base broken would be irresponsible.

I also agree that:
- labour must contribute,
- the solution has to be quantified,
- and the company needs an industrial plan that works beyond the next few quarters.

## Where I disagree sharply
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain normal or acceptable instruments in this situation.

That is the line I cannot cross.

Why? Because for this company, in this state, closures are not just an efficiency measure. They mean lasting damage to regional employment, supplier networks, municipal finances, skills, and trust in the co-determination model that has sustained the company for decades. If you turn closure into the default option, you are not just restructuring cost — you are weakening the company’s social and political foundation.

## To the works council and union
I hear your insistence on no closures and no forced redundancies. On the substance, I support the direction you are pushing:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- capacity adjustment,
- and negotiated contributions.

That is the right first line of response.

But I also need to say this plainly: it is not enough to repeat “no closures” and “no compulsory layoffs” as a slogan. If we want to defend that position credibly, we need to show that a **fully negotiated alternative package** can close the gap in a way that is economically real, not just politically attractive.

So I agree with you that closures should not be the starting point.  
I do **not** agree with treating the numbers as irrelevant. The company must show, transparently and plant by plant, what can be achieved without closures.

## To management
You are right to insist on competitiveness. I am not asking the company to preserve every existing structure unchanged.

But I am telling you that you will not get a stable restructuring by starting with threats to German plants and then asking the workforce to trust you. That is politically reckless and industrially risky.

If you want a settlement that the state shareholder can support, then I expect:
1. a quantified restructuring case,
2. a serious test of non-closure alternatives,
3. visible burden-sharing from management and owners,
4. and binding commitments to production, product allocation, and investment at German sites.

You have not yet made the case that closures are the only credible path. Until you do, I will oppose making them the centrepiece of the package.

## To the controlling family owners and investors
I understand your focus on long-term enterprise value. But value is not created by cutting plants on paper and destroying the company’s regional legitimacy in the process.

A settlement that preserves flexibility for ownership but removes trust from the workforce is not durable. It may look decisive in a spreadsheet, but it can generate prolonged conflict, strikes, reputational damage, and political backlash that undermine value further.

So I do not accept the argument that guarantees and site commitments are merely “political comfort.” They are part of the company’s capacity to function in this region.

## What I can support
I can support a restructuring package if it is built on the following principles:

- **No plant closures as the starting instrument**
- **No compulsory redundancies where avoidable**
- **Binding site and investment commitments**
- **Meaningful workforce contribution through negotiated, soft instruments**
- **Visible burden-sharing from management and owners**
- **A transparent, quantified industrial plan through 2030**

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a package that normalises closures,
- a package that treats forced redundancies as an ordinary fallback,
- or a package that asks the workforce to surrender protections while owners and management stay insulated.

## My bottom line
I am open to a difficult restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and co-determination as the price of “competitiveness.”

If management wants my support, it must bring a package that proves:
- the company can regain competitiveness,
- the regional industrial base can be preserved,
- and the workforce is not being asked to pay alone.

If that cannot be done, then I will use the leverage I have to stop a bad outcome.
