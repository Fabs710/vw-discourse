We are prepared to move from principles to substance — but let me be equally clear: **we will not negotiate under the threat that closures and compulsory redundancies are the default solution.** If management wants a deal, it must prove that it can secure the same restructuring effect **without** breaking the social compact.

## First: what we agree on
I do agree on several points raised by others:

- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic patch.
- The burden cannot fall **only** on the workforce.
- A negotiated settlement is better than an open-ended conflict.
- Labour can and should contribute through **working-time flexibility, capacity reduction, bonus restraint, and other negotiated cost measures**.
- Future investment and product commitments are necessary.

So yes: we are ready for a serious package.

## Where I strongly disagree
### 1) “Closures must remain on the table”
No. Not as a starting point, and not as a bargaining weapon.

A plant closure is not just another restructuring measure. It is a permanent destruction of jobs, skills, supplier networks, and regional industrial capacity. If management wants to argue that a closure is unavoidable, it must first show — transparently and concretely — that **no alternative combination** of capacity reduction, internal transfers, working-time measures, and negotiated cost relief can achieve the target.

So far, nobody has shown that.

### 2) “No forced redundancies only if the numbers work”
That is turning the logic upside down. Forced redundancies are not a neutral efficiency tool; they are a social transfer of risk onto employees. We reject them because the workforce must not pay for strategic and managerial decisions through compulsory job loss.

If jobs must be reduced, then the first instruments must be:
- natural attrition,
- voluntary departures,
- early retirement arrangements,
- internal redeployment,
- reduced working time,
- and negotiated temporary contributions.

Compulsory layoffs are the last resort, not the preferred instrument.

### 3) “Equivalent savings” cannot mean wage erosion only
Some here keep saying labour contributions must be “substantial and lasting.” Fine. But they must be **fairly distributed**.

That means:
- management must contribute visibly,
- owners must contribute visibly,
- and employees must not be expected to carry the entire adjustment through pay cuts alone.

We are open to:
- reduction or suspension of bonuses,
- moderate wage restraint for a defined period,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- flexibility agreements,
- and even employee financial contributions if they are matched by real site and job security.

What we will not accept is a package that preserves shareholder expectations while hollowing out wages, guarantees, and employment protection.

## To management
If you want credibility, put forward the actual numbers:
- What exact cost reduction is required?
- How much comes from labour costs?
- How much from management and overhead?
- How much from owners?
- What savings come from capacity reduction without closure?
- What investment is committed for which sites?

Until then, “closures may be necessary” sounds less like analysis and more like pressure.

## To the family holding and investors
You say you want long-term competitiveness. So do we. But competitiveness is not achieved by declaring German sites expendable and then calling that realism.

If the business needs a reset, then the reset must include:
- owner discipline,
- management discipline,
- and a binding industrial future for the sites that remain.

We are not interested in preserving every legacy structure. We are interested in preserving **jobs, bargaining standards, and viable industrial sites**.

## To the state government
Your insistence on site protection is correct, and we need you to use your influence accordingly. But that cannot remain rhetorical. If you mean no unnecessary closures, then help force management to present a serious non-closure restructuring plan with measurable commitments.

## Our position for round 2
We are ready to negotiate a package with these elements:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Capacity reduction through negotiated means**
4. **Working-time and bonus measures**
5. **A temporary but meaningful employee contribution**
6. **Visible management and shareholder contributions**
7. **Binding investment and product commitments**
8. **A durable collective agreement with clear end dates and safeguards**

If management can show that such a package delivers the required savings, we are prepared to work on the details immediately.

If management instead insists on closures as a precondition, then it is not seeking a settlement — it is seeking capitulation. In that case, it should expect industrial resistance, not consent.
