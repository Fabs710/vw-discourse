Let me be equally direct.

I hear the concern about jobs, sites, and regional impact. I do not dismiss it. But I cannot accept the premise that **German plant closures are off the table before the restructuring math has even been tested**. If we continue to treat every site as sacrosanct, then we are not solving the crisis — we are postponing it, while the cost base keeps drifting further out of line.

## Where I agree
- **A purely one-sided burden on employees would be wrong and unworkable.**
- **We need a negotiated settlement**, not a destructive open-ended conflict.
- **Future investment, product allocation, and an industrial perspective** are essential if we want any restructuring to hold.
- **Management, owners, and the company must all contribute**. That is not in dispute.

## Where I disagree
### 1. “No closures” is not a credible starting point
The company is facing a structural competitiveness problem:
- European demand is shrinking,
- competitors are leaner and faster,
- and our German cost base remains materially too high.

In that situation, saying “no plant closures, no matter what” is not a negotiating position — it is a veto on reality. I am not proposing closures for their own sake. I am saying we need to be open to them if they are the only way to achieve a **durable** reduction in capacity and fixed cost.

### 2. We need real structural savings, not a cosmetic deal
I will not support an arrangement that:
- preserves all current capacity,
- protects all legacy guarantees unchanged,
- and relies on temporary measures to bridge a permanent problem.

That would leave us with the same cost base, the same capacity mismatch, and no credible path back to margin targets.

### 3. Workforce contributions must be substantial and lasting
I welcome discussion of:
- working-time changes,
- bonus restraint,
- flexibility,
- capacity adjustment,
- and other negotiated labour-cost measures.

But they must produce a **material and durable** effect. A small, short-lived concession does not solve the problem. We need something that changes the economics of the German footprint through to 2030.

## On the state government’s position
I understand the political responsibility you carry. But regional protection cannot override the company’s survival logic. If the result is a company that cannot compete, then every site becomes vulnerable anyway — just later, and under worse conditions.

So yes, I am prepared to discuss alternatives to closures. In fact, if the employee side and political stakeholders can deliver **equivalent durable savings and capacity reduction without closures**, I will look at that seriously. But that equivalence must be real, not assumed.

## Bottom line
My position remains:
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No preservation of uncompetitive structures**
- **No short-term patch that leaves the company overburdened**
- **Yes to a negotiated package if it delivers deep, durable cost and capacity reduction**

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that genuinely replaces the structural savings closures would deliver. Otherwise, management’s restructuring case remains necessary and on the table.
