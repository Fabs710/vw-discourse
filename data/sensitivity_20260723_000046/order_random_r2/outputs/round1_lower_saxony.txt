I agree with both of you on one essential point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a symbolic one**, and the future industrial base must remain strong enough to justify continued investment.

But I need to be equally clear about where I draw the line.

## Where I agree
- **The burden cannot fall on the workforce alone.** That would be neither fair nor sustainable.
- **A negotiated package is preferable to confrontation.** A prolonged industrial conflict would harm the company, the regional economy, and ultimately the jobs we are all trying to protect.
- **Competitiveness matters.** If the company remains structurally unprofitable, then no one wins in the long run.

## Where I disagree
I **do not accept plant closures in Germany as a starting assumption**. For us as a regional state, that is not a small detail or a bargaining chip to be used casually. It would have direct consequences for:
- regional employment,
- supplier networks,
- public finances,
- and the company’s role as an economic anchor.

Likewise, I cannot support a restructuring logic that treats compulsory redundancies as the easiest route. If management wants support, it must first show that all serious alternatives have been exhausted:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- internal transfers,
- natural attrition,
- temporary sacrifice from management and owners,
- and a credible investment plan for the remaining sites.

## What would be acceptable to us
A solution can be supported if it meets these conditions:
1. **No outright closure of German sites unless there is no alternative at all, and that has been proven transparently.**
2. **No forced redundancies as the preferred instrument.**
3. **A fair burden-sharing package**, including management and shareholder contribution.
4. **Clear site commitments and future product allocation** for the plants that remain.
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s role**, including our blocking and supervisory-board rights.

## On the family’s point
You are right that the company must remain economically viable. But “competitiveness” cannot become a code word for pushing the full adjustment cost onto workers and regions that have carried the company for decades. If there is a real restructuring case, it must be proven in a way that justifies the social and political damage it would cause.

## My position
The state government is prepared to support a **serious negotiated restructuring** with visible contributions from all sides. But we will **not** back a plan that includes unnecessary plant closures, or that sidelines our co-determination role, or that guts regional employment.

If management and owners want our support, they need to come forward with a **site-preserving, job-protecting industrial plan** first. Only then can we discuss the scale and form of workforce contributions.
