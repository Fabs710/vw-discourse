We are aligned on the objective, but not yet on the burden-sharing standard.

## Where we agree
- **The company needs a durable return to competitive margins.**
- **A purely cosmetic settlement would be unacceptable.**
- **A prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would destroy value.**
- **Employees, management, and owners must all contribute.**

That said, I need to be very clear from the capital markets perspective:

## What we support
We would support a **credible, quantified restructuring package** if it delivers:
1. **material, durable cost reduction**,
2. **a real capacity adjustment**,
3. **clear timeline to margin improvement**,
4. **deal certainty**,
5. and **no repeat of the current cost overhang**.

If the package can reach that through negotiated means rather than closures, that is fine. We do not have an ideological attachment to plant closures. We have an attachment to **returns, competitiveness, and credibility**.

## Where we disagree
### 1. “No closures” cannot be an absolute
I understand the political and social objections, but from our standpoint an upfront ban on closures is not acceptable if it prevents the company from fixing an uncompetitive footprint.

If alternative measures can produce the same savings and capacity reduction, great. But if they cannot, then **preserving every site becomes value-destructive**. We will not back a solution that protects all locations while margins remain structurally impaired.

### 2. “Future investment” is not a substitute for hard savings
Investment commitments matter, but only if they are backed by a cost base that can actually fund them. Promising future models while leaving the current structure unchanged is not credible.

### 3. Temporary concessions are not enough
We are not interested in short-lived wage restraint or one-off measures that fade after a year or two. We need a package that changes the economics on a lasting basis. Otherwise the market will treat it as delay, not restructuring.

## To the trade union and works side
Your willingness to discuss flexibility, working-time changes, and negotiated contributions is helpful. But your red line of “no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies” is only sustainable if it still leaves the company with equivalent structural savings.

If it doesn’t, then you are asking shareholders to absorb the downside of an uncompetitive footprint indefinitely. That is not acceptable.

## To the state government
We understand your regional role, but the company cannot be treated as a permanent employment guarantee scheme. If the German footprint is too expensive, then refusing hard decisions merely postpones the problem and weakens the business further.

## To management
We will support a hard restructuring if it is:
- specific,
- credible,
- and executed with discipline.

But we will not support a vague threat of closures used as leverage for a weak deal. If closures are genuinely necessary, management must be ready to prove it. If they are not necessary, then it should not insist on them for their own sake.

## Our bottom line
As institutional investors, we are prepared to back **deep structural savings**. We are **not** prepared to back:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- or a package that protects every legacy structure while postponing adjustment.

If the other stakeholders can deliver **equivalent durable savings without closures**, we can support that. If not, then the restructuring must be real enough to restore margins, even if that means painful measures.
