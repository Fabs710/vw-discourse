Let me be equally direct: we are not going to solve a structural competitiveness problem by declaring every hard tool off-limits in advance.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on a few fundamentals:
- The company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic deal.
- A **one-sided burden on employees** would be unacceptable.
- A prolonged strike would damage value, output, and trust.
- Any solution must be **quantified plant by plant**.
- **Capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and visible management/owner contributions** are all legitimate parts of the package.

That is the common ground.

## Where I disagree — sharply
### 1) “No closures” is not a viable baseline
The union and works council keep treating closures as if they were merely a bargaining threat. They are not. They are a real restructuring instrument when a site is structurally uncompetitive.

The expert input was helpful, but it does **not** support the claim that every plant can be preserved. It says hybrid solutions can capture a large share of closure economics **in some cases** — not that they can always replace them. That distinction matters.

If a plant’s economics are weak enough that a hybrid package cannot close the gap, then closure remains on the table. I will not pre-commit to preserving all sites regardless of cost.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute red line
I understand the preference for attrition, voluntary exits, early retirement, and redeployment. Those should absolutely be used first.

But I will not accept a blanket rule that forbids compulsory redundancies under all circumstances. If the headcount bridge is insufficient, then the company cannot be trapped by an ideological veto on workforce reduction. The question is not whether redundancies are pleasant; the question is whether the cost base can be brought to a level that restores competitiveness.

### 3) Site guarantees cannot override business reality
To the state and labour side: a site guarantee only has value if the site is viable under the future cost structure. A promise that freezes an uncompetitive footprint is not protection; it is postponement.

I hear the desire for security. But security has to be earned by a structure that can actually deliver margins and fund future investment.

## On the expert input
The expert has said two things that matter:
- non-closure packages can often recover **60–90%** of closure-like savings in the right circumstances;
- but closures remain the strongest tool where asset or structural weakness is severe.

That is exactly why I keep saying: show the numbers plant by plant.

If the model demonstrates that a hybrid package is truly equivalent in durable economic effect, I will support it.  
If it does not, then closures and other hard tools stay available.

## To the union and works council
You keep asking management to prove that alternatives are insufficient. Fair enough — but you are not entitled to define the answer before the analysis is done.

Bring forward a package that is actually strong enough:
- real capacity reduction,
- multi-year labor-cost relief,
- genuine overhead cuts,
- a credible headcount bridge,
- and a future industrial plan.

A “no closures, no redundancies, no guarantees needed” position is not a restructuring proposal. It is a refusal to confront the economics.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree with you on one key point: we need a **durable** solution, not temporary relief.

Where I would push you harder is this: do not reduce this to a philosophical debate about closures. The real question is whether a non-closure or hybrid package can **permanently** restore the cost base and margins. If it can, I’m open. If it cannot, sentiment is not a substitute for strategy.

## To the state government
I respect your mandate and the statutory role you hold. But that role cannot be used to freeze industrial decline.

I am prepared to back a solution that preserves **viable** German sites and ties them to investment and product commitments. I am not prepared to back a framework that treats every existing site as untouchable regardless of economics.

## My position
I remain open to:
- avoiding closures where the numbers truly work,
- avoiding compulsory redundancies where the headcount bridge is credible,
- preserving viable sites,
- and agreeing a negotiated workforce contribution.

But only if the package delivers:
1. **material and durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity reduction**
3. **a credible headcount bridge**
4. **multi-year labor-cost relief**
5. **visible management and owner contributions**
6. **binding investment and product allocation for the retained footprint**

If those conditions are not met, then closures remain available, and so do compulsory redundancies.

## What will not move me
What will not move me is:
- a blanket “no closures” rule,
- a no-layoffs promise without quantified proof,
- or a settlement that protects legacy structures while leaving the company uncompetitive.

We do not restore competitiveness by preserving everything. We restore it by removing overcapacity, lowering fixed costs, and freeing capital for future products and technology through 2030.
