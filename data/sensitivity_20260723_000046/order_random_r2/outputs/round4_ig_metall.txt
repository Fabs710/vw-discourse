We have now reached the point where the debate is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **who bears the cost, how permanently, and under what guarantees**.

So I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with the parts of the other positions that are serious:

- The company needs a **durable** restructuring.
- A temporary patch is not enough.
- **Capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions** are legitimate elements.
- The discussion must be **quantified and plant-specific**.
- A long industrial conflict would be damaging for everyone.

That is exactly why we have been willing to negotiate a real labor contribution.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “Closures remain available” is not a neutral statement
Management, investors, and the family keep repeating that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available if the numbers demand it. That sounds reasonable in the abstract, but in practice it is a pressure tactic.

It shifts the burden onto the workforce before anyone has shown that a non-closure package is insufficient.

The expert input did **not** say closures are the default answer. It said:
- non-closure and hybrid measures can recover a **large share** of closure-like savings,
- sometimes **60–90%**,
- especially where the problem is excess capacity, labor cost, and overhead.

That is not a justification for plant closure as a first-line tool. It is a reason to test the non-closure path seriously before threatening irreversible destruction.

### 2) Forced layoffs are not a legitimate “fallback” if alternatives exist
We reject compulsory redundancies as a routine restructuring instrument.

If jobs must be reduced, then the sequence must be:
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reduction,
- and negotiated burden-sharing.

Only if those measures are shown, with real numbers, to be insufficient would any further discussion even begin. Until then, forced layoffs are not a solution; they are a choice to offload risk onto workers.

### 3) Site guarantees are not “anti-competitiveness”
I do not accept the claim that binding site protection is incompatible with competitiveness by definition.

If management wants labor to contribute, labor needs something in return:
- a real industrial future,
- real product allocation,
- real investment commitments,
- and protection against being sacrificed after concessions have been made.

A guarantee is not a slogan. It is the condition that makes worker contributions acceptable and durable.

## To management
You say you need structure and durability. Fine. Then stop hiding behind generalities.

Put the numbers on the table:
- savings target,
- plant-by-plant fixed-cost structure,
- current and target capacity,
- headcount by function,
- savings from shift reduction, line consolidation, and overhead cuts,
- the share borne by labor,
- the share borne by management and owners,
- and the investment plan for the retained footprint.

If a non-closure package truly cannot get the company to target, prove it. But don’t ask us to concede closures before that proof exists.

## To the controlling family and investors
You speak about capital discipline, but capital discipline is not the same as short-term footprint reduction.

The expert has made clear that a strong hybrid package can often deliver most of the economic effect. That means you do **not** get to assume closures are necessary just because they are cleaner on paper.

If you want our cooperation, then you need to support:
- visible management and owner contributions,
- multi-year labor-cost relief,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a credible headcount bridge without forced redundancies.

A settlement that protects shareholder optionality while forcing workers to absorb all the pain is not a fair restructuring. It is a transfer of risk.

## To the state government
Your role matters. But I also need to be blunt: saying closures should be last resort is not enough if, in the same breath, you accept them as an ordinary option.

Use your influence to insist on:
- plant-by-plant transparency,
- independent verification of alternatives,
- and a non-closure or hybrid package as the serious starting point.

If the company can be restructured without closures, then that is the path that should be taken. If not, the burden is on management to demonstrate why — not on the workforce to surrender in advance.

## What would move us
We remain open to a serious settlement if it includes all of the following:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures as the baseline outcome**
3. **Capacity reduction through internal measures**
4. **Working-time and bonus restraint**
5. **Voluntary exits and early retirement**
6. **Visible management and owner contributions**
7. **Binding investment and product commitments**
8. **A durable collective agreement with fair burden-sharing**

That is not rigidity. That is the minimum for a fair restructuring.

## What will not move us
What will not move us is:
- vague competitiveness language,
- closure threats without proof,
- a “no guarantee until after sacrifice” logic,
- or a package where employees pay first and maybe get security later.

We are not prepared to normalize plant closures as a bargaining lever. We are not prepared to accept compulsory redundancies as the preferred tool. And we are not prepared to trade away the collective wage framework for promises that can be withdrawn later.

## My position, clearly
We are ready to negotiate a **serious hybrid restructuring** that preserves jobs, avoids forced layoffs, and shares the burden fairly.

But that requires:
- quantified plant-by-plant analysis,
- genuine management and owner contributions,
- binding commitments on investment and product allocation,
- and a credible no-forced-layoffs path.

If that cannot be delivered, then the conflict will not be solved by repeating “closures remain available.” It will be solved by resistance.
