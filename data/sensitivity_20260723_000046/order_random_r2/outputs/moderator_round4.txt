## Moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
Across the parties, there is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a temporary fix;
- the package must be **quantified plant by plant**;
- **capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions** are legitimate tools;
- a prolonged strike would be damaging;
- the next step should be an **independent plant-by-plant comparison** of scenarios;
- a **hybrid/no-closure approach may recover a substantial share of closure-like savings**, but not necessarily all of them.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved disputes are:
- **Plant closures:**  
  Labor and the state want closures treated as last resort, not a bargaining baseline; management, the family, and investors want closures to remain available where economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor and the state reject them as a normal tool; management, the family, and investors will not rule them out in advance.
- **Site guarantees:**  
  Labor and the state want binding site/investment protection; management, the family, and investors argue guarantees must not freeze an uncompetitive footprint.
- **Sequence of bargaining:**  
  Labor wants proof that non-closure alternatives are insufficient before closures/layoffs are considered; management and capital holders want the analysis to remain open-ended until the numbers are tested.
- **Definition of a credible settlement:**  
  Labor defines credibility as no closures/no forced redundancies plus protections; management and investors define credibility as achieving competitiveness and margin restoration, even if some sites or jobs are reduced.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- how much cost reduction must come from **labor, management, owners, and operational changes** respectively;
- what level of **headcount reduction** can realistically be achieved through attrition, voluntary exits, retirement, and redeployment alone;
- which plants, if any, are **structurally unviable** even after internal restructuring;
- how to balance **industrial peace and regional stability** against **cost-base reduction and competitiveness**;
- what **binding investment/product commitments** would be sufficient to justify labor concessions;
- how to compare the **speed and certainty of closure-led savings** with the **lower disruption but potentially incomplete savings** of a hybrid package.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on a single neutral task:

> **Produce the independent plant-by-plant comparison of Scenario A (no-closure hybrid), Scenario B (partial closure), and Scenario C (closure-led), showing savings by site, headcount bridge, fixed-cost relief, timing, implementation risk, and residual gap to target.**

Please keep the next round focused on:
- quantified assumptions,
- site-specific economics,
- and the residual question of whether any plant still requires closure after internal restructuring is tested.
