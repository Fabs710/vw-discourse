### Moderator Summary — Round 3

#### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad alignment that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a temporary fix;
- the burden cannot fall **only on employees**;
- the discussion must be **quantified** and plant-specific;
- **capacity reduction, working-time changes, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions** are all legitimate tools;
- a **long industrial conflict would destroy value**;
- an **independent plant-by-plant comparison** is needed between hybrid/no-closure and closure-led options.

The expert also confirmed that **non-closure or hybrid packages can capture a substantial share of closure-like savings** in many cases, especially where the issue is excess capacity, labor cost, and overhead.

---

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:

- **Plant closures**
  - Labour, the works council, and the state oppose closures as a baseline or bargaining tool.
  - Management, investors, and the controlling family insist closures must remain available if the numbers require them.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour and the state reject them as a preferred tool and want them only, if ever, as a last resort.
  - Management, investors, and the family say they cannot be ruled out in advance if alternatives do not close the gap.

- **Site guarantees**
  - Labour and the state view binding site commitments as necessary to make sacrifices acceptable.
  - Management and investors argue guarantees are only meaningful if the sites remain economically viable.

- **What counts as “equivalent”**
  - Labour and the state see equivalence as a path that preserves jobs/sites and avoids forced layoffs.
  - Management, the family, and investors define equivalence as delivering comparable durable savings and competitiveness, even if that requires closures or layoffs.

---

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion still lacks hard answers on:

- the **exact savings target** required;
- plant-by-plant **capacity and cost data**;
- the **durability** of non-closure savings versus closure savings;
- the credible **headcount bridge** from attrition, voluntary exits, early retirement, and redeployment;
- how much of the burden should be borne by **labour, management, owners, and investors** respectively;
- which sites, if any, are **structurally uncompetitive** even under a hybrid package;
- what **investment and product commitments** would accompany any retained sites.

In short: the parties agree that a quantified solution is needed, but they still disagree on the thresholds that would justify closures or forced redundancies.

---

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on a single structured comparison:

- **Scenario A: no-closure / hybrid package**
- **Scenario B: partial closure package**
- **Scenario C: closure-led package**

For each scenario, the discussion should identify:
- savings by site,
- headcount impact,
- fixed-cost relief,
- timing of effects,
- implementation risk,
- and required contributions from labour, management, owners, and investors.

The immediate objective is to determine whether a **hybrid package is economically sufficient and operationally credible**, or whether some plants still require closure to meet the restructuring target.
