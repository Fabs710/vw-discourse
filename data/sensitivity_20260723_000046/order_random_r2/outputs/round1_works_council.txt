I will be equally clear: **the workforce will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of this restructuring.** That is not a tactical preference; it is a hard line.

I do acknowledge the company faces serious pressure. We are not denying the need for change, and we are not refusing responsibility. But any solution must be judged by one basic standard: **does it secure the German sites and protect the people who built this company’s success?** If not, it is not a solution.

## Where I agree with others
- **Yes, the company needs a durable path back to competitiveness.**
- **Yes, a purely cosmetic or short-term fix would be irresponsible.**
- **Yes, management, owners, and employees must all contribute.**
- **Yes, future investment, product allocation, and a credible industrial strategy matter.**

On that, there is common ground.

## Where I strongly disagree
### 1. Plant closures are not an acceptable “option on the table”
To management, the family, and investors: you keep presenting closures as a rational tool. For us, they are a line you do not cross. Once a plant is closed, jobs, skills, suppliers, and regional industrial capacity are destroyed permanently. That is not a “restructuring lever”; it is a social and industrial rupture.

If there is serious overcapacity, then we will discuss **capacity reduction**, **working-time adjustments**, **internal transfers**, **natural attrition**, **temporary sacrifices**, and **negotiated flexibility**. But we will not accept the destruction of sites as the starting point.

### 2. Forced redundancies are equally unacceptable
Management cannot expect the workforce to pay for strategic mistakes through compulsory layoffs. That is neither fair nor sustainable. We will not let this crisis be pushed onto shop-floor employees while leadership and ownership preserve their freedom of action.

### 3. “Equivalent savings” must not be code for weakening labour only
I hear the repeated demand for “durable” savings. Fine. But those savings must come from a **balanced package**, not from dismantling wage standards, job protections, and co-determination. If the only path to “equivalence” is to erode the workforce’s security, then that is simply a different route to the same unacceptable outcome.

## What a serious alternative would require
If management and owners want a credible alternative to closures, then they need to put real substance on the table:

- **Binding site guarantees to 2030**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **A negotiated reduction in capacity without shutting plants**
- **Investment commitments for all German sites that remain**
- **Product allocation that secures long-term utilisation**
- **A genuine role for co-determination in shaping the industrial plan**
- **Visible contributions from management and shareholders**, not just from labour

Without these elements, there is no basis for trust.

## To the controlling family
You say competitiveness must come first. We say: **the company’s industrial future comes first — and that requires the workforce, not against it.** You cannot claim to defend the long-term business while asking us to sacrifice the very people who make it run. If you want credibility, support a binding employment and site framework instead of treating closures as a clean solution.

## To the state government
Your insistence on regional responsibility is right, and we welcome it. But it cannot remain rhetorical. If you mean what you say about site protection, then use your influence to stop any attempt to force closures through the back door. The state’s role is not to bless socially destructive restructuring; it is to secure an industrial future for the region.

## To institutional investors
You ask for credibility and deal certainty. So do we. But credibility is not created by threatening communities. It is created by a fair plan that keeps the company productive without tearing up its social compact. If you support a settlement that protects sites and jobs, you will get stability. If you back closure threats, you will get resistance.

## To management
Let me be blunt: if you come to the workforce with a fait accompli, you will get fierce opposition. We have already mobilised warning strikes because workers understand what is at stake. That will continue if needed. The company’s historic job-security framework was not written to be torn up whenever management wants a cleaner balance sheet.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate a **serious, durable restructuring**. But the conditions are non-negotiable:
- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding protection for German sites**
- **Fair burden-sharing from all sides**
- **Real industrial commitments for the future**

If the other stakeholders are serious about a viable solution, they need to stop treating closures and forced layoffs as legitimate tools. They are not.
