## To the union and works council

The expert has said precisely that non-closure solutions are possible **only if** they include:
- significant capacity reduction,
- multi-year labor-cost relief,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and a quantified savings bridge.

That is not the same as “no closures, no layoffs, no real sacrifice.” If your offer is basically “keep all sites, preserve all jobs, and trim costs around the edges,” then it does **not** solve the structural overcapacity or the cost gap. It delays the pain and leaves the company uncompetitive.

So I agree with you on one thing only: **a one-sided burden on employees would be unacceptable**.  
But I do **not** agree that this can be solved with site guarantees alone. A guarantee is not a cost reduction.

## To the state government
You are right that closures are socially and politically serious. I do not take that lightly. But your position also cannot become a veto on restructuring.

If the German footprint stays above target on cost and capacity, and if we cannot reduce it meaningfully, then we are protecting a structure that continues to destroy competitiveness. ## To the controlling family

If the employee side wants closures off the table, then it must accept a package that is deep enough to actually replace the economics of closure. That means real capacity reduction, real labor-cost relief, and real operational simplification.

You are right to demand credibility, durability, and deal certainty. But deal certainty does not mean preserving every site and every legacy arrangement. It means getting to a cost base that supports returns, investment, and competitiveness through 2030. I will support that. I will not support a cosmetic compromise dressed up as balance.

## My position
Here is the reality:

- **I am open to avoiding closures**
- **I am open to avoiding compulsory redundancies**
- **I am open to a negotiated workforce contribution**
- **I am open to preserving sites that can be made viable**

But only if the package delivers:
1. a **material and durable reduction in cost**,
2. a **real reduction in capacity**,
3. a **credible headcount bridge**,
4. **multi-year labor-cost relief**,
5. and **visible contributions from management and owners**.

If those conditions are not met, then “no closures” is just a slogan that leaves the company stuck with an uncompetitive structure.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a deal that preserves all current capacity,
- a deal that relies on temporary concessions only,
- a deal that protects legacy guarantees while the cost base stays off-target,
- or a deal that funds today’s overcapacity by sacrificing tomorrow’s investment.

That would be a strategic failure.

- what exact cost reduction is required,
- how much comes from labor,
- how much from overhead and management,
- how much from capacity reduction,
