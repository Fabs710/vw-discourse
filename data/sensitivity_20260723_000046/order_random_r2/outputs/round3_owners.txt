We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

## Where I agree
I agree with the union, works council, state, and the expert on several core points:

- The company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one.
- A **one-sided burden on employees** would be unacceptable.
- A prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value for everyone.
- A serious package must include **capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and visible contributions from management and owners**.
- The discussion must be **quantified**, not driven by slogans.

That is the right framework.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute starting rule
I understand why labour and the state want that. But as controlling shareholder I cannot endorse a pre-commitment that rules out closures regardless of economics.

If a plant is structurally uncompetitive and the alternative package does not close the gap, then preserving it indefinitely would simply transfer value destruction into the future. That is not stewardship.

So I am **not** prepared to accept a blanket no-closure rule.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is only acceptable if the alternative is real and sufficient
I am open to avoiding forced layoffs, but not as a rhetorical promise. It must be backed by a credible headcount bridge and durable cost savings.

Natural attrition, early retirement, voluntary exits, redeployment, and reduced hours can absolutely be part of the solution. But if those measures do not deliver enough, then the company cannot be trapped by an unrealistic red line.

### 3) Site guarantees do not substitute for competitiveness
Binding guarantees are only meaningful if the sites remain viable. A guarantee that freezes an uncompetitive structure is not a solution; it is deferral.

## What the expert inputs actually mean
The experts did **not** say closures are unnecessary. They said:
- non-closure measures can recover a **substantial share** of the savings,
- hybrid solutions are often the most realistic,
- but closures remain the strongest tool where structural weakness is severe.

That is exactly how I read it.

So the real question is not “closures or no closures.”  
It is:

**Can a quantified non-closure or hybrid package deliver enough permanent savings, capacity reduction, and headcount adjustment to restore margins and competitiveness?**

If yes, I am open to it.  
If no, then closures remain available.

## To the union and works council
You are right that labour should not carry this alone. But your current position still assumes that site preservation can be declared first and economics solved later. That is backwards.

If you want closures avoided, then bring forward a package that is genuinely comparable in effect:
- quantified savings,
- real capacity reduction,
- multi-year labour-cost relief,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and industrial discipline across the workforce.

Without that, “no closures” is just a demand, not a restructuring plan.

## To management
I expect you to move from threats to numbers. We need a plant-by-plant comparison:
- required savings,
- cost by site,
- capacity by site,
- headcount bridge,
- timing of effects,
- and the investment/product commitments tied to the retained footprint.

If the non-closure case is strong enough, say so and prove it. If it is not, then say so honestly. Ambiguity helps no one.

## To the state government
I hear your concern about regional impact and I take it seriously. But your role cannot be to freeze industrial reality. The state should support a solution that preserves viable sites, not one that preserves every site regardless of cost structure.

## My position
I am prepared to support a **serious hybrid restructuring** if it delivers:
1. durable cost reduction,
2. meaningful capacity reduction,
3. a credible headcount bridge,
4. no value-destructive strike,
5. and visible contributions from management and owners.

I am **not** prepared to support:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a no-layoffs promise without quantified backing,
- or a settlement that protects legacy structures while leaving the company uncompetitive.
