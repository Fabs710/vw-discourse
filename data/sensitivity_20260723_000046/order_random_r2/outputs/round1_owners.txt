We share the assessment that the company is under serious pressure and that a durable solution must protect the long-term industrial base. We also agree that a one-sided approach would be neither fair nor stable.

But I need to be direct: **the current position of “no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies” cannot be the starting point if the numbers truly require a structural reset.** If the German cost base and capacity are materially out of line, then we cannot rule out every hard measure in advance and still claim to be solving the problem.

Our priorities are clear:

1. **Long-term competitiveness must come first**
   - The core asset has to earn its capital.
   - If margins remain structurally weak, then investment capacity, product renewal, and ultimately jobs are all at risk.
   - We will not support a package that preserves every site and every legacy guarantee while quietly eroding the company’s future.

2. **Labour must contribute, but not be the only contributor**
   - I agree that employees should not carry the burden alone.
   - Management, the company, and the owners must also accept visible discipline.
   - However, a credible contribution from labour can include more than just wage restraint; it can include flexibility, capacity adjustment, working-time arrangements, and negotiated changes to legacy protections.

3. **No value-destructive conflict**
   - A prolonged strike or public escalation would damage everyone, including the workforce and the owners.
   - So I would strongly prefer a negotiated settlement over confrontation.
   - But avoiding conflict cannot mean avoiding necessary restructuring.

4. **We need a credible industrial plan, not just a defensive one**
   - Any agreement must show where future models, technology, and investment will be placed.
   - If sites are to remain, they need a viable role, not just a political promise.

So my position is this: **I support a balanced restructuring package that restores competitiveness and margins, with shared sacrifice and a real industrial future.** I do **not** support a blanket commitment against closures or other structural measures before we have established whether there are alternative savings of equivalent durability.

If the employee side is willing to engage on a package that delivers comparable, lasting cost reduction without destroying the enterprise, that is worth serious consideration. If not, then management’s restructuring case remains on the table.
