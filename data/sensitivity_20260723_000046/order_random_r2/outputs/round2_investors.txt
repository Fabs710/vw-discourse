We are prepared to support a **serious, quantified restructuring** — but not a political holding action dressed up as compromise.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, the state, and even the labour side on the basic diagnosis:
- the company needs a **durable** reset,
- the burden cannot fall on employees alone,
- and a prolonged strike would destroy value.

We also accept the expert’s point that **non-closure alternatives can deliver a large share of the economic effect** if they are substantial enough. That is important, and it means closures are **not automatically required**.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be a blanket veto
We do **not** support an absolute ban on closures as a matter of principle. If the company cannot restore competitive margins and fixed-cost discipline otherwise, then protecting every site becomes value-destructive. That is not acceptable to shareholders.

So: if non-closure measures can truly match the savings, fine. If they cannot, then closures must remain available.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” only works if the numbers close
We are open to avoiding forced layoffs **only if** the package delivers equivalent, durable savings through:
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time cuts,
- bonus restraint,
- and overhead simplification.

If those measures are too weak, then ruling out compulsory redundancies in advance is simply refusing to solve the problem.

### 3) Site guarantees are not a substitute for economics
Binding site promises are useful only if the sites remain competitive. A guarantee that freezes an overextended footprint just delays the correction and worsens the eventual outcome.

## What would move us
We would back a non-closure settlement if it includes all of the following:
1. **A quantified savings target**
2. **Plant-by-plant capacity reductions**
3. **A credible headcount bridge**
4. **Multi-year labor-cost relief**
5. **Visible management and owner contributions**
6. **A clear timeline to margin improvement**
7. **Deal certainty, with no open-ended conflict**

That is the standard.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- temporary concessions that fade after a year or two,
- preserving all legacy structures while margins stay structurally impaired,
- or a “site protection” package that leaves the company unable to earn its capital.

## Our position
So our position is straightforward:

- **Yes** to a hybrid restructuring if it is economically equivalent to closure on a durable basis.
- **No** to an absolute no-closures rule.
- **No** to a no-layoffs stance unless the savings are real and quantified.
- **Yes** to a negotiated solution, but only one that restores competitiveness and protects shareholder value.

If management and labour can put forward a package that genuinely replaces closure economics, we will support it. If not, then the hard restructuring tools must stay on the table.
