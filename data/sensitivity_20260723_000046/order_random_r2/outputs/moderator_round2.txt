## Neutral moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch;
- the burden should **not fall only on employees**;
- a **prolonged strike would be damaging**;
- any settlement should be **quantified** and based on actual numbers;
- **capacity reduction, working-time adjustments, voluntary exits/redeployment, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions** are all legitimate elements of a package;
- **future investment and product allocation** are necessary if sites are to remain viable.

The expert inputs also clarified that **non-closure restructuring is operationally possible in principle**, especially as part of a hybrid package.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures:**  
  Union/works council/state strongly oppose them as a starting point; management, investors, and the family want them to remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour-side actors reject them; management/investors say they can only be ruled out if the alternative savings are sufficient.
- **Scope of site guarantees:**  
  Labour and state want binding site protection; management and investors say guarantees cannot freeze an uncompetitive structure.
- **Definition of “equivalent” savings:**  
  All sides say they want equivalence, but they disagree on what level of cost/capacity reduction is enough and who must bear it.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Still unresolved are the concrete balances between:
- **job security vs. cost reduction**;
- **site preservation vs. permanent fixed-cost relief**;
- **voluntary measures vs. the speed and certainty of savings**;
- **multi-year labor concessions vs. management/owner contributions**;
- **capacity reduction inside plants vs. full plant closure**;
- **short-term industrial peace vs. long-term competitiveness**.

No party has yet supplied a full plant-by-plant package showing how much savings can be achieved, from which measures, over what timeline, and with what employment impact.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to a **single quantified comparison**:
- the required savings target,
- plant-by-plant cost and capacity data,
- the savings from non-closure measures,
- the savings from closure options,
- the headcount impact under each,
- and the investment/product commitments tied to each scenario.

The key task is to test whether a **non-closure or hybrid package** can truly match the restructuring effect of closures, and if so, under what specific conditions.
