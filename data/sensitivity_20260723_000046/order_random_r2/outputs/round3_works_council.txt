I will be equally direct.

We are **not** going to accept closures or compulsory redundancies as the “default credible option” just because management, investors, and the family keep repeating that phrase. Repetition is not proof. And until someone produces a plant-by-plant, independently verified case showing that a non-closure solution cannot work, I will continue to treat closure threats as what they are: **pressure tactics**.

## Where I agree
I agree with one core point from several parties: the company needs a **durable restructuring**. I do not dispute that there is structural pressure. I also agree that:
- the burden cannot fall only on employees,
- management and owners must contribute visibly,
- a prolonged strike would be damaging,
- and any serious package must be quantified.

I also accept the expert input that **capacity reduction, working-time changes, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and multi-year labor-cost relief** can deliver substantial savings. That is exactly why I reject the notion that the only serious route is plant closure.

## Where I strongly disagree

### 1) Management’s “closures remain available” line
That is not a neutral statement. It is an attempt to keep a gun on the table while pretending to negotiate.

The expert did **not** say closures are necessary. The expert said non-closure and hybrid solutions can recover a large share of the effect if the package is serious enough. That means the burden is on management to prove that alternatives are insufficient — not on the workforce to surrender first and ask questions later.

So no: closures are **not** the starting point, and they are **not** an acceptable bargaining instrument.

### 2) Investors’ demand for “capital discipline”
Capital discipline does not mean destroying the industrial base that generates future value. A blanket willingness to close plants for the sake of faster margin repair is short-termist and socially reckless.

If the company wants durable competitiveness, it needs:
- real operational simplification,
- real internal capacity reduction,
- real management and overhead cuts,
- and a real industrial future for the sites that remain.

You do **not** get long-term value by permanently damaging trust, skills, and regional industrial capacity.

### 3) The family’s “we support non-closure if equivalent” framing
That sounds flexible, but it hides the trap: you define “equivalent” in a way that quietly pushes the burden onto workers until the package is so harsh it becomes closure by another name.

We will not accept that.  
Equivalence must mean:
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **no plant closures**,
- **binding site guarantees to 2030**,
- and a fair sharing of sacrifices between management, owners, and labour.

If you remove any of those, you are no longer talking about a balanced restructuring.

### 4) The state’s “site guarantees only matter if viable”
That is true in the abstract, but it is being used too often as a reason to postpone commitment.

Site guarantees are precisely what make worker contributions possible. Without them, employees are being asked to pay for a restructuring whose benefits may be reversed later by another round of cuts. That is not trust-building; it is bait-and-switch.

If the state wants a serious settlement, it must use its influence to **secure German sites first**, not merely to say after the fact that they must remain viable.

## What the experts actually support
The expert analysis supports my position more than management’s.

It says:
- non-closure restructuring can plausibly recover **60–90%** of closure-like savings in the right case,
- hybrid solutions are often the most realistic,
- closures remove the entire cost stack, but retained plants still preserve significant flexibility and future value.

That means the correct conclusion is **not** “therefore closures are needed.”  
The correct conclusion is: **there is a credible path that preserves plants and jobs, if management is prepared to do the hard work.**

And that is the path we are ready to negotiate.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time reductions,
- bonus restraint,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- temporary workforce contributions,
- line and shift rationalisation,
- and deeper management/owner contributions.

But let me be very clear: those discussions happen **within** the following red lines:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding protection for German sites**
4. **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**
5. **Real investment and product commitments**

That is not “inflexibility.” That is the only responsible line when tens of thousands of livelihoods are at stake.

---

## What I reject outright
I reject:
- closure threats dressed up as realism,
- compulsory layoffs framed as efficiency,
- “equivalence” that quietly means worker concessions only,
- and any deal that weakens the co-determination rights that protect the workforce from exactly this kind of unilateral restructuring.

The works council is not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that strips jobs, breaks guarantees, and calls it competitiveness.

## To management
If you want credibility, stop speaking in abstractions and put the numbers on the table:
- What exact savings target is required?
- What portion comes from labour, management, and owners?
- What portion comes from capacity reduction without closures?
- What investment is tied to which German site?
- What is the headcount bridge without forced redundancies?

Until you answer that, your closure talk is just leverage.

## To the controlling family and investors
If you want our willingness to contribute, then give us something real in return:
- binding site security,
- no forced layoffs,
- investment commitments,
- and a settlement that preserves co-determination.

If you are serious about long-term value, prove it by supporting the industrial base rather than threatening to dismantle it.

## To the state government
Use your rights. Do not just call for dialogue. Insist on:
- transparent plant-by-plant disclosure,
- independent verification of alternatives,
- and a non-closure package before any closure discussion is even entertained.

---

## My bottom line
We are ready for a **serious hybrid restructuring** — but only one that:
- keeps every German plant open,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- protects co-determination,
- and shares the burden fairly.

I will not move off that line because others keep insisting that closures are “available.” That is not a solution; it is a threat.
