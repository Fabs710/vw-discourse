We recognise that the company is under real pressure, and we are prepared to contribute to a viable solution. But let me be equally clear from the start: **we will not accept compulsory redundancies, plant closures, or a dismantling of the collective wage framework as the price of restructuring**.

Our priorities are:

1. **Job security and site security**
   - No closure of German plants.
   - No forced layoffs.
   - No shifting the crisis onto the workforce alone.

2. **A fair distribution of burden**
   - If employees are expected to contribute, then **management, owners, and the company must also contribute visibly**.
   - We can discuss labour-cost measures, capacity reductions, working-time adjustments, suspension or reduction of bonuses, and other negotiated contributions.
   - But these must be part of a balanced package, not a one-sided cut plan.

3. **A durable collective agreement**
   - We want a settlement that is stable, bargainable, and respects collective standards.
   - We will not allow “temporary crisis measures” to become a permanent lowering of wages and conditions.

4. **Future investment and industrial perspective**
   - If employees make sacrifices, then the company must commit to investment, product allocation, and a credible long-term industrial strategy for the German sites.

So our position is: **yes to negotiated savings and flexibility, no to closures and compulsory redundancies**. If management wants peace, it needs to put forward a genuine shared-sacrifice package. If it insists that workers carry the pain alone, it should expect resistance — including industrial action.
