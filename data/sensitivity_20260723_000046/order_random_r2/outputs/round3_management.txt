We are now well past the stage where vague statements about “responsibility” can substitute for an actual restructuring.

So let me respond plainly.

## To the union and works council
I acknowledge one important point: you are right that a one-sided burden on the workforce would be unacceptable. I also agree that a purely cosmetic deal would be worthless.

But I strongly reject your core red lines as stated:
- **no closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site guarantees as a starting point**

That is not a restructuring strategy. That is an attempt to pre-decide the outcome before the economics have been tested.

The expert input has been useful precisely because it does **not** support your absolute position. It says non-closure solutions can deliver a **substantial share** of the savings only if they include:
- real capacity reduction,
- multi-year labor-cost relief,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and a quantified savings bridge.

That is exactly my point. A non-closure package is only acceptable if it is **equivalent in effect**. If it is not equivalent, then it is not enough.

So I am not retreating from closures, and I am not retreating from the possibility of compulsory redundancies if they become necessary. Those tools remain legitimate if the alternative cannot close the gap.

## To the controlling family
I agree with you on the main strategic issue: a restructuring must be **quantified**, **durable**, and **credible**. We cannot save this business with slogans or by preserving every legacy arrangement.

Where I push harder than you do is this: the company does not need a philosophical debate about whether closures are “normal.” It needs a decision on whether the alternative can actually deliver the required savings. If it can, fine. If it cannot, then we cannot pretend that site preservation alone is a solution.

I support a hybrid approach only if it delivers:
- durable fixed-cost reduction,
- lower capacity,
- and a real labor-cost contribution.

Anything weaker leaves us structurally uncompetitive.

## To the regional state government
I understand your concern for regional employment and suppliers. But your role cannot become a veto over economic reality.

I respect co-determination and the special statute. I do not accept the idea that they entitle anyone to freeze an uncompetitive footprint in place.

The state says closures should be last resort. Fine — if an alternative package is truly capable of restoring competitiveness, then closures need not happen. But that is a conditional statement. It is not a free pass to rule them out in advance.

And let me be equally direct: a site guarantee is not a cost solution. It only has value if the site is viable under the new structure. We are not discussing sentiment; we are discussing whether the company can earn its capital and fund future product investment.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to demand deal certainty and returns. The brand must get back to a sustainable margin profile, and that requires structural savings — not temporary relief.

I share your view that:
- a no-closure rule cannot be a blanket veto,
- a no-layoff rule only works if the numbers close,
- and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value.

We need a package that produces real economic effect, not a political holding pattern.

## My position, unchanged
I remain open to:
- avoiding closures,
- avoiding compulsory redundancies,
- preserving viable sites,
- and agreeing a negotiated workforce contribution.

But only if the package is strong enough to replace the economics of closure. That means:

1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **A credible headcount bridge**
4. **Multi-year labor-cost relief**
5. **Visible management and owner contributions**
6. **Binding investment and product allocation for the retained footprint**

If those conditions are not met, then closures remain on the table, and so do compulsory redundancies.

## What will not move me
What will not move me is:
- a promise of “no closures” without numbers,
- a promise of “no layoffs” without a headcount plan,
- or a restructuring that protects every site while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.

That would fail the company, its owners, and its future.

- what non-closure measures can realistically deliver,
- what closure would deliver,

Until then, I will not endorse any framework that rules out the strongest restructuring tools in advance.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to support a serious negotiated solution.  
I am **not** prepared to support a compromise that preserves the current structure and simply asks management to hope for better times.

We need durable competitiveness by 2030, not a symbolic peace today.
