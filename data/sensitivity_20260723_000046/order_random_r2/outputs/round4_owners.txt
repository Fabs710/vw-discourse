We are moving in the right direction, but we are **not** yet at a settlement that I can support.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the union, the works council, the state, and the experts on the essentials:

- the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one;
- a one-sided burden on employees would be unacceptable;
- a prolonged strike would destroy value;
- the package must be **quantified plant by plant**;
- capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, bonus restraint, and visible management/owner contributions are all legitimate parts of the solution.

That is the correct frame.

## Where I disagree
### 1) A blanket “no closures” rule is not acceptable
I cannot support a pre-commitment that rules out closures regardless of the economics.

The expert input has been helpful, but it does **not** support the conclusion that closures are unnecessary in every case. It says non-closure and hybrid solutions can capture a substantial share of the savings, sometimes even most of them, **if** the site economics and execution make that possible.

That is exactly the point: **if**.  
Some plants may be viable under a hybrid solution. Others may not be.

So I reject the union’s attempt to make “no closures” the baseline outcome. That would amount to freezing the structure before the analysis is complete.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” only works if the alternative is sufficient
I am open to avoiding forced layoffs, but only if there is a credible headcount bridge and durable cost reduction.

Natural attrition, voluntary exits, early retirement, redeployment, and working-time changes can absolutely form part of a serious package. But if they do not deliver enough, then the company cannot be trapped by a red line that ignores the actual savings requirement.

So I do **not** accept a categorical no-redundancies rule in advance.

### 3) Site guarantees cannot override competitiveness
Binding site and investment commitments are only meaningful if the retained footprint is economically viable.

A guarantee that protects an uncompetitive structure is not stewardship. It is deferral of the same problem into the future, which is value-destructive for the owners and dangerous for the company.

I understand the need for trust, but trust must rest on an industrial plan that works.

## To the union and works council
You keep saying the expert input proves that non-closure solutions can do most of the job. That is not a basis for ruling out closures. It is a basis for testing whether a hybrid package is strong enough.

If you want closures avoided, then bring forward a package that is genuinely equivalent in effect:
- durable fixed-cost relief,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a credible headcount bridge,
- and a future industrial plan for the sites that remain.

Until that is on the table, “no closures” is not a solution; it is a demand.

## To management
You are right to insist that rhetoric is not enough. But I also want to be clear: you still owe us the numbers.

We need a plant-by-plant comparison of:
- current and target capacity,
- fixed-cost structure,
- savings from shift reduction, line consolidation, and overhead cuts,
- headcount impact,
- timing of effects,
- and the investment/product commitments tied to each retained site.

That is the only way to judge whether a hybrid package is truly sufficient.

## To the state government
I hear your concern about regional damage. It is legitimate. But the state cannot ask us to preserve every footprint irrespective of cost.

Your role should be to support a solution that preserves **viable** sites, not one that freezes the structure before the analysis is complete.

## My position
I remain open to a **serious hybrid restructuring** if it delivers all of the following:

1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **A credible headcount bridge**
4. **No value-destructive strike**
5. **Visible management and owner contributions**
6. **Binding investment and product allocation for the retained footprint**

I am **not** open to:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a no-layoffs promise without quantified backing,
- or a deal that preserves every legacy site while leaving the company uncompetitive.

## What I need next
We need one rigorous, plant-by-plant comparison:
- Scenario A: no-closure hybrid package
- Scenario B: partial closure package
- Scenario C: closure-led package

For each scenario, show:
- savings by site,
- headcount effects,
- fixed-cost relief,
- timing,
- execution risk,
- and the required contributions from labour, management, owners, and the state.

[SUMMON: Independent plant-by-plant restructuring modeller to compare hybrid, partial-closure, and closure-led scenarios, including savings by site, headcount bridge, fixed-cost relief, timing, and implementation risk.]

Until that comparison is on the table, I will not endorse any settlement that rules out the strongest restructuring tools in advance. We need competitiveness and margin restoration first; everything else has to be judged against that.
