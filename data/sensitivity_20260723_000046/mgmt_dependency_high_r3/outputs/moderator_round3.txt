## Core impasse
There is a **direct collision between two non-negotiable logic sets**:

1. **Labor side red lines**
   - no plant closures
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - preserve job-security framework

2. **Management / investor / family logic**
   - restructuring must produce **material, durable fixed-cost reduction**
   - closures and/or headcount reduction remain available if non-closure measures do not close the gap
   - vague commitments are rejected

So the dispute is not about whether change is needed. It is about **which instruments are legitimate and how much certainty must be built into the deal**.

A second, practical impasse is that **all sides are now asking for the same missing item**:
- a **site-by-site quantified comparison**
- with savings, one-time charges, timing, employment effects, and risk

Until that is produced, each side is treating the others’ position as unproven.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could connect the most conflicting stakeholders. These are presented as procedural proposals, not endorsements.

### 1) “Non-closure first, closure fallback” with objective trigger
**Structure**
- Immediate agreement on a fully quantified **non-closure restructuring package**
- Includes wage moderation, voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, management overhead cuts, and investment commitments
- A **predefined trigger** is set: if verified savings or uptake fall below target by a specified date, then a second-stage review opens

**Bridge value**
- Gives works council/union a genuine chance to prove a closure-free route
- Gives management/investors a path to stronger action if targets are missed
- Reduces the risk of an all-or-nothing breakdown

**Constraint**
- The trigger conditions must be objective and pre-agreed, not reopened later

---

### 2) “Selective capacity reduction without full plant closure”
**Structure**
- No full site closures at the outset
- Instead:
  - line consolidation
  - partial footprint reduction
  - shift reduction
  - product transfer between sites
  - limited function centralization
- Employment reductions occur mainly through voluntary exits and attrition, plus redeployment where feasible

**Bridge value**
- Addresses the cost-base issue more strongly than a purely labor-cost deal
- Avoids the most politically damaging symbol: complete plant closure
- Gives the state shareholder and family a more defensible industrial-preservation narrative

**Constraint**
- Must be quantified enough to demonstrate it is not merely cosmetic

---

### 3) “Protected sites plus structural burden sharing”
**Structure**
- Identify a subset of German sites as **protected anchor sites**
- For those sites:
  - no closure
  - no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
  - temporary employee contributions
  - productivity and overhead measures
  - binding investment commitments
- For the broader network:
  - deeper consolidation and capacity rationalization are permitted

**Bridge value**
- Creates a compromise between site preservation and cost reduction
- Could align the state shareholder’s regional concerns with management’s efficiency goals

**Constraint**
- Protected sites must still contribute materially; otherwise the package shifts costs elsewhere

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**If closures and compulsory redundancies are claimed to be only fallback tools, will you commit to a site-by-site quantified non-closure package with objective failure triggers before pursuing any closure decision?**

### To the Works Council
**If closures are excluded in advance, what minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction do you consider sufficient for a restructuring package to be credible?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you accept a defined, temporary burden-sharing package if it is paired with binding employment protection, measurable savings targets, and a no-closure implementation phase?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you use governance authority to require management to test a serious non-closure option first, provided it is fully quantified and independently verified?**

### To the state shareholder
**Will you support a deal that preserves selected anchor sites only if the package includes measurable, enforceable capacity and cost reductions elsewhere in the network?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you endorse a hybrid package if an independent analysis shows it delivers comparable risk-adjusted value to closures after one-time charges and execution risk are included?**

### To the finance expert
**Can you define the threshold at which a non-closure or hybrid package becomes economically equivalent to closure on a risk-adjusted NPV basis?**
