### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious competitiveness and cost problem**.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **durable**, not a short-term patch.
- Several parties accept that **capacity must be reduced or adjusted**, but the method remains disputed.
- There is shared interest in avoiding a **destructive labor conflict** and in preserving the company’s long-term industrial base.
- There is openness, in principle, to **alternatives to outright plant closures** if they deliver equivalent savings and certainty.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works Council and Union reject them; management says they cannot be excluded if needed; shareholders are split but not uniformly opposed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labor and state shareholder reject them as a starting point; management says it cannot guarantee they will never be needed.
- **Job-security framework through 2030:** Labor wants binding guarantees; management says blanket preservation is incompatible with competitiveness.
- **Scope of labor concessions:** Union is open to temporary and voluntary measures only, while management wants substantial, structural workforce-side contributions.
- **How much burden should fall on management/owners vs. employees:** All non-labor parties say the burden must be shared, but they differ on how far that goes in practice.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No party has yet presented a **fully quantified comparison** of:
  - closure scenario vs. non-closure alternatives,
  - expected savings,
  - timing of savings,
  - implementation certainty.
- There is no agreed package on the balance between:
  - **site preservation** and **cost reduction**,
  - **voluntary measures** and **structural measures**,
  - **investment commitments** and **labor-cost concessions**.
- The parties have not yet resolved the question of what counts as **“equivalent” savings** if closures are avoided.
- The risk of **industrial action** and the consequences of delay remain unresolved.
- No common framework yet exists for **who absorbs how much of the adjustment burden**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from position statements to a **single structured options comparison**:
- define the cost gap to be closed,
- list 2–3 restructuring scenarios,
- specify savings, timing, risks, and social impact for each,
- identify which elements could be negotiated as guarantees, voluntary measures, or management/owner contributions.

The next round should aim to test whether a **non-closure solution can match the scale and certainty** of a closure-based restructuring, and if not, where compromise might still be possible.
