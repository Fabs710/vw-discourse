Core impasse: **the sides disagree less about the need for structural cost reduction than about the permissible instruments and the sequencing of decisions.**

- **Labor side**: insists that German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and erosion of job-security/co-determination are off-limits.
- **Management / investors / family**: insist that the restructuring must be economically credible and may require closures or forced workforce reductions if alternatives fall short.
- **State shareholder**: wants regional and employment protection, but also a viable company.
- **Finance expert**: says the dispute cannot move forward without a quantified, site-by-site comparison of closure, non-closure, and hybrid options.

The immediate blocker is therefore **not a lack of principle, but the absence of a shared, quantified decision basis**.

## Facilitator proposals

### 1) Quantified “non-closure first” test with pre-agreed thresholds
**Proposal:** Commission a site-by-site plan that tests a non-closure restructuring first, but only under pre-agreed economic thresholds:
- minimum annual savings required,
- minimum implementation certainty,
- maximum acceptable one-time charges,
- and a defined deadline for assessment.

If the non-closure package fails the thresholds, the parties then review a second-stage hybrid option.

**Purpose:** Gives labor a real non-closure attempt while giving management and investors a disciplined fallback process.

---

### 2) Hybrid package built around protected anchor sites and controlled capacity reduction
**Proposal:** Create a hybrid design that combines:
- binding protection for selected German anchor sites,
- capacity reduction through shifts, lines, and product transfers,
- voluntary exits and working-time measures,
- management overhead cuts,
- and defined investment commitments.

**Purpose:** Preserves core sites while still producing structural cost reduction and allowing management to claim credible adjustment.

---

### 3) Employment protection in exchange for deeper structural burden sharing
**Proposal:** Negotiate a time-limited package with:
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- no closures for a defined period at designated sites,
- workforce contributions through wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexibility,
- plus mandatory management/owner contributions and operational simplification.

**Purpose:** Aligns burden sharing more symmetrically and may create room for a broader settlement.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after non-closure measures, and which specific sites or functions fail the threshold under your model?**

### To the works council
**What quantified cost-reduction package would you accept as sufficient to keep every German site open while still restoring competitiveness?**

### To the union
**Which mix of flexibility, voluntary exits, wage moderation, and capacity adjustment would you consider a legitimate alternative to closures?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you commit to an independent, site-by-site comparison before any closure option is treated as the preferred outcome?**

### To the state shareholder
**What level of site protection can you support without undermining the company’s long-term viability?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you support a hybrid plan if it achieves a risk-adjusted NPV close to closure, even if it preserves more sites and jobs?**

### To the finance expert
**What minimum data and threshold criteria are required for you to judge a non-closure or hybrid package economically equivalent to closure?**
