I will be direct.

We agree on the diagnosis: the company faces a real competitiveness problem, and a durable solution is needed. We also agree that a pure delay tactic would be irresponsible. But that is where the common ground ends.

## Our structured position

### 1) Closure-based restructuring
We **reject** any scenario based on closing German plants.

Why?
- Once a plant is closed, jobs, skills, supplier networks, apprenticeships, and regional industrial capacity are gone.
- That is not a “tool” in the package; it is a permanent destruction of value.
- A company that breaks its own historic job-security commitments destroys trust for years, not months.

If management still wants to argue for closures, then it must prove something it has not proven yet: that there is absolutely no alternative capable of delivering the same savings with less damage. So far, that proof has not been provided.

### 2) Non-closure restructuring with capacity adjustments
This is the only direction we are prepared to discuss — but only under hard conditions.

Potential elements:
- capacity reductions without site closures
- working-time adjustments
- voluntary exits only
- temporary employee contributions
- management and overhead reductions
- investment commitments for all German sites

But let me be equally clear: **capacity reduction is not a synonym for plant closure**, and **voluntary instruments are not a backdoor to forced redundancies**.

### 3) Forced layoffs
We **reject** compulsory redundancies outright.

Not as a bargaining slogan, but because this crosses a line:
- the workforce did not create this crisis
- forced layoffs would undermine trust and industrial peace
- they would also damage the long-term ability to implement any transformation

If the company cannot achieve savings without forced layoffs, then management has not done the work required to build a viable restructuring package.

## On the arguments from the other stakeholders

### To management
You keep saying “structural” and “durable,” but what you are really putting on the table is a one-sided transfer of the crisis onto labour.

That is not a plan; that is a demand.

You have not yet shown:
- a quantified gap that proves closures are unavoidable,
- a comparison of alternatives,
- or a credible burden-sharing model.

Until you do, your closure logic is not convincing. It is simply the most aggressive option.

### To the controlling family
You say you want value preservation, but value is not only short-term margin improvement. A company that blows up its social compact, triggers strikes, and destroys site trust is not preserving value; it is destabilising the enterprise.

If you truly want a durable solution, then use your governance power to stop the rush toward closures and force management to present a serious non-closure plan.

### To the state shareholder
You are right to reject closures as the default. But “preserve the industrial base” cannot remain a rhetorical statement. You should use your blocking rights to prevent a fait accompli and insist on a solution built around sites, not site destruction.

### To institutional investors
You keep saying “durable savings at scale,” and we understand that. But you are treating closures as if they were the benchmark for seriousness. They are not. The benchmark is whether the company can reach the necessary savings **without destroying viable plants and thousands of jobs**.

A non-closure solution is not “cosmetic” if management is willing to do the hard work:
- reduce overhead,
- adjust working time,
- limit investment priorities,
- use voluntary instruments,
- and make ownership and management contribute.

If that is considered impossible, then the real issue is not economics. It is the unwillingness to distribute the burden fairly.

## Our red lines
These are not negotiable:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the job-security framework**
- **No settlement that treats the workforce as the shock absorber while management and owners stay protected**

## What we would accept
If management wants any serious negotiation, it must put forward:
1. a non-closure restructuring plan,
2. binding site and employment guarantees through 2030,
3. a meaningful management and owner contribution,
4. voluntary and reversible workforce measures only,
5. a genuine co-determination role for employee representatives in the restructuring design.

## What happens if this is ignored
If the company continues to treat closures and forced layoffs as the starting point, then you should expect:
- mass resistance,
- warning strikes,
- and a governance conflict that management will not win by pressure alone.

We are not here to rubber-stamp a dismantling of the German industrial base. If there is a real alternative, bring it. If not, then the company is choosing conflict, not restructuring.
