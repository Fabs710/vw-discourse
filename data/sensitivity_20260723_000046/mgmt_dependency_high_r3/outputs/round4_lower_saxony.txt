I will be direct.

## My position
I support a durable restructuring. I do **not** support a package that destroys the regional industrial base, removes jobs en masse, or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.  
So far, I see **no proof** that German plant closures or compulsory redundancies are necessary. Until that proof exists, they remain **unacceptable** to me.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family on one point: the company has a **real structural cost problem** and needs a solution that is more than a delay.

I also agree with labor and the finance expert that:
- a cosmetic compromise would fail,
- the solution must be quantified,
- and a strike spiral would hurt the company and the region.

That is the common ground. But common ground is not the same as accepting closure pressure.

## Where I disagree with management
Management keeps saying closures and forced redundancies are available if alternatives fall short. That may be a fallback in your mind, but it is **not yet a demonstrated necessity**.

You have not provided the site-by-site comparison that proves:
- the current fixed-cost gap,
- what non-closure measures can close,
- what they cannot close,
- and why closures are unavoidable.

Until that analysis exists, I will not treat closures as a legitimate starting point.  
And I will not support forced redundancies as a default tool.

## Where I disagree with the works council and union
I support your resistance to a one-sided burden shift. I also support site preservation and job protection as core objectives.

But I have to be honest: saying only “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no framework change” is **not yet a full economic answer**. It is a red line, not a restructuring plan.

If you want my support for a non-closure path, then I need to see a package that is actually capable of closing the cost gap through:
- real capacity reduction,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management and overhead cuts,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is the standard I would expect from labor if closures are to remain off the table.

## Where I disagree with the family and investors
You are right that value destruction through paralysis would be disastrous.  
But you are too quick to treat closures as a normal strategic option without first exhausting a serious non-closure alternative.

A closure may produce savings on paper. It also destroys:
- skills,
- apprenticeships,
- supplier ecosystems,
- regional trust,
- and political legitimacy.

If the company can reach comparable durability without that damage, then closure is not the superior answer. That has not yet been tested properly.

## My red lines
Let me state them clearly:

- **No plant closures in the state unless no credible alternative exists**
- **No compulsory redundancies as a starting position**
- **No deal that strips the state of its co-determination and veto role**
- **No settlement that shifts the crisis mainly onto workers and the region**

Those are not bargaining chips. They define my legitimacy as a regional shareholder and political actor.

## What I can support
I am open to a package that includes:
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary wage restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- management overhead reduction,
- owner contribution,
- investment commitments to German sites,
- and a binding social-partnership framework.

If such a package is **quantified**, **credible**, and **protects core sites and jobs**, I can support it.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a closure-first strategy dressed up as inevitability,
- a vague hybrid that quietly becomes closures by another name,
- or a non-closure package that is too weak to restore competitiveness.

We need the numbers. Not slogans, not pressure, not warnings.

I want that comparison to answer, for each German site and major function:
- current fixed-cost base,
- utilization,
- break-even volume,
- savings under each scenario,
- one-time costs,
- job impact,
- timing,
- and risk-adjusted NPV.

## Bottom line
I will support a serious restructuring.  
I will **not** support the dismantling of our industrial anchor under the pressure of an unproven closure logic.

If management wants my agreement, it must first prove that it has exhausted a credible, quantified, non-closure path that preserves plants, protects as many jobs as possible, and keeps the company competitive for the long term.
