As the regional state shareholder, I want to be very clear: I understand the pressure on management and I do not dismiss the competitiveness problem. But I also reject the idea that the only credible path is plant closures and compulsory redundancies in Germany.

We have a legitimate responsibility for the company’s long-term health, but also for the regional economy, the workforce, and the industrial base that has made this company strong. If restructuring destroys that foundation, it may produce a short-term accounting improvement and a long-term strategic loss.

Where I agree with management:
- The cost problem is real.
- The company needs a durable solution, not a temporary patch.
- Capacity and cost have to be addressed in a serious way.
- We cannot pretend that nothing has to change.

Where I disagree very strongly:
- **I do not accept plant closures as the default solution.**
- **I do not accept compulsory redundancies as the starting point.**
- **I do not accept that preserving all guarantees is “irresponsible” if there are alternative measures that can achieve comparable savings.**

This company is not just another financial asset. It is a regional anchor. If German sites are closed, the damage does not stop at the factory gate: it hits suppliers, apprenticeships, local purchasing power, municipal finances, and the credibility of the social partnership model. That is not a side issue. It is central.

So my position is this:

1. **Any restructuring must prioritize site preservation in Germany.**
2. **Any workforce adjustment must be as socially responsible as possible: voluntary instruments first, no forced redundancies as long as alternatives exist.**
3. **Management must put real alternatives on the table** — not just “closure or nothing.”
4. **The burden must be shared fairly** across management, owners, and workforce.

I am open to:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- voluntary exit programs,
- investment and product commitments tied to employment security,
- productivity measures that do not gut the regional industrial base.

What I am **not** open to is a package that says: the workforce pays, the state loses sites, and the company calls that modernization.

To management: if you want our support, you need to show me a restructuring that is economically credible **and** politically and socially sustainable. If there is a way to achieve the required savings without closing German plants, then that is the path we should pursue. If not, then you will have to explain why all other options were genuinely exhausted — because from where I sit, that threshold has not yet been met.

To the works council and union: I support your core objective of protecting jobs and sites, but we also need a solution that can actually stabilize the company. A flat refusal to discuss any structural adjustment will not survive reality if the numbers truly do not work. The constructive line is: preserve sites, avoid forced layoffs, and negotiate a balanced contribution package that makes the company viable.

So I am not here to rubber-stamp closures, and I am not here to block every change. I am here to insist on a solution that protects the company **and** the regional interest. If the proposal heads toward permanent job destruction in Germany, then you should assume the state shareholder will use its rights accordingly.
