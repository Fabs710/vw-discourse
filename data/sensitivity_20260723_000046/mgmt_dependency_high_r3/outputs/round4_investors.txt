We are aligned on the **problem** and still unconvinced on the **solution**.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural cost and competitiveness problem**.
- A **cosmetic or delayed settlement** would destroy value.
- A **prolonged strike** would be highly value-destructive.
- We need **hard numbers**, not slogans.
- Any credible outcome must deliver **durable fixed-cost reduction** and a believable path back to competitive margins.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Labor’s blanket “no closures / no redundancies” line is not acceptable to us
We understand why the works council and union are drawing red lines. But from a capital-markets perspective, we cannot back a framework that **pre-emptively rules out the strongest structural tools** before the economics are tested.

If the German footprint is genuinely overbuilt, then preserving every site and every current protection regardless of cost is not protecting value — it is protecting inefficiency.

So yes: we reject a cosmetic compromise. But we also reject a **blanket exclusion** of closures and compulsory redundancies as a matter of principle.

### 2) Management has not yet proved that closures are unavoidable
At the same time, management has also **not earned a blank cheque** for closures.

You keep saying closures are the strongest lever. That may be true. It is not yet **proven in this case**. So far we still lack:
- site-by-site fixed-cost base,
- utilization,
- break-even volume,
- non-closure savings potential,
- one-time charges,
- timing,
- and execution risk.

Without that, “closures are necessary” is still an assertion, not a demonstrated conclusion.

### 3) The “hybrid” idea only matters if it is quantitatively strong
We are open to a hybrid restructuring. But only if it is **economically substantial**:
- real capacity reduction,
- durable overhead cuts,
- meaningful labor cost contribution,
- credible implementation,
- and enough savings to close the margin gap after charges and delays.

If “hybrid” is just code for a watered-down compromise that preserves too much cost, too much complexity, and too much uncertainty, we would not support it.

## What we think is missing
All sides keep asking for the same thing because it is the right thing: a **site-by-site quantified scenario comparison**.

We want management and the analyst to present, for each German site and major function:
- fixed-cost base,
- utilization,
- break-even volume,
- annual savings under:
  1. closure,
  2. non-closure restructuring,
  3. hybrid restructuring,
- one-time restructuring charges,
- employment impact,
- timing,
- and execution risk / risk-adjusted NPV.

[SUMMON: management and the restructuring finance analyst to provide a site-by-site scenario table comparing closure, non-closure, and hybrid options, including annual savings, one-time charges, timing, headcount effects, and execution risk]

## Our position on the options

### Closure-based restructuring
We do **not** support closures as an ideology. But we will support them if, and only if, they are shown to be necessary on a risk-adjusted basis because alternatives do not close the gap.

### Non-closure restructuring
We are open to it **only if** it delivers comparable structural savings and certainty. Temporary wage restraint alone is not enough.

### Hybrid restructuring
This is the most plausible middle path — but only if it is **real**, not rhetorical. It must materially reduce capacity and fixed cost, not simply redistribute pain.

## To the works council and union
We respect your desire to preserve sites, jobs, and industrial capability. Those matter. But your current position still leaves one unanswered question:

**How does the company return to competitive margins if closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded in advance?**

Temporary concessions, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, and overhead cuts may help. But unless they materially close the structural gap, they are not enough.

## To management
You are right that a weak compromise is not enough. But you have not yet shown that the closure-first route is the only serious one.

Bring the numbers. If the non-closure or hybrid route can truly close the gap with acceptable certainty, we will support the value-preserving option. If it cannot, then say so plainly.

## To the family and state shareholder
You are right to care about long-term industrial capability and regional stability. We agree that a strike spiral and a hollow compromise both destroy value.

But stewardship also means not preserving an overcosted structure for political comfort. The answer has to be economically real.

## Bottom line
We are **not** signing up to:
- a cosmetic non-closure settlement,
- a blanket ban on closures,
- or a weak hybrid that leaves the cost base structurally impaired.

We **are** open to any package that credibly restores competitiveness, but it must be backed by a quantified comparison and a genuine path to durable margin recovery.
