Let me be equally direct.

I hear the employee side’s red lines, and I understand why they are being drawn. But I do **not** accept that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no framework change” is a viable answer to a structural cost crisis of this magnitude.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on three things:
- the company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**,
- a **cosmetic compromise would be a failure**,
- and a **prolonged strike would damage value on all sides**.

I also agree that we need numbers, not slogans. That is exactly why I am pushing for a site-by-site comparison.

## Where I disagree

### 1) A blanket ban on closures is not serious
The works council and union keep treating German plant closures as illegitimate in principle. I reject that.

If the network is structurally overbuilt, then some capacity has to come out. You cannot restore margins, fund 2030 products, and close the cost gap while declaring the strongest structural lever off-limits before the analysis is even complete.

That is not a restructuring position. It is a refusal to confront the economics.

### 2) A blanket ban on compulsory redundancies is also not acceptable
I would prefer to avoid forced redundancies. But I will not promise that no workforce reduction can ever be necessary if the savings do not otherwise materialize.

If labor wants to rule out closures and forced reductions entirely, then it must show how the full cost gap is closed durably, not rhetorically. So far that has not been done.

### 3) “Binding job security to 2030” is incompatible with the scale of change
A blanket guarantee through 2030 would freeze the current cost structure through 2030. That would lock in uncompetitiveness when we need the opposite.

Security matters, yes. But security without competitiveness just delays the adjustment and makes the eventual correction harsher.

## On the claims made by labor

### “Management has not proven closures are necessary”
Not yet, because the numbers have not been tabled in the format I asked for. But the absence of that table does **not** prove the opposite. It simply means we now need the analysis urgently.

### “Selective capacity reduction is closure by another name”
No. It can be a legitimate restructuring tool if it is real:
- line consolidation,
- shift reduction,
- product transfer,
- footprint simplification,
- overhead reduction.

If those measures materially reduce fixed cost and capacity, they are part of the solution. If they do not, then they are too weak. That is exactly why we need the quantified comparison.

### “Protected sites must be truly protected”
I agree with the principle that if sites are retained, they need a credible future. But that does **not** mean every site is entitled to indefinite preservation regardless of economics.

## What I want to be clear about
I am **not** demanding closures as ideology. I am demanding an economically credible package.

I remain open to a non-closure or hybrid path **only if it is equivalent on the numbers**:
- magnitude of savings,
- durability,
- certainty of delivery,
- risk-adjusted margin recovery.

If it does not close the gap, then it is not enough.

## To the works council and union
You are asking us to accept all the burden-sharing instruments on labor’s terms while excluding the tools management may need if the package underperforms. That is not a balance.

You are free to propose a non-closure package. But if you want it to be taken seriously, it must show:
- how much fixed cost comes out,
- how much capacity comes out,
- how quickly,
- and with what certainty.

Temporary wage moderation and bonus restraint alone will not solve this. Working-time flexibility alone will not solve this. Voluntary exits alone will not solve this. If your proposal is real, show the full package and the full effect.

## To the state shareholder
I respect your concern for regional industrial capacity. But protecting the region long term requires a viable company, not just preserved structures.

I support a serious attempt to retain strategic sites where that is economically defensible. I do **not** support using the blocking position to freeze the footprint and preserve overcapacity.

## To the controlling family
I agree that stewardship means avoiding destructive conflict. It also means not forcing management to carry an uncompetitive network forever. We need your support for a package that is actually durable, not one that simply sounds gentler.

## To institutional investors
You are right to insist on hard numbers and a credible path to target margins. I share that. But do not mistake “no closures” rhetoric for seriousness. Seriousness is whether the package delivers structural savings at scale.

We need a real answer to the cost base, not a symbolic promise.
