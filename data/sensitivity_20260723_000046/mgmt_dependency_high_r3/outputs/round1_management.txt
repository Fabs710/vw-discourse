I hear both of you, and I will be equally clear.

We agree on one point: **this crisis must be solved through a durable industrial plan, not symbolic cuts**. A cosmetic arrangement that leaves our German cost base uncompetitive would fail the company, the brand, and ultimately the workforce as well.

But I must reject your core red lines as stated:

- **“No plant closures” cannot be an absolute if the network is structurally overbuilt.**
- **“No compulsory redundancies” cannot be guaranteed if the current capacity far exceeds demand and the cost gap is this large.**
- **A blanket preservation of the historic job-security framework through 2030 is not compatible with restoring competitiveness.**

The reality is simple: the company’s German operations are too expensive, the European market is shrinking, and we are losing ground to faster, lower-cost competitors. If we do not reduce capacity and labour costs in a structural way, we will continue to erode margins and then the industrial base you want to protect will become even weaker.

That said, I am not demanding closures as an ideological end in themselves. What I am demanding is this:

1. **A genuine, durable reduction in capacity**
2. **A substantial labour-cost contribution**
3. **A restructuring that restores the brand’s return on sales and frees investment for the next product cycle**
4. **A deal that is actually implementable and not just a public relations shield**

If there are alternative measures that achieve the same economic effect as closures — meaning real, not theoretical, savings — I am prepared to examine them. But they must be comparable in scale, timing, and certainty. Anything that simply postpones the problem is not acceptable.

To be specific:

- **Voluntary measures alone will not be enough** if they do not deliver the required scale.
- **Working-time flexibility** can help, but it is not a substitute for structural capacity cuts.
- **Investment commitments** are important, but they cannot be used to buy immunity from necessary restructuring.
- **Management and overhead contributions** are part of the package, but they cannot replace workforce-side adjustments where labour costs are far above target.

I also want to be frank about the process: if the employee side insists that no site can ever be touched, then it is effectively asking management to preserve excess cost indefinitely. That would be irresponsible. We have a fiduciary duty to restore competitiveness, and we will not sign up to a deal that leaves the company structurally weak.

So my position is this:

- I am open to a negotiated settlement.
- I am open to alternatives to outright closure if they deliver equivalent structural savings.
- I am **not** open to a deal that preserves all current sites, all current guarantees, and all current cost levels while calling it restructuring.

If you want a serious agreement, then bring forward a package that credibly reduces overcapacity and labour costs at scale. Otherwise, we are heading toward confrontation, not consensus.
