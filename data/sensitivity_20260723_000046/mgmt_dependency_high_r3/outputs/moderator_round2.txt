## Moderator Summary

### 1) What has been agreed
- The company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable** solution.
- A **pure delay tactic** or symbolic compromise would be inadequate.
- **Prolonged industrial conflict** would be value-destructive for all sides.
- A **non-closure option may be part of the solution**, but it must be economically credible.
- A **hybrid restructuring** has emerged as a possible middle path worth examining.
- All sides accept the need for **quantified comparisons** rather than slogan-based positions.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Labor representatives reject closures outright.
  - Management keeps closures on the table if needed.
  - State shareholder and controlling family are more open to alternatives, but not at any cost.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labor rejects them completely.
  - Management will not rule them out if other measures do not close the gap.
  - Other stakeholders prefer avoidance, but not a guaranteed veto.

- **Scope of job and site guarantees**
  - Labor wants binding protections through 2030.
  - Management says that is incompatible with deep restructuring.
  - Others see the need for some security, but not unconditional guarantees.

- **Who bears the burden**
  - Labor insists burden-sharing must include management and owners.
  - Management argues labor concessions alone are unlikely to be sufficient.
  - Investors and the family want a package that is materially effective and not merely cosmetic.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Savings size vs. social damage**
  - Closures appear strongest on certainty and scale of savings.
  - Non-closure approaches reduce social damage but may leave a savings gap.

- **Permanence vs. flexibility**
  - Labor prefers reversible or temporary measures.
  - Management prefers irreversible structural cost removal.

- **Implementation certainty vs. governance risk**
  - A more ambitious plan may save more, but also raises strike, delay, and execution risk.

- **Short-term restructuring charges vs. long-term cash savings**
  - The financial comparison still needs a clearer net-present-value view.

- **Site preservation vs. network simplification**
  - Stakeholders differ on how much industrial capacity must be retained to preserve future viability.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to a **single quantified scenario comparison**.
- Ask management to present a **site-by-site hybrid proposal** with:
  - expected savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - employment impacts,
  - timing,
  - and implementation risk.
- Ask the analyst to **stress-test whether the hybrid package closes the margin gap** after charges and execution risk.
- Keep the next round focused on **what combination of measures can deliver durable savings with the least irreducible conflict**.
