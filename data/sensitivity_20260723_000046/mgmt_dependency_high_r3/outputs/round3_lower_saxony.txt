I will be clear.

I agree with management on one point: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a postponement. I also agree with investors and the family that a cosmetic compromise would be a mistake. If the German cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, the problem will return.

But I **do not** accept management’s repeated framing that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain the default answer. That is too one-sided, and it is not yet proven.

## Where I agree
- The company faces a real competitiveness and cost problem.
- A weak deal that only delays the issue would be irresponsible.
- A prolonged strike would damage the company, the workforce, and the regional economy.
- Any serious solution must be **quantified**, not slogan-based.
- Capacity adjustment is unavoidable in some form.

## Where I disagree
### On plant closures
I do **not** accept that German plant closures should be treated as the first serious option.  
As the state shareholder, I cannot support a solution that destroys jobs, supplier networks, apprenticeships, and regional industrial capacity unless there is no credible alternative.

The finance input shows closures are economically powerful, yes. But it also shows that the real question is whether a **hybrid or non-closure package** can remove enough fixed cost and capacity with acceptable certainty. That has not yet been conclusively answered.

So I reject the idea that closures are justified simply because they are the cleanest managerial tool.

### On compulsory redundancies
I also do **not** accept making forced layoffs a normal planning assumption. They may be management’s fallback position, but they are not a starting point I can endorse. If there is still room for voluntary exits, working-time solutions, overhead cuts, product rationalization, and capacity reduction, then those must be exhausted first.

### On blanket guarantees
At the same time, I understand why management says it cannot sign up to unlimited job and site guarantees through 2030. I do **not** demand unconditional preservation of every existing structure.

But I also will not support a deal that gives the workforce no security at all. A serious restructuring must include:
- real site protection where economically defensible,
- phased adjustment,
- no forced redundancies except as a last resort,
- and investment commitments tied to the retained German footprint.

## My view on the current positions
### To management
You are right that a purely defensive non-closure deal may leave the company overcosted. But you have not yet shown, site by site, that a serious non-closure or hybrid package cannot close the gap.

So I support your demand for numbers, but not your implicit conclusion that closures are therefore unavoidable.

### To the works council and union
You are right to resist a one-sided burden shift onto labor. You are also right that jobs, skills, and trust are real industrial assets.

But I have to say plainly: your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no framework change” position is not yet a complete economic answer. It is a legitimate red line, but red lines do not by themselves solve the cost problem.

If you want my support, you must engage seriously with a quantified alternative that can actually deliver durable savings.

### To the controlling family
You are right that value preservation matters and that blunt closures can create lasting damage. But value is also destroyed if the company remains too expensive and cannot invest in its future. I expect you to use your governance influence to push for a serious restructuring package, not merely to keep all options vague.

### To institutional investors
You are right to insist on credibility and implementation certainty. But you should not treat closures as the only benchmark of seriousness. A properly designed hybrid package can be serious if it truly removes cost, simplifies the network, and preserves enough industrial base to remain stable.

## What I can support
I can support a restructuring that combines:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management overhead reductions,
- owner contribution,
- and binding investment commitments for the German sites.

I can also support a **hybrid** approach if it is genuinely necessary and if it protects the company’s regional anchor role.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a closure-first approach presented as inevitable before alternatives are proven,
- compulsory redundancies as a default tool,
- or a settlement that guts the state’s industrial base while calling it modernization.

## What must happen next
We need a **site-by-site, quantified comparison** of:
1. closure-based restructuring,
2. non-closure restructuring,
3. hybrid restructuring.

For each site and major function, I want:
- fixed-cost base,
- utilization,
- break-even volume,
- annual savings,
- one-time restructuring charges,
- employment impact,
- timing,
- and risk-adjusted certainty.

## Bottom line
My position is not “no change.”  
My position is: **no solution that destroys the state’s industrial anchor unnecessarily**.

If a non-closure or hybrid package can truly close the gap and protect the long-term health of the company, I am prepared to support it. If it cannot, then we will need to confront that honestly — but I will not approve plant closures or forced layoffs just because they are the easiest way to balance the books.

The company’s future matters. So do the jobs, plants, and regional economy that make it worth preserving.
