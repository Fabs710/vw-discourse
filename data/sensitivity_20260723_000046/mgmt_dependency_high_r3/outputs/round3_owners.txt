We have now narrowed this to the real issue: **can the company restore competitiveness without creating a larger, longer value destruction through paralysis?**

My answer is: **only if the package is economically real.** I am not interested in a symbolic settlement that preserves every comfort and leaves the business structurally underpowered.

## Where I agree

I agree with everyone on several basics:

- The company has a **serious structural cost problem**.
- A **temporary patch** would be irresponsible.
- A **prolonged strike** would destroy value.
- Burden-sharing must be **visible and credible**.
- A solution must be **durable**, not just politically convenient.

I also agree with the union and works council on one important point: management cannot simply demand that labour absorbs everything while everyone else stays protected. That would be unstable and ultimately self-defeating.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute rule
I cannot accept an unconditional ban on plant closures.

If the German network is genuinely overcapacity, then preserving every site regardless of economics is not stewardship. It is a guarantee of continued underperformance. That is not acceptable for a controlling shareholder whose duty is long-term value preservation.

So I reject any position that says closures are off the table before the numbers are even tested.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be a hard veto either
I prefer to avoid forced layoffs. But I will not support a framework that makes them impossible in all circumstances.

If alternatives cannot close the gap, then the company must retain the ability to adjust headcount. Otherwise, the restructuring becomes fiction.

### 3) A vague “hybrid” is not enough
I am open to a hybrid restructuring, but only if it is **specific, quantified, and enforceable**.

“Hybrid” is not a solution by itself. It can be a euphemism for delay, or for a watered-down package that looks balanced but leaves the cost base intact. I will not endorse that.

## Where I stand on the options

### Closure-based restructuring
Economically powerful, but socially and politically risky.  
I do **not** support closures as the default answer. But I do not exclude them if they are necessary after alternatives are properly tested.

### Non-closure restructuring
Potentially acceptable only if it removes enough fixed cost, not just temporary labour cost.  
A weak no-closure package is not a solution; it is a postponement.

### Hybrid restructuring
This is the only route I see as potentially workable **if** it genuinely reduces capacity, simplifies the network, and produces durable savings without triggering uncontrolled conflict.

## What matters to me

I need to see four things:

1. **Magnitude**  
   The package must materially close the margin gap.

2. **Durability**  
   It must change the economics structurally, not just for one year.

3. **Certainty**  
   It must be implementable without endless renegotiation and leakage.

4. **Stability**  
   It must not trigger a strike spiral or destroy the company’s legitimacy.

If a non-closure or hybrid package can deliver those four things, I can support it.

If it cannot, then we should stop pretending it is sufficient.

## My position, plainly stated

I am prepared to support:

I am **not** prepared to support:
- a weak non-closure compromise that leaves the company overcosted,
- a blanket prohibition on closures and compulsory redundancies,
