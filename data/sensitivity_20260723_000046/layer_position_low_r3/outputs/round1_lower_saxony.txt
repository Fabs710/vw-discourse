I will be very direct.

The state can agree that the company needs a serious restructuring. We do **not** deny the competitive pressure, the investment burden of the transformation, or the need for durable improvements in productivity and cost. But we **do** reject the idea that the only credible restructuring is one built around plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies.

That is not an acceptable starting point for us.

### Where we agree
I agree with management on one point: a merely cosmetic solution is not enough. A deal that postpones the problem, protects every inefficiency, and offers no structural improvement would be irresponsible.

I also agree with the works council and the union that the burden cannot fall one-sidedly on employees. If the company is asking for sacrifice, then management, owners, and capital allocation decisions must also be part of the solution. That is a matter of fairness and of political legitimacy.

### Where I disagree
I strongly disagree with the framing that plant closures are somehow the default or necessary consequence of seriousness. Closing German sites would be a major and irreversible decision, with lasting damage to regional employment, industrial capacity, and public trust. It would also risk undermining the company’s role as an economic anchor in the regions where it has a long-standing presence.

From our perspective, a plan that begins with closures is not a balanced restructuring. It is a high-risk shortcut.

I also disagree with any attempt to place long-standing job and site protections on the chopping block as if they were merely negotiable conveniences. These protections are part of the social partnership that has sustained this company and its regions for decades. They cannot simply be discarded because management wants a faster accounting result.

### What would be acceptable
We are prepared to support a restructuring that:
- preserves German sites where at all possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- uses voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time measures, and capacity adjustments,
- includes a fair contribution from labour **and** from management and ownership,
- and is tied to a credible long-term investment and product plan.

That is the path to a durable solution.

### What is not acceptable
I need to state this plainly: **we will not support a package that relies on plant closures as the main lever or that treats mass layoffs as unavoidable before alternatives are exhausted**. And we will not accept a process in which the state and workforce are presented with a done deal.

We have blocking rights for a reason, and we will use them if necessary to prevent a solution that destroys jobs and weakens the company’s regional foundation.

### On the questions management asked
1. **Which capacity comes out?**  
   That must be answered by examining alternatives first, not by pre-selecting closure. Capacity reduction can come through production shifts, volume adjustments, working-time tools, and product allocation.

2. **How much structural cost reduction is guaranteed?**  
   We are open to a meaningful contribution, but it must be delivered through negotiated, reversible means wherever possible, not through irreversible site destruction.

3. **What labour-cost contribution is on the table?**  
   A negotiated contribution is possible, but only in a package that protects jobs and sites and is balanced by contributions from management and ownership.

4. **How quickly does this restore competitiveness?**  
   Fast enough to be credible, but not so fast that it tears up the company’s social and regional foundations. A rushed closure strategy may look quick; it often creates longer-term costs, conflict, and political damage.

So our message is: **we are ready for a serious restructuring, but not for closures and mass layoffs dressed up as necessity**. Bring a proposal that protects the company’s future **and** its regional responsibility, and we can work with it. Bring a closure-led plan, and you will meet firm resistance from us.
