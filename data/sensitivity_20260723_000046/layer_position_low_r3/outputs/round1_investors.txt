We are listening carefully, and we are not going to support a solution that simply **pushes the cost problem into the future**.

From the capital markets’ perspective, the non-negotiable issue is not whether the restructuring is uncomfortable — it will be. The issue is whether it is **credible enough to restore competitive margins and protect long-term equity value**. On that basis:

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that a cosmetic deal is not enough. If the German cost base stays structurally uncompetitive, the equity story remains damaged.
- **The family shareholder is right** that the company needs durable change, not symbolic reassurance.
- **The state is right** that burden-sharing matters and that prolonged conflict destroys value.
- **The works council and union are right** that a one-sided solution imposed only on labour is neither sustainable nor investable.

### Where we disagree
#### To the works council and union
We do **not** accept “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute starting points if they prevent a credible turnaround.

That is not because we are indifferent to the workforce. It is because as shareholders we cannot back a structure that leaves the company with:
- excess fixed cost,
- chronically weak margins,
- and a weak return on sales versus peers.

If the restructuring is reduced to voluntary measures and reversible adjustments that do **not** actually remove enough cost, then it is not a restructuring. It is a delay tactic. And we will not support that.

#### To management
We also do **not** support using the existence of pressure as a blank cheque for a destructive conflict. If you want investor backing, you need to show that this is not just a blunt exercise in headline cuts, but a **disciplined plan** with:
- clear site/capacity decisions,
- quantified savings,
- a realistic implementation timeline,
- and a path to sustainable margins.

Investors do not need rhetoric about urgency. We need evidence that the measures will actually reset the cost base.

### What would make this acceptable to us
We would back a package if it includes all of the following:
1. **A real and measurable reduction in overcapacity**
   - not vague “efficiency,” but actual capacity exit where needed.
2. **Durable cost reduction**
   - enough to materially improve margins and competitiveness.
3. **A credible employee contribution**
   - flexibility, working-time measures, and where necessary wage moderation or equivalent cost relief.
4. **Protection against endless delay**
   - a deal with clear timing and implementation milestones.
5. **No purely cosmetic preservation of all legacy structures**
   - because that would leave value impairment unresolved.

### Our red line
What is fundamentally unacceptable to us is an outcome that:
- keeps all German capacity intact,
- preserves the current cost base in practice,
- and leaves the company still structurally below target on margins.

If that is the outcome, then investor confidence will continue to erode, and capital will reprice the company accordingly. We are not prepared to subsidize inaction.

### On industrial action
We also want to be explicit: a prolonged strike or open-ended confrontation would be value-destructive. It may increase pressure, but it does not create competitiveness. So we strongly prefer a negotiated outcome — but only one that is **real**, not cosmetic.

### Bottom line
We are not aligned with any side that wants to preserve everything and call it restructuring.

- If management brings a **credible structural plan**, we will support it.
- If labour offers only **limits that prevent real cost reduction**, we will not support it.
- If owners and the state insist on a deal that avoids conflict but does not solve the economics, we will regard that as unacceptable.

We want a solution that **restores competitive margins, not merely industrial peace**.
