I will speak plainly.

### To management
You are still not answering the central fairness issue. You say the company needs a durable reset, and I do not dispute that. But you continue to treat **closures and compulsory redundancies** as normal instruments rather than last-resort exceptions. That is not a balanced restructuring; it is shifting the full adjustment burden onto the workforce.

What I can acknowledge:
- The German cost base must come down.
- Capacity must be adjusted.
- A purely cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.
- Employees can and should contribute in a negotiated way.

What I do **not** accept:
- plant closures as a starting point,
- forced layoffs as an acceptable fallback,
- or the dismantling of collective wage protections as if they were disposable.

If management wants our support, then bring a package that proves you are not asking workers to absorb all the pain while executive structures, ownership returns, and investment priorities remain largely untouched.

### To the family shareholder and capital markets
You both speak about “structural change,” but your version too easily becomes a euphemism for permanently weakening labour standards and thinning out the German industrial base. That is not the only way to get competitiveness.

I agree that the company cannot live on delay. I disagree that closures are necessary simply because they are the cleanest accounting lever. That may look tidy on a spreadsheet, but it is not a fair or stable industrial solution.

If you want labour peace, skilled labour, and future capacity, then you must accept that:
- employees are not the only ones who must contribute,
- wage standards cannot be sacrificed one-way,
- and a viable company needs stable sites and trust, not just cost cuts.

### To the state government
Your position is closer to ours, but words are not enough. If you oppose closures and forced redundancies, then use your blocking rights consistently. Do not allow “restructuring realism” to become a backdoor for irreversible job destruction.

### What we can support
We are prepared to negotiate a serious, quantified package that includes:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures**
- **binding site and employment protection through 2030**
- **capacity reductions through working-time tools, shift adjustments, redeployment, and voluntary exit options**
- **qualification and internal transfer guarantees**
- **a negotiated employee contribution**, but only as part of a shared-sacrifice deal
- **visible contributions from management and owners**, including executive restraint and capital allocation changes
- **preservation of the collective wage framework**

That is a real restructuring path. It is not a “nice to have.” It is the minimum basis for a settlement we could defend to the workforce.

### What we will not accept
Let me be very clear: we will not accept a package that says:
- the company may close plants if talks do not deliver enough,
- jobs can be cut compulsorily if voluntary tools fall short,
- or the collective wage framework can be rewritten downward to force competitiveness.

Those are not neutral technical choices. They would mean employees bear the strategic failure almost alone. That is exactly what we will resist.

### On the savings question
If management says the economics require a specific savings amount, then the burden of proof is on you to show:
1. how much can be achieved through internal redeployment and capacity tools,
2. what management and ownership contribute,
3. how much employee contribution is actually necessary,
4. and why that still leaves no alternative to closures or forced redundancies.

If you cannot demonstrate that, then your closure logic is not necessity — it is preference.

### My bottom line
We are ready for a tough agreement. We are not ready to sign away jobs, sites, and collective standards in exchange for vague promises about future competitiveness.

A fair settlement must be:
- durable,
- shared,
- and reversible where possible.

If management refuses that logic, then I will say it openly: we remain prepared to escalate, including strikes, because our members will not pay for restructuring that others refuse to share.
