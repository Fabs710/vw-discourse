## My position remains unchanged

The family holding supports a **real restructuring that restores competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity**. We will **not** support a settlement that:
- leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- treats all German capacity as untouchable by principle,
- turns “no closures” into an absolute veto,
- or locks in prolonged conflict that destroys value.

That is the line.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that this cannot be cosmetic. If the cost base stays above target, the company will keep eroding value and crowding out future investment. We support a **real structural reset**, not a delay.

### With labour and the state
You are also right that the burden cannot fall only on employees. Management restraint, overhead reduction, executive discipline, and capital allocation changes all have to contribute. And yes, prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.

### With the expert
The expert is right on the method: we need a **quantified comparison** of scenarios, not slogans. We need to see savings, timing, one-time costs, implementation risk, and margin impact through 2030.

---

## Where I disagree strongly

### 1) “No closures” as a principle
I do **not** accept a blanket rule that no German site may ever be closed.

That is not stewardship; that is freezing the cost problem in place. If a site is structurally uncompetitive, pretending otherwise damages the whole enterprise over time. The family holding cannot back a permanent overcapacity problem just because closures are politically uncomfortable.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I would prefer to avoid forced reductions. But I will **not** rule them out in all circumstances.

If voluntary tools, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and other negotiated measures do not deliver enough durable savings, management must retain a credible fallback. Without that, the restructuring lacks discipline.

### 3) Binding guarantees through 2030 as untouchable
Guarantees can support trust, but they cannot become a straitjacket that prevents structural change. If they freeze an uncompetitive structure, they reduce long-term value and ultimately weaken the industrial base they are meant to protect.

---

## On the central question: can non-closure deliver equivalent structural effect?

That is the real test.

A non-closure package is only acceptable if it produces:
- **real capacity reduction**,
- **durable labor-cost relief**,
- **meaningful overhead cuts**,
- and **visible savings in 2025–2027**, not just a promise for 2030.

If it does not, then closures or major footprint reduction remain legitimate tools.

I am not choosing closure for its own sake. I am saying the economics must govern the decision.

Do not shortcut that work. Bring us:

## To the state

Your blocking role matters, but it cannot become a permanent shield for structurally weak capacity.

If the numbers show that some footprint reduction is needed to restore viability, then the state has to face that reality. The right position is not “never anything closed,” but “only what is necessary, justified, and carefully structured.”

That said, I understand the state’s responsibility to jobs and regional stability. That is precisely why we need a hard, quantified comparison now.

---

## To investors

You are right to demand credibility. But credibility is not created by forcing the largest possible headline cut regardless of implementation risk. A failed tough plan is worse than a durable hard compromise.

Still, your concern is valid: a non-closure package that does not materially change the cost base is not enough.

## What the family holding would require from any non-closure package

If labour wants us to back a no-closure route, it must be more than a declaration. It must be a package with measurable effect:

1. **Permanent savings**, not temporary concessions
2. **Real capacity reduction**, even if not via full plant closure
3. **Workforce contribution** through flexibility, moderated progression, and legacy changes
4. **Management and owner contribution** that is real, not symbolic
5. **Implementation certainty** with milestones and enforcement

If those conditions are met, I am open. If they are not, I will not pretend the numbers work.

## My bottom line

The family holding is **not** saying “closures at any price.”

We are saying:
- prove the non-closure route can deliver equivalent structural effect,
- prove the savings are durable and timely,
- and prove the company remains competitively viable.

If that can be done, I will support it. If it cannot, then deeper cuts, including closures or major footprint reduction, remain legitimate and necessary options.
