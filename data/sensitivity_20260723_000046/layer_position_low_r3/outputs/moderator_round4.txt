**Core impasse**

The dispute has converged on one central fault line:

> **Whether restructuring must be judged primarily by financial sufficiency and structural effect, or whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies are unacceptable regardless of their financial case.**

That creates a second, practical impasse:

> **No side trusts the others’ claims about what has been “fully tested,” so the burden of proof is being disputed as much as the outcome.**

In short:
- **Management / family / investors** want the right to use closures or forced reductions if the numbers require it.
- **Works council / union / state** want a binding restructuring without closures or compulsory redundancies.
- **The expert** says the answer depends on whether a non-closure package can deliver a durable, near-equivalent structural reset.

So the blockage is not just “what is the best plan,” but **what counts as an acceptable plan at all**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Non-closure first, independently verified”
A process compromise:
- Management submits a quantified non-closure package.
- An independent audit verifies savings, timing, one-time costs, and residual gap.
- Only if the audit shows a material shortfall do the parties reopen discussion on tighter structural measures.

**Purpose:**  
This bridges the trust gap by making the non-closure route a genuine test, not a symbolic gesture.

---

### Proposal 2: “Protected sites, adjustable internal levers”
A substance compromise:
- All current German plants remain open for a defined period.
- The parties agree to internal restructuring tools only:
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time changes,
  - overhead cuts,
  - product/line rationalization,
  - management and capital-side contributions.
- A jointly reviewed milestone system checks whether the savings target is being met.

**Purpose:**  
This gives labour and the state the site-protection they want, while giving management a real restructuring path.

---

### Proposal 3: “Conditional equivalence package”
A conditional settlement:
- If the non-closure package delivers a defined share of the target savings and capacity reduction by a set date, closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table.
- If it does not, the parties enter a second-stage negotiation on narrowly defined fallback measures.

**Purpose:**  
This creates a bridge between the “no closures ever” and “closures may be necessary” positions, while avoiding an immediate rupture.

**What specific measurable conditions would make you accept that a non-closure package has genuinely failed to close the savings gap, without treating any later fallback as inherently illegitimate?**

---

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-limited, independently audited non-closure package if it included binding management and owner contributions, provided that failure to meet targets triggers a second-stage review?**

---

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact non-closure measures, savings, and timelines can you put on the table now that would allow the other side to verify that closures are not being used as a first-choice bargaining lever?**

---

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to define in advance what level of verified shortfall would still justify your blocking role, and what level would require you to reconsider your position?**

---

### To the Controlling Family
**What concrete owner-side contribution are you willing to commit to up front so that “stewardship” is not interpreted as preserving optionality at the workforce’s expense?**

---

### To the Institutional Investors
**What minimum structural improvement would satisfy you that a non-closure package is not just politically palatable, but financially credible enough to support equity value?**

---

### To the Expert
**Can you specify the threshold criteria that would let the parties distinguish a “credible non-closure package” from a merely softer version of the status quo?**
