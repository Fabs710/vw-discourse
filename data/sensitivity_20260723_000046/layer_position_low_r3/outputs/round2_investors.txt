We are prepared to back a restructuring, but only if it is **credible, quantified, and sufficient to restore competitive margins**. We will not support a package that preserves the current cost problem under softer language.

### Where we agree
- **Management** is right that a cosmetic deal is unacceptable.
- **The family shareholder** is right that durability matters and that overcapacity cannot simply be wished away.
- **The state** and **labour side** are right that one-sided burden-shifting and prolonged conflict destroy value.
- We also agree that **management, owners, and capital allocation must contribute**, not just employees.

### Where we disagree
#### On “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies”
We do **not** accept these as absolute red lines if they prevent a real turnaround. If the package cannot remove enough fixed cost and capacity, then the company will remain structurally weak. That is not acceptable for shareholders.

The works council and union keep treating closures and redundancies as if they are inherently the problem. They are not the objective; they are tools. We would prefer alternatives, but only if they deliver the **same structural effect**. If they do not, then refusing closures in principle is just delay.

#### On guarantees through 2030
Binding guarantees may be useful if they support industrial peace, but not if they lock in an uncompetitive structure. We will not support a deal that freezes legacy protections while margins stay below target.

#### On the state’s blocking role
We respect the political reality, but blocking powers should not be used to protect structurally uncompetitive capacity indefinitely. That would impair enterprise value and ultimately weaken the company’s ability to invest in the regions as well.

### What would make this supportable
We would support a package if it includes:

1. **A clear capacity exit plan**
   - which plants or lines are reduced or closed, or what equivalent capacity leaves the system.
2. **Quantified durable savings**
   - enough to materially improve return on sales and competitiveness.
3. **A real employee contribution**
   - flexibility, working-time changes, and where needed wage moderation or equivalent cost relief.
4. **Concrete management/owner contribution**
   - executive restraint, overhead cuts, and disciplined capital allocation.
5. **Implementation certainty**
   - milestones, timelines, and enforcement so this does not become another deferred reset.

### Our red line
An outcome that keeps all German capacity in place, avoids meaningful structural cuts, and leaves margins impaired is fundamentally unacceptable. We will not back a cosmetic settlement.

### Bottom line
We are not here to defend plant closures for their own sake. We are here to defend **risk-adjusted equity value**. If the non-closure alternatives are real and deliver the same savings, fine. If not, then the company must make the hard choices and do so quickly enough to matter.
