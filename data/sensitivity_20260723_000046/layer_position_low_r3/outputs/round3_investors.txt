We agree with the central point emerging in this round: **this must be a real restructuring with quantified impact**, not a symbolic compromise. Where we part company is on how much faith to place in a non-closure path that has not yet demonstrated equivalent structural effect.

## Where we agree
- **Management** is right that the company cannot preserve an uncompetitive cost base and still restore margins.
- **The family holding** is right that long-term value, not short-term optics, has to drive the answer.
- **The state, works council, and union** are right that one-sided burden shifting and prolonged conflict destroy value.
- **The expert** is right that we need a scenario comparison, not slogans.

## Where we disagree
### On “no closures” as a principle
We do **not** support an absolute no-closure rule if it prevents a credible turnaround.

A closure is not desirable, but it is also not automatically irrational. If German sites remain structurally overcapacity and the savings gap is real, then refusing to consider footprint reduction in principle risks locking in weak margins and continued value erosion. That is worse for shareholders, and ultimately worse for the company.

### On compulsory redundancies
We would prefer to avoid them, but we will not rule them out absolutely if voluntary tools and redeployment cannot deliver enough durable savings. If management is forced to accept a settlement that has no credible enforcement mechanism, then the restructuring may fail.

### On 2030 guarantees
Binding guarantees are only acceptable if they do not freeze an uncompetitive structure. If the guarantees make it impossible to restore competitiveness, then they are not a solution; they are a delay mechanism.

## Our position on the scenario question
We are open to a **non-closure package only if it is financially equivalent in effect**. That means it must deliver:
- permanent fixed-cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- measurable labor-cost relief,
- visible savings in 2025–2027,
- and enough durability to restore return on sales through 2030.

If those conditions are not met, then a closure or major footprint-reduction fallback is not a threat; it is the remaining responsible tool.

## What we want to see next
We want management, with the expert’s support, to present a **hard comparison** of:
1. **Non-closure package**
2. **Hybrid package**
3. **Closure / footprint-reduction package**

For each, we need:
- annual run-rate savings
- one-time costs
- timing of savings
- implementation risk
- effect on 2030 ROS
- and probability of labor / governance delay

Without that, the debate stays political and unverifiable.

## Bottom line
We are **not** here to defend closures for their own sake. We are here to defend **risk-adjusted equity value**.

So our line is:
- yes to a serious negotiated restructuring,
- yes to burden-sharing beyond labour,
- yes to non-closure alternatives **if they are truly equivalent**,
- but **no** to a cosmetic settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

If the numbers support a non-closure route, fine. If they do not, then the hard choices must stay on the table.
