### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that **the company needs a real restructuring**, not a symbolic one.
- There is broad agreement that **burden-sharing cannot fall only on employees**; management, ownership, and capital allocation must also contribute.
- Most parties support some mix of:
  - **capacity reduction**,
  - **working-time flexibility**,
  - **redeployment/qualification**,
  - **voluntary exit tools**,
  - **management restraint**,
  - and **measurable savings**.
- There is also shared recognition that **prolonged conflict would destroy value**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:** Labour side and the state reject closures as a starting point; management, owners, and investors treat closures as legitimate if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour side and the state want these excluded or at least strongly limited; management and investors insist they remain available if alternatives fail.
- **Binding job/site guarantees through 2030:** Labour side wants strong guarantees; management, owners, and investors say guarantees cannot be absolute if they block competitiveness.
- **How “serious” restructuring is defined:** Labour side says alternatives must be exhausted first and can deliver the needed savings; management and investors say alternatives are only acceptable if they produce the same durable structural effect as closures or direct workforce cuts.
- **Role of the state’s blocking power:** Labour side and state emphasize protection of industrial footprint; management, owners, and investors object to any de facto veto over necessary restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. site preservation:** No shared calculation yet shows whether the required savings can be achieved without closures.
- **Flexibility vs. security:** The parties have not settled how much working-time, wage, or guarantee flexibility employees can give in exchange for job and site protection.
- **Voluntary tools vs. enforceable cuts:** It remains unclear how far voluntary exits, redeployment, and internal transfers can go before management demands compulsory measures.
- **Short-term adjustment vs. long-term trust:** The dispute remains open on whether stronger upfront cuts are worth the likely damage to industrial peace and co-determination.
- **Management/owner contribution size:** There is agreement that these contributions are needed, but not on their scale or whether they can materially reduce the burden on labour.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle to **numbers**: quantify the savings gap, the contribution of each party, and the effect of non-closure alternatives.
- Compare two concrete scenarios:
  1. a **non-closure restructuring package**, and  
  2. a **closure/forced-reduction package**,
  with savings, timing, and implementation risk for each.
- Clarify which items are **true red lines** and which are **negotiable only under defined conditions**.
- Aim for a structured discussion of **“equivalent structural effect”**: what combination of measures would make the labour-side alternative credible to management, owners, and investors.
