### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that the burden should **not fall only on employees**; management, ownership, and capital allocation also need to contribute.
- Several sides support using **alternatives to immediate compulsory layoffs** where possible, including:
  - voluntary exit programmes
  - redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - working-time measures
  - capacity adjustments
  - productivity improvements
- There is shared recognition that **prolonged conflict would be value-destructive**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and state reject closures as the starting point and want all German sites protected.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour and state oppose them.  
  - Management, owners, and investors will not rule them out absolutely if negotiated measures do not deliver enough savings.
- **Existing job/site guarantees:**  
  - Labour side wants binding protection through 2030.  
  - Management and owners say such guarantees cannot remain untouched if they block structural change.
- **Depth and speed of cost reduction:**  
  - Capital side and management want clear, durable capacity and cost cuts.  
  - Labour and state want restructuring that avoids irreversible damage and preserves employment.
- **Role of co-determination / blocking rights:**  
  - Labour and state insist on a genuine role in shaping the outcome.  
  - Management and owners want those rights not to prevent necessary structural decisions.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity must exit** versus how much can be preserved through softer measures.
- **What “enough” employee contribution means** in practice: wage moderation, flexibility, working time, or other concessions.
- **What management/owner contribution will be concrete and measurable** beyond general commitments.
- **Whether savings can be achieved without irreversible site closures**, and if not, which sites or capacities would be affected.
- **How to balance speed with social legitimacy**: faster implementation for competitiveness versus slower, negotiated change to avoid conflict.
- **How to secure long-term competitiveness** while preserving industrial peace and regional responsibility.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **specific package options**:
- identify the **concrete measures** each side could accept,
- quantify the **expected savings / capacity reduction**,
- specify **what concessions** are on the table from labour, management, and owners,
- and test whether there is a **non-closure pathway** that is still credible, or whether any closure-related fallback can be narrowly defined and time-limited.
