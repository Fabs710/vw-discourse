I agree with the core of what the works council has said: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no one-sided burden on employees**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side: the company’s current course is not an acceptable “restructuring” if it means workers pay for management’s strategic mistakes through job losses, wage cuts, and the dismantling of long-standing protections. We recognise the economic pressure, but that does **not** justify turning the workforce into the sole shock absorber.

What we can discuss is a **fair, negotiated contribution** from employees — but only if it is matched by real contributions from management, owners, and capital allocation decisions. That means:

- preserving sites and industrial presence in Germany
- ruling out compulsory redundancies
- avoiding pay cuts that become a permanent downward shift in collective standards
- using capacity reductions, working-time tools, voluntary programmes, qualification, and redeployment instead
- securing a binding settlement with a durable employment perspective

We are not rejecting change. We are rejecting **irreversible cuts that destroy jobs and weaken the collective wage framework** while others remain untouched. If management wants labour to contribute, then management must also contribute: through lower executive burden, reconsideration of dividends and investment priorities, and serious commitments to future products and jobs.

So my position is this: **we are prepared to negotiate a shared-sacrifice package, but only one that protects jobs and sites.** If the plan still depends on closures or forced layoffs, then we will oppose it decisively, including through industrial action.
