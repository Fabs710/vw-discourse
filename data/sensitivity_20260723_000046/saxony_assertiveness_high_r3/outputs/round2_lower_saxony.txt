I appreciate the clarity from all sides, but I need to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that uses plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or the erosion of co-determination as the primary adjustment mechanism**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one central point: **the company does need a durable competitiveness reset**. The current cost base, market pressure, and margin gap cannot simply be wished away.

I also agree with the union and works council that **a cosmetic deal would be irresponsible**. If the company is to ask the workforce for concessions, then the burden cannot fall on employees alone. Management, owners, and capital must contribute too.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Closures are not the starting point
Management keeps saying closures must remain “on the table.” From the state’s perspective, that is not an acceptable default position. Plant closures in Germany are not just a line item; they damage regional employment, supplier networks, tax bases, and industrial capability. Once a site is gone, the know-how and ecosystem do not come back.

So no: **we do not accept closures as the preferred or presumed solution**.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
We are not prepared to back a restructuring that treats forced layoffs as a normal instrument. If workforce reduction is needed, then the first instruments must be:
- voluntary departures,
- retirements,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity adjustments.

If management says it needs “material” savings, then it must show that it has exhausted these routes before moving to measures that inflict permanent social damage.

### 3) A blanket rejection of security is a mistake
Management says a binding employment guarantee through 2030 is incompatible with restructuring. I disagree. A credible guarantee is not a luxury; it is what creates the trust necessary for employees to cooperate in a difficult transition.

That does not mean no adjustment. It means **adjustment under a framework of site and employment security**, not open-ended insecurity with a promise of future consideration.

## What we will support
We are prepared to support a tough but socially responsible restructuring if it is built on these principles:

1. **No plant closures as the first resort**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Protection of core German sites and industrial capacity**
4. **A binding employment and site-security framework**
5. **Real burden-sharing from management, ownership, and capital**
6. **Investment and product commitments for the German operations**
7. **A restructuring that preserves the state’s effective co-determination and veto role**

## On the employee contribution
We understand that there must be a contribution from labour. But that contribution cannot be defined as “lower pay and fewer jobs until the numbers work.” That is too one-sided.

If employees are to contribute, then we expect:
- management restraint,
- overhead reduction,
- serious shareholder support,
- and a credible industrial strategy for the German plants.

I also want to underline that **temporary measures alone are not enough if they merely hide a permanent weakening of the sites**. The state will not back a paper solution.

## On governance
Let me be explicit: the special governance structure matters. The state is not a passive shareholder. We have a legitimate responsibility to the regional economy, and we will use our rights if necessary. Any attempt to sideline the state’s role or force through a closure-led plan will face resistance.

## My bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking a **socially destructive restructuring**.

A viable path must:
- restore competitiveness,
- protect jobs,
- preserve key sites in the state,
- and maintain the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.

If management wants our support, it should come back with a package that demonstrates, in measurable terms, how it will achieve savings **without** closures and **without** compulsory redundancies, and how it will secure the future of the German sites.

If that cannot be done, then the burden of proof lies with those proposing irreversible damage to the company’s social and regional foundation.
