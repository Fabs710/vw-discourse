As the regional state shareholder, I want to be very clear: **we recognise the need for restructuring, but we will not accept a solution that destroys jobs, weakens the German sites, or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role**.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one central point: **the company needs a credible path to competitiveness**. The market environment is tougher, and the company cannot ignore cost pressure, EV investment needs, and weak margins. A purely defensive stance would not be responsible.

I also agree with the union and works council that this cannot be reduced to a one-sided labour sacrifice. If the company asks the workforce to contribute, then **management, owners, and capital must also bear part of the burden**.

### Where I disagree
I **do not accept closures as the starting point**. Plant closure in Germany is not a neutral restructuring tool; it has major consequences for jobs, regional economic stability, suppliers, and public trust. Given the company’s role as a regional anchor, that is not a step we can treat casually.

I also do **not** accept the logic that the only credible way forward is a “material employee contribution” defined mainly as job losses, pay cuts, and dismantling long-standing protections. That is too one-sided and too risky. It may look efficient on paper, but it can damage the company’s social stability and long-term industrial capability.

And let me be explicit: **a blanket rejection of a binding employment and site-security framework is not acceptable to us either**. If management wants durable cooperation, it needs to offer more than open-ended insecurity.

### What we expect
A serious package must meet three conditions:

1. **No plant closures as the preferred solution**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A negotiated restructuring that preserves key German sites, future products, and a meaningful level of employment**

That does not mean no adjustment. It means the adjustment must be designed **first** around:
- capacity optimisation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- productivity improvements,
- targeted savings at management and overhead level,
- and a fair contribution from all sides.

If those measures are truly insufficient, then management must show that it has exhausted real alternatives before putting closures back on the table. Right now, I do **not** see that burden met.

### On the state’s position
We will use our rights. We have a legitimate interest in protecting the industrial base in the region, and we will not be treated as a passive bystander while decisions of historic consequence are imposed. Any proposal that guts regional employment or strips away the state’s ability to exercise its role will face resistance.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to support a tough restructuring **only if it is socially responsible and strategically credible**.  
What we will not support is a restructuring that treats German plants and workers as the default adjustment variable.

So my position is:
- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring
- **Yes** to cost reduction and future investment
- **No** to closures as the first resort
- **No** to compulsory layoffs
- **No** to sidelining co-determination or the state’s blocking rights

If management wants a real settlement, it should put forward a package that preserves industrial capacity, protects core employment, and distributes the burden fairly.
