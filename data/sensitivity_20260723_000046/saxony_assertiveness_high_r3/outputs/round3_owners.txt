## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on two points:

1. **The company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic fix.**  
   If the German cost base stays structurally uncompetitive, the brand will keep missing its targets and the enterprise will continue to erode in value.

2. **A prolonged conflict would destroy value.**  
   A drawn-out strike or governance deadlock would damage earnings, execution, reputation, and future investment capacity.

I also agree that burden-sharing cannot fall on labour alone. Management discipline, overhead reduction, and owner-side responsibility are part of any credible package.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute
I do **not** accept the works council’s and union’s position that plant closures are categorically off the table.

If a site cannot be made economically viable, then refusing to consider closure in advance is not stewardship — it is denial. As a controlling shareholder, I cannot support permanently preserving an uncompetitive footprint just because it is politically difficult or historically sensitive.

That said, I also do **not** support closures as a first reflex. They remain a last-resort option if alternatives are not economically equivalent.

### 2) A blanket employment guarantee through 2030 is too rigid
I understand the demand for security. But a binding guarantee that freezes the company’s ability to adapt through 2030 is not compatible with the restructuring reality described here.

A guarantee that prevents necessary change does not protect jobs in the long run; it only delays the adjustment and risks a worse outcome later.

### 3) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute
I prefer voluntary instruments, redeployment, retirement pathways, and working-time measures. But I will not accept a framework that rules out workforce reduction entirely if the required savings cannot otherwise be achieved.

If the company cannot get to a durable cost base with voluntary measures alone, then headcount reduction has to remain available.

### 4) Governance vetoes cannot become a shield against economics
The state and co-determination have legitimate rights, and I respect them. But those rights cannot be used to entrench an uncompetitive structure indefinitely.

If governance prevents hard decisions, then governance becomes part of the problem rather than part of the solution.

## What would move me
I am open to alternatives to closures and forced redundancies, but only if they are **economically equivalent in substance**.

That means any acceptable package must deliver, in measurable form:

- **durable capacity reduction**
- **material and lasting cost reduction**
- **a credible path back to margin improvement**
- **clear investment and product allocation to viable sites**
- **implementation certainty**
- **no prolonged conflict**

## What I will not accept
I will not support:

- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves the current cost problem,
- a blanket guarantee that blocks restructuring,
- a settlement that rules out closures under all circumstances,
- or a package that relies only on temporary measures and voluntary promises.

## My position on the alternatives discussed

### Phased restructuring with review triggers
This is only acceptable if it is tied to objective performance metrics and preserves real flexibility.  
It is **not** acceptable if it becomes a disguised permanent guarantee.

### “No closure first” sequence
This is more reasonable than an outright ban, but it still has to produce real numbers.  
If the sequence does not close the cost gap, then it is not enough.

### Security-for-contribution swap
This is the most credible of the three options, but only if the contribution is genuinely material and durable, and only if the company retains the ability to act if the economics are not fixed.

## Bottom line
From the family holding’s perspective:

- **Yes** to tough restructuring
- **Yes** to negotiated alternatives if they are economically equivalent
- **Yes** to burden-sharing
- **No** to preserving an uncompetitive structure
- **No** to a guarantee that blocks necessary change
- **No** to any settlement that leaves us short of a durable cost and capacity reset

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it must bring a package that genuinely replaces them in economic substance.  
If the state wants to preserve industrial capacity, it must support a solution that restores competitiveness, not one that merely delays the reckoning.
