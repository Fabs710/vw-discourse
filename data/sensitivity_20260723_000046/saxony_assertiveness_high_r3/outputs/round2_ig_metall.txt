We are not moving on the core points.

I agree with the Works Council, the state, and even parts of what management says: the company needs a credible industrial future, it needs lower costs, and it needs investment in EVs and future products. If there is no viable restructuring, there is no secure future for the workforce either. On that, we are aligned.

But I must be equally clear: **the answer cannot be plant closures and compulsory redundancies dressed up as “realism.”** That is not a fair restructuring; it is a transfer of the crisis onto employees. And it is not acceptable.

### Where I agree with other parties
- **With management:** the status quo is not sustainable. We need durable savings, not just temporary patches.
- **With the family and investors:** a cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base untouched would fail.
- **With the state and works council:** the solution must preserve industrial capability in Germany and avoid unnecessary social destruction.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Plant closures are not the benchmark of credibility
Management keeps saying closures are “on the table.” Fine — but having something on the table is not the same as proving it is necessary. So far, I do not see a credible case that closures are the only route to the needed savings. Before even discussing closures, management must show:
- what cost gap must be closed,
- what savings are expected from each measure,
- and why alternative measures cannot close that gap.

Until then, closure talk is pressure, not proof.

#### 2) Forced redundancies remain off-limits
We will not accept compulsory layoffs. That is a hard line. If workforce reduction is needed, then it must come through:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement pathways,
- internal transfers,
- working-time adjustments,
- and negotiated capacity reduction.

If management wants industrial peace, it should stop treating forced redundancies as a normal instrument.

#### 3) A binding employment and site framework is not optional
The works council is right that trust is impossible without security. We need a **binding settlement** with:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- and meaningful site and employment protection.

I do not accept that this is “incompatible” with restructuring. What is incompatible with restructuring is a workforce that sees only insecurity while being asked to sacrifice. That will not produce cooperation; it will produce resistance.

### On burden-sharing
Here we are open, but only under fair terms.

Yes, the workforce can contribute. We have already signaled openness to:
- labour-cost moderation,
- capacity reduction,
- flexible working-time arrangements,
- and voluntary instruments.

But that contribution must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**. It cannot be:
- workers give up wages,
- workers lose jobs,
- workers lose protections,
- while management, owners, and capital merely issue statements.

If employees contribute, then we expect:
- management restraint and overhead cuts,
- owner-side support,
- investment commitments,
- and a credible plan to protect the sites.

A contribution that only flows downward to the workforce is not a negotiated settlement; it is an imposition.

### On what would be a serious path forward
A credible package would need to include:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Binding site and employment security**
4. **Capacity reduction through flexible instruments and voluntary measures**
5. **A negotiated employee contribution that is fair and time-limited**
6. **Management and shareholder contributions**
7. **Investment and product allocation for the German sites**
8. **A durable collective-bargaining framework, not permanent erosion of wage standards**

That is the kind of package we could defend to members.

### On escalation
If management continues to insist that the only serious option is closures plus layoffs, then we are not talking about a settlement — we are talking about confrontation. The peace obligation has ended. We are prepared to escalate industrial action strategically, because our members will not pay for a restructuring they did not create and do not control.

### Bottom line
We want a durable agreement. We are ready to contribute. But our contribution has limits:
- no forced redundancies,
- no closures,
- no one-sided sacrifice.

If management wants the workforce on board, it needs to come back with a real shared-sacrifice plan that protects jobs and sites, not a demand that employees absorb the pain while others decide.
