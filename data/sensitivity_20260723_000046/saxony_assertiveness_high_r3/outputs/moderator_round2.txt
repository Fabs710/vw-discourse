### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between two incompatible requirements:

- **Employee-side / state-side demand:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding employment/site guarantees through 2030.
- **Owner/investor-side demand:** a restructuring that delivers durable, measurable capacity and cost reduction, with closures and job reductions remaining available if needed.

In short, one side is asking for **security first**, while the other is asking for **economic flexibility first**.  
The current plateau comes from each side treating its preferred constraint as non-negotiable.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to explore
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tested in the next round:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a review trigger
- Immediate agreement on **no compulsory redundancies for a defined initial period**.
- Closure decisions deferred, not canceled, and tied to **objective performance triggers**.
- A joint review after 12–18 months using agreed metrics: cost base, utilization, EV ramp-up, and margin progress.

**Why it may help:**  
Gives workers and the state time-bound security, while preserving management/owner flexibility if targets are missed.

#### 2) “No closure first” package with binding alternatives
- Commitment to exhaust a defined sequence before closure:
  1. voluntary departures,
  2. retirements,
  3. internal redeployment,
  4. working-time reduction,
  5. temporary capacity cuts.
- If these measures do not achieve the agreed savings threshold, closure remains a last-resort option.

**Why it may help:**  
Addresses the employee-side demand that closures not be the first instrument, while acknowledging the investor/management requirement for an economically sufficient fallback.

#### 3) Security-for-contribution swap
- Employees receive **site and employment protection** for a fixed period.
- In return, labor accepts a **time-limited contribution** through working-time flexibility, labor-cost moderation, and redeployment.
- Management, owners, and possibly the state align on **investment commitments, overhead reductions, and governance transparency**.

**Why it may help:**  
Creates a balanced exchange: the workforce does not shoulder the burden alone, and owners get verifiable cost relief.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
What specific savings gap must be closed, and which parts of that gap can be closed **without** closures or forced redundancies?

#### To the Works Council
Under what exact conditions would you accept a time-limited restructuring framework that preserves jobs now but includes an objective review of plant viability later?

#### To the Industrial Union
What level of temporary labour contribution would you consider acceptable if it were matched by binding management and owner contributions?

#### To the Regional State Government
Would the state support a phased agreement if it guaranteed no closures and no forced redundancies for a defined period, but left later action to measurable performance triggers?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
What minimum operational outcomes would make you accept a deferred closure decision rather than an immediate one?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What quantitative threshold for durable cost and capacity reduction would you require before supporting a compromise that avoids immediate closures?
