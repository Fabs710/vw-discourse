### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company needs **restructuring** and a **credible long-term industrial future**.
- There is broad agreement that the package must include **investment in EV/future technologies**, **product allocation for viable sites**, and **a durable improvement in competitiveness**.
- All parties reject a **prolonged conflict** and say they are open to negotiation.
- There is some shared openness to **alternative measures** such as capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, voluntary instruments, overhead reduction, and management/shareholder contributions.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**: Management and investors keep closures on the table; Works Council, union, and state reject closures as the starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Employee side and state reject them; management and investors imply job losses may be part of any credible solution.
- **Employment/site guarantee**: Employee side and state want a binding guarantee; management and investors say a blanket guarantee through 2030 is not compatible with restructuring needs.
- **Scale and nature of employee contribution**: Management and investors want a material, durable labour-side contribution; employee side insists burden-sharing must also include management, owners, and capital, and must not come mainly as job losses or permanent erosion of protections.
- **Governance and veto role**: State and employee representatives insist their co-determination and blocking rights must remain effective; management and investors want flexibility to implement structural change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No shared definition yet of what counts as an **“economically equivalent” alternative to closures**.
- No agreed balance between **capacity reduction**, **job security**, and **cost savings**.
- No concrete formula yet for how the burden is split between **labour, management, and shareholders**.
- No common understanding of the minimum level of **site protection** and which sites/products are considered viable.
- No implementation framework yet that would make a deal both **socially acceptable** and **board-/market-credible**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Ask management to specify a **minimum credible package** in measurable terms: target savings, capacity reduction, site impacts, and timeline.
- Ask employee and state पक्ष to specify **which alternative measures** they would accept in place of closures and under what conditions.
- Focus the next round on a **structured trade-off matrix**:
  - cost reduction
  - employment impact
  - site protection
  - investment commitments
  - governance approval probability
