Let me be equally direct.

I acknowledge two points from both the Works Council and the union:

- We do need a **credible industrial future for the German sites**
- We do need **investment in EVs, core technologies, and future products**

On that, we are not in disagreement.

Where I strongly disagree is on the idea that this can be achieved with **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no hard structural adjustment**. That is not a plan; it is a refusal to confront the numbers.

The reality is this:

- Our **German factory costs are far above target**
- The **European market is shrinking**
- Competitive pressure, including from lower-cost and Chinese manufacturers, is intensifying
- The brand is **missing its return-on-sales target**
- We cannot fund future technology if we keep carrying an **uncompetitive cost base**

So I will say plainly: **a cosmetic agreement is not acceptable**.  
A deal that simply stretches pain over time, preserves excess capacity, and leaves the structural cost problem unresolved will fail the company and, in the end, fail the workforce too.

### On plant closures
I have made clear that **closures are on the table** because they are part of a credible restructuring response. If alternative measures can achieve the same durable effect on cost and capacity, I am open to them. But those alternatives must be real, not rhetorical.

That means:

- measurable capacity reduction
- lasting labour-cost contribution
- reduction of fixed overheads
- binding changes that actually move the cost base

If you want to avoid closures, then present a package that delivers **equivalent, durable savings**. Not promises. Not temporary deferrals. Not voluntary measures that may or may not materialise.

### On job security
A blanket employment guarantee through 2030, as a precondition, is not compatible with the scale of restructuring we need. I will not sign up to a framework that freezes the current structure while expecting management to restore competitiveness by wishful thinking.

We can discuss social cushioning and orderly transition. We cannot accept a guarantee that blocks necessary structural change.

### On labour cost
The workforce side says management, owners, and capital must also contribute. Fine — but the largest lever we control here is the **manufacturing and labour cost base in Germany**. That is where the uncompetitiveness sits, and that is where the savings must be delivered.

So yes, I expect a **material employee contribution**.  
Not symbolic. Not temporary. Material and sustainable.

### On negotiation
I am prepared to negotiate a path that avoids unnecessary confrontation if it delivers the following:

1. **Substantial and durable cost reduction**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **A real labour-cost contribution**
4. **Clear allocation of future products and investment to viable sites**
5. **A settlement that restores the brand’s margin trajectory to 2030**

What I will not accept is a situation where management is asked to keep all options off the table while the company continues to lose competitiveness.

### Bottom line
We are open to an alternative to closures — but only if it is structurally equivalent in impact.

If the employee side wants us to rule out closures categorically, then it must also accept a package that produces:
- lower capacity,
- lower labour costs,
- fewer jobs overall,
- and a more competitive production footprint.

That is the condition for a serious settlement.
