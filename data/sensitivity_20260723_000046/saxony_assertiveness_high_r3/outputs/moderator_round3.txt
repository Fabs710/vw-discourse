### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **labor/state demands for binding protection** and **management/owners/investors’ insistence on preserved flexibility**.

More specifically, the dispute sits on four linked questions:

1. **Are German plant closures still an acceptable fallback option?**  
   - Employee side and state: no.  
   - Management/owners/investors: yes, if economics require it.

2. **Are compulsory redundancies categorically excluded?**  
   - Employee side and state: yes, excluded.  
   - Management/owners/investors: no, they must remain possible if savings are otherwise insufficient.

3. **Can there be binding job/site guarantees through 2030?**  
   - Employee side and state: yes, as a trust-building condition.  
   - Management/owners/investors: no, too rigid to permit restructuring.

4. **What counts as a credible alternative to closures and layoffs?**  
   - Employee side says management has not yet quantified a full alternatives package.  
   - Management says alternatives are acceptable only if economically equivalent.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

#### 1) Conditional no-closure moratorium with objective review gates
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a **time-limited moratorium on German plant closures** for a defined initial period, paired with **pre-agreed objective triggers** and a **joint review mechanism**.

**How it bridges conflict:**
- Gives works council, union, and state real short-term security.
- Preserves management’s ability to revisit the issue if agreed metrics are not met.
- Reduces the sense of an open-ended closure threat.

**Design features:**
- No closure decisions during the moratorium.
- Jointly agreed performance metrics.
- Independent verification of cost-gap progress.
- A structured second-stage review only if predefined targets fail.

---

#### 2) No compulsory redundancies first, with a quantified fallback ladder
**Proposal:**  
Agree that **compulsory redundancies are excluded as a first instrument**, but create a **sequenced fallback ladder** of measures with quantified targets.

**How it bridges conflict:**
- Aligns with labor’s red line in the first instance.
- Allows management to insist on a documented path if voluntary tools do not reach the required savings.
- Forces the parties to quantify the effect of each step.

**Possible sequence:**
1. Management/overhead cuts  
2. Owner-side contributions  
3. Investment reprioritization  
4. Voluntary departures / retirement pathways  
5. Working-time and capacity adjustments  
6. Only then: review of further options under strict governance oversight

---

#### 3) Binding security package in exchange for measurable labor and management contributions
**Proposal:**  
Create a **balanced security-for-contribution package** with reciprocal obligations from labor, management, and owners.

**How it bridges conflict:**
- Addresses labor’s demand that employees not carry the burden alone.
- Gives management a cost-reduction path without immediate closures.
- Makes owner participation explicit and measurable.

**Possible elements:**
- Time-limited employment and site guarantees for viable plants
- Temporary labor-side moderation measures
- Binding management overhead reductions
- Capital-side financial support or reinvestment commitments
- Investment and product allocation commitments for German sites
- A joint implementation body with co-determination and reporting duties

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
**Question:**  
If a joint package delivered binding protection for all viable sites for a defined period, plus measurable management and owner contributions, what specific review mechanism would you accept that would preserve your “no closure, no compulsory redundancies” red lines while still allowing the company to verify whether the cost gap is being closed?

#### Industrial Union
**Question:**  
Which temporary labor-side measures would you consider negotiable, and under what exact conditions would you rule out any later transition to compulsory redundancies if the agreed savings targets are not reached?

#### Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
What is the minimum quantified savings package, by measure and timing, that would let you commit to a moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies for the initial restructuring phase?

#### Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What specific state-linked governance conditions or support instruments would you attach to a temporary security package so that it remains credible for labor while still allowing a later reassessment if targets are missed?

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What measurable owner-side contribution are you prepared to make now in exchange for a time-limited closure pause, and what objective trigger would you require before any future closure option could be reconsidered?

#### Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What exact combination of capacity reduction, cost improvement, and implementation certainty would you require to regard a non-closure restructuring as economically equivalent to closure-led restructuring?
