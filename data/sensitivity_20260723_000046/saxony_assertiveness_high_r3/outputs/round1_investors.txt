From the standpoint of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the only acceptable outcome is one that **restores competitive margins on a durable basis** and does so with enough certainty to be credible to the market.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and partly with the others on one central point: **the status quo is not investable**.

- German production costs are too high.
- Capacity appears misaligned with market reality.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- Future EV and technology investment depends on fixing the cost base.

I also agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value. A drawn-out strike or years of negotiation without decisive action would be bad for earnings, bad for the share price, and bad for capital allocation.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the idea that the outcome must be framed as either:
- **full plant closures and forced layoffs**, or
- **a soft, cosmetic compromise that preserves the same cost structure**.

Both extremes are unattractive. But I am much more worried about the second one. A deal that avoids hard restructuring while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive would be fundamentally unacceptable from an investor perspective.

So let me be explicit:
- **No cosmetic settlement**
- **No arrangement that merely delays the pain**
- **No outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired**

### On plant closures
I do not treat closures as desirable in themselves. But I also will not support a posture that rules them out in advance if they are necessary to achieve credible, durable savings.

What matters is not whether a closure is politically difficult. What matters is whether the final package delivers:
- meaningful and permanent capacity reduction,
- lower fixed costs,
- a more competitive footprint,
- and a believable path to restored profitability.

If alternative measures can achieve the same effect, fine. But “alternative” must mean economically equivalent, not simply more palatable to the workforce or the state.

### On labour contribution
The union and works council are right that labour should not carry the burden alone. I agree that management, owners, and the company’s overhead structure must also contribute.

But from our side, a labour contribution must be **material and durable**. Temporary gestures, voluntary-only measures, or time-limited concessions are not enough if the structural cost problem remains. We need changes that show up in the P&L and stay there.

### On job security
A binding employment guarantee through 2030 sounds reassuring, but I would be cautious if it prevents necessary restructuring. Markets change, and the company needs flexibility. I would not support a guarantee that effectively locks in an uncompetitive structure.

That said, I am also not asking for open-ended insecurity. I would prefer a **credible restructuring package with measurable milestones**, rather than a vague promise of protection with no economic substance.

### On governance and deal certainty
The governance reality matters. Because of the blocking positions held by co-determination, the family, and the state, **deal certainty is essential**.

A package that cannot realistically pass the supervisory board or survive industrial action is not credible. Investors need to know that whatever is agreed will actually be implemented. That means all parties need to move beyond symbolic positions and produce an executable deal.

### Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:

- **Support a tough restructuring**
- **Support durable cost and capacity reduction**
- **Support a negotiated solution if it is economically real**
- **Do not support a cosmetic settlement**
- **Do not support an outcome that leaves the cost base uncompetitive**
- **Do not support prolonged conflict that destroys value**

If management can bring forward a credible package that materially improves margins and competitiveness, we are prepared to back it. If not, we will remain opposed to any arrangement that merely preserves today’s problems under a different label.
