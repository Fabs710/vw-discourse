We have heard enough to identify the real fault line.

## First: where I agree
I agree with all parties on one essential point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness reset, not a cosmetic compromise**.  
If the cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, the brand will keep missing its margin target, future investment capacity will shrink, and the long-term value of the enterprise will erode. That would be unacceptable for us as long-term owners.

I also agree that **prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value**. A drawn-out strike or open-ended confrontation would damage earnings, reputation, execution, and the company’s credibility in the market.

And I agree that **burden-sharing must not fall on labour alone**. Management discipline, overhead reduction, and owner-side responsibility are part of any credible solution.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” as an absolute position is not credible
The works council and union keep presenting “no plant closures” as a non-negotiable baseline. I do not accept that.

If a site cannot be made economically viable, then excluding closure in advance is not stewardship; it is denial. We cannot protect every structure simply because it is historic or politically difficult. That would risk permanently impairing the company’s value.

That said, I do **not** support closure as a first reflex. I support it only if it is part of a package that clearly and durably restores competitiveness and if alternatives are not economically equivalent.

### 2) A blanket employment guarantee through 2030 is too rigid
I understand why the employee side and the state want security. But a binding guarantee that freezes the company’s ability to restructure through 2030 is not compatible with the economic reality described here.

A guarantee that prevents necessary change would not protect jobs in the long run; it would simply delay the adjustment and make the eventual outcome worse.

### 3) Forced redundancies are not ideal — but job reduction cannot be ruled out
I do not want unnecessary social damage. But I also cannot accept the claim that compulsory redundancies are categorically off-limits while expecting the company to achieve a major structural reset.

If voluntary instruments, internal redeployment, working-time flexibility, and capacity measures are sufficient, I would prefer them. But if they are not sufficient, then the company must retain the ability to reduce headcount in a way that is economically real and durable.

### 4) Governance vetoes cannot become a shield against economics
The state and co-determination have legitimate rights. We respect that. But those rights cannot be used to entrench an uncompetitive footprint indefinitely. If the company cannot make hard decisions, then governance becomes part of the problem rather than part of the solution.

## What we need to see
We would support a settlement if it produces all of the following in measurable form:

1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Material improvement in the labor and fixed-cost base**
3. **Clear allocation of future products and investment to viable sites**
4. **A credible margin path back to competitiveness**
5. **No prolonged conflict that destroys value**
6. **A package that can actually pass the board and be implemented**

## What is not acceptable
Let me be explicit about what we will not support:

- a cosmetic deal that preserves the current cost problem
- a blanket guarantee that blocks restructuring
- a settlement that rules out closures under all circumstances
- temporary measures that do not change the economics
- a prolonged dispute that damages the company’s long-term value

## Our position
So our position is not “closures at any cost.”  
It is: **structural change must be real, durable, and economically sufficient**.

If that can be achieved without closures and without forced redundancies, we are open.  
If it cannot, then those options cannot be removed from the table simply because they are politically uncomfortable.

### Bottom line
We will support a **credible restructuring plan** that:
- restores margins,
- reduces excess capacity,
- preserves viable German operations,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and avoids value-destructive instability.

We will not support a deal that preserves every plant, every guarantee, and every legacy arrangement while hoping competitiveness returns on its own.
