We align with the Works Council on the essentials.

Let me be equally clear: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points**. They would shift the whole adjustment burden onto the workforce, destroy trust, and undermine the collective bargaining framework. That is not a durable solution.

We recognise the economic reality: the company needs lower costs, more flexibility, and a credible path back to competitiveness. But that must be achieved through a **fair burden-sharing package**, not through one-sided cuts. If management wants our cooperation, then it needs to put real alternatives on the table.

What we can discuss:

- **Capacity reductions instead of closures**
- **Working-time measures and flexible instruments**
- **Voluntary departures only, not forced layoffs**
- **Temporary or negotiated wage contributions**, but only if management, owners, and capital also contribute
- **Investment and product commitments for the German sites**
- **A binding employment and site-security framework**

What we cannot accept:

- **Forced redundancies**
- **Closure of German plants**
- **Erosion of the collective wage framework as a permanent principle**
- **Open-ended insecurity for employees while management calls for sacrifice**

If the company is serious about restructuring, then it must show that it is willing to accept its share of the pain: management restraint, owner contributions, and a plan that protects jobs and keeps industrial capacity in Germany. We are prepared to negotiate hard and responsibly. We are also prepared to escalate if the message from management remains: workers must pay, everyone else decides.
