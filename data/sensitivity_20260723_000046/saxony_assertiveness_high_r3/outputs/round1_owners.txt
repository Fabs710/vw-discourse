We, as the controlling family holding, take the situation very seriously. Our first duty is to protect the long-term value and continuity of the enterprise, not to preserve any single instrument for its own sake.

### Where we agree
I agree with management on the core diagnosis:  
the company cannot continue with a structurally uncompetitive cost base, persistent margin pressure, and excess capacity in a shrinking market. If we do not restore competitiveness now, we risk much worse outcomes later: ongoing erosion of earnings, weakened investment capacity, and a slower decline that hurts everyone.

I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state on one important point: **a durable settlement must create industrial stability**. A conflict that drags on for months would damage value, reputation, and the company’s ability to execute.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the position that restructuring must automatically mean plant closures and forced redundancies as the default outcome. That would be too blunt, too destabilising, and potentially value-destructive if better alternatives exist.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept the notion that a binding guarantee through 2030, with no meaningful structural adjustment, is realistic under current conditions. A promise that freezes the company’s options while the market deteriorates would not protect jobs in the end; it would merely postpone the bill.

So I reject both extremes:
- **not** a cosmetic restructuring that preserves the full cost problem
- **not** a closure-led shock that sacrifices stability unnecessarily

### What would move us
We would support a package if it credibly delivers all of the following:

1. **Durable improvement in margins and cost base**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **A meaningful employee contribution**
4. **Protection of viable German sites and core industrial capabilities**
5. **A settlement that avoids prolonged industrial conflict**

That means we are open to alternatives to closures, but only if they are economically equivalent in substance, not just politically easier to sell.

### On employee protections
The works council and union are right that this cannot be a one-sided burden on labour. If employees are asked to contribute, then management discipline, overhead reduction, and serious commitments from the shareholder side must also be part of the solution.

But I need to be equally clear: **a contribution from labour has to be material and durable**. Temporary measures alone will not solve a structural problem. If there is to be no closure, then the savings must come from somewhere else in a way that lasts.

### On the state’s position
We respect the state’s role and its legitimate interest in preserving industrial strength in the region. But that role does not mean the company can be insulated from hard economic reality. The special governance structure cannot become a shield against necessary adjustment.

### Bottom line
Our position is:

- **Yes** to a tough restructuring
- **Yes** to serious cost reduction and capacity adjustment
- **Yes** to protecting the long-term value of German operations
- **No** to pretending the status quo is sustainable
- **No** to a settlement that blocks all structural change
- **No** to a prolonged conflict that destroys value

If management can bring a package that achieves real savings without permanent damage to the enterprise, we are prepared to support it. But if the discussion remains stuck between “no change” and “closures only,” then we will not support either path.
