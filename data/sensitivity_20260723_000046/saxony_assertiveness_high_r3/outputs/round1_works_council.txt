As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs**
- **No dismantling of co-determination and no breaking of the historic job-security framework**

We recognise that the company faces serious competitive pressure and that restructuring may be necessary. But we will not accept a solution that shifts the burden onto the workforce by destroying jobs, closing sites, or turning a structural management problem into a social one.

What we will argue for instead is:

1. **A binding site and employment guarantee through 2030**
2. **Alternative savings measures that protect jobs**, such as:
   - capacity adjustments
   - shorter working time / flex measures
   - voluntary instruments only
   - negotiated contribution from management and capital, not one-sided cuts to labour
3. **A genuine industrial strategy for the German sites**
   - clear future product allocation
   - investment commitments for EV and core technologies
   - real participation of employee representatives in shaping the restructuring

If management wants cooperation, it must come with a credible plan that preserves the future of the German plants and the workforce. If it comes instead with closures, forced layoffs, or unilateral fait accompli decisions, then we will resist with all available means, including industrial action and use of our governance rights.

We are ready to negotiate a solution that secures the company’s future — but not one built on sacrificing workers and shutting plants.
