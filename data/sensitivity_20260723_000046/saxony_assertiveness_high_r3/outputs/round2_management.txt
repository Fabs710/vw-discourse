Let me respond directly.

I agree with everyone on one point: **the company needs a durable industrial future in Germany, and it needs investment in future products, EV technology, and core capabilities**. There is no disagreement there.

But I do **not** agree with the central premise being repeated by the employee side and, to a lesser extent, the state: that we can deliver that future while ruling out closures, ruling out compulsory redundancies, and insisting on a binding employment guarantee through 2030.

That combination is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand to preserve the current structure while the market moves against us.

## On what is undeniable
The facts are not comfortable, but they are real:

- our **German factory costs are far above target**
- the **European market is shrinking**
- competition is intensifying, including from **lower-cost and Chinese manufacturers**
- the brand is **missing its return-on-sales target**
- we need free cash flow to fund **2025–2030 product and technology investment**

If we do not structurally reduce the cost base and capacity, we will not restore competitiveness. And if we do not restore competitiveness, the jobs we are all trying to protect will erode anyway — only more slowly and more painfully.

## To the Works Council
You say closures are not justified and that management has not proven they are necessary. I disagree.

We have been explicit about the scale of the gap. The burden of proof is not on management to pretend the status quo is workable; it is on all of us to close a structural cost gap in a shrinking market. If a site cannot be made competitive, then refusing to consider closure is not protecting the workforce — it is postponing a worse outcome.

You also insist on **no compulsory redundancies** and a **binding guarantee through 2030**. I am not prepared to sign away management’s ability to restructure in that way. A blanket guarantee would freeze the very changes needed to restore the business. That is incompatible with the mandate we have to secure the company’s future.

You ask why we have not exhausted all alternatives. The answer is simple: we are still here discussing them. But alternatives must be real. They must deliver:
- measurable capacity reduction
- a durable labour-cost contribution
- lower fixed costs
- a credible margin path

Not just internal redeployment slogans or temporary working-time tricks that preserve an uncompetitive footprint.

## To the Union
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. On that, we agree. A deal that merely delays the pain or leaves the cost base untouched would fail.

Where I part company with you is your insistence that **forced redundancies remain off-limits in all circumstances**. I understand why you say it. I do not accept it as a hard constraint on the company’s future. If we cannot achieve sufficient reduction through voluntary measures, working-time flexibility, capacity changes, and negotiated contribution, then job reductions have to remain on the table.

That is not a threat for its own sake. It is the reality of restructuring.

You also say the burden must be shared by management, owners, and capital. Fine — I have not denied that. But the largest structural lever here is still the cost base in production and labour. That is where the savings must come from if they are to be durable.

## To the State Government
I respect the state’s role and its concern for the regional industrial base. But I need to be equally direct: the special governance structure cannot become a shield against hard decisions.

You say closures should not be the starting point. I can accept that closures should not be the first reflex. But they absolutely must remain an available option if the alternatives do not close the gap in a way that is economically equivalent.

You also reject a labour contribution defined mainly as pay cuts and job losses. I am not asking for symbolism. I am asking for a **material, durable contribution** that actually changes the economics. If that can be achieved through another combination of measures, then present it. If not, then we cannot pretend the answer is simply to preserve every site and every guarantee.

## To the Family Holding and Investors
I note your support for structural change, and I agree with your basic point: **a cosmetic settlement is unacceptable**.

But I want to be very clear: market credibility will not come from vague declarations that “something has to happen.” It will come from a package that actually reduces capacity, cost, and complexity. If the final arrangement leaves us with the same fixed-cost burden, then it will not restore margins and it will not satisfy the capital markets for long.

## What I am prepared to accept
I remain open to an alternative to closures — but only if it is **structurally equivalent** in effect.

That means a package must include, at minimum:

1. **Substantial and durable capacity reduction**
2. **A meaningful labour-cost contribution**
3. **Lower fixed overheads**
4. **A credible path to improved margins through 2030**
5. **Clear investment and product allocation to the viable German sites**
6. **A settlement that is implementable and not easily undone**

If you can bring me that, then we can discuss how it is achieved.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:

- a blanket employment guarantee through 2030
- a deal that rules out closures under all circumstances
- a package that relies only on voluntary measures
- a cosmetic compromise that protects optics but not competitiveness
- a structure that keeps German production overcapacity in place

## Bottom line
I hear the concern about social impact. I do not dismiss it. But my responsibility is to the company’s competitiveness and its ability to invest for the future.

So let me state the position plainly:

- **Yes** to tough restructuring
- **Yes** to negotiated alternatives if they are economically equivalent
- **Yes** to burden-sharing
- **No** to preserving an uncompetitive structure
- **No** to a guarantee that blocks necessary change
- **No** to any settlement that leaves us short of a durable cost and capacity reset

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that genuinely replaces them in economic substance. Until then, closures and deeper workforce adjustment remain on the table.
