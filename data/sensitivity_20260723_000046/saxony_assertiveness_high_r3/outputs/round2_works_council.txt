Management is still trying to turn a strategic failure into a workforce problem. We reject that.

I hear the points from the family, the state, and investors about competitiveness, and we do not deny the pressure. But the line that **closures and forced job cuts are simply “necessary”** has not been justified in this room. It is an assertion, not a solution. And it ignores the leverage and responsibility of those who actually designed the company’s strategic path.

### Where we agree
- Yes, the company needs a credible industrial future.
- Yes, future products, EV investment, and a sustainable margin path matter.
- Yes, a prolonged conflict would damage the company.

That is exactly why we insist on a solution that **keeps every German plant open**, protects jobs, and gives workers planning security.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable “credible” option
Management and investors keep saying closures are on the table, but they have not shown that they are the only way to achieve savings. We will not accept plant closures as a first resort or a bargaining chip. Once you close a site, you destroy know-how, livelihoods, supplier networks, and trust. That damage is irreversible.

#### 2) No compulsory redundancies
We are not negotiating forced layoffs. Period. If management wants workforce reduction, then the only acceptable routes are:
- retirement pathways,
- voluntary departures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time and capacity measures.

Anything else is a direct attack on the workforce and on the historic job-security framework.

#### 3) No blanket rejection of site and employment guarantees
Management says a guarantee through 2030 is “not compatible” with restructuring. That is exactly the wrong framing. A binding guarantee is what creates the trust necessary for workers to accept change. Without it, all “plans” are just paper promises while the company keeps the option to cut and run.

### On the so-called “material employee contribution”
We will not accept the idea that labour must absorb the main burden through lower pay, fewer jobs, and weaker protection while management and capital call that “shared sacrifice.”

If you want a contribution from the workforce, then it must be matched by:
- management restraint,
- overhead cuts,
- capital-side contributions,
- and binding investment commitments.

And let me be absolutely clear: **temporary measures are not enough if they are used to disguise a permanent assault on jobs and sites**.

### What a serious alternative looks like
If management wants our cooperation, then it must put forward a package with these elements:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding employment and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **Capacity adjustments through working-time flexibility and internal reallocation**
5. **Voluntary instruments only**
6. **Product and investment commitments for all viable sites**
7. **A real co-determination role in shaping implementation**

Anything short of that is not a negotiated restructuring. It is coercion dressed up as realism.

### On governance
The employee side is not a passive recipient of management decisions. Co-determination means exactly what it says. With half the supervisory-board seats and the legal blocking position over closures and relocations, we can and will use our rights. If the company tries to force through a closure-led plan, it will not get a smooth path.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate a tough restructuring. We are **not** prepared to negotiate away German plants, forced redundancies, or the job-security framework.

So my question to management is simple:
If closures and compulsory layoffs are supposedly unavoidable, why have you not brought forward a credible package that proves every alternative has been exhausted?

Until that answer is on the table, our position remains unchanged.
