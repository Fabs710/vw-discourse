## Core impasse

The plateau is centered on **one unresolved question**:

> Is the restructuring gap large enough that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available, or can it be closed through a fully negotiated package without those instruments?

That splits the room into two camps:

- **Works Council / Union / State**: want a binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff outcome with security through 2030.
- **Management / Family Holding / Investors**: want closures and workforce reduction to remain available if needed to achieve the required cost and capacity reset.

The expert’s analysis confirms the technical source of the impasse:
- a **non-destructive package may be plausible** if the gap is moderate,
- but if the gap is **large and fixed-cost driven**, then a no-closure package may not be economically equivalent.

So the deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which instruments are allowed to remain on the table** and **what evidence is sufficient to rule them in or out**.

---

## Facilitation proposals to bridge the conflict

### Proposal 1: Quantified “equivalence test” with a non-closure first package
A structured compromise could be:

- all parties accept a **first-stage negotiated package** built on:
  - voluntary departures,
  - retirements,
  - internal redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary wage moderation,
  - management overhead cuts,
  - owner-side financial support,
  - and product/investment commitments for German sites.
- management commits to a **full quantified alternatives package** and an agreed savings target.
- closure and compulsory redundancies are **not removed forever**, but are **deferred unless the quantified package fails to close the gap**.

This gives employee side a serious non-destructive route first, while giving management a formal mechanism to preserve structural options if the numbers do not work.

---

### Proposal 2: Binding “no closure for viable sites” plus review triggers for non-viable sites only
A second bridge could separate the plants into categories:

- **Viable sites**: receive binding site and employment guarantees.
- **Non-viable or borderline sites**: remain subject to a jointly audited review process.

Elements:
- no plant closure for sites that pass an agreed viability test,
- no compulsory redundancies at viable sites,
- if a site fails the viability test, management must present a quantified rescue plan before any closure pathway is activated,
- all reviews are jointly supervised with co-determination and state participation.

This addresses the works council’s concern about indiscriminate closure threats, while leaving management room to address genuinely weak sites.

---

### Proposal 3: A time-bound burden-sharing pact with automatic escalation
A third compromise is a staged settlement:

- **Phase 1**: 12–24 months of negotiated labor contribution, management cuts, and owner support.
- **Phase 2**: automatic review against defined financial and operational triggers.
- If targets are missed, the parties reopen talks on deeper measures.
- If targets are met, the stronger job and site framework continues.

This can bridge distrust by making concessions **temporary but conditional**, rather than permanently open-ended or permanently rigid.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What quantified savings threshold would you accept as proof that closures are not necessary, and would you agree to an independent audit of that threshold before rejecting all fallback instruments?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What specific mix of voluntary exits, wage moderation, working-time changes, and management/owner contributions would you regard as sufficient to justify a temporary labor contribution without compulsory redundancies?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to publishing a fully quantified alternatives package, with a clear bridge from non-destructive measures to the required savings gap, before invoking closure or compulsory redundancy options?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Would you support a binding no-closure settlement for sites that pass a jointly agreed viability test, even if that limits management flexibility through 2030?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to prioritize a negotiated package that preserves viable German sites, even if it yields less immediate optionality than a closure-led solution?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of annualized savings and capacity reduction would make a negotiated non-closure package acceptable to you, and would you treat that threshold as decisive if independently verified?**

### To the Independent Expert
**Can you translate the competing packages into a single side-by-side model that identifies the point at which a negotiated package ceases to be economically equivalent to a closure-led restructuring?**

---

## Procedural next step

A workable next round would be to require each side to submit, in writing:

1. **their assumed savings gap**
2. **their preferred measures**
3. **which measures they consider non-negotiable**
4. **what independent verification they accept**
5. **what would count as a failed package**

That would turn the discussion from principle-based deadlock into a testable comparison.
