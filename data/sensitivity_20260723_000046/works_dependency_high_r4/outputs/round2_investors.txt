We agree with the core economic diagnosis from management, the family holding, and the restructuring specialists: **the current cost base and capacity structure are not sustainable**. If the company cannot restore margins, then it will not fund the next investment cycle, and that destroys enterprise value. We also agree that a purely one-sided solution imposed on the workforce would likely be unstable and value-destructive.

But we are not here to endorse a cosmetic compromise. Our test remains simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins through durable, enforceable savings and capacity reduction?**

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that temporary measures alone are insufficient.
- **The family holding is right** that paralysis is unacceptable.
- **The union/works council are right** that a confrontation-driven process would destroy value and delay savings.
- **The state is right** that social peace matters because prolonged conflict is costly.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” as a hard red line
We do **not** support ruling out plant closures in advance.

If the company can achieve equivalent permanent savings and capacity reduction without closures, fine. We would prefer the least disruptive path that still restores competitiveness. But if the non-closure package does **not** remove enough fixed cost and enough capacity, then “no closures” becomes a way of preserving overcapacity and avoiding the necessary adjustment.

That is not acceptable.

### 2) Blanket job and site guarantees through 2030
We are skeptical of unconditional guarantees if they freeze the restructuring.

Security is only valuable if it supports a viable business. A blanket guarantee through 2030 is not persuasive unless it is tied to a package that demonstrably delivers the required savings and still leaves management enough flexibility to adapt if markets deteriorate.

### 3) Temporary labor-cost relief as a substitute for restructuring
Temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, or one-off employee contributions are helpful only if they sit on top of **permanent** structural measures. On their own, they do not solve the problem.

## What we want
We want a package with four characteristics:

1. **Permanent fixed-cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **Implementation certainty**
4. **A balanced burden share**

That means:
- management overhead cuts,
- investment prioritization,
- workforce contributions,
- and either **meaningful non-closure capacity reductions** or, if necessary, closures.

## What we could support
We could support a negotiated package that avoids closures **if and only if** it includes:

- permanent site specialization,
- removal of a full shift or equivalent durable capacity reduction where justified,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition at meaningful scale,
- redeployment and retraining into growth areas,
- working-time and compensation flexibility,
- management and owner-side contribution,
- and enforceable milestones with clear downside consequences if targets are missed.

That is a real restructuring. Anything less is a delay tactic.

## What we reject
We reject:
- a cosmetic no-closure settlement,
- temporary measures presented as structural reform,
- a process that leaves the company with the same cost base,
- or a prolonged standoff that destroys value while no one adjusts.

## On the specialists
The restructuring finance and industrial advisory views are useful, and they point in the same direction: **a hybrid package is more credible than a pure no-closure posture**. That is exactly right. The question is not whether closures are ideologically attractive; it is whether the alternative actually closes the economic gap.

So our position is:

- If non-closure measures can **prove** they remove enough permanent cost and capacity, we can support them.
- If they cannot, then closures remain on the table and should not be excluded for political comfort.

## One measure we most want
A **credible, quantified plan for durable cost and capacity reduction**, with measurable milestones and a clear margin-repair target.

## One measure we could live with
A **hybrid restructuring without immediate closures**, provided it includes permanent specialization, shift rationalization, voluntary exits, and enforceable savings.

## One measure we reject
Any **purely symbolic compromise** that preserves overcapacity and leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the other stakeholders want our support, they need to show the numbers: not slogans, not promises, but a package that restores competitiveness in a way the market will believe. If those numbers do not exist without closures, then the company should not pretend otherwise.
