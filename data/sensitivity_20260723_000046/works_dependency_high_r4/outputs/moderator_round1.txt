### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a serious competitiveness and capacity problem that requires action.
- There is broad support for discussing **alternatives to immediate plant closures**, including:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - working-time solutions,
  - retraining,
  - redeployment,
  - temporary measures,
  - management overhead reductions,
  - and some form of employee contribution.
- There is also shared recognition that **a one-sided solution imposed on the workforce would be unstable and value-destructive**.
- Several sides agree that any restructuring should include **management and owner-side contributions** and not place the burden solely on employees.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and state reject closures as the first or preferred answer; management and investors keep closures on the table; the family holding will not rule them out either.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject them; management and investors see them as potentially necessary if overcapacity must be reduced.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees; management, family holding, and investors say blanket guarantees may be too rigid if they block restructuring.
- **Scope and depth of restructuring:**  
  Management and investors argue for deep structural cost and capacity reduction; employee side and state want alternatives that preserve industrial sites and jobs as far as possible.
- **Method versus result:**  
  Employee and state emphasize the method must be negotiated and socially balanced; management and investors emphasize the result must be economically sufficient, even if painful.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is required** to restore competitiveness, and by what mix of measures.
- **What level of employee contribution** would be acceptable, and what concrete counter-guarantees would make it acceptable.
- **Whether savings can be achieved without closures**, or whether equivalent structural measures are realistically available.
- **How to balance short-term labour peace with long-term economic viability**.
- **What commitments are possible on investment, product strategy, and future site roles** in exchange for workforce concessions.
- **What constitutes a “fair burden share”** across employees, management, owners, and possibly the state’s regional interests.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **specific package elements**:
- quantify the minimum savings and capacity reduction each side believes is necessary;
- identify which measures are acceptable, conditional, or excluded;
- specify what guarantees or protections would be required in return for employee concessions;
- and clarify what owner/management contributions would be on the table alongside workforce measures.

A useful next step would be for each party to state:
1. **the one measure it most wants**,  
2. **the one measure it could live with**, and  
3. **the one measure it would still reject**.
