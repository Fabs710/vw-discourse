Core impasse identified

The stalemate is now centered on a single unresolved issue:

**Whether permanent competitiveness can be achieved without plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and if so, under what binding conditions.**

All sides now accept that:
- the current structure is not sustainable,
- temporary relief alone is insufficient,
- burden-sharing is required,
- and any solution must be durable and enforceable.

The impasse is that stakeholders disagree on **which measures are genuinely sufficient**:
- **Management/investors/family holding** say non-closure measures may be helpful but are not yet proven to match closure-level economics.
- **Works council/union/state** say closures and forced redundancies are not justified until all site-preserving alternatives are fully quantified.
- **Everyone rejects “closure by attrition,”** but they differ on what minimum investment, product allocation, and capacity reduction are needed to keep sites viable.

Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Binding hybrid package with a hard review trigger
A possible bridge is a **time-bound hybrid agreement** that:
- avoids immediate closures,
- includes one-shift reduction or equivalent capacity cut at selected sites,
- requires voluntary exits, retraining, redeployment, wage/bonus moderation, and management overhead cuts,
- includes binding product and investment commitments for each German plant,
- and contains a **hard review trigger** if savings or utilization targets are missed.

This gives management a credible restructuring path while giving labour and the state protection against immediate closure decisions.

### 2) No-closure covenant with conditional fallback
Another possible bridge is a **no-closure covenant through 2030** paired with:
- quantified annual savings targets,
- site-by-site milestone monitoring,
- owner and management financial contributions,
- and a fallback clause that only activates if jointly verified targets are not met.

This preserves the employee-side red line while addressing the investor demand for enforceability.

### 3) Site-specific security plus network rationalization
A third compromise is to distinguish between:
- **sites guaranteed to remain open with named products/investment**, and
- **sites allowed to reduce shifts or specialize**, but only with a defined minimum workload and redeployment pathway.

This avoids a blanket across-the-board commitment while preventing hollowing-out by attrition.
