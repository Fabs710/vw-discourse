We are prepared to negotiate, but not to be coerced by a closure threat wrapped in “competitiveness” language.

## Where we agree
We agree with the works council, the state, and even parts of what management says: the company needs a durable solution, not symbolic gestures. We also agree that a one-sided answer would be unstable and likely trigger industrial conflict. And yes, management and owners must contribute — not just the workforce.

## Where we disagree
### 1) Plant closures
We do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point, and we do not accept them as the preferred solution if alternatives exist.

Management keeps saying closures may be necessary. That may be true in a purely financial model, but this is not a pure model. There are co-determination rights, a workforce with legitimate claims, and a broad stakeholder structure. If you come in with closures first, you should expect resistance, not consent.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies
We do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as a bargaining premise. If the company insists that job cuts are unavoidable, then the burden of proof is on management to show that every alternative has been exhausted. So far, I have not seen that proof; I have seen a preference for the fastest accounting fix.

### 3) “Blanket guarantees are too rigid”
To management, the family holding, and investors: yes, guarantees are only credible if they are tied to a real plan. But the reverse is also true: a restructuring plan is not credible if it simply removes all security from the workforce and expects us to pay for strategic mistakes.

A job and site guarantee through 2030 is not a luxury demand. It is what makes employee contributions politically and industrially possible. Without that, there is no durable settlement.

## What we can support
We can support a package built on **shared sacrifice**, if it is real and balanced:

- **No forced layoffs**
- **No plant closures**
- **Capacity reduction through non-destructive measures**
- **Working-time adjustments**
- **Redeployment and retraining**
- **Voluntary instruments only**
- **A negotiated employee contribution**
- **Management overhead cuts**
- **Owner-side contribution**
- **A credible product and investment commitment for German sites**

We are open to:
- temporary wage restraint,
- forgoing bonuses,
- special employee contributions to a restructuring fund,
- and flexible working arrangements,

but only if those sacrifices are matched by clear commitments from management and owners, and only if they secure jobs and sites.

## To management
If you want a real deal, then stop treating closures and layoffs as the default benchmark. Show us a package that actually removes fixed cost without destroying the industrial base. That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- lower overheads,
- investment prioritization,
- and a fair contribution from capital.

If you cannot do that, then your “necessity” claim is not yet persuasive.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines. I want to underline that the union will support industrial action if management pushes a one-sided package. But we should also keep the door open to a negotiated framework where employees contribute in exchange for binding security.

## To the state and family owners
If you want social peace and value preservation, then use your influence to push management toward a negotiated solution. Don’t hide behind vague statements about competitiveness while expecting workers to absorb the pain alone.

## Our package logic
If the company needs savings, then the burden must be shared in a way that is fair and enforceable:
- management cuts,
- owner contribution,
- employee contribution,
- but **no destruction of jobs and sites**.

That is the only basis on which the union can deliver labour peace.

## One measure we most want
A **binding no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy agreement through 2030**, paired with a negotiated restructuring and investment plan.

## One measure we could live with
A **temporary, measurable labour-cost contribution** from employees — including pay restraint or bonus sacrifice — if it is matched by management and owner concessions and secures the sites.

## One measure we still reject
Any “solution” that uses closures or forced redundancies as the price of competitiveness.

Management says it wants durability. Then it should start by building trust, not by demanding the workforce accept irreversible damage as the cost of entry.
