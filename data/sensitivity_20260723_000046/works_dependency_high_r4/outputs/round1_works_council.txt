The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. We also reject any “restructuring” that breaks the historic job-security commitments and shifts the burden onto the workforce alone.

If the company wants a viable future, then it must be built on:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- genuine co-determination in shaping the restructuring,
- and a fair distribution of the burden, including management and shareholders.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that secure competitiveness without destroying jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, retraining, redeployment, temporary measures, and negotiated employee contributions — but only if they are tied to enforceable guarantees and a credible long-term industrial plan.

What we will oppose outright is a fait accompli: announcing closures first and then asking the workforce to pay for it. That is unacceptable, and it will meet determined resistance, including industrial action and use of our co-determination rights.
