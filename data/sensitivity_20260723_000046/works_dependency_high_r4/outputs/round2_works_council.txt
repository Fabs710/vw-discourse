Management still has not answered the central question: **why should the workforce pay for a restructuring that starts by threatening German plants and breaking historic job security?** That is not a credible basis for trust, and it is not acceptable to us.

Let me be direct.

## What we agree on
Yes, the company needs a solution.
Yes, the cost base must be addressed.
Yes, we are prepared to discuss alternatives that actually secure the future.

But that does **not** mean we accept your framing that closures and forced redundancies are the necessary starting point. They are not. They are a choice. And on our side, they remain a red line.

## What we reject
We reject:
- **any closure of German plants**
- **any compulsory redundancies**
- **any restructuring that removes the historic job-security framework**
- **any attempt to use fear as leverage for one-sided wage or job cuts**

If management wants cooperation, it must stop presenting the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort.

## Our position on the package
If you want a serious deal, then the package must contain all of the following:

1. **Binding guarantees for all German plants**
2. **Job guarantees through 2030**
3. **No forced layoffs**
4. **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
5. **A fair burden-sharing contribution from management and owners**

Without these, there is nothing to negotiate in good faith.

## On your “durable savings” argument
We do not dispute the need for savings. We dispute your method. There are other ways to reduce cost and capacity without destroying sites:
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary short-time instruments,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- internal mobility,
- voluntary departures only,
- deferred investment sequencing,
- management overhead cuts,
- and owner-side contributions.

If those are said to be insufficient, then show the numbers. Do not simply repeat “closures may be necessary” as though that ends the discussion.

## To the investors and owners
You speak of enterprise value. Fine. Then understand this: **value is not only a spreadsheet issue.** A plant closure battle, mass warning strikes, reputational damage, and the destruction of trust in co-determination will also destroy value. If you force confrontation, you will not get a clean restructuring. You will get resistance, delay, and instability.

So if you want sustainable value, back a negotiated solution that protects the industrial base.

## To the state
We appreciate your clear rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But that support has to be more than rhetorical. Use your position to ensure the company is pushed toward an industrial plan that preserves jobs and sites, not one that exports the pain onto workers and regions.

## What we can live with
We can live with:
- capacity adjustments **if they do not mean closures**
- redeployment and retraining
- flexible working-time solutions
- temporary, reversible measures
- a negotiated and fair employee contribution
- investment prioritization that secures future products at German sites

## What we will still reject
We will still reject:
- closures dressed up as “deep consolidation”
- “voluntary” exits under threat
- forced redundancies in any form
- permanent erosion of the job-security framework
- a package that offers only sacrifices from workers and promises from management

## The measure we most want
A **binding guarantee for every German site and every job through 2030**, with genuine co-determination over the restructuring path.

## The measure we could live with
A **negotiated capacity and working-time package** with redeployment, retraining, and temporary adjustments, provided there are no closures or forced layoffs.

## The measure we reject
**Any closure-driven restructuring** and any compulsory redundancy program.

That is our position. If management wants movement, bring a plan that protects the workforce first and proves the savings with real alternatives, not threats.
