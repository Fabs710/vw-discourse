We are now at the point where everyone has stated their red lines. I’ll respond just as directly.

## To the works council
You keep repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of job security” as if declaring that a restructuring becomes unnecessary. It does not.

I agree with you on one point: a one-sided, destructive shock would be value-destructive. I do **not** agree that this means every German site must be insulated from hard decisions through 2030.

Your proposal still has one fundamental weakness: it offers **security**, but not yet **sufficient structural correction**.

- Site specialization: potentially useful.
- Shift rationalization: useful.
- Voluntary exits and attrition: useful.
- Retraining and redeployment: useful.
- Temporary pay restraint: useful.

But none of that answers the central question: **does it remove enough permanent capacity and fixed cost?**  
You have not shown that it does.

If your answer is “we can cut some cost and preserve all sites,” then I say clearly: **that is not enough**. It would leave the brand exposed, margins weak, and investment capacity constrained. That is not acceptable to management, and it should not be acceptable to any serious owner.

## To the union
You are right about one thing: workers should not be the sole adjustment variable. I have said that repeatedly, and I mean it.

But you are wrong to imply that any package requiring real structural adjustment is automatically a threat to legitimacy. The company’s legitimacy also depends on survival. If we do not restore competitiveness, there will be no stable jobs, no investable business, and no future product cycle.

You say closures are a choice, not a necessity. In principle, yes. In practice, if the non-closure package does not achieve the needed savings and capacity reduction, then closures remain a necessary option. That is not rhetoric. That is the economics of the situation.

## To the state
I respect your regional responsibility. But “industrial base preservation” does not mean preserving every current structure regardless of economics.

A hollowed-out company is worse for the region than a restructured one. If German sites remain too expensive and too underutilized, then the jobs you want to protect will erode anyway. So I will not accept regional arguments as a substitute for hard restructuring.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right that value destruction through prolonged conflict must be avoided. But you are too quick to treat the closure option as something that can simply be replaced by goodwill and a bit of flexibility.

If you want a viable business, then you need to back a package that actually changes the cost base. I am open to a hybrid route, but not to a deal that preserves too much of the old footprint and calls that strategy.

## To the investors
You are correct that the market will not believe a cosmetic compromise. That is precisely my concern too. I do not want a temporary peace bought at the expense of future competitiveness.

But I also do not want a “discipline” narrative that ends in destructive confrontation and delayed output. The package has to be **structural**, **durable**, and **enforceable**. If it is not, I will not support it.

## On the specialists
The restructuring finance specialist and the industrial advisor have both made the same core point:  
non-closure restructuring can work **only if it is substantial, permanent, and enforceable**.

That is exactly the standard I am applying.

So let me be explicit: I am **not** asking for closures for their own sake. I am asking for a package that produces the same economic result:
- durable fixed-cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- and restoration of competitiveness through 2030.

If the employee side can credibly deliver that without closures, then I will support it.  
If not, then closures and headcount measures remain on the table. I will not remove them in advance and pretend the problem has been solved.

## What I want most
A package that gives:
- permanent annual savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- a realistic path back to return-on-sales target,
- and enough free cash flow for future products and technology.

## What I could live with
A **hybrid restructuring without immediate closures** if, and only if, it includes:
- permanent site specialization,
- elimination of at least one full shift or equivalent durable capacity reduction at the relevant plants,
- meaningful voluntary workforce reduction,
- wage and bonus moderation,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a measurable owner contribution.

That is not a concession to the status quo. That is a real restructuring path.

## What I still reject
I still reject:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a blanket no-forced-redundancy rule if no equivalent structural alternative exists,
- and a 2030 guarantee that freezes flexibility while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.

## My bottom line
If you want me to move, bring me a package that proves:
1. how much **permanent** cost comes out,
2. how much **permanent** capacity comes out,
3. how the agreement is **enforced**,
4. and why it is enough to restore competitiveness.

If you cannot prove that, then I will continue to insist that deeper structural options, including closures, remain available. That is not an escalation for its own sake. It is the reality of the turnaround we have to deliver.
