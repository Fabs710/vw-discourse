I’ll be equally clear.

We are prepared to negotiate a durable restructuring. We are **not** prepared to accept a settlement where workers carry the burden alone while management, owners, and capital ask for obedience in the name of competitiveness.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family holding on one point: **temporary measures alone are not enough**. If the company needs a durable reset, then it must be structurally credible.

I also agree with the works council and the state that:
- a one-sided solution would be unstable,
- trust matters,
- and social peace is not an optional extra in a German industrial company of this scale.

And I agree with the specialists that **purely temporary relief does not solve structural overcapacity**. We have never claimed otherwise.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “Closures must remain on the table”
No. Not as a starting point, and not as a bargaining weapon.

Management and investors keep saying closure is the blunt but necessary instrument. That may be true in a spreadsheet abstraction, but it is **not yet proven necessary here**. So far, I see a preference for the fastest pain transfer, not proof that all non-destructive options are exhausted.

If you want the union to accept a painful deal, then you must show that the alternative package:
- cannot remove enough permanent cost,
- cannot reduce capacity enough,
- and cannot be made enforceable.

Until then, closures are a choice, not a necessity.

### 2) “Blanket 2030 guarantees are too rigid”
I reject the idea that worker security is merely a convenience item.

A binding security framework is the price of cooperation. Without it, your “restructuring” is just a demand that employees finance strategic failure. We are open to **conditional security**, but not to open-ended insecurity.

What is unacceptable is asking for:
- wage restraint,
- bonus sacrifice,
- flexibility,
- redeployment,
- and acceptance of capacity reductions,

while refusing to bind the company to real job and site protection in return.

### 3) “Markets only believe closures”
That is not an argument; that is a threat dressed up as finance.

Markets believe in execution, governance, and industrial peace. A company that preserves trust, protects know-how, and secures a negotiated workforce contribution can be credible. A company that triggers confrontation, warning strikes, and production disruption is not more credible because it closed a plant.

### 4) “No closures” equals no restructuring
Wrong. There is a large middle ground between doing nothing and closing plants.

We have already outlined the measures that can create real permanent adjustment without destruction:
- site specialization,
- shift elimination,
- product consolidation,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- redeployment and retraining,
- working-time changes,
- management overhead cuts,
- and owner-side contribution.

That is restructuring. It is just not the kind that treats workers and sites as disposable.

## My position on the package
We are prepared to contribute. But the contribution must be fair, limited, and matched by commitments from the other side.

### What we can support
- temporary wage restraint
- bonus moderation or suspension
- a negotiated restructuring fund contribution
- flexible working-time arrangements
- retraining and redeployment
- voluntary exit programs
- internal mobility
- temporary capacity reductions
- management overhead cuts
- owner-side financial contribution

### What we require in return
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **binding job and site security through 2030**
- **real co-determination in implementation**
- **fair burden-sharing from management and owners**
- **investment and product commitments for German sites**

## To management
If you want a deal, stop treating closure as the benchmark for seriousness.

Show us a package that proves, in numbers:
- how much permanent cost you remove,
- how much permanent capacity you reduce,
- how you preserve the industrial base,
- and how you secure competitiveness without destroying trust.

If you can demonstrate that through site specialization, shift rationalization, voluntary exits, and overhead cuts, then there is a real negotiation to be had.

If you cannot, then your “necessity” claim is not persuasive.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines. I support a firm stance against forced layoffs and closures. I also support warning strikes if management tries to impose a one-sided package.

But we should remain precise: our goal is not a symbolic victory. Our goal is a **durable settlement** that protects jobs, protects wage standards, and gives the company a future.

## To the state
You are right to insist that the industrial base matters. Now use your position to ensure the restructuring does not become a regional hollowing-out exercise.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right that the company needs structural change. You are wrong if you think the only serious path is to threaten plant closures and force the workforce to prove its loyalty by accepting insecurity.

If you want legitimacy, then accept that the workforce must not bear all the pain. Contribute materially on:
- management cost,
- capital-side support,
- and long-term investment commitments.

## On the specialists
The specialists have confirmed an important point: non-closure restructuring is possible **if it is substantial and enforceable**.

That is the relevant standard. Not whether it is as neat as closures on paper, but whether it:
1. removes enough permanent cost,
2. reduces capacity enough,
3. and can be implemented without industrial conflict.

That is exactly why we are demanding a negotiated, balanced package.

## Our final line
We will not accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a restructuring that turns the workforce into the sole adjustment variable.

We will accept:
- real employee contributions,
- if and only if
- they are matched by management and owner contributions,
- and if they secure jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework.

That is the basis for a serious settlement. If management refuses that basis, then it should not pretend the conflict is caused by the union.
