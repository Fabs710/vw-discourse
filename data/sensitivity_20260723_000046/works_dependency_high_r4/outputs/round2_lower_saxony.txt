I will be direct.

The specialist’s assessment is useful, but it does not change my core position. It confirms what we already know: **closure is the bluntest instrument for savings, but it is not the only instrument, and it is not automatically the best one for a company that is a regional anchor and governed under co-determination**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point:  
**the current cost and capacity structure cannot remain untouched.** A mere postponement would be irresponsible.

I also agree with the workforce representatives and the union that:
- this cannot be a fait accompli,
- a one-sided solution would be unstable,
- and management, owners, and the broader group must contribute materially.

And I agree with the specialist that **temporary measures alone are not enough**. We need something permanent.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that plant closures are therefore the preferred or default solution.

The finance assessment says a hybrid package is “more realistic” than a total no-closure/no-forced-redundancy position. That may be true in a narrow financial model, but my job is not to optimize a spreadsheet in isolation. My job is to protect:
- jobs in the state,
- the industrial base,
- regional stability,
- and the company’s long-term legitimacy.

A closure-first approach would damage all of that and would almost certainly create a prolonged conflict that erodes the very value management claims to protect.

## My bottom line on the package
I can support a **serious restructuring**, but only if it is built around **site preservation, no forced redundancies, and real capacity reduction through negotiated, reversible, and socially responsible measures**.

That means I will not support:
- closures of German plants as the starting or preferred answer,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or any package that strips away the state’s co-determination and blocking role in practice.

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are core interests.

## What I want most
A **negotiated industrial and employment pact** that:
- preserves all German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- secures jobs through 2030,
- includes a fair employee contribution,
- and commits management and owners to real burden-sharing and investment discipline.

## What I could live with
A **hybrid restructuring without closures**, if it includes:
- permanent shift or line rationalization where genuinely justified,
- redeployment and retraining,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary and targeted pay or bonus moderation,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a measurable owner-side contribution.

But I need to be clear: **“hybrid” cannot become a code word for closure by another name.** If a proposal removes a site’s future in practice, I will treat it as a closure.

## What I reject outright
I reject:
- closure-driven restructuring,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and any “solution” that treats the workforce and the state as obstacles to be bypassed.

## What I need to see before I move
If management and shareholders want my support, then bring forward a package that answers three concrete questions:

1. **How much permanent cost reduction is achieved without closures?**
2. **How much capacity reduction is achieved without destroying sites?**
3. **What binding guarantees secure jobs, investment, and co-determination in return?**

If the answer is “not enough,” then I will not pretend otherwise.

## My political and economic logic
A regional anchor company does not remain an anchor by hollowing out the state’s industrial base. If you remove the plants, you remove the ecosystem: suppliers, skills, tax base, apprenticeships, and local legitimacy. That is a long-term loss, not just a short-term social issue.

So I will continue to push for a negotiated solution that preserves the company’s future **and** its regional role. I am open to meaningful restructuring. I am not open to the destruction of German sites as the price of it.
