We are in broad agreement on the core red lines.

Let me be equally clear: **we will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies**. If management comes to the table with a package that treats the workforce as the sole adjustment variable, then there will be no settlement from our side. The burden must be shared, and it must be shared fairly.

I also agree with the works council on the central point that this cannot be a fait accompli. Announce closures first, then ask employees to finance the damage later — that is not restructuring, it is one-sided cost shifting. That path will face resistance.

Where I want to add specificity is this:

- **Capacity adjustments can be discussed**, but only as an alternative to closures, not as a disguised first step toward them.
- **Working-time solutions, retraining, redeployment, and temporary instruments** are legitimate tools.
- **An employee contribution is possible**, but only if management and owners also contribute materially.
- **Binding site and job guarantees** are essential if the workforce is being asked to accept sacrifices.

What we need is a package that protects the industrial base and spreads the pain across all sides:
- no forced layoffs,
- no plant closures,
- a fair labor-cost contribution from employees,
- savings from management overheads, investment prioritization, and owner-side contribution,
- and a credible medium-term product and investment plan.

If management insists that “competitiveness” only means cutting jobs and closing plants in Germany, then we will not treat that as a serious proposal. It would simply mean that workers are being asked to pay for strategic failures they did not cause.

We are prepared to negotiate hard on alternatives. We are not prepared to accept a restructuring that destroys jobs, undermines collective bargaining standards, and leaves the workforce carrying all the risk.
