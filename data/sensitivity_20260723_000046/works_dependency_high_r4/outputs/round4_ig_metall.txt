## Where I agree

## Where I disagree

### 1) With management and investors: “closure remains on the table” is not a plan
You keep repeating that closures and forced redundancies must remain available if the non-closure package does not fully match your preferred economics. That may be a negotiating posture, but it is **not** proof that closures are necessary.

From our side, the burden is still on management and capital to show that:
- all site-preserving measures were fully tested,
- the resulting permanent savings are insufficient,
- and closures are truly unavoidable rather than simply the easiest lever.

Until you show that with hard numbers, closures are **not justified**. They are a threat.

### 2) With the family holding: “conditional flexibility” cannot mean open-ended insecurity
We do not accept a framework where workers give concessions now and management keeps a permanent right to reopen closures later. That is not a settlement; that is insecurity with a smile.

If employees make real sacrifices, they need **real counter-guarantees**:
- job protection,
- site protection,
- and enforceable rules through 2030.

### 3) With the notion that “no-closure” equals “no serious restructuring”
That is simply wrong. There is a serious middle ground between doing nothing and closing plants.

A credible industrial package can include:
- shift reductions,
- site specialization,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- management overhead cuts,
- and owner contribution.

That is real restructuring if it is permanent and enforceable.
