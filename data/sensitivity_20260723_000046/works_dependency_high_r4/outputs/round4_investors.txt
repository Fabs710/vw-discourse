We agree with the broad diagnosis: this company needs **permanent** restructuring, not temporary relief. A cosmetic compromise would be unacceptable because it would leave margins structurally impaired and the equity case weakened.

That said, we are **not** prepared to endorse a package that rules out closures and compulsory redundancies in advance **if** it cannot show closure-level economics. Value preservation comes first.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that temporary concessions alone are insufficient.
- **The family holding is right** that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- **The state, works council, and union are right** that a one-sided shock would be unstable and value-destructive.
- **The specialists are right** that the key test is permanence: savings and capacity reduction must be durable, not symbolic.

## Where we disagree

### 1) On a blanket no-closure stance
We cannot support a blanket commitment to keep every German site open through 2030 if that leaves the cost base uncompetitive. If non-closure measures do not remove enough fixed cost and enough capacity, then “no closures” is just a delay of the same problem.

### 2) On blanket job-security guarantees
We understand why labour wants security, but a rigid 2030 guarantee is only acceptable if it is tied to **real, enforceable structural delivery**. Security without hard savings is not investable.

### 3) On “closure by stealth”
We agree with the works council that closure by attrition would be unacceptable. A plant cannot be stripped of product, shifts, and future role and then called preserved. That would be a deception, not a compromise.

## What we need to see
Before we can back a non-closure route, we need a **quantified, site-by-site comparison** showing:

1. which German plants can lose a full shift or equivalent durable capacity without losing strategic viability,  
2. what permanent annual savings that produces,  
3. what one-time costs are involved,  
4. what product and investment commitments are needed to keep those sites viable through 2030, and  
5. what enforcement mechanism exists if targets are missed.

## What we could support
A **hybrid restructuring without immediate closures** could be acceptable if it includes:
- permanent site specialization,
- elimination of at least one full shift or equivalent durable capacity reduction where justified,
- meaningful voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- redeployment and retraining,
- working-time flexibility,
- wage and bonus moderation,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a measurable owner contribution.

That is a real restructuring. It is only acceptable if it truly closes the economics gap.

## What we reject
- a cosmetic no-closure compromise,
- temporary relief presented as structural reform,
- a package that preserves too much of the current footprint,
- prolonged conflict that destroys value,
- and any arrangement that leaves the company with structurally impaired margins.
