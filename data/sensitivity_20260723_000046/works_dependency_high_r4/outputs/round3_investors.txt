We agree with the core diagnosis from management, the family holding, the state, and the specialists: **the current German cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**. If the company cannot restore competitive margins, then the investment case weakens materially. We are not interested in a cosmetic settlement that merely postpones the problem.

But we also do **not** support a value-destructive closure fight unless it is truly necessary. The burden of proof is on management and the employee side to show whether a non-closure package can deliver enough permanent savings, capacity reduction, and execution certainty.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that temporary measures alone are insufficient.
- **The family holding is right** that paralysis is unacceptable.
- **The union, works council, and state are right** that a one-sided shock strategy would be unstable and destroy value.
- **The specialists are right** that the key test is permanence: savings must be durable, not just a short-term cash bridge.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A blanket no-closure position is not acceptable
We cannot back a package that rules out closures in advance **if** it leaves structural overcapacity in place. If non-closure measures do not remove enough fixed cost and enough capacity, then “no closures” is just another way of saying “preserve uncompetitive economics.”

That is not acceptable for shareholders.

### 2) Blanket 2030 guarantees are too rigid unless tied to real structural delivery
We understand why employees want security. But unconditional guarantees are not investable if they freeze adaptation and block necessary restructuring. Security is only defensible if it is matched by **credible, enforceable structural change**.

### 3) Temporary concessions are not enough
Wage restraint, bonus moderation, and working-time flexibility may help close a gap, but they do **not** substitute for permanent cost and capacity reduction. If the package is mostly temporary, the market will discount it.

## What we need to see
For us to support a non-closure route, it must demonstrate all three of the following:

1. **Permanent fixed-cost reduction**  
   management overhead cuts, simplified support functions, less duplication

2. **Permanent capacity reduction**  
   shift elimination, specialization, product concentration, lower installed load

3. **Implementation certainty**  
   milestones, enforcement, and credible downside if targets are missed

If those conditions are met, we can support a negotiated solution that avoids closures. If they are not, closures must remain available.

## What we could live with
A **hybrid restructuring without immediate closures** can be acceptable to us **if and only if** it includes:
- permanent site specialization,
- elimination of at least one full shift or equivalent durable capacity reduction where justified,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition at meaningful scale,
- retraining and redeployment,
- working-time and compensation flexibility,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a measurable owner contribution.

That is a real restructuring. Anything less is a delay tactic.

## What we reject
We reject:
- a cosmetic no-closure compromise,
- temporary relief presented as structural reform,
- a package that preserves the current cost base,
- and a prolonged standoff that destroys value while no one adjusts.

## To the works council and union
We hear your red lines, but we cannot endorse a stance that says:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- full job security through 2030,
- and no hard structural adjustment.

That combination only works if you can prove the same permanent savings and capacity reduction that closures would deliver. So far, that proof has not been provided.

## To management
You are right to insist on hard numbers, but you must be equally disciplined:
- do not turn the process into a closure threat exercise,
- do not assume the workforce alone must absorb the adjustment,
- and do not settle for a “win” that leaves margins structurally impaired.

## To the state
Your regional concerns are legitimate, but preserving the industrial base only matters if the company stays investable and profitable. A weak company is not a durable anchor.

## To the family holding
We agree that value destruction through conflict would be unacceptable. But governance also means backing a package that truly restores competitiveness, not just one that sounds cooperative.

## Bottom line
We will support **any outcome that credibly restores competitive margins through durable, enforceable cost reduction and capacity rationalization**.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a structurally impaired cost base,
- or a prolonged conflict that destroys enterprise value.
