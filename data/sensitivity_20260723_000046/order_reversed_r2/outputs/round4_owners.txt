We are now at the point where the discussion must become concrete, because the repeated slogans have not yet answered the core question:

**Can we restore competitiveness without destroying value through unnecessary conflict, or do we need a harder footprint adjustment?**

From the controlling family’s perspective, the answer must be guided by **long-term enterprise value, enforceability, and labour peace**. Not by symbolism, and not by ideological lines.

## Where I agree

### To investors
Your standard is the correct one: no cosmetic settlement. If the package does not produce **durable, measurable, enforceable savings**, then it is not acceptable. I also agree that a mere promise of peace is not enough.

### To management
Your diagnosis of overcapacity and an uncompetitive cost base is credible. The company cannot keep missing margin targets and pretend the structure is fine.

### To the union and works council
You are right that prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive. A strike spiral or a settlement that detonates trust would hurt the company for years.

### To the state government
You are right that deindustrialisation would be a bad outcome. We have no interest in hollowing out the company’s German base.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be treated as a hard principle
I do **not** accept a blanket rejection of closures as a matter of principle.

If a site is structurally uncompetitive and cannot be brought into line through other means, then refusing to adjust would simply preserve a problem until it becomes worse. That would not protect jobs in the long run; it would endanger them.

### 2) But closures are also not the first-choice solution
I also do **not** accept the idea that closures are automatically the best proof of seriousness.

A closure destroys capacity, know-how, flexibility, and trust. If a **closure-minimized package** can truly remove enough fixed cost and capacity, that is preferable from a stewardship perspective.

### 3) “Internal rationalization” is only credible if it is deep
The expert made the key point plainly: non-closure measures can be substantial, but they usually do not fully replicate closure economics unless they remove **real capacity** and **permanent fixed cost**.

That means the burden is now on those opposing closures to show a package that is not just softer, but **economically equivalent**.

## What I take from the latest exchange

The investors are right to insist on:
- fixed-cost reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- durability,
- and enforcement.

Management is right that the company needs a real structural adjustment.

The union and works council are right that social peace and legitimacy matter.

But what I do **not** yet see is a fully credible answer to this question:

**What exact combination of measures closes the competitiveness gap without leaving the company overcosted?**

Until that is answered, neither “no closures” nor “closures” can be treated as self-evidently correct.

## Our position

We are prepared to support a **closure-minimized restructuring** if, and only if, it meets all of the following:

1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **Permanent overhead simplification**
4. **A meaningful workforce contribution**
5. **Enforceable milestones and a real backstop**
6. **Protection of future investment capacity**
7. **No prolonged conflict that undermines the company**

If those conditions are met, I will support a solution that avoids unnecessary plant closures.

If they are not met, then I will not support a weak compromise just because it is politically easier. A deal that preserves the appearance of peace while leaving the business structurally weak is value destructive.

- labour-cost moderation,

### To the works council
Co-determination is real, but it is not a veto on economic reality. If you want site preservation, then support a package that actually changes the footprint and the cost base.

### To the state government
Your role should be to help produce a viable alternative, not to block every difficult adjustment. A veto without a credible alternative is not a solution.

## Bottom line

We are **open** to a closure-minimized path.

We are **not open** to a compromise that merely postpones the crisis.

If the parties can show, with numbers, that non-closure measures truly close the gap, I will back them.

If they cannot, then closures remain a legitimate option.
