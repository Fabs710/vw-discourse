We are at the point where the positions are fully exposed, and I will not pretend they are closer than they are.

## First: what I agree with

### To management
I agree that the company needs restructuring. I do **not** deny overcapacity, cost pressure, or the need for real savings. I also agree that a cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.

### To investors
I agree that savings must be durable, enforceable, and large enough to matter. A settlement that only delays the problem is not acceptable.

### To the family
I agree that prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value. A strike spiral helps nobody.

### To the state government
I agree that deindustrialization would damage the company and the region. The German industrial base must be protected.

### To the expert
I accept the benchmark: a non-closure package must remove real fixed cost, real capacity, and future cash drain if it wants to be treated as serious. That is the correct test.

---

## Where I disagree — sharply

### 1) I reject the idea that closures are the natural benchmark
Management and investors keep talking as if closures are the “clean” or “credible” answer. They are not the default answer. They are the most destructive answer.

The expert did **not** say closures are the only serious route. The expert said non-closure measures must be deep enough to be economically comparable. That is a very different statement.

So no, I do not accept the logic:
> “If you don’t accept closures, you’re not serious.”

That is not analysis. That is pressure.

### 2) I reject the claim that “no closures” is merely a political slogan
Keeping every German plant open is not rhetoric. It is a legitimate strategic and social objective, and in this company it is also institutionally protected. Co-determination is not decoration. It is a hard structural reality.

If management wants to change the footprint, then it must prove that every less destructive route has been exhausted. So far, that proof is missing.

### 3) I reject compulsory redundancies outright
This is not a bargaining chip. It is a red line.

Forced layoffs would break trust, trigger resistance, and destroy the job-security framework that has held this company together for decades. If management chooses that route, it should not be surprised by escalation.

### 4) I reject any weakening of co-determination or the historic job-security framework
If this restructuring becomes a vehicle to roll back workers’ governance rights or dismantle guarantees that protect employment, then it crosses a line.

That is not “competitiveness.” That is a power grab dressed up as restructuring.

---

## My answer to management

You ask for numbers. Fine. Then let’s be honest about the burden of proof.

If you want closures on the table, then show me:

1. which internal measures you have **fully exhausted**  
2. how much fixed cost is removed by non-closure measures  
3. how much real capacity is actually taken out without closures  
4. what management-side cuts you are making first  
5. what owner-side contribution is on the table  
6. why the workforce should accept plant closures before these measures are tried  

Until that is answered, closures are not “necessary.” They are your preferred instrument.

And let me be equally clear: if management continues to present closures as the only serious option, then it is inviting resistance, not cooperation. We know how to respond.

---

## My answer to investors

You keep asking for equivalence. Fine. Then define it honestly.

What exact savings, capacity reduction, and duration would you accept from a no-closure package **before** demanding closures and compulsory layoffs?

You cannot demand a mathematically perfect substitute while ignoring the social, governance, and industrial damage of closures. If your threshold is truly so high that no non-closure deal can meet it, then say so plainly. Do not hide that behind neutral language.

We are prepared to accept real restructuring. What we will not accept is a one-sided restructuring that treats employees as the only adjustment variable.

---

## My answer to the controlling family

You say you care about long-term enterprise value and labour peace. Then support a package that actually preserves both.

A plan that:
- closes plants,
- forces layoffs,
- and breaks trust in labour relations

may look decisive, but it can destroy more value through conflict than it creates through savings.

If you want stewardship, then back a settlement that keeps every German site open, avoids compulsory redundancies, and protects the job-security framework. That is the path that preserves industrial legitimacy.

---

## My answer to the state government

You are right to reject deindustrialization. But that means more than saying “no closures” in principle.

Use your blocking power to insist on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding investment commitments,
- real management and owner contributions,
- and a restructuring that keeps the German footprint intact.

Do not let “competitiveness” become a code word for rolling back employment standards and weakening worker protections.

---

## What the workforce side is prepared to discuss

We are not refusing adjustment. We are refusing a one-sided adjustment.

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility
- shift-model changes
- internal redeployment
- voluntary exits
- temporary labour-cost contributions
- bonus suspension
- management overhead cuts
- procurement and indirect-cost reductions
- line consolidation
- real capacity reduction **without** plant closures

But this only works if management and ownership also contribute materially and if the outcome protects:
- every German plant
- jobs
- co-determination
- and the collective wage framework

---

## The crucial point management keeps trying to skip over

The company does **not** need to prove that every site can remain untouched forever.

It does need to prove that:
- there is no fairer way to achieve the required savings,
- internal alternatives have truly been exhausted,
- and the restructuring burden is not being dumped primarily on employees.

That proof has **not** been delivered.

---

## My line remains unchanged

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework**

If the company needs cost reduction, then it must come through:
- negotiated work-time and utilization changes,
- voluntary instruments,
- internal redeployment,
- management-side savings,
- and owner contributions.

If management and capital refuse that, then the conflict is not caused by labour being unreasonable. It is caused by your choice to treat workers as the easiest target.

And yes — if you try to force that path, we will resist it with all means available to us, including mass mobilization.

## Bottom line
I am open to a serious restructuring.  
I am **not** open to closures, forced layoffs, or the erosion of co-determination.

Bring a package that proves the non-closure route is not just gentler, but materially real — and that it shares the burden fairly. Until then, our position stands.
