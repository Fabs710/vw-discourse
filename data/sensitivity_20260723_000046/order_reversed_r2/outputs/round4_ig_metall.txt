We have now had enough repetition to see the real line of conflict clearly.

## Where I agree

### To the expert
Your benchmark is useful, and I accept the core point: **a non-closure package has to remove real fixed cost, real capacity, and future cash drain if it is to be taken seriously**.  
I am not disputing the need for restructuring. I am not defending a frozen status quo.

### To management, investors, and the family
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic settlement would be worthless. If the company remains structurally overcosted, the problem will come back. We do not want that either.

### To the state government
You are right that deindustrialisation would be damaging. We share the goal of keeping the company a strong industrial employer in Germany.

---

## Where I disagree

### 1) You keep treating closures as the benchmark and then acting as if that settles the debate
That is the problem.

The expert did **not** say closures are the only serious route. The expert said closures are the cleanest way to remove fixed cost and capacity. That is not the same thing.

Management is trying to turn that into: “If you don’t accept closures, you’re not serious.”  
That is simply false.

We are serious. We already showed that with our counter-proposal and with the willingness to discuss labour-cost contributions, working-time flexibility, and capacity reduction **without** plant closures and **without** forced redundancies.

### 2) You are still underestimating what can be done without shutting plants
You keep speaking as if the only alternatives to closures are symbolic. That is wrong.

A serious package can include:
- working-time adjustments
- shift and utilisation changes
- internal redeployment
- voluntary exits
- wage moderation or temporary labour-cost contributions
- bonus suspension
- management overhead cuts
- procurement and indirect-cost reductions
- line consolidation
- targeted capacity reduction inside the current footprint

That is not “nothing.” That is a real restructuring if it is deep enough and shared fairly.

### 3) The burden cannot fall mainly on workers
This is the point where your position becomes unacceptable.

If the package means:
- workforce gives up wages, flexibility, and security,
- workers lose jobs,
- and management/owners mostly preserve their side,

then that is not shared sacrifice. That is one-sided burden shifting.

We will not accept a restructuring where employees carry the pain while the top side presents itself as merely “demanding credibility.”

---

## Our line remains unchanged

We are prepared to contribute to a durable settlement. But the boundaries are still the boundaries:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
- **Protection of sites and jobs**
- **A fair contribution from management and ownership as well**

That is not posturing. That is the minimum for a settlement we can carry to the workforce.

---

## Direct response to management

You keep asking for numbers. Fine. Then answer the same question in a fair way:

1. What management-side cuts are you putting on the table first?
2. What owner contribution is included?
3. What exactly is the internal footprint reduction you are prepared to do before talking about closures?
4. Why are you not starting with the least destructive measures?

You present closures as the “necessary” option, but you have not shown that all serious internal alternatives are exhausted. Until then, closures are not proven necessary — they are your preferred instrument.

And let me be equally clear: if management continues to push closure rhetoric as the default answer, it is inviting conflict. Warning strikes remain available, and we will use them if needed.

---

## Direct response to investors

You ask what savings threshold would make a no-closure package acceptable. That is a fair question, but you also have to answer it in reverse.

What exact numbers are you willing to accept **before** demanding closures and forced redundancies?

You cannot just say “equivalent to closure” and then define equivalence as whatever management prefers. If you want a workable settlement, then spell out the minimum savings, capacity reduction, and duration you require.

And if your real answer is that closures are still needed, then say so honestly instead of presenting it as neutral financial logic.

---

## Direct response to the controlling family

You say you care about long-term enterprise value and labour peace. Then support a package that actually preserves both.

A plan that:
- cuts jobs compulsorily,
- closes plants,
- and breaks trust in labour relations

is not the only route to value protection. It may destroy more value through conflict than it creates through savings.

If you want stewardship, then back a deal that shares the burden fairly and protects the company’s industrial legitimacy.

---

## Direct response to the state government

You are right to reject deindustrialisation. But that means more than opposing closures in principle.

Use your blocking role to insist on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding investment commitments,
- and real burden-sharing from management and owners.

Do not allow “competitiveness” to become a code word for rolling back employment standards and social protections.

---

## What we are prepared to put on the table

We are not refusing adjustment. We are refusing a one-sided adjustment.

We are prepared to discuss:
- wage moderation or temporary concessions
- bonus suspension
- working-time flexibility
- shift-model changes
- voluntary exits
- internal redeployment
- line consolidation
- capacity reduction through non-closure instruments
- and a joint package to reduce labour and overhead costs

But this only works if:
- management cuts overhead and executive burden,
- ownership contributes materially,
- and the outcome protects jobs, sites, and the collective bargaining framework.

---

## The critical point

The company does **not** need to prove that every site can remain unchanged forever.  
It does need to prove that:
- there is no fairer way to achieve the required savings,
- internal alternatives have truly been exhausted,
- and the restructuring burden is not being dumped primarily on employees.

That proof has **not** been delivered.

So our answer remains:

**No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures. Shared sacrifice.**  
If management and capital refuse that, then the conflict is not caused by labour being unreasonable. It is caused by your decision to choose the most destructive route instead of the most balanced one.

## Final word

If you want a settlement, bring:
- a real management contribution,
- a real owner contribution,
- quantified savings from non-closure measures,
- and binding protection against layoffs and closures.

If you cannot do that, then do not ask workers to accept a restructuring that treats them as the only adjustment variable. We will resist that — with all means available to us.
