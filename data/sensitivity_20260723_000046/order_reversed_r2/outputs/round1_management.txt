I’ll be direct.

I agree with one part of what has been said by the investors, the controlling family, and the state: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company’s German cost base is structurally too high, and if we do not fix that now, we will keep bleeding margin, investment capacity, and competitiveness. We are not in a position to preserve every legacy arrangement and hope the market recovers.

Where I disagree is on the implication that we can achieve the necessary scale of change with broad promises, soft measures, or open-ended negotiation. We cannot.

### To the institutional investors
You are right on the objective: **durable margin recovery, not temporary peace**. But that requires realism about how much labour cost and capacity must come out. If alternative measures cannot deliver the same economic effect as closures and headcount reduction, then they are not serious alternatives. I will not support a settlement that looks stable on paper but leaves the cost base uncompetitive in practice.

### To the controlling family
You are right that we need something implementable and value-preserving. I share your concern about prolonged industrial conflict destroying value. But we should be clear: **the status quo is already destroying value**. A deal that avoids hard decisions simply shifts the damage into future years. If plant closures or deep employment cuts are needed to restore competitiveness, then we should not rule them out in advance.

### To the regional state government
I understand the political responsibility you carry, but I need to be equally clear: **regional sensitivity cannot override industrial reality**. We cannot preserve all sites and all jobs if the economics no longer support them. If the state wants to keep a strong industrial base, then it should support a restructuring that makes that base viable — not defend overcapacity until the business weakens further.

### Our position
Management’s view is that the company needs a **structural and sustainable reduction in cost and capacity**. That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- a meaningful labour-cost contribution,
- removal of legacy cost burdens,
- and a credible path to restoring returns by 2030.

I am open to a negotiated solution that avoids closures **only if** it produces savings of comparable scale, durability, and certainty. But if that cannot be shown, then we will not pretend that a softer package is enough.

So let me be explicit:  
**We are prepared for tough restructuring, including plant closures if necessary.**  
What we will not accept is a deal that buys short-term peace while leaving us structurally uncompetitive.  

If the employee side wants to avoid the most drastic measures, then it must come forward with a package that genuinely closes the gap on cost and capacity — not just a promise of goodwill.
