### Core impasse

The central impasse is now very clear:

**All sides agree that the company needs a real, durable cost and capacity reset.**  
The deadlock is over **how much restructuring is required, and whether that must include plant closures and compulsory workforce reductions**.

More specifically, the conflict sits on four linked points:

1. **Capacity reduction vs. site preservation**  
   - Management, investors, and the family believe real capacity reduction may require closures or at least very deep site rationalization.  
   - The union and works council reject closures and want capacity cuts achieved internally.

2. **Durable savings vs. job-security guarantees**  
   - Shareholders want enforceable, front-loaded, lasting savings.  
   - Labour wants strong job and site guarantees through 2030.  
   - The tension is whether those guarantees are compatible with the required savings.

3. **Burden-sharing vs. who absorbs the adjustment**  
   - Labour insists management, ownership, and the state must also contribute materially.  
   - The capital side says workforce contributions must be quantified and structurally meaningful, not temporary.

4. **Credibility of non-closure alternatives**  
   - The expert benchmark suggests non-closure measures can help but often do not fully replicate closure economics.  
   - The dispute is whether a closure-minimized package can match the needed scale, certainty, and speed.

---

### Concrete compromise positions

#### Compromise 1: Conditional site-preservation package with hard savings triggers
A negotiated package could preserve sites and avoid compulsory redundancies **only if** it includes:
- quantified annual savings targets,
- front-loaded labour and overhead contributions,
- measurable capacity reductions through line consolidation and internal footprint shrinkage,
- and automatic corrective measures if targets are missed.

**Facilitator framing:**  
This bridges the shareholder demand for enforceability with labour’s demand for site protection.

---

#### Compromise 2: Two-stage restructuring with a genuine, pre-agreed backstop
Stage 1 would implement:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement and support-function reductions,
- and immediate capacity rationalization.

Stage 2 would be pre-authorized and triggered only if agreed metrics are not met within a defined period.

**Facilitator framing:**  
This gives labour time and protection up front, while giving investors and management a credible fallback if the first phase fails.

---

#### Compromise 3: Partial footprint adjustment without full plant closure
Instead of closing plants outright, the parties could agree to:
- shut specific lines or shifts,
- consolidate production across fewer operating modules,
- reduce fixed overhead by carving out underused functions,
- and bind the company to investment commitments for the remaining core sites.

**Facilitator framing:**  
This may satisfy the state and works council more than closures, while still giving investors and management genuine structural change.
