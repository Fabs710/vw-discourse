## Core impasse
The parties disagree on **what counts as a credible restructuring**.

- **Management / investors / family** want proof of durable fixed-cost and capacity reduction, and they see closures as the clearest benchmark if non-closure measures are too weak.
- **Union / works council / state** want to preserve sites, jobs, and co-determination, and they see closures and compulsory redundancies as unacceptable first options.
- The **expert** has narrowed the dispute: a non-closure package is acceptable only if it is **economically comparable** to closure in permanent fixed-cost removal, capacity reduction, and enforceability.

So the dispute is no longer about whether change is needed; it is about **whether a closure-minimized package can be made numerically and legally equivalent enough to satisfy the value test**.

## Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled for discussion:

### 1) Phased footprint adjustment with a binding backstop
- Immediate non-closure measures: shift reductions, line consolidation, overhead cuts, working-time changes, voluntary exits.
- A quantified savings target is set up front.
- If the target is missed by a fixed date, a pre-agreed second-stage footprint reduction automatically activates.
- This gives labour a chance to avoid closures, while giving investors and management an enforceable fallback.

### 2) Site-preservation package tied to capacity cuts
- No outright plant closures for a defined period.
- In exchange, one or more sites must permanently surrender specific capacity, product lines, or shifts.
- The package includes durable labor-cost contributions and management-side overhead reductions.
- This directly addresses the demand for real capacity reduction without immediate closures.

### 3) Joint burden-sharing compact with measurable contributions from all sides
- Labour: moderated compensation, flexible working-time, voluntary exits.
- Management: executive restraint, overhead simplification, procurement and support-function cuts.
- Owners: capital support or deferred distributions tied to restructuring milestones.
- State: limited facilitation or guarantees conditioned on verified restructuring outcomes.
- A monitoring mechanism verifies whether savings are actually materializing.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
What is the smallest quantified non-closure package you would accept as genuinely equivalent to closure on a present-value basis?

### To investors
What specific fixed-cost, capacity, and durability thresholds would make you support a no-closure deal?

### To the controlling family
How much short-term disruption are you willing to tolerate if it meaningfully reduces long-term restructuring risk?

### To the union
What permanent capacity and cost reductions are you prepared to accept if plant closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table?

### To the works council
Which specific site-level adjustments could you support that materially reduce cost while preserving as many sites as possible?

### To the state government
What concrete incentives or guarantees are you willing to offer if the parties reach a verifiable no-closure restructuring package?
