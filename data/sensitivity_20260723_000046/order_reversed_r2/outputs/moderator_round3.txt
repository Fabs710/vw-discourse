## Core impasse
The parties agree on the diagnosis — the company is overcosted and needs a real restructuring — but they diverge on **what counts as a credible restructuring**.

The conflict sits on three linked questions:

1. **Closure vs. no closure**
   - Management, investors, and the expert view closures as a legitimate benchmark if non-closure measures cannot remove enough fixed cost and capacity.
   - The union and works council reject closures outright.
   - The state government also rejects closures as the default route.

2. **How much burden can be placed on labour**
   - Employee-side actors will accept contribution only if it is matched by management and ownership.
   - Management and investors want measurable labour-cost and capacity reductions.
   - The main dispute is whether the workforce can be asked to contribute materially without plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

3. **What “equivalent” really means**
   - Investors want closure-minimized packages only if they are economically equivalent in scale and durability.
   - The union and works council argue equivalence must include social trust, co-determination, and job security.
   - There is no shared definition of equivalence yet.

## Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the gap

### Compromise position 1: Conditional no-closure package with hard numerical triggers
A negotiated package could preserve all sites initially, but only if it includes:
- quantified annual savings,
- permanent overhead cuts,
- line or shift consolidation,
- a defined capacity reduction target,
- and an automatic review or corrective mechanism if targets are missed.

**Bridging value:**  
This addresses investors’ demand for enforceability and the union’s refusal of immediate closures.

---

### Compromise position 2: Two-stage restructuring with a real, pre-agreed backstop
Stage 1 would implement:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time and utilization changes,
- management-side savings,
- internal redeployment,
- and footprint simplification.

Stage 2 would only activate if agreed financial targets are not met, with its scope and conditions defined in advance.

**Bridging value:**  
This gives management and investors a credible backstop while giving labour and the state a chance to avoid immediate closures.

---

### Compromise position 3: Partial footprint adjustment instead of full plant closure
Instead of closing plants, parties could consider:
- shutting specific lines,
- consolidating production across sites,
- reducing support functions,
- and removing redundant shifts.

**Bridging value:**  
This may satisfy the economic requirement for real capacity reduction while preserving the political commitment to keep sites open.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the smallest verified package of non-closure measures that would close the competitiveness gap, and what exact evidence do you have that it is insufficient without closures?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum combination of savings, capacity reduction, and enforcement would you accept as closure-equivalent, even if no plant is closed?**

### To the controlling family
**Which outcome would you prioritize if you had to choose between a tougher restructuring with closures and a less disruptive package with slightly lower immediate savings but better long-term labour peace?**

### To the state government
**What concrete burden-sharing measures are you prepared to support or facilitate so that “no closures” is paired with measurable economic improvement rather than becoming a political veto?**

### To management/executive leadership
**Which management-side cuts are you prepared to put on the table first, before asking labour for further concessions?**

### To the union
**What specific capacity reductions, working-time changes, or cost concessions are you prepared to accept that would be material enough to satisfy the investor and management threshold without closures?**

### To the works council
**If every site must remain open, what operational changes will you support to ensure the footprint still shrinks enough to matter financially?**

### To the expert
**Can you specify the point at which a non-closure package becomes economically close enough to closure that it can reasonably be treated as equivalent?**
