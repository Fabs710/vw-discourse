We hear the same diagnosis repeated by management and the shareholders: the cost base is too high and the company must become competitive again. We do **not** dispute the need for a real restructuring.

What we **do** dispute is the idea that the only credible path runs through plant closures, forced redundancies, and workers carrying the full burden while ownership and management remain largely untouched.

### Where we agree
- **No cosmetic solution.** A paper compromise that leaves the company uncompetitive is not acceptable to us either.
- **Durable savings matter.** Temporary measures alone are not enough.
- **Future investment must be protected.** Without a viable company, there are no secure jobs.
- **Capacity must be addressed.** We are not defending every unit of overcapacity regardless of economics.

### Where we disagree
Management is presenting closures and headcount cuts as if they were the default proof of seriousness. They are not. They are the most destructive option, not automatically the smartest one.

We will not accept:
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **plant closures**
- a settlement that destroys collective bargaining standards
- an outcome where workers pay the price alone while shareholders demand competitiveness and management demands flexibility

If the company wants a contribution from the workforce, then it must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**. That means:
- management and ownership also contributing,
- labour-cost measures being negotiated, not imposed,
- capacity adjustments through agreed instruments first,
- and binding guarantees on sites, employment, and future investment.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We have already signalled openness to:
- a **temporary or permanent employee contribution**,
- working-time and capacity adjustments,
- forgoing bonuses or other cost elements,
- and a negotiated package that helps restore competitiveness.

But this is only acceptable if it is tied to:
1. **site security**
2. **job security**
3. **no forced layoffs**
4. **no plant closures**
5. **respect for the collective wage framework**

### Our warning
If management insists that “real restructuring” must mean closures and compulsory redundancies, then we are on a collision course. We have already shown that the peace obligation is over, and the workforce is prepared to escalate. Warning strikes are not a symbolic gesture; they are the beginning of a serious response.

### To the institutional investors
You ask for durable margin recovery. Fine — but durability does not come from tearing up trust and provoking long conflict. A deal that preserves productive sites and know-how, while the workforce contributes fairly, is more durable than a scorched-earth approach that leaves industrial relations broken.

### To the controlling family
If you want long-term enterprise value, then support a settlement that preserves the company’s industrial core. Value is not protected by destroying social peace and then hoping the brand and the workforce stay loyal.

### To the state government
We agree that the company must remain an industrial anchor in the region. But that requires more than political statements: it requires support for a negotiated solution that rules out closures and protects jobs.

### Bottom line
We are ready to talk about a **fair contribution from labour**. We are **not** ready to accept a restructuring where workers absorb all the pain through closures, layoffs, and the dismantling of collective protections.

If management wants a serious agreement, it must come back with a package that matches these conditions. Otherwise, industrial action will continue and intensify.
