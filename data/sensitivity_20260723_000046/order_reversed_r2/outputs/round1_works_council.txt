We are not going to sign off on a restructuring that destroys German plants, throws people out on the street, and tears up the historic job-security framework.

Let me be equally clear: **we do not deny the need for a real solution**. We have heard the diagnosis from management, investors, and the family owners: the cost base must come down, capacity must be adjusted, and the company must remain investable. We understand that pressure. But the answer cannot be a blunt instrument of closures and compulsory redundancies.

### Where we agree
- **No cosmetic deal.** If the company remains structurally uncompetitive, that helps no one, least of all the workforce.
- **Durable savings matter.** Temporary fixes alone are not enough.
- **Future investment is essential.** There is no secure employment if the company loses its technological future.
- **Capacity has to be addressed.** We are not defending inefficiency for its own sake.

### Where we disagree
Management keeps presenting plant closures and forced job cuts as though they were the only serious route. That is not acceptable. It is not a neutral “economic necessity”; it is a choice to shift the burden onto workers and regions that have carried this company for decades.

To the **investors**: you keep saying you want durable margin improvement. Fine. Then prove that closures are necessary. Do not demand the most destructive option just because it is the easiest to model on a spreadsheet. A deal that preserves industrial know-how, avoids social rupture, and still delivers savings through negotiated measures is not “cosmetic” — it is smarter and more sustainable.

To the **controlling family**: if you are serious about long-term enterprise value, then you must not support an approach that breaks trust with the workforce. Once the company violates the job-security promise, it destroys the very industrial discipline and loyalty that made this company strong.

To the **state government**: we appreciate your stated opposition to deindustrialisation. But that means you must be prepared to use your blocking position against any closure logic. General concern is not enough; we need a hard line that German sites stay open.

To **management**: we reject the framing that “real restructuring” requires plant closures. Real restructuring means:
- reducing capacity through negotiated and reversible instruments first,
- using working-time, utilisation, and redeployment measures,
- securing a fair employee contribution only as part of a balanced package,
- protecting sites and jobs,
- and preserving co-determination, not bypassing it.

### Our position
Our red lines are unchanged:
- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or job-security guarantees**

We are prepared to discuss:
- a fair workforce contribution,
- targeted, negotiated capacity adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- temporary or phased measures,
- and investment commitments linked to the future product plan.

But that only makes sense if there is a **binding guarantee of site security and employment security through 2030**. Without that, workers are being asked to pay now for promises that can be broken later. We will not accept that.

### About the strike situation
The warning strikes are not a bluff, and they will not stop because management repeats the word “competitiveness.” If the company continues to push closures and forced layoffs, the workforce will escalate. We are not looking for conflict for its own sake — but we will defend every site and every job.

So the message is simple:
**Bring a serious restructuring plan that preserves German plants, rules out forced layoffs, and guarantees employment and investment to 2030 — or expect full resistance.**
