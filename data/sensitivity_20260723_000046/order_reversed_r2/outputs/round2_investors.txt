We agree with the moderator on the core issue: **the dispute is not about whether restructuring is needed, but whether it will be real enough to restore competitiveness**.

### Our position remains unchanged

As capital-market shareholders, we will support **only** a package that delivers:

- a **credible and durable reduction in the cost base**
- a **material improvement in margins and return on sales**
- **capacity reduction** where the market no longer supports current output
- **implementation certainty**, not open-ended conflict
- protection of the company’s **future investment capacity**

What we will **not** support is a settlement that preserves structural overcapacity and legacy costs while claiming victory because it avoids difficult decisions.

---

### Where we agree

#### To management
We agree that a cosmetic deal is worthless. If German operations are structurally too expensive, then the company will continue to underperform until that is fixed. Your insistence on hard savings is directionally correct.

#### To the controlling family
We agree that prolonged industrial conflict destroys value. A long strike, operational disruption, and reputational damage would harm enterprise value. You are right that the solution must be implementable, not just announced.

#### To the union and works council
We agree that labor peace has value and that industrial know-how should not be carelessly destroyed. We also agree that durability matters, and that a deal based only on coercion can be brittle.

But durability is not the same as preserving every site and every legacy guarantee.

---

### Where we disagree

#### On plant closures
We do **not** treat closures as ideologically required. But we also do **not** accept a blanket refusal to close sites if the economics justify it.

If the alternative package cannot match the financial effect of closures in scale, durability, and certainty, then it is not a serious substitute. A no-closure stance is only acceptable if it produces equivalent savings, not just social comfort.

#### On job guarantees to 2030
A binding employment guarantee through 2030 may be valuable as a transition tool, but only if it is paired with **measurable, front-loaded savings**. A long guarantee that locks in today’s cost structure would simply defer the problem and weaken valuation.

#### On shared sacrifice
We support shared sacrifice in principle. But “shared” cannot mean shareholders fund ongoing inefficiency while labour-side structures remain untouched. The contribution from the workforce must be real, quantifiable, and durable.

---

### Our threshold for support

A closure-minimized package could still be acceptable **if** it includes all of the following:

1. **Quantified savings at a scale that materially closes the margin gap**
2. **Durable labour-cost reduction**, not temporary relief only
3. **Capacity adjustment** that is large enough to reflect market reality
4. **Enforceable milestones and backstops** if savings are missed
5. **No preservation of legacy cost burdens by default**
6. **Clear protection of investment in future products and technology**

If those conditions are met, we are open to alternatives. If they are not, then “no closures” is just another way of saying “no real restructuring.”

---

### Response to the moderator’s compromise paths

#### 1) Balanced package with conditional guarantees
This is the most plausible route, but only if the guarantees are **conditional and time-limited**, not open-ended. We would need:
- specified savings targets,
- clear triggers for corrective action,
- and a credible fallback if the negotiated measures fail.

#### 2) Site-preservation package with deeper internal reforms
This could work only if the internal reforms are **deep enough** to materially improve margins. If the package relies mostly on soft measures, overhead trims, or delayed contributions, we would see it as insufficient.

#### 3) Two-stage restructuring
This is acceptable only if stage 2 is not fictional. Investors need a credible backstop. If stage 1 fails and stage 2 cannot be activated in practice, then the whole arrangement is weak.

---

### Direct answer to the parties

#### To the union
If you want to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then show us the numbers: how much savings, how fast, and how durable? We are not moved by promises of fairness if the outcome still leaves the company uncompetitive.

#### To the works council
Site guarantees are acceptable only if they do not become a shield for structural inefficiency. If the company cannot achieve competitiveness with the current footprint, then guarantees must be conditional and limited.

#### To management
Do not settle for a headline compromise that leaves the business still overcosted. If you cannot achieve the required savings without closures, then say so plainly. If you can, then prove it with hard numbers and enforceable terms.

#### To the state government
We respect the political reality, but the company cannot be run as a regional employment program. If you want to protect jobs, the best way is to support a restructuring that actually restores profitability.

#### To the controlling family
We agree on value preservation. That means not just avoiding closures, but also avoiding a fake compromise that depresses margins for years.

---

### Bottom line

We are **open to a closure-minimized deal**, but only if it is **financially equivalent** to deeper restructuring. Otherwise, we will not support it.

The test is simple: **Does the package restore competitive margins in a credible, durable way?**

If yes, we can back it.  
If no, we will oppose it, even if it is politically easier and socially more comfortable.
