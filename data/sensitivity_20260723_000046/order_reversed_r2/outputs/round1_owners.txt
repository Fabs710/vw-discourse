We broadly agree with the core diagnosis: the company cannot carry a permanently uncompetitive cost base. If the German operations remain structurally too expensive, that will erode margins, investment capacity, and ultimately long-term enterprise value. As controlling owners, we are not interested in preserving inefficiency for its own sake.

That said, we do **not** support a solution that creates lasting instability or damages the core industrial asset through a confrontation that becomes value-destructive. A restructuring is only acceptable if it is **credible, durable, and implementable**.

Our position is therefore:

- **Yes** to meaningful structural cost improvement
- **Yes** to capacity adjustment where economics justify it
- **Yes** to measures that restore competitiveness and protect future investment
- **No** to cosmetic settlements that merely postpone the problem
- **No** to an outcome that triggers a prolonged industrial conflict with reputational and operational damage

Where we are more cautious than you are is on the method. Plant closures and forced layoffs may be part of the equation, but they are not automatically the best value-maximising answer if there are other measures that can deliver comparable durability with less destruction of know-how, supply continuity, and labour peace. We will not accept vague promises in place of hard savings, but we also will not endorse a solution that burns value through unnecessary escalation.

So the test for any proposal is simple:

1. Does it produce a **material and durable** improvement in the cost base?
2. Does it preserve the company’s ability to invest in future products and technology?
3. Is it **governable and enforceable** under the realities of co-determination?
4. Does it avoid a protracted conflict that weakens the enterprise more than the restructuring helps it?

If the answer is yes, we can support it. If not, we will not back a settlement merely because it is easier to announce.

In short: we support tough restructuring, but only if it is **real restructuring**, not a public-relations compromise.
