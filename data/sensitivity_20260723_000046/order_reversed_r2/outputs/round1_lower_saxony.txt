We agree on one central point: **this company needs a credible restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. If the German operations remain structurally uncompetitive, that threatens margins, investment capacity, and ultimately the long-term future of the enterprise. On that diagnosis, we are not far apart.

Where we differ is on the implied remedy and on what is politically and socially acceptable in a company of this size and significance.

### What we agree with
- **Durable competitiveness matters.**  
  The company cannot simply preserve all legacy structures and hope the market improves.
- **A real solution must be enforceable.**  
  Endless talks without implementation would be irresponsible.
- **Future investment must be protected.**  
  Without a viable industrial base, there is no future product strategy.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the idea that the only serious restructuring is one built around **plant closures and mass layoffs in Germany**. That would be a very high-risk path: economically, socially, and politically.

As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, we cannot support an outcome that:
- guts regional employment,
- removes productive capacity from the state,
- or undermines the company’s role as an industrial anchor in the region.

That is not a secondary concern for us; it is central to our responsibility.

### On the substance
If management’s target is lower cost and better competitiveness, then we expect it to **prove** that the same savings cannot be achieved through a negotiated package that preserves sites and avoids forced redundancies. That package should at least explore:
- capacity reductions short of closures,
- working-time and utilization adjustments,
- temporary or stepped labour-cost contributions,
- internal restructuring and redeployment,
- investment commitments tied to transformation and new products.

We are open to difficult measures. We are **not** open to a unilateral solution that treats regional sites and jobs as disposable.

### Our threshold
A settlement becomes acceptable only if it:
1. **preserves the core German sites and a substantial employment base,**
2. **delivers real, durable competitiveness gains,**
3. **keeps the company investable for the long term,** and
4. **respects co-determination and our statutory veto role.**

If the proposal instead relies on closures as the default answer, then we will oppose it. And if management or any shareholder bloc tries to force through a solution that sidelines the state’s role or destroys the regional economic anchor, that will not pass.

So our message is clear: **restructuring yes, but not at the price of deindustrialising our region or hollowing out the company’s social and governance fabric**. If there is a serious alternative that matches the financial effect, we are prepared to engage. If not, then the burden is on those advocating closures to justify why no less destructive path is possible.
