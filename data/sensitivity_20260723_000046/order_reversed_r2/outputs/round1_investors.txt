Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable return to competitive margins** and a **material reduction in the cost base**.

What matters to us as shareholders is not preserving every existing structure; it is restoring the company’s ability to earn an acceptable return on sales in a shrinking and highly competitive market. If German operations remain structurally too expensive, then that will continue to weigh on earnings, cash flow, and the valuation of the shares.

So we would argue for:

- **Decisive cost reduction**, not incremental gestures
- **Real capacity adjustment** if current output is above what the market can support
- **Durable labour-cost improvement**, including changes to legacy guarantees if needed
- **A credible implementation path with certainty**, rather than open-ended conflict
- **Protection of future investment capacity**, especially in technology and product development

What we would not support is:

- A **cosmetic compromise** that leaves the cost base uncompetitive
- Any arrangement that simply delays the problem while margins keep eroding
- A settlement that preserves too much overcapacity or too many legacy cost burdens without equivalent savings

We are open to alternatives to plant closures if they can **match the financial effect** in a believable way. But the burden of proof is on those alternatives: they must show the same scale and durability of savings, not just temporary peace.

In short: **we want the company to become competitively lean, not merely conflict-free**.
