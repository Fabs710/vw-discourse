We agree with the basic frame from the moderator and from capital markets: **this must be a real restructuring, not a symbolic one**. The company cannot carry a permanently uncompetitive German cost base and remain strong over the long term.

That said, we do **not** share the view that the only credible path is to push immediately toward closures and maximal confrontation.

### To the institutional investors
You are right on the objective: margin repair, durable cost reduction, and protection of future investment capacity. We support that.

Where we differ is on the means. If a no-closure or closure-minimized package can produce **equivalent, enforceable, durable savings**, then that is better value preservation than a plant-closing fight that damages operations, brand, and labour relations. We will not support a “soft” deal that leaves the business overcosted, but we also will not support a solution that is financially neat on paper and destructive in practice.

So the question is not whether savings are needed. The question is: **what combination of measures actually delivers them with the least long-run damage to the enterprise?**

### To management
Your diagnosis is plausible, and your insistence on seriousness is correct. But you still need to prove that the harshest measures are truly unavoidable.

We are not giving you a blank cheque for closures. If there are alternatives that can close the gap with comparable scale and certainty — capacity reductions, working-time changes, redeployment, labor-cost contributions, overhead cuts, procurement and footprint rationalisation — then those should be pursued first.

What we cannot accept is management treating closures as the default proof of resolve without exhausting credible alternatives. That would create avoidable conflict and put value at risk.

### To the union and works council
We take seriously your concern about social stability, know-how, and industrial peace. We also recognize that this company’s strength has long rested on a stable workforce and co-determination.

But I need to be equally direct: **a blanket no to closures, no to compulsory redundancies, and a hard guarantee to 2030 regardless of economics is not realistic** unless you are prepared to show the same financial effect from other measures.

A job-security promise is only defensible if it is paired with a credible, durable contribution to competitiveness. Otherwise it becomes a commitment to preserve structural weakness. That is not stewardship; it is delay.

If labour wants to avoid the most severe measures, then it must come forward with a package that is substantial enough to matter:
- meaningful labour-cost contributions,
- flexibility on working time and utilisation,
- voluntary rather than forced adjustment where possible,
- internal redeployment,
- and a willingness to accept some capacity reduction.

### To the regional state government
We understand your political responsibility and your statutory role. We also understand why you reject deindustrialisation as a headline outcome.

But we cannot treat regional employment protection as if it overrides the economic reality of the business. If the company remains uncompetitive, the region loses more over time than it would from a disciplined restructuring now. The goal should be to preserve a viable industrial base, not to freeze every existing structure in place.

That means we will support a solution that protects the core industrial footprint **if and only if** it delivers real competitiveness gains. We will not endorse an arrangement that blocks necessary change simply because it is politically easier.

### On the compromise paths
The moderator’s three routes are sensible, but our threshold is clear:

1. **Balanced package with conditional guarantees**  
   This is the most promising route, but only if the guarantees are limited, conditional, and tied to measurable performance. Open-ended guarantees that lock in today’s cost structure are not acceptable.

2. **Site-preservation package with deeper internal reforms**  
   Possible, but only if the internal reforms are deep enough to move the margin materially. Cosmetic savings or temporary concessions will not be enough.

3. **Two-stage restructuring**  
   This can work only if the second stage is credible and enforceable. A staged deal that never allows corrective action would simply postpone the problem.

### Our position, plainly stated
We support:
- a **material and durable** cost reduction,
- **capacity adjustment** where needed,
- a **shared contribution** from labour,
- and a restructuring that preserves the company’s ability to invest and compete.

We do **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a prolonged conflict that destroys value,
- or a settlement that preserves too much overcapacity and legacy burden.

We are open to a closure-minimized path **only if** it delivers equivalent results. If it does not, then we will not pretend otherwise.

If the parties want a serious basis for agreement, then management and labour need to put hard numbers on the table: not rhetoric, not slogans, but verifiable savings, timing, and enforceability. Until then, we will not commit the family holding to a solution that protects appearances more than enterprise value.
