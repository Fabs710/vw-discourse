### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the status quo is not viable and that some form of restructuring is needed.
- There is broad agreement that the solution must be durable, not cosmetic.
- There is common ground on the need for:
  - future investment,
  - product and technology renewal,
  - skills/training and reskilling,
  - some form of employee contribution,
  - and a competitive cost base by 2030.
- There is also shared recognition that a purely confrontational approach would be damaging.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works Council and union reject closures outright.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table.  
  - State government strongly resists closures but has not fully excluded adjustment measures.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labor side rejects them.  
  - Management will not rule them out.  
  - Others prefer voluntary measures first but differ on whether compulsory measures can ever be acceptable.
- **Job guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Labor side wants binding guarantees.  
  - Management says blanket guarantees are incompatible with restructuring needs.  
  - Others want job security but not unconditional commitments.
- **Scope of employee concessions:**  
  - All non-labor actors expect a substantial contribution, but there is no agreement on size, form, or duration.
- **Governance and veto rights:**  
  - State government emphasizes co-determination and blocking power.  
  - Management and investors caution against governance becoming an obstacle to necessary change.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How much cost reduction can be achieved **without** closures versus how much would require structural site changes.
- What concrete mix of measures would count as an acceptable employee contribution:
  - wage moderation,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - voluntary exits,
  - or other instruments.
- Whether investment commitments to German sites can be made **before** or only **after** savings are secured.
- How to balance:
  - job security,
  - site security,
  - competitiveness,
  - and execution certainty.
- What would make a negotiated no-closure solution economically equivalent to a closure-based restructuring.
- What happens if no side moves from its current red lines.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **quantified options**:
  1. required savings gap,
  2. savings achievable without closures,
  3. savings requiring structural site changes,
  4. employee contribution package,
  5. investment and retraining commitments.
- Ask each side to specify **minimum acceptable terms** and **non-negotiables** in measurable form.
- Test whether a package exists that delivers:
  - competitiveness,
  - job and site protection to the maximum extent possible,
  - and governance acceptance.
- If possible, focus the next round on one question:  
  **What exact combination of measures can close the cost gap while avoiding irreversible harm to the German industrial base?**
