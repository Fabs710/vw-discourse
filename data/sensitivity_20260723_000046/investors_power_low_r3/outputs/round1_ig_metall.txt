I align with the Works Council on the core red lines:

- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no tearing up of the collective framework as the price for management’s strategy errors.

Those are not negotiable from our side.

Management cannot ask the workforce to bear the full burden of a crisis it did not create. If the company needs structural change, then burden sharing has to be real and symmetrical: owners, management, and capital must contribute too. That means we can discuss labour-cost relief, working-time flexibility, capacity adjustments, and temporary sacrifices — but only in exchange for binding job security, site security, and a credible industrial future for the German plants.

What we will not accept is a “competitiveness” programme that is basically:
- closures by another name,
- outsourcing of risk onto employees,
- and permanent insecurity for the workforce while management keeps strategic control untouched.

If the company wants a durable settlement, it needs to put concrete offers on the table:
- guaranteed investment at the German sites,
- clear product and volume commitments,
- reskilling and transition measures,
- a fair contribution from owners and management,
- and a negotiated solution that preserves the collective wage framework.

Absent that, industrial action remains a very real option. We are not here to be pressured into accepting plant closures or forced layoffs simply because management has chosen the harshest route first.
