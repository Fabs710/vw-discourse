## 1) What has been agreed
Across all sides, there is broad agreement that:
- the current situation is not sustainable,
- the company needs durable restructuring, not a cosmetic deal,
- future investment, products, and skills are essential,
- employee contributions will be required,
- and a prolonged conflict would harm the company.

There is also shared recognition that any solution must be economically credible and implementable.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issue is whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies are truly necessary.

- **Labor side**: says closures and forced layoffs are not justified, and that alternatives have not been fully tested or quantified.
- **Management / investors / family**: say the business needs real, durable cost and capacity reduction, and that closure-free options have not yet been shown to close the gap.
- **State government**: supports restructuring but wants closures and forced redundancies avoided unless clearly unavoidable.

So the dispute is not about whether change is needed, but about the necessity and extent of irreversible measures.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The key trade-offs still lacking a shared, quantified basis are:
- how much savings can be achieved through voluntary exits, redeployment, wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures,
- how much capacity reduction can be achieved without plant closures,
- what residual cost gap would remain after those measures,
- whether that residual gap is large enough to require closures or forced redundancies,
- and what binding job, site, investment, and product commitments would be exchanged for employee concessions.

In short: the parties are still arguing principles, but not yet agreeing on the numbers or the package structure.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to a common factual test:
- define the exact savings gap,
- quantify each alternative measure,
- compare closure-based and closure-free scenarios,
- and identify which commitments would make a negotiated settlement credible.

A useful next step would be a side-by-side package comparison with numbers, timing, and binding conditions, so the discussion can shift from red lines to verifiable trade-offs.
