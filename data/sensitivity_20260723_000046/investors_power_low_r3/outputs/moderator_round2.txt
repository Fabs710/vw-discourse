## Moderator Summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad alignment that:
- the status quo is not viable,
- the company needs a durable restructuring,
- future investment, product renewal, and skills development are essential,
- a cosmetic compromise would not be sufficient,
- and prolonged conflict would damage value for all sides.

There is also shared recognition that any employee contribution must be linked to credible commitments on investment, training, and future industrial prospects.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures in Germany**: labor-side actors and the state oppose them as a baseline; management, the family holding, and investors say capacity reduction may be necessary and closures cannot be ruled out.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: labor rejects them entirely; management and ownership do not accept an absolute prohibition.
- **Job guarantees through 2030**: labor and the state want binding protection; management and ownership say that is incompatible with the required restructuring scale.
- **Whether alternatives are sufficient**: labor says closures and forced layoffs have not been proven necessary; management and investors say alternatives have not yet shown equivalent, durable savings and capacity reduction.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- the quantified savings gap and how large it really is,
- how much of that gap can be closed by voluntary measures, working-time changes, wage moderation, redeployment, and reskilling,
- what level of capacity reduction is required and whether it can happen without closures,
- what concrete guarantees or commitments would justify employee concessions,
- and how to balance regional industrial protection against competitiveness and execution certainty.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to numbers. Each side should bring a quantified proposal or counterproposal showing:
- expected savings,
- capacity effects,
- timeline,
- and the corresponding guarantees or protections sought in return.

The key question to answer next is: **what package closes the cost and capacity gap in a durable way while avoiding unnecessary destruction of jobs, sites, or enterprise value?**
