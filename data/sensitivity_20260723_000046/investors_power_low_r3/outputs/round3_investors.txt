We see the positions clearly. Our view remains unchanged: **the company needs a credible, durable path back to competitive margins, not a symbolic settlement that preserves structural overcapacity**.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and to some extent the state government that:
- the status quo is not viable,
- the German cost base is too high,
- the company needs durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix,
- and prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.

We also agree with the labor side on one important point: **a strike spiral and governance deadlock would be expensive and value-destructive**. That is not in shareholders’ interest either.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Labor’s “no closures / no compulsory redundancies / guarantees through 2030” line is not enough
We do **not** accept that as a viable end-state unless it is backed by a package that is economically equivalent to structural change.

Right now, we have not seen proof that:
- every German plant can remain open,
- compulsory reductions can be ruled out entirely,
- and the company can still reach a competitive cost base by 2030.

If the alternative package does not close the gap, then it is not a solution; it is delay.

### 2) Management still has not quantified necessity
At the same time, we do **not** support management relying on closures as a leverage tool without a transparent case.

If plant closures are being presented as necessary, then management should show:
- the exact margin and savings gap,
- what each alternative measure delivers,
- what residual gap remains,
- and why that residual gap cannot be closed without closures or some deeper structural step.

Without that, closure rhetoric is not credible enough for capital markets support.

### 3) The state’s caution is understandable, but a veto cannot protect an uncompetitive structure
We respect the state’s concern for the regional industrial base. But shareholders cannot finance a permanently impaired margin structure.

If the state wants to defend jobs over the medium term, it needs to back a package that actually restores profitability and investment capacity. Blocking all meaningful adjustment would only postpone value destruction.

### 4) The family’s stewardship test must be about outcomes, not symbolism
We broadly agree with the family that long-term value matters more than short-term optics. But stewardship is not preserving every site at any price.

Stewardship means:
- a viable cost base,
- enough cash to invest,
- a product portfolio that can compete,
- and a settlement that does not leave the company structurally weaker.

## What would move us
We can support a negotiated no-closure solution **only if** it is truly equivalent in economics and durability. That means we would need to see:

1. **Material and durable labor-cost reductions**
   - wage moderation,
   - bonus restraint,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - and other recurring cost actions.

2. **Real capacity reduction**
   - not just softer utilization,
   - but actual output and fixed-cost reduction.

3. **Enforceable employee contribution**
   - with clear timing and measurable savings.

4. **A credible investment and product plan**
   - so any concessions are tied to future competitiveness, not just near-term pain.

5. **Execution certainty**
   - a deal that lowers the risk of prolonged strikes, legal conflict, and governance paralysis.

## What is unacceptable to us
We will **not** support an outcome that:
- leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- preserves overcapacity under a softer label,
- or keeps margins below a credible return threshold.

We are also not asking for “efficiency” in the abstract. We are asking for a package that improves **risk-adjusted shareholder value**.

## Our practical stance
So our position is:

- **Open to avoiding plant closures** if the savings and capacity effects are genuinely equivalent and durable.
- **Not open** to a cosmetic compromise that protects every current site while leaving the business weak.
- **Not open** to a plan that relies on repeated conflict instead of a durable settlement.
- **Not open** to blank-check guarantees through 2030 if the economics do not support them.

## What we want next
We want the parties to stop arguing in slogans and put numbers on the table:
- quantified savings from each measure,
- residual gap,
- timing,
- and the exact trade-off for any job or site protection.

If labor can show a closure-free package that genuinely restores competitiveness, we will support it.  
If management cannot show that closures are truly necessary, it should stop using them as a negotiating threat.  
But if neither side can produce an economically credible path, then we will side with the measures that most clearly restore margins and capital discipline.

That is the only standard that matters to us.
