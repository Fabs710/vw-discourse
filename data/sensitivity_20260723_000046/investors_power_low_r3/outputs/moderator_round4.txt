## Core impasse

The discourse has now converged on one central blockage:

**There is no shared, quantified basis for deciding whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies are truly necessary.**

More specifically, the parties are split on three linked issues:

1. **Necessity vs. preference**
   - Labor and the state treat closures/forced layoffs as unproven and therefore unacceptable.
   - Management, the family, and investors treat them as potentially necessary unless an alternative is shown to close the gap.

2. **Conditions for labor concessions**
   - Labor conditions any concession on binding guarantees: no closures, no forced redundancies, job/site security, and investment commitments.
   - Management and investors want flexibility and reject blanket guarantees that could block restructuring.

3. **Evidence standard**
   - Everyone is asking for numbers, but no side has yet tabled a joint, side-by-side model showing:
     - the exact savings gap,
     - what each alternative measure contributes,
     - the residual gap,
     - and which measures are unavoidable.

So the impasse is not just “disagreement on outcomes.” It is **disagreement on the test that would justify the outcome**.

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: Phased restructuring with a proof threshold
A two-stage arrangement could be created:

- **Stage A:** immediate labor contributions are implemented for a fixed period:
  - temporary wage moderation
  - bonus restraint
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exit programs
  - redeployment and retraining
  - productivity measures

- **Stage B:** closures and compulsory redundancies are **not permitted during Stage A**, unless a jointly audited calculation shows a defined residual gap remains after these measures.

This bridges:
- labor’s demand for no premature irreversible cuts,
- and management/investors’ demand for a credible path to competitiveness.

### Proposal 2: Conditional job and site security with review triggers
A compromise framework could include:

- **No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined interim period**
- **Binding investment and product commitments**
- **A joint review mechanism at set milestones**
- If targets are not met, then specific structural measures may be reopened for negotiation

This does not promise permanent immunity, but it gives the workforce a real period of security in exchange for measurable contributions.

### Proposal 3: Narrowly tailored structural adjustment instead of broad closure authority
Instead of blanket closure authority, the parties could agree to:

- preserve all German sites initially,
- reduce capacity through non-closure tools first,
- and if a residual gap remains, limit any structural adjustment to:
  - one site,
  - one line,
  - or one specific function,
  rather than opening the door to broad plant-closure pressure.

This may help bridge:
- labor’s rejection of general closure threats,
- and management’s insistence on actual capacity reduction.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to publishing a jointly audited, site-by-site savings model showing the exact gap after each alternative measure, and will you suspend any closure decision until that model is reviewed?**

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**If the numbers show that a smaller residual gap remains after all voluntary and flexibility measures, what specific additional instrument would you accept short of closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited package with wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexibility in exchange for binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitments only during the interim phase?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you use your blocking power only if management refuses a quantified comparison, or also if the comparison is produced and still shows a residual gap?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What would you regard as sufficient evidence that closures are not necessary, and are you prepared to accept a no-closure settlement if that evidence is demonstrated?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**If a closure-free package delivers recurring savings, capacity reduction, and execution certainty, will you support it even if the adjustment mechanism is slower than you would prefer?**
