As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden, and the company cannot solve its structural problems by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and hollowing out the German sites that built this company. A restructuring that relies on shutting plants and forcing people out is neither fair nor sustainable.

What we are prepared to discuss is a serious alternative that secures:
- every German plant,
- binding job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- a real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the transformation,
- and a viable industrial plan that protects future investment, products, and skills.

If management wants contributions from employees, then it must also offer credible commitments in return: investment at the sites, clear product allocation, training and reskilling, and a fair burden-sharing model. But any “solution” that starts with closures, mass cuts, or tearing up protections is unacceptable to us.

We are not here to rubber-stamp a fait accompli. If needed, we will resist with the full strength of the workforce and the rights co-determination gives us.
