I will respond clearly.

I agree with management on one point: the status quo is not viable. The company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix. I also agree that future investment, product renewal, and a competitive cost base are essential.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table as the default solution.

## To management
You have not yet shown that closures are **necessary** rather than merely the easiest way to force a result. That distinction matters.

If you want the state’s support, you need to demonstrate:
- how much of the cost gap can be closed **without** site closures,
- what exact savings closures would deliver beyond other measures,
- why working-time changes, targeted wage moderation, redeployment, and voluntary exits are insufficient,
- and what investment you are committing to the German sites in return.

Right now, you are asking for irreversible damage to our regional industrial base before proving that all reversible options have failed. That is not acceptable.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your insistence that this cannot be a one-sided burden shift onto employees. You are right that employees should not “pay first and trust later.”

I also agree that a negotiated solution must include:
- meaningful employee contribution,
- training and reskilling,
- investment commitments,
- and real industrial prospects for the German sites.

Where I stop is at any assumption that “no closures, no forced redundancies” is enough by itself. It is only credible if the alternative package is genuinely quantified and durable. If the employee side is saying “we can contribute,” then that contribution has to be substantial enough to close the gap. Otherwise, we are just postponing the conflict.

That said, I am **not** prepared to accept plant closures as the starting point, and I am **not** prepared to accept a model where the state and workforce are presented with a fait accompli.

## To the controlling family
You speak the language of stewardship, and I welcome that. Stewardship means protecting enterprise value over the long term, not just imposing the harshest possible adjustment.

But stewardship also means preserving the company’s legitimacy in its home region. A restructuring that guts employment, alienates the workforce, and sidelines co-determination would be a very risky way to “restore value.” It may look decisive on paper and create deeper damage in reality.

So yes, I support the need for durable savings. No, I do not support treating closures as the unavoidable baseline before alternatives have been fully tested.

## To investors
You are right that a cosmetic settlement is not enough. I share that concern.

But you should also be clear-eyed about the real costs of a closure-driven confrontation:
- strike risk,
- legal blockages,
- governance deadlock,
- reputational harm,
- and lasting damage to the company’s role as a regional anchor.

From a shareholder perspective, a negotiated settlement that preserves sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness can be better than a “clean” closure plan that destroys trust and triggers prolonged conflict. That is especially true in a company with this governance structure.

## My position
The state government can support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:
1. **Protection of German employment to the maximum extent possible**
2. **No plant closures unless absolutely unavoidable and demonstrably necessary**
3. **No compulsory redundancies if negotiated instruments can achieve the same aim**
4. **A serious, quantified employee contribution**
5. **Binding investment, product, and training commitments**
6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s legitimate veto role**

If management can present a package that genuinely closes the cost gap without destroying the regional industrial base, I am ready to engage constructively. If the answer is simply “close plants and cut deeply because that is faster,” then I will say plainly: that path will face resistance from us.

## What I want next
I want a **quantified alternative package** on the table:
- savings from working-time flexibility,
- savings from wage moderation and bonus restraint,
- savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- capacity adjustments that do not involve closures,
- and the investment needed to support that transition.

If that package proves insufficient, then we can discuss where the line is. But we are not there yet, and I will not concede closures simply because they are being repeated often.

I am prepared to broker a serious settlement. I am not prepared to sacrifice the company’s regional role and the rights of the state and workforce as the price of speed.
