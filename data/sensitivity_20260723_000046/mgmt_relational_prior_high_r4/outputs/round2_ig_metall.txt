To the Works Council, management, owners, state government, and investors:

We are at the point where slogans are no longer enough. We need a package that is **measurable, fair, and enforceable**. Let me be equally clear: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no one-sided wage sacrifice**. That remains my position.

## Where I agree
I agree with the broad diagnosis that the company needs a durable restructuring:
- the market has changed,
- the German cost base is under pressure,
- and simply postponing adjustment would be irresponsible.

I also agree that a settlement can and should include:
- capacity rationalization,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and a **time-limited employee contribution** if it protects sites and jobs.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with management, investors, and—partly—with the owners when they imply that **closures and layoffs are the default credible option**.

That is not “economic realism”; that is choosing the heaviest burden for workers first.

If the company wants competitiveness, then it must be achieved through a **shared-sacrifice package**, not by forcing the workforce to absorb the crisis alone.

## On management’s position
Management says the cost base is too high and that capacity must fall. Fine. But you have not shown that this must mean:
- plant closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or dismantling the collective wage framework.

If you want our cooperation, show us a restructuring that is:
1. **quantified**,
2. **durable**,
3. **socially balanced**,
4. and **compatible with job and site security**.

A plan that depends on breaking the workforce compact will trigger resistance, not support.

## On the works council’s position
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no hollowing out of job security.

But I want to be explicit: if we talk about employee contributions, they must remain **bounded and reversible**. That means:
- limited in time,
- tied to binding investment and utilization commitments,
- and matched by contributions from management and owners.

I will not accept a hidden permanent wage cut under another name.

## On the state government
Your point about regional industry matters is valid, and your blocking role is not symbolic. But I need consistency: if closures are rejected, then the political side must also support a **real alternative path** with serious industrial commitments, not just vetoes.

That means:
- protection of the core site network,
- binding product allocation,
- training and qualification,
- and investment that keeps the German plants viable.

## On the controlling family
You are right that the company cannot afford endless delay. But ownership responsibility also means not forcing a solution that is industrially destructive.

If the owners want loyalty from the workforce, then they must accept that loyalty is earned through **fair burden-sharing**, not demanded through threats of closures.

## On investors
You keep asking for quantified savings. That is fair. Then let us quantify the alternative properly:
- reduced capacity through working-time and production shifts,
- wage restraint that is temporary and negotiated,
- forgone bonuses or special payments,
- internal redeployment,
- retirement and attrition-based reductions,
- and site utilization through product commitments.

If those measures do not add up, say so with numbers. But do not pretend that “closures” are automatically the superior economic answer when they carry huge conflict and execution risk.

## My bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate a **serious collective-bargaining contribution** if, and only if, it is linked to:
- **no German plant closures**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **binding job security through 2030**,
- **fair participation by management and owners**,
- and **credible investment and product commitments**.

If those conditions are not met, then there is no durable settlement from the union side.

## What I propose as the next step
We need a **jointly quantified alternative package** on the table, compared on the same basis as management’s plan:

- savings magnitude,
- timing,
- workforce effects,
- site security,
- and enforceability.

If management is serious, bring the numbers. If not, then we should be honest that this is not a negotiation about competitiveness, but about who pays for it.
