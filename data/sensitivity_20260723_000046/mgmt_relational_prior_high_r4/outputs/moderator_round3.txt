## Core impasse

The main impasse is no longer about whether the company needs change. It is about **the form of change**:

1. **Management / investors / family** want a restructuring that is clearly large enough to restore competitiveness and are keeping **closures and deeper reductions** on the table.
2. **Works council / union** will only accept a package that preserves **all German plants, avoids compulsory redundancies, and protects job-security and co-determination through 2030**.
3. **State government** wants to protect the regional industrial base but will not endorse a hollowed-out compromise.
4. The **expert input** suggests that a non-closure or hybrid route can deliver **meaningful** savings, but it is disputed whether it can deliver **enough** savings to satisfy the financial side.

So the deadlock is:  
**How to secure durable cost and capacity reduction without triggering plant closures or forced redundancies.**

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridging compromises

### Proposal 1: “Hard hybrid” package with quantified non-closure capacity cuts
A deal built around:
- no German plant closures for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- real capacity reduction through shift cuts, model transfers, and line simplification,
- voluntary exits and attrition only,
- a time-limited employee contribution,
- binding investment and product commitments for German sites,
- and a joint verification mechanism.

**Purpose:**  
To test whether a non-closure path can close most of the competitiveness gap while preserving the site network.

---

### Proposal 2: Phased restructuring with mutual trigger clauses
A staged arrangement in which:
- employee contributions and flexibility measures begin immediately,
- management receives defined savings and capacity changes in phase one,
- the company must meet binding investment, utilization, and product milestones,
- if management misses those milestones, employee contributions expire or are rolled back,
- if targets are met, the labor side maintains the agreed flexibility.

**Purpose:**  
To address the trust problem by making concessions **reciprocal and reversible**, rather than one-sided.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-role differentiation with explicit viability guarantees
A network redesign in which:
- every German plant remains open,
- each site receives a clearly defined industrial role,
- duplicated functions are removed,
- some sites specialize more deeply,
- and each site is paired with a viability plan: products, investment, and workforce development.

**Purpose:**  
To reduce complexity and duplication without formally closing plants, while trying to prevent “token shell” operations.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact annual recurring savings do you require, and how much of that number would you accept being delivered through a no-closure hybrid package before you conclude that closures are unavoidable?**

### To the works council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table, what specific capacity reductions or working-time adjustments are you prepared to accept as sufficient to make the package economically credible?**

### To the union
**What precise form and duration of employee contribution would you accept if it were tied to binding investment, product allocation, and a hard rollback clause if commitments are missed?**

### To the state government
**What conditions would you require for your blocking role to support a restructuring package that preserves all sites but still reduces capacity and cost materially?**

### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to back a hybrid compromise even if it yields less than the maximum theoretical savings, provided it is durable, enforceable, and reduces conflict risk?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you support a hybrid package if the net present value is somewhat below a closure plan but the execution risk, strike risk, and political risk are materially lower?**
