## To the Works Council
I understand your red lines. I also understand why you are trying to anchor the discussion around “no closures, no redundancies, job security through 2030.” But I need to be equally clear: **that package is not acceptable as a starting point for management if it prevents the structural reset the business needs**.

You keep treating closures as a choice management prefers for convenience. That is not the reality. The reality is:
- German factory costs are materially above target,
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we preserve the full footprint and cost structure, we are not restructuring — we are postponing the problem.

You say capacity can be reduced through shift changes, model transfers, voluntary exits, and internal redeployment. Fine. Those tools are part of the answer. But they are **not automatically sufficient**. The expert has already said that non-closure measures can help, but often do **not** fully replicate the structural savings of closures or deep consolidation when overcapacity is structural.

So my position remains:
- **No blanket guarantee that every German plant stays open**
- **No acceptance of a package that rules out compulsory reductions entirely**
- **No 2030 job-security pledge if it blocks the cost reset**

If you want to avoid closures, then you must bring a package that is **quantified and structurally equivalent**, not merely politically more comfortable.

## To the Union
You are right about one thing: employee contributions must not become a permanent hidden wage cut. I agree with that. But that does **not** mean the contribution can be so small or so temporary that it leaves the business uncompetitive.

I am prepared to accept a time-limited employee contribution only if it is:
- material,
- enforceable,
- and part of a broader package that actually reduces the cost base.

What I will not accept is a model where:
- wages are protected,
- sites are protected,
- headcount is protected,
- and the company is expected to become competitive by magic.

That is not a restructuring. That is wishful thinking.

You also say closures destroy trust and skills. Of course they do. That is why they are a serious option, not a casual one. But we cannot run the company on the basis that any difficult adjustment is off-limits because it is unpleasant. If we do that, we lose competitiveness first and jobs later.

## To the Regional State Government
I hear your emphasis on regional value and legitimacy. I do not dismiss it. But I do need to push back on the idea that your blocking role should effectively freeze the industrial structure.

Protecting the region does **not** mean preserving every current site configuration forever. It means preserving a **viable industrial base**. That requires hard choices.

If you support a package that leaves the company structurally weak, you are not protecting the region — you are delaying a larger problem.

So yes, I am open to a settlement that preserves important German production, but only if it:
- removes real overcapacity,
- lowers labor cost materially,
- and improves medium-term margins.

Without that, we are just defending the present at the expense of the future.

## To the Controlling Family
I agree that stewardship matters. But stewardship means more than avoiding conflict. It means ensuring the enterprise can fund its future.

We need to finance:
- EV transition,
- software,
- future product cycles,
- and industrial investment to 2030.

That will not happen if the brand remains trapped in an uncompetitive cost structure. So I reject any implication that structural cuts are somehow optional because they are unpopular.

You say you want long-term value. Then support a package that creates it. Not one that merely preserves the status quo while margins continue to underperform.

## To Institutional Investors
You are right to demand quantified, enforceable savings. I support that. But I want to be equally explicit: **if the numbers show that a non-closure package does not close the competitiveness gap, then I will not pretend otherwise**.

I will not sign off on a cosmetic compromise just because it avoids controversy.

What I need is:
1. **annual recurring savings**
2. **headcount reduction**
3. **capacity reduction**
4. **timing**
5. **one-time costs**
6. **enforceability**

If a hybrid or non-closure package truly delivers equivalent structural effect, then it is worth considering. If it does not, then closures remain on the table because they are economically necessary.

## On the moderator’s compromise paths
I appreciate the attempt to structure the debate, but I need to be blunt:

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with site guarantees
This is only acceptable if the “guarantees” do not become a permanent shield against real adjustment. A hybrid is not enough if it merely preserves all sites and trims at the edges.

### 2) Phased agreement with trigger-based review
This can be useful only if the triggers are hard and the consequences are real. I will not accept a phased deal that simply delays the same confrontation until later, with management unable to act when targets are missed.

### 3) Differentiated site roles
This is economically plausible, but only if the differentiated sites still contribute to a lower-cost, higher-productivity network. “Specialization” that just disguises underutilization is not enough.

## My bottom line
I am not moving to the employee-side red lines.

I remain open to:
- a hybrid package,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a time-limited employee contribution,

but only if they produce **real, durable, structural cost and capacity reduction**.

What I cannot accept is:
- no closures as a blanket rule,
- no compulsory reductions in any form,
- and job security through 2030 if that freezes the structure in place.

That would leave the company uncompetitive, underfunded, and unable to invest for the future. And that outcome is not acceptable.
