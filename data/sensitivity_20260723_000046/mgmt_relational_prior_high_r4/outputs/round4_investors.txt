We are prepared to be blunt.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, and the experts on the **core diagnosis**:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and the current margin performance is not good enough.

We also agree with the employee side on one important point:
- a prolonged strike, or a settlement that simply postpones the issue, would destroy value.

That matters to us as shareholders. We are not indifferent to conflict costs.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support a cosmetic compromise that keeps the cost structure essentially intact while calling it a restructuring.

Equally, we do **not** support a closure-first outcome unless management can show that the net, risk-adjusted value case is clearly superior after:
- transition costs,
- strike risk,
- political veto risk,
- supply disruption,
- and execution delay.

So our position is not “closures at any price.” It is also not “no closures no matter what.”  
Our test is simpler: **what package credibly restores competitive margins on a durable basis?**

## To management
You still owe the market a clear, quantified case.

“Structural reset” is not enough. We need:
- annual recurring savings,
- one-time costs,
- timing,
- headcount effect,
- capacity effect,
- and the expected return-on-sales improvement.

Without that, the debate remains rhetorical. We would also caution against treating closures as a default instrument unless you can show that a hard hybrid cannot close the gap.

## To the works council and union
We understand your red lines, but we do **not** accept them as the starting constraint if they prevent a credible competitiveness reset.

A package that says:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- job security through 2030,
- and no meaningful labor-cost relief

would leave the company structurally impaired. That is unacceptable to us.

At the same time, if you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be **real**, not symbolic:
- material capacity reduction,
- genuine overhead cuts,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

If those are not sufficient, then the non-closure route does not solve the problem.

## To the regional state government
We support protecting the regional industrial base, but not by freezing an uncompetitive structure in place.

The state’s role should be to ensure that any deal preserves **viable** German operations and future investment, not to guarantee every existing configuration indefinitely. A regional anchor is only valuable if it remains economically viable.

## To the controlling family
We agree that long-term value matters. But stewardship also means not allowing sentiment or avoidance of conflict to substitute for restructuring.

If a hard hybrid is sufficient, support it. If it is not, do not block deeper action just because it is politically difficult. The equity value problem does not disappear because the decision is uncomfortable.

## Our position on the compromise paths
### 1) Hard hybrid
This is the only non-closure route we would consider seriously, and only if it delivers:
- real recurring savings,
- real capacity reduction,
- verifiable workforce adjustment,
- and binding commitments on investment and product allocation.

### 2) Phased trigger model
Potentially useful, but only if the milestones are hard and the consequences are real. We do not want a structure that simply defers the same problem into a later round.

### 3) Site-role differentiation
Acceptable only if it truly improves productivity and does not create slow-motion decline.

## What would move us
We would support a hybrid or non-closure package if it credibly demonstrates:
1. durable cost reduction
2. meaningful capacity rationalization
3. lower breakeven volume
4. improved return on sales
5. manageable execution risk
6. enforceable commitments

If that cannot be shown, then management should stop presenting the non-closure path as sufficient.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- or a closure-driven plan whose transition risk overwhelms the savings.

## Bottom line
Our support is conditional and narrow:
- **Yes** to a tough, quantified restructuring that restores competitiveness.
- **Yes** to a hybrid if it is genuinely hard and durable.
- **No** to a weak compromise.
- **No** to a deal that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the parties want capital-market backing, bring us a package that clearly shows the path back to competitive margins. Without that, we would rather withhold support than endorse a structurally weak outcome.
