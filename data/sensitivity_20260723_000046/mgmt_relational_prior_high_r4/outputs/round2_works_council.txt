Management, owners, and investors are still treating workers as the adjustable part of the business model. That is exactly what we will not accept.

Let me respond point by point.

### To management
You say the cost base is too high and capacity too large. We do not deny the company faces pressure. What we reject is your conclusion that the only serious answer is closures, forced reductions, and the dismantling of job security.

You have not demonstrated that every German plant must stay open only if workers take a one-sided hit. That is a choice, not a law of nature. If you want our cooperation, then you need to put forward a restructuring that:
- keeps every German plant open,
- excludes compulsory redundancies,
- preserves the existing job-security framework through 2030,
- and gives the workforce a genuine seat at the table in shaping the future product and site plan.

You have not met that standard yet.

### To the works council’s own baseline
I will be equally clear: capacity reductions, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, and temporary measures are discussable **only if** they are used to protect sites and jobs, not as a back door to closures or mass exits. If you use “flexibility” as a euphemism for weakening protections, we will oppose it.

### To the union
We are aligned on the red lines. But “bounded employee contribution” cannot become a permanent erosion of the collective framework. If there is any contribution at all, it must be:
- strictly time-limited,
- tied to binding site guarantees,
- matched by management and owner contributions,
- and reversible if the company fails to deliver investment and utilization commitments.

No blank cheque. No open-ended sacrifice.

### To the state government
You say you do not support closures, and that is correct. But then you must be consistent: the blocking-minority role and co-determination are not ornamental. They exist to prevent exactly this kind of one-sided restructuring. If the company wants state support, it must prove that the regional industrial base is being preserved, not reduced in the name of abstract “competitiveness.”

### To the controlling family
You speak about governance stability and long-term value. Then do not destabilize the industrial base to chase a short-term margin fix. A family owner should understand that breaking the workforce compact destroys trust for years and invites industrial conflict that is far more expensive than negotiated adjustment.

### To investors
You keep repeating “quantified savings” as though only one model counts. Fine: quantify savings from alternatives too. Show us the numbers on:
- capacity rationalization without closures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary cost measures,
- and utilization through new products and investment.

If the argument is serious, then it should withstand comparison. But until then, do not tell us that closures are somehow the rational default.

### Our position in this round
We are prepared to discuss a **non-closure restructuring package** with:
- capacity adjustments,
- flexibility measures,
- qualification and redeployment,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- and binding investment and product commitments.

But there are three conditions that are non-negotiable:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job-security guarantees through 2030**

If management cannot live within those conditions, then there is no basis for a settlement from our side. We will not agree to a deal that puts plants and livelihoods on the line while pretending that “competitiveness” requires sacrificial jobs.

If management wants credibility, bring a quantified alternative that preserves the German site network. Until then, we stand by our resistance.
