I agree with the Works Council on the core red lines: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

Management cannot put the entire burden of a structural crisis onto the workforce and then call that “competitiveness.” If the company needs a durable reset, then the pain must be **shared** and the solution must be **reversible, socially responsible, and industrially credible**.

Where I am prepared to engage constructively is here:

- **capacity reductions** instead of closures,
- **working-time adjustments** and flexible models,
- **temporary or targeted labour-cost contributions**,
- **foregoing bonuses or variable pay components**,
- **internal redeployment and qualification**,
- and a **binding investment and product commitment** for the German sites.

But let me be equally clear where we disagree:

- **Plant closures are not a negotiation baseline.**
- **Forced layoffs are not acceptable.**
- **Permanent wage cuts that shift the full adjustment burden onto employees are not acceptable.**
- **Any “restructuring” that only buys management a cost reduction while leaving workers with insecurity and no future is unacceptable.**

If management and the owners want our cooperation, then they must come with a package that includes:

1. **guaranteed job security** for the German sites,
2. **no compulsory redundancies**,
3. **site protection**,
4. **fair contributions from management and owners as well**,
5. **a limited and clearly bounded employee contribution**, tied to concrete guarantees,
6. **investment in future products and plant utilization**.

We have already signaled openness to difficult measures. That is not weakness; it is responsibility. But responsibility is **not** one-sided sacrifice. If the company insists on closures or mass layoffs, then it should expect escalating resistance, including industrial action.
