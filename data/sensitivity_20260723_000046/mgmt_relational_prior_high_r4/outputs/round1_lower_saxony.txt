I take note of management’s economic pressure, and I do not dismiss the need for a serious restructuring. The company has to remain competitive, and the German sites cannot be insulated from reality.

But I must be equally clear: **plant closures in Germany are not a acceptable starting point**, and I will not support a solution that permanently damages the regional industrial base or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role. That would be politically and economically irresponsible, and it would ignore the company’s role as a regional anchor.

Where I agree:
- The cost base must come down.
- Capacity has to be addressed.
- The future needs investment, not just cuts.
- A negotiated contribution from the workforce is not off the table.

Where I disagree:
- **Closure is not the only credible path.**
- **Mass layoffs are not an acceptable default.**
- **A restructuring that treats jobs as disposable would be too risky and too one-sided.**

If management wants my support, then the burden is on it to prove that the same structural effect cannot be achieved through a more balanced package: capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, working-time measures, targeted cost savings, and a time-limited employee contribution, all tied to binding commitments on sites, investment, and long-term utilization.

I am prepared to consider difficult measures, but only if they meet three conditions:
1. **No gutting of regional employment**
2. **No erosion of co-determination and veto rights**
3. **A durable industrial strategy, not a short-term accounting fix**

To the works council and union: I share your core concern that the workforce must not carry the full burden. That is the right principle. But I also think we should not close the door entirely on a negotiated contribution if it genuinely helps preserve sites and secure future investment. That flexibility may be necessary.

To management: bring forward a package that is economically credible **without** making closures the first and only instrument. If the package still depends on shutting German plants and cutting jobs on a large scale, then I will not support it.
