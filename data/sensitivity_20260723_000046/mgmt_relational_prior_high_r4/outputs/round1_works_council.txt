As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for management’s restructuring.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it will not be made to pay for it alone.

Our priorities are straightforward:

- keep every German plant open,
- protect all jobs through 2030,
- preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and secure a credible industrial future for the sites, not just a short-term cost cut.

If management says competitiveness requires sacrifices, then we say: yes, but those sacrifices must come from negotiated, socially responsible alternatives, not from closures and forced layoffs. That means we are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time arrangements,
- temporary or reversible savings measures,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- and a fair employee contribution, if and only if it is matched by binding guarantees for sites, jobs, and investment.

What we will not do is accept a fait accompli disguised as “necessary restructuring.” This company has obligations to its workforce, its regions, and its own long-term industrial base. If management wants cooperation, it must come with a binding commitment: no German plant closures, no operational layoffs, and a genuine co-determination role in shaping the future plan.

Without that, we will resist decisively.
