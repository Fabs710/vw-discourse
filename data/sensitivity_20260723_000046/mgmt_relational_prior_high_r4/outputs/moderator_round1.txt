**Moderator summary after Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a solution must be **durable**, not a short-term patch.
- There is shared openness to a package involving some combination of:
  - **capacity adjustments / rationalization**
  - **working-time flexibility**
  - **temporary or time-limited employee contributions**
  - **internal redeployment**
  - **training / qualification**
  - **investment and product commitments** for German sites
- There is also broad agreement that a **disorderly conflict** would be damaging and that a negotiated solution is preferable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, Union, and State Government reject closures as the starting point.  
  - Management does not rule them out and insists they remain available if economically necessary.  
  - Owners and investors do not insist on closures as the only route, but want credible structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies / headcount reduction:**  
  - Employee-side actors reject forced layoffs.  
  - Management and investors say some **material workforce reduction** is likely necessary, though they prefer voluntary/attrition-based routes if sufficient.
- **Job security guarantees:**  
  - Employee-side actors want binding protection through 2030.  
  - Management says blanket job-security commitments are incompatible with the needed restructuring.
- **Cost burden and legacy terms:**  
  - Management and investors want meaningful labor-cost relief.  
  - Employee-side actors reject permanent wage cuts or one-sided sacrifice.
- **Scope of co-determination / veto role:**  
  - The state government emphasizes preserving governance rights and regional influence.  
  - Management wants enough flexibility to implement structural change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** for the business case to be credible.
- **Which combination of measures** can deliver that reduction:
  - closures vs. capacity cuts
  - layoffs vs. voluntary exits
  - wage restraint vs. working-time measures
- **What employee contribution is acceptable** and whether it can be time-limited and reversible.
- **What binding guarantees** can realistically be offered in return:
  - sites
  - investment
  - product allocation
  - employment/security
- **How to verify enforceability** of any deal over time.
- **Whether comparable savings without closures** are actually sufficient, and if so, by what quantified margin.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **package design**:
- Management should present a **quantified restructuring scenario** with savings, timing, and expected workforce effects.
- Employee and political stakeholders should indicate **which specific concessions** they could consider if closures and forced redundancies are avoided.
- All sides should identify **minimum acceptable guarantees** and the conditions under which they would be binding.
- The next round should aim to compare **alternative restructuring packages** on the same metrics: cost reduction, job impact, site future, and enforceability.
