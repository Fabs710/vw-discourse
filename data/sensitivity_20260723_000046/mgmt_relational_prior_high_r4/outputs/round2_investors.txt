We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not on any outcome that leaves the company structurally weak.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current German cost base and capacity footprint are not sustainable if the company is to restore margins and compete.
- **The workers’ side is right** that a disorderly conflict would destroy value and that purely cosmetic savings are unacceptable.
- **The expert is right** that non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings, but may fall short if overcapacity is truly structural.
- **The family and the state are right** that governance stability and regional legitimacy matter, because prolonged conflict is value destructive.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that simply preserves the status quo and calls it “socially balanced.” That would be a cosmetic compromise, and it would leave shareholders with:
- a still-uncompetitive cost base,
- margins that remain impaired,
- and a risk that the same problem reappears in two years.

We also do **not** support management’s implicit framing that closures are automatically the default or best answer. Closures may be justified if they are truly needed, but they carry major execution and strike risk. From a risk-adjusted value perspective, that risk must be weighed seriously.

### Our position
We would support **only** a package that credibly and durably restores competitiveness. Concretely, that means:

- **measurable annual savings**, not promises
- **durable capacity reduction**
- **real workforce adjustment**, preferably via voluntary exits and attrition, but sufficient in scale
- **material labor-cost relief**
- **binding commitments on product allocation and investment**
- **enforceable triggers and milestones**

A non-closure or hybrid solution is acceptable **if it closes the competitiveness gap in practice**, not just in theory. If it does not, then it is not good enough for capital markets.

### What we need before we can support any deal
We need a side-by-side, quantified comparison of:
1. **closure-based restructuring**
2. **hybrid restructuring**
3. **non-closure restructuring**

Each scenario must show:
- annual recurring savings
- one-time transition costs
- timing of realization
- headcount impact
- capacity impact
- strike / execution risk
- and expected effect on return on sales

### Our bottom line
- **No** to a cosmetic settlement.
- **No** to preserving all inefficiencies in the name of peace.
- **Yes** to a tough restructuring if it is quantified, enforceable, and durable.
- **Yes** to a hybrid or non-closure package **only if** it restores competitive margins credibly.
- **Otherwise, management must be prepared to use deeper structural measures.**

We will judge the outcome by one criterion: whether it restores shareholder value through a credible, durable return to competitiveness.
