I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally direct.

From the perspective of the controlling owners, there are two realities we cannot ignore:

1. **The company must restore competitiveness and margins.**
2. **We cannot afford a value-destroying conflict that damages the industrial base, the brand, or the governance balance.**

So I agree with management on the diagnosis: the current cost and capacity structure in Germany is not sustainable if it remains unchanged. Pretending otherwise would be irresponsible ownership.

But I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state government on one essential point: **a restructuring that simply detonates labour peace and permanently weakens the company would be a bad outcome for everyone, including the owners.**

### Where I agree
- The company needs a **credible structural solution**, not a temporary patch.
- There must be **real savings**, not symbolic gestures.
- The future of the German sites must be tied to **investment, product commitments, and utilization**.
- A **negotiated employee contribution** can be part of the answer if it is durable and fair.
- There should be a strong preference for **stability over disorderly confrontation**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible path is immediate plant closures and large-scale forced layoffs.

That may be one instrument, but it is not the only one, and if used too bluntly it risks destroying more value than it creates:
- industrial disruption,
- reputational damage,
- extended conflict,
- weakened execution on the transition,
- and lasting damage to trust between governance, management, and workforce.

For a long-term owner, that is not a trivial downside.

### To management
You are right to insist that “everything stays” is not a plan. But you need to distinguish between:
- **structural adjustment**, which I support, and
- **a confrontation strategy that may solve the spreadsheet but damage the enterprise**, which I do not.

If you want ownership support, you need to bring a package that is economically convincing **and** governable. That means:
- durable capacity reduction,
- lower labour cost,
- more flexibility,
- and a clear route back to acceptable returns.

But it does **not** automatically mean we should start by forcing plant closures as the first answer.

### To the works council and union
I hear your red lines, but I will not endorse a position that assumes the status quo can be preserved without meaningful concessions. The business is under pressure, and the workforce will have to contribute in some form.

That said, I would regard the following as a reasonable basis for serious talks:
- **no compulsory redundancies where avoidable**,
- **preference for voluntary and attrition-based reductions**,
- **capacity reductions before closures**,
- **working-time and flexibility measures**,
- **time-limited employee contributions**,
- and **binding investment commitments** for the sites that remain.

What I would need in return is not a slogan about “protecting everything,” but a plan that demonstrably improves competitiveness.

### To the regional state government
Your concern for the industrial base is legitimate, and I value that. But the state should also recognize that a company can lose value through indecision as well as through overreach.

A veto posture that blocks every difficult measure would not protect the region in the long run. The goal should be to preserve a viable industrial footprint, not necessarily every existing line in every existing plant.

### My position
I am **open to a negotiated restructuring that avoids closures if, and only if, it achieves comparable economic effect**. That means:
- real and durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity rationalization,
- a measurable workforce adjustment,
- and enforceable labour-cost contributions.

If those conditions cannot be met, then I will not support a cosmetic compromise that merely postpones the inevitable. But I am also not prepared to endorse a course that turns plant closures into the default instrument without exhausting alternatives.

So my message is simple:

- **Yes** to a hard restructuring.
- **Yes** to shared burden and negotiated adjustment.
- **No** to wishful thinking.
- **No** to a value-destroying industrial conflict.
- **No** to an outcome that permanently impairs the company’s long-term value or governance stability.

Bring forward a solution that is tough enough to restore competitiveness and disciplined enough to preserve the company’s future. That is the standard the owners will apply.
