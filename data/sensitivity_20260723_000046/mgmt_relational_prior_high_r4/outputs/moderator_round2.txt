### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **workforce-side red lines** and **management/owner/investor insistence on structural cost reduction**.

More specifically:

- **Works Council / Union / State** want a restructuring that preserves the German site network, avoids compulsory redundancies, and maintains job security through 2030.
- **Management / Investors / Controlling Family** want a package that delivers **durable, quantified structural savings** and do not accept a model that merely defers the adjustment.
- The **expert assessments** indicate that non-closure or hybrid solutions can work, but only if they include **real capacity reduction, material labor-cost relief, and enforceable commitments**.

So the impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed; it is over **how much structural adjustment is non-negotiable** and **whether site preservation can be guaranteed in advance**.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

#### 1) Hybrid restructuring with site guarantees for core operations
A package could combine:
- no immediate German plant closures,
- targeted capacity rationalization inside the German network,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- time-limited employee contribution,
- and binding investment/product commitments.

In return, management would receive:
- measurable annual savings,
- flexibility tools,
- and a defined review mechanism if targets are missed.

This may bridge the gap between “no closures” and “structural reset” if the capacity reduction is real and quantified.

---

#### 2) Phased agreement with trigger-based review
A second option is a **two-stage deal**:

- **Phase 1:** immediate non-closure measures, temporary employee contribution, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and investment commitments.
- **Phase 2 trigger:** if agreed savings/utilization milestones are not met by a set date, pre-agreed additional measures are unlocked for negotiation, such as deeper capacity rationalization or site reconfiguration.

This can reduce the conflict over up-front closure decisions while still protecting management’s demand for enforceability.

---

#### 3) Protected network, but with differentiated site roles
A third compromise is to preserve all German plants **as legal sites**, but assign them differentiated roles:
- one or more sites become core EV, battery, or software-adjacent industrial hubs,
- others receive reduced or specialized production mandates,
- surplus capacity is removed through shift reductions, line transfers, and voluntary exits rather than closures.

This preserves the footprint politically while still allowing substantive structural adjustment economically.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If management presents a package with **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding investment commitments**, but requires **measurable capacity cuts and a time-limited employee contribution**, what exact form of capacity reduction would you accept as the minimum needed to call this a genuine compromise?

#### Industrial Union
What is the maximum **scope, duration, and reversibility** of employee contribution you could endorse without crossing your red line that this becomes a permanent wage erosion?

#### Management
Which specific combination of measures, expressed in numbers, would you accept as sufficient to avoid closures: voluntary exits, indirect headcount cuts, shift reductions, wage restraint, and investment commitments—what is the minimum package that still meets your competitiveness target?

#### Regional State Government
If a hybrid package preserves the German network but includes site specialization and some internal consolidation, what enforceable commitments would you require before using your influence to support it?

#### Controlling Family
Would you support a no-closure or hybrid solution if it produced slightly lower savings than closure but significantly reduced conflict and protected long-term regional value? If yes, what economic threshold must it meet?

#### Institutional Investors
Would you accept a phased, trigger-based restructuring if the first phase produces material recurring savings and the second phase preserves downside protection for shareholders? What performance trigger would you consider credible?

---

### Procedural next step
A useful next round would be to require each side to submit, in the same format:

1. **target annual savings**
2. **headcount effect**
3. **capacity effect**
4. **one-time costs**
5. **site impact**
6. **milestones / triggers**
7. **what they are prepared to concede**

That would make the remaining disagreement visible in comparable terms.
