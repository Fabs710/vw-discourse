## On the moderator’s suggested paths
I will answer those directly.

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with site guarantees
This is acceptable **only if** “site guarantees” are real and not symbolic.

For us, that means:
- no closures,
- no hollowing out of sites into shells,
- no hidden path to future shutdowns,
- and a genuine future role for each German plant.

If “hybrid” means workers give concessions while management keeps all exit options open, then no, that is not a compromise.

### 2) Phased agreement with triggers
I am skeptical, for the same reason I said earlier: triggers can become a pressure machine that simply postpones the same attack.

If there is any phased deal, then the trigger logic must cut both ways:
- if management fails on investment, utilization, or product commitments, then employee contributions end or reverse;
- if management meets them, then labor maintains the agreed flexibility.

No one-sided trap.

### 3) Differentiated site roles
This can only work if every plant remains viable.

I am not prepared to endorse a model where one or more plants are “specialized” into slow decline. That is just closure by another route.

## To the specific question on capacity reduction
The works council asked what minimum form of capacity reduction I would accept as a genuine compromise.

My answer is:

A genuine compromise must remove **real excess capacity**, but through measures that do **not** close plants or force layoffs. That can include:
- shift reductions,
- smarter line allocation,
- model transfers between existing plants,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- retirement and attrition,
- and working-time adjustments.

What I will not accept is capacity reduction that depends on:
- plant closures,
- forced transfers out of the system,
- or a hidden erosion of site security.

The burden is on management to present the exact volume reduction and savings target. We are not here to bless a number we have not seen.
