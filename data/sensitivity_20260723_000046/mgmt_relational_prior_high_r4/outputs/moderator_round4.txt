## Core impasse
The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **what counts as an acceptable restructuring instrument**.

The central clash is between:
- **management / investors / controlling family**, who want a restructuring that is **economically sufficient even if it requires closures or compulsory reductions**, and
- **works council / union / regional state**, who insist that restructuring must be **economically credible without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

The expert input has narrowed the disagreement but not resolved it:
- a **hard hybrid** can likely capture much of the savings,
- but **not everyone agrees it is enough**, and
- no party has yet produced a **site-by-site quantified bridge** that all sides can inspect.

So the impasse is fundamentally:
> **Is there a non-closure package that is both economically sufficient and politically acceptable?**

Until that is answered with numbers, the dialogue will remain circular.

---

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Quantified hard-hybrid test package
A mediator-led package would be built **site by site** and compared against a closure baseline.

It would include:
- exact annual recurring savings
- one-time restructuring costs
- headcount effect
- capacity reduction by site
- overhead reduction
- implementation risk
- return-on-sales impact
- binding investment and product commitments
- rollback triggers if commitments are missed

This proposal does **not** decide the outcome. It creates a common factual test:
> Can a hard hybrid close the economic gap without closures?

---

### Proposal 2: Two-step deal with automatic reversion clauses
Parties could agree first on a **temporary hard-hybrid phase** with:
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary employee contribution,
- and investment commitments.

Then a second phase would only remain in force if milestones are met.

Key feature:
- if management misses investment, volume, or product commitments, then **worker concessions automatically expire**
- if milestones are met, the package continues

This creates symmetry and reduces fear of a one-way sacrifice.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-role differentiation with non-closure guardrails
Each site would be assigned a **defined industrial role**:
- core production
- specialized production
- component / support role
- future EV / technology role

Guardrails would include:
- no site may be reduced to a shell
- no site may lose its future role without joint review
- no compulsory redundancies during the initial phase
- no closure decision inside the negotiated period

This may bridge the gap if the real disagreement is about **site dignity and future viability**, not just cost.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact recurring savings gap remains after a hard-hybrid package, and which specific line items cannot be eliminated without closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the works council
**What minimum capacity reduction and labor-cost relief would you accept as sufficient to treat a hard hybrid as economically credible without reopening the closure question?**

### To the union
**What duration, scale, and rollback mechanism would make a temporary employee contribution acceptable as a genuinely reversible trade-off rather than a permanent cut?**

### To the regional state government
**What specific conditions would make you support a restructuring package even if it involves major internal site consolidation, so long as no German plant is closed?**

### To the controlling family
**What financial threshold or risk-adjusted return test would lead you to back a hard hybrid instead of preserving closures as an option?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of quantified, enforceable savings would be sufficient for you to support a no-closure settlement despite it delivering less than the theoretical maximum of a closure plan?**

### To the expert
**What would be the most realistic site-by-site savings range for a hard hybrid that avoids closures but still materially lowers breakeven volume and fixed cost?**
