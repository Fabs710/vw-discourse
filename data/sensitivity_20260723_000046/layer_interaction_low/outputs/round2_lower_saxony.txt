I will be direct.

I agree with the basic point that this company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic one. I also agree that the status quo is not acceptable and that competitiveness must improve. But I do **not** accept the conclusion that this therefore justifies German plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

The expert’s numbers are useful because they show something important: **a no-closure solution is not impossible**. It becomes difficult only if management’s savings target is extremely high. That means the real dispute is not whether workers are “unreasonable,” but whether management is demanding a level of adjustment that goes beyond what a socially responsible restructuring should require.

### To management
You have made the strongest argument for change, but not yet the strongest case for closures.

Your own position now depends on the claim that the savings gap is so large that preserving all sites is unworkable. The expert says that may be true only in the upper range of the gap. So I would ask you plainly: **what is the actual quantified savings target, and how much of it is supposed to come from closures rather than other measures?**

Until that is answered, closures remain a choice, not an inevitability.

I also disagree with the tone that treating site preservation as somehow unrealistic automatically makes the workforce the obstacle. That is too narrow. A company with this importance in the regional economy must first exhaust the options that preserve industrial capacity, employment, and trust.

### To the works council and union
I support your core red lines:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided burden shifting onto the workforce.

That is not just political rhetoric from the state side; it is a legitimate governance position under the special statute and in the public interest.

At the same time, I do hear the expert’s warning that redeployment and working-time changes alone may not be enough if the gap is very large. That is exactly why I expect management and ownership to contribute visibly as well. If labour is asked for concessions, they must be matched by real contributions from management, overhead, investment prioritization, and capital allocation.

### To the family owners
You are right that value destruction must be avoided. But value is also destroyed by forcing through a settlement that tears up trust, triggers open conflict, and damages the company’s role as an industrial anchor. A “hard reset” that leaves the social and political basis of the company broken is not durable value creation.

### To investors
I understand the demand for credibility and enforceability. But an outcome that is financially neat on paper and socially explosive in reality is not credible either. This is a German company with co-determination, a special statute, and regional responsibilities. That is part of the investment reality. You do not get a stable turnaround by pretending those structures do not exist.

### My position
So my minimum acceptable outcome remains:
- **no plant closures in Germany,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **binding site and job security,**
- **capacity reduction through operational measures, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and qualification,**
- **a fair contribution from labour, management, and ownership,**
- **and clear investment commitments for the company’s future in Germany.**

My red line remains unchanged:
- any closure of a German plant,
- any forced layoff,
- any settlement that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.

### What I could support
I could support a negotiated restructuring if it is built around:
- shift and capacity adjustments,
- temporary labour-cost restraint,
- voluntary exit and retirement pathways,
- internal redeployment and conversion,
- management overhead cuts,
- and binding commitments to future products and investment in Germany.

But I would need to see the actual numbers and a credible plant-by-plant scenario before accepting that closures are necessary. On the information now available, they are **not yet proven necessary**.

If management wants us to engage constructively, then bring a quantified package that shows:
1. savings required,
2. savings by measure,
3. redeployment capacity by site,
4. job impact by year,
5. and enforceable guarantees.

Until then, I will not back a restructuring that uses closures as the first answer to a problem that may still be solvable by a better shared effort.
