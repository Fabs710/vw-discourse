I will be direct.

I agree with the repeated point from all sides that this company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic compromise. I also agree that a mere postponement of the problem would be irresponsible. The company must become competitive enough to invest and survive in the long term.

But I do **not** agree with management’s continuing move to treat **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default answer**. That has still not been proven necessary. It may be an option in theory, but not a demonstrated necessity.

### Where I agree
- Capacity has to come down.
- Working-time flexibility matters.
- Internal redeployment and qualification matter.
- Overhead restraint matters.
- A fair contribution from employees may be part of the answer.
- Management and owners also have to contribute.

That is all correct.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the logic that says: because the cost gap exists, closures must therefore follow. That is not a conclusion; it is a choice. And in this governance structure, it is a choice that cannot be imposed by pressure or repetition.

I also disagree with the way some speakers frame “no closures” as if it were refusal to face reality. For us, preserving German sites and jobs is part of the reality of this company. It is not sentimentality; it is a core condition of social legitimacy and long-term stability.

### To management
You keep saying the footprint is too large. Fine. Then show the numbers in a way that proves it.

So far, what is missing is still missing:
- the exact savings target,
- the savings bridge by measure,
- the headcount impact by site and year,
- the redeployment capacity,
- and the one-time transition costs.

Until that exists, I cannot accept closures as “necessary.” They remain unproven.

### To the union and works council
I support your core line:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided burden shifting onto labour,
- and real social partnership.

That said, I also think it is important that we not hide behind slogans. If we want to preserve sites, then we must also be able to show how the package works operationally. So I support your insistence on a quantified model.

### To the family owners
If you mean stewardship, then stewardship means protecting the industrial base, not only control and value discipline. A deal that damages labour relations for years is not good stewardship, even if it looks neat in the short term.

### To investors
I understand the concern for margins and credibility. But credibility will not come from a conflict that destabilizes the workforce and weakens the company’s regional anchor. A settlement has to be enforceable in the real company, not just defensible on a spreadsheet.

### My position remains unchanged
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site security through 2030**
- **A real co-determination role in shaping restructuring**
- **Fair burden-sharing across management, owners, and labour**
- **Capacity reduction through operational measures, not site destruction**

### What I could support
I could support a negotiated restructuring that includes:
- shift and capacity adjustments,
- temporary employee contribution,
- voluntary exit and retirement pathways,
- redeployment and conversion,
- overhead and management restraint,
- and investment commitments for German operations,

**provided that all German sites remain open and forced redundancies are off the table.**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures disguised as “consolidation,”
- forced layoffs disguised as “necessary adjustment,”
- or any arrangement that removes job and site security and then asks the workforce to trust that this will somehow stop there.

That would be a betrayal of the regional economy and of the co-determination balance that has kept this company stable for decades.

### What is still missing
We still do not have the one thing needed to decide this properly: a **verified, site-by-site restructuring model** showing:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the savings from each instrument,
3. the headcount impact by site and year,
4. the redeployment capacity,
5. the one-time transition costs,
6. and whether a no-closure or hybrid package actually closes the gap.

So I again support bringing in the specialist immediately.

[SUMMON: a site-by-site industrial planning and workforce redeployment model to quantify savings, utilization, headcount impacts, redeployment capacity, one-time transition costs, and compare no-closure, hybrid, and closure scenarios]

Until that evidence exists, my answer remains clear:  
**No closures. No forced redundancies. German plants stay open. Co-determination remains intact.**
