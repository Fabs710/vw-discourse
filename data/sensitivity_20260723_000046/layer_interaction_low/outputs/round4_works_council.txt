I will speak plainly.

We have now had enough rounds to see the pattern: management and the capital side keep saying “the numbers may force closures,” while the workforce is asked to accept that as a starting premise without a verified site-by-site case. I do not accept that.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.

I also agree that the following tools belong in the package:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exits and early retirement,
- overhead restraint,
- and a fair contribution from everyone.

That is not in dispute.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with the repeated implication that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are somehow the natural or inevitable answer**.

They are not inevitable. They are a choice. And in a co-determined company with this governance structure, that choice cannot be forced through by assertion.

### To management
You still have not provided what is actually needed:
- the exact savings target,
- the savings bridge by measure,
- the headcount impact by site and year,
- the redeployment capacity,
- and the one-time transition costs.

Without that, “closures may be necessary” is not a proven conclusion. It is a bargaining position.

And let me be clear: if you come to us with a plan that starts from closures and layoffs, you are asking the workforce to pay for management’s strategic failures by destroying sites and livelihoods. That is not acceptable.

## On the moderator’s compromise proposals

### 1) Time-limited no-closure test
I do not support a “no-closure phase” that is only a bridge to later closures. That is not security; it is deferred confrontation.

If you want a no-closure period, then it must be more than a probationary period for shutting plants later. It must be built on binding guarantees to 2030. Otherwise, the workforce is being asked to surrender in installments.

### 2) Hybrid restructuring
A “hybrid” only matters if it preserves every German plant and avoids forced redundancies. If “hybrid” is just a softer word for selecting plants to shut later, then I reject it.

### 3) Burden-sharing package
I support burden-sharing, but not one that makes workers the only adjustable variable.

If management and owners want labor concessions, they must first commit to:
- binding site protection,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- measurable management overhead cuts,
- real owner contribution,
- and investment commitments for German operations.

Otherwise this is not burden-sharing. It is transfer of risk downward.

## To the union
I am aligned with your red lines:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- collective standards protected,
- fair contributions only if matched by management and owners.

But I want to be equally clear: any labor contribution must be tied to **job and site security through 2030**, not vague promises.

## To the state government
Your role is not to mediate a graceful closure package. Your responsibility is to defend industrial stability, regional employment, and the company’s long-term presence in Germany.

I therefore expect you to insist on:
- binding site guarantees,
- binding investment commitments,
- no forced redundancies,
- and a governance solution that preserves the workforce’s rights.

## To the family holding
If you really believe in stewardship, then prove it where it matters: protect the industrial base. Do not back a restructuring that breaks the company’s relationship with its workforce for years.

## To investors
I understand your concern about value and margins. But value is not preserved by triggering a labour conflict, destroying trust, and gambling that the company can absorb the fallout.

A settlement that tears up industrial peace is not “discipline.” It is risk.

## What I require
My position remains unchanged:

- **Every German plant stays open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site security through 2030**
- **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
- **Fair burden-sharing across management, owners, and labour**

## What would move me
Only one thing would move me: a verified, site-by-site model showing that a preserve-all-sites package with serious management and owner contributions, working-time adjustments, redeployment, qualification, and voluntary exits can or cannot close the gap.

We still do not have that proof.

So I again support this being sent to a specialist immediately:

[SUMMON: a site-by-site industrial planning and workforce redeployment modeler to quantify savings, capacity reduction, headcount impact, redeployment options, one-time costs, and margin effects under no-closure, hybrid, and closure scenarios]

Until that evidence exists, my answer is simple:

**No closures. No forced layoffs. No dismantling of co-determination. German plants stay open.**
