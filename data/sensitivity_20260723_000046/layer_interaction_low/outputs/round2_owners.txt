I will be direct.

I agree with the basic diagnosis from management, investors, and the expert: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company needs a **durable structural reset**, not a cosmetic arrangement. If margins stay weak and the cost base remains uncompetitive, the long-term value of the business will continue to erode. That is unacceptable.

Where I **do not** agree is with treating **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default answer**. That is not a governance principle I can support lightly, because it risks triggering exactly the kind of prolonged conflict, reputational damage, and industrial instability that destroys value faster than it creates it.

### Where I agree with management
- The company needs **real and lasting cost reduction**.
- Any settlement must be **credible to the market** and **durable in operations**.
- Temporary fixes or symbolic concessions are not enough.
- We cannot simply preserve every current structure and hope the economics improve by themselves.

### Where I disagree with management
I do **not** accept the implication that if the gap is large, then closures are automatically the right or only answer. The expert’s scenarios show something more nuanced: a **no-closure package can work if the required savings are in the moderate-to-upper range and if labour contributes meaningfully**. That means closures are a possible instrument, not a proven necessity on the information given so far.

So I would not support a governance push for closures unless management can show, in a quantified and site-specific way, that:
1. the savings gap is truly beyond what alternatives can cover, and  
2. the alternatives have been tested seriously, not just dismissed in principle.

### Where I agree with the workforce and state
- A settlement that preserves **social peace** and avoids a destructive fight has real value.
- **Redeployment, working-time measures, qualification, and capacity adjustment** are legitimate tools.
- A solution that simply loads the burden onto employees while management and ownership stay insulated is not durable.

### Where I disagree with the workforce and state
I cannot endorse an absolute position of **“no plant closures, no matter what”** if the economics ultimately do not support it. That would risk turning the company’s adjustment problem into a permanent strategic trap. Protecting every site at any cost would also be a value destruction strategy, just a slower one.

So I will not back a line that says closures are categorically off the table under all conditions. That would be too rigid for an owner with a long-term stewardship duty.

### My position
My position is this:

- **I support a negotiated restructuring**
- **I support real structural savings**
- **I support burden-sharing across management, ownership, and labour**
- **I do not support a cosmetic compromise**
- **I do not support a prolonged conflict**
- **I do not support closures as a reflexive first choice**
- **I will not support any outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise’s value or the family holding’s governance control**

### What I need before taking a firmer stance
Right now, the discussion is still too abstract. We need a **quantified site-by-site scenario** that shows:
- the required annual savings,
- the savings by measure,
- the headcount effects by site and by year,
- what redeployment is actually possible,
- and whether a no-closure package really closes the gap.

[SUMMON: a quantified site-by-site scenario model comparing closure, no-closure, and hybrid options, with savings bridge, headcount impact, and redeployment capacity]

### Minimum acceptable outcome from my side
A solution that:
- materially improves competitiveness and margins,
- preserves labour peace,
- avoids unnecessary destruction of enterprise value,
- and keeps strategic control and long-term governance intact.

### Red line
A settlement that:
- drags the company into a long, value-destroying conflict,
- or leaves it structurally uncompetitive,
- or permanently weakens the family holding’s governance position.

### One compromise option I could support
A **hybrid restructuring**:
- major capacity reduction,
- strong working-time and redeployment measures,
- a meaningful employee contribution,
- management overhead and cost discipline,
- targeted investment commitments,
- and only if absolutely unavoidable, narrowly defined structural changes rather than broad-brush closures.

That is not me drifting toward consensus. It is me saying: **prove that the economics require more before asking for irreversible measures**. Until then, closures remain an option, not a conclusion.
