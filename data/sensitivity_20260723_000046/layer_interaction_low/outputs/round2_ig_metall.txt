I will respond directly.

I agree with the expert on one important point: **if the company’s required savings are extremely deep, then a no-closure package becomes harder to make work.** That is exactly why management must finally put real numbers on the table instead of using “competitiveness” as a blanket justification for closures and forced redundancies.

But I do **not** accept the leap from “harder to make work” to “therefore closures or compulsory redundancies must happen.” That is not proven here. It is a managerial preference unless and until the actual savings gap is shown and all other levers are exhausted.

### Where I agree
- A negotiated solution is better than open conflict.
- Real structural savings are necessary.
- Capacity reduction, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, and labour-cost contributions are all legitimate tools.
- Implementation must be enforceable and durable.

### Where I disagree
- **I do not accept plant closures in Germany.**
- **I do not accept compulsory redundancies.**
- **I do not accept that the workforce alone should carry the adjustment.**
- **I do not accept a “preserve the options of management and owners, but lock in sacrifices for employees” model.**

That is not a fair restructuring; it is burden shifting.

### To management
You keep saying the economic reality forces your hand. Then be specific:

- What exact savings do you need?
- By when?
- From which cost blocks?
- How much comes from overhead, management compensation, supplier terms, investment prioritization, and capital allocation?
- How much from working-time changes and capacity reduction?
- How much from employee contribution?

Until you answer that, “closures are unavoidable” remains an assertion, not a case.

And I want to be equally clear: if management’s plan only works by breaking the job-security framework and creating open-ended insecurity, then it is not a viable solution from our side. We are not trading a temporary balance-sheet fix for permanent damage to trust, skills, and industrial peace.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the core boundary: **no closures, no forced layoffs**. Your insistence on binding job and site security is correct. The expert’s point about redeployment and attrition is useful, but it does not change the basic fact that employees cannot be made the sole adjustment variable.

I support your call for:
- capacity reductions through operational measures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and training,
- and a fair, limited employee contribution.

But I would add one point: if the company asks for contribution, then that contribution must be **temporary and negotiated**, not a permanent rewriting of labour standards.

### To the state government
Your position is consistent with ours on the core issue, and that matters. You are right that closures destroy regional value and trust. But “supporting competitiveness” must also mean supporting **fair burden sharing**. If the state wants a durable settlement, it should back a package that binds management and ownership to real contributions too, not just rhetorical support.

### To the family owners
You say you do not want value destruction, and I take that seriously. Then do not back a solution that provokes strikes, destabilizes the company, and humiliates the workforce while asking for loyalty.

If the family wants a durable turnaround, it needs to accept that:
- labour peace is not free,
- worker contributions require security in return,
- and governance cannot be used to force through a one-sided solution.

### On the expert’s assessment
The useful part of the expert’s intervention is this: **a preserve-all-sites solution can work only if the savings target is moderate enough and if the workforce reduction can be handled by attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, and labour-cost concessions.**

That is precisely why we need management to state the target and show the math. If the target is moderate, then closures are not necessary. If the target is so large that only closures can achieve it, then we are in a conflict over the scale of management’s demands, not over whether workers are “unreasonable.”

### My minimum acceptable outcome
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and job security**
- **A negotiated labour-cost contribution**
- **Capacity reduction through operational and working-time measures**
- **Internal redeployment and training**
- **Management and owners contributing visibly and measurably**

### My red line
- Any closure of a German plant
- Any forced layoff
- Any settlement that leaves employees carrying the full burden

### One concrete compromise option
I am prepared to discuss a package with:
- temporary pay restraint or bonus moderation,
- shift and working-time reductions,
- voluntary exit and retirement pathways,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and a jointly monitored savings plan,

**provided that all German sites remain open and there are no compulsory redundancies.**

**Bring the numbers, bring a fair burden-sharing package, and bring a solution that preserves jobs and sites. Otherwise, we will not support the restructuring, and we will use the means available to us.**
