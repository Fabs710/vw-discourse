To answer the direct question in my mandate:

## Minimum dataset needed for a defensible site-by-site comparison

To compare **closure**, **no-closure**, and **hybrid** scenarios credibly on an industrial-planning basis, I would need at minimum:

### 1) Current baseline by site
For each German site:
- headcount by function: production, logistics, maintenance, engineering, admin, quality, etc.
- shift pattern and utilization
- current annual output and product mix
- direct labor hours and labor cost
- fixed site costs
- variable conversion cost
- asset condition and major bottlenecks
- current vacancy and absenteeism rates

### 2) Product and capacity mapping
- which models/components are made at each site
- line capacities and bottleneck operations
- product end dates and run-out assumptions
- planned successor products, if any
- feasibility of reallocating volume between plants
- tooling constraints and changeover lead times

### 3) Workforce structure
- age profile and retirement eligibility
- skill matrix by employee group
- qualification gaps for redeployment
- contract categories and flexibility constraints
- agency/temporary labor reliance
- apprentices and pipeline intake

### 4) Redeployment capacity
- internal open positions by site and function
- retraining duration and cost per pathway
- likely acceptance rate for transfers
- geographic mobility assumptions
- limits from skills mismatch and certification requirements

### 5) Cost bridge by measure
For each scenario, itemize savings and costs from:
- plant closure or consolidation
- shift removal / capacity reduction
- working-time reduction or increases
- wage restraint or other labor-cost concessions
- overhead cuts
- management and administrative reductions
- procurement or supply-chain savings, if part of the case

And also the offsetting costs:
- severance
- early retirement
- retraining
- relocation support
- downtime and ramp-up losses
- dual-running costs
- decommissioning and environmental costs
- contract termination penalties

### 6) Timing
For each measure:
- when savings start
- when they reach full run-rate
- one-time implementation period
- transition risk period
- any lock-in or reversal risk

### 7) Operational continuity and risk
- customer service risk by site
- quality risk during transitions
- supplier disruption risk
- labor-relations risk if industrial action occurs
- feasibility of maintaining output during a shutdown/consolidation

### 8) Financial assumptions
- target margin or return-on-sales threshold
- discount rate or NPV approach
- volume forecast by region
- energy and wage inflation assumptions
- EV mix assumptions
- FX and pricing assumptions, if relevant

## What I would produce from that
With those inputs, I could build:
- a **site-by-site savings bridge**
- a **headcount impact by site and year**
- a **redeployment matrix**
- a **one-time cost estimate**
- and a **scenario comparison** of:
  1. closure/consolidation,
  2. no-closure with concessions,
  3. hybrid package.

## Main analytical constraint
Without verified baseline data, any claim that a no-closure solution is sufficient — or that closure is unavoidable — remains unproven. The decisive issue is whether the **permanent savings delta** between the options exceeds the **transition and disruption costs** and whether redeployment can absorb the displaced labor.
