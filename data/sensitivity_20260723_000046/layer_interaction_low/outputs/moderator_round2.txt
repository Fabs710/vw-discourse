1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties accept that the current situation is not sustainable and that a **durable restructuring** is needed.
- There is broad agreement that a cosmetic or temporary fix is insufficient.
- There is also shared recognition that the solution should, if possible, combine several tools:  
  **capacity reduction, working-time measures, internal redeployment, qualification/training, overhead restraint, and some form of employee contribution**.
- The expert and most parties agree that a **no-closure, no-forced-redundancy** model is **possible in principle**, but only under certain economic conditions.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and state representatives reject them.  
  - Management, investors, and the family holding treat them as possible or necessary depending on the savings gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee-side and state actors reject them.  
  - Management and some capital-side actors do not rule them out if required for structural savings.
- **How large the savings gap really is:**  
  - Management says the gap is large and structural.  
  - Employee and state actors say closures have not yet been proven necessary.  
  - Investors and owners want quantified proof either way.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  - Employee side insists adjustments must be shared by management, ownership, and labor.  
  - Management and investors emphasize that labor must make a meaningful structural contribution if sites are preserved.  
  - The extent and permanence of any employee contribution remain disputed.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- **Preserving all sites vs. achieving sufficient permanent cost reduction**
- **Avoiding forced layoffs vs. reaching the required pace and depth of restructuring**
- **Using redeployment and working-time flexibility vs. the likely limits of those measures**
- **Temporary labor concessions vs. permanent changes to the cost base**
- **Industrial peace and trust vs. investor certainty and margin recovery**
- **Regional/social stability vs. long-term competitiveness and capital discipline**
- **Site-by-site viability:** no party has yet provided a full quantified breakdown showing which sites can absorb adjustments and which cannot.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principle-based positions to a **quantified site-by-site scenario comparison**.
- Require a clear savings bridge showing:
  - total savings target,
  - savings by measure,
  - headcount impact by site and year,
  - redeployment capacity,
  - and the effect of labor-cost concessions.
- Test at least three options side by side:
  1. **full closure/consolidation**
  2. **no-closure with concessions**
  3. **hybrid compromise**
- Ask each side to state not only its red lines, but also **which trade-offs it is prepared to make if the numbers require it**.
