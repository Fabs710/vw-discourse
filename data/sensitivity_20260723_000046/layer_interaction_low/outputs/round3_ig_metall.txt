### Where I agree
I agree with the works council on the core point: **a durable settlement is needed, and a cosmetic compromise is not enough**. I also agree that the company has to make real structural adjustments, including:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- overhead restraint,
- and a fair employee contribution.

I also agree with the expert’s basic assessment: **a no-closure solution is possible in principle, but only if the savings gap is not too large and the burden is shared fairly**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated move to present **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** as the default or inevitable answer.

Management still has **not proven** that the only way to reach the required savings is by breaking the job-security framework. What has been shown is that a preserve-all-sites package gets harder if the gap is very large. That is not the same thing as proving closures are necessary.

And I will say this clearly:  
**If the workforce is asked to carry the restructuring, then management and owners must contribute visibly and measurably as well.**  
We will not accept a model where workers pay through wage restraint, insecurity, and job loss while others keep strategic flexibility untouched.

### To management
You keep saying the gap is structural and large. Then put the numbers on the table in a way that can actually be tested:

- What is the exact savings target?
- What comes from labor cost?
- What comes from overhead reduction?
- What comes from capacity adjustment?
- What comes from management restraint and ownership contribution?
- What is the timetable?
- What is the site-by-site impact?

Until that is done, closures are still a **claim**, not a demonstrated necessity.

And I reject the framing that we are refusing “reality.” We are refusing a **one-sided restructuring** that turns workers into the shock absorber of every strategic mistake.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the essential red lines:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **collective wage standards must remain intact**
- **burden-sharing must be fair**

We need a **credible alternative package** that actually closes the gap. That means we need the numbers, not just the principle.

So I support your line, but I also want us to stay focused on what can practically absorb the adjustment:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- training,
- working-time reductions,
- and genuine labor-cost concessions that are temporary and negotiated.

Your position is helpful because you understand that regional stability is not a side issue. But stability cannot be just a political slogan. If the state wants to help, it should support a settlement that:
- protects sites,

- economically sound,
- socially durable,
