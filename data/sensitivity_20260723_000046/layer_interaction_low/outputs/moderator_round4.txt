Core impasse identified:

1. **Burden of proof**
   - Management/investors/family want restructuring latitude, including possible closures.
   - Works council/union/state want a hard presumption against closures and forced redundancies.
   - The debate is stuck because **neither side accepts the other side’s starting premise**.

2. **Security vs. flexibility**
   - Labour-side stakeholders want **binding site and job security through 2030**.
   - Capital-side stakeholders want **structural savings that are durable and enforceable**.
   - The unresolved issue is whether savings can be achieved **without** irreversible site actions.

3. **Decision method**
   - All sides now agree the case must be **quantified site by site**.
   - The impasse is that the **required model has not yet been presented**, so positions are hardening ahead of evidence.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal A: Time-bound “preserve-first” restructuring with audit trigger
- All German sites remain open for a defined period.
- During that period, the company implements:
  - working-time changes,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - voluntary exits,
  - management overhead cuts,
  - owner contributions.
- An independent expert produces a site-by-site savings bridge.
- A closure decision is only permitted if the expert certifies that the non-closure package cannot close the gap.

**Bridge function:** gives labour-side stakeholders near-term security while giving management a defined proof-based review point.

### Proposal B: Hybrid restructuring with protected core sites
- Identify a set of **strategic core sites** that receive explicit protection.
- Less efficient overlaps are addressed through:
  - partial consolidation,
  - shift reductions,
  - function transfers,
  - product specialization,
  - and voluntary workforce reductions.
- No compulsory redundancies are used unless redeployment and retirement pathways are exhausted.

**Bridge function:** gives capital-side stakeholders some structural savings while preserving the most politically and socially sensitive footprint.

### Proposal C: Burden-sharing pact with conditional guarantees
- Labour accepts temporary concessions only if matched by:
  - binding management overhead cuts,
  - owner capital commitment,
  - investment pledges,
  - and a no-forced-redundancy rule.
- In return, the company receives a multi-year cost flexibility package.
- If management or owners miss their commitments, the labour contribution automatically sunsets.

**Bridge function:** links workforce concessions to reciprocal commitments, reducing distrust.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
If a verified site-by-site model shows that some capacity reduction is necessary to close the gap, **what specific forms of non-closure restructuring would you accept before any discussion of closures begins**?

### To Industrial Union
What level of **temporary labor contribution** would you consider acceptable if it were tied to **binding job and site security, redeployment guarantees, and measurable management/owner contributions**?

### To Group & Brand Management
What is the **minimum quantified savings bridge** you can present that separates:
- savings from labor measures,
- savings from overhead cuts,
- savings from capacity changes,
- and the one-time cost of transition?

### To Regional State Government
What **specific conditions** would you require before you would support any restructuring package as socially and economically acceptable?

### To Controlling Family Holding
What concrete **owner contribution** are you prepared to place on the table to show stewardship beyond governance oversight?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a package that preserves all sites **if** it produces the same net present value savings and margin recovery as a closure-led plan, and what evidence would you require to accept that comparison?
