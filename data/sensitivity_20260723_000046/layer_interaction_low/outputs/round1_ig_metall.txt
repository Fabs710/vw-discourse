We share the core line on this: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a bargaining chip for us; it is the basic boundary of any acceptable settlement.

Management’s current course looks like it wants the workforce to absorb the full adjustment while ownership and top management keep their options open. That is not a fair restructuring, and it will not produce a durable one. If employees are asked to contribute, then **all sides must contribute**: management, owners, and the company’s cost base as a whole.

We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary or permanent **capacity reductions**,
- **working-time adjustments**,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- restraint on bonuses and other cost elements,
- and a **fair employee contribution** in a negotiated package.

But that only works if the outcome includes:
- **binding job security**,
- **site security for the German plants**,
- and a clear commitment that there will be **no forced layoffs**.

What we will not accept is a restructuring that starts from plant closure and then offers “socially cushioned” job cuts as the compromise. That is just forcing the burden onto the workforce under another name.

So my message is simple: if management wants a solution, it needs to come with a credible alternative that preserves jobs and sites and distributes the pain fairly. Otherwise, it will meet resistance.
