I will be direct.

I agree with the broad diagnosis from management and the capital side: the current cost base is not sustainable, and a cosmetic compromise would simply defer the problem. If margins do not improve, long-term enterprise value erodes, and that is not acceptable.

Where I do **not** agree is with the repeated attempt to treat **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default or inevitable answer**. That is too blunt, and it carries a serious risk of prolonged conflict, reputational damage, and industrial instability — all of which can destroy more value than they create.

### My position on the arguments so far

#### To management
You are right that the company needs a structural reset. You are not yet right that closures are proven necessary.

What is still missing is a **quantified savings bridge**:
- exact savings target,
- savings by measure,
- savings by site,
- headcount impact by year,
- redeployment capacity,
- and the durability of any labor-cost contribution.

Until that is on the table, closures remain an option, not a demonstrated necessity. I am not willing to support irreversible measures on the basis of assertions.

#### To the works council and union
I hear your insistence on no closures and no forced layoffs. I understand the political and social logic, but I do **not** accept an absolute rule that German sites must remain untouched regardless of economics.

A preserve-all-sites package may work if the savings gap is moderate and the labor contribution is meaningful. But if the required reset is larger, then simply repeating “no closures” does not solve the arithmetic.

You are right to demand fair burden-sharing. But burden-sharing cannot mean that the enterprise remains structurally uncompetitive while everyone else is protected.

#### To the state government
You are right to emphasize regional stability and co-determination. But stability without competitiveness is just delayed decline. I support a solution that preserves industrial capacity where possible, but not one that locks in an uncompetitive structure.

#### To investors
You are right to demand credibility and durability. I agree that a weak compromise would be value-destructive. But credibility also requires a settlement that can actually survive in the German governance and labor-relations framework. Forcing through conflict is not the same as creating value.

### What I support
I support:
- real structural cost reduction,
- a meaningful labor contribution,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- management overhead restraint,
- and investment commitments that support the company’s future.

### What I do not support
I do **not** support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a prolonged value-destroying confrontation,
- or a package that permanently weakens the company’s governance control or enterprise value.

I also do **not** support accepting closures as the first answer without proof that alternatives cannot close the gap.

### My concrete view
The burden is now on management to show, in numbers, whether a no-closure or hybrid package can close the gap. If it can, that is the preferable route because it reduces instability. If it cannot, then we can discuss stronger structural measures — but not before the case is made.

At this stage, I would only support a **hybrid restructuring** if it is genuinely equivalent in savings and enforceable in practice. Otherwise, it is just a delay mechanism.
