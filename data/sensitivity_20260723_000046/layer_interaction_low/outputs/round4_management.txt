I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the company cannot continue with the current cost base and overcapacity**. A cosmetic deal would be worthless. We need a **durable structural reset** that restores competitiveness, improves the brand’s return on sales, and frees funds for investment to 2030.

I also agree that the solution, if it is to be durable, will likely combine several tools:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- overhead restraint,
- and a meaningful employee contribution.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to make **“no closures” and “no forced redundancies”** a fixed framework.

That may be politically attractive, but it does not answer the economic problem. If the German footprint remains too large for the market, then preserving every site as a principle is not restructuring — it is postponement.

### To the works council
You say closures have not been proven necessary. Fair enough. But you still have **not shown** that a preserve-all-sites package can close the gap with the same durability and certainty.

Your position is:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- job and site security through 2030.

My problem with that is simple: **you are demanding the outcome before the economics are demonstrated**. That is not an answer to a structural cost crisis.

If a no-closure model is to be credible, it must show:
- exact savings,
- exact headcount reduction by site and year,
- redeployment capacity,
- and a durable labor-cost contribution.

Until that exists, your stance is still an assertion, not a solution.

### To the union
I agree that workers cannot be the only adjustment variable. But I reject the idea that labor contributions must be purely temporary and small. If the problem is structural, then the contribution must be **structural enough to matter**.

That does not mean I am demanding gratuitous pain. It means the package has to actually restore competitiveness. A short-term concession that leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not enough.

### To the state government
I understand your concern for regional stability. But stability without competitiveness is just delayed decline. A solution that protects every structure on paper but leaves the company weaker is not responsible stewardship.

### To the family owners
I agree that prolonged conflict destroys value. But value is also destroyed if we refuse to take the steps needed to reset the business. Stewardship means supporting a hard restructuring when the economics require it, not just avoiding headlines.

### To the investors
You are right to demand credible margins and disciplined cost reduction. I share that objective. But credibility will not come from a vague promise or a political compromise that preserves the current structure. It will come from a package that actually closes the gap and is enforceable in practice.

## My position remains unchanged
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- a **durable structural reduction in cost and capacity**
- a **meaningful labor-cost contribution**
- a **credible path back to target margins**
- freed resources for future products and technology

### Red line
- any settlement that preserves the current overcapacity and cost base under the banner of “no closures”
- any cosmetic compromise that delays the real adjustment
- any package that fails to restore competitiveness in a durable way

### What I could support
I remain open to a **hybrid restructuring** or a **no-closure package** only if — and this is the condition — it produces **equivalent structural savings**.

That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- shift elimination,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- labor-cost concessions that are durable enough to matter,
- overhead and management restraint,
- and targeted investment commitments.

But I will not support an arrangement that simply preserves every site and hopes the economics improve later.

2. the savings from each instrument,
3. the headcount effect by site and year,
4. redeployment capacity,

6. and whether the no-closure or hybrid route actually closes the gap.
