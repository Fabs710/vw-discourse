We agree on the end state, but not on the standard for getting there.

### Where we agree
- The current cost base is not acceptable.
- A cosmetic settlement would destroy value.
- The company needs a **durable** restructuring with real savings.
- Capacity reduction, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, overhead restraint, and employee contribution are all legitimate tools.

### Where we disagree
Our key concern is not whether plant closures are ideologically attractive. It is whether the final package restores **risk-adjusted value** through **credible, enforceable, structural savings**.

Right now, I do **not** see enough evidence that:
- a no-closure package is sufficient for a truly large gap, or
- a closure-led package is preferable if it triggers prolonged conflict, implementation risk, and margin slippage.

So we are not taking a side on principle. We are taking a side on **outcome quality**.

### To management
You keep saying the gap is structural and large. That may well be true, but you still have not provided the hard bridge that investors need:
- exact savings target,
- savings by measure,
- savings by site,
- timing,
- and the margin path back to competitiveness.

Without that, “closures may be necessary” remains a bargaining position, not an investable plan.

If closures are truly required, show us why the alternatives fail quantitatively. If they are not required, then stop using them as leverage while the company’s reputation and labour relations deteriorate.

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on no closures and no forced layoffs is understandable, but from our perspective it is only acceptable if it still delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- lower fixed cost,
- and a credible return to target margins.

If the settlement preserves every site but leaves the company structurally uncompetitive, we will not support it. That would be a transfer of pain into the future, not a solution.

Temporary concessions, voluntary exits, and redeployment can help. But if they are just a way to avoid hard decisions while the cost base stays bloated, they are not enough.

### To the state and family owners
We agree with the need to avoid value-destroying conflict. But avoiding conflict is not the same as preserving value. A settlement that is politically neat but financially weak would still be a bad outcome.

### Our position
We will support **whatever package credibly restores competitive margins and disciplined cost reduction**. That could be:
- a no-closure package,
- a hybrid package,
- or, if genuinely unavoidable, a package with structural consolidation.

But our support depends on one thing: **the numbers must close, and the implementation must be enforceable**.

### What would move us
- A quantified savings bridge showing how the target is reached.
- A site-by-site headcount and cost impact.
- A credible redeployment and attrition path.
- A durable labor-cost contribution.
- A clear timeline to margin recovery.

### What would not move us
- A compromise that preserves current structures and calls it restructuring.
- A closure plan that creates prolonged disruption without proving it delivers better net value.
- A settlement driven by political convenience rather than economics.
