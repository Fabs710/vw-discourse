I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one core point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic compromise**. The current cost base is clearly under pressure, and pretending otherwise would be irresponsible. I also agree that any settlement must be **credible, enforceable, and able to restore competitiveness**.

Where I disagree is with the repeated move to treat **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default answer**. That is not acceptable from my side, and it has not been proven necessary on the basis presented so far.

### To management
You are right that the company needs structural change. But you still have **not shown, in quantified form, that closures are unavoidable**. Repeating that the gap is “large” is not enough.

The expert’s assessment actually cuts both ways:
- A **no-closure** solution is possible in principle.
- It becomes difficult only if the savings gap is truly very large.
- The decisive question is therefore **the size of the gap and the savings bridge**, not an abstract preference for consolidation.

So my position is unchanged:
- I will not support **German plant closures** as a first resort.
- I will not support **compulsory redundancies**.
- I will support a **real restructuring** if it preserves sites and jobs while improving competitiveness through capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, overhead restraint, and a fair burden-sharing arrangement.

If management wants my support for more difficult measures, then bring the numbers:
1. exact savings target,
2. savings by measure,
3. headcount impact by site and year,
4. redeployment capacity,
5. and what management and owners are contributing themselves.

### To the works council and union
I agree with your red lines:
- no closures in Germany,
- no forced layoffs,
- no one-sided burden shifting onto employees.

I also agree that workers cannot be treated as the sole adjustment variable. That would be unfair and politically unsustainable. Your insistence on social partnership is legitimate.

Where I would caution is this: simply repeating “no closures” is not enough unless there is also a **credible operating package**. The expert has already shown that redeployment, attrition, and working-time changes have limits. So I need to see whether your alternative actually closes the gap.

That said, those limits do **not** prove closures are necessary. They prove only that the restructuring must be serious and quantified.

### To the family owners
You are right that the company must not drift into long-term decline. I support that. But long-term stewardship is not served by forcing through an adjustment that breaks trust, destroys industrial peace, and weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor.

A “hard reset” that leaves the social base of the company damaged is not stewardship. It is a high-risk bet.

### To the investors
You are right to demand a credible path back to margins. I do not dismiss that. But investor certainty does not come from provoking a conflict that endangers sites, skills, and labour relations. A turnaround is only credible if it is **durable in the real company**, not just neat on a spreadsheet.

### My position
My minimum acceptable outcome remains:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job security**
- **capacity reduction through operational measures**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **internal redeployment and qualification**
- **management and ownership contributing visibly**
- **investment commitments for the company’s future in Germany**

My red line remains unchanged:
- any closure of a German plant,
- any forced layoff,
- any settlement that removes the state’s co-determination and veto role,
- or any outcome that guts regional employment.

### What I could support
I could support a negotiated restructuring that includes:
- shift and capacity adjustments,
- temporary employee contribution,
- voluntary exit and retirement pathways,
- redeployment and conversion,
- overhead and management restraint,
- and binding future-product investment,

**provided that all German sites remain open and forced redundancies are off the table.**

### What is still missing
We still do not have the one thing that would allow a serious judgment:
a **site-by-site, quantified restructuring model** comparing:
1. closure/consolidation,
2. no-closure with concessions,
3. and a hybrid option.

So I support the call for that model. Until then, closures remain an unproven instrument, not an inevitability.
