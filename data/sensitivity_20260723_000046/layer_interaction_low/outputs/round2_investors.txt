We agree on the central point: **this company needs a real, durable cost reset**. A cosmetic deal that preserves the current cost base and merely postpones the problem would be value-destructive, and we would not support it.

Where we differ is on the **instrument**. We are **not** attached to plant closures as a principle. But we are also **not** willing to support a package that protects every site, protects too many legacy costs, and then calls that competitiveness. If the outcome leaves margins structurally impaired, capital will leave.

### To management
Your argument is credible only if you can show the gap is truly large enough that alternatives cannot close it. Right now, you have not done that. You keep asserting that closures may be necessary, but the discussion has also shown that a **preserve-all-sites package can work if the gap is moderate and the employee contribution is meaningful**.

So from our side, the burden is on management to be precise:
- what is the quantified savings target?
- what is the timing?
- how much is coming from labor, overhead, capacity, and investment prioritization?
- what is the enforceable path back to target margins?

Without that, “necessary closures” is not a financial argument; it is a bargaining position.

### To the works council and union
We understand your red lines, but an **absolute no** to closures and an **absolute no** to compulsory redundancies is only credible if you can still deliver the required savings. If you cannot, then you are not offering a restructuring solution; you are defending the status quo.

We are open to:
- capacity reduction,
- shift and working-time changes,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary or permanent labor-cost restraint,
- and voluntary exits.

But we will not back a package that leaves the company structurally weak just to preserve every existing site. Trust is important; competitiveness is non-negotiable.

### To the state government
Your concern for regional stability is legitimate, but stability without competitiveness is just delayed decline. We do not dismiss the social consequences of closures or forced layoffs, but we also do not accept regional politics as a veto over economic reality.

If a no-closure package can credibly restore margins, fine. If not, then insisting on preservation at all costs would be a mistake for jobs, investment, and shareholder value alike.

### To the family owners
We agree with your insistence on durability and avoiding value-destroying escalation. Where we would push harder is this: **do not let governance preferences block the only credible route to margin repair**. If the numbers show that deeper structural action is required, then protecting control is not the same as protecting enterprise value.

### Our position
Our position remains straightforward:
1. **We support a negotiated settlement only if it materially restores competitiveness.**
2. **We do not support a cosmetic compromise.**
3. **We are open to alternatives to closures, but only if the savings are real, durable, and enforceable.**
4. **We want deal certainty and a credible return to margins.**

### What would move us
A package that shows, in numbers:
- a durable reduction in fixed cost,
- a believable reduction in overcapacity,
- a credible labor contribution,
- and a timeline that gets the company back to competitive returns without endless conflict.

### What would not move us
- protected sites with no real cost reset,
- vague promises of future efficiency,
- strikes and uncertainty with no economic clarity,
- or a settlement that is politically neat but financially weak.

If management cannot produce a credible no-closure alternative with equivalent savings, then we will not oppose stronger structural measures in principle. But we are not giving a blank check to either side. We will support the path that best restores risk-adjusted value.
