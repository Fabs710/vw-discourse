### Core impasse

The discussion is no longer mainly about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is now about **the burden of proof and the burden of adjustment**:

1. **Management / capital side** says the cost gap is large enough that closures and deeper consolidation may be necessary.
2. **Works council / union / state** say closures and compulsory redundancies have not been proven necessary and must not be the default.
3. **All sides lack the same missing item:** a **verified site-by-site savings bridge** showing what each option delivers on savings, jobs, redeployment, timing, and one-time costs.

So the stalemate is not over the abstract need for restructuring, but over:
- whether a **no-closure package** can close the gap,
- whether a **hybrid package** can do so credibly,
- and what level of **job/security commitment** is compatible with competitiveness.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure test with quantified triggers
A compromise could be a **binding no-closure phase for a defined period** (for example, 12–18 months), during which all sides commit to a quantified restructuring plan with:
- agreed savings target,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and management/owner contribution.

**Trigger:** If the agreed savings bridge is not met by a set checkpoint, then the parties reopen site consolidation options under pre-agreed rules.

This gives the workforce a real no-closure attempt while giving management a credible fallback if the numbers do not close.

---

#### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with protected strategic sites
A second compromise is a **hybrid footprint**:
- preserve strategically important German sites,
- consolidate weaker or overlapping functions,
- eliminate capacity by shifts, product allocation, or partial specialization rather than full closure where possible,
- and pair this with binding investment commitments for the retained sites.

This would aim to avoid full closure as the first instrument while still allowing meaningful structural reduction where needed.

---

#### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package with enforceable reciprocity
A third compromise could link employee concessions to explicit management and ownership commitments:
- employees contribute through temporary wage restraint, working-time flexibility, and voluntary exits;
- management contributes through overhead reduction, executive cost cuts, and capital allocation commitments;
- owners/investors commit to a staged investment plan;
- the state supports through transition tools, training, and regional adjustment measures.

**Condition:** No forced redundancies during the concession period, provided the company meets its own contribution targets.

This directly addresses the concern that workers are being asked to carry the restructuring alone.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target do you need to reach by 2030, and what is the quantified savings contribution from each measure—capacity, labor, overhead, and closures—site by site?**

#### To Works Council
**If a no-closure package fails to close the savings gap by the agreed checkpoint, what specific additional measures are you prepared to negotiate so the company does not return to the same stalemate?**

#### To Industrial Union
**Which temporary or structural labor-cost concessions would you accept, and under what conditions, to make a no-closure or hybrid solution economically credible?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What concrete support can the state provide that is conditional on binding site protection, job security, and investment commitments, rather than on general assurances alone?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a hybrid restructuring if it preserves core German sites but requires measurable owner contribution and a binding investment path?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What level of implementation risk and time-to-margin-recovery would you tolerate before preferring a hybrid settlement over a faster but more disruptive closure-led option?**
