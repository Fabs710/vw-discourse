I will be equally direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with the union, the works council, the state, the family holding, and the investors on one essential point: **the current situation is not sustainable**. A cosmetic agreement is useless. We need a **durable structural reset** that restores competitiveness and creates room to invest to 2030.

I also agree that:
- capacity reduction matters,
- working-time flexibility matters,
- redeployment matters,
- qualification matters,
- overhead restraint matters,
- and employee contribution matters.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** agree with the repeated attempt to make “no plant closures” and “no forced redundancies” an absolute framework.

That position may be understandable politically, but it does not answer the economic problem. If the company’s German footprint remains too large for the market, then preserving every site as a principle is not restructuring — it is postponement.

I also do **not** accept the argument that closures are merely a governance choice and therefore can be ruled out in advance. They are a possible instrument when the savings gap is large enough and durable alternatives do not close it. That is the reality of the situation.

### To the works council
You say management has not proven that closures are necessary. That is only half true. You have also not shown that a preserve-all-sites package can deliver the same permanent savings with the same certainty.

The expert’s modelling was quite clear:
- a no-closure solution can work **in principle**,
- but only if the savings gap is moderate or if labor makes a very substantial contribution,
- and only if capacity is truly removed, not merely disguised through temporary measures.

That means the burden is not only on management. If you insist on preserving all German sites through 2030, then you need to accept that this requires a **material** and **durable** labor-cost contribution, plus real capacity reduction. If that package does not close the gap, then it is not a solution.

I reject the idea that management is somehow asking workers to bear all the pain. What I am asking for is a fair restructuring that actually restores competitiveness. If the workforce wants site security, then the workforce side must help deliver equivalent savings, not just oppose the instruments.

### To the union
You keep asking for exact numbers, and that is fair. But your position remains incomplete because you still treat closures as unacceptable regardless of the savings requirement.

I am saying plainly: if the gap is large enough, then **no-closure and no-forced-redundancy** is not automatically viable. The expert confirmed that. You can call that a management preference if you want, but you have not disproven it.

You also say any employee contribution must be temporary. I disagree. If we are restructuring a structural cost problem, then the contribution must be **structural enough to matter**. Temporary relief does not fix a permanent competitiveness gap.

### To the state government
I understand your responsibility for regional stability. But stability without competitiveness is a dead end. Protecting every existing structure at all costs may feel responsible in the short term, but it risks leaving the company weaker, less investable, and ultimately with even fewer jobs.

So I do not accept a state-side veto by default on any meaningful consolidation. The special governance structure does not change the arithmetic.

### To the family owners
You are right that value destruction from conflict must be avoided. I agree. But value is also destroyed if we refuse to take the steps needed to reset the business. A settlement that preserves trust but leaves the cost base uncompetitive will not protect long-term value.

I need your support for a hard reset, not for symbolism. If the numbers show that structural consolidation is required, then governance must back it.

### To the investors
You are right to demand a credible return to margins. I agree with that completely. But credibility will not come from a deal that protects every site and leaves the cost base too high. We need real savings, not just political comfort.

At the same time, I am not asking for a blank check for closures. I am asking for a package that closes the gap. If alternatives can do that, fine. If they cannot, then we need the full set of instruments available.

## My position remains unchanged
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- A **durable structural reduction in cost and capacity**
- A **meaningful labor-cost contribution**
- A **credible path back to target margins**
- Freed funds for future products and technology to 2030

### Red line
- Any settlement that preserves the current overcapacity and cost base under the banner of “no closures” or “no forced redundancies”
- Any cosmetic compromise that delays the real adjustment
- Any package that fails to restore competitiveness in a durable way

### One concrete compromise option
I remain open to a **hybrid restructuring** if — and only if — it produces equivalent structural savings. That means:
- capacity reduction,
- shift elimination,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary or structural labor-cost concessions,
- management and overhead restraint,
- and targeted investment commitments.

But I will not sign up to a package that simply preserves every site and hopes the economics improve later.
