1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties agree that a **negotiated settlement** is preferable to open conflict.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs **real competitiveness gains**, not a cosmetic compromise.
- Most stakeholders accept that any solution may involve some combination of:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - working-time solutions,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - management/owner contribution,
  - and some form of employee contribution.
- There is also shared recognition that **durability and enforceability** matter: the settlement must hold long enough to stabilize the business.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and state government reject them; management and investors see closures as potentially necessary unless an equivalent savings package is offered.
- **Compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs:**  
  Employee-side and state side reject them; management and investors have not accepted a blanket prohibition.
- **Scope of job and site security:**  
  Employee-side and state side want binding guarantees; management says it cannot freeze the structure through 2030 regardless of economics.
- **What counts as “equivalent” restructuring:**  
  Management and investors want demonstrable structural savings and lower fixed costs; employee-side and state side argue those goals can be achieved without closures or forced redundancies.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  There is agreement that not only employees should contribute, but the parties disagree on how far management/owners can or should go, and what contribution is sufficient.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- **Exact savings target vs. acceptable instruments:** no shared threshold for how much cost reduction is required, or which measures can credibly deliver it.
- **Speed vs. social protection:** management/investors want fast, durable change; employee-side wants site and employment protection.
- **Capacity reduction vs. plant preservation:** no consensus on whether reduced output must imply closures or can be achieved through operational adjustments alone.
- **Binding guarantees vs. flexibility:** employee and state sides seek firm commitments; management wants room to adapt to market conditions.
- **Value preservation vs. political/social stability:** investors emphasize financial credibility; state and workforce emphasize regional and employment stability.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to **specific package design**:
  - What cost savings are required?
  - Which measures are proposed to achieve them?
  - What guarantees can be given on jobs, sites, and duration?
  - What contribution comes from each stakeholder group?
- Ask each side to present:
  1. a **minimum acceptable outcome**,
  2. a **non-negotiable red line**,
  3. and **one concrete compromise option** they could support.
- Keep the discussion centered on **verifiable numbers, timelines, and enforcement mechanisms** rather than general principles.
