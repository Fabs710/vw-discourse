We agree on the objective, but not yet on the standard for accepting a deal.

## Where we agree
- The current cost base is too high.
- A cosmetic compromise would destroy value.
- The company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a temporary truce.
- Capacity reduction, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, overhead restraint, and employee contribution are all legitimate tools.

## Where we disagree
Our position is still simple: we will support **whatever credibly restores competitive margins and disciplined cost reduction**. That means:
- the package must **close the savings gap**,
- it must be **enforceable**,
- and it must produce a **credible path back to target margins**.

At this point, nobody has shown that a no-closure package definitely does that, and nobody has shown that closure-led restructuring definitely does it better once implementation risk is included.

So we are not taking sides on principle. We are taking sides on **results**.

### To management
You keep saying the gap is structural and large. Fine. Then show the bridge.

What we still need is:
- exact savings target,
- savings by measure,
- savings by site,
- headcount impact by site and year,
- redeployment capacity,
- one-time transition costs,
- and the margin recovery path.

Without that, closures remain a bargaining position, not a value case.

If closures are really necessary, prove that the alternatives fail quantitatively. If they are not necessary, stop using them as leverage while the company absorbs conflict risk and reputational damage.

### To the works council and union
We understand your red lines, but from a capital-markets perspective they are only acceptable if they still deliver:
- durable cost reduction,
- lower fixed cost,
- and restored competitiveness.

A settlement that preserves every site but leaves the company structurally uncompetitive is not one we can support. That would just push the problem forward and weaken the share value further.

Temporary concessions, voluntary exits, and redeployment can help, but if the cost base remains bloated, that is not enough.

### To the state and family owners
We agree that prolonged conflict is value-destructive. But avoiding conflict is not the same as restoring value. A politically neat deal that leaves margins impaired would still be the wrong outcome.

## Our line
We will support **any package that credibly restores competitive margins and disciplined cost reduction**. That could be:
- a no-closure package,
- a hybrid package,
- or, if genuinely unavoidable, a package with structural consolidation.

But our support depends on one thing: **the numbers must close, and the implementation must be enforceable**.

## What would move us
- A quantified savings bridge showing how the target is reached.
- A site-by-site headcount and cost impact.
- A credible redeployment and attrition path.
- A durable labor-cost contribution.
- A clear timeline to margin recovery.

## What would not move us
- A compromise that protects current structures and calls it restructuring.
- A closure plan that creates prolonged disruption without proving it delivers better net value.
- A settlement driven by political convenience rather than economics.

Until that evidence exists, we remain neutral on the instrument but firm on the outcome: **restore competitiveness, or we will not back the deal.**
