I will be direct.

## My view remains unchanged

The company needs a **durable structural reset**. A cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base untouched would destroy value over time, and that is not acceptable.

At the same time, I do **not** accept that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** should be treated as the starting assumption. They are instruments, not an ideology. If used too bluntly, they can trigger prolonged conflict, reputational damage, and operational instability that can erase the very value they are meant to protect.

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that:
- the current cost base is not sustainable,
- capacity must come down,
- competitiveness has to improve,
- and the company needs room to invest in future products and technology.

I also agree that a serious restructuring must include more than small concessions:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- overhead restraint,
- and some form of labour contribution.

That is the right direction.

### With investors
I agree with the demand for **credible, enforceable, structural savings**. A weak compromise that postpones the adjustment would be value-destructive.

### With the state government
I agree that the solution must preserve social stability and industrial capability where possible. A breakdown in labour relations would be costly and should be avoided.

### With the union and works council
I agree that a negotiated settlement is preferable to open conflict, and that burden-sharing should not fall on employees alone.

## Where I disagree

### On the works council and union’s absolute “no closures, no layoffs” stance
I understand the political logic, but I do **not** accept it as a governing principle.  
If the savings gap is large enough, then an unconditional ban on closures may simply protect overcapacity and shift the problem into the future.

However, I also do **not** accept the opposite extreme: that closures are automatically necessary just because management says so. That is not proven yet.

So I am not on either side’s slogan. I am on the side of the numbers and the long-term value of the enterprise.

### On management’s current case
Management still has **not produced the quantified bridge** that would justify irreversible measures. Repeating that the gap is “large” is not enough.

What I want to see is:
- exact savings target,
- savings by measure,
- savings by site,
- headcount impact by year,
- redeployment capacity,
- one-time transition costs,
- and the durability of any labour-cost contribution.

Until then, closures remain an **option**, not a demonstrated necessity.

## What I support

I support a **hybrid restructuring** if, and only if, it delivers equivalent structural savings and is enforceable in practice. That can include:
- capacity reduction,
- shift trimming,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- retirement pathways,
- management overhead restraint,
- and a meaningful employee contribution.

But I will not support a package that merely preserves every site and hopes the economics improve later.

## What I do not support

I do **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a prolonged value-destroying conflict,
- or any settlement that permanently weakens enterprise value or governance control.

I also do **not** support agreeing to closures before the case is proven.

## What is still missing

We still do not have the one thing required for a responsible decision: a **verified, site-by-site restructuring model** showing:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the savings from each instrument,
3. the headcount impact by site and year,
4. redeployment capacity,
5. one-time transition costs,
6. and the comparison of closure, no-closure, and hybrid options.

[SUMMON: a quantified site-by-site restructuring and redeployment model comparing closure, no-closure, and hybrid options, with savings bridge, headcount impact, transition costs, and margin recovery path]

## Bottom line

My position is simple:

- I support **competitiveness**.
- I support **durable structural savings**.
- I support **burden-sharing**.
- I do **not** support closures as a default.
- I do **not** support a settlement that protects the current cost base and calls it restructuring.

If the numbers later show that stronger measures are unavoidable, then we can discuss them. But that case has not been made yet.
