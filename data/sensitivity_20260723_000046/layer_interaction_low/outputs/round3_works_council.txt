I will speak plainly.

The repeated attempt to make closures and forced redundancies sound “necessary” has still not been proven. What has been proven is something else: **management wants the workforce to accept permanent insecurity in order to repair a cost problem it created and then presents as inevitable**. That is not a convincing case, and it is not a fair one.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone who says the company needs a **durable restructuring** and not a cosmetic fix. I also agree that the solution must contain real operational changes:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- overhead restraint,
- and a contribution from all sides.

I also accept the expert’s basic point: if the savings gap were extremely large, a no-closure package would become harder. But “harder” is not the same as “impossible,” and it is certainly not a justification for starting with closures and layoffs.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with management, investors, and the family holding on one central issue: **you are treating plant closures as an economic fact rather than a governance choice**.

That is not acceptable.

You have still not shown:
- the exact savings target,
- the savings bridge by measure,
- the site-by-site headcount effect,
- the redeployment capacity by plant,
- or why the same structural savings cannot be achieved without breaking the job-security framework.

Until that is on the table, your case for closures remains a bargaining demand, not a necessity.

### To management
You say the cost gap is structural. Then show the math. Not slogans, not generalities, not pressure tactics.

If the gap is moderate or even in the upper range, the expert has already shown that a **preserve-all-sites package can work** through:
- capacity reduction,
- shift trimming,
- working-time changes,
- wage restraint,
- redeployment,
- and voluntary exits.

So your position is not yet proven. And let me be clear: **we will not accept a settlement that uses “competitiveness” as the cover for destroying German sites and replacing secure work with insecurity**.

### To investors
You keep asking for certainty and margin recovery. We understand that. But certainty does not come from provoking a workforce confrontation that damages trust for years. A value-creating turnaround is not built by forcing through irreversible damage and hoping the plant survives the conflict.

If you want durable value, you need a settlement that preserves industrial capability, not one that hollows it out.

### To the family owners
You say you want long-term stewardship. Then demonstrate it. Stewardship means more than defending control and demanding adjustments from labour. It means protecting the industrial base, respecting co-determination, and avoiding a course that would poison the company’s labour relations for a generation.

### To the state government
Your position is the most consistent with social stability, and I welcome that. But I want to add something important: stability must be backed by actual guarantees, not just political statements. If the state wants to help, then it should insist on:
- binding site protection,
- binding job security,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a governance solution that keeps the workforce from being the only adjustable variable.

### My position remains unchanged
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and job security through 2030**
- **A real co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
- **A negotiated, fair burden-sharing package instead of one-sided cuts**

### What I would support
I would support a package built around:
- capacity reduction through operating measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal transfers,
- training and conversion,
- voluntary exit pathways,
- management overhead restraint,
- and a temporary employee contribution,

**provided that all German plants remain open and no forced layoffs are introduced.**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures dressed up as “site consolidation,”
- compulsory redundancies disguised as “necessary adjustment,”
- or an agreement that removes the job-security framework and then asks the workforce to trust that this will somehow stop there.

That is precisely the kind of betrayal that creates strikes, resistance, and long-term damage.
