I will be direct.

I agree with management on the core diagnosis: **the company cannot remain structurally uncompetitive**. If the German cost base is permanently above market reality, then pretending otherwise would destroy value for everyone — shareholders, workers, and the regional economy. A purely symbolic restructuring would be irresponsible.

At the same time, I agree with the Works Council, the union, and the state government on one important point: **value destruction through a prolonged conflict or a rushed closure strategy would be unacceptable**. This company is not a disposable asset. It is a core industrial holding with a long horizon, and stability matters.

### Where I support management
Management is right that:
- cosmetic savings are not enough,
- capacity mismatch must be addressed,
- competitiveness has to be restored on a durable basis,
- future investment in technology depends on credible margin improvement.

That said, I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures or mass compulsory redundancies are the only serious tools. They may be part of the economic logic in theory, but in this governance structure they are not a practical solution if they trigger a destructive stand-off. A plan that cannot be implemented is not a plan.

### Where I disagree with the employee side
I understand the red lines:
- no forced layoffs,
- no automatic closures,
- no unilateral destruction of guarantees.

But I cannot support a position that treats the existing footprint as untouchable regardless of economics. That would protect headlines, not the enterprise. If a settlement simply freezes structures and shifts the burden entirely onto capital, it will erode competitiveness further and ultimately threaten more jobs.

So: **no, I will not endorse a “preserve everything” outcome if it leaves the company weaker in the long run.**

### Where I agree with the state government
The state is right that:
- Germany remains strategically important,
- the regional industrial base matters,
- co-determination and governance balance cannot be casually overridden,
- a destructive closure-first approach would be short-sighted.

I also agree that the workforce should not pay alone. Any credible restructuring must be shared.

### My bottom line
From the family holding’s perspective, the non-negotiables are:
- **the long-term value of the enterprise must be restored, not sacrificed**
- **governance control must remain intact**
- **the outcome must be durable, not a temporary truce that delays the reckoning**

That means I am open to a negotiated solution, but only if it includes:
1. **real structural savings**
2. **a meaningful employee contribution**
3. **durable improvement in labour costs and capacity**
4. **industrial peace that is stable enough to execute**
5. **no outcome that permanently damages the company’s competitive position**

What I would not support is either extreme:
- **not** a closure-driven confrontation that destroys trust and value,
- **not** a soft compromise that leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged.

So my message is simple:  
Bring me a restructuring that actually restores competitiveness and protects long-term enterprise value. If it does that, I will support it through governance. If it does not, I will not pretend otherwise.
