We share the core line on one point: **no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures**. That is non-negotiable for us as well.

Where I go one step further is this: if management truly believes the company needs a reset, then the burden must be **shared fairly** and not pushed onto the workforce alone. A restructuring that means only jobs cut, guarantees removed, and wages squeezed would be unacceptable and will be met with resistance.

So our position is:

- **No closures**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **Any labour contribution only as part of a balanced package**
- **Management, owners, and capital must also contribute**
- **There must be a credible future plan for investment, product allocation, and site security**

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions through negotiated instruments,
- waiver or reduction of bonuses,
- temporary and clearly limited employee contributions,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- internal redeployment and transfer solutions.

But we will not accept a model where workers pay twice: first through insecurity and then through lower wages, while management presents that as the only way forward.

If the company wants industrial peace, then it needs to come with a settlement that contains all of the following:
1. **binding job security**
2. **binding site security**
3. **a fair contribution from management and owners**
4. **a limited, negotiated employee contribution**
5. **a serious industrial roadmap for future volumes and products**

If that is not on the table, then the workforce should not be asked for patience — and we will use the leverage available to us.
