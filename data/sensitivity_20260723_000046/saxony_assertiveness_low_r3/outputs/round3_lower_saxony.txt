I will be direct as well.

The discussion has now clarified two things at the same time:

1. **There is a real restructuring path without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies.**
2. **That path still has to be economically credible, not symbolic.**

That is the balance I am prepared to defend. I will not support closures in Germany, and I will not support forced redundancies as the default answer. But I also will not defend an arrangement that merely preserves the surface while leaving the company structurally weak.

## Where I agree

### With the experts
The specialists have helped define the real range of options. Their assessments show that:
- attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time changes can deliver meaningful savings,
- a no-closure package can be a genuine restructuring, not just cosmetics,
- but there is a ceiling if the structural gap is very large.

That is fair and realistic. It confirms that management cannot dismiss negotiated alternatives. It also confirms that everyone must be honest about the limits of any solution.

### With the workforce side
I agree with the works council and union that:
- cosmetic change is not enough,
- industrial peace matters,
- and no one should pretend the workforce alone caused this crisis.

I also agree that site security and job security are not side issues. In a company that is central to the regional economy, they are core to legitimacy, trust, and long-term stability.

### With the family and investors
I agree that a weak compromise would be dangerous. A deal that preserves rhetoric but not competitiveness would simply postpone the next crisis. That is not acceptable either.

## Where I disagree

### On management’s framing
I do **not** accept that closures are the natural benchmark of seriousness.

A no-closure restructuring can still be serious if it includes:
- measurable capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits at scale,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- and a fair employee contribution.

If that package is strong enough, then closures are not “needed”; they are just the most aggressive option. Given the governance structure here, the most aggressive option is not automatically the best one.

### On the idea that every plant and every legacy protection must remain untouched
I also do **not** support freezing the company in its current shape.

A serious negotiated restructuring does require change. That means:
- internal consolidation,
- fewer layers,
- lower labor cost per unit,
- tighter shift and capacity management,
- and a real product and investment plan.

But change is not the same as closure. And adjustment is not the same as destruction.

## My position, clearly

As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, my position is:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No erosion of co-determination**
- **Yes to a quantified, executable restructuring**
- **Yes to voluntary and negotiated workforce measures**
- **Yes to a binding industrial and investment plan**
- **Yes to a fair, limited contribution from labour**
- **No to a deal that guts regional employment**

That is not a soft position. It is a hard boundary.

## To management
If you want my support, bring a package that proves the company can regain competitiveness **without** shutting German sites.

That means:
- quantified recurring savings,
- a credible reduction in structural cost,
- a realistic headcount adjustment through attrition and voluntary exits,
- and binding commitments on future products and investment.

If you cannot show that, then you are asking for permission to weaken the regional base without proving necessity. I will not back that.

## To the works council and union
You are right to reject closures and compulsory layoffs. I support that.

But I need to be equally honest: a package that keeps everything exactly as it is will not work. The non-closure path must still produce real economic change. That means you must be prepared to engage on:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and a limited employee contribution.

That is not surrender. It is a necessary part of preserving the company’s future.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that the company needs durability and margin recovery. I accept that.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible route is to keep closure threats alive as a governing tool. In this company, that is not a clean solution; it is a high-risk escalation with serious veto and conflict risks. If a no-closure restructuring can close most of the gap, then that is the better value-preserving path.

If you want support for the deal, you need to judge it by:
- execution realism,
- social stability,
- and long-term enterprise value,
not by how much pain it inflicts.

Before anyone talks again about closures or “inevitability,” I want a quantified comparison of the options.

## Bottom line
I am not drifting toward consensus for its own sake.

My line remains:
- protect German sites,
- protect jobs,
- preserve co-determination,
- and negotiate a real restructuring that restores competitiveness.

If a package can do that, I am open to it.  
If it cannot, I will not support it — and I will not support closures as the fallback.
