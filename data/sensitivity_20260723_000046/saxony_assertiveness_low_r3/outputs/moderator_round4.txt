## Core impasse

The discussion is now blocked on one central point:

**Whether the quantified no-closure package is sufficient to close the remaining recurring savings gap, or whether the residual gap still justifies closures / compulsory redundancies as a fallback.**

That impasse has three layers:
1. **Economic sufficiency**: how large the remaining gap is after strong no-closure measures.
2. **Governance credibility**: whether any closure-based fallback is executable without triggering value-destroying conflict.
3. **Security and sequencing**: whether workforce protections can remain binding while still allowing enough adjustment to restore competitiveness.

In short:  
**Employee side wants “prove the gap first, then discuss anything beyond no closures.”**  
**Management/capital want “show that no-closure measures are enough; if not, keep footprint changes available.”**

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

### Proposal 1: Quantified no-closure first, conditional second-stage review
A structured settlement could be built in two phases:

- **Phase 1:** implement a strong no-closure package immediately:
  - voluntary exits
  - attrition / non-replacement
  - redeployment / qualification
  - working-time and shift changes
  - productivity measures
  - time-limited employee contribution

- **Phase 2:** pre-agree a **review trigger** after a defined measurement period, based on audited savings against a jointly agreed baseline.

**Purpose:**  
This preserves the no-closure commitment for now while allowing management and capital a verified fallback review if the package underperforms.

---

### Proposal 2: No closures, but stronger operational flexibility and deeper labor contribution
A second bridge would be:

- keep all German plants open,
- prohibit compulsory redundancies,
- retain co-determination,
- but extend the negotiated package with:
  - more ambitious shift rationalization,
  - longer-duration working-time adjustments,
  - stronger temporary wage-related contribution,
  - stricter redeployment obligations,
  - and enforceable productivity milestones.

**Purpose:**  
This increases the economic depth of the no-closure route so management/capital can test sufficiency without immediate footprint reduction.

---

### Proposal 3: Site protection in exchange for binding capacity and product commitments
A third compromise could link workforce concessions to hard management commitments:

- no plant closures and no forced layoffs,
- in return for:
  - binding investment commitments,
  - confirmed product allocation,
  - measurable capacity reduction through non-closure means,
  - and explicit implementation milestones.

**Purpose:**  
This addresses the employee side’s security concerns while giving management and investors a credible plan for structural adjustment.
