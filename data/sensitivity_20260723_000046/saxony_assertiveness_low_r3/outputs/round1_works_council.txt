As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for a restructuring that management failed to prepare properly.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. If the company needs a restructuring, then it must come through negotiated, socially responsible measures: capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, working-time solutions, qualification and transformation programs, and a binding industrial strategy that protects German sites.

My priorities are straightforward:

- secure every German plant
- protect jobs, especially against forced layoffs
- preserve co-determination and real worker influence over any restructuring
- achieve binding guarantees through 2030
- ensure that any employee contribution is fair, limited, and tied to a credible future plan

If management wants cooperation, it must come with concrete guarantees: no closures, no forced layoffs, and a binding commitment to the long-term future of the sites. Without that, we will resist this proposal with all available means, including mobilising the workforce and using our rights in the supervisory board.
