The expert’s assessment is useful, and it actually confirms part of our position: **a no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy restructuring can deliver real savings**. That matters. It means management cannot credibly say, “there is no alternative.”

But I also want to be just as clear about the limits. The expert’s numbers show exactly why we keep insisting on a **fair package**: if the company needs a durable reset, then workers cannot be the only side making permanent sacrifices while ownership and management insist on structural pain only from labor.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert on several points:

- **Cosmetic savings are not enough.**
- **Voluntary exits, attrition, working-time changes, and redeployment can produce meaningful savings.**
- A negotiated package can indeed create a **competitiveness bridge** without closures.
- The success of such a package depends on **real product and volume commitments**, not just labor concessions.

That reinforces our own proposal: **site security, no forced layoffs, and negotiated adjustment instruments**.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with management and, to some extent, the shareholders when they imply that the only credible restructuring is one that keeps plant closures on the table as a default weapon.

The expert has just laid out that:
- a **strong negotiated restructuring without closures** can achieve roughly **10–20% headcount reduction over time**,
- with **6–12% labor-cost reduction** on the affected base,
- which is not trivial.

So let’s be honest: **this is not a refusal to restructure**. It is a refusal to destroy sites and job security when other substantial instruments exist.

What I will not accept is the familiar pattern:
1. management demands “real change,”
2. workers are told to concede first and more,
3. closures remain as a pressure tool,
4. and then the final package still leaves labor carrying the main burden.

That would violate fairness and would not produce durable industrial peace.

## What the expert’s numbers mean for us
The expert says no-closure measures can be enough for a **competitiveness bridge** and possibly a meaningful restructuring. That is exactly why we say:

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Yes to negotiated capacity reduction**
- **Yes to working-time flexibility**
- **Yes to voluntary exit options**
- **Yes to limited and time-bound employee contributions**
- **Yes to internal redeployment and qualification**
- **Yes to product and investment commitments**

But the contribution must be shared. If the company wants labor to accept wage moderation, bonus restraint, or working-time concessions, then management and owners must also commit to:
- no dividend-driven pressure tactics,
- a binding investment plan,
- transparent volume allocation,
- and protection of the collective bargaining framework.

## My bottom line
The expert has shown that a socially responsible restructuring is **possible**. That means the burden of proof is now squarely on management:

- If you want change, bring a package that **does not require closures or forced layoffs**.
- If you want employee contributions, make them **limited, negotiated, and matched by contributions from capital and management**.
- If you want industrial peace, stop using plant closures as the opening move.

We are still prepared to negotiate seriously. But our red lines remain:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **no hollowing out of the collective wage framework**

If management can bring forward a quantified package using the expert’s logic — with fair burden-sharing and binding future commitments — we will engage. If not, then there is no basis for asking the workforce to absorb the pain alone.
