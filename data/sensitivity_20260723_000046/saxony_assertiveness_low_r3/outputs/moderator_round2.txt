## Neutral Moderator Summary

### 1) What has been agreed
- **Cosmetic savings are insufficient**; all sides accept that a real restructuring must be durable.
- There is broad agreement that **a prolonged conflict would destroy value**.
- Most stakeholders accept that **non-closure measures can generate meaningful savings**, including:
  - attrition / non-replacement
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment and qualification
  - working-time reductions / shift changes
  - some form of labor-cost contribution
- There is shared recognition that any solution needs:
  - **credible future product/investment commitments**
  - **quantified savings**
  - **an executable implementation path**

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures vs. no-closure restructuring**
  - Works council, union, and state government reject closures as the default path.
  - Management, investors, and parts of the family holding see closures as potentially necessary if non-closure measures do not close the cost gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee-side stakeholders reject them categorically.
  - Management and investors keep them as an implied fallback if savings are insufficient.
- **Scale of employee contribution**
  - Employee side supports limited, negotiated contributions.
  - Management/investors/family want a more substantial and durable labor-cost reduction.
- **What counts as “enough”**
  - Employee side sees a strong no-closure package as a viable restructuring.
  - Management/investors/family view it as potentially useful but possibly insufficient for structural overcapacity.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Economic adequacy vs. social acceptability**
  - How much savings are required, and how much can be delivered without closures or forced layoffs?
- **Speed vs. stability**
  - Faster structural cost cuts may raise conflict and execution risk.
- **Short-term burden vs. long-term viability**
  - Voluntary exits, wage moderation, and working-time changes may preserve sites but require concessions and may not fully solve overcapacity.
- **Execution risk**
  - A closure-based plan may be stronger on paper but harder to implement in this governance setting.
- **Burden-sharing**
  - No common formula yet for how costs should be split among labor, management, owners, and investors.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **quantified scenarios**:
  1. no-closure package
  2. closure-based package
  3. hybrid package
- For each, specify:
  - expected savings
  - one-time costs
  - timing
  - execution risk
  - impact on sites, jobs, and governance
- The next round should focus on **what exact combination of measures closes the gap while remaining executable and politically sustainable**.
