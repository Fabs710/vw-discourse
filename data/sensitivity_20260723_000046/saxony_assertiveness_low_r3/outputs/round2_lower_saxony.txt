My position remains unchanged in its core: **I cannot support plant closures in Germany, and I cannot support compulsory redundancies as the default answer.** The regional state government will not back a solution that tears out jobs and industrial capacity in our state, especially when there are still credible alternative instruments on the table.

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that:
- cosmetic measures are insufficient,
- the company needs durable cost improvement,
- and a credible industrial plan is necessary.

The expert confirms that point. So nobody here should pretend that a few temporary concessions would solve this. They would not.

### With the workforce side
I also agree with the Works Council and the union that:
- the workforce cannot be made to pay alone,
- industrial peace matters,
- and site security is not a side issue but central to the company’s legitimacy and future.

The expert’s assessment actually strengthens the case for a **negotiated restructuring path** rather than a closure confrontation. That is important.

## Where I disagree

### On management’s line
Management keeps treating closures as the implicit benchmark for seriousness. I do **not** accept that framing.

The expert has shown there is a real restructuring path through:
- attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- and labour-cost contribution.

That may not satisfy a maximalist cost-cutting target, but it is not trivial. It can be a genuine restructuring if it is well designed and backed by a serious product and investment plan.

So I reject the idea that:
- “no closures” means “no restructuring,” or
- “no forced redundancies” means “no competitiveness.”

That is simply not true.

### On the union and Works Council
At the same time, I have to be candid: if the employee side insists that **every current structure must remain untouched**, then that also becomes unsustainable. The expert has made that clear.

So I support the red lines on:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- preservation of co-determination,
- protection of the company’s regional role.

But I do expect the workforce side to engage seriously on:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- and a limited, fair contribution.

A blanket “nothing changes” position would not be responsible either.

## My bottom line

For the state government, the acceptable path is a **socially negotiated restructuring** that:
1. preserves German plants,
2. avoids compulsory redundancies,
3. delivers real but measured cost reduction,
4. protects co-determination and the state’s veto role,
5. and is tied to a credible future industrial and investment plan.

What I will **not** support:
- a closure-led restructuring,
- a strategy that forces the state and workforce into a corner,
- or an outcome that strips Germany of jobs and long-term industrial capacity.

What I **can** support:
- a substantial negotiated package,
- with quantified savings,
- a clear timeline,
- fair burden-sharing,
- and binding commitments on future products and investment.
