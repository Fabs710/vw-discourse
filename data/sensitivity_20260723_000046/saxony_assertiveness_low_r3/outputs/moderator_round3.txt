## Neutral Moderator Summary — Round 3

### 1) What has been agreed
- **All sides accept that cosmetic measures are insufficient.**
- **All sides accept that some form of restructuring is necessary.**
- **There is broad agreement that non-closure measures can produce real savings**, including:
  - voluntary exits,
  - attrition/non-replacement,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - shift rationalization,
  - and limited employee contributions.
- **All sides agree that a prolonged conflict would damage value and execution.**
- There is also shared interest in a **binding industrial and investment framework** tied to future products and competitiveness.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and state government reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures may still be needed if the gap cannot be closed otherwise.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side and state reject them.  
  - Management and capital side keep them at least conceptually in play if no-closure measures prove insufficient.
- **What counts as “enough” restructuring:**  
  - Employee/state side define “enough” as competitiveness restored **without** closures and forced layoffs.  
  - Management/capital side define “enough” as whatever **durably closes the cost and capacity gap**, even if that requires footprint changes.
- **Binding job-security horizon:**  
  - Employee side wants strong guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management and capital side are not yet willing to accept that as a fixed constraint.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Magnitude of the savings gap:**  
  The parties still lack a common quantified baseline for how much recurring savings are required.
- **Sufficiency of no-closure measures:**  
  It remains unresolved whether voluntary exits, working-time changes, and productivity measures can close most of the gap or only part of it.
- **Execution risk vs. economic ambition:**  
  A stronger package may be economically better but harder to implement; a weaker package may be more acceptable but insufficient.
- **One-time restructuring costs vs. long-term savings:**  
  Severance, transfer, and qualification costs have not yet been weighed against the durability of the savings.
- **Governance and industrial peace:**  
  The balance between preserving co-determination/site security and achieving a structurally viable cost base remains open.
- **Impact on regional employment and long-term investment:**  
  The discussion has not yet resolved how much capacity reduction is needed to support future products and return-on-sales targets.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **numbers**:
  - required annual recurring savings,
  - expected savings from no-closure measures,
  - remaining gap,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - and execution/strike risk.
- Compare **three scenarios** explicitly:
  1. strong no-closure restructuring,
  2. hybrid restructuring,
  3. closure-based restructuring.
- Ask each side to identify **which trade-off it is prepared to accept** if the quantified gap cannot be fully closed without footprint changes.
