Management, the family holding, and the investors keep talking about “economic reality” as if it were a one-way street that only the workforce must walk down. I reject that framing.

Let me be equally clear again: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the job-security framework through 2030.** Those are not bargaining chips. They are the minimum conditions for any responsible restructuring.

### Where I agree
I agree with one point from management, the family, and the investors: **cosmetic savings are not enough**. A serious restructuring must be durable, not just a press release. And yes, the company needs a credible future industrial plan, investment, and product commitments.

I also agree that a prolonged conflict would hurt everyone. That is exactly why management should stop pushing a closure logic that provokes confrontation and instead negotiate a real, socially responsible solution.

### Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the idea that competitiveness can only be restored by:
- closing plants,
- cutting jobs by force,
- or breaking long-standing protections that were part of the social contract in this company.

That is not “structural adjustment”; it is shifting management’s strategic failure onto the workforce.

The works council and the workforce did not create overcapacity alone. Management made the strategic decisions. The owners approved them. Now the company is asking workers to pay the full bill through insecurity and sacrifice. That is not fair, and we will not accept it.

### On management’s argument
You say temporary measures are not enough. Fine — then bring forward **real alternative structural measures** that do not destroy sites or livelihoods:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- voluntary attrition,
- qualification and transfer schemes,
- capacity adjustments through shifts and lines,
- fair reductions in bonuses and other temporary employee contributions,
- management and shareholder contributions to the restructuring burden.

If those measures are not being explored seriously, then the issue is not that employees are inflexible — it is that management wants the quickest path, not the fairest or the most sustainable one.

### On the family holding
You say you will not support a soft compromise that leaves the company weak. I say the same about a hard-line closure plan: it may look decisive, but it can destroy trust, industrial peace, and the company’s long-term capability. A company that burns through its social compact is also destroying value.

If the family wants long-term value, then it should back a solution that protects the industrial base in Germany, preserves co-determination, and avoids irreversible damage.

### On the investors
You are right that a weak cosmetic deal is unacceptable. But you are wrong if you think the only credible solution is to force structural pain onto the workforce. Value creation is not just cost cutting. It is also execution stability, skilled labour retention, and industrial peace.

A strike, legal conflict, and reputational damage are not “side effects”; they are the direct result of refusing to respect the workforce’s red lines.

### On the state government
I welcome your clear support for site security and no forced layoffs. But support must be backed by action. If there is any attempt to bypass the state’s blocking position or to present a closure package as a fait accompli, we will treat that as a direct escalation.

### What we will accept
We are prepared to discuss:
- negotiated capacity reductions **without closures**
- voluntary rather than compulsory job reductions
- internal mobility across plants
- shorter working time and flexible working-time instruments
- limited and time-bound employee contributions
- qualification, transformation, and investment programs
- a binding future product allocation for German sites

### What we will not accept
- any German plant closure
- compulsory redundancies
- erosion of co-determination
- removal or hollowing out of the job-security guarantee
- a restructuring that treats workers as the only adjustable cost factor

### Bottom line
If management wants a deal, it must come back with a package that is:
1. **site-secure**
2. **job-secure**
3. **socially fair**
4. **co-determined**
5. **credible for the future**

If it cannot do that, then we will continue to resist with every legitimate means available — including mobilisation of the workforce and using our rights in governance.
