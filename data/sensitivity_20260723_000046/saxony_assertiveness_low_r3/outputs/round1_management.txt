I hear the clear red lines from both the Works Council and the union, but I need to be equally clear from management’s side:

**We cannot accept a settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.**  
A purely defensive package of guarantees, internal shifts, and temporary measures is not enough. If we do that, we postpone the problem, burn cash, and weaken the brand further. That is not a responsible outcome for the company, its long-term jobs, or its shareholders.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- the workforce did not create this crisis alone,
- forced redundancies are not the starting point we prefer,
- transformation needs a credible industrial roadmap,
- employee contributions must be tied to a broader package, not demanded in isolation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** agree that:
- **all German plants can simply be protected regardless of economics**,
- job guarantees can remain unchanged if the business case has fundamentally deteriorated,
- the company can restore competitiveness without **real capacity reduction and labour-cost contribution**.

Let me be precise:  
**If the outcome is only working-time tweaks, soft redeployment, and temporary concessions, then it is not sufficient.** It does not solve overcapacity. It does not close the gap to competitors. It does not get us back to the required return on sales. And it does not create the funding room for future products and technology.

### What management needs
For a deal to be credible, it must include:
- **durable capacity reduction**
- **a meaningful and sustained labour-cost contribution**
- **structural savings, not cosmetic savings**
- **a clear path to restoring competitiveness by 2030**

That means we are discussing more than “pain-sharing.” We are discussing whether the company actually restructures its cost base.

### On closures
I am not here to announce closure for its own sake. But I will not rule out plant closures if that is what the economics require. The governance structure means this cannot be imposed unilaterally, and I recognize that. But equally, that does not change the underlying reality: **some sites may no longer be viable in their current form**.

### On jobs and guarantees
A blanket extension of long-standing job-security guarantees through 2030, with no structural adjustment, is not acceptable. Those guarantees were based on a different business environment. The business environment has changed materially.

### On a possible settlement
I am open to a negotiated solution if — and only if — it produces:
- a **durable cost reduction**
- a **credible reduction in overcapacity**
- a **genuine employee contribution**
- and enough flexibility to protect the company’s future investment capacity

That could include capacity consolidation, working-time measures, internal redeployment, and other negotiated instruments — **but only if they add up to real structural savings**. I will not sign up to a package that simply preserves the current footprint and expects management to absorb the losses.

So my message is simple:  
**We are prepared to negotiate, but not to pretend.**  
If the employee side wants industrial peace, then the proposal on the table must confront the cost problem directly.
