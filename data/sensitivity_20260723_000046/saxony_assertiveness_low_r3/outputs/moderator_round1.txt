### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement on several points:
- The company needs a credible restructuring and a path back to competitiveness.
- Purely cosmetic or temporary measures are not sufficient.
- A prolonged conflict would be damaging for jobs, operations, and value.
- Any solution should be negotiated and should include some form of shared burden.
- Internal redeployment, working-time adjustments, qualification, and transformation measures are all on the table in principle.
- A future industrial plan with clearer product and investment commitments is considered necessary.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main disputes are still unresolved:
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state government reject closures in Germany; management, family holding, and investors will not rule them out if economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side rejects forced layoffs; management and capital want meaningful structural change and have not accepted a strict no-redundancy approach as sufficient.
- **Job-security guarantees:** Employee and state side want binding protection through 2030; management and capital say existing guarantees may not be compatible with the new business reality.
- **Scope of employee contribution:** Workers’ representatives accept only limited, negotiated contributions; management and shareholders want a larger, durable cost contribution.
- **How much structural change is required:** Some parties want site preservation first, while others insist that real capacity reduction must be part of any deal.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several central trade-offs remain open:
- **Site security vs. cost competitiveness**
- **No forced layoffs vs. achieving sufficient savings**
- **Negotiated social protection vs. rapid structural adjustment**
- **Preserving co-determination and political support vs. management flexibility**
- **Short-term industrial peace vs. long-term margin recovery**
- **Protecting current footprint vs. consolidating capacity to fund future investment**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on a concrete package rather than repeating red lines:
- Which specific sites or capacities, if any, are under discussion?
- What quantified savings are needed to meet the competitiveness target?
- What can employees contribute, and for how long?
- What binding guarantees or investment commitments can management and owners offer in return?
- What combination of measures could achieve restructuring without an open conflict?

A useful next step would be to test a few package options against two criteria: **economic sufficiency** and **social/political acceptability**.
