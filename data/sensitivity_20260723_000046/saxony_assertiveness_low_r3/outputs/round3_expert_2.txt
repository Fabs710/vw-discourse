## Bottom line
A **no-closure package can be sufficient only if it closes most of the recurring cost gap** to the brand’s return-on-sales target. In this governance setup, the key question is not whether labor can contribute — it clearly can — but whether the contribution is **large, durable, and executable enough** to make plant closures unnecessary.

## Indicative minimum threshold
For a large German OEM with structural overcapacity, I would normally test sufficiency against this kind of benchmark:

- **Recurring annual savings needed:** roughly **high hundreds of millions of euros** if the brand’s cost base is materially above target.
- **Labor-only no-closure contribution likely achievable:** often **€300 million to €800 million** in a strong negotiated package, depending on workforce size, wage structure, and the depth of headcount reduction.
- **Therefore:** a no-closure deal is only credible if it covers **a material majority of the gap**, not just a symbolic share.

## What can realistically drive those savings
A serious package without plant closures typically combines:

1. **Attrition / non-replacement**
2. **Voluntary exits / severance programs**
3. **Working-time reductions and shift rationalization**
4. **Redeployment / qualification / internal mobility**
5. **Wage moderation, premium removal, or temporary labor-cost contributions**
6. **Productivity measures and management overhead reduction**

The strongest recurring savings usually come from:
- permanent headcount reduction,
- structural working-time change,
- and a durable labor-cost reset.

## Practical rule
If after all no-closure measures the company still has:

- materially excess fixed capacity,
- a labor cost base still above peers,
- and an ROS still below target,

then management will continue to have an economically plausible case for **selective consolidation or closures**.

## Turnaround view
From a restructuring perspective, the choice is:

- **No-closure package succeeds** if it delivers a **durable competitiveness bridge** with acceptable one-time costs and low execution risk.
- **No-closure package fails** if it preserves sites but leaves the underlying cost structure intact enough that the next crisis is merely delayed.
