We agree with the core diagnosis from management, the family holding, and the experts: **the German cost base is too high, and cosmetic measures will not fix it**. As capital-market shareholders, that is our starting point as well. We want a durable return to competitive margins, not a temporary truce that delays the problem.

That said, we also agree with the state government and, to a lesser extent, the workforce side on one important practical point: **a prolonged conflict would destroy value**. A strike-heavy or closure-war outcome would hit earnings, delay the turnaround, and likely force the market to price in a weaker long-term business. We do not support that path.

Where we differ from the employee side is on the implied limit of change.  
A blanket **“no closures, no forced redundancies, no deep labour contribution”** position is not acceptable if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. The expert testimony made clear that no-closure measures can be real, but they have a ceiling. That ceiling matters to us. If the package cannot close most of the competitiveness gap, then it is not sufficient.

Where we differ from management and the family holding is on the temptation to treat closures as the default benchmark of seriousness. In this governance structure, a closure-first strategy may look theoretically clean, but **if it triggers vetoes, industrial escalation, and execution failure, it destroys value**. We would not back a plan that is “optimal” on paper but impossible in practice.

So our position is this:

- **We support real restructuring, not symbolic compromise.**
- **We support meaningful, quantified, durable cost reduction.**
- **We support capacity reduction and workforce adjustment if they are real and executable.**
- **We do not support an outcome that preserves the legacy cost base and calls it transformation.**
- **We do not support a closure war that creates value destruction and governance deadlock.**

What would move us is a package with:
1. **clear, quantified savings**
2. **durable headcount and capacity reduction**
3. **working-time, redeployment, and voluntary exit measures used aggressively**
4. **a measurable employee contribution**
5. **binding product and investment commitments**
6. **a credible implementation timetable**
7. **enough industrial peace to actually deliver the plan**

If the no-closure route can genuinely deliver a meaningful share of the needed savings, then we can support it as the better risk-adjusted option. But if it is only a bridge that leaves the company still above target on cost and still below target on margins, then we will not pretend it is good enough.

We are not here to defend any stakeholder’s preferred storyline. We are here to protect long-term equity value. That means:
- no cosmetic deal,
- no prolonged conflict,
- and no restructuring that fails to restore competitiveness.

If management can bring a quantified package that is operationally credible and strong enough to fix the cost base, we will back it. If not, we will say so clearly.
