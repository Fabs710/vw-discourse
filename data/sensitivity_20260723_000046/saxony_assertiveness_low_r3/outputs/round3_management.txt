- a prolonged conflict would damage value,

I do **not** accept the claim that the mere existence of a no-closure path makes closures irrelevant.

The experts have now said twice what management has been saying all along:

- non-closure measures have a **ceiling**
- a deep structural cost reset may still require more
- and if the gap is severe, a no-closure package may be **insufficient**

That is the reality you are avoiding.

You keep talking as if preserving every site and every legacy guarantee is compatible with restoring competitiveness. It may preserve the footprint, but it may also preserve the uncompetitive cost base. That would not be a turnaround. It would be managed decline with nicer language.

- lower fixed cost per unit,

not just temporary pain reduction.

There is no investor case for a sentimental compromise that leaves return on sales below target and starves future investment.

## What would move management
I am not asking for blind acceptance. I am asking for a package that **actually works**.

Specifically, I would consider a no-closure route acceptable only if it delivers all of the following:
1. **substantial, quantified capacity reduction**
2. **durable labor cost reduction, not just temporary relief**
3. **a meaningful headcount reduction over time**
4. **working-time and shift changes with real economic impact**
5. **voluntary exits at scale**
6. **binding implementation milestones**
7. **clear product and investment commitments**
8. **a credible path to restore return on sales by 2030**

If those conditions are met, management does not need closures as the first answer.

If they are **not** met, then keeping closures on the table is not a threat — it is honesty.
