## First: where I agree
I agree with the expert, the state shareholder, and even management on one point: **the company needs a durable, quantified restructuring**.  
I also agree that a half-measure would fail and that industrial peace matters.

But that is where the agreement ends.

## Where I strongly disagree

### 1) Management is still treating closures and forced redundancies as the benchmark of seriousness
That is exactly the wrong benchmark.

A “serious” restructuring is not one that sacrifices German sites and jobs first. A serious restructuring is one that:
- fixes the cost base,
- protects industrial capability,
- preserves the workforce’s know-how,
- and remains implementable without blowing up labour relations.

You have not proved that plant closures are required. You have only repeated that they are convenient for management. That is not the same thing.

### 2) Investors and the controlling family are overclaiming the economics
You keep saying that closure-free solutions are “too weak” unless proven otherwise. Fine — then prove that the closure-based path is economically worth the social, political, industrial, and governance damage it causes.

Because closures are not free:
- they destroy know-how,
- they damage supplier networks,
- they trigger conflict,
- they create political backlash,
- and they risk making future launches harder.

If you want to argue for closures, you need to show that the gain truly outweighs those costs. So far, I do not see that case made convincingly.

### 3) The state shareholder is correct to reject closures, but not yet forceful enough
I appreciate the state’s position, but a “preference” is not enough.

If the state shareholder believes in regional industrial continuity, then it must use its blocking power to make it real. That means:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- and no hidden backdoor restructuring that achieves the same result later.

Anything less is just verbal comfort.

## On the expert’s numbers
The expert has given ranges, not company facts. That matters.

The ranges show exactly what we have been saying:
- closure-free restructuring can be smaller in savings,
- hybrid can be deeper,
- but none of that automatically means closures are unavoidable.

What the numbers do **not** show is that closure-free restructuring is impossible.  
They show only that it must be **deep, durable, and negotiated**.

And that is what we are willing to do.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a **closure-free restructuring package** that includes:
- shift reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary wage restraint if strictly time-limited and fairly shared,
- voluntary departures only,
- redeployment and retraining,
- internal consolidation of production,
- and commitments on future products and investment.

But we are **not** prepared to discuss:
- plant closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or any “temporary exception” that becomes permanent later.
