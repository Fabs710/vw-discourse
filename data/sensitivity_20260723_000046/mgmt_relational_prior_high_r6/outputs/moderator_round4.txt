## Core impasse

The central deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; all sides now accept that it must be **durable, quantified, and implemented**.

The impasse is specifically over **the instrument mix**:

1. **Management / capital side** wants a package whose savings and capacity reduction are strong enough to be economically equivalent to closures if possible, and is not willing to rule closures out.
2. **Works council / union / state shareholder** insist that **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table** and want a closure-free or at least closure-avoiding path.
3. **The expert analysis** indicates that closure-free solutions are possible in principle, but may be insufficient unless unusually deep and durable.
4. **The unresolved issue** is therefore whether the company can produce a **closure-free or hybrid package that closes the quantified gap through 2030** with acceptable governance, labor burden, and execution risk.

In short:  
**the debate has shifted from “whether” to “how deep,” but the sides disagree on whether a no-closure solution can be deep enough.**

---

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Two-track restructuring with a binding review gate
A compromise structure could be:

- **Track A:** immediate closure-free measures
  - shift reductions
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment/retraining
  - temporary wage restraint / special-payment moderation
  - productivity measures
  - internal consolidation

- **Track B:** a pre-agreed fallback mechanism
  - if independent verification shows the package misses defined savings or capacity targets by a set date, the parties reopen a limited set of additional measures
  - these would be **predefined and capped**, rather than an open-ended closure mandate

This would let employee representatives keep closures off the table initially, while giving management and capital a credible enforcement backstop.

---

### Proposal 2: “No closures now” plus quantified structural equivalents
A second bridge would be to convert the no-closure demand into a **numbers-based equivalence test**:

The employee side would accept that closure-free measures must deliver:
- a defined annual run-rate savings target,
- a defined permanent capacity reduction target,
- a defined headcount path,
- and durability through 2030,

with an **independent expert certifying whether the package is actually equivalent** to the required restructuring effect.

This would not concede closures, but it would force the closure-free offer to be judged against hard industrial-financial metrics rather than rhetoric.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-specific differentiation instead of blanket rules
A third compromise is to avoid one universal rule for all German sites:

- **Core sites** could receive stronger protection, investment commitments, and no-closure assurance.
- **Non-core or structurally weak operations** could face deeper internal consolidation, shift cuts, and selective outsourcing.
- If absolutely necessary, only **partial line rationalization or non-production consolidation** would be considered before any full site closure.

This may help reconcile the state’s regional mandate with management’s need for structural simplification.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact minimum package, in euros and capacity units, would you accept as “economically equivalent” to closures if closures remain off the table?**

### To Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**What is the largest quantified annual savings and permanent capacity reduction you are prepared to deliver through closure-free measures alone, and by what date would you commit to it?**

### To Industrial Union
**Will the union accept a multi-year, binding labor contribution with measurable wage, time, and productivity changes if it is paired with enforceable job-security and investment commitments?**

### To Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to convert your anti-closure stance into a governance-backed condition with explicit performance thresholds, so that the state is not simply opposing closures but actively enabling an alternative?**

### To Controlling Family Holding
**If closure-free and hybrid options are shown to meet the required financial gap, will you commit to supporting that route even if it produces less immediate leverage than a closure-based plan?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a closure-free or hybrid package if an independent expert certifies that it delivers comparable run-rate savings, durability, and margin recovery through 2030?**

### To Independent Industrial Finance Expert
**Please define the exact threshold at which a closure-free or hybrid package becomes economically equivalent to closures, including minimum savings, capacity reduction, and durability criteria.**
