We are aligned on the core red lines: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective bargaining and job-security framework.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: management cannot solve a structural problem by pushing the full cost onto the workforce. If there is to be a deal, it must be a shared-sacrifice package. That means:

- management and the owners must contribute materially,
- labour can discuss a negotiated contribution,
- capacity can be adjusted through reversible and socially responsible measures,
- and any reduction in labour cost must be tied to binding guarantees for jobs and sites.

I support your call for a credible industrial future plan. Workers will not give up protections for vague promises. If the company wants flexibility, then it must offer certainty in return: investment commitments, product allocation, and a clear path that keeps German sites viable.

Where I disagree with management’s logic is simple: “competitiveness” does not justify making employees carry all the pain through closures, forced layoffs, and unilateral wage cuts. That would be unacceptable and will trigger resistance. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce expects us to act.

So our position together should be:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of the collective framework,
- but openness to negotiated contributions and capacity adjustments if the burden is shared fairly and the sites are secured.

If management is serious about a durable settlement, it should come back with a package that protects employment and future production in Germany rather than trying to buy competitiveness through destruction of trust.
