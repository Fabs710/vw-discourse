Let me respond directly.

I agree with three things the employee side and the state shareholder have said:

1. **This cannot be cosmetic.**  
   We do need a durable restructuring, not a press release.

2. **Industrial peace matters.**  
   A prolonged conflict would damage output, reputation, and the company’s ability to invest.

3. **Worker participation matters.**  
   Any settlement that ignores co-determination is not durable and will fail in implementation.

Where I **fundamentally disagree** is on the central claim that a credible solution can preserve every German site, rule out compulsory redundancies entirely, keep all historic guarantees intact, and still close the cost gap.

That is not a restructuring. That is a delay.

### To the Works Council and Union
You both keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of job security.” I understand the political and social logic of that position. But from an executive responsibility standpoint, it is not enough.

The problem is not abstract. We are facing:
- structurally too high factory costs,
- shrinking European demand,
- intense low-cost competition,
- and a brand that is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we leave the cost base essentially intact, the company will have less room to invest in:
- future products,
- battery and software capability,
- and the 2030 portfolio.

That is the real trade-off. Protecting every existing structure at all costs may feel safe, but it weakens the enterprise over time.

You both argue that closures destroy trust and create hidden costs. I do not deny that closures have costs. But you are ignoring the cost of **not** acting:
- continued overcapacity,
- ongoing margin erosion,
- delayed investment,
- and eventual deeper cuts later.

So I will say this plainly:  
**A package that excludes closures and mandatory workforce reductions in principle is not acceptable unless it produces equivalent, quantified, and durable savings.**

Not promises. Not flexibility “in theory.”  
**Equivalent savings, in euros, over time, with binding effect.**

### To the State Government
I appreciate your recognition that the company needs a credible restructuring. But I cannot accept the idea that “no closures” should be the starting point if the economics demand capacity reduction.

The state has a legitimate interest in regional employment and stability. So do I. But our duty is also to ensure the company survives competitively. If the cost base stays too high, everyone loses in the end — including the region.

I am open to solutions that avoid closures **if** they achieve the same structural effect:
- capacity consolidation,
- shift and working-time reform,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and a genuine labour-cost contribution.

But if the package merely preserves sites and shifts the pain into accounting tricks or temporary measures, then it will not be supportable.

### To the Controlling Family and Investors
I agree with your core point: we cannot accept a “soft” settlement that leaves the business structurally weak.

You are right to ask for:
- quantified savings,
- a lower fixed-cost base,
- durable capacity reduction,
- and a credible implementation path.

That is exactly what management must deliver.

But I also want to be clear: support for a credible restructuring cannot mean automatically insisting on closures as the only legitimate instrument. I am open on the means. What matters is the result:
- sustainable cost reduction,
- improved margins,
- and capital freed for the future.

If the employee side comes forward with a package that truly delivers that, then I will back it. If not, then yes, deeper restructuring including closures remains on the table.

### On what management is prepared to discuss
We are not here to impose for the sake of confrontation. But we are also not here to preserve the status quo.

I am prepared to discuss:
- site-specific restructuring,
- capacity consolidation,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary workforce reduction,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted pay and guarantee adjustments,
- and investment commitments tied to the agreement.

What I am **not** prepared to accept is:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a blanket no-redundancies rule,
- and a settlement that leaves the company with the same structurally uncompetitive cost base.

### The real test
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then bring a package that closes the gap in a way that is:
- **quantified,**
- **durable,**
- **implementable,**
- and **competitive against our benchmark markets.**

That means real numbers, not generalities.

So here is the management position in one sentence:

**We are willing to negotiate the form of restructuring, but not the need for deep, lasting cost and capacity reduction.**

If you want to avoid plant closures, then show us how you will deliver the same savings and flexibility. If you cannot, then you are not offering a solution — you are only asking the company to postpone the inevitable.
