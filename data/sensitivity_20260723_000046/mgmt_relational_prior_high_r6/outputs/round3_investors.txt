We are at the point where **capital markets need hard evidence, not positioning**.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the controlling family, and the expert on the central point:
- the company has a **real structural cost and capacity problem**,
- a **cosmetic settlement would destroy value**,
- and whatever is agreed must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.

We also agree with the employee side and the state shareholder on one important practical point:
- a prolonged strike or governance deadlock would be value-destructive.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the employee-side attempt to make **“no closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** the default outcome **unless** it is backed by a package that is truly equivalent in:
- annual run-rate savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- and fixed-cost relief.

Right now, the employee proposals remain too qualitative:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- future investment,
- internal restructuring.

Those are not sufficient on their own. They only matter if they **close the margin gap in hard numbers**.

We also do **not** accept the state shareholder’s implied position that preserving every German site should outrank competitiveness. It does not. If the cost base stays structurally too high, the long-term damage to enterprise value will exceed the short-term pain of restructuring.

### Our position on closures
We are **not ideologically attached** to closures.  
We are attached to **risk-adjusted value preservation**.

So our test is simple:
- if a closure-free or hybrid package is **economically equivalent, durable, and enforceable**, we can support it;
- if not, then deeper restructuring, including closures, remains justified.

That is not a threat. It is the minimum requirement for a credible capital-market position.

### What we need to see
Before we can support any deal, we need a comparative package showing, side by side:
1. baseline cost gap,
2. annual run-rate savings,
3. one-off restructuring costs,
4. headcount impact,
5. capacity reduction,
6. implementation timeline,
7. durability over the cycle,
8. governance feasibility.

Without that, there is no basis for confidence that margins will recover.

### On burden-sharing
We are open to a **hybrid** route if it delivers real value.  
But burden-sharing must be material, not symbolic:
- labor contribution,
- management discipline,
- owner support where needed,
- and genuine industrial simplification.

If labor concessions are modest and temporary, while the company keeps most of the structural overcapacity, then the package is not a solution. It is a delay.

### On industrial peace
We absolutely recognize that industrial peace has value. But peace that preserves an uncompetitive cost base is not cheap. It is deferred loss.

So our support is conditional:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to a closure-free or hybrid solution only if it matches the economics.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to a deal that leaves margins structurally impaired.
