**Neutral Moderator Summary – Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a **serious structural cost and capacity problem**.
- There is broad agreement that the solution must be **durable**, not cosmetic.
- All sides accept that **some form of restructuring** is likely necessary.
- There is shared openness, in principle, to **negotiated solutions** rather than unilateral action.
- Several parties agree that any settlement should include:
  - **investment in future products/technologies**,
  - a **credible industrial plan**,
  - and **worker participation/co-determination** in shaping the outcome.
- There is also common recognition that a prolonged conflict would be damaging.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, Union, and State Government reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, Family Holding, and Investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee representatives and the State Government reject them.
  - Management, Family Holding, and Investors insist they cannot be excluded in principle if economics require it.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030**
  - Employee side seeks binding guarantees.
  - Management and capital-side actors say blanket guarantees may be incompatible with structural change.
- **Scale of labor contribution**
  - Management, Family Holding, and Investors want a **material, quantified** labor-cost contribution.
  - Employee side is open to contribution only if burdens are shared and core protections remain.
- **Definition of a viable compromise**
  - Employee and State sides emphasize social and industrial continuity.
  - Management, Family Holding, and Investors emphasize quantified competitiveness and durable cost reduction.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are needed**, and over what time horizon, to restore competitiveness.
- **Which measures can substitute for closures/redundancies** while still producing equivalent economic effects.
- **How burden-sharing will be allocated** between labor, management, owners, and possibly the state.
- **What binding investment and product commitments** can credibly offset workforce concessions.
- **What level of flexibility** on working time, redeployment, pay, and guarantees is sufficient for each side.
- **How to ensure durability** of any agreement so it is not seen as temporary or symbolic by investors and management.
- **What governance role** the state shareholder, supervisory board, and co-determination structures will have in final approval.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **quantified options**:
- Management: specify the **gap to be closed** in euros, capacity, and timeline.
- Employee side: specify **which alternative measures** could close that gap without closures or compulsory redundancies.
- Owners/investors/state: specify **what level of savings and flexibility** would be sufficient for support.
- All sides: identify **one or two package designs** that combine savings, investment, and employment protections in a way that could be tested for durability.

A productive next step would be to compare **concrete package scenarios**, rather than restating red lines.
