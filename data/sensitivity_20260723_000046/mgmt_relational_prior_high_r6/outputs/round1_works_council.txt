As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of the historic job-security commitments that have protected our workforce for decades.

If management believes the company needs a restructuring, then it must be a restructuring that preserves every German site, protects jobs, and shares the burden fairly. That means:

- no closure of any German plant,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding job and site guarantees through 2030,
- a credible industrial plan with investment in future products and technologies,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping any solution.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives to closure and mass layoffs, including capacity adjustments, working-time measures, internal redeployment, and a fair negotiated contribution from labour — but only if the burden is shared and the core guarantees remain intact.

Management cannot present the workforce with a fait accompli and expect cooperation. If this is turned into an attack on jobs, plants, and the co-determination model, we will resist it with full force, including industrial action and use of our rights on the supervisory board.

Our position is simple: the crisis must not be solved by sacrificing the workforce.
