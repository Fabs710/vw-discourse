We are still not at a real solution if the discussion remains stuck on “closures are serious” versus “closures are unacceptable.”

### Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state shareholder, and the expert on one essential point: **this company needs a durable, quantified restructuring**.  
I also agree that a cosmetic solution, or one that just postpones the problem, would be irresponsible.

And I agree with management and the capital side on one further point: **the package must produce hard savings, not slogans**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s repeated attempt to treat plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the benchmark of seriousness.

That is not a neutral economic statement. It is a chosen instrument.

And I do **not** accept the works council’s presentation as if “no closures, no forced layoffs” by itself were already the restructuring plan. It is not. It is a boundary condition. A real plan still has to be built inside it.

So I am not moving from the union’s position:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **no destruction of the collective wage framework**
- **yes to meaningful employee contribution**
- **yes to internal capacity reduction**
- **yes to working-time flexibility**
- **yes to voluntary exits, redeployment, and qualification**
- **yes to a fair burden-sharing package**
- **no to workers bearing all the pain**

### On the expert’s ranges
The expert’s comparison is useful because it confirms something important:  
a closure-free package is **not impossible**, but it must be **deep** and **durable**.

That matters. It means the burden cannot be cosmetic:
- temporary concessions are not enough,
- symbolic savings are not enough,
- and promises without binding duration are not enough.

But the expert also makes clear that closures are not the only path to a serious outcome. That is exactly why I reject management’s framing.

### To management
You have still not answered the decisive question:

**What is the quantified gap, and what exact mix of measures closes it without closures and without forced layoffs?**

If you want labour to contribute materially, then show us:
- the annual run-rate savings you need,
- the one-off costs,
- the headcount impact,
- and the duration of the required changes.

Do not simply say “more flexibility” or “more discipline.” That is too vague to justify taking jobs off the table.

And let me be explicit: if the company’s answer is still “first accept closures and redundancies, then we’ll talk,” then there is no basis for trust and no basis for peace.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right to insist on durability and competitiveness. But you are wrong if you think only the harshest instrument proves seriousness.

A deal that:
- preserves the sites,
- protects core employment,
- lowers labour cost materially,
- and locks in productivity gains,

can also be serious.

What would not be serious is forcing conflict now and calling the damage “discipline.”

### To the state shareholder
Your stance is closer to the line we need. But it must be backed by action, not sympathy.

If closures and forced redundancies are politically and industrially unacceptable, then the governance side must support a package that makes them unnecessary:
- binding site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- and investment commitments tied to the agreement.

### On what I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a real package with:
- temporary wage restraint or reduced special payments,
- working-time reductions or flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment and retraining,
- productivity measures,
- internal capacity consolidation,
- and investment commitments.

But only if the company also commits to:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **durable employment security**
- **collective bargaining integrity**

### What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- closures as the “necessary” default,
- forced layoffs as a bargaining lever,
- or a deal where workers pay most of the bill while management and owners merely observe.

### My bottom line
The union is not blocking restructuring. We are blocking **one-sided restructuring**.

If management wants a settlement, it must bring a **fully quantified closure-free package** with real burden-sharing from all sides. If it cannot, then the issue is not labour’s unwillingness. It is management’s refusal to pursue a socially and industrially responsible path.
