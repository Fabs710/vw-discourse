We are now at the point where everyone is making the same demand in different words: “show me a package that is real.” Fine. Then I will be equally direct about where I stand.

### First, what I agree with
I agree with the expert, the state shareholder, and even management on one key point: **this has to be structural, durable, and quantified**.  
I also agree that a cosmetic solution would simply postpone the next crisis.

And I agree with the works council on a second point: **workers cannot be the only ones asked to carry the pain**. If the burden is one-sided, there will be no stable settlement.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s attempt to keep plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default benchmark of seriousness. That is not a neutral technical standard; it is a pressure tactic.

But I also do **not** accept the works council’s attempt to turn “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” into a complete answer by itself. A slogan is not a restructuring plan.

So the real issue is this:  
**Can the company achieve a durable cost and capacity reduction without destroying jobs and the collective bargaining framework?**

My answer remains: **yes, if the package is deep, balanced, and binding.**

### On the numbers
The expert has made something important clear: a closure-free or hybrid package can work **if it is deep enough**. That means we need real industrial measures, not symbolic concessions.

What I would consider a serious basis for negotiation is a package with all of the following:

- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures as the starting point**
- **binding site and employment security for the agreement period**
- **capacity reduction through internal restructuring**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **voluntary exits and attrition**
- **redeployment and qualification**
- **real labor-cost contribution**
- **management and owner contribution**
- **binding investment and product commitments**

That is the minimum structure of a fair deal.

### To management
You keep saying you need equivalent savings. Then spell them out clearly:

- What is the annual run-rate gap?
- What savings are needed by year?
- Which parts come from labor?
- Which parts come from management overhead?
- Which parts come from owners accepting lower returns or funding transition costs?

Do not put the whole bill on employees and then call it “competitiveness.”

If you want worker concessions, then the company must also commit to:
- preserving sites,
- preserving collective-bargaining standards,
- and avoiding forced layoffs.

Without that, there is no basis for trust.

### To the works council
You are right to reject forced redundancies and closures. I support that.  
But you are wrong if you think that simply repeating those red lines is enough to secure a settlement.

If we want to avoid closures, we must show a package that can actually close the gap in practice. That means being prepared to discuss:
- temporary wage restraint,
- reduced special payments,
- working-time reductions or flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary exits,
- internal transfers,
- and temporary labor-cost contributions.

That is not surrender. That is burden-sharing.

### To the state shareholder
You are right that the company is more than a balance sheet. But if the state wants to prevent closures, it must help ensure that the alternative is credible. That means backing a package that is:
- economically real,
- socially balanced,
- and enforceable.

A political no to closures is not enough unless it is paired with a real industrial solution.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right to insist on durability and fixed-cost reduction. But if you insist on judging only by the most brutal instrument, you will provoke exactly the conflict that destroys value.

If you want industrial peace, then you need to accept that the workforce will only contribute if the company and owners also contribute. That is not weakness. That is the only way a deal lasts.

### My bottom line
I am **not** moving from the central position:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No closure-first restructuring.**
- **Yes to a fair, quantified burden-sharing package.**
- **Yes to real capacity and cost reduction.**
- **Yes to a durable settlement that protects jobs, sites, and the collective agreement.**

If management can bring a package that truly closes the gap without forcing workers to bear all the pain, I am ready to negotiate seriously.  
If not, then the union will not help administer job destruction dressed up as restructuring.

So the next step is not another general debate. It is a concrete package:
- quantified savings,
- quantified worker contribution,
- quantified management/owner contribution,
- and binding protections for employment and sites.

If needed, I support the request for a formal comparative scenario paper from the expert, but only if it includes the full burden-sharing picture, not just labor concessions.
