I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on the basic diagnosis: **the status quo is not sustainable**.  
German operations need a structurally lower cost base, and the company must regain margin strength if it is to fund future products, software, and EV competitiveness.

I also agree that:
- employees should not be the only ones contributing,
- a prolonged conflict would destroy value,
- and any solution must preserve the company’s ability to invest.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated assumption that the only serious route is either:
1. plant closures and forced redundancies, or
2. a settlement that freezes the current footprint and merely postpones the problem.

Both are unsatisfactory.

### To management
You are right that the economics must change. But you have not yet convinced me that closures should be the first instrument rather than the last resort.

From an ownership perspective, plant closures are not just a cost item. They are a **permanent destruction of industrial capability**, reputation, and optionality. That is a very serious step, especially in a company with this governance structure and this public role.

So I reject any attempt to present closures as an automatic necessity before alternatives have been genuinely tested.  
At the same time, I also reject the idea that “no closures” can be an absolute red line if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on no forced redundancies and no one-sided burden-sharing is understandable. I support the principle that the workforce cannot be the sole adjustment variable.

But I do **not** support a package that treats every plant, every role, and every current protection as untouchable regardless of economics. That would protect the wrong thing: not value, but inefficiency.

A job guarantee through 2030 may be useful as a negotiating instrument, but it is not credible if it blocks a real competitiveness reset. If the company remains too expensive, then such guarantees become promises built on declining value.

### To the Regional State Government
I agree that closures carry real regional damage and should not be used casually. Your insistence that alternatives be exhausted first is reasonable.

But “alternatives first” must mean something concrete, time-bound, and measurable. It cannot become a veto that prevents necessary change indefinitely. The company cannot be turned into a protected regional employment scheme if that undermines its market viability.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring if it delivers **hard, verifiable savings** without unnecessary destruction. That means:

- substantial capacity reduction, if needed,
- but only after real alternatives have been tested,
- meaningful working-time and productivity measures,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- a material labour contribution,
- management-side restraint,
- and a credible investment path for future products.

I am not attached to any particular instrument. I am attached to the outcome:
**restored competitiveness, durable margins, labour peace, and protection of long-term enterprise value**.

## What I need to see
Before I would support closures or other irreversible steps, I would expect a package that answers three questions clearly:

1. **What savings can be achieved without closures?**
   Not rhetorical promises — quantified, time-bound measures.

2. **What is the minimum footprint required for a viable future?**
   If management claims closures are necessary, that needs to be demonstrated, not asserted.

3. **What are each stakeholder’s contributions?**
   The burden must be visibly shared by employees, management, owners, and the broader corporate structure.

## My position
So my position is this:

- **I do not support value-destructive closures as a first move.**
- **I do not support an inflexible no-change stance that preserves overcapacity.**
- **I do support a serious negotiated solution that restores competitiveness and protects long-term value.**

If the other stakeholders want my backing, bring a package that is:
- economically credible,
- time-bound,
- and capable of closing the margin gap without needless permanent damage.

If not, then I will not endorse a settlement that merely postpones an even worse restructuring later.
