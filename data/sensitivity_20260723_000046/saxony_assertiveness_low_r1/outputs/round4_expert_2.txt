# 6) Decision interpretation

## If the structural gap is modest

- **Scenario A** may be enough, especially if product allocation improves.

## If the structural gap is medium
If the gap is roughly **€400m–€800m**, then:
- **Scenario A alone is unlikely to be enough**
- **Scenario B becomes the most plausible solution**

## If the structural gap is large
If the gap is above **€800m**, then:
- partial footprint reduction may still be insufficient,
- and **Scenario C** becomes economically more likely.

# 7) Neutral conclusion

## My evidence-based assessment
- **Non-closure measures can plausibly deliver material savings**, but usually not enough to eliminate major overcapacity on their own.
- **Partial footprint reduction** is often the most economically balanced route when labour resistance to closures is high.
- **Full closure** yields the biggest recurring savings but also the largest one-time cost and greatest execution risk.

## Best-practice restructuring logic
1. Quantify plant economics site by site
2. Exhaust non-closure measures first
3. Use partial footprint reduction if the residual gap remains material
4. Reserve closure for the true last resort
