I will respond plainly, as the regional state government and anchor shareholder.

## First: where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company needs a real restructuring, not cosmetic measures.

I also agree that:
- employees should not carry the burden alone,
- non-closure measures must be tested seriously,
- and the discussion now has to be quantified rather than ideological.

And I agree with the expert that **closures are not automatically the first move**. That is important.

## Where I disagree with management
Management keeps saying closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available because the cost gap is structural. I do not dismiss that concern. But I do **not** accept closures as a first-line instrument, and I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the default fallback.

What has still not been shown is a convincing, plant-by-plant demonstration that all non-closure alternatives have truly been exhausted before moving to irreversible steps.

That burden of proof matters, because closures are not just a cost item. They mean:
- permanent job losses,
- damage to suppliers,
- loss of regional purchasing power,
- and lasting weakening of industrial capacity in our state.

So: **yes to restructuring, no to closure as the starting point**.

## Where I disagree with the works council and union
I support your insistence that closures and forced redundancies must not be the default answer. That is correct.

But I cannot support a position that effectively says **every German site must remain open under all circumstances**, with no room for adjustment at all. That would also be irresponsible if the company remains structurally uncompetitive.

So my position is:
- restructuring yes,
- closures only as a last resort,
- compulsory redundancies only if absolutely unavoidable,
- and only after serious, quantified alternatives have been tested.

## On the expert’s scenario framework
The expert’s hierarchy is the right one:
1. non-closure measures,
2. partial footprint reduction if needed,
3. closure only as the last resort.

I support that sequence.

But it must be made concrete. I want a **plant-by-plant, numbers-based test** of:
- working-time reductions,
- temporary flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- bonus restraint and management-side contribution,
- and product/volume reallocation.

If that package closes the gap, then closures are unnecessary. If it does not, then we can discuss what further step is unavoidable. But that discussion must come **after** the alternatives are proven insufficient, not before.

## On job and site guarantees
I understand why the workforce wants binding guarantees through 2030. They are not asking for a gift; they are asking for security before accepting sacrifice.

But I cannot support an unconditional guarantee that freezes every site and every job regardless of economics. What I **can** support is a credible, enforceable protection framework that is:
- time-limited,
- conditional,
- and tied to real investment and restructuring commitments.

So: not empty promises, but also not a blank cheque for closures.

## On burden-sharing
If labour is to contribute, then the other sides must contribute visibly too.

That means:
- management restraint,
- owner support,
- and concrete investment commitments into future products and technology in Germany.

I will not support a model where workers carry the burden while the corporate side merely observes and investors demand speed without sharing responsibility.

## To the controlling family owners
You say you support testing alternatives first. Good. Then support it in practice, not just in framing.

If the alternatives are sufficient, back them. If they are not sufficient, then you must also accept that the company cannot be protected by words alone.

## To the investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive. But a settlement that damages industrial legitimacy and triggers lasting conflict is also value-destructive.

You want deal certainty; so do I. But certainty does not come from threatening the workforce with irreversible measures from the outset. It comes from a package that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable.

## My position
So my position is:
- **I support restructuring.**
- **I do not support closures as the first move.**
- **I do not support compulsory redundancies as the easy fallback.**
- **I support a serious, quantified alternative package.**
- **I support conditional protections for jobs and sites.**
- **I support burden-sharing from management, owners, and labour.**
- **I support investment commitments that keep Germany industrially relevant.**

## Bottom line
The state government will not support a restructuring that treats our region as disposable.

We are willing to back a tough but fair settlement that protects the company’s long-term health **and** preserves its role as a regional economic anchor.

What we will not support is:
- closures pushed as a first reflex,
- forced redundancies as a bargaining tool,
- or a one-sided restructuring that leaves the region to absorb the damage.
