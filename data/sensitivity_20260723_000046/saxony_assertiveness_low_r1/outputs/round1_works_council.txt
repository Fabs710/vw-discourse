As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and management cannot now solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and tearing up long-standing protections. A fait accompli will be met with firm resistance.

What we are prepared to discuss is a genuinely negotiated restructuring that secures:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- and a real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the future production footprint.

If savings are needed, then they must come from alternatives that preserve sites and employment: capacity adjustments, temporary working-time measures, internal redeployment, training, and a fair contribution from management and owners. But the burden cannot be placed one-sidedly on employees.

We are also demanding that any restructuring be tied to future investment, new products, and a credible industrial strategy for Germany. We will not trade away jobs today for vague promises tomorrow.

So my position is straightforward: no closures, no forced layoffs, no dismantling of co-determination. If management wants a solution, it must come to the table with a plan that protects the workforce and the German plants.
