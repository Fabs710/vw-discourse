### Core impasse
The central impasse is between:

1. **Labour-side red lines**  
   - no closures,  
   - no compulsory redundancies,  
   - binding site/job security through 2030,

and

2. **Capital/management-side flexibility demands**  
   - closures must remain available,  
   - compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out,  
   - guarantees must stay conditional.

A second, related impasse is **sequencing**:
- labour wants a genuine non-closure test first, with binding protections,
- management/investors want the option to move to footprint reduction or closure if the quantified gap remains.

The expert view partially bridges this by favoring:
- non-closure first,
- partial footprint reduction second,
- closure only as last resort.

But the parties disagree on whether “last resort” can still be kept meaningfully available and on what counts as a binding commitment during the test phase.

---

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise paths

#### Proposal 1: Time-bound non-closure test with hard triggers
A structured compromise could be:
- a **fixed implementation window** for a quantified non-closure package,
- binding contributions from labour, management, and owners,
- independent verification of savings,
- and **pre-agreed trigger criteria** for what happens if targets are missed.

This would allow labour to obtain a genuine first test of alternatives, while giving management and capital a defined path forward if the gap remains.

#### Proposal 2: Conditional protection framework instead of absolute guarantees
A second compromise could replace the clash over “2030 guarantees” with:
- **site-specific and role-specific protection periods**,
- protection tied to measurable investment and utilization commitments,
- and a clear prohibition on unilateral closure decisions during the test period.

This would preserve meaningful security for labour without freezing all strategic flexibility.

#### Proposal 3: Partial footprint reduction as the only fallback before closure
A third possible bridge is to formalize the expert’s middle path:
- non-closure measures first,
- then **partial footprint reduction only** if the gap is still material,
- with full closure explicitly reserved as a final, jointly reviewed last resort.

This could narrow the conflict by removing closure as an immediate bargaining lever while still avoiding an absolute closure taboo.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If a verified, independently audited non-closure package delivered substantial savings but not the full target, would you accept a **pre-defined partial footprint reduction** as a fallback, provided no compulsory redundancies occurred during the test phase?

#### To the Trade Union
What specific combination of temporary labour measures would you be prepared to trade for **binding job protection during the implementation window**, and what threshold would make that package acceptable?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap must remain after non-closure measures before you would activate footprint reduction, and can you define that threshold in advance rather than leaving it open-ended?

#### To the Regional State Government
Are you prepared to make state support conditional on a **verified alternatives test with enforceable milestones**, even if that means accepting some site rationalization later if the targets are not met?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a legally or contractually binding protection framework through the test period if it is paired with measurable productivity and investment commitments, or do you require immediate structural optionality from day one?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What level of recurring margin improvement would be sufficient for you to support a non-closure or partial-footprint solution, and are you willing to accept a longer implementation timeline if the economics are sound?

#### To the Expert
Can you specify the **minimum decision thresholds** at which the model shifts from non-closure measures to partial footprint reduction, and from partial reduction to closure, so the parties have a common trigger framework?
