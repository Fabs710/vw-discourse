I’ll be equally clear: **the status quo is not an option**.

We are facing a structural cost and capacity problem, not a temporary dip. German plant costs are far above target, European demand is shrinking, and our brand is still missing its return-on-sales target. If we do not make a durable correction now, we will simply burn through cash that should be going into future products, software, battery technology, and competitiveness to 2030.

So I need to challenge the red lines you have set.

### On plant closures
I understand the political and social sensitivity, but **I do not accept a package that rules out closures in advance**. If the footprint is structurally too large, then capacity has to come out of the system. That may mean plant closure, partial closure, or another equivalent structural reduction. What I cannot accept is a cosmetic solution that keeps every site open while the cost base remains uncompetitive.

### On compulsory redundancies
I prefer negotiated solutions over forced layoffs, but I will not rule out workforce reductions as a consequence of restructuring if we cannot secure sufficient voluntary and structural measures. The company cannot carry excess capacity indefinitely.

### On job and site guarantees
A blanket job guarantee through 2030 is not compatible with the scale of change we need. We can discuss transitional protections, but only if they are tied to **real structural savings** and a credible path to lower costs. Guarantees without transformation would freeze the problem in place.

### On employee contributions
I do agree on one point: the burden cannot fall on employees alone. Management, the broader corporate side, and all stakeholders must contribute. But an employee contribution must be **material**, not symbolic. If we are to avoid harsher measures, then labour costs must come down in a way that is durable and measurable.

### On future investment
You are right that restructuring must be tied to future products and investment. That is exactly why I am pressing for this now. We need the capacity and margin to fund the next generation of vehicles and technology. Without a competitive base, there is no future industrial strategy to protect.

### Bottom line
If you are asking me to commit to **no closures, no forced redundancies, and full site guarantees**, then I have to say clearly: **that will not solve this crisis**.

What I am prepared to discuss is a package that delivers:
- a **substantial and durable reduction in capacity**,
- a **meaningful labour-cost contribution**,
- **organizational and working-time flexibility**,
- and a credible investment commitment for the future.

If the employee side wants to avoid the most drastic outcomes, then it needs to engage on the scale of savings, not just on protecting every existing structure. Without that, we are postponing the inevitable and weakening the company further.
