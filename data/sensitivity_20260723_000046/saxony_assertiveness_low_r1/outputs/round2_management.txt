I’ll respond directly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one point: **the current situation is not sustainable**.  
German factory costs are too high, the European market is shrinking, and we do need a restructuring that is durable, not cosmetic. I also agree that the burden cannot fall on employees alone and that future investment is essential.

So let me be equally clear: **I am not here to defend a symbolic cut or a temporary bridge.** We need a structural reset that improves competitiveness through 2030 and beyond.

## Where I disagree — fundamentally
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to make “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” the starting and ending point of the discussion.

That position may be understandable politically, but it does not solve the economics.

If the footprint remains too large, then:
- fixed costs stay too high,
- utilization stays too low,
- margins stay under pressure,
- and the company keeps funding excess capacity instead of future products.

That is exactly the outcome I cannot accept.

## To the Works Council and Union
You are demanding:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- and a binding right to shape the footprint.

I have to say plainly: **that combination is not compatible with the scale of restructuring this company needs**.

A guarantee through 2030 that freezes the current footprint would not be a solution; it would be a lock-in of the cost problem. I will not sign up to a package that preserves every site regardless of economics while asking management to pretend the margin gap can be closed some other way.

That said, I am not dismissing alternatives. I am saying they must be **sufficient in scale**.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am prepared to discuss a package that avoids unnecessary destruction **if** it delivers hard numbers. Specifically:

- **substantial capacity reduction**
- **material labour-cost contribution**
- **working-time and productivity measures**
- **redeployment and qualification**
- **product and volume reallocation**
- **a credible investment path into new products and technology**

If the employee side can help produce the required savings through alternatives to full plant closure, I am open to that. But I need to be honest: **alternatives only matter if they close the gap**.

## On plant closures
I am not declaring closures as ideology. I am saying they remain on the table because they may be necessary.  
If, after serious analysis, we can achieve the required structural savings without closing a plant, I would prefer that. But I will not commit in advance to ruling closures out.

That would be irresponsible to the company and to the owners.

## On compulsory redundancies
I prefer negotiated reductions, voluntary exits, internal transfers, and working-time solutions. But I will not rule out workforce reductions if the package does not deliver enough structural savings.

The company cannot be expected to carry overcapacity indefinitely just because layoffs are uncomfortable. The question is not whether job losses are painful; it is whether the business survives competitively.

## To the Regional State Government
I hear your concern about regional damage. It is real, and I do not dismiss it.

But your position risks confusing **regional protection** with **industrial viability**. If we protect every site regardless of utilization, we weaken the company’s ability to invest, and that ultimately hurts the region too.

So yes: exhaust alternatives. But exhaust them in a **time-bound, outcome-driven** way — not as an open-ended veto on necessary change.

## To the controlling family owners
You are right that unnecessary value destruction must be avoided. I agree.

But I also need a clear statement from ownership: **are you prepared to back a package that truly reduces cost and capacity, even if that means difficult footprint decisions?**

Because “prefer alternatives” is not enough if those alternatives do not deliver the necessary savings. If the owners want competitiveness preserved, they must support a serious restructuring, not just defer the hard choices.

## On labour contributions
I do accept that employees can contribute — but the contribution must be **meaningful and durable**, not symbolic.

That can include:
- temporary wage restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- bonus moderation,
- redeployment,
- productivity commitments,
- and other measurable cost measures.

But I will not accept a deal where labour contributes modestly while the structural cost problem remains intact.

## Bottom line
Here is the line I will hold:

- **I will not accept a cosmetic compromise.**
- **I will not accept a package that leaves German overcapacity untouched.**
- **I will not accept site or job guarantees that prevent necessary restructuring.**

What I am prepared to accept is a negotiated settlement that delivers:
1. a **real and durable reduction in costs**,  
2. a **substantial reduction in capacity**,  
3. a **fair labour contribution**, and  
4. **investment capacity for future products and technology**.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring a package that actually achieves the savings. If it cannot, then we are simply postponing the problem — and I will not do that.
