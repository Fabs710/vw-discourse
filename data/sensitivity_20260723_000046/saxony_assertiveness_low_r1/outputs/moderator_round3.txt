## 1) What has been agreed

- The company needs restructuring; the current situation is not sustainable.
- Employees should not be the only group contributing to the solution.
- Purely cosmetic fixes are not acceptable; the discussion needs quantified options.
- Non-closure measures should be seriously tested before irreversible steps.
- The restructuring question should be assessed on a plant-by-plant, numbers-based basis.
- An independent expert assessment is useful and should inform the next stage.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain

- **Plant closures:**  
  Labor-side stakeholders reject closures as a first move and, in some cases, reject them outright; management, investors, and parts of ownership insist closures must remain available if needed.

- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor rejects them; management and capital-side actors say they cannot be ruled out in advance.

- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  Labor wants binding site and employment guarantees through 2030; management and investors say guarantees must be conditional and cannot freeze the footprint.

- **Burden-sharing:**  
  All sides say burden-sharing is needed, but they disagree on how much labor, management, owners, and investors should each contribute, and in what form.

- **Definition of “last resort”:**  
  Labor and the state want closures and layoffs only after alternatives are demonstrably exhausted; management and investors want that test to be time-bound and not become a veto on restructuring.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed

- **How much savings non-closure measures can realistically generate** versus the structural gap management says must be closed.
- **Whether partial footprint reduction could bridge the gap** without full plant closures.
- **The cost of one-time restructuring** versus the long-term savings from deeper footprint changes.
- **The balance between competitiveness and employment protection**, especially if current capacity exceeds future demand.
- **How conditional any job or site guarantees would need to be** to remain credible to capital while still acceptable to labor.
- **What specific management/owner contributions are sufficient** to make labor concessions politically and socially viable.
- **The timeline:** whether the process is time-bound enough to produce a decision, but not so rushed that alternatives are not genuinely tested.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round

The next round should move from principles to a **shared quantified scenario comparison**:
- Scenario A: non-closure measures only
- Scenario B: partial footprint reduction
- Scenario C: closure scenario

Each should be compared on:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- implementation timeline,
- effect on return on sales,
- and employment/site impact.

The immediate task is not to restate red lines, but to test which combination of measures can close the structural gap with the least irreversible damage.
