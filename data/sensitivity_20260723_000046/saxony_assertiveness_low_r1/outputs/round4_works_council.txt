I will be equally direct.

## My core position remains unchanged
The works council does **not** accept:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a restructuring that tears up the job-security framework and then calls that “competitiveness.”

That is still my line. Nothing I have heard so far justifies moving it.

## What I agree with
I do agree with one thing that has finally become clearer in this round:  
we now need a **shared quantified comparison** of the options.

That is useful, because it forces everyone to stop hiding behind slogans like “minimum footprint” or “last resort.”

I also accept the expert’s point that non-closure measures can produce **material savings**. Good. That is exactly why closures are not the automatic answer. If there are real savings available through:
- working-time changes,
- bonus restraint,
- productivity measures,
- redeployment,
- training,
- product allocation,
- and management/owner contributions,

then management must exhaust those measures first.

## What I disagree with
I do **not** accept the shareholder and management claim that closure must remain available as a normal option simply because the cost base is uncomfortable.

That is not “discipline.”  
It is a preference for irreversible job destruction before the company has proven that alternatives fail.

The expert’s own framework actually supports our position more than management’s:
1. non-closure restructuring first,
2. partial footprint reduction only if absolutely necessary,
3. closure only as the last resort.

But for us, the burden of proof is still on management to show that the first two steps truly cannot solve the problem. That burden has **not** been met.

## To management
Your argument still has the same flaw: you keep saying the gap must be closed, but you have not shown that closure is the only way.

The expert said non-closure measures can plausibly deliver:
- hundreds of millions in annual savings,
- faster payback,
- lower risk,
- and less irreversible damage.

So do not tell us there is no alternative until you have actually negotiated and implemented the alternatives.

And let me be clear: a “conditional guarantee” that can be withdrawn whenever management decides the footprint should shrink is **not** a guarantee. It is a temporary truce.

## To the family owners
You say you support testing alternatives first. Then support them **for real**.

That means:
- no hidden closure agenda,
- no moving the goalposts after labour concedes,
- no pretending that “flexibility” means a standing right to shut plants later,
- and no forcing workers to accept concessions while ownership keeps full strategic freedom.

If ownership wants a credible settlement, it must accept a **binding site and employment framework through 2030**. Not a vague promise. A binding one.

## To the state government
You are right to insist that restructuring cannot simply sacrifice the region.

But your suggestion of a “time-bound, measurable process” must not become a polite runway to the same closure plan. If the process is serious, then it must be built around a **genuine no-closure test** first.

The state should use its position to insist on:
- transparent plant economics,
- independent verification,
- and a negotiated path that keeps sites open.

I support your call for transparency. I do **not** support using it as a prelude to closure.

## To the investors
You keep saying a settlement must restore margins. Fine. But let’s be honest about what also destroys value:
- a prolonged conflict,
- warning strikes,
- loss of trust,
- damaged output,
- reputational harm,
- and an industrial-relations breakdown that poisons the company for years.

A closure-driven approach may look neat in a spreadsheet, but it can create a much bigger strategic problem.

You want “deal certainty”? Then stop demanding a deal that starts by threatening the workforce’s existence.

## On the expert scenario comparison
The expert was useful, but I want to be very precise about how that comparison should be read:

- **Scenario A: non-closure measures only**  
  This is not “soft” or “cosmetic.” It is the first serious option and must be tested in full.

- **Scenario B: partial footprint reduction**  
  This may be a management preference, but it is not automatically legitimate. If it means closing parts of plants or hollowing out sites, it will be opposed hard.

- **Scenario C: closure**  
  That is not a negotiating baseline. It is the nuclear option. It should not be normalized.

So yes, compare them. But do not smuggle in the conclusion that because closure has bigger savings on paper, it becomes acceptable politically or socially. It does not.

## What a serious alternative package must include
If management wants the works council to even discuss a package, it must put forward a **real, quantified non-closure package** with binding commitments, including:

1. **Working-time reductions**  
   With protection against involuntary job loss.

2. **Temporary wage and bonus moderation**  
   Limited in time and fairly shared.

3. **Redeployment across sites**  
   Not layoffs disguised as restructuring.

4. **Training and qualification**  
   Funded properly, not as PR.

5. **Product allocation to German plants**  
   So our sites remain part of the future, not the past.

6. **Management and owner contributions**  
   Executive restraint, dividend discipline if needed, and visible top-side sacrifice.

7. **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**  
   Not optional, not review-only, not conditional on management’s unilateral interpretation.

That is the only basis on which labour can consider contributing.

## What I will not accept
I want this on the record:

- I will not accept “every plant open for now, but closures later if targets miss.”
- I will not accept “no layoffs unless unavoidable,” because that is just a delayed layoff.
- I will not accept guarantees that can be broken once the workforce has given up leverage.
- I will not accept a restructuring that preserves shareholder freedom but removes worker security.
- I will not accept compulsory redundancies under any normal restructuring scenario.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate a **real non-closure restructuring** with quantified savings, but only if it is anchored in:
- binding site security,
- binding employment security,
- fair burden-sharing,
- and genuine co-determination.

If management and the capital side come back with numbers, fine — bring them.  
But do not confuse numbers with legitimacy.

The works council’s position is unchanged:
**No closures. No compulsory redundancies. No dismantling of the job-security framework.**

If that is treated as non-negotiable by the employee side, that is because it is.
