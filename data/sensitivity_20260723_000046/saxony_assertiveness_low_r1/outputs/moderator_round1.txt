### 1) What has been agreed
- The current situation is **not sustainable** and some form of restructuring is needed.
- **Employees alone should not bear the burden**; management, owners, and other stakeholders must contribute.
- Any solution should be tied to **future investment, new products, and long-term competitiveness**.
- There is broad support for exploring **alternatives before irreversible steps**, including working-time measures, redeployment, training, productivity improvements, and other negotiated savings.
- All sides prefer, in principle, to avoid open conflict and reach a **negotiated settlement**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works Council, union, regional government, and family owners oppose or strongly resist closures; management and institutional investors say closures or equivalent footprint reductions may be necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Labour side and political stakeholders reject them; management and investors will not fully rule them out if savings are insufficient.
- **Job and site guarantees**: Labour side wants binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors view broad guarantees as too rigid unless matched by major savings.
- **Scale and form of restructuring**: Dispute remains over whether savings can be achieved through alternatives alone or require substantial capacity reduction.
- **Role of co-determination and regional interests**: Labour and state actors insist on strong involvement; management and investors are focused on speed, certainty, and economic viability.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much employment protection** can be offered without undermining the required cost reduction.
- **Whether temporary measures** can deliver sufficient durable savings, or whether structural footprint changes are unavoidable.
- **What burden-sharing is acceptable** from workers, management, and owners, and in what proportions.
- **How to define “exhausting alternatives”** in a way that is credible, time-bound, and not open-ended.
- **What concrete investment commitments** would accompany concessions from the workforce.
- **How to balance short-term social and regional impacts** against long-term competitiveness and capital market expectations.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **specific package design**:
- identify which restructuring measures are on the table,
- quantify the savings each side can realistically contribute,
- specify what employment and site protections could be time-limited or conditional,
- and define the investment commitments that would make concessions politically and socially acceptable.
