I agree with the Works Council on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. Those are not negotiable from our side either.

Management is treating the workforce as the adjustment variable for strategic and cost errors it did not create. That is unacceptable. If the company wants the employees to contribute, then that contribution must be **fair, limited, and matched by contributions from management, owners, and the broader corporate side**. Workers cannot be expected to carry the full burden while others remain untouched.

Where I am prepared to be constructive is on a **negotiated package** that delivers real savings without destroying trust or permanently weakening collective standards. That can include:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- restraint on bonuses or other agreed employee contributions,
- and possibly a negotiated wage-related contribution if, and only if, jobs and sites are secured.

But let me be equally clear:  
**pay cuts alone are not a solution**, and **“voluntary” exits cannot become disguised compulsory layoffs**. We will not accept a restructuring that erodes the collective wage framework, creates open-ended insecurity, or uses the threat of closure as leverage to force the workforce to pay for everything.

If management wants industrial peace, it needs to come with:
1. **binding guarantees for sites and jobs**,  
2. **a credible industrial plan for future products and investment**,  
3. **real burden-sharing from management and owners**, and  
4. **no plant closures, no forced redundancies**.

Without that, we will use our leverage. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to act. We are ready for a serious settlement — but not for a one-sided cut-and-close strategy disguised as restructuring.
