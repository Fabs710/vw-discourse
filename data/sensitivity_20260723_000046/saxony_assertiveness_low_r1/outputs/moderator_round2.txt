### 1) What has been agreed
- The current situation is not sustainable and requires restructuring.
- All sides agree that employees should not bear the full burden alone.
- There is broad openness to alternatives such as:
  - working-time reductions,
  - temporary flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - productivity measures,
  - bonus restraint and other management-side contributions.
- There is general support for a negotiated solution rather than an uncontrolled conflict.
- All sides acknowledge the importance of future investment and long-term competitiveness.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  Labor, the state government, and the family owners want closures treated as a last resort; management and investors keep them on the table as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  Labor and the state strongly oppose them; management and investors refuse to rule them out.
- **Scope of guarantees**:  
  Labor wants binding site and employment guarantees through 2030; management and investors say such guarantees may be too restrictive if they block restructuring.
- **Meaning of “exhausting alternatives”**:  
  Labor and the state want a real, binding, time-bound test before any closure; management and investors want alternatives only if they produce sufficient savings within a clear timetable.
- **How much capacity must come out**:  
  Management and investors insist on a substantial footprint/capacity reduction; labor and the state argue this must not happen through closures or forced layoffs as the default path.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Job and site security vs. structural cost reduction**
- **Avoiding closures vs. achieving sufficient savings**
- **Voluntary/negotiated measures vs. the possibility of compulsory measures**
- **Short-term labor concessions vs. long-term ownership/management commitments**
- **Protecting co-determination and regional industrial capacity vs. giving management flexibility to restructure**
- **Temporary concessions now vs. whether they remain temporary and reversible**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please narrow the discussion to a quantified comparison of options:
1. What savings can be achieved **without** closures or compulsory redundancies?
2. What minimum footprint is considered necessary for competitiveness?
3. What concrete, measurable contributions will each stakeholder group make?
4. What exact conditions would make any site/job guarantees acceptable to all sides?

The next round should focus on hard numbers, sequencing, and conditional commitments rather than repeating general red lines.
