I agree on one point that matters: this cannot be a unilateral or purely tactical exercise. If the company is to recover competitiveness, the solution has to be durable, enforceable, and capable of surviving industrial relations pressure. A prolonged confrontation would destroy value for everyone, including the owners.

That said, I do **not** support an outcome that simply rules out structural change in advance.

To the works council and union: I understand the insistence on avoiding forced layoffs and preserving as much employment as possible. But “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no fundamental cost change” is not a restructuring plan; it is a wish list. If the German cost base remains structurally above target, the company will keep losing margin, investment capacity, and ultimately jobs in a slower and more damaging way. I will not back a settlement that protects every legacy arrangement while the enterprise itself erodes.

To the regional state: I hear the concern about regional stability and the company’s role as an anchor employer. That concern is legitimate. But blocking every serious capacity adjustment would also be irresponsible if it prevents the company from restoring competitiveness. Long-term regional employment depends on a viable company, not on preserving every site at any price.

To management: I also want to be clear that “plant closures and layoffs” cannot be treated as the only serious instrument if there are credible alternatives. A deal that relies on brute force alone risks avoidable conflict, reputational damage, and political backlash. If you want support from the supervisory board and the controlling owner, you need to show that you have examined all realistic options for equivalent savings.

My position is therefore straightforward:

- I support a **material restructuring** of the German operations.
- I do **not** support preserving the current cost structure.
- I do **not** support a settlement that permanently weakens the company’s value.
- I am open to **alternatives to closures**, but only if they are substantial, measurable, and deliver durable savings at a scale that truly restores competitiveness.

What would move me is a package that combines:
- real capacity reduction,
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- productivity and flexibility improvements,
- targeted investment commitments,
- and a credible path to restore margins.

What will not move me is a proposal that protects all sites and all guarantees while asking ownership to accept continued underperformance.

So yes, I want a negotiated solution. But it has to be a serious one. If the alternative to closures cannot deliver comparable economic effect, then the company cannot be expected to carry the full burden indefinitely.
