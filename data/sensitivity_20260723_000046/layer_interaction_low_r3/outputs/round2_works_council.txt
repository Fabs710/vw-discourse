Management is asking us to treat closures and forced redundancies as the benchmark for “seriousness.” We reject that. Serious restructuring is not defined by how much damage it does to the workforce. It is defined by whether it secures the company’s future **without destroying its German sites and the people who built them**.

I will respond to the main points made in this round.

**To management:**  
You say the cost base is too high and the business needs structural savings. We do not deny the pressure on margins. But you have not shown that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only way to achieve those savings. A demand for “structural change” is not the same as a license to tear up the job-security framework.

If you want us to engage on package design, then bring a package that is actually designed to avoid closures and forced layoffs. That means:
- capacity reduction through shift and line adjustments,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- lower executive and overhead burdens,
- and concrete investment commitments that secure future volume at the German sites.

What you have **not** earned from us is a blank cheque for closures “if needed later.”

**To the family owners and investors:**  
You keep saying “durable,” “measurable,” “competitive.” Fine. Then measure alternatives fairly. Do not assume that only plant closures create durability. A deal that preserves sites while reducing costs through negotiated instruments can be durable if it is binding and enforced. What is not durable is a confrontation that provokes strikes, destroys trust, and undermines the company’s social foundation.

Your argument still ignores the long-term cost of breaking the historic job-security arrangement. Once that barrier is broken, there is no guarantee that the next round will not simply target more sites and more jobs. We are not prepared to open that door.

**To the regional state:**  
Your position is closer to ours, and I appreciate that. But “no closures as a starting point” is not enough. We need a clear commitment that the German sites remain open and viable. If the state is serious about its role as anchor shareholder, then it should support a negotiated future package that explicitly excludes closures and compulsory redundancies and protects industrial capacity in the region.

Now, on the matter of package design:

We are prepared to discuss a **real employee contribution**, but only under strict conditions:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and employment guarantees to 2030**
4. **A co-determined restructuring process with full works-council involvement**
5. **Targeted investment commitments for the affected sites**
6. **Internal flexibility before external cuts**

That is the minimum framework for serious talks.

What we are **not** prepared to accept:
- “temporary” measures that become permanent job erosion,
- outsourcing by another name,
- site-by-site erosion through underinvestment,
- or any package that keeps closures on the table as a pressure tactic.

If management wants numbers, then here is the principle: labour can contribute to savings, but only in a way that is **reciprocal** and **reversible** unless and until management honors its side of the deal. We will not pre-pay for a restructuring by accepting the closure threat over our heads.

So my message is simple:  
We are ready to negotiate a serious, measurable alternative to closures. But that alternative must preserve every German plant, rule out forced layoffs, and restore trust through binding guarantees. If that is not on offer, then there is no basis for agreement.
