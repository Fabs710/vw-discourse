**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that **some form of restructuring is necessary**.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should be **negotiated rather than imposed unilaterally**.
- There is also shared recognition that the company needs a **credible future for its German operations**, including **investment in products and technology**.
- Several parties accept that **alternatives to plant closures and compulsory redundancies should be explored**, provided they are substantial and durable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, and state reject closures; management, family owner, and investors do not rule them out.
- **Compulsory redundancies / workforce reductions:** Employee side and state oppose them; management and owners view workforce reduction as potentially necessary.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour seeks binding job and site guarantees through 2030; management and owners refuse blanket guarantees that limit structural change in advance.
- **Cost-cutting method:** Labour and state want burden-sharing without site destruction; management, family owner, and investors insist on **structural, measurable, durable savings**.
- **Degree of restructuring required:** Management, owners, and investors want deep enough change to restore competitiveness; labour side argues for restructuring that preserves employment and sites.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve comparable savings without closures or layoffs**, if those are excluded.
- **What labour-cost concessions** would be acceptable, and for how long.
- **What concrete investment commitments** would be made in exchange for job/site protections.
- **How capacity reduction would be implemented** if plant closures are off the table.
- **How to balance short-term industrial peace** against long-term competitiveness and investment capacity.
- **What enforceable metrics** would define a “durable” and “credible” alternative package.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **package design**:
- identify the **minimum savings and capacity adjustments** needed,
- specify which **non-closure measures** could deliver them,
- define any **job, site, and investment guarantees** that could be reciprocal,
- and clarify which trade-offs each side is prepared to make if the goal is a negotiated deal.

The next round should focus on **numbers, instruments, and conditions** rather than repeating red lines.
