We agree on the central point that this must be a negotiated restructuring, not an imposed one. And we also agree that the company needs a durable future in Germany, not just a short-term patch.

But I need to be very clear: **we will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of restructuring.** That is not a negotiable detail for us; it would break the collective job-security framework and place the full burden on the workforce.

### To management
You are asking for “structural” savings. Fine. But structural does **not** automatically mean closures and layoffs. If you want the union to move, you need to show a package that delivers real savings through other instruments:

- capacity reductions without closing sites,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary labour-cost relief,
- fair concessions on bonuses or other components,
- and contributions from management, overhead, and ownership as well.

What we will not do is sign off on a plan where employees give up wages and guarantees while management keeps closures on the table as leverage. That is not shared sacrifice; that is one-sided pressure.

### To the family owners and investors
You keep using words like “durable,” “measurable,” and “credible.” We agree with those standards. But you are too quick to assume that only closures can meet them. A negotiated package can be durable if it is binding, enforceable, and paired with real investment commitments.

What is **not** durable is a settlement that destroys trust, triggers strikes, and undermines the company’s social partnership model. Once that is broken, the long-term risks rise for everyone — including shareholders.

### To the state
Your insistence that closures are not acceptable as a starting point is right. But we need more than a vague political preference. The state should actively support a package that preserves every German site, excludes compulsory redundancies, and secures future product and investment commitments. If the state wants to use its influence responsibly, it should back a real alternative, not just warn against the worst case.

### On package design
We are prepared to discuss a labor contribution, but only on a reciprocal basis. That means:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **Meaningful investment commitments for the affected sites**
5. **Capacity reduction through internal, negotiated measures**
6. **A fair burden-sharing package involving management and ownership**

That is the minimum basis for serious talks.

We are **not** prepared to accept:
- closures “later if needed,”
- forced layoffs disguised as voluntary measures,
- underinvestment followed by site erosion,
- or a deal where the workforce pre-pays for restructuring without guarantees.

If management and the owners want a real agreement, they should come back with a package that removes the threat to sites and jobs. If not, then you should expect the workforce to defend itself accordingly, including through industrial action.
