Here is a procedural summary of where the discourse is blocked, plus bridge proposals and stakeholder questions.

## Core impasse
The main impasse is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. Most parties now accept that.

The impasse is over **the instrument set and guarantees**:

- **Works council / union / regional state** want a restructuring that is **fully non-closure**, with **no compulsory redundancies** and binding site security.
- **Management / owners / investors** insist the package must deliver a **closure-equivalent, durable cost and capacity reset**, and they want to keep closure options available if the non-closure package falls short.

So the dispute has narrowed to this:
> Can a no-closure package be made quantitatively equivalent to closure-based restructuring, and if so, under what binding conditions?

The expert’s intervention sharpened the issue: a non-closure package is only acceptable if it removes **roughly the same fixed cost, capacity, and break-even volume** as closures would. The question is whether the parties can agree on a package with that level of verified effect, while also providing acceptable security on jobs and sites.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified equivalence test with trigger-based fallback
A structured package could be negotiated in two phases:

1. **Primary path: no-closure restructuring**
   - permanent shift elimination
   - line consolidation
   - product/variant simplification
   - overhead and management cuts
   - labor-cost contribution
   - redeployment and qualification

2. **Independent equivalence test**
   - an external expert jointly appointed by all sides
   - plant-by-plant comparison against a closure scenario
   - assessment of:
     - annual fixed-cost savings
     - installed capacity reduction
     - break-even volume
     - 3–5 year cash flow

3. **Trigger-based fallback**
   - only if the package misses an agreed threshold would the parties reopen a limited second-round discussion on further structural measures

This would let labour and the state avoid immediate closures, while giving management and investors a hard economic test.

---

### Proposal 2: Non-closure package with binding floor and shared sacrifice
A second compromise position would be a **strictly non-closure agreement** tied to measurable sacrifices from all sides:

- **Labour side**
  - temporary or medium-term wage moderation
  - reduced bonuses or allowances
  - permanent shift reductions
  - flexibility on working time and deployment
  - redeployment and qualification

- **Management / ownership side**
  - management overhead cuts
  - executive compensation restraint
  - investment commitments for future products
  - contribution to restructuring financing

- **State side**
  - support for investment, training, and transition funding
  - active role in monitoring compliance

- **Company side**
  - binding site security for all German plants for an agreed period
  - no compulsory redundancies during the pact term
  - no unilateral closure decisions while the package is in force

This would require the parties to accept that “security” is paired with verifiable concessions and performance metrics.

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid footprint adjustment without closures
A third possible bridge is a **hybrid footprint solution** that preserves every German plant legally, but removes capacity structurally through internal reconfiguration:

- one or more shifts permanently removed
- model allocation concentrated into fewer lines
- certain variants discontinued
- support functions centralized
- output volume redistributed within the existing footprint
- workforce reduced mainly by attrition, transfers, and negotiated exits

This may be a middle path if the parties can agree that:
- no site is closed,
- but not every plant keeps its current role or output,
- and the resulting savings are verified against the closure benchmark.

This would preserve the symbolic commitment to sites while still delivering a real industrial reset.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
If an independent comparison shows that a non-closure package achieves closure-level savings and break-even improvement, are you prepared to accept permanent internal capacity cuts and labor-cost concessions in exchange for binding site security?

### To the Union
What specific labor contribution would you put on the table if it is the condition for a verified no-closure settlement with no compulsory redundancies?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you back a package only if it includes a hard, quantified no-closure guarantee, or are you prepared to support a solution that preserves all sites but still requires significant internal footprint reduction?

### To Management
What minimum quantified savings threshold would make you withdraw the closure option for the full term of the agreement, and are you prepared to bind that threshold in writing?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a legally binding non-closure settlement if an independent expert certifies that it is economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring over 3–5 years?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a non-closure plan if it demonstrably lowers fixed cost and break-even volume to the same extent as closures, even if it leaves some residual overcapacity inside the German footprint?

### To the Independent Expert
What single measurable threshold should the parties use to decide whether a non-closure package is truly equivalent to closure-based restructuring?
