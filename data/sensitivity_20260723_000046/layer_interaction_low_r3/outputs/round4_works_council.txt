I will be direct.

The expert has clarified the real issue: management and the owners want a **closure-level cost reset**. We understand that. But that does **not** mean closures are necessary, and it certainly does **not** justify compulsory redundancies or making plant shutdowns a standing threat over the workforce.

## To management
You keep saying a non-closure package must be “economically equivalent.” Fine. Then let us test that **properly**.

What I reject is your assumption that only closures can produce serious savings. That is not an economic law; it is a management preference. The expert has already confirmed that capacity can come out through:
- permanent shift removal,
- line consolidation,
- product and variant simplification,
- overhead cuts,
- and a durable labour-cost contribution.

That is the route to take.

But let me be clear: I will not accept a package that uses “equivalence” as a disguised ultimatum. If you want us to discuss internal restructuring, then stop treating plant closures as the default benchmark. Every German plant stays open. That is the line.

## To the union
I agree with your position and your insistence on no compulsory redundancies. The workforce should not be asked to pay for management mistakes by losing jobs, security, and sites.

Where I would sharpen it is this: we should not get dragged into endless abstract “equivalence” debates if the purpose is obvious. The purpose is to preserve the German footprint and make it viable. That requires burden-sharing, yes — but **inside** the existing sites and with **binding protection**.

## To the regional state
I appreciate that you are not endorsing a symbolic compromise. But “supporting a non-closure path” is only meaningful if you use your influence to rule out site destruction, not just postpone it.

The state should be backing:
- preserved sites,
- no forced redundancies,
- investment,
- training,
- and a real industrial future.

If the state keeps closure options alive “in case of failure,” then workers are being asked to pre-accept blackmail. That is not acceptable.

## To the controlling family holding
You talk about stewardship and value preservation. Then act like it.

Value is not preserved by breaking the social compact and provoking long conflict. Value is preserved by keeping production stable, retaining skills, and avoiding a destructive escalation with the workforce.

I do, however, reject your repeated attempt to make “economic flexibility” override everything else. Co-determination is not an obstacle. It is part of the legal and industrial framework of this company. Any solution that sidelines it is not durable.

## To the investors
You say we need a real reset, not a delay mechanism. On that point, we agree. What I do **not** accept is your conclusion that this can only be achieved by keeping closure as an available instrument.

The expert has said the key test is **steady-state fixed-cost reduction, capacity removal, and break-even improvement**. That test can be met without closing plants if the company is willing to:
- remove shifts permanently,
- simplify production,
- reduce overhead,
- adjust working time,
- and commit to investment that secures future volumes.

If you want a durable solution, then support that. If you insist on keeping closures in reserve, then you are not really supporting negotiation; you are supporting coercion.
