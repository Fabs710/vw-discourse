We are past the point where broad language will solve this. I will respond plainly.

### To the works council and the union
I acknowledge one thing: you are right that this must be negotiated, and I do not dismiss the need to preserve a viable German industrial base. But I reject your attempt to make **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees to 2030”** the starting and ending point.

That is not a restructuring framework. It is a demand to freeze the cost structure while the market moves against us.

You keep saying capacity can be reduced internally. In principle, yes. In practice, it only matters if it produces **hard, durable, measurable savings** at the scale we need. Shift changes and line consolidation are not enough if they leave the break-even point too high. Working-time measures, redeployment, and qualification are useful instruments, but they do not by themselves reset the fixed-cost base.

So let me be direct: I am prepared to examine a no-closure package, but only if it includes a **real and permanent** reduction in capacity and labor cost. If it is just a way to avoid hard decisions, then it is not a solution.

### To the regional state
I respect your political role, but I do not accept a categorical no-closure stance. If the company remains structurally uncompetitive, then protecting every site at all costs is not responsible governance. It may preserve appearances in the short term, but it weakens the company over time.

If you want to help, support a package that makes the German footprint viable on a durable basis. That means real capacity reduction, real labor-cost relief, and binding investment commitments — not an approach that rules out the instruments before the numbers are tested.

### To the controlling family holding and investors
I agree with the core of your position: we need durable, measurable savings and a credible path back to margins. A cosmetic compromise is unacceptable.

Where I am not willing to go is the idea that we should accept a package that preserves every site and every guarantee simply because closure is politically difficult. If the economics do not work, then we would be sacrificing future competitiveness for short-term peace.

### What would move management
I am willing to consider an alternative to closures if it contains all of the following:
- permanent removal of enough capacity to materially lower fixed costs,
- durable labor-cost contributions, not temporary gestures,
- productivity and flexibility measures that are binding,
- management and overhead contribution,
- and a credible investment path toward 2030.

That is the bar.

### What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a blanket guarantee to 2030 with no structural downside for the company,
- “voluntary” measures that do not materially reduce overcapacity,
- temporary concessions dressed up as transformation,
- or a package that keeps the company’s cost base uncompetitive while claiming success.

### Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution, and we do not insist on closures as a matter of principle. But we do insist on a result that is economically equivalent to a structural reset.

If the non-closure package can truly deliver that, then we can work with it. If it cannot, then I will continue to argue that the company must retain the option of deeper structural measures, including closures.

If needed, I will support a final independent check of the proposed non-closure package against closure-level savings, utilization, and 3–5 year cash flow.
