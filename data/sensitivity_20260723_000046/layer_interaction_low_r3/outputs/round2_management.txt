We are not going to get to a serious solution by pretending that the current structure is sustainable.

I’ll respond directly to each of you.

### To the works council
I agree with you on one point: this must be negotiated, not imposed. And I also agree that the company needs a future in Germany, not a symbolic restructuring that destroys trust.

Where I disagree is fundamental: you are treating **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees to 2030”** as the starting point. That is not a restructuring framework; it is a shield against the very changes the business needs. If we accept that as a hard line, then we preserve the cost structure that is already undermining the brand’s return on sales.

You say we have not shown that closures and workforce reductions are the only route. That is fair. But the burden is not on management to prove that every alternative is impossible. The burden is on the parties to present an alternative that delivers **comparable, durable savings** at the scale required. So far, what I hear are instruments, not a credible economic package.

Capacity reductions, redeployment, flexibility, and overhead savings may help, but unless they produce a structural and material effect on the cost base, they will not be enough. A cosmetic compromise that keeps all sites open while leaving the economics broken is not acceptable.

### To the union
You are right that this cannot be one-sided. I am not asking employees alone to carry the burden. But I am also not prepared to sign a deal that simply redistributes pain without solving the problem.

You say closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table. I understand that position, but I do not accept it as a veto over structural change. If the alternative package is economically equivalent, then fine — show it. If it is not equivalent, then we are just postponing the adjustment and storing up an even larger crisis.

On labour contribution: yes, there must be one. But it must be real, measurable, and durable. Temporary sacrifices alone will not restore competitiveness. If the package does not materially lower labour costs over time and reduce overcapacity, it will not meet the company’s needs.

### To the regional state
I appreciate the political and regional responsibility you carry, and I do not dismiss it. But I need to say clearly: a position that rules out closures at the outset also rules out one of the few instruments that can achieve the required structural change quickly enough.

I am not saying closures are the only answer. I am saying they cannot be excluded in advance if the goal is to restore competitiveness. If the state wants to help secure the German base, then it should support a package that actually makes that base viable — not one that simply preserves every site regardless of economics.

### To the controlling family owners and investors
I agree with the core of what both of you said: we need durability, measurability, and a credible path back to margins. A weak compromise that protects appearances but not the business would be a mistake.

Where I differ is this: if we keep insisting that every solution must preserve all existing sites and guarantees, then we are effectively choosing slow decline. That is not value protection. It is value erosion.

### What would move management
We are prepared to examine an alternative to closures — but only if it includes all of the following in a credible package:
- a meaningful and durable reduction in labor costs,
- real and enforceable reduction in capacity,
- productivity and flexibility improvements that are binding, not voluntary,
- investment commitments that support future volume and technology,
- and savings large enough to restore the brand’s return-on-sales target.

If that package can be delivered without closures, I will examine it seriously. But if the proposal remains: “keep every site, keep every guarantee, and manage competitiveness through hope,” then there is no deal.

### My bottom line
I am not here to threaten for effect. I am saying plainly: **a settlement that does not create structural cost relief is not acceptable to management, and it will not restore the company’s competitiveness.**

We can discuss instruments. We can discuss sequencing. We can discuss how to avoid unnecessary disruption. But we cannot discuss away the need for a material and durable reduction in cost and capacity.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that genuinely replaces them. If it doesn’t do that, then you should expect management to continue to insist on structural options, including closures, because the alternative is an uncompetitive company.
