We agree on the diagnosis: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value, damage the brand, and weaken the German base that remains central to the group.

Where I do **not** agree is with turning **“no closures”** or **“no compulsory redundancies”** into absolute starting points before the economics have been tested.

### To the works council and the union
I respect the insistence on negotiated change and the concern for the workforce. But I do **not** accept a package that freezes the industrial footprint first and asks management and owners to hope the cost problem disappears. If the company is structurally uncompetitive, then value erodes whether we call it “protection” or not.

You have said capacity can be reduced internally. That may be true — but only if it is **hard**, **measurable**, and **permanent**. Shift removals, line consolidation, redeployment, and qualification can be part of a real solution. What they cannot be is a substitute for actual structural savings if the break-even point remains too high.

I also do **not** accept a blanket job and site guarantee to 2030 if it effectively removes governance flexibility irrespective of economic reality. That is not stewardship; it is a freeze. I am not prepared to lock the company into a structure that prevents correction if the market deteriorates further.

### To the regional state
I understand your role as anchor shareholder and regional protector. But I cannot support a categorical no-closure stance as a principle. If the independent numbers show that the required savings cannot be achieved without deeper structural moves, then excluding those tools in advance is not responsible.

That said, I do agree with you that closures should not be the first reflex. They should be a fallback only if a non-closure package truly fails the economic test. That is the right sequencing.

### To management
I support your insistence on a **real** restructuring and a material labor-cost contribution. I do **not** support using closures as a blunt bargaining weapon if a credible alternative exists. But I also will not back a deal that preserves all legacy arrangements and leaves the margin problem unresolved.

If you want support for a non-closure path, then the package must show:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- durable labor-cost relief,
- productivity and flexibility gains,
- management and overhead contribution,
- and a credible investment path that supports future volume and technology.

That is the minimum.

### To the institutional investors
I agree with your point that we should not confuse political comfort with value preservation. A weak compromise would simply defer the problem. I will not support that. But value preservation also means avoiding needless destruction of industrial capability and social trust if a hard, quantified alternative can deliver the same result.

### My position
I am **open to a negotiated solution without closures**, but only if it is economically equivalent to closure-level restructuring. That means:
- measurable output and capacity removal,
- permanent fixed-cost reduction,
- a durable labor-cost reset,
- and enforceable investment commitments.

If the package does not achieve that, then I will not pretend it is sufficient just because it avoids conflict in the short term.
