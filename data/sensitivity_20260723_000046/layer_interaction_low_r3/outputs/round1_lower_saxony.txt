We share the starting point that this cannot be a unilateral move and that the company needs a credible future in Germany. I also hear management’s concern that the cost base is too high and that the group must remain investable. That is a legitimate concern.

But I need to draw a clear line on what is not acceptable from the state’s point of view:

- no plant closures in Germany as the starting point,
- no mass job destruction,
- no solution that strips the company of its role as a regional economic anchor,
- and no attempt to sideline the co-determination and blocking rights that are part of this company’s governance.

The company is not just another balance-sheet exercise. It is deeply embedded in the region, in employment, in supplier networks, and in public confidence. A restructuring that destroys sites or treats the workforce as a cost to be cut away would create long-term damage that is not justified by short-term accounting gains.

At the same time, I do not dismiss the need for change. If there is a negotiated package that:
- preserves the German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- secures investment in future products and technology,
- and delivers real, durable productivity and cost improvements,

then the state can engage constructively. But that has to be a genuine social-partnership solution, not closures dressed up as necessity.

So my position is this:

- **I can support restructuring.**
- **I cannot support closures or a settlement that guts employment in Germany.**
- **I expect management, controlling owners, and labour to come forward with an alternative that is credible on competitiveness and fair on burden-sharing.**

To management: if you insist that the only serious options include closures and large layoffs, then you will meet a very strong veto position here.  
To labour: if you want to avoid that outcome, the alternative must be substantial enough to restore competitiveness, not just delay the decision.

In short: I am prepared to back a tough but negotiated restructuring. I am not prepared to endorse a socially and regionally destructive one.
