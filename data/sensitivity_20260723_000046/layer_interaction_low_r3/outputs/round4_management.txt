I’ll be equally direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this cannot be cosmetic**. A pretend restructuring that leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged would be worse than no deal. We need a durable reset in fixed cost, capacity, and break-even economics.

But I disagree with the works council and union on the core premise that **“every German plant stays open”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** can be fixed red lines regardless of the economic outcome. That is not a restructuring framework; it is a veto on the very tools that may be necessary to restore competitiveness.

## Where I agree
- **A real test of alternatives is necessary.**  
  I accept the expert’s logic: if a non-closure package can truly remove enough fixed cost and capacity to match the closure case, then it is serious.
- **Plant closures are not the goal in themselves.**  
  They are an instrument, not a virtue.
- **Management and ownership must contribute.**  
  I have no interest in asking only the workforce to carry the burden.
- **Investment for future products and technology matters.**  
  Without that, there is no 2030 case.

## Where I disagree
### To the works council
You say every German plant stays open. I cannot commit to that as a principle. If the company remains structurally overcapacitated, then preserving all sites at all costs would lock in a high fixed-cost base and weaken the company further.

You also reject closures as a fallback. I cannot accept that. If the non-closure package does not deliver closure-level economics, then management has to retain structural options. Otherwise we are not restructuring; we are postponing the problem.

### To the union
You are right that workers should not pre-pay for management failure. But your position still reads as if no-closure, no-layoff, and no-closure-threat can be guaranteed before the numbers are proven. That is too strong.

The expert has already said the key test:
- permanent capacity removal,
- permanent fixed-cost reduction,
- lower break-even volume,
- and a credible 3–5 year cash-flow improvement.

If you can help deliver that without closures, I’m prepared to work with it. If not, then insisting on absolute exclusions is not a solution.

### To the regional state
I appreciate your role and your concern for industrial stability. But a categorical no-closure stance is not responsible if it prevents the company from reaching a viable structure. The company’s competitiveness matters too. A protected but uncompetitive footprint is not a win for the region in the long run.

### To the family holding and investors
I agree with the principle that we need measurable, durable savings. I do **not** support a weak compromise or a delay mechanism. If the non-closure package does not materially reset the economics, then it is not acceptable.

## What would move management
I am prepared to back a **no-closure path** only if it delivers all of the following in a verified, binding way:

1. **Permanent reduction in capacity**
   - shift elimination where needed
   - line consolidation
   - product/variant rationalization
   - site-level footprint shrinkage inside the German network

2. **Permanent reduction in fixed cost**
   - headcount reduction through attrition, redeployment, or negotiated exits
   - management and overhead cuts
   - simplification of support functions

3. **Durable labour-cost contribution**
   - wage moderation or freeze
   - bonus/allowance reductions
   - flexibility on working time and deployment
   - all with a long enough horizon to matter

4. **Lower break-even volume over 3–5 years**
   - not just year-one savings
   - a real improvement in steady-state cash generation

5. **Binding investment commitments**
   - to future products, platforms, and technology
   - so the remaining footprint has a future

If that package reaches closure-level economics, I am open to it. If it does not, then the company must keep the option of deeper structural measures on the table.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a blanket guarantee to 2030 that freezes the industrial structure,
- a package that preserves all sites while leaving the cost base uncompetitive,
- temporary concessions dressed up as transformation,
- or a settlement that eliminates management flexibility irrespective of market reality.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve a political comfort zone. I am here to restore competitiveness, protect the brand’s return on sales, and free funds for future investment through 2030.

That requires:
- structural savings,
- real capacity reduction,
- real labour-cost contribution,
- and a settlement that changes the economics, not just the optics.

So I am prepared to negotiate a serious non-closure package — but only if it is economically equivalent to the hard restructuring we need. If it is not, then we should stop calling it a solution.
