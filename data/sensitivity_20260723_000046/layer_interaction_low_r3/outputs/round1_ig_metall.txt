We agree on the core point that restructuring, if it is to be durable, cannot simply be loaded onto the workforce alone. The burden must be shared fairly, and there has to be a credible future plan for the German sites.

But I need to be equally clear on where we do **not** agree: we will not accept a “solution” built on plant closures or compulsory redundancies. That would not be a restructuring agreement; it would be a break with the collective job-security framework and a direct attack on the workforce that has carried this company through the transformation.

From the union’s side, our position is:

- **No forced layoffs**
- **No plant closures**
- **Preserve the collective bargaining framework**
- **A negotiated contribution from labour is possible**
- **Management, ownership, and the company must also contribute materially**

We already showed openness with our counter-proposal. That means we are not refusing change. We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time measures,
- temporary sacrifices on labour costs,
- waiving bonuses or creating an employee contribution,
- internal flexibility and redeployment,
- productivity measures that do not destroy sites.

But that openness is conditional. It only makes sense if there are hard guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment. We will not ask our members to pay for the restructuring while management preserves all strategic options for itself and shifts the pain downward.

So my question to management and the owners is simple: are you prepared to pursue savings through a fair package that keeps every German site open, or are you still insisting on closures and layoffs as the starting point? If it is the latter, then you should expect strong resistance, including industrial action.
