We are aligned with the expert’s core point: **the real test is economic equivalence, not whether a package is politically comfortable**.

From the capital-markets side, that means:

- **No support for a cosmetic settlement**
- **No support for a deal that preserves all sites but leaves the cost base broken**
- **No support for temporary concessions that expire before the turnaround is complete**
- **Yes to a package that produces durable, measurable savings and restores margins**

### Where we agree
- With management: the current structure is too expensive, and the company does need structural change.
- With the family owner: durability matters, and a weak compromise would just defer value destruction.
- With the expert: if there is true overcapacity, then **capacity must come out somewhere**.
- With the state and labour: strikes and prolonged conflict would destroy value and should be avoided if a credible alternative exists.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** accept the works council and union position that “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” can be a hard red line **regardless of economics**.  
That is not a restructuring constraint; it is a veto on the very instruments that may be required to restore competitiveness.

I also do **not** accept the state’s framing if it implies that plant closures are off-limits as a matter of principle. If the economics require hard capacity removal, then the company cannot be forced to carry excess footprint indefinitely just to preserve appearances.

### What actually matters to us
If the alternative package can credibly deliver:
- real capacity reduction,
- permanent fixed-cost reduction,
- lower unit costs,
- and a clear path back to competitive return-on-sales,

then we can support it — even if it avoids formal plant closures.

But if the package is built around:
- working-time tweaks,
- temporary wage give-backs,
- vague productivity promises,
- and binding guarantees that freeze the footprint,

then we will regard it as **value-destructive**.

### Our bottom line
We are open to a **non-closure** restructuring **only if** it is economically equivalent to closure-level savings.  
Otherwise, management should not be pressured into accepting a weak compromise just to avoid conflict.

If no one can show the numbers, then the discussion is not about strategy anymore; it is about preserving legacy arrangements at shareholders’ expense.
