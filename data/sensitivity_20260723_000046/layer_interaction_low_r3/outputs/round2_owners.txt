We agree on the diagnosis that the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one. I also agree that prolonged conflict would destroy value and that the German sites matter for the company’s future.

Where I disagree is on the attempt to convert “no closures” into an absolute starting point. That is not a governance position I can support, because it removes a necessary instrument before we have seen whether the alternatives can actually deliver the scale of savings required.

To the works council and union:
- I respect the refusal to accept forced layoffs as the first answer.
- I do **not** accept a blanket guarantee through 2030 that effectively freezes the industrial footprint regardless of economics.
- I also do **not** accept the argument that preserving every site is automatically the most responsible long-term choice.

If the company remains structurally uncompetitive, then value erodes slowly, investment falls, and jobs disappear later and more broadly. That is not stewardship. So if you want the family holding to support an alternative to closures, it must be concrete enough to replace them economically, not just morally.

To the state:
- I share your concern about regional stability and social partnership.
- I do **not** share the view that closures are unacceptable as a matter of principle.
- But I do agree that they should not be the starting point if a credible non-closure package can deliver equivalent results.

To management:
- I support a serious restructuring and a material labour-cost contribution.
- I do **not** support using closures as a blunt threat if a negotiated alternative can do the job.
- But I also will not back a deal that preserves every legacy arrangement and leaves the company’s margin problem unresolved.

The test is simple: does the package restore competitiveness in a durable way?

What would move me:
- real, measurable capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- binding productivity and flexibility measures,
- management/overhead contribution,
- and investment commitments that protect future volume and technology.

What will not move me:
- temporary measures dressed up as structural reform,
- guarantees that freeze the footprint first and ask for a business case later,
- or a package that buys industrial peace while leaving the economics broken.

So my position is this: I am open to a negotiated solution that avoids closures, but only if it is economically equivalent and enforceable. If it is not, then I will not support preserving the current structure at the expense of the company’s long-term value.
