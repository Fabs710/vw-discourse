### Core impasse
The central deadlock is over **whether structural adjustment can be accepted only if it preserves all German sites and rules out compulsory redundancies**, or whether **some form of hard capacity removal, including possible closures, must remain available** to achieve a durable cost reset.

More specifically, the parties are split on three linked issues:

1. **Red lines vs. bargaining scope**
   - Labour, works council, and the state want closures and forced redundancies excluded up front.
   - Management, owners, and investors want those options retained unless a non-closure package proves economically equivalent.

2. **What counts as an equivalent alternative**
   - Employee-side actors see shift changes, redeployment, flexibility, investment, and overhead cuts as a sufficient basis if binding.
   - Capital-side actors and the expert say those measures only work if they remove enough capacity and fixed cost to match closure-level savings.

3. **Trust and sequencing**
   - Labour will not “pre-pay” without guarantees.
   - Management and owners will not give full guarantees before seeing a credible, quantified restructuring package.

---

### Possible compromise positions
These are framed as procedural bridge options, not endorsements:

#### 1) “No-closure unless equivalence is proven” package
A negotiated framework in which:
- plant closures are **not the preferred option**,
- but remain a **fallback only if an independent assessment finds the non-closure package insufficient**,
- and the parties jointly define the savings/capacity thresholds that count as equivalent.

This could bridge labour’s desire to avoid closures with management’s insistence on measurable, structural savings.

#### 2) “Site preservation with hard capacity reduction”
A package that explicitly preserves German sites **but removes capacity through internal restructuring**, such as:
- shift elimination,
- line consolidation,
- product/platform concentration,
- reduced overhead,
- permanent labour-cost changes,
- and binding investment commitments.

This would aim to give the employee side site security while still delivering the economic effects that capital-side stakeholders demand.

#### 3) “Conditional guarantees to 2030”
A staged deal where:
- no closures or compulsory redundancies apply **if** defined savings and productivity milestones are met,
- management receives the ability to reopen structural options only if milestones fail,
- and labour receives binding investment and redeployment commitments in return.

This attempts to convert the current clash over absolute guarantees into a conditional, performance-based framework.

---

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
What minimum quantified savings, capacity reduction, and productivity measures would you accept as proof that a no-closure package is truly equivalent to closure-level restructuring?

#### To the Industrial Union
Would you accept a package with no closures and no compulsory redundancies if it includes permanent shift removal, line consolidation, and binding cost-reduction targets verified by an independent expert?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What specific savings threshold, by site and by year, would convince you that a non-closure package is economically sufficient to replace closures as a restructuring tool?

#### To the Regional State Government
If an independent review shows that preserving every site would leave the company structurally uncompetitive, would you support partial capacity reduction short of closure, or do you require a categorical no-closure stance?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a conditional no-closure agreement if it includes enforceable performance triggers, but reserves the right to revisit site-level measures if those triggers are missed?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What evidence would you require to treat a no-closure package as value-preserving rather than as a delay mechanism?

#### To the Independent Restructuring Adviser
Can you specify the concrete threshold at which a non-closure package becomes economically equivalent to a closure-based restructuring in fixed cost, utilization, and 3–5 year cash flow terms?
