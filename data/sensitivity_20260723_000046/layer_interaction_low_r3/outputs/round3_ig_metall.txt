We are at the point where words need to turn into a **binding package**, not a threat exchange.

### To management
I agree with you on one thing: this company needs a **real restructuring**, not a decorative one. A durable solution must improve competitiveness, not just postpone the problem.

I do **not** agree that this requires plant closures or compulsory redundancies. You keep presenting those as the serious option, but seriousness is measured by whether the package removes enough cost and capacity — not by how much damage it does to the workforce.

What I can accept is:
- permanent shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- durable labour-cost contributions,
- and management/overhead cuts.

What I cannot accept is:
- closures kept in reserve as a pressure tool,
- forced redundancies,
- or a deal where employees pre-pay while management keeps the right to break the job-security framework later.

If you want the union to move, then bring a package that is **economically credible without closure threats**. Until then, your “fallback” is not a solution; it is escalation.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the core line:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **protection of the collective wage framework**
- **binding site and employment security**

I also agree with your point that capacity reduction does not automatically mean closing a site. It can and must be done through internal measures first.

Where I would sharpen it is this: we should not let the other side turn “durability” into a code word for breaking collective standards. If there is a contribution from labour, it must be **fair, reciprocal, and time-limited unless matched by binding guarantees**.

### To the regional state
Your role matters, and I acknowledge that you are closer to the workforce side than the capital side. But “no closures as a starting point” is not enough. We need you to back a package that **excludes forced redundancies and site destruction** in practice, not just in tone.

If the state wants to be an anchor shareholder, then it should support:
- site security,
- investment commitments,
- and a negotiated industrial future.

It should not normalize the idea that plant closures are a legitimate bargaining chip against the workforce.

### To the family owners and investors
You are right about one thing: a weak compromise would just defer the crisis. I am not offering that.

But you are wrong if you think the only durable answer is to keep closures alive at all times. That may look clean on a spreadsheet, but it destroys trust, triggers conflict, and makes the company less governable.

If you want a durable package, then it has to be:
- measurable,
- enforceable,
- and shared in burden.

That means management and ownership must also contribute, not just ask the workforce for cuts. A settlement that loads the pain onto employees while preserving all strategic options for the top is not durable; it is one-sided.

### On the expert’s assessment
The expert has made the key point: a non-closure package can work **only if it is a real footprint and cost reset**, not just a labour-relations compromise.

That is exactly where I stand. If capacity has to come out, then let it come out through:
- shift elimination,
- line consolidation,
- lower complexity,
- productivity measures,
- and redeployment.

What I do **not** accept is the leap from “capacity reduction is needed” to “therefore closures and layoffs must remain available.” That leap is not forced by the economics; it is a management preference.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate a **serious labour contribution** if the company offers:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site security**
4. **Durable capacity reduction through internal measures**
5. **Management, overhead, and ownership contribution**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and volumes**
7. **A co-determined implementation path**

That is the minimum basis for a settlement.

If that is not on the table, then we have no agreement — and the union will defend the workforce accordingly. I am not going to dress that up as compromise.
