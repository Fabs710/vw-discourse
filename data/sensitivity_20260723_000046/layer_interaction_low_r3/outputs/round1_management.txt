We are prepared to discuss a negotiated restructuring, but let me be equally clear: a package that simply preserves every site, rules out compulsory redundancies, and leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged is not acceptable.

I do agree with one point from both the works council and the union: this cannot be a unilateral diktat. If we want a durable solution, it has to be negotiated, and it has to create a credible future for the German operations. But credibility also means confronting the reality we are in now: our factory costs are too high, our European market is shrinking, and the current structure does not support the margins we need to fund future products and technology.

So my position is this:

- We need a **structural** reduction in cost and capacity.
- We need a **material labour-cost contribution**.
- We need a package that restores the brand’s **return-on-sales** to a competitive level.
- We need to free up funds for investment into the 2030 product and technology roadmap.

That means I am not prepared to sign up today to blanket guarantees that rule out closures, deep capacity reductions, or workforce reductions. If the only offer on the table is “no closures, no layoffs, no hard structural change,” then it does not solve the problem.

At the same time, I am not saying that plant closures are the only route. If the employee side can put forward an alternative that delivers equivalent, durable savings — including real labour-cost relief, reduced overcapacity, and binding productivity gains — then I am prepared to examine it seriously. But it must be measurable, enforceable, and large enough to matter. Cosmetic adjustments or temporary measures will not be enough.

On the question of strikes: prolonged industrial action would damage output, reputation, and the very investment case we are trying to protect. But I also have to be honest that management will not accept a settlement that preserves the current cost base and merely postpones the adjustment.

So my message is straightforward:  
we are open to negotiation, but not to a settlement that fails to deliver structural competitiveness. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it needs to bring a package that genuinely replaces them with comparable savings.
