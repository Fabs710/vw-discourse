We are now at the point where the discussion must stop hiding behind abstractions.

### To management
I acknowledge one thing: you are right that the company needs a **real** restructuring, not a symbolic one. We are not denying the pressure on margins, capacity, or competitiveness.

But I reject your attempt to make **closures and compulsory redundancies the benchmark of seriousness**. That is not a neutral economic claim; it is a choice to put the burden of management failure onto the workforce and the German sites.

You still have not shown that closures are necessary. You have shown only that they are convenient for your preferred cost-reset model. That is not enough.

If you want a serious alternative, then test the alternatives seriously:
- permanent shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- management and overhead cuts,
- and durable labour-cost concessions.

But do **not** ask us to accept plant closures “as fallback” while you keep the threat over the workforce. That is not a negotiation. It is coercion.

### To the union
I agree with you on the core red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective job-security framework.

That is the right line. The workforce should not be forced to pay for restructuring by losing its sites and security.

### To the regional state
Your position is closer to ours, and I welcome that. But I need to be blunt: a “no closures as starting point” position is not enough if it still leaves the door open to site destruction later. The state should use its influence to **exclude closures and forced layoffs**, not merely postpone the decision.

### To the family holding and investors
You are asking for economic equivalence. Fine. Then let us be precise: equivalence does **not** mean we must accept plant closures first and hope the alternatives fail. It means the alternatives must be given a fair and quantified test **before** anyone is allowed to talk about closing German plants.

What I do not accept is the logic that if a non-closure package is not identical to a closure package on paper, then it must be rejected. That is too narrow. A company is not only a spreadsheet; it is also a production system, a workforce, and a regional industrial base. Destroying that base has its own long-term cost.

### Where I stand
I am prepared to discuss a **hard restructuring without closures**, but only if it is built on these principles:

1. **Every German plant stays open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding employment and site security**
4. **Internal capacity reduction first**
5. **Management, overhead, and ownership must contribute**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and volume**
7. **Co-determined implementation, not unilateral diktat**

That is the only basis on which we will continue.

### What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- “conditional guarantees” that are just a cover for later closures,
- voluntary measures used to mask forced job destruction,
- underinvestment that weakens sites over time,
- or a restructuring that treats co-determination as an obstacle rather than a legal reality.

### My position on the expert’s logic
The expert may be right that some capacity must come out. But capacity reduction does **not** automatically require plant closures. If management wants output reduction, then reduce output, shift patterns, and complexity. Do it inside the existing German footprint.

That is the challenge: show that the company can become competitive **without breaking its historic commitment to German sites and jobs**.

So let me be clear:
- I do **not** accept closures as an option to be kept alive in reserve.
- I do **not** accept forced redundancies under any restructuring scenario.
- I do accept a negotiated package of real savings if — and only if — it preserves every German plant and protects the workforce.

If management cannot accept that, then the conflict will continue, and the workforce will defend itself accordingly.
