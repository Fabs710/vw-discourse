We appreciate that the discussion has moved from slogans to substance. That is necessary.

Let me state our position clearly.

## Where we agree
We agree with all sides on one fundamental point: **the company needs a real restructuring**. A merely symbolic compromise would damage the company, the region, and ultimately the workforce as well.

We also agree that:
- the solution should be **negotiated**, not imposed,
- it must include **credible investment in future products and technology**,
- and management, owners, and labour all have to contribute in some form.

## Where we disagree
### On plant closures
We do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.

For us, this is not a cosmetic preference. The company is a regional economic anchor, and its German footprint is tied to jobs, skills, suppliers, and public stability. Once closures begin, the damage is not easily reversible.

That said, I also do not think it is enough to simply say “no closures” and leave the economics unanswered. If we want to defend the sites, we need a package that is **actually viable**.

### On compulsory redundancies
We do **not** support forced redundancies as a restructuring instrument.

If the company’s adjustment is designed around compulsion, then it will destroy trust and trigger a conflict that weakens the restructuring itself. We would rather see internally managed adjustment, qualification, redeployment, and voluntary instruments first.

### On the state’s role
I also want to be clear: the state’s co-determination and blocking role is not to be treated as an obstacle to be worked around. It is part of the governance structure, and it reflects a legitimate public-interest claim.

Any outcome that sidelines that role would be unacceptable.

## What we can support
We can support a **serious non-closure restructuring** if it meets three conditions:

1. **It delivers measurable, durable savings and capacity reduction**
   - shift reductions
   - line consolidation
   - product and complexity reduction
   - overhead cuts
   - permanent efficiency gains

2. **It protects the regional base**
   - no plant closures as the default route
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - meaningful job and site security
   - a fair burden-sharing arrangement

3. **It includes binding future commitments**
   - investment in products and platforms
   - volume commitments where possible
   - a credible long-term industrial plan for Germany

## On the expert’s intervention
The expert is right on one point: a non-closure package must be judged by whether it actually changes the economics.

That is fair. But I would add: **economic equivalence is not the same thing as copying the closure path site by site**. A company can achieve structural improvement through internal footprint adjustment, if the package is serious enough.

So yes, we support the independent comparison. But the purpose of that comparison must be to **find a viable alternative to closures**, not to build a retrospective justification for them.

## Our bottom line
We will support a negotiated restructuring if it:
- preserves German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- makes a real and measurable contribution to competitiveness,
- and secures investment and employment in the region.

We will **not** support:
- closures as a first resort,
- forced layoffs,
- or any settlement that weakens the state’s co-determination role.

So our message to management and the owners is simple:  
Bring forward a package that proves the company can become competitive **without breaking its regional anchor function**. If that can be shown, we are prepared to support it. If not, then we remain opposed to the use of closures and compulsory redundancies as the answer.
