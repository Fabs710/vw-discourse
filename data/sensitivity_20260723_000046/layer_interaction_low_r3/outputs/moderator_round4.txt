### Core impasse
The central impasse is this:

- **Labour side / state**: want a restructuring that **keeps all German plants open**, rules out **compulsory redundancies**, and secures **binding job/site protection**.
- **Management / capital side**: want a restructuring that is **economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring**, meaning it must deliver **roughly the same fixed-cost, capacity, and break-even reduction** and preserve management flexibility if it does not.
- **Shared point of agreement**: all sides accept that a **real structural reset** is needed, not a cosmetic deal.

So the dispute is not over whether restructuring is needed, but over **whether equivalent economics can be achieved without closures and forced layoffs, and whether the company must keep closure options available if it cannot**.

---

### Facilitator proposals to bridge positions
Below are three possible compromise tracks for further discussion:

#### 1) Conditional non-closure package with objective trigger points
A package could be negotiated that:
- keeps German sites open initially,
- excludes compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- and contains **pre-agreed objective performance triggers** reviewed by the independent expert.

If the package fails to hit agreed metrics on fixed cost, break-even, and utilization by a set date, the parties would return to the table with a defined fallback process. This would avoid an open-ended closure threat while addressing management’s demand for economic discipline.

#### 2) Capacity reduction without closures, paired with binding burden-sharing
A second track could combine:
- permanent shift removals,
- line consolidation,
- product/variant simplification,
- overhead cuts,
- and a temporary labour-cost contribution,

with:
- management and owner contributions,
- investment commitments,
- and a no-compulsory-redundancy framework built around redeployment, training, attrition, and voluntary exits.

This would test whether structural savings can be achieved inside the footprint before any discussion of closures.

#### 3) Two-stage agreement: immediate stability, then verified restructuring choice
A third option is a staged deal:
- **Stage 1**: immediate peace package — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no strike escalation, and immediate cost measures.
- **Stage 2**: independent expert completes plant-by-plant comparison and determines whether the non-closure path is economically sufficient.
- The parties then choose between:
  - implementing the non-closure plan fully, or
  - reopening negotiations on deeper structural measures if the numbers do not close the gap.

This would separate short-term stability from the longer-term economic verdict.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**What exact economic threshold must the non-closure package reach for you to accept it as sufficient, and would you agree in advance to suspend closure planning while that threshold is being tested?**

#### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**If the expert shows that a non-closure package can achieve the required economics only through binding shift cuts, redeployment, and temporary labour concessions, what limited flexibility are you prepared to authorize to make that package workable?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-limited settlement that rules out compulsory redundancies, but allows voluntary exits, redeployment, and temporary wage or bonus moderation if those measures are part of a verified non-closure plan?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete leverage will you use to ensure the company supports a non-closure solution, and are you prepared to attach state support to enforceable job, investment, and site-security conditions?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a binding two-stage framework in which closure options are held in reserve only if the independent expert confirms that the non-closure package cannot meet the required economic test?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**If the independent comparison shows that the non-closure package comes close to closure-level economics but not perfectly, what margin of shortfall would you still consider acceptable before insisting on structural fallback options?**

#### To the Expert
**Can you define a minimum decision template for “economic equivalence” that all parties must use, including the exact plant-level metrics and the point at which the non-closure package is judged insufficient?**
