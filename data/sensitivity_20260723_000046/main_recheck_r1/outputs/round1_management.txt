I hear the message from both the Works Council and the union, but I need to be equally clear: **we cannot defend a structure that is permanently uncompetitive**.

We are in an acute crisis, not a theoretical debate. The European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our German cost base is materially above target. If we leave the current structure intact, we will damage the brand’s return, crowd out investment, and weaken the company’s ability to fund the products and technologies we need for 2030.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to escalation**.
- **Industrial peace matters**.
- **We need a credible future for the German sites**.
- **One-sided measures won’t work**.

I also agree that the workforce should not be treated as the only adjustment variable. We need a package, not a blunt unilateral cut.

### Where I disagree
What I cannot accept is the claim that:
- every German plant must stay open,
- no compulsory redundancies can ever be on the table,
- and long-standing guarantees must remain untouched regardless of economic reality.

That is not a viable starting point. If we lock in all sites and all guarantees, we preserve overcapacity and keep a cost base that the market will not support. That would be a cosmetic compromise, not restructuring.

### What management needs
To be very direct: we need a **durable structural package** that delivers:
1. **real capacity reduction**,
2. **a genuine labour-cost contribution**,
3. **lower fixed costs**, and
4. **freedom to invest in future products and technology**.

That means we are looking for more than temporary flexibility or deferred pain. Working-time adjustments, voluntary exits, productivity gains, and negotiated wage contributions can all be part of the solution — **but only if they add up to a material and lasting cost base reduction**.

### On closures and job cuts
I am not attached to closures as an end in themselves. What matters is the result. If equivalent savings can be achieved through other structurally credible measures, I am open to that. But I will not pretend that voluntary measures alone will close the gap if they do not actually change the economics.

So my position is:
- **No cosmetic deal**
- **No arrangement that preserves excess capacity**
- **No agreement that leaves us unable to compete by 2030**

### On next steps
If the employee side is prepared to bring forward a package that is genuinely equivalent in savings and durability to plant consolidation and workforce reduction, then we can negotiate on the mechanism. But the burden of proof is on anyone arguing that we can fix this without hard structural measures.

If that proof is not there, then we will have to pursue the restructuring that restores competitiveness, even if that means confronting difficult decisions.
