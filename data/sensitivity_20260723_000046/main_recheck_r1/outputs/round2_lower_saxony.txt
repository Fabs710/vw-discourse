I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and the investors on one point: **this company needs a real competitiveness reset**. A purely cosmetic deal that leaves the cost base untouched would simply postpone the crisis and deepen it later.

I also agree with the works council and the union that **the burden cannot fall only on employees**. If there is to be a restructuring, it must be shared, negotiated, and paired with real investment commitments.

## Where I disagree with management
What I cannot support is the assumption that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default solution**.

That approach is too blunt, too irreversible, and too risky for the regional economy. It would weaken the company’s industrial base, damage trust, and create political and social fallout that could easily outweigh the supposed savings. If there is a viable alternative that achieves the same economic effect with less damage, then management must take it seriously.

But I also need to be clear: **simply saying “no closures, no layoffs” is not enough either**. If the employee side insists on absolute guarantees without showing how the economics still work, that is not a settlement path.

## My position
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, my priorities are:

- **protect German jobs and sites as far as possible**
- **preserve the company as a long-term regional anchor**
- **avoid irreversible damage to the industrial base**
- **keep co-determination and the state’s governance role intact**
- **support a restructuring that is economically credible, not symbolic**

That means I am open to:
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity measures,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- site specialization,
- and binding investment commitments in Germany.

What I am **not** open to is a solution that:
- guts regional employment,
- treats closures as the easy answer,
- or sidelines the state and workforce from decisions with lasting consequences.

## On the specific arguments
### To management
You have not yet demonstrated that closures are unavoidable **instead of** negotiated alternatives. If you want support for hard restructuring, then bring a package that proves:
1. the savings gap,
2. why it cannot be closed otherwise,
3. and why your preferred path is better for long-term viability than a negotiated alternative.

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are politically understandable, and I share the goal of protecting sites and jobs. But I cannot endorse a position that simply freezes the current structure and calls that a plan. There must be room for negotiated capacity reduction and real contributions if the company is to remain competitive.

### To the family and investors
I hear the demand for durability and margin recovery. I do not dismiss it. But a solution that destroys the company’s regional legitimacy, triggers prolonged labor conflict, and weakens its German footprint is not value-preserving. It may look efficient on paper and fail in practice.

## My bottom line
I am **not** here to block restructuring. I am here to block a restructuring that sacrifices the regional industrial base unnecessarily.

So my test is simple:
- If there is a package that delivers **real, durable savings** while **preserving as many German jobs and sites as possible**, I will engage constructively.
- If the only answer is closures and forced layoffs, I will resist it.
- If the only answer from labor is “nothing may change,” I will not support that either.

We need a **numerically credible, socially balanced package** with binding commitments on:
- site protection,
- investment,
- employment transition,
- and governance stability.

Until then, my position remains: **no rush to closures, no sidelining of co-determination, and no transfer of all adjustment risk onto workers and the region**.
