I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the state on the central diagnosis: **this company has a real structural cost and competitiveness problem**. If the German footprint remains too expensive and too inflexible, long-term value will erode. That is unacceptable.

I also agree with the expert that this cannot be solved by slogans. The right question is whether a package delivers **durable, enforceable, risk-adjusted savings**. That is the proper standard.

And I agree with the workforce side on one point: **a cosmetic deal would be worse than no deal**. A short-lived truce that leaves the economics unchanged is not stewardship.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position that:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- full job guarantees through 2030,
- and preservation of all legacy protections

can be treated as immovable red lines **and** still produce a credible restructuring.

That is not a restructuring settlement unless it closes the savings gap in a hard, durable way. If it does not, then it is a defense of the status quo.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept a simplistic closure-first approach as automatically superior. For a controlling owner with a long horizon, value destruction from strikes, political escalation, reputational damage, and trust collapse is real. A bad closure strategy can destroy more value than it creates.

## My position
The question is not “closures or no closures” as a matter of principle. The question is:

**What package restores competitiveness with the least value destruction, and can it be made durable enough to hold?**

That means I am open to a **hard hybrid solution** if it is genuinely equivalent on economics and enforceability.

## What I need to see
The parties are still arguing from positions. We need a factual basis.

[SUMMON: independent restructuring finance specialist to compare, by site and by year, the closure-led, hybrid, and non-closure options on risk-adjusted recurring savings, one-time costs, strike risk, reversal risk, and 5-year NPV.]

That analysis should answer:
1. What is the exact savings gap?
2. What does closure actually save, net of costs and delays?
3. What does a hybrid package save, net of concessions and enforcement?
4. What does a no-closure package save, net of execution risk and reversal risk?
5. Which path best protects enterprise value over five years?

## To the Works Council
I respect the insistence on co-determination, job security, and avoiding irreversible harm. That is legitimate.

But I do **not** accept the claim that management must prove closure necessity while labor withholds any equivalence test. If you want closures ruled out, then a non-closure package must be shown to close the gap in a way that is recurring and enforceable.

Voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, specialization, and productivity measures may help. But I have not yet seen evidence that they are enough on their own.

## To the Union
You are right that workers should not bear the burden alone. They should not.

But “no closures, no forced redundancies, full guarantees” is only credible if paired with a package that actually changes the cost base. If the measures are temporary or reversible, they are not equivalent. They do not restore competitiveness; they postpone conflict.

## To Management
You are right to insist that the numbers matter. But you still need to prove the case, not just assert it.

I expect:
- the exact annual savings gap,
- by site and function,
- implementation costs,
- strike and delay risk,
- and the 5-year risk-adjusted comparison versus a negotiated alternative.

If closures or forced redundancies are being kept as options, they must be justified on that basis, not assumed.

## To the State Government
I understand your concern about regional damage. It is valid. But protecting the industrial base means protecting a **viable** industrial base, not freezing an uncompetitive structure.

I can support a solution that protects sites and jobs **if** it genuinely produces the needed savings. I cannot support a solution that preserves everything and leaves the company weak.

## To Institutional Investors
Your focus on margins, discipline, and long-term value is correct. But I would caution against pretending that any closure path is automatically value-maximizing. Execution risk matters, and so does labor peace.

The right answer is the package with the best **risk-adjusted** value outcome, not the cleanest spreadsheet line.

## Bottom line
I am **not** open to:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a status quo package disguised as reform,
- or a deal that preserves structural uncompetitiveness.

I am **open** to:
- a hard, quantified hybrid settlement,
- real capacity consolidation,
- measurable labor contributions,
- binding investment commitments,
- and enforceable triggers if targets are missed.

If the parties want my support, they must bring a package that is **numerically sufficient, durable, and enforceable**. Until then, I will not endorse a settlement merely because it is politically convenient.
