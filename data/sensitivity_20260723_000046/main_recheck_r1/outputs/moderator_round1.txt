**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **real competitiveness and cost problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **negotiated solution is preferable** to escalation or unilateral action.
- Most parties accept that the workforce should **not bear the adjustment alone**.
- There is openness across the table to discuss:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - productivity measures,
  - training,
  - temporary labor-cost contributions,
  - and investment commitments.
- Several parties agree that any solution must be **durable**, not merely a postponement.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works Council and union reject them outright.  
  - Management, investors, and the family see them as potentially necessary if no equivalent alternative exists.  
  - State government is skeptical of closures and wants them avoided if possible.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council and union reject them.  
  - Management and investors keep them as an implicit option; the family and state are reluctant but not fully categorical.
- **Binding job guarantees through 2030 / preserving all existing guarantees:**  
  - Employee side wants them preserved.  
  - Management and investors say this may be incompatible with restructuring.
- **Scope of structural change:**  
  - Employee side wants restructuring without major job/site losses.  
  - Management and capital-side actors insist that real capacity and cost reduction must be substantial enough to restore competitiveness.
- **Role of the state and co-determination:**  
  - The state insists on a meaningful role and rejects being sidelined.  
  - Management wants room to execute restructuring; boundaries of that role remain unsettled.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** how much employment/site protection can be maintained while still producing meaningful cost savings?
- **Durable savings vs. social stability:** whether voluntary and negotiated measures can equal the savings of closures/layoffs without causing later backsliding.
- **Short-term peace vs. long-term restructuring:** whether a compromise can avoid strikes and conflict while still changing the economics enough.
- **Investment commitments vs. labor concessions:** what concrete future investment, product, and site commitments would be offered in exchange for workforce contributions?
- **Equity of burden-sharing:** how costs, risks, and sacrifices are distributed among labor, management, owners, and the state.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **concrete package design**:
- identify which sites/functions are in scope,
- quantify the savings gap that must be closed,
- specify which non-closure alternatives are genuinely equivalent,
- define what workforce concessions are on the table,
- and state what binding investment/job-security commitments could accompany a deal.

The next round should aim to test whether a **credible, numerically sufficient, socially balanced package** exists that can be accepted by the main sides.
