We agree on the diagnosis, but not on any outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- A **cosmetic settlement** would destroy value.
- The workforce should **not bear the burden alone**.
- Any package must be **durable, enforceable, and numerically sufficient**.
- A **prolonged strike** would be value-destructive and should be avoided if possible.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council
Your red lines — **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full job guarantees through 2030** — are understandable politically, but they are **not acceptable** if they prevent a package that restores margins and competitiveness.

You still have not shown that voluntary exits, working-time changes, redeployment, and wage moderation can **fully replace** the fixed-cost and capacity reduction management says is needed. If you can demonstrate equivalence on a **risk-adjusted recurring savings** basis, then fine. But so far that has not been proven.

What we will not support is an agreement that:
- preserves excess capacity,
- protects every legacy guarantee,
- and still expects shareholders to absorb structurally weak returns.

That is not a turnaround. It is deferred decline.

### To the Union
We support burden-sharing, but not if it is limited to measures that are too temporary or too reversible to matter.

If you want to avoid closures, then the package must produce **hard, permanent savings**:
- meaningful capacity consolidation,
- measurable headcount reduction,
- wage/bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- and enforceable duration.

If those savings do not match the closure path on a **risk-adjusted 5-year basis**, then the alternative is not equivalent.

### To Management
You are right that the current cost base is too high. But you still need to prove, not just assert, that closures or forced redundancies are necessary.

We want the hard numbers:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. by site and function,
3. implementation costs,
4. strike and delay risk,
5. reversal risk,
6. and the 5-year risk-adjusted comparison versus a negotiated hybrid.

If a closure-led route is superior after those adjustments, say so clearly. If a binding hybrid can deliver the same value outcome, then that is the better path. But do not ask investors to endorse closures on faith.

### To the State Government
We understand your concern about irreversible regional damage. That is legitimate. But protecting the industrial base means protecting a **viable** industrial base, not freezing an uncompetitive structure.

We can support a negotiated path that preserves jobs and sites **if** it actually restores competitiveness. We cannot support a “protect everything” outcome if it leaves the company weak and underinvested.

### To the Controlling Family
You are right that value destruction from strikes, trust loss, and political deadlock matters. That is exactly why we do **not** want a crude closure-first strategy unless it is clearly superior on a risk-adjusted basis.

But the reverse is also true: preserving every site and guarantee while leaving the cost base broken is also value-destructive. Long-horizon ownership does not justify structural drift.

## Our position
We are open to a **hybrid settlement** — but only if it is genuinely equivalent on economics and enforceability.

That means:
- real capacity consolidation,
- measurable labor contributions,
- binding investment commitments,
- clear targets by site and year,
- and enforcement if savings are missed.

What we are **not** open to:
- a cosmetic no-closure deal,
- a package that postpones the problem,
- or a settlement that preserves structural uncompetitiveness.

## Bottom line
- **Yes** to real restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to a negotiated hybrid if it is economically sufficient.
- **No** to any settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.
- **No** to a compromise that is “socially balanced” but financially weak.

We will back the package that restores **competitive margins** and **disciplined cost reduction**. Anything else is not acceptable.
