Management, shareholders, and even the state are still trying to turn this into a debate about “necessary pain.” Let me be equally direct: **we will not negotiate away plant closures or compulsory redundancies**. That line remains unchanged.

### Where I agree
I agree with the repeated statements that the company has a real competitiveness problem. I also agree that a cosmetic solution would be pointless. If there is a sustainable turnaround, it must be real, durable, and investable.

I also agree that the burden cannot fall only on one side. The workforce is prepared to contribute, but only within a framework that protects jobs and sites.

### Where I disagree
I strongly reject the idea that “real restructuring” necessarily means closure logic or forced job destruction. That is a management choice, not an economic law.

You keep saying capacity must be reduced. Fine — then reduce it through **working-time adjustments, voluntary instruments, internal reallocation, retraining, and product-mix changes**, not by shutting German plants or threatening thousands with involuntary unemployment.

To management and investors: you have not shown that closures are the only economically credible path. You have asserted it. That is not enough when the consequences are irreversible.

To the family and the state: I acknowledge your concern for viability, but viability is not achieved by sacrificing the workforce as the easy adjustment reserve. If you want long-term value, then you need long-term industrial commitment.

### What is non-negotiable for us
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job guarantees through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**
- **Any capacity reduction only through negotiated, reversible, socially fair measures**

### What we can discuss
If management brings a serious package, we can discuss:
- working-time accounts and flexibility,
- voluntary early retirement or severance only on a voluntary basis,
- internal transfers and qualification,
- temporary wage or bonus moderation if fully paired with binding safeguards,
- productivity improvements,
- investment commitments into future products and technologies at German sites,
- and a credible plan to use existing plants more efficiently.

But I will be very clear: **a “contribution” from labor is not a blank cheque for management to close plants later**. We have seen how quickly guarantees get broken once workers have conceded. That trust is not there.

### On the idea of “equivalent savings”
If management claims that closures are unavoidable, then it must prove:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. why negotiated alternatives cannot close it,
3. and why the burden cannot be shared differently.

Until then, the employee side will treat closure proposals as a political choice, not a necessity.

### Our bottom line
We are ready for a negotiated turnaround. We are **not** ready for a restructuring that destroys jobs, tears up guarantees, and weakens co-determination. If the company wants industrial peace, it must offer:
- hard site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- real investment,
- and a future for all German plants.

Absent that, you should assume continued resistance — including mobilisation, warning strikes, and public pressure. We prefer an agreement, but not at the price of accepting plant closures by another name.
