As dispersed institutional shareholders, our lens is straightforward: **we need a credible, durable path back to competitive margins and a stronger return on sales**. We are not here to defend management tactics, labor orthodoxy, or regional politics. We care about the economics.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, and the state on one essential point: **the current cost base is not acceptable**. If the company preserves excess capacity and structurally high labor costs, then value destruction continues. That would be unacceptable for shareholders.

We also agree with the employee side on one practical point: **a prolonged strike or a disorderly confrontation would destroy value**. Delays, uncertainty, and operational disruption are costly, and the share price will reflect that quickly.

### Where we disagree with the employee side
We do **not** accept the position that every German plant must remain open, that compulsory redundancies are off the table in all circumstances, and that job guarantees through 2030 should stand regardless of economics.

That is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand to freeze the cost structure while the market moves on. If that is the outcome, margins remain structurally impaired and capital is trapped in an underperforming asset base. We would not support that.

### Where we disagree with management
At the same time, we do **not** support a simplistic “closures and layoffs first” approach if it creates unnecessary governance conflict, strike risk, and reputational damage while failing to secure a stable long-term settlement.

A cosmetic or purely coercive solution is just as bad for shareholders as no solution at all. If management cannot get a deal that is durable, the supposed savings may be eaten up by labor unrest, lost output, and political backlash.

### Our bottom line
We are prepared to support **hard restructuring** only if it is:
- **economically credible**,
- **durable**,
- and **sufficient to restore competitiveness**.

That means we need to see:
1. **Real capacity reduction**, not just temporary measures.
2. **Material labor-cost improvement**, not symbolic concessions.
3. **A clear path to margin recovery** with hard numbers and timing.
4. **Deal certainty**, because uncertainty itself is a cost.

### On alternatives to closures
We are open to alternatives to plant closures **only if they genuinely replicate the economic effect**. That could include:
- capacity consolidation,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exit programs,
- productivity improvements,
- wage and benefit concessions,
- and removal of legacy guarantees.

But if those tools do not deliver equivalent and lasting savings, then they are not substitutes — they are postponements.

### What would concern us
We would be firmly against:
- a settlement that preserves structural overcapacity,
- a package that protects every legacy guarantee but leaves margins weak,
- or a prolonged conflict that burns cash while management and labor posture.

### Our message to the parties
- **To management:** bring a precise restructuring case with quantified savings and timing. We need evidence, not rhetoric.
- **To labor:** if you want support for a negotiated solution, it must materially change the economics.
- **To the family and state:** value preservation requires realism, not symbolic blocking of difficult decisions.

So our position is conditional but firm: **we will back the option that credibly restores competitiveness and margin discipline**. If no such package emerges, then the market will continue to discount the company — and shareholders will respond accordingly.
