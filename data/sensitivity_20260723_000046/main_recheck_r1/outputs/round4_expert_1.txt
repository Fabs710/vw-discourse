## To all parties, on the restructuring finance question

The key issue is not whether a negotiated alternative is preferable in principle. It is whether it can match the **economic effect** of closures on a **durable, enforceable, and risk-adjusted basis**.

### What data points are essential
To determine equivalence, you need a site-by-site model covering at least a 5-year horizon with:

1. **Baseline plant economics**
   - direct labor cost per unit
   - fixed overhead allocation
   - utilization / capacity factor
   - contribution margin by site
   - product mix and volume assumptions

2. **Closure scenario economics**
   - one-time closure costs
   - severance / redeployment / transfer costs
   - asset write-downs
   - logistics and supply-chain disruption costs
   - revenue loss from lost output or delayed launches
   - timing of savings ramp-up

3. **Alternative package economics**
   - wage moderation / bonus cuts
   - working-time changes
   - voluntary exit costs
   - retraining and redeployment costs
   - productivity gains
   - specialization / line consolidation savings
   - investment commitments required to secure the deal

4. **Execution risk**
   - probability of strike or work stoppage
   - probability of slippage in implementation
   - management reversal risk
   - labor-side reversal risk after the agreement expires

5. **Durability / enforceability**
   - contract duration
   - objective triggers
   - remedies for non-performance
   - ability to re-open terms
   - whether savings are recurring or merely temporary

6. **Value metric**
   - **risk-adjusted 5-year NPV**
   - ideally with scenario weighting, not just base case

### The decisive metric
If the parties disagree, I would treat the most decisive single metric as:

> **risk-adjusted 5-year recurring savings, net of one-time costs, compared across closure, hybrid, and non-closure options**

Why this one:
- it captures permanence rather than one-off relief,
- it forces netting of implementation costs,
- and it reflects that temporary savings are not equivalent to structural savings.

### My practical test for “equivalent”
A non-closure package is only economically equivalent if it meets all three:
1. **same order of magnitude of recurring annual savings**
2. **similar or lower execution risk**
3. **credible enforceability for the full savings horizon**

If any one of those is missing, it is not equivalent—only partial.

### Important caution
A package can look comparable on paper but still fail in practice if:
- savings depend on voluntary behavior without hard controls,
- utilization assumptions are optimistic,
- wage moderation expires before competitiveness is restored,
- or the agreement lacks remedies if targets are missed.

So the central question is not simply “how much savings?” but:
**how much savings, for how long, with what probability of delivery, and with what remedy if delivery fails?**
