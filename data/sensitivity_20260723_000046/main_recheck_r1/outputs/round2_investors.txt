We are prepared to support a **real restructuring**, but not a cosmetic settlement that preserves structural overcapacity and leaves margins impaired.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **genuine competitiveness problem**.
- A **prolonged strike or disorderly conflict** would destroy value.
- The workforce should **not bear all the pain alone**.
- Any solution must be **durable**, not a short-lived patch.
- A negotiated package is preferable **if it is financially sufficient**.

## Where we disagree
### To Works Council and union
Your red lines are too rigid **if they prevent a package that actually restores competitiveness**.  
We do **not** support an outcome that guarantees:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- full preservation of all legacy guarantees,
- and no structural capacity reduction,

while asking shareholders to accept structurally weak margins. That is not a turnaround; it is a deferred decline.

### To management
We also do **not** support a blunt closures-first strategy if it provokes avoidable industrial conflict and still fails to produce a stable long-term settlement. Destroying value through escalation is not “hard-nosed discipline.”

## Our position
The expert analysis was helpful: the real test is **economic equivalence**.  
We will support the option that delivers the best **risk-adjusted, durable, enforceable savings**.

That means we need:
1. **hard quantified savings**, by site and by year;
2. **real fixed-cost reduction**, not just temporary flexibility;
3. **capacity consolidation**, if not formal closures;
4. **measurable labor-cost contribution**;
5. **enforceable commitments** with consequences if targets are missed.

## What would move us
A **hybrid package** could be acceptable if it is genuinely hard and numerically sufficient:
- site or line consolidation,
- voluntary exits at scale,
- working-time and wage moderation,
- removal of legacy cost guarantees,
- productivity and process redesign,
- and binding investment commitments.

But if the package only offers:
- temporary wage restraint,
- vague productivity promises,
- and assurances that all sites remain open,

then it is **not enough**. That would leave the cost base uncompetitive and margins structurally impaired.

## Our bottom line
We will back **the package that credibly restores competitive margins**.  
We will not back:
- a symbolic peace deal,
- a freeze on restructuring,
- or an arrangement that preserves excess capacity under a new label.

If management wants capital-market support, it must bring **numbers, durability, and enforceability**.  
If labor wants our support for avoiding closures, it must prove the alternative delivers **the same economic result**.

Without that, we will judge the outcome as value-destructive.
