I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all parties on the diagnosis that this company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**. Pretending otherwise would be irresponsible. The current cost base, market pressure, and capacity imbalance cannot simply be wished away.

I also agree that a **cosmetic compromise is unacceptable**. If the settlement does not materially improve the economics of the company, then it is not a turnaround.

And I agree with the expert framing that the real question is **equivalence**: whether a non-closure or hybrid package can deliver **durable, enforceable, risk-adjusted savings**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that German plant closures or compulsory redundancies should remain the default answer.

That is still being treated too casually by management, investors, and even the controlling side. For me, that is not an acceptable starting point. We are not talking about an abstract asset shuffle; we are talking about irreversible damage to regional employment, industrial know-how, and the company’s role as a regional anchor.

If the company wants my support, it must show that it has genuinely exhausted a negotiated path that protects jobs and sites.

## To management
You say closures may be necessary if no equivalent alternative exists. Fine. But “may be necessary” is not the same as “proven necessary.”

I want the numbers, clearly and site by site:
- exact savings gap,
- by plant and function,
- one-time closure costs,
- execution delay,
- strike risk,
- and a 5-year risk-adjusted comparison with a binding alternative package.

Until that is on the table, I will not treat closures as justified.

And I will say this plainly: a restructuring that “works” only by gutting regional employment is not one I can support.

## To the works council and union
I agree with you on the essentials:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- and no one-sided burden shifting onto workers.

Those are not just bargaining positions for me; they are core conditions for keeping the company anchored in the region.

That said, I also need to be honest: simply saying “no” is not enough if the company truly needs structural savings. If a negotiated package is to protect sites, it must also be economically credible. That means real measures:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage and bonus moderation,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity gains,
- specialization across sites,
- and capacity adjustment short of closure.

I can support that direction. I cannot support “no closures” as a slogan with no economic substance behind it.

## To the controlling family and investors
I understand your concern about long-term value and structural uncompetitiveness. I share the concern that the company cannot remain permanently above target on cost.

But I reject any approach that treats labor and regional employment as the easy adjustment variable while preserving strategic options for everyone else.

A deal that destroys trust with the workforce, weakens the German footprint, and creates prolonged conflict may be value-destructive even if it looks clean in a spreadsheet.

If you want my support, you need to accept that long-term value includes:
- social stability,
- industrial trust,
- regional legitimacy,
- and a workforce that remains committed to the company.

## To the state government
I agree with your position that we need **real restructuring, not a cosmetic patch**. But that must not become a pretext for irreversible site closures.

Your role as anchor shareholder is not just to “soften” the impact of management’s plan. It is to prevent the regional base from being hollowed out.

So I would expect you to use your blocking role to insist on a package that:
- preserves German sites wherever possible,
- avoids mass layoffs,
- maintains co-determination,
- and ties labor contributions to binding investment commitments.

If the state does not defend the regional base, nobody else will.

## On the expert test
The expert is right that the comparison must be hard-headed. I would therefore support an independent comparison, but only if it is genuinely neutral and not designed to certify closures after the fact.

[SUMMON: independent restructuring finance specialist to compare closure, hybrid, and no-closure packages by site over 5 years, including risk-adjusted recurring savings, one-time costs, strike risk, reversal risk, and value impact on regional employment.]

That analysis must not only measure EBITDA or cash savings. It must also make clear what the company loses if it destroys trust, skills, and regional legitimacy. Those are not soft factors; they are part of long-term viability.

## My position
I remain open to a **hard negotiated hybrid solution** if, and only if, it genuinely protects:
- German jobs,
- German sites,
- co-determination,
- and the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.

That means:
- no automatic closures,
- no forced redundancies as a first resort,
- no sidelining of the state,
- and no “restructuring” that simply shifts the burden downward.

I can support structural change. I cannot support regional depletion.

## What would move me
A credible package would need to show:
1. a verified savings gap,
2. a non-closure or hybrid response that is economically sufficient,
3. binding investment commitments,
4. job and site protection that is meaningful, not symbolic,
5. and enforceable mechanisms so the deal cannot be undone at the first convenient moment.

## Bottom line
- **Yes** to real restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to a negotiated solution if it protects jobs and sites.
- **No** to closures as the default answer.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies as a bargaining shortcut.
- **No** to any outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

If the table wants my support, it must bring a solution that is **numerically credible, socially fair, and regionally responsible**. Otherwise, I will not move.
