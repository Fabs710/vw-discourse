## Core impasse

The deadlock is centered on **plant closures and forced redundancies**.

More specifically, the parties are split on three linked questions:

1. **Whether closures are necessary at all**
   - Management, investors, and the controlling family treat closures as a legitimate option if the numbers require it.
   - Works council, union, and regional state government reject closures as a default or acceptable outcome.

2. **Who must prove what**
   - Labor-side stakeholders say management must prove site-by-site necessity before any closure can be discussed.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders say labor must prove that a no-closure package can match the economic result.

3. **What counts as an acceptable restructuring**
   - Labor-side stakeholders want restructuring only within a protected framework: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, job security, co-determination, and investment commitments.
   - Management and investors want restructuring that can credibly reduce fixed cost and capacity, even if that requires closures or deeper headcount cuts.

The result is a **burden-of-proof stalemate**: each side is asking the other to move first, while each fears that moving first would surrender leverage.

---

## Possible compromise positions

### 1) Time-limited closure moratorium with binding review trigger
**Facilitator proposal:**  
A temporary commitment not to close German plants for a defined period, paired with an independent, site-by-site review and pre-agreed trigger conditions.

**Core elements:**
- No German plant closures for 12–24 months.
- Independent restructuring model by site and year.
- Predefined performance thresholds:
  - savings gap,
  - utilization,
  - product allocation,
  - margin improvement.
- If thresholds are missed, closures are not automatic, but the parties must re-open negotiations on a defined escalation path.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**
- Gives labor protection against immediate irreversible action.
- Gives management and owners a serious test of whether alternatives can work.
- Preserves closure as a contingent outcome rather than a first move.

---

### 2) Hybrid package with protected sites plus capacity consolidation
**Facilitator proposal:**  
A package that rules out blanket closures but allows negotiated site specialization, line transfers, and limited consolidation, combined with measurable labor concessions.

**Core elements:**
- No forced redundancies.
- No wholesale closures.
- Some sites may lose product lines, but remain operational.
- Labor contributions may include:
  - voluntary exits,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary wage/bonus moderation,
  - retraining,
  - redeployment.
- Management commits to binding investment and product allocation for the protected sites.
- Savings and employment effects are reviewed against agreed targets.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**
- Offers real structural change without immediate plant destruction.
- Provides management with recurring savings and capacity reduction.
- Gives labor a face-saving distinction between “shutdown” and “restructuring through specialization.”

---

### 3) Burden-sharing pact with enforceable guarantees and re-opener clauses
**Facilitator proposal:**  
A multi-year agreement where labor contributes to cost relief only if management, owners, and the state all accept binding constraints and enforcement mechanisms.

**Core elements:**
- Job security commitment through a defined date.
- No compulsory redundancies during the agreement term.
- Investment and product commitments for German sites.
- Temporary labor concessions with expiration dates.
- If the company misses agreed milestones, the deal automatically re-opens for renegotiation.
- If milestones are met, closure options remain suspended.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**
- Converts abstract trust into enforceable commitments.
- Allows temporary relief without making concessions indefinite.
- Makes labor participation conditional on delivery by management and shareholders.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
**Question:**  
What exact site-by-site savings gap remains after accounting for voluntary exits, working-time measures, redeployment, specialization, and productivity gains, and what evidence shows those measures cannot close the gap within the agreed horizon?

### To the Works Council
**Question:**  
If an independent model showed that a no-closure package produces the same 5-year risk-adjusted savings with binding enforceability, would you accept site specialization or product-line transfers short of closure, and if not, what additional safeguards would be required?

### To the Industrial Union
**Question:**  
Which specific labor measures are you prepared to trade for a binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff agreement, and what minimum enforcement mechanism would make those concessions acceptable to the workforce?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Will you use your blocking position to support a time-limited moratorium on closures paired with an independent review, or do you require a firmer unconditional no-closure commitment before you will intervene?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
If closure and non-closure scenarios come out close on risk-adjusted value, what threshold of execution risk, trust loss, or political damage would lead you to favor a hybrid or protected-site solution over plant closures?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum recurring savings, duration, and enforceability standard would make you support a hybrid settlement instead of closure-led restructuring, and would you accept a temporary no-closure moratorium if review triggers are built in?
