## Core impasse

The main blockage is **not over whether restructuring is needed**. Most parties accept that it is.

The impasse is over **what counts as an acceptable “equivalent” restructuring path**:

- **Management / investors / family** want **hard, durable, risk-adjusted savings**, and they see closures as the clearest way to get them.
- **Works Council / union** reject closures and compulsory redundancies as the price of any deal, and only accept labor contributions inside a no-closure, no-forced-layoff framework.
- **State government** wants to avoid irreversible regional damage but also does not want a cosmetic agreement.
- The **expert** has narrowed the dispute to a technical question: whether a non-closure or hybrid package can match closure economics on a **recurring, enforceable, risk-adjusted basis**.

So the core impasse is:

> **Whether the required savings can be achieved without closures, and if so, under what binding conditions.**

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified “equivalence test” first, decision second
**Structure**
- Commission a jointly agreed, independent model comparing:
  1. closure-led restructuring,
  2. hybrid restructuring,
  3. no-closure restructuring.
- Compare by site and year:
  - gross savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - execution risk,
  - strike risk,
  - reversal risk,
  - and 5-year risk-adjusted NPV.

**Purpose**
- This does not settle the policy dispute.
- It creates a shared factual basis for deciding whether a non-closure package is truly equivalent.

**Why it may bridge**
- Management gets a hard savings test.
- Labor gets a factual test before any closure decision.
- State and owners get a risk-adjusted basis for governance decisions.

---

### Proposal 2: Time-limited non-closure trial package with trigger points
**Structure**
- Agree a fixed-term package, e.g. 24–36 months, with:
  - voluntary exits,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - bonus moderation,
  - redeployment,
  - specialization,
  - productivity measures,
  - and investment commitments.
- Include **predefined trigger points**:
  - if savings targets are missed,
  - if volume/cost milestones are not met,
  - or if investment is not delivered,
  then parties return automatically to a pre-agreed escalation table.

**Purpose**
- Gives labor a non-closure path.
- Gives management and owners a built-in enforcement mechanism.
- Avoids an immediate closure decision while preserving consequences if results fail.

**Why it may bridge**
- It converts abstract trust into measurable performance.
- It reduces the “closure as default” dispute by replacing it with a test period.

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid footprint adjustment with protected core sites
**Structure**
- Identify a limited set of **protected core German sites/functions** with binding guarantees.
- Allow capacity reduction elsewhere through:
  - specialization,
  - consolidation of lines,
  - redeployment,
  - and voluntary exits.
- Combine this with:
  - targeted investment commitments,
  - wage/bonus moderation,
  - and a phased fixed-cost reduction plan.

**Purpose**
- Preserves an industrial base and co-determination.
- Gives management real structural savings.
- Avoids treating every site identically.

**Why it may bridge**
- It is more acceptable to labor than broad closures.
- It may satisfy owners/investors if the savings are real and site-specific.
- It gives the state a defensible regional protection framework.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What is the minimum annual recurring savings gap, by site and year, that you must close to avoid failure of the restructuring, and what evidence shows that only closures—not a binding hybrid package—can close it?**

### To the Works Council
**What specific multi-year, quantified savings package would you accept as a genuine substitute for closures, and what enforcement mechanism would make that commitment credible to the other parties?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which combination of labor measures—voluntary exits, working-time changes, wage moderation, productivity gains, specialization—would you treat as sufficient if it were legally binding and measured against a fixed savings target?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete regional safeguards and governance conditions would you require before supporting a non-closure or hybrid package that still delivers structural cost reduction?**

### To the Controlling Family
**What level of execution risk, strike risk, and governance delay would make a closure-led path less attractive than a negotiated hybrid, from a long-term ownership perspective?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum 5-year risk-adjusted NPV improvement would you require to support a non-closure package, even if it involves slower or more complex implementation?**

### To the Expert
**What single threshold—on recurring savings, enforcement, or durability—would you use to classify a non-closure package as genuinely equivalent to closure-led restructuring?**
