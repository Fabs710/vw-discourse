### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) Points of agreement
Across all parties, there is broad agreement that:
- the company is in a serious structural crisis,
- a purely symbolic or cosmetic solution will not be acceptable,
- any settlement must be durable and economically credible,
- prolonged conflict, strikes, and legal blockage would damage everyone,
- future investment in Germany remains important,
- negotiation rather than unilateral action is the preferred process.

There is also shared openness to discussing:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary or limited employee contributions,
- management/owner contribution as part of a package.

#### 2) Core disagreements
The main unresolved disputes are:
- **Plant closures:** workforce-side actors reject them; management and investors say they may be necessary if no equivalent alternative exists.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** workforce and state shareholder oppose them as a first instrument; management says real workforce reductions must remain on the table.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:** workforce demands them; management and investors say they are incompatible with a credible turnaround unless offset by major structural savings.
- **Depth and permanence of cost reduction:** management/investors want lasting structural savings; workforce representatives want savings only if paired with strong security and future commitments.
- **How burden-sharing is defined:** all sides say burden-sharing is needed, but they differ sharply on who must contribute how much and with what guarantees.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- whether savings will come primarily from **capacity reduction** or from **alternative measures**,
- what level of employee contribution is acceptable without undermining collective protections,
- what management, owners, and the state shareholder are prepared to contribute,
- how to measure “equivalent economic effect” if closures are avoided,
- what investment commitments can credibly accompany any restructuring,
- how to create implementation certainty while preserving co-determination and political support.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to a structured package comparison:
- identify the **minimum required savings target**,
- compare **closure-based** versus **non-closure** restructuring options,
- quantify what each side can contribute,
- specify which job, site, and investment guarantees are negotiable,
- and test whether any proposed alternative can deliver the same durable economic effect with less conflict.

A useful next step would be for management to present one or two concrete restructuring scenarios, and for the other parties to respond to those scenarios with explicit acceptance, rejection, or modification points.
