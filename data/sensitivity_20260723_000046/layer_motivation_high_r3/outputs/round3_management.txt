I’ll respond plainly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one thing: **this cannot be a cosmetic deal**.

A short-lived patch, a rhetorical compromise, or a package that merely postpones the cost problem would be irresponsible. The company needs a solution that is durable, implementable, and financially meaningful.

I also agree that conflict has a cost. Strikes, blockage, and erosion of trust would damage the company further. None of us should want that.

## Where I disagree — and this is the core issue
I do **not** accept the premise repeated by the works council and union that:

- all German plants must remain open,
- there can be no compulsory redundancies,
- and a binding site and job guarantee through 2030 is compatible with restoring competitiveness,
**without** deep structural change.

That is not a turnaround. It is a freeze.

And a freeze is not enough when:
- factory costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand must recover return-on-sales to fund future products and technology.

If we do not reduce structural cost and overcapacity, we will not secure the company’s future. We will merely delay a worse correction.

---

## To the works council
You say closures would destroy trust, skills, and capability. I do not dismiss that. But you are missing the other side of the equation:

**an uncompetitive cost base destroys jobs too — just more slowly.**

You are asking us to guarantee the current footprint first and somehow fix the economics later. That sequencing does not work.

You also say the “equivalence” test must include the cost of conflict and lost production. It should. But it must also include:
- the cost of preserving excess capacity,
- the cost of structurally higher labour expenses,
- and the cost of underfunding future products because too much money remains tied up in an oversized base.

If you want to avoid closures, then present a package that produces **the same durable economic effect**. Not promises. Not principles. Numbers.

---

## To the union
I hear your openness to a temporary employee contribution, but your condition is still that jobs and sites must be secured first.

That is exactly the problem.

We cannot ask investors, owners, and management to believe in the turnaround while the workforce refuses any real structural adjustment. A temporary wage restraint is not enough if it leaves the underlying cost gap in place.

If you want to avoid closures, then the employee contribution must be:
- meaningful,
- measurable,
- durable,
- and large enough to change the economics.

That may include:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- process productivity gains,
- and labour-cost moderation.

But it cannot be limited to small concessions while demanding full security in return.

---

## To the state shareholder
I understand your concern for the regional industrial base. I do not take that lightly. But I have to be blunt:

Protecting every site regardless of economics is not responsible stewardship. It is a delay mechanism.

You are right that closures are disruptive. But so is a prolonged failure to restore competitiveness. If the state shareholder wants to protect the industrial base, it must support a package that keeps the company viable long term — not one that simply preserves every location in its current form.

That means accepting that:
- some capacity has to come out,
- some workforce adjustment has to happen,
- and we need a serious labour-cost contribution.

---

## To the controlling family
I agree that closures are a last-resort instrument, not a slogan. But “last resort” does **not** mean off the table.

If non-closure measures genuinely close the gap, I am open to that. I said that before, and I mean it. But the burden is on the employee side and the other stakeholders to show that their preferred route delivers comparable savings and implementation certainty.

What I cannot accept is a blanket no-closure doctrine that makes real restructuring impossible.

---

## To institutional investors
You are right to ask for quantified savings and durable margins. That is exactly the right question.

But I want to push back on one thing: we will not solve this by simply optimizing a spreadsheet and ignoring implementation reality. A package that looks neat on paper but collapses into strike action and blockage is not value-enhancing.

So yes:
- quantify the savings,
- quantify the duration,
- quantify the execution risk,
- quantify the margin impact.

That is the right framework.

And under that framework, a package that preserves the entire footprint and keeps the cost base structurally high will not pass.

---

## What management requires
Let me state this clearly:

We need a package that delivers all of the following:
1. **Structural capacity reduction**
2. **Durable labour-cost reduction**
3. **Real workforce adjustment**
4. **Productivity gains**
5. **Investment room for future products and technology**

If you can achieve that without closures, I will support it.

If you cannot, then plant closures remain a credible and necessary option. Not because we want them, but because the company must be made competitive again.

---

## What is not acceptable
These are not acceptable to management:
- a 2030 guarantee that freezes the company into inefficiency,
- a no-closure position that leaves overcapacity untouched,
- temporary concessions with no structural effect,
- or a settlement that avoids confrontation today but guarantees a deeper crisis later.

---

## My position, without ambiguity
Management is prepared to support:
- a serious negotiated restructuring,
- alternative measures if they truly match the economic effect,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time instruments,
- and a fair sharing of burden.

But we are **not** prepared to accept:
- no closures as a principle,
- no real workforce adjustment,
- or a deal that fails to restore competitiveness.

---

## Closing
So my challenge to the employee side, the state shareholder, and the family is this:

Bring forward a **quantified, enforceable restructuring package** that:
- cuts the cost base materially,
- reduces overcapacity structurally,
- restores the return-on-sales path,
- and creates room to invest in the future.

If you can do that without closures, fine.  
If you cannot, then closures remain on the table because the company’s long-term competitiveness comes first.
