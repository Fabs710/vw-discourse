I will be very clear.

## My position has not changed
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No settlement that destroys the job-security framework**

Anything short of that is not a solution for the workforce; it is a managed shutdown by another name.

---

## To management
You keep repeating “structural overcapacity” as if saying it often enough makes closures acceptable. It does not.

What I hear is:
- first ask workers for pain,
- then threaten sites,
- then call that “implementation certainty.”

That is not a credible turnaround. It is pressure politics.

You say a 2030 guarantee “freezes” the company. I reject that. A binding guarantee is exactly what is needed after management has already broken trust. If you want real commitment from the workforce, you must first commit to:
- keeping all German plants open,
- avoiding forced layoffs,
- and investing in the future of those sites.

Without that, your “restructuring” is just a demand for workers to surrender before the numbers are even tested properly.

### Where I agree
I do agree that this cannot be cosmetic. I agree that the company needs durable savings. I also agree that implementation risk matters.

### Where I disagree
I disagree completely that closures are the starting point or that they become legitimate because they are called “last resort.”  
In this governance system, you do not earn a right to closures by repeating the word “competitiveness.” You would need to prove, with hard numbers, that **every non-closure alternative** has been exhausted and that the same result cannot be reached otherwise.

So far, that proof has not been given.

---

## To the union
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no forced redundancies,
- no closures,
- no dismantling of collective protections.

That alignment matters. But I want to be precise: **any temporary labor contribution must be strictly conditional**. It cannot be used to buy time for management to come back later with the same closure demands.

If there is any discussion of restraint, it must be matched by:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory layoffs,
- investment in German production,
- and protection of co-determination.

Anything less would be workers paying for strategic failure twice.

---

## To the state shareholder
You say you oppose closures as a first resort. That is right, but it is not enough.

If you really mean it, then use your blocking position to prevent any plan that puts closures on the table as an acceptable outcome. The state shareholder should not be a commentator warning about social costs after the fact. It should be an active blocker of a closure-led logic.

I want concrete support for:
- preserving all German sites,
- protecting employment,
- and ensuring restructuring does not become deindustrialization.

General statements about “balance” are too weak when jobs are on the line.

---

## To the controlling family
You talk about stewardship. Then act like a steward.

A steward does not treat long-term industrial capability as disposable once margin pressure rises. The long-term value of this company is not only in cost cutting. It is in:
- skilled labour,
- production know-how,
- trusted co-determination,
- and stable industrial roots in Germany.

If you back closures and forced redundancies, you will damage that asset irreversibly.

I agree with one part of what you say: temporary fixes are not enough. But your conclusion is wrong.  
The correct conclusion is not “therefore close plants.”  
The correct conclusion is “therefore bring forward a real alternative that preserves sites and jobs.”

---

## To institutional investors
You keep demanding “equivalence.” Fine. Then be honest about what equivalence means.

Equivalence is not just:
- euros saved on paper,
- headcount reduced,
- and a cleaner margin chart.

It must include:
- execution risk,
- strike risk,
- loss of industrial capability,
- legal deadlock,
- reputational damage,
- and the cost of permanently weakening the workforce’s commitment.

If you strip those out, you are not being disciplined. You are just preferring closures and calling that financial objectivity.

I reject that framing.

## On the expert/modeler’s “hybrid” and “phased” ideas
I note the attempt to bridge the positions. I am not interested in a compromise that smuggles closures in later through “review triggers” and “fallback mechanisms.”

Let me say this plainly:  
**A phased plan that keeps closures alive as a trigger is not acceptable to the works council.**

That is not a guarantee. That is a conditional threat.

Likewise, “preserve all sites formally while reducing them until they become hollow” is not acceptable either if it leads to:
- permanent job losses,
- degraded production,
- or a slow-motion closure.

If the company wants my engagement, then the plan must begin from a different premise:
- keep all German plants open,
- protect jobs,
- and redesign the economics without using closures as a bargaining weapon.

A real alternative.
