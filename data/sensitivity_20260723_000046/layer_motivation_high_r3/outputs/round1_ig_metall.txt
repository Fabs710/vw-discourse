We share the assessment that the company is in a serious crisis and that action is needed. But let me be equally clear: **a restructuring that shifts the full burden onto the workforce will not be accepted**.

The works council is right on the essentials:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **A binding job and site guarantee**
- **A future for production and investment in Germany**

Those are not negotiation cosmetics; they are the minimum conditions for any durable settlement.

Where I differ is only in emphasis: we are prepared to discuss **real employee contributions** if, and only if, they are part of a **fair burden-sharing package**. That can include:
- temporary wage restraint or deferred increases,
- working-time reductions,
- foregoing bonuses,
- voluntary flexibility instruments,
- a solidarity contribution tied to job security,
- and productivity measures.

But this only works if **management and owners also contribute materially**. We will not sign up to a deal where employees pay through lower wages, worse conditions, and insecure jobs while the balance sheet is cleaned up elsewhere. If there are cost gaps, then capital, management, and the ownership side must also shoulder them.

So our position is:
1. **No closures.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
4. **Yes to negotiated, temporary, and fair labour-cost contributions if they secure sites and jobs.**
5. **Yes to capacity adjustment and transformation, but not to industrial hollowing-out.**

If management wants a settlement, it should put forward a plan that protects the workforce and the German industrial base, not one that treats employees as the last reserve fund. If that is the path chosen, we are ready to negotiate seriously. If not, then members are already clear that escalation is on the table.
