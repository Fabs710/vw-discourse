Let me be equally direct: I hear the demand for security, but **a promise of “all plants stay open, no forced layoffs, no material labour-cost change” is not a viable answer to this crisis**.

We are not debating a normal adjustment. We are facing a **structural competitiveness problem**:
- German factory costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competitors are moving faster and cheaper,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we respond with a cosmetic deal that preserves every site, every cost structure, and every legacy entitlement, then we are **not solving the problem** — we are postponing it until it becomes worse.

### Where I agree
I agree on one point: **this cannot be a simple management diktat**.
A sustainable solution must be negotiated, and it must be credible for the workforce as well as for the company’s future.

I also agree that there must be a **future for investment, products, and technology in Germany**. But that future requires a cost base that can actually finance it.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that:
- all German sites must remain untouched,
- there can be no compulsory job reductions,
- and the company can still restore competitiveness without a meaningful structural adjustment.

That combination is not realistic.

And I want to be very clear: **a binding job and site guarantee through 2030, without deep and durable concessions on cost and capacity, is incompatible with the turnaround we need**.

### What management is prepared to insist on
We need a package that delivers:
1. **Structural capacity reduction**
2. **A lasting reduction in labour costs**
3. **A durable improvement in productivity**
4. **Room for investment in future products and technology**

That means we are prepared to discuss:
- plant closure or consolidation where capacity is plainly excess,
- workforce reductions that are real, not merely deferred,
- changes in working time and operating flexibility,
- and a genuine employee contribution to the cost turnaround.

But it must be **durable**, not temporary. I am not interested in a short-term wage bridge that leaves the company still uncompetitive in two years.

### On fairness
I do not dismiss the burden on employees. But fairness also means this:
- we cannot ask shareholders and the company to carry an uncompetitive structure forever,
- we cannot keep all the old guarantees if the market no longer supports them,
- and we cannot protect every legacy site at the expense of the company’s long-term survival.

If the workforce side wants to avoid closures, then it must put forward **equivalent structural savings**. That means not just symbolism, but real and measurable reductions in cost and capacity.

### Bottom line
So my position is:
- **No to a cosmetic peace deal**
- **No to preserving the status quo**
- **Yes to negotiation**
- **Yes to a durable restructuring**
- **Yes to alternative measures only if they are large enough to match the scale of the crisis**

If you want to avoid closures, show me a package that delivers the same economic effect. If not, then management will continue to argue that closures and deeper cuts remain necessary.
