## Core impasse
There are two incompatible decision logics on the table:

1. **Employee-side logic**
   - Any credible settlement must start from:
     - no German plant closures,
     - no compulsory redundancies,
     - no weakening of co-determination,
     - no bargaining with closure threats.
   - Concessions are only acceptable if they are matched by binding guarantees.

2. **Management / owner / investor logic**
   - Any credible settlement must deliver:
     - durable structural savings,
     - real capacity reduction,
     - implementation certainty,
     - and enough flexibility to adjust the footprint if non-closure measures do not close the gap.
   - They will not pre-commit to a no-closure structure before the economics are proven.

So the deadlock is not just over “how much sacrifice,” but over **what must come first: guarantees or savings proof**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure standstill with quantified test phase
A possible bridge would be a **defined standstill period** in which:
- no plant closures are implemented,
- no compulsory redundancies are triggered,
- and the company tests a fully quantified non-closure package.

In return, employee-side measures would be:
- temporary labor contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity and overhead measures.

Conditions:
- the package must have explicit savings targets,
- clear milestones,
- and an agreed independent verification point at a fixed date.

If the agreed savings are achieved, the no-closure framework continues. If not, the parties revisit the structure on the basis of the verified gap.

---

### Proposal 2: Site protection plus capacity reduction through internal measures only
A second compromise position would be:
- preserve all German sites formally for a defined period,
- but allow significant capacity reduction through:
  - shift cuts,
  - product reassignment,
  - line consolidation,
  - reduced overtime,
  - voluntary attrition,
  - and redeployment.

This would give management real cost reduction tools while respecting the works council’s core red line against closures.

To make it credible, the package would need:
- binding investment commitments,
- management/owner contribution,
- and a clause preventing later use of “hollowing out” as a disguised closure path.

---

### Proposal 3: Phased restructuring with a jointly governed review gate
A third bridge would be a **phased plan**:
- Phase 1: immediate non-closure measures and labor contributions
- Phase 2: joint review of actual savings, execution risk, and market outlook
- Phase 3: only if the verified gap remains, a jointly negotiated second-stage adjustment

To reduce mistrust, the review gate could be:
- jointly governed,
- based on pre-agreed metrics,
- and supported by an independent expert.

This does not eliminate disagreement, but it replaces open-ended threat with a structured decision process.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the minimum quantified savings package that would allow you to suspend closure planning for a defined period, and which elements of that package would you accept as binding rather than merely aspirational?**

### To the works council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies were formally suspended for a fixed period, what specific, measurable employee-side contributions would you be prepared to authorize to close the cost gap?**

### To the union
**Which labor concessions can you support temporarily without undermining the collective framework, and what hard guarantees would you require in exchange?**

### To the state shareholder
**How far are you prepared to use your blocking position: only to prevent immediate closures, or also to require a structured no-closure test phase before any site action is considered?**

### To the controlling family
**Would you support a time-limited standstill on closures if an independent model shows the non-closure package could deliver most of the required savings with lower conflict risk?**

### To institutional investors
**What threshold of risk-adjusted savings would make you regard a non-closure package as financially equivalent to closures in this governance setting?**

### To the expert/modeler
**What exact metrics should trigger a transition from the standstill phase to a second-stage restructuring review, so that “temporary” does not become indefinite delay?**
