### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties reject a merely cosmetic or short-lived settlement.
- There is broad agreement that the company faces a real structural competitiveness problem.
- There is also broad agreement that any solution must be durable and implementable.
- Most sides accept that some form of burden-sharing will be needed.
- Several measures are viewed as potentially usable in a package:
  - working-time instruments
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - productivity/process improvements
  - temporary labor-cost moderation
  - management and owner contributions
  - investment commitments in Germany

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works council, union, and state shareholder reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table as a last resort or necessary option.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee representatives and the state shareholder oppose them as the default tool; management says real workforce adjustment must remain possible.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees; management and capital side say such guarantees may block needed restructuring.
- **Extent of capacity reduction:**  
  All agree capacity must be addressed, but disagree on whether this can be achieved without closures and how much reduction is enough.
- **Who bears the adjustment burden:**  
  Employee side insists on meaningful management/owner contribution and protection of labor rights; management and investors want stronger labor and capacity concessions.
- **Meaning of “equivalent economic effect”:**  
  Investors and management want quantified, durable savings; employee side says the comparison must also include conflict costs, legal risk, and loss of industrial capability.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve meaningful cost reduction without triggering closures, layoffs, or prolonged conflict.
- What exact mix of labor, management, owner, and state-side contributions would be sufficient and acceptable.
- Whether non-closure measures can match the economic impact of closure-based restructuring.
- How to balance short-term savings against long-term industrial presence, skills retention, and investment capacity.
- What level of implementation risk is tolerable if the alternative is socially and politically destabilizing.
- Whether any binding site/job guarantee can coexist with deep restructuring requirements.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Ask each side to submit a **quantified package comparison**:
  - required savings
  - duration of effect
  - execution risk
  - impact on jobs, sites, and investment
- Separate the discussion into two questions:
  1. What savings are economically necessary?
  2. Through which mix of measures can those savings be achieved with the least conflict and highest durability?
- Require explicit answers on:
  - management and owner contributions
  - labor-cost contribution limits
  - capacity reduction options without closures
  - conditions under which, if any, closures become unavoidable
- Keep the focus on **verifiable trade-offs**, not repeated position statements.
