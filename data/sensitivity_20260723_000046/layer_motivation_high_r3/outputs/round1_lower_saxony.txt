I share the assessment that this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. If the company’s cost base is structurally too high, then pretending otherwise would damage the company, the sites, and ultimately the region.

But I also need to be equally clear: **German plant closures and a hollowing-out of domestic employment are not an acceptable default solution**. As a regional government and anchor shareholder, we cannot support a restructuring that shifts the burden almost entirely onto workers and turns a historic industrial base into a casualty of short-term cost logic.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on three points:
- the company needs a **credible turnaround**,
- savings must be **durable**, not symbolic,
- and future investment in technology and products in Germany depends on a viable economic base.

I also agree with the works council and union that **any solution must be negotiated**, socially fair, and tied to a real future for the plants and workforce.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the framing that closures are the necessary starting point. Once you start from plant closures, you make the entire process politically and socially destructive before alternatives are even properly tested.

And I do **not** accept a package in which:
- jobs are cut deeply,
- sites are closed or permanently weakened,
- and employees alone carry the adjustment,
while capital and management contribution remain comparatively modest.

That would not be a fair restructuring. It would be an imposed contraction.

### What I expect from management
If management wants support from the state shareholder, it must bring forward a package that includes:
1. **Protection of existing German sites wherever economically possible**
2. **No forced redundancies as the first instrument**
3. **A credible job and site perspective through 2030**
4. **Real burden-sharing from management, owners, and workforce**
5. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technology in Germany**

Alternative measures absolutely have to be on the table:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and temporary employee contributions.

But those measures must be part of a settlement that **preserves the industrial core**. If management’s “equivalent savings” require permanent closures and large-scale job destruction, then I would question whether that is really equivalent or simply politically easier for management than a negotiated solution.

### On fairness and long-term responsibility
Fairness here means more than balancing a spreadsheet. It means:
- preserving a regional industrial anchor,
- protecting skilled employment,
- keeping co-determination meaningful,
- and ensuring the company remains a long-term employer in Germany.

The state shareholder will not back a solution that sidelines the workforce or strips the regional economy for parts. We are prepared to support a tough restructuring — but only one that is socially responsible, strategically credible, and respectful of the company’s broader obligations.

### Bottom line
So my position is:
- **No automatic acceptance of plant closures**
- **No forced layoffs as the first or preferred instrument**
- **Yes to durable restructuring**
- **Yes to negotiated savings and flexibility**
- **Yes to a fair contribution from all sides**
- **No to any plan that destroys regional employment and weakens co-determination**

If management wants our support, it should present a package that protects the German footprint as far as possible and proves that competitiveness can be restored without gutting the company’s social foundation.
