## Core impasse
There are **two incompatible sequencing logics**:

1. **Employee-side logic**  
   Savings can be discussed only if there are **binding guarantees first**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, protection of sites/jobs, then limited burden-sharing.

2. **Management/investor logic**  
   Binding guarantees are seen as **too restrictive before the savings gap is closed**; they want a package that proves structural cost reduction first, with closures remaining available if needed.

So the deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over:
- **whether closures are ruled out up front**, and
- **what level of quantified non-closure savings is sufficient to replace them**.

There is also a second, related impasse:
- the employee/state side wants **maximum social protection with real guarantees**,
- while management/investors want **maximum economic certainty with enforceable restructuring**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional triggers
A first compromise could be a **two-stage package**:

- **Stage 1:** immediate non-closure measures  
  - voluntary exits  
  - working-time flexibility  
  - temporary wage/bonus moderation  
  - management/owner contribution  
  - overhead cuts  
  - investment commitments in Germany

- **Stage 2:** pre-agreed contingency step  
  - if quantified savings or utilization milestones are missed by a fixed date, a second round opens, including site consolidation or closure options under defined governance rules

This would not settle the closure question permanently, but it could bridge the need for both:
- employee-side reassurance that closures are **not the first move**, and
- management/investor assurance that there is **a fallback if the numbers do not materialize**.

---

### 2) Site protection with capacity reduction instead of outright closure
A second compromise could preserve all German sites formally, while still allowing **material footprint reduction** through:

- shift elimination
- line consolidation
- partial site specialization
- inter-site transfer of production
- internal redeployment
- temporary downtime or reduced capacity

This would aim to deliver the **economic effect of closure** in part, while avoiding the symbolic and political rupture of an outright shutdown.

It could be structured as:
- no site closure in phase 1,
- but clear minimum output, headcount, and cost targets by site,
- with automatic review if targets are missed.

---

### 3) Reciprocal guarantees for burden-sharing
A third compromise could be a **balanced exchange package**:

- employee-side: temporary wage restraint, flexibility, voluntary exits
- management/owners: investment commitments, no dividend/capital extraction for a defined period, executive restraint
- state shareholder/family: formal support for site continuity through the review period
- company: binding no-forced-redundancy period for a defined horizon, but not a permanent guarantee

This does **not** eliminate all risk for either side, but it may create a credible exchange:  
**labor contributes now, and capital/governance commits to a protected transition period.**

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**What minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction would make you willing to exclude closures for a defined period, and can you table that threshold now?**

### To the works council
**Would you accept a phased package with binding review triggers, if it preserved all German sites for an initial period but allowed a later structural review if the savings target is missed?**

### To the union
**What temporary labor contribution would you authorize, and for how long, if it were matched by enforceable job and site protections plus management/owner contributions?**

### To the state shareholder
**Will you use your blocking position to support a staged non-closure package, provided it contains hard milestones and a formal fallback mechanism?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you commit to treating non-closure measures as the primary route for a defined period, if they are independently verified to close most of the cost gap?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of risk-adjusted savings would you accept as equivalent to closures, and are you prepared to endorse a hybrid package if it meets that threshold?**

### To the expert/modeler
**Can you specify the savings threshold and implementation probability at which a phased non-closure package becomes financially equivalent to a closure-based scenario?**
