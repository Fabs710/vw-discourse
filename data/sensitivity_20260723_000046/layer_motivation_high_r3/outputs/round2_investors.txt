We are aligned on the diagnosis that this is a real structural crisis, not a cosmetic one. But from the capital markets’ perspective, the only acceptable outcome is one that **credibly and durably restores margins, reduces structural overcapacity, and is implementable**. Anything less is value destruction.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the cost base is too high and that the company cannot remain competitive on the current footprint.
- **The workforce-side actors are right** that a drawn-out conflict would destroy value, disrupt production, and weaken the company further.
- **The family and state shareholders are right** that a settlement has to be durable and not merely a temporary bridge.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** support a package that simply preserves all sites, freezes the cost base, and calls that a restructuring. That would be a cosmetic compromise, and it is not acceptable.

At the same time, I also do **not** think management should treat plant closures as a default opening move. In this governance structure, that is likely to produce blockage, strikes, and delay. If the company can get to equivalent savings through a negotiated package with less disruption, that is preferable — but only if the economics are truly equivalent.

### What matters to us
For institutional investors, the test is straightforward:

1. **Quantified margin improvement**
   - We need a believable path back to competitive return-on-sales.
   - Vague commitments are not enough.

2. **Durable structural savings**
   - Temporary wage restraint or one-off concessions do not solve a structural cost problem by themselves.
   - The package must reduce the cost base for years, not months.

3. **Implementation certainty**
   - A deal that cannot survive co-determination conflict, strikes, or repeated renegotiation is not value-enhancing.
   - We need something that can actually be executed.

4. **Fair burden-sharing**
   - Employees may need to contribute.
   - Management and owners may also need to contribute.
   - But the total package must be large enough to matter economically.

### On the workforce position
I understand the insistence on no forced layoffs and no closures as a political and social stance. But as shareholders, we cannot back a solution that leaves the company structurally weak. If “no closures” means “no serious capacity reduction,” then we reject it.

If, however, the employee side can deliver a package with:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- and real labour-cost moderation,

then that could be acceptable — **but only if the net effect is truly equivalent to a hard restructuring**.

### On management’s position
Management is right to insist on structural action, but it should be careful not to overplay the plant-closure card. If you make closures the headline answer before testing credible alternatives, you risk making implementation impossible and destroying value in the process.

What we need from management is not rhetoric, but a **clear comparison of scenarios**:
- what savings are required,
- what each scenario delivers,
- what the implementation risks are,
- and what deal structure best restores competitiveness.

### On the family and state shareholders
We appreciate the concern for social stability and industrial presence. But stability is not value if it simply preserves an uncompetitive earnings base. The region, the workforce, and the shareholders all lose if the company remains trapped in a structurally weak position.

### Bottom line
Our position is unchanged:

- **We will support deep restructuring, including closures, if they are genuinely necessary and no equivalent negotiated package exists.**
- **We will also support a non-closure package if it delivers comparable, durable savings with implementation certainty.**
- **We will not support a cosmetic settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive.**

If management and the other stakeholders want capital-market support, bring forward a package that clearly answers:
- how much cost comes out,
- how permanently,
- at what execution risk,
- and with what impact on margins.

That is the standard.
