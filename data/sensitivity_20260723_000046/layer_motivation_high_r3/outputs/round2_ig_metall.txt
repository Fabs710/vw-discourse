I agree with the works council on the essentials: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, and no one-sided burden shifting onto the workforce**. That remains the line.

I also agree with management, the family holding, investors, and the state shareholder on one point: **a cosmetic deal will not work**. The company needs a durable restructuring, not another short-lived postponement.

Where I disagree sharply is on the conclusion that the only credible route is closures or forced layoffs. That is not a “neutral economic necessity”; it is a choice about who pays.

### To management
You keep saying the crisis requires deep structural cuts. Fine — but **structural cuts do not automatically mean closures**. If you want labour peace and implementation certainty, then stop framing the workforce as the first and easiest cost item.

If management wants a serious negotiation, it needs to put real numbers on the table:
- what exact savings are required,
- what part comes from capacity reduction,
- what part comes from management and owner contribution,
- what part comes from labour-cost restraint,
- and what investment commitments secure the future footprint.

So far, you are asking for durability from the workforce while offering insecurity in return. That is not a settlement.

### To the works council
We are aligned in rejecting forced redundancies and closures. But I want to be clear: the union will not allow “job protection” to become a slogan that freezes every adjustment while still expecting employees to carry the burden through wage restraint.

If the company needs a contribution, then we can discuss:
- temporary wage restraint,
- deferred increases,
- bonus waivers,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- and productivity measures.

But only as part of a **package** that includes:
- a binding job and site guarantee,
- a serious investment commitment,
- and no forced layoffs.

That is fair burden-sharing. Anything else is a disguised wage cut.

### To the state shareholder and family holding
I hear your concern about value destruction, and I share it. But value is also destroyed if the company breaks its social contract with the workforce and triggers prolonged industrial conflict. A “hard” solution that cannot be implemented is not tough — it is reckless.

If you want stability, then use your governance power to push management toward a negotiated alternative with real burden-sharing from all sides. Do not treat plant closures as the default benchmark.

### To institutional investors
You keep asking for “equivalent economic effect.” That is the right question in principle, but you are too narrow if you define equivalence only by headcount and site closures. The true cost of a forced restructuring includes:
- strike risk,
- lost output,
- damaged trust,
- legal and supervisory-board blockage,
- and long-term erosion of skills and commitment.

If you want durable margins, then support a negotiated package that avoids these self-inflicted costs. A deal that is slightly less elegant on paper but implementable in reality is better than a spreadsheet victory and an industrial war.

### Our position
Let me state it clearly:

1. **No plant closures.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
4. **Yes to a fair, temporary employee contribution if jobs and sites are secured.**
5. **Yes to capacity reduction, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and voluntary exits.**
6. **Yes to binding investment in Germany.**

### What would move us
We are prepared to discuss a package if management and owners also contribute materially. That could include:
- executive restraint,
- owner-funded transition support,
- productivity and process reforms,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- and a joint commitment to preserve the German industrial base.

What will **not** move us is a demand that employees accept layoffs, insecurity, and site closures in exchange for vague promises.

### Bottom line
If management wants a settlement, it should present a **shared-sacrifice deal**: workers contribute, yes — but management, owners, and the company’s governance side must contribute too. If that is on the table, we can negotiate seriously. If not, then the union will support escalation to defend jobs, wages, and the collective framework.
