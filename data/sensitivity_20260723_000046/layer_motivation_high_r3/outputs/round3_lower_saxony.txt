I will speak plainly.

I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one crucial point: **this must be a real restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. The company has a structural competitiveness problem, and if that is not solved, jobs and investment in Germany will erode anyway.

But I **do not** accept the conclusion that plant closures in Germany, or compulsory redundancies, should be treated as the default solution. For a regional anchor shareholder with a blocking minority and a responsibility for the industrial base, that is not an acceptable starting point.

### Where I agree
- A durable solution is needed.
- The cost base must come down.
- Burden-sharing must be real.
- Conflict, strikes, and deadlock would damage the company.
- Management, owners, and labor all have to contribute.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Plant closures
I do **not** accept closures as the benchmark unless every serious alternative has been fully tested and shown to fail. The expert is right that closures can be a clean way to remove fixed costs, but that is not the same as saying they are the right answer here.

In this governance structure, closures are not just an economic lever. They are a political and social rupture. If used too early, they risk:
- industrial conflict,
- board blockage,
- damage to the regional supply chain,
- and lasting harm to trust in co-determination.

That is not a theoretical risk. It is the practical reality of this company.

#### 2) Compulsory redundancies
I oppose making forced layoffs the first instrument. If workforce adjustment is needed, then the order of precedence must be:
1. voluntary departures,
2. redeployment,
3. working-time instruments,
4. productivity and overhead reforms,
5. only then, if absolutely unavoidable and after exhaustion of alternatives, harder measures.

### On the expert assessment
The expert’s framework is useful, especially the point that the relevant question is **risk-adjusted NPV**, not slogans. I accept that.

But the key condition is this: if the non-closure package can deliver a genuinely large, durable effect — not just temporary wage restraint — then it should be preferred. That is the path that best protects regional employment and preserves the company as an industrial anchor.

What I do **not** accept is a settlement that:
- preserves all the pain for workers,
- protects investor comfort,
- and calls that “realism.”

Realism also includes the cost of losing a plant, losing skills, and losing social legitimacy.

### What would move me
A proposal could win my support if it contains all of the following:
- no plant closures as the first resort,
- no compulsory redundancies as the default,
- meaningful capacity reduction through alternatives,
- substantial management and owner contribution,
- binding investment in Germany,
- and a credible social-partnership framework that preserves co-determination.

That is the standard.

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- a closure-led restructuring presented as inevitable from the outset,
- a plan that guts regional employment,
- or a deal that sidelines the state shareholder and weakens its co-determination role.

### To management
If you want my support, show me a quantified non-closure scenario that really closes the cost gap. Not rhetorically — financially and operationally.

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are understandable, and I support the defense of jobs and sites. But for a durable settlement, there must be real economic substance, not just protection claims. If you can help produce a serious alternative package, I will stand with you in insisting that closures are not necessary.

### To the family and investors
You are right to demand economic credibility. But you are wrong if you assume that credibility requires immediate closures. In this governance system, a forced closure strategy may be the least credible path of all.

### Bottom line
I am prepared to support a tough restructuring.  
I am **not** prepared to support a restructuring that sacrifices German sites and jobs as the first answer.

If management cannot bring forward a quantified alternative that preserves the company’s regional role, then we are not yet at a settlement.
