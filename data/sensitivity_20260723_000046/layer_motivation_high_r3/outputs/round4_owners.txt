## My position remains unchanged
The family holding supports a restructuring that **materially restores competitiveness and margins**. We do **not** support a cosmetic compromise that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. But we also **do not** support a closure-first path that produces prolonged conflict, governance deadlock, and value destruction.

So the burden is still on the parties to show a **quantified, durable, implementable package** that closes the gap without needlessly destroying industrial stability.

---

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- temporary patches are not enough,
- and future investment must be funded by a stronger operating result.

Where I disagree is with the way you continue to frame the alternative:
you are still treating closures and workforce reductions as the implicit default if labor does not concede enough. In this governance setting, that is not a serious first-order solution; it is a recipe for escalation.

You have not yet shown, in a way that is decision-grade, that the non-closure route fails to deliver sufficient durable savings. That proof matters.

What I need from you is:
- a quantified savings target,
- a scenario comparison,
- a split between structural and temporary effects,
- and a realistic assessment of execution risk.

If the non-closure package cannot close the gap, say so clearly with numbers. If it can, then closures should not be the starting point.

---

## To the works council
I understand and respect the emphasis on trust, skills, and co-determination. You are right that a company cannot be turned around by destroying the workforce relationship.

But I do **not** accept the categorical claim that all German plants must remain open under all circumstances, and that no compulsory redundancies can ever be part of the answer regardless of the economics. That is too absolute.

A controlling owner cannot endorse a position that makes adjustment impossible before the facts are tested.

What I can support is:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary employee contribution,
- management and owner contribution,
- and investment commitments in Germany.

What I cannot support is a package that simply freezes the legacy footprint and calls that a turnaround.

---

## To the union
You are right that burden-sharing must be real and that employees should not carry the entire adjustment alone.

But I reject the sequencing that says job and site guarantees must come first, with the economics somehow following later. That does not work.

A guarantee that blocks all meaningful adjustment is not a solution; it is postponement.

If the union wants our support, it must help produce a package that is:
- measurable,
- durable,
- and large enough to matter economically.

That may include labor-cost moderation. It may also include capacity reduction. But it cannot be a deal that buys peace for a year or two while the structural gap remains untouched.

---

## To the state shareholder
I respect your concern for the regional industrial base, and I agree that this cannot be handled casually.

But I do not accept the idea that plant closures should be ruled out in principle. Nor do I accept a structure where the adjustment burden is pushed mainly onto management and owners while labor resistance becomes a de facto veto on reform.

You are right that closures can trigger blockage and conflict. That is precisely why they should remain a last resort, not the opening position. But last resort does not mean never.

If the numbers require deeper structural change, then the governance system must be mature enough to face that.

---

## To institutional investors
You are right to insist on durability, margin improvement, and implementation certainty. That is the correct standard.

What I push back on is any assumption that a closure-based route is automatically the most value-enhancing one. It may be, but only if non-closure alternatives fail to produce a comparable durable effect.

The family holding will judge any package by long-term enterprise value, not by the optics of toughness. A deal that looks decisive but triggers industrial war is not good governance.

So yes: show us the savings, the permanence, the execution risk, and the impact on margins. But also recognize that a technically neat solution that cannot be implemented is not a real solution.

---

## On the expert assessments
The modeling logic is useful. The right lens is risk-adjusted NPV, not slogans.

That does support one important point: closures may deliver stronger gross savings, but they also carry high one-time costs and high conflict risk. Non-closure packages are acceptable only if they deliver nearly equivalent durable effects.

That is exactly why I am not rejecting alternatives outright. I am rejecting insufficient alternatives.

If the employee side can produce a package that captures most of the economic benefit with lower disruption, I am open to it. If not, then deeper structural measures, including closures, remain on the table.

## What would move me
A credible package would need to include:
1. a quantified savings target,
2. a clear mix of structural and temporary measures,
3. meaningful capacity reduction,
4. durable labour-cost contribution,
5. management and owner contribution,
6. and a credible investment plan for Germany.

That is the minimum for serious consideration.

What would not move me:
- blanket refusal of any closures under all circumstances,
- a 2030 guarantee that prevents real restructuring,
- temporary concessions with no structural effect,
- or a compromise that merely delays the crisis.

---

## Bottom line
The family holding is prepared to support a tough restructuring, but only if it is **economically real, durable, and implementable**.

We will support:
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time instruments,
- temporary labor-cost moderation,
- management and owner contribution,
- and binding investment commitments.

We will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a freeze of the current cost structure,
- or a package that permanently impairs competitiveness.

If the employee side and management can show a quantified non-closure alternative with equivalent durable savings, I will consider it seriously. If they cannot, then plant closures remain a necessary last-resort instrument.
