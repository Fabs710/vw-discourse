## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** agreement on the need for restructuring. Almost everyone now accepts that the company has a structural competitiveness problem and that a cosmetic deal will not work.

The impasse is this:

**Who must carry the burden of proving necessity, and what instruments remain permissible before that proof is complete?**

More specifically, the sides are split on three linked issues:

1. **Evidence threshold**
   - Works council, union, and state/family voices want plant-level proof that no site-preserving option can close the gap.
   - Management and investors say the company does not need to rule out closures in advance and that keeping them available is part of serious restructuring.

2. **Red lines vs. flexibility**
   - Employee-side actors want binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy commitments.
   - Management, family holding, and investors want structural options left open if the numbers justify them.

3. **Durability of contributions**
   - Employee side is willing to discuss limited, temporary, reversible contributions.
   - Management and capital-side actors argue the fix must be durable enough to materially reduce the structural gap, not merely postpone it.

So the dispute is no longer about whether to restructure, but **whether the settlement must be pre-committed to site preservation, or whether site preservation must itself be proven economically sufficient first**.

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the most conflicting stakeholders

### Proposal 1: Two-stage settlement with a non-prejudicial evidence gate
**Structure**
- Stage 1: Immediate adoption of a short, binding framework:
  - no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
  - no plant closures during the evidence phase,
  - full access for the independent auditor,
  - no party may declare the final instrument mix in advance.
- Stage 2: After plant-level audit results, parties negotiate the permanent package based on verified residual gap.

**Why this may bridge positions**
- Gives works council, union, and state a genuine protection window.
- Gives management, family, and investors a structured path to test whether closures are actually necessary.
- Converts the current argument from rhetoric to sequenced decision-making.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-preserving first option, with a quantified fallback trigger
**Structure**
- The parties agree that the preferred route is a site-preserving package.
- A fallback consolidation route remains available only if the auditor confirms a defined residual gap after all non-closure measures.
- The trigger must be numeric and pre-agreed, for example:
  - residual margin gap above a stated threshold,
  - or remaining excess capacity above a stated utilization level,
  - or fixed-cost reduction shortfall after specified measures.

**Why this may bridge positions**
- Satisfies the employee side’s demand that closures are not the first resort.
- Satisfies management and capital-side actors that structural options are not ruled out arbitrarily.
- Forces the disagreement onto measurable criteria rather than open-ended threat language.

---

### Proposal 3: “No forced redundancies, conditional capacity adjustments” package
**Structure**
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, working-time measures, and indirect cost cuts are used first.
- If the audit shows a remaining gap, capacity adjustments may occur only through negotiated attrition, transfers, and voluntary schemes before any closure discussion.
- Any contribution by employees must be temporary, reversible, and matched by binding site and investment guarantees.

**Why this may bridge positions**
- Preserves the strongest employee red line: no forced layoffs.
- Gives management a path to real cost reduction.
- Keeps closures as a last-step issue rather than an opening demand.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What specific plant-level residual gap, after all non-closure measures, would you consider sufficient to justify keeping closures on the table?**

### To the works council
**Would you accept a time-limited no-closure / no-forced-redundancy moratorium while the independent auditor tests whether a site-preserving package can meet the required savings gap?**

### To the trade union
**What exact form of temporary employee contribution would you consider acceptable if it were paired with binding no-redundancy and no-closure guarantees for the evidence phase?**

### To the regional state government
**Are you prepared to attach concrete public support to a site-preserving package, or only to the principle of site preservation?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Would you commit to a site-preserving first option if the independent audit shows the savings gap can be closed without closures, even if that reduces management discretion?**

### To investors
**What minimum level of quantified structural savings would you require before accepting a package that excludes closures and compulsory redundancies?**

### To the independent auditor
**Can you define a plant-by-plant threshold at which a site-preserving package is economically sufficient, and if so, what data would be required to apply it consistently?**
