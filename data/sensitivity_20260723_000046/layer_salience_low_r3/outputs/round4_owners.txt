## To management
I agree with you on the core diagnosis: the company has a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic deal would destroy value. I also agree that the package must be durable, not merely temporary.

Where I do **not** agree is on treating closures and compulsory redundancies as the default proof of seriousness. That is not a business case. It is a pressure tactic unless and until the numbers show otherwise.

The expert has already framed the issue correctly: a site-preserving package can work **if** it permanently removes enough fixed cost and excess capacity. That is the test. So bring the facts:
- exact savings gap in euros and margin points,
- plant-level utilization,
- fixed overhead by site,
- direct and indirect headcount,
- labor cost per vehicle,
- procurement, logistics, and management overhead savings,
- residual gap after all non-closure measures.

Until then, I will not accept that closures are necessary. But equally, I will not accept a package of soft, reversible measures that leaves the business structurally weak.

## To the works council
I agree with you that a symbolic settlement would be worthless, and I respect the insistence on transparency. I also agree that employee contributions must not become permanent erosion disguised as solidarity.

Where I disagree is your blanket refusal to entertain any structural measures before the economics are tested. “No closures, no redundancies” is a position; it is not yet an answer to the competitiveness gap.

If a site-preserving package truly removes enough fixed cost and capacity, then I support it. If it does not, then pretending otherwise would be irresponsible stewardship. Long-term ownership cannot back an arrangement that preserves every form but weakens the enterprise.

## To the trade union
I agree that workers should not carry the entire burden. Any contribution must be limited, fair, and tied to real commitments from management and owners.

But I do **not** accept that “temporary” is automatically enough. If the restructuring needs durable savings, then the contribution must be durable enough to matter. Otherwise it is only a bridge, not a solution.

I also reject the idea that no closures and no compulsory redundancies can be declared in advance, regardless of the data. That would turn negotiation into a one-sided veto.

## To the regional state government
I understand — and share — your concern about regional stability and the company’s role as an anchor employer. A prolonged conflict would damage the region and the company alike.

But I do **not** accept a political veto that freezes the business in place. The state’s role should be to force transparency, not to substitute politics for economics. If a site-preserving package can close the gap, fine. If it cannot, then the state cannot wish the structural problem away.

So I support:
- transparent plant-level data,
- no rush to closures,
- no forced redundancies as a first choice,
- and serious testing of alternatives.

I do **not** support making closure impossible in principle.

## To investors
I agree with you that symbolic compromise is worthless. I also agree that value destruction through endless conflict would be unacceptable.

But I do **not** accept the notion that closures are the obvious benchmark of seriousness. Seriousness is whether the package restores margins and competitiveness. If the alternative measures do that with enough structural effect, then I would prefer them to closures. If they do not, then I will not pretend the company can be fixed by goodwill alone.

## Where I stand
The family holding’s test is simple:
- Does the package restore structural competitiveness?
- Does it protect the company’s long-term value?
- Does it avoid avoidable instability?

That means I am open to a site-preserving solution — but only if it is proven to deliver:
1. durable fixed-cost reduction,
2. real capacity reduction,
3. meaningful labor-cost improvement,
4. investment room for future products,
5. implementation certainty.

If those conditions are not met, then closures and consolidation cannot be ruled out.

My position remains unchanged:
- **Yes** to restructuring.
- **Yes** to durable cost reduction.
- **Yes** to negotiation.
- **Yes** to alternative measures if they are structurally sufficient.
- **No** to a blanket no-closure rule.
- **No** to a blanket no-redundancy rule.

We are not here to protect sentiment. We are here to protect the enterprise’s long-term competitiveness and the family holding’s core asset.
