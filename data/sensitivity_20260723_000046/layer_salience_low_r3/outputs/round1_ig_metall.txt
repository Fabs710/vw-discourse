As the trade union, I will speak just as clearly.

We are **not prepared to accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies** as the price of restructuring. If management insists that the workforce should carry the full burden while owners and executives are protected, then there will be conflict, not consent. The peace obligation has ended, and our members are already mobilised.

That said, unlike a pure blockade, we **have put a real counter-proposal on the table** because we do recognise the company’s economic pressure. We are prepared to discuss:

- a **fair employee contribution** to support the turnaround,
- **capacity reductions** through negotiated, socially responsible means,
- **working-time instruments**, redeployment, and qualification,
- and temporary or reversible measures that help the company through the crisis.

But this only works if it is a **shared-sacrifice package**. That means:
- **no forced layoffs,**
- **no plant closures,**
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework,**
- and a credible commitment that management, owners, and capital also contribute.

On the substance, I agree with the Works Council that the future must be secured through **alternative measures**, not through destroying jobs and sites. I also agree that investment commitments and protection for German locations are essential. Without that, any talk of “transformation” is just a euphemism for downsizing at workers’ expense.

Where I draw the line is this: if management treats labour costs as the only problem and demands open-ended concessions, we will not move. Employees are not the shock absorber for every strategic failure. A durable settlement must distribute the burden fairly and preserve the collective wage standard.

So our position is:
**Yes to restructuring. Yes to negotiated contributions. No to closures. No to compulsory redundancies. No to a one-sided wage and job massacre.**

If management wants a solution, it must come with concrete guarantees and balanced sacrifice from all sides. If not, we are fully prepared to escalate industrial action strategically.
