We appreciate that the discussion has finally moved from slogans to the real question: **can we restore competitiveness without destroying long-term value?** That is the standard we will use.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, investors, and the state on the diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high,
- capacity is out of line with demand,
- the European market is weaker,
- and the brand must improve margins to fund future investment.

We also agree with labour and the state on one important point:
- a prolonged, uncontrolled conflict would be value-destructive.

So far, so good. But agreement on the diagnosis does **not** mean we accept the same remedy.

### Where we disagree with management
Management keeps saying that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain fully open-ended because the numbers may require them. That may be true in the abstract, but it is **not yet a proven necessity**.

What I do **not** accept is:
- using closures as the default discipline mechanism,
- treating job guarantees as negotiable from the start,
- or forcing a settlement that triggers years of conflict and reputational damage.

If you want the family holding to support a hard restructuring, you need to show that the package is **structurally effective**, not merely severe. Severity is not a strategy.

### Where I disagree with the works council and union
I respect the insistence on protecting jobs and sites. I also understand why you draw red lines. But I have to be equally clear: a blanket **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies”** position is not enough unless you can demonstrate an alternative that delivers the same structural savings.

At the moment, you have named instruments, but not proven sufficiency:
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- production shifts,
- negotiated contributions.

These may help materially. They may even be preferable. But unless they close the gap, they are not a complete answer. We will not support a solution that preserves every site and every guarantee on paper while the company continues to erode in practice.

### To the regional state government
Your emphasis on regional stability is legitimate and important. But the state cannot simply oppose closures in principle and then expect the economic problem to disappear. If the alternatives do not add up, the company’s future weakens — and with it the very regional base you want to protect.

So I agree with your demand for a transparent comparison of options. That comparison needs to be done seriously, not as a cover for pre-decided positions.

### What would move us
The family holding would support avoiding closures **if and only if** the alternative package delivers:

1. **Measured, durable cost reduction**
   - not symbolic savings,
   - not temporary relief,
   - but a real improvement in structural competitiveness.

2. **Clear capacity adjustment**
   - aligned with realistic demand,
   - with actual volume reduction, not accounting tricks.

3. **A meaningful labour contribution**
   - fair, but not merely reversible theatre.

4. **A credible investment path**
   - future products, software, battery capability, and platforms.

5. **Implementation certainty**
   - enough stability to avoid recurring conflict and governance paralysis.

If those conditions are met, we have no ideological interest in closures for their own sake. If they are not met, then closures cannot be ruled out.

### What we will not accept
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base unchanged,
- a package that preserves every structure regardless of economics,
- or an outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise’s value and weakens governance control.

Let me be candid: a settlement that avoids hard decisions at the cost of long-term decline is not “socially responsible” in our view. It is merely deferred damage.

### Our position, plainly stated
- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to alternative measures if they are genuinely sufficient.
- **Yes** to a fair employee contribution.
- **Yes** to investment commitments and future capability.
- **No** to a blank no-closure rule.
- **No** to a blanket no-redundancy rule.
- **No** to a deal that is politically comfortable but economically weak.

If the employee side and the state want us to back a site-preserving solution, then bring forward a quantified package that closes the gap. If you can do that, we will support it through governance. If you cannot, then we should be honest that the restructuring remains incomplete.
