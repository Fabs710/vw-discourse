I will speak plainly.

We all agree that this company needs a **real restructuring**. We also agree that the current cost base is too high and that future investment is necessary. Where we do **not** agree is on the assumption that the only credible way to achieve that is to keep closures and compulsory redundancies fully open-ended.

From the state’s perspective, that is **not an acceptable starting point**.

## To management
You have still not shown that closures are necessary. You keep saying they must remain available, and you are right that you need credible tools. But a tool is not the same as a proven necessity.

The expert has made the key point: a no-closure package can work if it **permanently removes enough fixed cost and excess capacity**. That is the test. So far, I have not seen a plant-level comparison that proves the alternative measures are insufficient.

What I need from you is not another general statement about “structural savings,” but a **transparent, quantified comparison**:
- savings gap in euros and in margin terms,
- plant-level utilization,
- fixed overhead by site,
- direct and indirect headcount,
- labor cost per vehicle,
- savings from working-time, redeployment, voluntary exits, procurement, and management overhead,
- and the residual gap after those measures.

Until that is on the table, I will not accept plant closures or forced redundancies as justified.

## To the works council and union
I agree with you on the central principle: **German sites and jobs are not disposable**. The company is a regional anchor, and we cannot treat its workforce as the default adjustment variable.

I also agree that any workforce contribution must be **limited, reversible, and fair**. Temporary working-time measures, qualification, redeployment, and negotiated contributions are all legitimate instruments.

Where I part company with you is on the tone that the answer is already fixed as “no closures, no redundancies” regardless of the numbers. I understand the red lines politically and socially, but we should not defend them in a way that ignores the possibility that the economic comparison may still require adjustment somewhere.

That said, I am **not** moving off the state’s core position:
- no closures as a first resort,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- and no weakening of the company’s regional role.

Those are not bargaining decorations. They are conditions for any solution I can support.

## To the family holding
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is worthless. I agree. But I do not accept the leap from “the package must be real” to “therefore closures are the natural answer.”

Long-term value is not protected by destroying trust in the industrial base. If this becomes a contest of who can impose the hardest cut, the company may look cleaner on paper and weaker in reality. That would damage the enterprise, the region, and the governance culture.

If you want long-term value, then support a settlement that:
- preserves industrial capability in Germany,
- protects the company’s role as an anchor employer,
- and delivers durable savings without breaking social partnership.

## To investors
You are right that symbolic compromise is not enough. I agree. But credibility is not synonymous with closures.

A site-preserving restructuring can be credible if it is backed by:
- measurable capacity reduction,
- real cost reductions,
- investment commitments,
- and implementation certainty.

The burden is to prove whether that can be done. It has **not** yet been proven impossible.

## My position on the actual trade-off
I am open to a serious restructuring that includes:
- capacity shifts,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- procurement and overhead savings,
- and a fair employee contribution.

I am **not** open to:
- using closures as the default instrument,
- compulsory redundancies as a fallback you have not justified,
- or a package that leaves the region with permanent industrial damage.

## What I would support
I would support a package if it meets all of the following:
1. **Transparent plant-level data**
2. **A quantified comparison of site-preserving and consolidation options**
3. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
4. **No compulsory redundancies**
5. **A clear role for co-determination in implementation**
6. **A durable path to competitiveness without gutting regional employment**

## What I would not support
I will not support a settlement that:
- makes closures the implicit threat from the outset,
- shifts the burden mainly onto workers,
- or weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That role is not ornamental. It exists because this company is of strategic importance to the region and the public interest.

## Bottom line
My position is unchanged, and I want it understood clearly:

- **Yes** to restructuring.
- **Yes** to durable savings.
- **Yes** to future investment.
- **Yes** to a negotiated employee contribution.
- **No** to closures as the default answer.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies.
- **No** to sidelining the state’s role in protecting regional employment and industrial capacity.

If the numbers eventually show that more drastic measures are truly unavoidable, then we will confront that question on the basis of facts, not pressure. But we are not there now, and I will not concede that point just because others repeat it often.
