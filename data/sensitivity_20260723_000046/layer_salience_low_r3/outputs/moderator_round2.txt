### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real, durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that the company faces a **cost and competitiveness problem** and needs room for future investment.
- Most parties agree that a **prolonged conflict** would be damaging.
- There is shared interest in **investment in future products, software, battery capability, and platforms**.
- There is general openness to discussing **alternative measures** such as working-time adjustments, redeployment, qualification, procurement/overhead savings, and voluntary departures.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors keep them open as a possible measure.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee side and state reject them; management and capital-side actors do not rule them out.
- **No-closure/no-forced-layoff guarantees**:  
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors say this is only acceptable if an alternative package delivers equivalent savings.
- **Collective bargaining / job-security framework**:  
  - Employee side opposes weakening it; management is unwilling to exclude changes if needed for structural cost reduction.
- **Whether non-closure measures are sufficient**:  
  - The expert says they can help materially, but may not fully close a structural gap without stronger assumptions and data.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No shared, verified **savings gap** has been established.
- No agreed breakdown exists for how much savings must come from:

  - management/overhead,

  - capital allocation.
- There is no agreed **quantified comparison** of:
  - closures vs. non-closure measures,
  - temporary vs. permanent savings,
  - site-preserving restructuring vs. capacity removal.

- Management and capital-side actors have not yet shown that alternative measures are insufficient.
