**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad support for **negotiated restructuring** rather than a purely unilateral approach.
- Several parties agree that **alternative measures** should be explored, including:
  - working-time solutions,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - internal efficiency improvements,
  - negotiated employee contributions.
- There is shared recognition that **investment in future products, technologies, and platforms** is necessary.
- Most parties also acknowledge that a prolonged conflict or strike would be harmful.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  The works council, union, regional state government, and family holding all reject closures as a first choice or red line; management and, to a degree, investors insist closures must remain an option if needed for structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour representatives and the state reject them; management will not exclude them if necessary.
- **Scope of concessions:**  
  Labour is willing to discuss limited, reversible contributions; management and investors want **durable, substantial cost reductions**.
- **Meaning of “shared sacrifice”:**  
  Labour and the state want balanced burden-sharing across management, owners, and workforce; management argues current proposals do not yet deliver enough real savings.
- **Protection of all German sites:**  
  Labour and the state seek site guarantees; management and investors say this cannot be promised without proof that competitiveness can still be restored.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus **how much site/job protection is feasible**.
- Whether **equivalent structural savings** can be achieved **without closures or forced layoffs**.
- What level of **workforce contribution** is acceptable if it is to remain limited, fair, and reversible.
- What **binding investment commitments** management can make, and under what conditions.
- How to balance **short-term social stability** against **long-term competitiveness and return-on-sales targets**.
- What governance or co-determination role would be sufficient to secure trust in any deal.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from general positions to **specific package design**:
- quantify the savings target,
- list which measures are on the table and in what order of preference,
- identify any non-negotiable commitments that could make a deal credible,
- and test whether a **no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy** solution can still produce the required structural effect.

The next round should focus on **concrete trade-offs and verifiable numbers**, not on repeating red lines.
