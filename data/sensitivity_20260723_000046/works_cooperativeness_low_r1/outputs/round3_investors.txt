We agree with the broad diagnosis, but we are **not** yet convinced the process has produced a credible, investable restructuring path.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **structural competitiveness problem**.
- The current cost base is too high.
- Capacity must come down in a **durable** way.
- Future investment is necessary.
- A prolonged conflict would destroy value.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” cannot be absolute
To the works council, union, and state: your position is understandable, but from a capital-markets perspective it is **not acceptable if it leaves the business structurally impaired**.

If the savings gap cannot be closed otherwise, then closures and deeper headcount reduction must remain available. Investors do not fund a permanent margin shortfall because it is socially easier to avoid hard decisions.

### 2) The management case is still under-quantified
To management: saying “the numbers already prove it” is not enough. You have repeatedly asserted that non-closure measures may be insufficient, but you have not yet laid out a **site-by-site, measure-by-measure bridge** from today’s cost base to target margins.

We do **not** support a cosmetic settlement. But we also do **not** support a blank-cheque restructuring narrative without the quantified proof.

### 3) Guarantees are only useful if they do not freeze the cost base
Binding job and site guarantees through 2030 may sound stabilizing, but if they lock in an uncompetitive structure, they simply defer the reckoning and destroy equity value.

We are open to guarantees **only if** they sit inside a package that demonstrably restores competitiveness.

---

## What would move us
We would support a package that is:
- **durable**: not temporary optics,
- **quantified**: with clear annual savings,
- **credible**: enough to restore returns,
- **balanced**: with meaningful contributions from labour, management, and owners,
- **implementable**: not dependent on wishful thinking.

That can include:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity agreements,
- capacity rationalization,
- selective site exits if economically necessary,
- and investment commitments tied to restructuring.

What we will **not** support is a package that preserves every legacy site, rules out forced headcount adjustment in advance, and then expects capital markets to absorb the value erosion.

## On the expert/audit request
The expert has made the central point correctly: the answer depends on the numbers.

We want an independent audit that compares:
1. closure-led restructuring,

3. and a hybrid package.

It must show:
- the exact cost gap,
- savings by site and instrument,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- investment requirements,
- and the break point at which non-closure measures fail.

Without that, the discussion remains political rhetoric, not investable strategy.

## Bottom line
Our position is simple:
- **Yes** to serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to negotiated solutions if they restore margins and cash flow.
- **No** to cosmetic settlements.
- **No** to an uncompetitive structure protected by absolute red lines.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that erodes enterprise value.

If management brings a quantified package that credibly restores competitive returns, we can back it. If not, then capital markets will treat this as delay, not restructuring.
