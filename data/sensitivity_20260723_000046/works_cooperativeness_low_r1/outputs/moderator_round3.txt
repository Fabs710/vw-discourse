## Core impasse

The central impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the acceptable instruments and the sequencing of decisions**:

1. **Works council / union / state** want:
   - no plant closures in Germany,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - binding job and site security,
   - and a proof-first, site-by-site alternatives test.

2. **Management / family / investors** want:
   - a restructuring that is economically decisive,
   - with closures and deeper headcount reduction still available if needed,
   - and do not want to preclude hard options before the numbers are fully tested.

So the dispute is:
- **whether closures and forced redundancies may remain on the table now**, or
- **whether they must be excluded unless an independent review proves all alternatives fail**.

That is the procedural deadlock.

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure / no-forced-redundancy phase with hard review gate
**Structure**
- A defined first phase, e.g. 9–12 months.
- During this phase:
  - no German plant closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - capacity reductions only through shift changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, and working-time instruments.
- In return:
  - workforce contribution on pay, time, or flexibility,
  - management and owner contribution on investment and cost restraint.
- A genuinely independent audit runs in parallel.
- A clearly defined trigger for a second phase exists, based on objective metrics and independent verification.

**Why this could bridge**
- Gives workers and state real protection for now.
- Gives management a structured path to deeper measures if the non-closure package fails.

---

### Proposal 2: Binding site guarantees for a limited number of “strategic core sites,” plus an open review for the rest
**Structure**
- Identify a smaller set of plants that have a strong specialization or future-product case.
- These sites receive:
  - binding job and investment guarantees,
  - no closure and no forced redundancies for the term of the agreement.
- Other sites are subject to an independent site-by-site viability review.
- For weaker sites, the process can include:
  - redeployment,
  - conversion,
  - volume transfer,
  - or, only as a last resort after the review, deeper restructuring.

**Why this could bridge**
- Gives the works council/state concrete protection for core employment centers.
- Gives management and investors flexibility where viability is genuinely weakest.

---

### Proposal 3: “No compulsory redundancies first” with a quantified burden-sharing package
**Structure**
- All sides agree to prioritize:
  - voluntary exits,
  - retirement bridges,
  - internal transfer,
  - working-time changes,
  - productivity measures,
  - and temporary labor-cost relief.
- Closures are not approved at the outset, but are not permanently excluded.
- If the agreed savings target is not met by a fixed date, the parties reopen the question on the basis of audited numbers.
- The workforce contribution is matched by:
  - owner-side capital support,
  - management compensation restraint,
  - and explicit investment commitments.

**Why this could bridge**
- Removes immediate threat of forced layoffs.
- Preserves a fallback if the package proves insufficient.
- Forces all sides to contribute, not just labour.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact annual savings gap, by site, must be closed for you to say a non-closure package has failed, and will you publish the measure-by-measure calculation now?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to commit owner capital and governance support to a site-preserving package if an independent audit shows it can close the gap without closures?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure phase if it is backed by binding savings targets, independent verification, and a defined trigger for deeper measures if targets are missed?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you accept an independent review with real access to plant-level financial data, even if it may conclude that some sites are not viable without deeper restructuring?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What concrete, quantified workforce contribution are you prepared to place on the table if it is paired with binding investment commitments and a temporary no-closure commitment?**

### To the Works Council
**If an independent audit shows that one or two sites cannot be made viable without major conversion or volume transfer, are you prepared to discuss that outcome within a protected no-forced-redundancy framework?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum data set would you require to determine, site by site, whether a non-closure package can close the gap with credible durability?**
