I will speak plainly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree that the company needs a real restructuring, not slogans. I also agree that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem and that future investment is necessary.

I even agree that capacity has to be addressed. That is not the dispute.

The dispute is whether the workforce is supposed to accept **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and the destruction of the job-security framework** as the price of management’s strategy. On that point, my answer is still: **no**.

---

## To Group & Brand Management
You keep saying the numbers force closures and that reversible measures are not enough. But you have still not demonstrated that transparently and site by site.

Your position is basically: “trust us, the gap is too big, so the hard options must remain available.” That is not a restructuring plan. That is a threat.

### Where I disagree
- You cannot start from closure.
- You cannot treat compulsory redundancies as a default instrument.
- You cannot demand a binding workforce contribution while refusing binding site and job security.
- You cannot ask the works council to co-design a plan while retaining the right to impose a fait accompli later.

If you want credibility, then put the hard facts on the table:
1. exact savings gap,
2. site-by-site economics,
3. savings from each measure,
4. investment commitments,
5. and what has genuinely been tested before any closure is discussed.

Until then, I see no basis to accept your closure logic.

---

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You talk about value preservation, but value is not preserved by tearing out the industrial base and triggering mass conflict.

I disagree with your insistence that closures must remain available as a real option from the outset. That position may be convenient for governance, but it is not acceptable socially or industrially.

### Where I agree
- The company needs competitiveness.
- The burden cannot fall on labour alone.
- Management must be accountable.
- The restructuring must be durable.

### Where I disagree
- No, “economically necessary” is not yet proven.
- No, closing German plants is not a neutral adjustment.
- No, compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable bargaining tool.
- No, co-determination is not decorative and not to be overridden by shareholder impatience.

If the family wants to be taken seriously as a responsible owner, then it should back a site-preserving solution and fund it properly instead of pushing closure as the fallback.

---

## To Institutional Investors
You want durability, returns, and a credible path to target margins. Fine. But your current position still treats jobs and sites as disposable inputs.

### Where I disagree
- An absolute “no closures” line is not acceptable to you, you say — but your alternative is effectively to force the workforce to accept the burden until it gives way.
- You also say guarantees freeze structure. That is only true if management refuses to build a restructuring around them.
- You keep asking for “durable” savings, but a closure-led solution that destroys skills, triggers strikes, and undermines trust is not durable. It is unstable.

### What you are missing
This company is not a generic portfolio asset. It is a major industrial employer in Germany with co-determination, regional responsibility, and social obligations. If you ignore that, you will get resistance, not recovery.

---

## To the Regional State Government
I support your resistance to closures and your insistence on a site-preserving test. That is the right instinct.

But I also need to be clear: a “maybe later, maybe never” posture is not enough if it leaves management free to keep using closure threats as leverage.

### Where I agree
- No unilateral restructuring.
- No shifting the burden primarily onto workers.
- Yes to quantified alternatives.
- Yes to investment commitments.
- Yes to strong co-determination.

### Where I disagree
I do not need a “stronger no-closure presumption” in rhetoric. I need a **binding commitment**: no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies as part of this restructuring.

If the state is serious about industrial stability, then it should support that line, not blur it.

---

## To the Industrial Union
You are right to support the works council’s core red lines.

### Where I agree
- The workforce is prepared to contribute.
- Temporary and reversible instruments can be discussed.
- Working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and voluntary exits are legitimate tools.
- Industrial peace matters.

### Where I disagree or remain cautious
I will not let “shared sacrifice” become a euphemism for permanent insecurity.

If the union offers labour-cost relief, that must be:
- temporary or clearly bounded,
- tied to binding site and job guarantees,
- tied to real management and owner contributions,
- and not used to open the door to closures later.

I am not giving you a blank cheque in the form of wage concessions or bonus deferrals if the end result is still plant shutdowns.

---

## On the facilitator’s three proposals

### Proposal 1: Two-stage restructuring with a non-closure first phase
This is closer to reality than the others, but only if Phase 1 is genuinely protected.

I will not accept a “first phase” that is merely a delay before the same closure agenda returns under another name. If there is a Phase 2 trigger, then it must be so high and so constrained that closures are effectively excluded unless the company can prove an extraordinary failure of the entire plan.

And even then: I am not volunteering to negotiate away sites now so someone can reopen the knife later.

### Proposal 2: Binding guarantees with reciprocal concessions
This is the only form of concession I can even discuss seriously:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job and site guarantees to 2030,
- investment commitments,
- and then a fair, temporary workforce contribution.

But let me stress: this is not a concession to closure. It is the condition for any cooperation at all.

### Proposal 3: Independent alternatives test
If someone genuinely wants a technical review, I would support that **only if it is clearly framed as a test of closure avoidance**, not as a procedural path to legitimize closure.

So yes to experts — but not to experts being used as fig leaves.

[SUMMON: independent industrial restructuring expert with plant-level cost modelling and site-comparison analysis]

We need an independent expert who can assess, site by site, whether a non-closure restructuring package can meet the required savings **without forced redundancies** and with investment commitments intact. That is not optional if management wants to claim there is no alternative.

---

## My position, clearly and unchanged
Let me make it unmistakable:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding job security through 2030.**
- **Binding site guarantees for all German plants.**
- **Meaningful co-determination in shaping the restructuring.**
- **Investment commitments for future products and technologies.**

Anything else is not a negotiated solution; it is an attempt to shift the crisis onto the workforce.

---

## What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss:
- capacity reductions through shift and working-time changes,
- internal redeployment across sites,
- qualification and retraining,
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary labour-cost measures,
- and a fair contribution from all sides.

I am **not** prepared to discuss:
- plant closures,
- forced layoffs,
- breaking the job-security framework,
- or a restructuring that keeps all the downside for workers and all the upside for capital.

---

## Final word
If management, owners, and investors want to avoid conflict, they need to stop treating closures as the opening assumption.

Bring a real site-preserving plan. Bring binding guarantees. Bring investment. Bring proof. Then we can talk.

Until then, the works council will defend every German plant, every job, and the co-determination rights that protect both.
