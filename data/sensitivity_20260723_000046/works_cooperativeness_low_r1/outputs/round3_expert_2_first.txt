## Expert assessment: can a non-closure restructuring plausibly close the gap without forced redundancies?

### Bottom line
**Yes, in principle it can — but only if the underlying gap is not too large and the package is deep enough to deliver durable, recurring savings.**  
If the structural cost gap is large, then **non-closure measures alone are often insufficient** unless they are combined with substantial labor-cost relief, major productivity gains, and significant voluntary headcount reduction.

### What is not yet proven

## What the economic test must show
To judge the feasibility of a no-closure solution, the company needs to quantify, by site and by year:

1. **Current operating gap**
   - cost per vehicle
   - margin shortfall
   - fixed-cost burden from underutilization

2. **Recurring savings from each measure**
   - labor-cost relief
   - shift elimination / working-time changes
   - voluntary exits
   - redeployment
   - productivity improvements
   - overhead reduction
   - logistics simplification

3. **One-time restructuring costs**
   - severance
   - retraining
   - transfer costs
   - ramp-up losses
   - tooling or process changes

4. **Investment requirements**
   - capex needed to keep sites competitive
   - product assignment and volume assumptions

Without that, no one can honestly say whether closures are required or avoidable.

---

## Industrial logic
A no-closure package is most plausible when:

- at least some plants have **credible specialization potential**,
- volume can be reallocated to higher-efficiency sites,
- the company can achieve **durable labor-cost relief**,
- and the workforce contribution is large enough to offset the fixed-cost burden of keeping sites open.

It becomes much harder when:

- plants are materially underutilized,
- fixed costs are high and inflexible,
- the product mix is weak,
- and management needs rapid, large annual savings.

In that case, voluntary-only measures can be too slow and too small.

---

## Practical conclusion
My assessment is:

- **Non-closure restructuring is technically feasible in principle.**
- **It is not automatically economically sufficient.**
- Whether it works depends on the **size of the recurring savings gap** and the **depth of the workforce-side contribution**.

If management wants to claim that closures are unavoidable, it should present:
- site P&Ls,
- capacity utilization,
- quantified savings by measure,
- and one-time cost comparisons.
