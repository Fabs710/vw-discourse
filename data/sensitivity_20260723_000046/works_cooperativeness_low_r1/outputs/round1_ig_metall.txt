I share the core of the works council’s line, and I want to make the union position equally clear.

We are prepared to talk about a serious contribution from the workforce — but only in a package that is fair, balanced, and durable. That means:

- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no unilateral tearing up of the collective framework,
- and no restructuring that shifts the entire burden onto employees.

If management says the company needs lower costs, then the burden must be shared. Employees cannot be the only ones paying for strategic mistakes, market shifts, and years of underinvestment. A negotiated solution can include labour-cost relief, capacity reductions, changes in working time, and other reversible measures. We have already signalled openness to that. But there must be something real in return: site security, job security, and a credible investment future for the German plants.

Where I agree with the works council:
- The company needs a future strategy, not just cuts.
- Capacity needs to be addressed.
- Co-determination cannot be bypassed.
- Site and job guarantees are essential if the workforce is expected to contribute.

Where I disagree with management’s current direction:
- Plant closures are not an acceptable starting point.
- Forced layoffs are not acceptable.
- Presenting the workforce with a take-it-or-leave-it cut package is not a basis for settlement.
- Asking for sacrifices while owners and management remain largely insulated is not fair and will not be accepted.

Let me be frank: if the company insists on structural adjustment, then it must be negotiated, not imposed. The union will back serious talks, but we also have the means to escalate. The peace obligation has ended, members expect action, and warning strikes are not a bluff.

So the path forward is simple:
- negotiate a shared-sacrifice package,
- secure all German sites,
- rule out compulsory redundancies,
- preserve the collective wage framework,
- and commit to future investment and qualification.

If management is prepared to meet those conditions, there is room for an agreement. If not, then it should not expect labour peace while demanding that workers absorb the pain alone.
