## Core impasse

The deadlock is now concentrated in one central conflict:

**Employee-side and state-side actors want restructuring only if it is site-preserving and job-secure; management, the controlling family, and investors insist that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available if needed to close the cost gap.**

That splits into three linked fault lines:

1. **Red lines vs. flexibility**
   - Works council, union, and state: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, strong guarantees.
   - Management, family, investors: cannot preclude closures or redundancies in advance.

2. **Trust vs. proof**
   - Employee/state side: prove that alternatives fail before any closure discussion.
   - Management/family/investors: the need for adjustment is already evident, and delaying closure decisions risks competitiveness.

3. **Burden-sharing vs. burden transfer**
   - Employee-side actors will contribute only if management, owners, and capital also contribute.
   - Capital-side actors want durable savings but are unconvinced that voluntary or reversible measures will be sufficient.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

### Proposal 1: Two-stage restructuring with a strict non-closure first phase
A settlement could separate the process into **Phase 1** and **Phase 2**:

- **Phase 1 (12–18 months):**
  - no plant closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - use only redeployment, voluntary exits, working-time instruments, site specialization, and productivity measures,
  - paired with binding investment commitments and measurable savings targets.

- **Phase 2 (trigger-based review):**
  - an agreed, jointly verified review occurs only if Phase 1 savings fall short by a specified threshold.
  - only then would closure or deeper measures re-enter the discussion, under defined procedural rules.

This could bridge the gap by giving employee/state actors a real non-closure period while giving management/investors a contingent escape if targets are missed.

---

### Proposal 2: Binding job and site guarantees with conditional reciprocity
A second compromise could pair **security guarantees** with **measurable concessions**:

- The company offers:
  - binding job/security protections through a defined date,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - no plant closures during the guarantee period,
  - and firm investment commitments.

- In return, employee-side actors agree to:
  - a structured package of labor-cost relief,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exit programs,
  - productivity agreements,
  - and temporary, measurable concessions.

This may bridge the main trust gap if the guarantees are made conditional on the workforce contribution and if management/owners also commit financially.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-by-site “alternative test” with independent verification
A third pathway is to create a **formal alternatives test** before any closure decision:

- For each at-risk site, an independent panel or jointly appointed expert team evaluates:
  - capacity reduction options,
  - redeployment potential,
  - specialization options,
  - investment needs,
  - and whether the site can reach competitiveness without closure.

- Closure would be permitted only if:
  - the panel certifies that alternatives fail to meet a defined economic threshold.

This does not settle the substantive disagreement, but it can convert the current abstract dispute into a structured, evidence-based process.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1. Works Council
**What exact combination of measures would you accept as economically equivalent to a closure, and what minimum cost-saving threshold must be met for you to endorse it?**

### 2. Industrial Union
**How much durable labor-cost relief is the union prepared to contribute through wages, working time, or bonus deferrals, and under what conditions would that contribution be withdrawn?**

### 3. Group & Brand Management
**Which specific savings measures have already been tested, what were their quantified results, and at what exact shortfall would management conclude that non-closure options have failed?**

### 4. Regional State Government
**Will the state support a conditional process that allows closures only after an independently verified alternatives test, or does it require a stronger no-closure presumption than that?**

### 5. Controlling Family Holding
**What level of restructuring burden is the family prepared to absorb itself through capital support, guarantees, or foregone distributions, before expecting labor-side concessions?**

### 6. Institutional Investors
**What minimum margin-recovery timeline and return profile would make you accept a no-closure or delayed-closure settlement, even if near-term savings are less than originally planned?**
