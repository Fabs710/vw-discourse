Management is asking the workforce to pay for a crisis we did not create, with plant closures and forced redundancies that would tear apart jobs, communities, and the company’s own industrial base. I reject that approach.

Our position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No breaking of existing job-security protections.
- No weakening of co-determination or sidelining the works council in decisions that shape the future of the sites.

If the company needs cost relief and restructuring, then it must come through negotiated, reversible, and socially responsible measures: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal transfers, training and qualification, natural attrition, and a fair contribution from management and owners. But the burden cannot fall mainly on employees, and it cannot be imposed as a fait accompli.

We are prepared to discuss a serious industrial future only if it includes:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- binding job guarantees through 2030,
- real investment commitments for future products and technologies,
- and a genuine co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping the restructuring.

Without those guarantees, there is no basis for agreement. If management insists on closures or forced layoffs, we will resist that forcefully, including through industrial action and use of our co-determination rights.
