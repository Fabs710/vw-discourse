The question directed to my area is the expert/audit requirement: **what minimum data set is needed to determine, site by site, whether a non-closure package can close the gap with credible durability?**

## Minimum data set required

To assess this properly, I would need, for **each German site** and for the **group as a whole**:

### 1) Profitability and cost baseline
- Site-level P&L, ideally on a **contribution margin** basis and a **fully loaded cost** basis
- Fixed vs variable cost split
- Labor cost per vehicle and per hour
- Conversion cost per unit
- Allocation methodology for shared corporate overhead
- Depreciation/amortization by site
- Energy, maintenance, logistics, and internal transfer pricing

### 2) Capacity and utilization
- Installed annual capacity by line and site
- Current utilization
- Planned utilization under each product/volume scenario
- Shift pattern, downtime, and bottleneck analysis
- Mix effects by model, derivative, and powertrain
- Scrap, rework, and changeover losses

### 3) Product and volume forecast
- 3–5 year volume forecast by site, model, and variant
- Assumed launch/phase-out timing
- EV/ICE/hybrid mix assumptions
- Sensitivity cases for low, base, and high demand
- Export share and market allocation assumptions

### 4) Labor structure
- Headcount by site, function, and skill group
- Wage bill, premiums, bonuses, social costs
- Working-time regime
- Overtime reliance
- Absenteeism and productivity metrics
- Age profile and natural attrition potential
- Temporary labor share
- Union/collective agreement constraints that affect effective cost and flexibility

### 5) Non-closure measure effects
For each proposed measure, I need:
- **quantified annual savings**
- **one-time implementation cost**
- **timing of savings**
- **risk of reversal**
- **impact on productivity and quality**

Measures would include:
- voluntary exits / severance
- early retirement bridges
- working-time reduction or flexibility
- shift model changes
- wage concessions or temporary deferrals
- bonus suspension
- redeployment across sites
- insourcing/outsourcing changes
- product specialization and volume transfer
- productivity programs
- logistics simplification

### 6) Investment and capex needs
- Site-specific capex required to keep each plant competitive
- Tooling and retooling costs for future products
- Infrastructure upgrades
- Automation and digitalization needs
- Ramp-up costs and timing
- Payback assumptions

### 7) Scenario comparison
At minimum, three scenarios:
1. **Status quo / partial measures**
2. **Non-closure package**
3. **Closure or major restructuring fallback**

For each scenario:
- EBITDA / operating margin impact
- cash impact
- one-time restructuring cost
- payback period
- break-even volume
- sensitivity to demand downside

### 8) Durability checks
To judge whether a package is credible beyond year 1–2:
- does it structurally reduce fixed cost?
- does it improve unit cost at realistic utilization?
- does it preserve flexibility if volumes fall further?
- does it avoid simply deferring the problem?
- does it create enough recurring savings to offset lost competitiveness?

## What I would not accept as sufficient
- Group averages only
- Aggregated “Germany” numbers with no plant split
- Headcount reductions without site economics
- Savings claims without one-time cost and timing
- Productivity assumptions without evidence
- A proposal that counts one-off labor concessions as if they were durable structural savings

## Practical threshold test
A non-closure package is only plausible if it can:
- close the savings gap with **recurring** measures,
- reduce fixed costs materially,
- preserve acceptable utilization at the remaining sites,
- and still leave the company competitive under a downside volume case.

If the plan depends mainly on temporary concessions, deferred costs, or optimistic volume recovery, it is not durable.
