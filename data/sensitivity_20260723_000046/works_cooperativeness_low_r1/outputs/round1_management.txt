I hear the concern about jobs and sites, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic package that preserves today’s cost base is not an option**. The company is facing a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary inconvenience. German manufacturing costs are too high, European demand is shrinking, and our margins are not where they need to be to fund the next product cycle and the technology investments to 2030.

So let me respond directly:

### Where I agree
- **We do need a negotiated solution.** I am not pretending this can be done by decree alone.
- **Capacity has to come down.** On that point, we are not far apart.
- **Investment in future products and technologies is essential.** But it has to be financed by real savings, not wishful thinking.

### Where I disagree
- **No plant closures in Germany** is not a credible starting position. I am not saying closures are the only answer, but I am saying explicitly that **all options remain on the table** if they are required to achieve a durable restructuring.
- **No compulsory redundancies** may be your preference, but if there is no meaningful labour-cost contribution and no structural reduction in overcapacity, then we are left with a bill the company cannot carry.
- **Binding job guarantees through 2030** without binding structural savings would freeze the problem in place. I will not sign up to a guarantee that simply locks in an uncompetitive structure.
- **Management and owners “remaining largely insulated”** is not accurate. The entire point of this restructuring is to protect the long-term viability of the brand and the group. But the workforce also has to contribute materially if we are to restore returns and preserve future investment capacity.

### My position
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that is **equivalent in economic effect**:
- substantial and durable labour-cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- flexibility in working time and deployment,
- meaningful efficiency measures at plant level,
- and a credible path to restoring the cost base to competitive levels.

That means more than natural attrition and training. Those alone will not close the gap.

### On co-determination
No one is being sidelined. But co-determination does not mean vetoing necessary restructuring while expecting management to somehow make the numbers work. We will engage seriously, but the outcome must be fit for the market reality we face.

### Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate a **serious industrial package**, but not one that simply trades time for decline. If the union and works council are willing to deliver a genuine, durable cost and capacity solution, then we can talk about site security, investment commitments, and social mitigation. If not, then we will have to proceed on the basis that deeper structural measures, including closures, remain necessary.

I would ask both employee representatives to come back with a concrete proposal that closes the cost gap materially and permanently. Without that, we are not solving the crisis — we are postponing it.
