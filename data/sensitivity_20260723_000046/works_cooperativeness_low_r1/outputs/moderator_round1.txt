**Moderator Summary**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that **a negotiated solution is preferable** to unilateral action or prolonged conflict.
- Most parties accept that **capacity must be reduced in some form** and that **future investment in products and technologies is necessary**.
- There is shared recognition that **co-determination and formal governance processes must be respected**.
- There is common interest in avoiding **open-ended industrial conflict**, since all sides see it as value-destructive.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point; management, shareholder groups, and investors insist closures must remain possible if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee representatives and the state oppose them; management and capital-side actors say they may become necessary if the gap is not otherwise closed.
- **Binding job/site guarantees:** Employee side wants strong guarantees; management and investors view long rigid guarantees as incompatible with restructuring needs.
- **Burden sharing:** Employees and state want management, owners, and labour to share the burden; management and capital-side actors insist labour must deliver substantial, durable cost relief.
- **Scope of co-determination:** Employee and state actors emphasize a strong role; management emphasizes consultation but not veto over economically necessary measures.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The parties have not yet quantified **how much cost reduction** is required or how much each measure would contribute.
- There is no shared answer on whether **non-closure measures alone** can achieve the needed savings.
- The conversation has not resolved the tension between:
  - **job and site security**, and
  - **the need for structural flexibility and competitiveness**.
- No one has yet presented a fully worked package showing:
  - exact capacity reductions,
  - labour-cost effects,
  - investment commitments,
  - timing,
  - and safeguards for affected sites and workers.
- The issue of **who absorbs short-term pain versus who secures long-term value** remains open.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to a **quantified scenario comparison**.
- Ask management to specify the **required savings gap** and the effect of each restructuring option.
- Ask employee representatives to set out **which flexible measures could be accepted** if paired with concrete guarantees.
- Ask state, shareholder, and investor representatives to clarify **which elements are non-negotiable** and where they see room for compromise.
- The next round should aim to identify a **package structure** rather than restate red lines.
