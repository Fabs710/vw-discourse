We are still not at a credible investment case.

## Where we agree
We agree with all parties on the basic diagnosis:
- the company has a structural cost problem,
- capacity has to come down,
- future investment is necessary,
- and a prolonged conflict would destroy value.

We also agree with the expert’s central point: **without site-by-site numbers, nobody can honestly prove that non-closure measures are sufficient or insufficient**.

That is the key issue. Not slogans, not principles.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council
We respect co-determination, but your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no exceptions” line is **not investable if it leaves the business structurally impaired**.

We are not prepared to fund a permanently uncompetitive cost base because closure is politically uncomfortable. If the numbers show that the gap cannot be closed otherwise, then closures and deeper headcount reduction must remain available.

What we disagree with is your attempt to turn a valid demand for proof into an absolute veto on hard options.

### To the industrial union
You are right that labor should not carry the whole burden. But “no closures as the starting point” is only acceptable if it sits inside a package that is demonstrably sufficient.

We would support:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- temporary labor-cost relief,
- redeployment,
- and a genuine alternatives test.

We would **not** support a deal that protects every site, rules out forceful adjustment in advance, and then asks capital markets to absorb the value destruction.

### To management
You are correct that the restructuring must be durable and not cosmetic. But you still have not given us the **quantified bridge** from today’s cost base to target margins.

Saying “the numbers already prove it” is not enough. Publish the site-by-site economics:
- current cost gap,
- savings by measure,
- one-time costs,
- capex needs,
- and the break point where non-closure options fail.

Until then, we cannot endorse closures as necessary, but we also will not endorse a hollow no-closure promise.

### To the regional state government
Your insistence on a site-preserving review is understandable, but it cannot become a political shield against economically necessary restructuring.

We support an independent review. We do **not** support using the state’s role to freeze change if the numbers show some sites are not viable.

### To the controlling family holding
You are right that value must be preserved, but value is not preserved by delay or by protecting legacy structures at any cost.

We agree that:
- the company needs durable savings,
- labor must contribute,
- owners should contribute too,
- and the settlement must be executable.

We disagree with any assumption that closures are merely routine instruments. They are not. They should only be used if the independent numbers show they are necessary.

## What would move us
We would support a package only if it is:
- **quantified**,
- **durable**,
- **balanced**,
- and **credible** as a route back to competitive returns.

That means:
- a real independent audit,
- site-by-site numbers,
- a clear savings target,
- meaningful contributions from labor, management, and owners,
- and an explicit decision gate if the non-closure package underdelivers.

## [SUMMON: independent industrial restructuring audit team with access to plant-level P&Ls, capacity utilization data, recurring savings by measure, one-time restructuring costs, and investment requirements]
We need an independent audit that answers, site by site:
1. What is the exact annual savings gap?
2. What do non-closure measures save?
3. What are the one-time restructuring costs?
4. What investment is required to keep each site competitive?
5. At what point do non-closure measures fail to close the gap?

## Bottom line
Our position is simple:
- **Yes** to serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to negotiated solutions if they restore competitiveness.
- **No** to cosmetic settlements.
- **No** to an uncompetitive structure protected by absolute red lines.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that destroys enterprise value.

If a non-closure package can truly close the gap, we can back it.  
If it cannot, then deeper measures must remain on the table.

Bring the numbers. Until then, no one should pretend the outcome is settled.
