Here is the current impasse, followed by facilitation proposals and one question for each stakeholder.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which options may remain permissible during the restructuring process**.

The main fault line is:

- **Employee side:** restructuring is acceptable only if it starts from a binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy framework.
- **Management / investors / family:** restructuring is only credible if closures and deeper headcount reductions remain available if the numbers require them.
- **State government:** wants a site-preserving, proof-first process, but is not prepared to endorse a closure-led approach.
- **Expert:** says the dispute can only be resolved by a site-by-site audit and scenario comparison.

So the core impasse is not the goal, but the **sequence and scope of admissible measures**:
1. whether closures are excluded up front,
2. whether a review can reopen them,
3. and whether non-closure measures can be tested before any shutdown option is activated.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Bounded no-closure testing phase with hard evidence gate
A time-limited first phase could be established in which:
- no German plant closures occur,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- the audit team gets full plant-level access,
- management, owners, and labour each commit quantified contributions,
- and the parties agree to a predefined review date.

At the review date, the parties would assess only whether the **non-closure package closes the verified gap**.  
If it does, it becomes the basis for settlement.  
If it does not, the parties move to a second-stage negotiation with no pre-decided outcome, but with the audit findings as the common reference point.

Why this may bridge: it gives labour real protection during testing, while giving management and investors a disciplined decision gate.

---

### Proposal 2: Company-wide no-forced-redundancy, with site-specific restructuring tools
A second bridge option would be:
- no compulsory redundancies company-wide,
- no immediate closures,
- but allowance for site-specific measures such as voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, product transfers, specialization, and investment commitments.

This would be paired with:
- site-by-site viability thresholds,
- an independent audit,
- and a commitment that any future closure discussion requires an additional, higher evidentiary threshold.

Why this may bridge: it preserves the workforce’s central red line on forced layoffs, while leaving room for economically differentiated site solutions.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-preservation commitment for a defined core period, with conditional owner support
A third compromise would be a multi-year stabilization package:
- German sites remain open for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- owners and management provide capital support and restraint on distributions,
- labour provides quantified flexibility and cost relief,
- and the audit determines whether the savings bridge is closed by recurring measures.

If the company still falls short after the period, the parties would reconvene with the audit data and a renewed burden-sharing proposal.

Why this may bridge: it links industrial peace and investment to measurable ownership commitment, while avoiding an open-ended commitment that some stakeholders reject.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If the audit shows a residual savings gap after all non-closure measures, what additional labour-side flexibility would you still consider acceptable without crossing your red lines?

### To the Industrial Union
What specific, quantified workforce contributions are you prepared to put on the table immediately, and what conditions would make them acceptable to your members?

### To Group & Brand Management
Will you commit now to a defined no-closure testing phase with full plant-level data access, or do you insist that closure options remain actionable from day one?

### To the Regional State Government
What exact conditions would need to be met for you to support a time-limited process that keeps closures off the table during the audit phase?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
What concrete capital or distribution-side contribution are you willing to make to demonstrate burden-sharing before any closure option is pursued?

### To Institutional Investors
What level of recurring, not one-off, cost improvement would make a non-closure settlement investable for you?

### To the Expert
What minimum threshold in the audit would indicate that a non-closure package is plausible, and what threshold would indicate that it is not?
