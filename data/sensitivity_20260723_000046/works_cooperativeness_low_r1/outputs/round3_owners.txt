## Where I agree
There is now broad recognition that this company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**. I agree with that, and I agree that:
- a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict,
- capacity must be reduced in some form,
- future investment is necessary,
- and the outcome must be durable, not cosmetic.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with the employee side and the state on one central point: **you are still trying to make “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute starting constraints.**  
That may be understandable politically and socially, but it is not yet compatible with restoring the company’s competitiveness if the savings gap is large.

### To the works council
You say closures are a historic breach and that co-determination is not decorative. I agree that co-determination must be respected, and I agree that workers should not be treated as a disposable cost item.

But I do **not** accept your conclusion that closures and compulsory redundancies can be ruled out in advance. That would amount to freezing the industrial structure regardless of economics. If the company cannot restore margins otherwise, that is not stewardship; it is delay.

### To the union
I hear your emphasis on shared sacrifice, voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, and labour-cost relief. Those are legitimate tools. But you still present binding job and site guarantees through 2030 as a precondition.

I do **not** accept that as a blanket rule. A guarantee that prevents economically necessary restructuring is not value-preserving. It may feel stabilizing now, but it can lock in decline.

### To the regional state government
I understand your responsibility for regional stability and your insistence on a site-preserving review. That is reasonable as a process.

But I do **not** accept a line that turns “last resort” into “effectively never.” If the company needs deeper restructuring to remain competitive, the state cannot create an informal veto over necessary change.

### To the expert
Your assessment is useful, but it does not move the core issue enough. You confirm that non-closure restructuring is possible **in principle** only if the gap is moderate and the package is deep. That is exactly the point: if the gap is not moderate, then non-closure measures may be insufficient.

So the expert does **not** justify ruling out closures. It just justifies doing the numbers properly.
