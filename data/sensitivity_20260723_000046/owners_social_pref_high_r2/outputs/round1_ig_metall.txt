As the trade union, I share the core line of the Works Council, but I want to be equally clear on the bargaining dimension:

**We will not accept compulsory redundancies, plant closures, or a destruction of the collective wage framework.**  
If management believes the crisis can be solved simply by forcing the workforce to absorb the full cost, then we are in open conflict.

That said, we are not refusing responsibility. We have already signaled willingness to discuss a **shared-sacrifice package** if it is genuinely balanced and durable. That means:

- **capacity reduction without closures**
- **voluntary instruments instead of forced layoffs**
- **working-time solutions**
- **temporary labour-cost contributions**
- **sacrifices from management and owners as well, not just employees**
- **binding investment and product commitments for the German sites**

Where I agree with management is that the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness. But I strongly disagree with the idea that competitiveness must be purchased by closing plants and breaking trust with the workforce. That is not a restructuring plan; it is a unilateral transfer of risk onto employees.

A durable settlement must meet three conditions:

1. **Jobs and sites are secured**
2. **The burden is shared fairly**
3. **The collective bargaining system remains intact**

If management comes forward with a plan that includes a real contribution from the top — not just demands from below — then we are prepared to negotiate seriously. If not, the workforce is ready to escalate. The peace obligation has ended, and we will use warning strikes and further industrial action if necessary.

So my message is simple:  
**We are ready for restructuring, but not for social demolition.**
