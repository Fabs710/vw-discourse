From the perspective of dispersed institutional investors, the key issue is simple: **does the package credibly restore margin competitiveness, or does it merely postpone the adjustment while value continues to erode?**

### Where I agree
I agree with management, and partly with the family holding, on the diagnosis:

- The cost base is too high.
- Capacity is too high for the market reality.
- Temporary fixes alone will not be enough.
- The company needs a durable path back to its return-on-sales target.

I also agree that prolonged conflict is value-destructive. A long strike, repeated escalation, or an unstable halfway settlement would weigh on earnings, cash flow, and sentiment. That is not in shareholders’ interests.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible route is plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

Why? Because from an investor’s point of view, the best outcome is not the most aggressive headline cut. It is the one that is:

- **credible**
- **durable**
- **deliverable under the governance constraints**
- and **less disruptive to operations and future investment**

If closures trigger a prolonged confrontation, legal obstruction, political resistance, or operational damage, then the apparent savings can be quickly offset. That is not disciplined restructuring; that is value leakage.

At the same time, I am also not persuaded by the workforce line if it amounts to “no closures, no forced redundancies, no framework changes” without a savings package of equivalent scale. That would leave the cost base structurally uncompetitive, which is fundamentally unacceptable to us.

### What we need
We are looking for a package that produces **real, measurable, recurring cost reduction**. That could come from a mix of:

- capacity rationalisation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- labour-cost contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- and targeted site adjustments,

but it has to add up to something substantial enough to move margins materially.

### Our boundary
Let me be explicit: **we will not support a cosmetic settlement**.

If the result is just:
- deferred conflict,
- vague future efficiencies,
- symbolic sacrifice,
- and continued margin underperformance,

then that is not a compromise we can back. We can reallocate capital elsewhere if needed.

### On management
Management needs to be careful not to present closure as the only serious option if a negotiated alternative can deliver the economics. Investors care about outcomes, not rhetoric. If a site-preserving solution is genuinely equivalent on cost and timing, then that is preferable because it reduces execution risk.

But if the alternative package is materially weaker, then it will not be enough. We need proof, not assurances.

### On the workforce side
The workforce representatives should understand that shareholder patience is not unlimited. Institutional holders have been pressing for decisive action because the share price and margins have already come under pressure. We will not reward a settlement that protects all existing structures while sacrificing competitiveness.

### Bottom line
Our support depends on one test:

**Does this restructuring credibly restore competitive margins on a durable basis?**

If yes, we can support a tough negotiated settlement, even if it is not the most aggressive version.
If no, we will not endorse a deal that leaves the company structurally impaired.

So my message to all sides is this:  
**Bring forward a package with real savings, durable implementation, and no cosmetic outcomes. Otherwise, investor support will not be there.**
