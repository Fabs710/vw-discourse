Management, owners, and investors keep speaking as if the only “serious” restructuring is one that begins with closures and pressure on wages. We reject that logic.

### Where I agree
I agree on one point: the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness. We are not denying the market reality, and we are not defending inefficiency for its own sake. We know the brand must invest, modernize, and improve productivity.

### Where I disagree
I **strongly disagree** with the claim that this requires:
- closing German plants,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or dismantling the job-security and co-determination framework.

That is not a necessary economic conclusion; it is a management choice. And it is a choice we will not accept.

### On management’s position
Management says temporary or voluntary measures are not enough. Then bring evidence, not slogans. If you claim a site-preserving solution cannot work, you need to show:
- the exact savings target,
- the required timing,
- and why a mix of capacity cuts, working-time solutions, voluntary exits, productivity gains, and investment commitments would not add up.

So far, what I hear is a desire for maximum flexibility for management and maximum risk for the workforce. That is unacceptable.

### On the family holding and investors
You both talk about “durable savings” and “value protection,” but you are ignoring the value destruction caused by a confrontation that tears apart labor peace, destabilizes the plants, and wrecks trust. A rushed closure strategy may look decisive on paper, but it is not credible if it triggers strikes, legal blockage, political resistance, and long-term damage to the industrial base.

If you want durable savings, then support a package that is durable in the real world:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding investment,
- and a negotiated contribution from capital and management as well, not just labor.

### On the state government
I appreciate the clear stance against closures and forced layoffs. But that position must now be backed by substance, not just declarations. The workforce will not accept vague reassurance. We need a binding framework that protects sites through 2030 and makes relocation or closure impossible without our consent.

### What we can support
We are prepared to discuss a serious restructuring package that includes:
- capacity adjustment **without closures**
- working-time reductions or flexibility
- voluntary instruments only
- targeted productivity measures
- management and owner contributions
- binding investment in German plants
- a **site and job guarantee through 2030**
- full respect for co-determination

### What we will not accept
Let me be equally clear: we will not sign up to a package that:
- leaves plant closure on the table as a routine option,
- introduces forced layoffs through the back door,
- weakens the historic job-security framework,
- or uses “competitiveness” as a cover for social dumping.

### The actual test
If management, owners, and investors want a solution, then table a concrete alternative that shows how the required savings can be achieved **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**. If you cannot do that, then the conclusion is not that the workforce must surrender; the conclusion is that your restructuring model is the wrong one.

We are ready to bargain over how to secure the future of the company. We are **not** ready to bargain away the German plants or the people who make this company work.
