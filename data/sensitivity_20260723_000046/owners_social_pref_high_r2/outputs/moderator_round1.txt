1) **What has been agreed**
- All sides accept that the company needs restructuring and a credible path to restored competitiveness.
- There is broad support for avoiding a drawn-out conflict if a negotiated solution is possible.
- Most parties express openness to some combination of capacity reduction, productivity measures, working-time solutions, voluntary instruments, and investment commitments.
- There is shared recognition that a durable settlement must be economically credible and must address the company’s future investment needs.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state government reject closures in Germany; management, owners, and investors do not rule them out if needed for structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state government reject them; management/owners/investors keep them available or do not fully exclude workforce reductions.
- **Scope of restructuring:** Management and capital providers want a durable structural reduction in cost and capacity; employee representatives want restructuring without dismantling site security, co-determination, or collective frameworks.
- **Means vs. outcome:** Dispute persists over whether the required savings can be achieved through site-preserving measures or whether closures and/or stronger labor-cost changes are necessary.
- **Governance/authority:** The state asserts an active co-determination/veto role; ownership stresses limits to state veto over economic reality and protection of governance interests.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- How to quantify the required savings and compare the credibility of different restructuring options.
- Whether site-preserving measures can deliver savings equal to closures in timing, scale, and durability.
- What exact burden-sharing package would be acceptable to employees, management, owners, and investors.
- Which sacrifices by management/owners are meaningful, and how they relate to workforce contributions.
- How to ensure investment commitments are binding enough to make job and site guarantees credible.
- How to balance competitiveness targets against social stability, regional impacts, and execution risk.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to numbers: define the size, timing, and durability of the savings required.
- Ask each side to table one concrete restructuring package with:
  - capacity measures,
  - workforce measures,
  - investment commitments,
  - and the expected economic effect.
- Compare packages against the same criteria: cost reduction, legal/political feasibility, operational risk, and social impact.
- Focus discussion on which combination of measures can meet the company’s targets without escalating conflict.
