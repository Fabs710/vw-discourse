## Core impasse

The main impasse is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **which instruments remain legitimate and when**:

1. **Labour / works council / state** want a restructuring that is **site-preserving by default**:
   - no plant closures as a bargaining tool,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - strong job-security and co-determination protections.

2. **Management / investors / controlling family** want to keep **closure and workforce reduction available** if the numbers require it:
   - they see those tools as necessary to make the package credible,
   - and they argue that without them the savings gap may not close.

3. **All sides** accept the need for quantified evidence, but **disagree on the baseline comparison**:
   - labour compares against a hard no-closure package,
   - management compares against the right to close if needed,
   - investors compare risk-adjusted NPV under execution uncertainty.

So the stalemate is essentially:
**“Is closure a legitimate last-resort instrument, or must the restructuring be designed so that closure is off the table from the start?”**

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: “No closure unless a jointly verified trigger is met”
A compromise could be a **phased site-preservation framework** with an explicit escalation rule:

- immediate implementation of a hard site-preserving package,
- a plant-by-plant monitoring process,
- closure or compulsory redundancy only if a **jointly agreed trigger** is met:
  - savings shortfall,
  - demand deterioration,
  - failure to reach productivity or voluntary-exit targets,
  - or missed investment milestones.

This bridges the gap by:
- giving labour real protection now,
- while giving management and investors a conditional fallback later.

---

### Proposal 2: “Binding no-closure package in exchange for deeper, measurable concessions”
A second compromise could be a **stronger site guarantee through 2030** paired with **more substantial and enforceable labour/owner contributions**:

- voluntary exits, partial retirement, redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage and bonus moderation,
- productivity commitments with milestones,
- investment/product commitments from owners and management,
- joint oversight with regular review.

This bridges the gap by:
- replacing closure leverage with a stronger cost-reset package,
- but only if the labour side accepts more concrete and quantified contributions.

---

### Proposal 3: “Hybrid structure: preserve most sites, but define a narrow fallback list”
A third compromise is a **hybrid settlement**:

- most plants are protected under a site guarantee,
- but a small, jointly reviewed subset of sites or lines is reserved for structural adjustment if needed,
- with closure/reduction allowed only after:
  - independent validation,
  - failed mitigation measures,
  - and a formal co-determination process.

This bridges the gap by:
- avoiding an all-or-nothing closure battle,
- while keeping a limited adjustment path available for the most problematic units.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What specific annual recurring savings must be achieved, plant by plant, for you to accept a no-closure package as sufficient?**

### To Works Council / Union
**What quantified concessions on working time, wage moderation, capacity reduction, and voluntary exits would you accept in exchange for a legally binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff commitment?**

### To Regional State Government
**What concrete legal or political support are you prepared to provide to make a no-closure settlement enforceable, beyond public opposition to closures?**

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Under what verified conditions would you accept a site-preserving package as economically preferable to closure, even if it means lower short-term flexibility for owners?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum risk-adjusted return and margin recovery would make you support a negotiated site-preserving deal instead of a closure-based restructuring?**

### To Independent Expert
**What objective plant-by-plant threshold would you use to determine when a site-preserving package becomes economically equivalent to closure, after all transition and conflict costs are included?**
