The expert’s assessment is useful, and it confirms something we have said from the start: **closures are not the only economically credible route**. That matters.

## Where I agree
I agree that the company needs a **real, durable cost reset**.  
I agree that a package built only on slogans, symbolic sacrifice, or vague promises would be worthless.  
And I agree that a site-preserving solution has to be **hard-edged**, not soft.

So let me be very clear: **we are not defending the status quo.**  
We are prepared to contribute to a serious restructuring.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the inference that because the company needs a deep reset, it must therefore accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a break with the collective bargaining framework.

That leap is not justified by the expert’s statement. The expert said a site-preserving package can be credible **if it combines multiple levers** and is strong enough. That is exactly the space we are prepared to work in.

## What we can support
We have already signaled the direction of travel. A viable package can include:

- **capacity reductions without closures**
- **voluntary departures and retirement instruments**
- **working-time adjustments**
- **targeted productivity measures**
- **temporary labour-cost contributions**
- **bonuses or other variable pay components foregone**
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **site and job guarantees**

That is real restructuring. It is not a free ride.

## What we cannot accept
I need to state the boundary again because management keeps blurring it:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No German plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No “temporary” arrangement that becomes permanent insecurity for workers**

If the company insists that the only way forward is to keep closures and forced layoffs on the table, then it is choosing conflict, not settlement.

## On management’s argument
Management says it needs “maximum flexibility.” What that often means in practice is maximum flexibility for management, minimum security for workers. That is not a balanced restructuring model.

If you want employees to contribute, then the burden must be shared fairly:
- management and owners must contribute too,
- the cost must not fall only on wages and jobs,
- and the company must commit to the German sites as part of the deal.

## On the ownership side
To the family holding and institutional investors: you are right that the solution must be durable and credible. But you are wrong if you think credibility requires threats of closures and forced layoffs to discipline the workforce.

A workforce that is asked to make sacrifices will do so if it sees:
1. **jobs are protected**
2. **sites remain viable**
3. **management and owners also give something up**
4. **the company is investing in the future, not just cutting into the present**

That is the only basis on which we can take a difficult package back to members and defend it.

## On the state government
The state government’s line against closures and forced redundancies is important, but it cannot remain a political declaration only. It must become part of a binding settlement that prevents closures from being used as leverage after employees have already conceded.

## The real test
If management wants proof that site-preserving measures can deliver savings, then table the numbers. Show us:
- the scale of the required savings,
- the timeline,
- the volume reductions,
- and how much management and capital are contributing.

If the package is genuinely balanced, it can be negotiated. If it is just a dressed-up version of “workers pay, everyone else waits,” then it will be resisted.

## Bottom line
We are ready for a hard restructuring.  
We are **not** ready for a restructuring that destroys trust, breaks the wage framework, and uses plant closures as a bargaining club.

If management wants an agreement, it must come back with a **shared-sacrifice package that protects jobs, protects sites, and treats the workforce as a partner rather than a cost problem**.  
If not, the workforce is prepared to escalate further.
