## Core impasse

The central impasse is no longer over **whether restructuring is needed**. All sides now accept that it must be hard and durable.

The impasse is over **the adjustment instrument and enforceability**:

- **Labour / works council / state** want a **binding site-preserving settlement** with no compulsory redundancies and no closures as routine tools.
- **Management / investors / family holding** want **credible structural flexibility**, including closure as a fallback if savings cannot be achieved otherwise.
- The **expert** says both routes can be viable, but only if the site-preserving route is hard enough and the closure route survives risk-adjusted comparison.

So the deadlock is essentially:

> **Can the company secure enough durable savings and capacity reduction through a binding no-closure package, or must closure remain available as the final adjustment mechanism?**

A second, related impasse is **trust and verification**:
- Labour does not trust closure “availability” not to become leverage or a disguised closure list.
- Management does not trust a no-closure promise not to lock in underperformance.
- Everyone is waiting for plant-by-plant quantified proof rather than broad claims.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Binding site-preserving package with a capped review mechanism
A possible bridge is a **binding no-closure package through 2030** paired with:
- quantified recurring savings targets,
- plant-by-plant capacity measures,
- voluntary exits and retirement pathways,
- working-time and productivity commitments,
- management/owner contributions,
- and **narrow, jointly reviewed correction mechanisms** if targets are missed.

This would not be an open-ended closure option. It would be a **failure-triggered review** limited to predefined remedies, with co-determination involvement and strict evidentiary standards.

### 2) Hybrid network restructuring with site protection for viable plants
A second compromise is a **hybrid package**:
- strategically important plants get site protection and investment commitments,
- weaker plants are subjected to deeper capacity rationalization,
- but still via negotiated measures first: model reassignment, shift reductions, consolidation, voluntary exits, and productivity measures.

This allows management to claim structural change while giving labour a real site-preserving framework for the core network.

### 3) Verification-first settlement with deferred instrument choice
A third route is to agree now on:
- the savings target,
- the plant-by-plant data set,
- the risk-adjusted NPV methodology,
- and the independent expert’s mandate,

while deferring any final decision on closures until after a jointly verified comparison.

This does not solve the conflict immediately, but it may stop the argument over instruments until the factual basis is complete.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact plant-by-plant savings gap remains after the strongest feasible negotiated measures, and what evidence shows that closures outperform those measures once strike risk, legal delay, and supplier disruption are included?**

### To the Works Council
**Which specific, quantified capacity and cost measures are you prepared to accept plant by plant so that a no-closure settlement is not just a principle but a complete savings plan?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What temporary concessions are you prepared to put on the table that are durable enough to count as real recurring savings, while still staying within your red lines?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a legally binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff framework with enforcement mechanisms, even if that requires you to support tougher operational concessions from labour?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Under what quantified risk-adjusted conditions would you support a hard site-preserving settlement over closure, and what minimum evidence would you require before treating closure as unavoidable?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum recurring annual savings and capacity reduction would make you support a site-preserving package, and how will you weight conflict risk versus nominal savings in your NPV judgment?**

### To the Independent Expert
**What plant-level variables and threshold assumptions would most change your conclusion between a hard site-preserving package and a closure-based restructuring?**

## Procedural next step

The most workable next step is to ask the independent expert to produce a **plant-by-plant comparison table** with:
- required savings,
- negotiated-package savings,
- closure savings,
- transition cost,
- strike/legal risk,
- supplier spillovers,
- and risk-adjusted NPV.

That would convert the dispute from a clash of red lines into a decision against agreed criteria.
