I hear the positions, and I will be equally clear from the ownership side.

First: **I agree with the core diagnosis**. This business cannot keep carrying a structurally uncompetitive cost base and excess capacity in Germany and still expect to fund the next investment cycle. If we fail to restore margins, we will damage the company’s long-term value and, with it, the family holding’s core asset.

Second: **I also agree that a durable settlement is preferable to a drawn-out conflict**. Prolonged confrontation would destroy value, weaken trust, and risk the very investment capacity the company needs.

But I need to be explicit where I do **not** share the direction that is being implied here:

### On the union and works council position
I respect the refusal to accept social demolition. However, **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no framework changes” is not a credible response to a structural problem**.

If the cost and capacity problem is real, then the company must have room to make structural changes. I am not interested in preserving every existing arrangement simply because it is historic. That would be a managed decline, not stewardship.

At the same time, I do **not** want a blunt, value-destructive closure strategy either. A rushed shutdown that triggers a prolonged industrial conflict would be worse than the problem it is meant to solve. So I share the demand for a serious, negotiated solution — but it must be a solution that actually changes the economics.

### On management’s line
Management is right that this cannot be solved by cosmetics. But I would caution against framing closures as the default or as a negotiating hammer. **If plant closure becomes the first reflex, you risk destroying labor peace, political support, and ultimately shareholder value.**

So my expectation is this:
- any restructuring must be **credible on cost**
- it must be **durable, not temporary**
- it must be **deliverable under co-determination and the special statute**
- and it must preserve the company’s ability to invest in future products

### On the regional state government
I understand and respect the state’s role as an anchor shareholder and regional guardian. But I would also say clearly: **the state cannot veto economic reality indefinitely**. If the company’s competitiveness is not restored, the regional base will suffer anyway, only more slowly and more painfully.

That said, I agree that the state’s support matters, and any viable package has to be politically and socially sustainable. A solution that tears through the regional economy would be self-defeating.

### What I can support
From the family holding’s perspective, I can support a restructuring package if it meets three tests:

1. **Real, measurable cost reduction and capacity adjustment**  
   Not just promises, not just short-term sacrifice.

2. **A negotiated, socially durable implementation path**  
   Preferably through working-time, voluntary instruments, redeployment, and other site-preserving measures where they genuinely achieve the required savings.

3. **A credible commitment to future investment and competitiveness**  
   The workforce cannot be asked to contribute if the company does not also commit to products, technology, and a viable industrial future in Germany.

### What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a plan that permanently impairs the company’s value,
- a conflict that drags on without resolution,
- or a settlement that pretends there is no need for structural adjustment.

And I want to be very clear on ownership control: **any path that threatens to erode the governance position of the controlling shareholders is off the table**. The family holding is prepared to back a tough restructuring, but not one that creates a precedent of governance drift or weakens the ownership structure.

### My conclusion
So my position is not “closure at any cost,” and it is not “no change.” It is this:

**I support a hard but negotiated restructuring that restores competitiveness, protects long-term value, and avoids unnecessary instability.**  
If there is an alternative to closures that genuinely achieves the same durable savings, I am open to it.  
If there is not, then the company will need to make difficult decisions — but they must be handled in a way that preserves the enterprise, not just one side’s red lines.

Bring forward a package that can prove those economics. If it exists, I will support it. If it does not, we should be honest about that now rather than pretend otherwise.
