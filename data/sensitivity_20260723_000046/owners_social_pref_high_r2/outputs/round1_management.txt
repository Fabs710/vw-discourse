I hear the core message from both the Works Council and the union: you want restructuring, but without closures, without compulsory redundancies, and without any erosion of the job-security framework.

Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

**I agree that the company needs a future in Germany. I do not agree that we can preserve the current structure and still restore competitiveness.**  
That is the fundamental issue.

We are facing a structural cost problem, not a temporary fluctuation. German factory costs are materially above target, European demand is shrinking, and competitors are moving faster and cheaper. If we do not reduce **both cost and capacity** in a durable way, then we will not reach the brand’s return-on-sales target, and we will not free up the funds needed for product and technology investment through 2030.

So I need to say plainly:

- **A cosmetic restructuring is not acceptable.**
- **A solution that avoids any hard capacity reduction is not acceptable.**
- **A package that depends only on voluntary measures, working-time tweaks, or temporary contributions is not enough.**

You say “no plant closures.” I understand that this is your position, but I cannot pretend that every site is automatically protected if it is structurally uncompetitive. The board and owners will not support a plan that leaves us with the same cost base and the same overcapacity while expecting the market to improve by itself.

At the same time, let me be equally direct on what I am prepared to discuss:

- I am open to **alternative structural measures** if they deliver the same durable savings as closures.
- I am open to **capacity reductions without closures** if they are real and measurable.
- I am open to a **negotiated labour-cost contribution**, including on pay and flexibility, if it is substantial and lasting.
- I am open to **investment commitments** for the German sites, but only if the cost base is being fixed at the same time.

What I am **not** open to is a settlement that simply buys time, preserves all capacity, and delays the correction until the crisis becomes worse.

On compulsory redundancies: I am not using that as the first instrument, but I will not rule out workforce reduction as part of a serious restructuring if that is what a durable solution requires. We need a structure that works in 2030, not just a promise that avoids difficult decisions today.

On your call for management and owner sacrifices: I accept that all stakeholders must contribute. But management or shareholder contributions cannot replace the need for real operational restructuring. Those are supporting elements, not the core solution.

So my position is this:

**We need a binding package that delivers a structural reduction in cost and capacity, a genuine labour-cost contribution, and a credible path to restore margin and competitiveness.**  
If the answer is only “no closures, no layoffs, no framework changes,” then there is no solution to the crisis we are actually in.

I would rather negotiate a tough, durable settlement now than drift into months of confrontation. But if necessary, management will continue to press for the restructuring that the company’s future requires.
