### Core impasse
The impasse is now **not about whether restructuring is needed**, but about **which instruments are allowed to achieve it**:

1. **Management, investors, and the controlling family** want a restructuring that is demonstrably strong enough to restore competitiveness, and they do not want to rule out closures or forced workforce reductions if the numbers require them.
2. **Works Council and union** want a binding, site-preserving settlement with no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and a job/site guarantee through 2030.
3. **The state government** rejects closures and compulsory redundancies as primary tools, but also wants a credible economic reset.
4. **The expert** has narrowed the dispute: site-preserving restructuring is possible in principle, but only if it is hard enough to deliver a real permanent cost reset. The open question is whether that threshold can be met without closures and compulsory redundancies.

So the core conflict is: **Can the required savings be achieved through a sufficiently hard negotiated package without using the instruments labor refuses?**  
No shared quantified answer to that question has yet been tabled.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to test

#### 1) Hard site-preserving package with conditional protection
A negotiated package could be structured as:
- no immediate closures,
- no compulsory redundancies in the first phase,
- substantial voluntary exits, retirement pathways, and redeployment,
- binding capacity reductions and productivity measures,
- labor-cost moderation for a fixed period,
- and **conditional site protection** through 2030, with review milestones and pre-agreed triggers if targets are missed.

**Purpose:** This may bridge labor’s demand for site security and management’s demand for real economic correction.

---

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring with plant-specific differentiation
Instead of a blanket rule across all sites:
- classify plants into **retain / reshape / review** categories,
- preserve strategically important sites with investment and specialization,
- allow deeper reductions at weaker or more redundant sites through shift cuts, model consolidation, or conversion,
- postpone closure decisions pending quantified plant-by-plant evidence.

**Purpose:** This may bridge the gap between labor’s no-closure red line and management’s demand for structural change by making decisions conditional on plant-specific economics.

---

#### 3) Socially protected adjustment package
A third route would combine:
- workforce reduction primarily through voluntary means,
- a jointly financed adjustment fund,
- guaranteed retraining and internal mobility,
- management and owner contributions,
- and a temporary wage/bonus moderation package tied to investment commitments.

**Purpose:** This may create a shared-sacrifice model that reduces conflict while still producing measurable recurring savings.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management / Group & Brand Management
**What exact recurring savings gap must be closed, by when, and what minimum combination of voluntary exits, capacity reduction, and labor-cost moderation would you accept as sufficient without closures?**

#### To Works Council
**What quantified savings, headcount reduction, and capacity reduction are you prepared to accept in a no-closure package so that the company’s competitiveness is genuinely restored?**

#### To Industrial Union
**If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what binding and enforceable concessions on working time, pay, and staffing flexibility will the union accept to make the package economically credible?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What concrete support or enforcement mechanism will the state provide to make a no-closure settlement binding and credible, rather than only politically declared?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Would the family support a time-limited no-closure guarantee if it is paired with hard milestones, plant-specific review clauses, and measurable cost targets?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum evidence would you require to conclude that a negotiated site-preserving package is superior to a closure-based one on a risk-adjusted NPV basis?**

#### To the Independent Expert
**Can you specify, in plant-by-plant terms, what threshold of recurring annual savings and capacity reduction would make a site-preserving package economically equivalent to closure once transition and conflict risk are included?**
