We are prepared to be pragmatic, but not to endorse a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base.

## Where we agree
- Management is right that the current situation requires a **durable structural reset**, not a symbolic one.
- The workforce side is right that a **prolonged strike or confrontation destroys value**.
- The family holding and state government are right that the solution must be **deliverable under this governance structure**, not just neat on paper.
- The expert’s point is important: **closures are not automatically value-maximizing** once transition costs, legal delay, and industrial conflict are included.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” is not enough by itself
The Works Council and union keep saying site-preserving restructuring can work. That may be true — **but only if it is hard enough to cut recurring cost materially**.

What we cannot support is a package that:
- avoids closures,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- avoids meaningful labour-cost correction,
- and still claims competitiveness will improve.

That would be a cosmetic settlement, and we will not back it.

### 2) Management cannot use closures as a bluff if it has not proven the economics
I agree with the expert that a closure-led strategy may destroy value if it triggers blockage and delay. So management should not present closures as the default answer unless it can show, plant by plant, that they are economically superior **after** transition risk.

We want the numbers, not rhetoric.

### 3) An unconditional site guarantee through 2030 is too rigid
I understand why labour wants that. But from an investor perspective, an absolute guarantee that removes future adjustment options is not acceptable if the market deteriorates further or a site remains structurally uncompetitive.

A guarantee can be part of a deal, but only if it is tied to:
- real savings,
- measurable milestones,
- and a credible economic logic.

## What would move us
We would support a negotiated settlement if it demonstrates all of the following:
- **material permanent cost reduction**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **measurable workforce reduction, preferably voluntary but substantial**
- **labor-cost moderation that is durable, not symbolic**
- **binding productivity improvements**
- **credible investment commitments for the German footprint**
- **clear milestones and enforcement**

That is the minimum for investor support.

## What we will not support
- A deal that merely postpones restructuring.
- A package that protects all sites and all guarantees while margins remain weak.
- Any settlement that leaves the company structurally impaired.
- A prolonged conflict that burns cash and market confidence.

## Our position on the current choices
If a **site-preserving package** can genuinely deliver the same recurring savings as a closure-based one, we would prefer it because it reduces execution risk. But the burden of proof is on those proposing it.

If it cannot, then the company must be honest that harder measures are required — though even then, they must be executed in a way that minimizes value destruction.

### Bottom line
We are not siding with “closures at any cost.”  
We are siding with **the option that most credibly restores competitive margins on a durable basis**.

Right now, the discussion still lacks the quantified comparison we need.  
So our demand is straightforward:

[SUMMON: independent restructuring finance assessment comparing, plant by plant, the NPV of closure-based, site-preserving, and hybrid packages including transition costs, strike risk, legal delay, and operational spillovers.]

Until that comparison is on the table, we will not endorse any package that is either:
- too weak to fix the cost base, or
- too destructive to preserve value.

We are open to a hard compromise, but only one that genuinely restores competitiveness.
