I hear the concerns, but I need to be equally clear: **the status quo is not an option**.

We are facing a structural competitiveness crisis, not a temporary downturn. German factory costs are materially above target, capacity is too high for the market we are actually selling into, and our return on sales is below what we need to fund the next generation of vehicles and technology. If we do not fix the cost base now, we will simply erode value, investment capacity, and ultimately more jobs over time.

So let me respond directly:

- **No plant closures in Germany**: I cannot accept that as a blanket principle. If a site is structurally uncompetitive and capacity has to come out, then we must be prepared to make hard decisions, including closures if necessary.  
- **No compulsory redundancies**: I understand why that is the preferred position, but I am not going to lock management into a promise that prevents us from achieving a durable restructuring. Voluntary instruments can play a role, but they will not by themselves solve the scale of the overcapacity problem.
- **Job-security guarantees through 2030**: That is not credible if the company is to restore competitiveness. A long guarantee that preserves all sites and all jobs regardless of economics would simply freeze the crisis into the future.

Where I do agree is this: **we need a negotiated solution, and employees cannot be the only ones contributing**. I am open to a package that includes:
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- and targeted investment commitments tied to the restructuring.

But let me be explicit about the condition: **I need real, structural savings**. Not symbolic cuts. Not a cosmetic deal. Not a short-term deferral that leaves the cost base uncompetitive and forces us back into crisis in two years.

To the Works Council and the union: if you want me to rule out closures and compulsory reductions, then bring me an alternative that delivers the same economic effect — materially lower fixed costs, lower capacity, and a durable improvement in margins. If that proposal is credible, I will engage seriously. If it is just a demand to preserve everything and ask management to “make it work,” then it is not a solution.

We are prepared to negotiate hard. But the benchmark is clear: **the company must regain competitiveness and free up funds for future products and technology to 2030**. Anything less is not acceptable.
