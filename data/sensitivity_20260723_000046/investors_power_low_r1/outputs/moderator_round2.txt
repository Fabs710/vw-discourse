The discourse has plateaued because the parties are no longer disagreeing only on **means**; they are disagreeing on the **decision rule** for restructuring.

## Core impasse
The central deadlock is this:

- **Management / investors / family holding** want restructuring that can deliver **material fixed-cost and capacity reduction**, and they do **not** want to rule out closures or compulsory reductions in advance.
- **Works council / union** insist that any viable deal must preserve **all German sites**, avoid compulsory redundancies, and keep employee protections intact.
- **Regional state government** rejects closures and forced layoffs as the default, but is also asking for a solution that is **quantitatively credible** and governance-feasible.

So the impasse is not simply “how much pain,” but:
1. **Whether closures remain an acceptable instrument at all**
2. **Whether a no-closure package can be proven equivalent in savings**
3. **Whether any temporary employee sacrifice is conditional on binding, enforceable site/job guarantees**

---

## Facilitation proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: “Two-track restructuring with a trigger mechanism”
A mediated compromise could separate the plan into two stages:

- **Track A:** immediate non-closure measures
  - working-time adjustments
  - voluntary exits / early retirement
  - redeployment and qualification
  - temporary pay/bonus restraint
  - product allocation changes
  - overhead and process efficiencies

- **Track B:** a narrowly defined contingency track
  - closures are **not activated immediately**
  - instead, they become a **last-resort trigger** only if pre-agreed financial and operational milestones are not met by a fixed date

This could bridge:
- labour’s demand for a no-closure first approach
- management’s demand for a credible fallback
- investors’ demand for enforceable economics

---

### Proposal 2: “Binding site security in exchange for measurable labor-cost and capacity commitments”
A second compromise position would be:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No immediate closures**
- **Binding site guarantees for a defined period**
- In return:
  - a time-limited employee contribution
  - quantified productivity and flexibility gains
  - defined capacity reduction through redeployment, shifts, and footprint optimization
  - management and owner contributions to the same burden-sharing package

This bridges:
- the works council / union’s need for enforceable guarantees
- management’s insistence on real savings
- the state’s preference for regional stability
- the family’s interest in preserving enterprise value

---

### Proposal 3: “Site-by-site equivalence test”
A third compromise would replace the absolute closure/no-closure standoff with a structured test:

For each site, parties jointly assess:
- economic contribution
- required savings
- achievable non-closure savings
- implementation risk
- investment needs
- social impact

Then:
- if a site can be stabilized through non-closure measures, it stays open
- if not, the parties must negotiate an alternative intervention package before any closure decision

This may help because it shifts the conflict from ideology to **site-specific evidence** and may reduce the current all-or-nothing posture.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**Question:** What quantified level of savings would you accept as proof that a no-closure package is economically equivalent to selective closures, and what verification mechanism would you accept to test that claim?

### 2) Industrial Union
**Question:** Would you support a time-limited employee-contribution package if it were paired with binding no-compulsory-redundancy rules and enforceable investment commitments, even if management keeps a narrowly defined fallback closure option on the table?

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**Question:** Can you specify, by site and function, the minimum cost and capacity reductions required so the other parties can test whether non-closure options are sufficient, rather than arguing in the abstract?

### 4) Regional State Government
**Question:** What concrete leverage will the state use to turn political rejection of closures into an enforceable negotiating framework, rather than a non-binding preference?

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**Question:** Will you commit to backing a negotiated no-closure package if it meets a jointly defined savings threshold, even if it limits closure as a fallback bargaining tool?

### 6) Institutional Investors
**Question:** What combination of savings, implementation certainty, and timeline would make you regard a no-closure restructuring as sufficiently “economically real” to support?
