### Core impasse

The plateau is centered on **one structural disagreement**:

- **Management / investors / owners / expert**: a no-closure package is only acceptable if it can prove equivalent savings and capacity reduction; otherwise closures or selective footprint reduction must remain available.
- **Works council / union / state**: closures and compulsory redundancies are treated as unacceptable premises, and employee concessions are only discussable if they come with binding site and job security.
- **Result**: both sides demand **proof before movement**, but they define the admissible solution space differently. One side insists on keeping closures in reserve; the other insists that keeping closures in reserve is itself the problem.

So the deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **whether the restructuring must preserve all German sites**, and if so, **how much burden each side is willing to take to make that possible**.

---

### Faciliator compromise positions to test

#### 1) Time-bound no-closure trial with hard triggers
A possible bridge is a **time-limited no-closure package** for a defined period, paired with:
- quantified savings targets,
- quarterly verification,
- a jointly agreed independent review,
- and **predefined triggers** that reopen the site-structure question only if the targets are missed.

This would not be a permanent closure veto, and it would not be an immediate closure mandate. It would convert the dispute into a **measured test period**.

**Purpose:** give the workforce binding protection now, while giving management and investors a structured off-ramp if the package underperforms.

---

#### 2) Mixed burden-sharing package with asymmetrical security
A second bridge is a package in which:
- employees provide temporary concessions,
- management and owners provide visible financial contributions,
- the state supports financing / investment conditions where lawful,
- and in return there is a **binding no-compulsory-redundancies commitment**, while the status of plant closures is handled through a **separate, delayed review** rather than an immediate fallback option.

This would separate two questions:
1. immediate job security, and
2. long-term footprint optimization.

**Purpose:** reduce the sense that concessions are being traded against an active closure threat, while not removing all strategic flexibility from ownership.

---

#### 3) Site-by-site preservation test with a “keep-open” default
A third bridge is a **site-by-site restructuring test** with a default assumption that each site should be kept open unless a joint analysis shows that:
- the site cannot reach a viable break-even trajectory,
- no feasible product / volume allocation exists,
- and no realistic productivity or labor-cost package can close the gap.

This would not ban closures absolutely, but it would make closure the **exception requiring proof**, not the leverage point.

**Purpose:** align the analytical framework with the labor-side demand for preservation, while still allowing an evidence-based exception.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If an independent site-by-site review shows that one specific German plant cannot be made viable even after substantial concessions, what exact evidence threshold would you require before you would revise your absolute no-closure position?

#### To the Industrial Union
What minimum mix of temporary labor concessions and binding employer commitments would you consider sufficient to justify entering a time-limited no-closure trial?

#### To Group & Brand Management
Would you accept a fixed-duration no-closure trial with independent monitoring and predefined review triggers, provided the package includes measurable employee contributions and management/owner contributions?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete instrument, within your legal and political powers, would you use to convert your “no closure as default” stance into a binding framework rather than a political preference?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to publicly support a time-bound no-closure settlement if and only if it includes quantified savings, governance safeguards, and a trigger-based review mechanism?

#### To Institutional Investors
Would you treat a no-closure package as economically acceptable if it delivered verified recurring savings within a fixed time window, even if it left some higher-cost capacity in place temporarily?

#### To the Independent Industrial Expert
What measurable indicators would you use to determine, within 6–12 months, whether a no-closure package is actually converging toward equivalent economics?

#### To the Plant-Level Financial Expert
Which two or three site-level metrics would most clearly distinguish a genuinely viable no-closure path from a delay strategy?
