## To institutional investors
You keep talking about “economically real.” I agree with the standard. I reject your conclusion.

A deal is not economically real just because it cuts headcount or closes a site. It is economically real if it:
- can be implemented,
- preserves industrial peace,
- protects execution capability,
- secures future products,
- and avoids a conflict that burns cash and destroys trust.

Your assumption seems to be that closure automatically equals credibility. That is too simplistic. If closure triggers strikes, governance blockage, reputational damage, and long-term mistrust, then the supposed savings are not savings — they are a delayed cost explosion.

## On the “two-track” idea
I reject the trigger-closure logic outright.

A two-track model sounds tidy on paper, but in practice it means:
- Track A: ask workers for concessions now
- Track B: keep the threat of closure alive if we refuse to concede enough

That is not a fair negotiation architecture. That is a coercive framework dressed up as prudence.

If the company truly wants a settlement, then the restructuring must be designed from the outset on the basis that:
- German plants remain open,
- jobs are protected,
- and the savings come from operational and negotiated measures, not site destruction.

## On the “site-by-site equivalence test”
I would support a site-by-site review **only if its purpose is to identify what is needed to keep each site open**, not to produce a closure recommendation disguised as analysis.

If the test is genuinely:
- what savings are needed,
- what measures can deliver them,
- how can employment be protected,

then yes, that is useful.

If the test is really:
- which sites can be closed first,

then no, I reject it.

This remains my minimum:

- **No compulsory redundancies**

- **Strengthened co-determination in implementation**
- **Concrete investment and product commitments for German sites**

- voluntary early retirement,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal mobility,
- training and reskilling,
- efficiency and overhead reduction.

But again: **no closures and no forced layoffs**. That is not a fallback; that is the boundary.
