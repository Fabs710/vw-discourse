## Plant-level financial assessment

I will answer the questions directed to the **plant-level restructuring / industrial accounting** area and stay within that mandate.

### Short conclusion
On the facts provided, a **no-closure package can be credible only if the footprint problem is moderate and the employee contribution is unusually large and durable**. If the German footprint is structurally oversized, **selective adjustment with at least one plant closure or major footprint reduction is more likely to deliver the full recurring savings**.

## 2) Comparison of the two restructuring paths

### A. No-closure restructuring
This would typically use:
- voluntary exits / early retirement
- working-time reduction
- temporary wage or bonus restraint
- redeployment
- product and shift reallocation
- overhead reduction

#### Likely recurring savings
For a large German automotive footprint, a serious no-closure package often produces something like:

- **€300m to €800m per year** in a substantial but not extreme case
- **€500m to €1.2bn per year** only if concessions are unusually deep and the company can materially reallocate volumes

#### One-off costs
- severance / early retirement
- retraining / redeployment
- transition disruption
- possible productivity loss during implementation

These are usually **material but lower** than plant closure costs.

#### Implementation risk
- **Moderate**
- lower industrial disruption initially
- but risk that savings prove insufficient if the footprint remains too large

---

### B. Selective adjustment / selective closure
This would include:
- closure of one or more sites or major footprint reductions
- concentration of output on fewer plants
- removal of duplicate fixed cost
- larger workforce reduction, including compulsory measures if unavoidable

#### Likely recurring savings
This is generally the **stronger fixed-cost reset**.

Indicative recurring savings range:
- **€700m to €1.5bn+ per year**, depending on plant size and how much overhead is removed

#### One-off costs
- severance
- shutdown costs
- relocation
- write-downs / impairment
- disruption to supply chain and output

These are usually **high**.

#### Implementation risk
- **High**
- because of industrial conflict, governance blockage, and transition disruption

---

## 3) Does no-closure equal selective adjustment?
### Professional answer: usually no
A no-closure package can narrow the gap, sometimes materially. But if the structural issue is **underutilized capacity plus high fixed German overhead**, then a no-closure solution is often **not fully equivalent**.

The reason is accounting logic:
- closures remove **fixed cost structurally**
- no-closure measures mostly reduce **labor cost, time cost, and overhead**, but leave much of the fixed site burden in place

So if the problem is mainly **labor cost and flexibility**, no-closure may work.
If the problem is mainly **excess footprint**, selective adjustment usually wins.

## 5) Site-level decision rule
A site is economically defensible under a no-closure package if, after measures:

1. it can achieve **positive contribution margin**
2. fixed costs are absorbed at competitive utilization
3. it does not require recurring subsidy
4. there is a credible product pipeline
5. unit costs are in line with group target

A site becomes a likely adjustment candidate if:
- utilization stays low
- break-even volume remains above realistic demand
- it needs ongoing cross-subsidy
- or there is no credible future product allocation

That is the core plant-level test.

---

## 6) Practical conclusion for the decision
### If the company needs a partial repair:
A **no-closure restructuring may be sufficient**, especially if management can secure:
- sizable voluntary exits
- working-time flexibility
- temporary labor cost relief
- real product reallocation

### If the company needs a full structural reset:
A **selective-adjustment plan is more likely to deliver it**.

So my judgment is:

- **No-closure is not implausible**
- **No-closure is unlikely to be fully equivalent if overcapacity is deep**
- **Selective adjustment is more likely to produce the strongest recurring savings**
- **No-closure has lower implementation risk initially, but higher risk of insufficient savings**
