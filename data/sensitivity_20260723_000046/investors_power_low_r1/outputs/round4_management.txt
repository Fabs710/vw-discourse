I will not do that.

## My position on the compromise tracks

### 1) Time-bound no-closure trial with hard triggers
I can consider a **time-bound trial** only if it is genuinely capable of proving equivalence.

But I will not accept a fake trial that merely locks in costs while postponing hard choices. The trial must include:
- explicit savings targets,
- quarterly verification,
- and a predefined outcome if the targets are not met.

If that sounds uncomfortable, it should. The company cannot afford open-ended ambiguity.

### 2) Mixed burden-sharing with asymmetrical security
This is closer to something workable.

I can support:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and no compulsory redundancies **as part of a serious package**.

But I cannot accept “asymmetrical security” if it means workers give up pay while management keeps every strategic option and the company never achieves the needed cost reset.

Security must be matched by structural savings. Otherwise it is just a subsidy for the status quo.

### 3) Site-by-site preservation test with a keep-open default
I support a site-by-site test **only if it is evidence-based and not ideologically predetermined**.

But I reject the idea that the default must be “keep every site open unless closure is proven.” That sounds fair, but if the starting assumption prevents the business from taking necessary action, then it is not a test — it is a closure ban in disguise.

The correct default is this:
- every site is reviewed on hard economic criteria,
- every alternative is tested,
- and the final decision is based on whether the site can contribute to a competitive future.

Not on whether it is politically uncomfortable to change it.

## What management can accept
I will put our position plainly:

We can accept:
- substantial capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- qualification and reskilling,
- productivity and overhead reductions,
- targeted investment commitments,
- and a negotiated settlement with real structural impact.

We can also consider:
- a time-limited no-closure period,
- if and only if the package is quantified and binding,
- and if it includes a real off-ramp when the economics fail.

## What management cannot accept
We cannot accept:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees that freeze the footprint through 2030 regardless of economics,
- or a package that preserves the current cost base and calls that restructuring.

That is the line.

## Final point
I am not interested in symbolic compromise. I am interested in a durable restructuring that restores competitiveness, lifts margins, and frees funds for the products and technology we need by 2030.

If the other parties can bring a no-closure package that truly does that, I will support it.  
If they cannot, then closures and selective capacity adjustment must remain available.

That is not a threat. It is the reality of the company’s situation.
