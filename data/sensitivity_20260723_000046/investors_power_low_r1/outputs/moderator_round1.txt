### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the current situation is **not sustainable** and that a **negotiated restructuring** is preferable to unmanaged conflict.
- There is broad support for the idea that any solution should include **some combination of**:
  - labour-cost relief,
  - capacity adjustment,
  - working-time instruments,
  - voluntary exits/redeployment,
  - and some form of future investment commitment.
- There is also shared recognition that a settlement must be **durable**, **implementable**, and **governance-compatible**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management and institutional investors will not rule them out if needed.  
  - The family holding is open to them only as a last resort.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour side and state reject them.  
  - Management and investors will not commit to excluding them.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Labour side seeks binding guarantees.  
  - Management and investors say such guarantees are not credible if competitiveness must be restored.
- **Burden sharing:**  
  - Labour side insists on shared sacrifice by owners/management.  
  - Management and investors emphasize the need for real structural savings and are not accepting a deal that mainly shifts costs to capital.
- **Governance constraints:**  
  - The state and family holding emphasize that any solution must pass through the company’s governance structures, which limits unilateral action.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve the required cost reduction** without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- **Whether “no closures” can be replaced by equivalent capacity and cost reductions** through redeployment, product allocation, and productivity measures.
- **How much employee contribution is acceptable** in exchange for what level of binding security.
- **What investment commitments are concrete enough** to justify concessions now.
- **How to balance social peace versus speed and depth of restructuring**.
- **Which sites or functions, if any, would bear disproportionate adjustment burdens** if a no-closure solution is not feasible.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **testable package options**. Each side should come with:
- one **minimum acceptable package**,
- one **fallback package**,
- and, where relevant, the **specific economic or governance condition** under which it would be acceptable.

A useful next step would be to compare two scenarios side by side:
1. a **no-closure restructuring** and
2. a **selective capacity-adjustment restructuring**,

showing the expected effects on cost, employment, investment, and implementation risk.
