I will be blunt: I agree with the diagnosis that the company needs a real restructuring, but I do **not** agree that German plant closures or compulsory redundancies are an acceptable baseline for that restructuring.

## To management
You are right on one point: the status quo is not sustainable. But you are still treating closures as the default negotiating leverage, and I reject that approach. For this company, in this region, that is not a neutral business tool — it is a decision that would damage the regional industrial base, intensify conflict, and likely increase implementation risk.

What I need from management is not another general statement that “real savings” are required. I need:
- a **quantified no-closure package**,
- a **site-by-site cost and capacity analysis**,
- and a credible explanation of why non-closure measures cannot meet the target if that is your claim.

Until that is shown, I will not accept the argument that closures are economically necessary.

## To the Works Council and Union
I agree with you that:
- doing nothing is not an option,
- the burden cannot fall only on employees,
- and industrial peace is economically valuable.

I also agree that **forced redundancies should be avoided** and that any employee contribution must buy something real.

Where I do **not** fully follow you is on absolutism. I understand the insistence on protecting every site, but I need a package that is not only principled, but also demonstrably capable of restoring competitiveness. If you want the state to stand behind a no-closure solution, then that solution must be presented in **hard numbers**, not just in good intentions.

That said, I am aligned with your red line on **no compulsory redundancies** and your demand for **binding commitments** in exchange for concessions.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. But I do not accept the assumption that a closure-led route is the only way to discipline the restructuring. Governance stewardship also means preserving the company’s social legitimacy and regional anchor function.

I will support a serious restructuring that:
- protects core German sites,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- and delivers measurable savings through negotiated operational measures.

I will **not** support a plan that makes closure the easy answer before all other options have been seriously tested.

## To institutional investors
You are right that the economics must be real. I do not dispute that. But “real” does not automatically mean closure-heavy. It means:
- durable savings,
- credible implementation,
- manageable conflict risk,
- and a stable path to future investment.

A plan that looks efficient on paper but triggers strikes, board deadlock, and lasting damage to trust is not risk-adjusted value creation. It is a fragile plan.

## My position
From the state’s perspective, the minimum acceptable framework is still:

- **No German plant closures as the starting point**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the default instrument**
- **Binding protection of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **Capacity adjustment through negotiated, reversible measures**
- **Temporary employee contribution only if matched by management and owner contributions**
- **Concrete investment and product commitments for German sites**
- **A durable settlement that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor**

## What could move me
A no-closure solution could gain my support if it proves, concretely, that it can:
- deliver material fixed-cost reduction,
- reduce capacity enough to restore competitiveness,
- preserve the German industrial base,
- and remain governable under our special statute and co-determination structure.

That is the standard.

## What will not move me
- a closure-led plan presented as inevitability,
- a threat-based negotiation that uses German sites as bargaining chips,
- or a “compromise” that really just shifts the cost burden onto workers and regions.

## My call for the next round
Bring me:
1. a **quantified no-closure scenario**,
2. a **quantified selective-adjustment scenario**,
3. the **jobs, cost, investment, and implementation risk** implications of each,
4. and a clear burden-sharing proposal from management and owners.

If the no-closure package can truly do the job, I will support it. If not, then we are still not talking about a real restructuring proposal — we are talking about pressure.
