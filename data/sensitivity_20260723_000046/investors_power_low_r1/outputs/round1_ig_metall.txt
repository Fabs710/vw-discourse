The union’s position is clear and I support the core line of the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no one-sided burden shifting onto employees**.

Let me be equally clear about where we stand.

Management is describing this as a competitiveness problem, but it is not acceptable to solve it by making the workforce pay for every strategic mistake, every delay in transformation, and every gap in product planning. **If the company needs restructuring, then the sacrifices must be shared fairly**. That means: owners, management, and capital must also contribute, not only employees.

We have already signaled that we are prepared to negotiate on a serious package that can include:
- temporary labour-cost concessions,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary exit options,
- and an employee financial contribution,

**but only if there is binding security for jobs and sites**.

What is not on the table:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or the dismantling of the collective wage framework as a one-way cut.

If management wants a durable settlement, it must bring forward a credible alternative that preserves the German sites, protects the workforce, and shows that leadership and shareholders are also taking pain. Otherwise, we will defend our position with the means available to us, including industrial action.

So the message is simple: **we are ready for a negotiated restructuring, but not for a restructuring that sacrifices workers while others remain untouched.**
