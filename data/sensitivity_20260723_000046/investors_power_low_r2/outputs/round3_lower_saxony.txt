I will be equally direct.

## My position
I **cannot support plant closures in Germany** or any restructuring that **removes jobs from the state on a large scale**. I also will not support any path that weakens the state’s **co-determination and veto role** under the special statute. Those are not bargaining chips for me; they are the basis of my legitimacy as an anchor shareholder and political actor.

At the same time, I do **not** deny the economic problem. The company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one. But that restructuring must preserve the company as a regional industrial anchor, not hollow it out.

---

## To management
I agree with you on one point: the current cost and capacity situation is not sustainable.

Where I disagree is your repeated attempt to turn that diagnosis into a closure-first conclusion. You have **not** demonstrated that closures are the only serious path. You have argued that they are available, and that alternatives may be insufficient — that is not the same thing.

What I need from management is:
- a **plant-by-plant business case**
- a quantified savings target
- a serious non-closure package
- and proof that the package is durable, not just temporary optics

If you cannot show that non-closure measures are exhausted, then closure is not “necessary”; it is simply preferred from your side. I will not accept that.

I do agree that voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, productivity improvements, and internal reallocation are not enough if used superficially. But that is an argument for a **serious negotiated package**, not for starting with closures.

---

## To the Works Council and the union
I support your red lines on:
- no plant closures in Germany
- no compulsory redundancies
- no dismantling of co-determination
- no one-sided burden shifting onto workers

On that, we are aligned.

But I will also be frank: a viable compromise cannot be only a refusal. If the company is to remain strong in the long term, then the workforce side must be prepared to discuss **real, measurable contributions** that do not destroy jobs or sites.

That means I would expect a package built around:
- temporary working-time reductions
- shift and utilization changes
- voluntary exit schemes only
- productivity gains
- internal redeployment
- fair but limited employee contributions
- and binding investment commitments for all German sites

What I will **not** accept is any attempt to frame closures as a legitimate fallback that can be reintroduced later. If you want my support, the settlement must keep German plants open and protect jobs. That is the line.

---

## To the family holding
You are right that a long conflict destroys value. You are also right that the company needs a durable, governable solution.

But I do not accept the implication that “durable” means tolerating closures as a normal tool. This company is not just a portfolio asset; it is an industrial center with regional responsibility, and its governance reflects that.

If the family wants stability, it should support:
- a negotiated restructuring
- material ownership and management contribution
- investment commitments
- and a solution that protects the company’s industrial base in Germany

I can support difficult measures. I cannot support a course that treats German plants as disposable.

---

## To investors
You are right that the final package must be economically credible and durable. I do not dispute that.

But credibility is not only about cost cuts. It is also about:
- labour peace
- social legitimacy
- industrial capability
- and the company’s ability to operate without years of conflict

A “hard” solution that triggers strikes, destroys trust, and weakens the company’s German base is not a successful solution. It may look strong on a spreadsheet and fail in reality.

So yes, I accept the need for a material savings package. I do **not** accept the conclusion that closures must be part of it.

---

## On the facilitator’s options

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with closure freeze and quantified trigger mechanism
This is the only option that has any realistic chance of being acceptable from my side, **but only if the freeze is genuine**.

That means:
- no plant closures during the negotiation window
- no compulsory redundancies
- no backdoor closure through outsourcing or silent dismantling
- and no pre-authorised closure trigger that makes the process a staged ultimatum

I could support an **independent audit** of the savings effect, provided the purpose is to test whether a non-closure restructuring works — not to manufacture a closure decision afterward.

### 2) Site-by-site package with protected core sites and conditional flexibility elsewhere
I am cautious to the point of rejecting this as currently framed.

Why? Because it risks creating a two-tier system where some German sites are treated as “adjustable,” which quickly becomes a softer form of closure pressure. For me, that is not an acceptable basis if it undermines site security and regional stability.

If there is to be differentiation, it must not become a pathway to shutting plants in my state.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding burden-sharing rules
This is the direction I can work with, **if** “shared sacrifice” is real.

That means:
- management restraint
- visible ownership contribution
- fair employee contribution
- and no plant closures or compulsory redundancies

If the burden is shared, the package can be justified. If the workforce is asked to give up jobs and guarantees while everyone else keeps flexibility, then it is not shared sacrifice — it is one-sided damage.

---

## What would move me
I will not move because the pressure continues or because the numbers are repeated more often.

I would move only if I see:
1. a restructuring package that **preserves German plants**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. real employee contributions that are limited and fair
4. visible management and ownership restraint
5. binding investment for the German footprint
6. a credible path to restoring competitiveness **without gutting regional employment**

That is the only middle ground I can support.

---

## My bottom line
I am prepared to back a tough restructuring.  
I am **not** prepared to back closures in Germany, mass job loss, or any weakening of the state’s co-determination and veto role.

So let me be unambiguous: if the choice is between competitiveness and the regional industrial base, I will not sacrifice the latter. The task is to find a restructuring that achieves both. If management cannot, then it has not made the case for the measures it is demanding.
