The union shares the core line of the Works Council: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. On those points there is no room for ambiguity.

We also agree that this crisis cannot be solved by putting the full burden on the workforce while management and owners are left untouched. If the company wants the employees to contribute, then that contribution must be **fair, limited, and matched by sacrifices from management, shareholders, and the wider governance side**.

Where we are prepared to engage:
- temporary or negotiated capacity reductions
- working-time measures
- waiving or reducing certain payments
- a solidarity or transformation contribution from employees
- flexibility arrangements that help bridge the crisis

But our willingness to discuss these measures depends on clear conditions:
1. **No forced layoffs**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
4. **A binding future and investment commitment for the German sites**
5. **Shared sacrifice**, not one-sided wage dumping

Let me be equally clear on what will not work:
- “Competitiveness” arguments that translate into workers paying for all past strategic mistakes
- open-ended insecurity for the workforce
- using restructuring as a backdoor to dismantle collectively bargained standards
- demands for pain from labour without any meaningful contribution from owners or management

If management wants a durable settlement, it needs to present a package that protects jobs and sites and spreads the burden fairly. If instead the plan remains centered on closures, dismissals, and wage cuts imposed from above, then the union will use its full bargaining power accordingly. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce is prepared to defend its interests.
