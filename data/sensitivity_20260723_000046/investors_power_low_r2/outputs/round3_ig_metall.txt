I will respond plainly.

## To the Works Council
We are aligned on the essentials, and I support your refusal of plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

But I want to sharpen one point: I do **not** want the discussion to become a pure defense of the status quo in which the company does nothing except preserve every existing arrangement. That would be a mistake, and it would eventually weaken our position.

So yes:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no erosion of the collective wage framework.

But we must also be able to say what a serious non-destructive adjustment looks like. That means concrete, measurable contributions on the labour side **without** job loss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- shift and utilisation changes,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary programmes only,
- internal redeployment,
- and a limited, fair employee contribution.

What I will not accept is any package that treats “voluntary” as a mask for job cuts or makes temporary sacrifice into permanent erosion. If management wants our agreement, the burden has to be shared visibly and fairly.

## To management
Your reply still misses the central point.

You keep trying to make **closures and compulsory redundancies** the benchmark for seriousness. That is exactly where we disagree.

You say we have not shown an equivalent package. But the burden is not on labour to prove a miracle while you hold the closure threat over the table. The burden is on management to present a **credible non-destructive restructuring** first.

And let me be explicit: we will not bargain as if plant closures are an acceptable fallback. They are not.

What I can discuss is a package built from:
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions through utilisation and shift changes,
- voluntary exits,
- internal transfers,
- productivity gains,
- management restraint,
- and investment commitments.

What I cannot discuss is a package that starts from “closures remain on the table” and uses that to force wage concessions. That is not shared sacrifice; that is pressure.

If you believe the economics require a durable cost reset, then show a plant-by-plant and measure-by-measure plan using non-destructive tools first. Until then, your closure logic remains a choice, not a necessity.

## To the regional state government
I welcome your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But I need to be just as direct with you: if you mean that position, then you need to use your leverage **now**, not later.

We do not need polite support. We need a blocking position against any closure-first strategy and against any attempt to turn “controlled adjustment” into a backdoor plant exit.

I agree with you that the company needs a real adjustment path. But I will not accept a “limited footprint adjustment” if that means opening the door to closures in Germany. On that point, I am clear: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies**.

If the state wants to help secure a settlement, then it should push for:
- management and owner contribution,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a negotiated working-time and cost package that preserves every German site.

## To the controlling family holding
You say you want stability and value preservation. Then support a settlement that preserves the industrial base rather than forcing a showdown.

Where I agree with you:
- prolonged conflict destroys value,
- a cosmetic deal is not enough,
- and the company does need a material adjustment.

Where I disagree:
- you cannot treat closures as an acceptable “structural” answer if there are other ways to achieve savings,
- and you cannot expect workers to pay for competitiveness with their jobs and long-term security.

If the family wants a durable solution, it should back:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- visible management restraint,
- and a genuine shared-sacrifice package.

That is what stability looks like. Not a closure ultimatum.

## To investors
You ask whether we would support a package that avoids closures and compulsory redundancies if it delivers equivalent or near-equivalent durable savings.

The answer is: **yes, provided the savings are real, durable, and shared fairly**.

That is not a retreat. That is exactly our position.

What we will support:
- real labour-cost contributions,
- temporary flexibility,
- productivity and utilization improvements,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- investment commitments,
- and a binding, independent verification of savings.

What we will **not** support:
- token concessions dressed up as restructuring,
- one-sided labour sacrifice,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or any solution that quietly reintroduces closures by another name.

If the company can demonstrate a durable cost reset **without** plant closures and **without** compulsory redundancies, then that is the kind of settlement we can negotiate. If not, then do not pretend closures are the only serious answer while refusing to test alternatives properly.

## On the facilitator options

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with closure freeze and audited trigger mechanism
This is the only path that comes close to respecting the red lines.

But let me be very clear: the freeze must be **real**.
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no outsourcing as a substitute for closures,
- no unilateral management action during the test phase.

I can support a time-limited restructuring window if it is used to verify whether non-destructive measures deliver the savings needed.

I will **not** support a trigger mechanism that pre-authorises closure as the fallback. If closure is built in as the punishment for not reaching some target, then the threat remains alive and the exercise is poisoned from the start.

### 2) Site-by-site package with protected core sites and conditional flexibility elsewhere
I do **not** support this framework as stated.

It creates a two-tier logic: some plants become “adjustable,” which is just a softer word for disposable. That is not a path I can endorse.

If the aim is to preserve the company’s long-term industrial base, then every German site needs binding protection against closure and forced layoffs. Otherwise, the package is simply an organized weakening of the workforce through the back door.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding burden-sharing rules
This is the direction we can work with — **if** shared sacrifice really means shared sacrifice.

That requires:
- management restraint,
- ownership responsibility,
- fair employee contributions,
- and no job destruction as the price of adjustment.

If the only “sacrifice” from labour is insecurity, then it is not shared. It is imposed.

5. and delivers a quantified, durable savings effect verified independently.

## Final point
Do not confuse our willingness to discuss real cost measures with willingness to accept closures. We will not.

We are prepared to help build a serious restructuring, but it must be one that protects jobs, sites, and the collective wage agreement.

If management and ownership are serious about a durable future, they should come back with a **non-closure, no-forced-redundancy package** that is materially strong enough to work. If they do not, the workforce will resist.
