We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not on the standards for a solution.

## Where we agree
- The status quo is not acceptable.
- The company needs **material, durable structural savings**.
- A purely cosmetic settlement would destroy value.
- Labour peace matters, because a prolonged strike cycle would damage cash flow, output, and the share price.
- Investment in future products and technology must be protected.

## Where we disagree

### To management
You are right that the economics must change. But from an investor perspective, **“closures or nothing” is not an acceptable framing** if it triggers prolonged confrontation and value destruction.

What matters to us is not the instrument; it is whether the package **actually restores margins on a durable basis**. If closures are the only way to get there, then the burden is on you to prove that no alternative combination can deliver the same economics with less disruption.

So far, I do not see evidence that you have made that case conclusively.

What I would need to see is:
- quantified savings targets,
- a credible path to return-on-sales improvement,
- durable labour-cost reduction,
- and a package that is bankable, not just threatening.

If the final result leaves the cost base uncompetitive, we will not support it — even if it avoids strikes in the short term.

### To the Works Council and union
Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable, but it is **not sufficient**.

From a capital-markets view, a solution that preserves every site and guarantee while only delivering token concessions is just a slower route to the same problem: weak margins, lower valuation, and eventually deeper pain.

We do **not** support one-sided labour sacrifice. But we also do **not** support a structure that leaves the company with:
- unchanged overcapacity,
- unchanged labour costs,
- and an unchanged competitive deficit.

If you want to avoid closures and layoffs, then the substitute must be **material**, not symbolic. Temporary flexibility and voluntary exits may help, but only if they produce real structural change.

### To the regional state and family holding
We recognize the legitimacy of governance stability and regional responsibility. We also recognize that a destructive conflict would impair long-term value.

But “shared sacrifice” cannot become a euphemism for avoiding the hard parts. If the final deal protects every legacy feature and asks too little of the cost base, the market will treat it as delay, not restructuring.

## Our threshold
We will back **any** outcome that credibly and durably restores competitiveness.

That means:
- competitive margins,
- visible and lasting cost reduction,
- no open-ended conflict,
- and sufficient deal certainty to make the savings real.

We will **not** back:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that leaves structural overcapacity in place,
- or a compromise that trades short-term peace for long-term underperformance.

## Bottom line
We are not attached to closures as such.  
We are attached to **risk-adjusted equity value**.

If the parties can produce a package that materially improves economics without destructive conflict, we are open. If not, we will judge the outcome inadequate, regardless of how balanced it sounds politically.
