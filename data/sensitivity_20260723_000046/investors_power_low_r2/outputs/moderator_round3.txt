Core impasse identified:
- **Management / investors / family holding** require a **material, durable reduction in cost and capacity**, and keep **closures / footprint change** as a legitimate tool if non-destructive measures do not close the gap.
- **Works Council / union** and, to a large extent, the **regional state government** treat **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as non-negotiable red lines**.
- The central deadlock is therefore not “whether restructuring is needed,” but **whether restructuring may include site exits or forced job losses as fallback instruments** if a non-closure package falls short.

Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap:

### Proposal 1: Time-limited “non-closure test phase” with hard protections
- A defined negotiation and implementation window, e.g. 6–12 months.
- During that window:
  - **no plant closures**
  - **no compulsory redundancies**
  - **no outsourcing used as a substitute for closure**
- In return, labour side implements a quantified package of:
  - working-time reductions
  - shift/utilisation changes
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - productivity measures
- An **independent auditor** measures whether the package closes the savings gap.
- At the end, parties review only **verified numbers**, not assumptions.

Why it may bridge:
- Gives labour genuine protection during testing.
- Gives management/investors a serious, auditable attempt to prove whether non-closure measures work.

### Proposal 2: Conditional burden-sharing package with a tiered fallback
- All sides commit to a **shared-sacrifice package** with explicit contributions from:
  - management cost cuts
  - ownership support/investment
  - labour-side flexibility
- If the audited savings fall short, the parties do **not automatically close plants**.
- Instead, they enter a second-stage negotiation over a **pre-agreed menu of less destructive structural measures**, such as:
  - deeper working-time adjustments
  - additional redeployment
  - revised product allocation
  - further capacity rationalisation through attrition and voluntary exits
- Closure is deferred as a **last-resort option**, not a pre-set trigger.

Why it may bridge:
- Preserves labour’s red lines in the first stage.
- Gives management/investors a pathway to stronger measures if the first package proves insufficient.

### Proposal 3: Site protection for core sites + conditional industrial commitments for the rest
- Identify a limited number of **protected core sites** with binding commitments on:
  - no closures
  - no compulsory redundancies
  - investment and product commitments
- For other sites, agree to:
  - stronger flexibility
  - productivity targets
  - utilisation changes
  - voluntary restructuring tools
- This is paired with a **single company-wide savings target** and independent monitoring.

Why it may bridge:
- Could satisfy management/investors’ need for footprint adjustment while protecting politically and socially critical sites.
- Could give the state government a defensible “anchor footprint” to support.

Pointed question to each stakeholder:

### To management
Can you specify the **minimum quantified savings gap** that must be closed for you to rule out closures as economically unnecessary, and accept a **time-limited no-closure test phase**?

### To the Works Council
Would you accept a **strictly time-limited audit phase** in which labour provides measurable non-destructive concessions, on the condition that closures and compulsory redundancies remain frozen during that period?

### To the trade union
What exact **labour-side measures and scale** would you bind into a package that is large enough to be credible to investors, while still preserving the no-layoff principle?

### To the regional state government
Will you commit to using your governance influence to support a **structured audit-and-test process**, even if that process requires stronger operational changes short of immediate plant closure?

### To the controlling family holding
Are you prepared to publicly back a **non-closure first process** with a genuine independent review, even if it means postponing the option of site exits unless the audited savings gap remains after testing?

### To investors
What level of **audited, durable savings and margin improvement** would you regard as sufficient to support a settlement that avoids closures and compulsory redundancies?
