I will be very clear: **I still cannot support a restructuring that starts from plant closures in Germany or that shifts the adjustment burden mainly onto the workforce.** That remains my line.

At the same time, I am not ignoring the economic problem. The company does need a credible, durable competitiveness package. But credibility does **not** automatically mean closures, and it does **not** mean that job security and co-determination are treated as disposable.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: the status quo is not viable. The company needs real savings, a stronger margin base, and investment capacity for future products.

Where I disagree is your insistence that **closures and compulsory redundancies remain the default answer** if alternatives are not immediately proven to your satisfaction. That is too narrow, too destructive, and too early in the process.

You have not shown that every non-closure path has been exhausted. Before talking about shutting German sites, I expect a serious package with:
- meaningful working-time adjustments,
- internal reallocation and utilization changes,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- productivity improvements,
- management restraint,
- and a fair employee contribution.

If you cannot present that in concrete form, then the closure argument is premature. As a shareholder with a blocking minority and responsibility for the regional economy, I will not treat closures as the first resort.

## To the Works Council and the union
I support your core red lines:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no dismantling of co-determination**
- **no one-sided burden shifting**

That said, I will also be frank with you: a settlement that simply says “no” without offering a credible adjustment path will not hold. The company does need change, and it does need some form of workforce contribution if we want to protect the sites long term.

What I can support is a **limited, negotiated, and fairly shared contribution** if it comes with:
- binding site guarantees,
- clear investment commitments,
- protection against operational closures,
- and a plan that preserves the company as a regional anchor.

What I cannot support is a situation where “fair contribution” means only symbolic measures while the company’s competitiveness continues to erode. That would eventually threaten the very jobs you are defending.

## To the family holding
I agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value and trust. I also agree that the company cannot be locked into an uncompetitive structure forever.

But I do not accept any suggestion that German plants are simply expendable if the spreadsheet says so. This company is not a generic portfolio asset. It is an industrial anchor with regional responsibility, long-term labour relations, and a governance structure that explicitly reflects that reality.

If the family wants stability, it should back a settlement that:
- preserves German industrial capacity,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- avoids closure-first logic,
- and shares the burden visibly across management, ownership, and labour.

## To investors
You are right that the numbers must add up. I do not dispute that. But the numbers do **not** add up if the company triggers a strike cycle, destroys trust, or weakens its regional industrial base in the process.

A settlement is only credible if it is:
- economically material,
- socially sustainable,
- and politically and industrially durable.

A “hard” package that is not governable will still fail. So I would urge you not to reduce credibility to short-term cost cutting alone.

## Where I stand
My position is unchanged but practical:

### I will support:
- a negotiated restructuring
- material but fair employee contributions
- working-time and capacity flexibility
- productivity and internal reallocation measures
- management restraint
- investment commitments for German plants
- solutions that preserve jobs, sites, and co-determination

### I will not support:
- plant closures in Germany as a first or default solution
- compulsory redundancies
- a restructuring that guts regional employment
- any attempt to sideline the state’s veto and co-determination role
- one-sided labour sacrifice disguised as “competitiveness”

## The key point
If management wants my support, it must bring forward a plan that proves restructuring can be done **without sacrificing the regional industrial base**. If it cannot, then I will use the leverage I have.

So: I am open to a tough compromise, but only one that genuinely protects German sites, jobs, and long-term industrial capacity. That is the condition. Without it, I do not move.
