I will answer directly.

## To management
Your “minimum quantified savings target” question is not the real issue. The real issue is that you are still trying to make **closures and forced layoffs the benchmark solution** and then asking labour to prove a miracle.

We do **not** accept that framing.

If you want a serious restructuring without crossing our red lines, then you must first accept these principles:
- **every German plant stays open**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **co-determination remains intact**
- **job-security guarantees remain in force**

Only on that basis can we discuss how the savings are achieved.

You ask what measures we would accept. I have already named them:
- working-time reductions
- shift and utilisation adjustments
- voluntary exits only
- internal transfers and redeployment
- productivity improvements
- fair employee contribution, if and only if management and ownership also contribute visibly
- binding investment and product commitments for all German sites

What I will **not** do is negotiate as if plant closure were an acceptable pressure point. It is not. It is a red line.

And let me be equally clear: if your cost gap can only be closed by destroying sites and throwing people out, then your restructuring model is socially unacceptable and governance must stop it.

## To the trade union
We are aligned on the essentials, and I welcome that. But I want to sharpen one point: I do **not** want us to be pushed into validating a “limited, time-limited contribution” that becomes a permanent erosion of standards.

So yes, we can discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- temporary moderation elements,
- productivity changes,
- voluntary programmes.

But only if there are hard guarantees:
- **no layoffs**
- **no closures**
- **no weakening of collective protection**
- **full reversal once the crisis phase ends**

If those guarantees are not there, then any contribution becomes a one-way street.

## To the regional state government
Your words are correct, but words are not enough.

If you truly reject closures and compulsory redundancies, then use the leverage you have:
- in the supervisory board,
- through the blocking minority,
- and through political pressure on management and the family holding.

You ask what conditions would make us accept a limited footprint adjustment. My answer is simple: **none**. Not if it means a closed plant in Germany or forced redundancies. We are not negotiating the disappearance of industrial sites.

What I expect from the state is not “managed reduction.” I expect **defence of German jobs and plants**.

## To the controlling family holding
You say you want stability and value preservation. Then back a real industrial settlement that preserves the workforce and the sites.

You cannot claim to protect long-term value while accepting a plan that tears up trust, destroys morale, and breaks the job-security framework. That is not stability; it is short-term pressure that stores up conflict for the future.

If the family wants credibility, it should commit to:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- and real investment in the German footprint.

Anything less tells the workforce that long-standing assurances can be discarded whenever profitability becomes inconvenient.

## To investors
You ask whether we would support a package that avoids closures and compulsory redundancies if an independent assessment shows “equivalent or near-equivalent” durable savings.

The answer is: **yes, in principle — but only if the outcome truly preserves jobs, sites, and co-determination, and if the savings are real, not cosmetic.**

That is not a retreat from our position. It is exactly the point:
- if there is a credible alternative path, use it;
- if there is not, then do not claim closures are the only truth while refusing to test non-destructive options seriously.

But let me be very clear: an “independent assessment” cannot become a backdoor to legitimising closures after the workforce has already been told to accept them. It must be used to verify whether non-closure solutions can work **without violating our red lines**. If they can’t, then the plan is not acceptable.

## On the facilitator’s three options

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with closure freeze and audited trigger mechanism
This is the only direction that even begins to respect labour’s position, **but only if the freeze is real and comprehensive**:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no disguised closure through outsourcing or silent attrition,
- no unilateral management action.

I would support a testing phase for alternatives.  
I would **not** support any trigger mechanism that makes closure a pre-authorised outcome. Once you build in closure as the fallback, the threat remains and trust is destroyed.

### 2) Site-by-site package with protected core sites and conditional flexibility elsewhere
No. I reject this as stated.

Why? Because it normalises the idea that some German plants are “adjustable” and therefore potentially disposable. That is exactly the logic we are resisting.

We are not agreeing to a two-tier system where one plant today, another plant tomorrow, and then “controlled consolidation” becomes a polite term for closure.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding burden-sharing rules
This is a better principle, but again only if the sacrifice is **not** imposed on the workforce through job loss or plant closure.

If you want fairness:
- management must cut its own costs visibly,
- ownership must accept responsibility,
- and workers must not be the only side giving up security.

But I will not call a package “shared sacrifice” if the workforce is asked to surrender the historic guarantees that protect jobs and sites.

## What I require before any real discussion moves forward
1. **Public, binding exclusion of plant closures in Germany**
2. **Public, binding exclusion of compulsory redundancies**
3. **Written preservation of co-determination and job-security protections**
4. **Concrete management and ownership contribution**
5. **Serious investment commitments for all German sites**
6. **A quantified savings proposal built entirely from non-destructive measures**

Without those six points, we are not in a negotiation; we are in a pressure campaign.

## Final word
Let me be blunt:  
You keep asking labour to prove that the company can be fixed without closures. But the burden is on management and ownership to prove that closures are truly unavoidable. So far, they have not done that.

And because co-determination gives us the power to stop plant closures and forced restructuring, you should not assume we will move because the numbers are repeated with greater intensity.

We will defend:
- every German plant,
- every job we can protect,
- and the collective framework that keeps this company socially legitimate.

If management wants agreement, it must come back with a real industrial plan — not a closure agenda disguised as inevitability.
