## Core impasse

The deadlock is not over whether the company needs change; all sides accept that it does.  
The impasse is over **the mechanism and burden-sharing of that change**:

1. **Management / investors / family** want a restructuring that produces **material, durable savings**, and they see **footprint reduction, including possible closures and staffing cuts**, as potentially necessary.
2. **Works council / union / regional state** reject **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and any weakening of co-determination/job security** as a starting point.
3. The resulting gap is this:  
   **Can the company achieve sufficiently large structural savings without closures and compulsory layoffs, and if so, who guarantees that the savings are real and durable?**

A second, related impasse is **credibility and sequencing**: labor side wants closures off the table first; management side wants alternatives proven first. Neither side trusts the other’s order of operations.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with a “closure freeze” and quantified trigger mechanism
**Proposal:**  
Agree to a fixed restructuring window during which **no plant closures or compulsory redundancies occur**, while the parties negotiate a package of quantified measures: working-time reductions, voluntary exits, internal transfers, productivity changes, and investment commitments.  
At the end of the window, an **independent jointly accepted audit** tests whether the savings target has been met. If not, the parties return to the table with pre-agreed escalation steps.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
- Gives labor the immediate non-closure assurance it wants.  
- Gives management/investors a credible test of whether alternatives are sufficient.  
- Reduces the argument over “trust me” versus “prove it.”

---

### 2) Site-by-site package with protected core sites and conditional flexibility elsewhere
**Proposal:**  
Classify German sites into:
- **protected core sites** with binding job and investment guarantees, and
- **sites subject to controlled adjustment** where capacity shifts, redeployment, or voluntary consolidation can be negotiated.

Any footprint change would require:
- prior exhaustion of voluntary measures,
- a jointly reviewed business case,
- and a commitment to avoid compulsory redundancies where possible.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
- Preserves a visible industrial base and addresses state/family concerns about regional responsibility.  
- Gives management some room for structural adjustment without an across-the-board closure taboo.  
- Creates a more granular solution than “all plants protected” versus “closures allowed.”

---

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding burden-sharing rules
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a package where all constituencies contribute under explicit rules:
- employees: temporary working-time reduction, moderated pay elements, voluntary exit programs;
- management: top-level pay restraint, headcount reduction at corporate levels, governance commitments;
- owners: investment commitment and possibly balance-sheet support;
- state/family/investors: endorsement of a multi-year industrial plan.

In exchange, there would be:
- no compulsory redundancies during the agreement period,
- no unilateral closure decisions,
- and a binding review point if market conditions or savings fall short.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
- Responds to the labor demand that sacrifices not fall only on workers.  
- Responds to capital’s demand that the package be materially real.  
- Makes the burden-sharing explicit rather than symbolic.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What is the minimum quantified savings target you require, and which non-closure measures would you accept as sufficient to reach it without compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Works Council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what exact measurable concessions are you prepared to accept so the company can still achieve a durable cost reset?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Will you commit to a specific, time-limited package of working-time, pay, and productivity measures if it is paired with binding no-layoff guarantees and independent verification of the savings?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete leverage are you prepared to use if management refuses a non-closure restructuring path, and what exact conditions would make you accept a limited footprint adjustment?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you support a settlement that includes visible ownership and management sacrifice, even if it falls short of the most aggressive footprint reduction option?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you endorse a restructuring package that avoids closures and compulsory redundancies if an independent assessment shows it delivers equivalent or near-equivalent durable savings and margin improvement?**
