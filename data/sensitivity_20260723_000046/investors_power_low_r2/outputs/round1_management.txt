We hear the position of the Works Council and the union clearly, but I need to be equally clear in return:

**A settlement that preserves every German plant, rejects compulsory redundancies entirely, and leaves the cost base essentially intact is not a viable outcome.**  
It would not restore competitiveness, would not close the gap to our rivals, and would not create the financial room we need to invest in future products and technology through 2030.

Let me respond point by point.

### Where we agree
- **We also want a durable future for the German sites.**
- **We also do not want disruption for its own sake.**
- **We also understand that any solution must be negotiated and ultimately sustainable.**
- **We agree that the company needs investment and product commitment in Germany.**

### Where we disagree
#### 1) No plant closures, no job reductions: that is not a credible restructuring
The current cost structure is too high. Capacity in Europe is too large relative to demand, and the company cannot carry every site unchanged while the market shrinks and competition intensifies.

If we pretend otherwise, we are not protecting jobs — we are postponing a much harder crisis.  
A cosmetic agreement that avoids hard choices would simply weaken the company further.

#### 2) “Shared sacrifice” cannot mean workers alone make a token contribution
I am open to a fair labour-cost contribution, working-time flexibility, and negotiated measures. But these must be **material enough to change the economics**. Small, reversible tweaks will not be enough.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, then we need a package that delivers:
- real capacity reduction,
- a genuine reduction in labour costs,
- and durable structural savings.

#### 3) The company cannot be asked to keep all historic guarantees unchanged
Those guarantees were built for a different market environment. The EV transition, China shift, and competitive pressure have changed the basis on which the business must operate.

I am not prepared to lock the company into a structure that makes it permanently uncompetitive.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am not insisting that the only route is immediate plant closure. I am saying the outcome must produce **equivalent structural savings**. That can come from a combination of measures, but only if the result is economically comparable.

That means we can discuss:
- capacity cuts across sites,
- productivity and working-time adjustments,
- pay and benefit moderation,
- a meaningful employee contribution,
- selective restructuring of production footprints,
- and investment commitments tied to a credible savings package.

### What I cannot accept
- A deal with **no closures, no meaningful capacity reduction, and no substantial labour-cost contribution**
- Any arrangement that leaves us with the same overcapacity and the same cost base
- A delay tactic that pushes the problem into the next cycle while margins remain under pressure

### Bottom line
We are willing to negotiate the **form** of restructuring.  
We are **not** willing to negotiate away the need for a **deep, durable cost and capacity correction**.

If the employee side can bring forward a package that genuinely delivers structural savings and restores medium-term margins, then we can talk. If the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no sacrifices,” then there is no solution to this crisis.
