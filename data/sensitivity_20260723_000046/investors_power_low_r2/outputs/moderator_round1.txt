### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides acknowledge that the current situation is unsustainable and that some form of restructuring is necessary.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs a credible long-term future in Germany, including continued investment in products and technology.
- Most parties accept that cost and capacity issues are real and that a purely cosmetic solution would not work.
- There is some openness to negotiated measures such as working-time adjustments, temporary flexibility, productivity improvements, and fair employee contributions.
- Several parties agree that any settlement must be durable and economically credible, not just politically acceptable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures; management says some form of structural capacity reduction is necessary and closures remain on the table; shareholders are split between opposing closure-first logic but not endorsing a no-reduction position.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour representatives and the state reject them; management has not accepted a firm exclusion; investors want savings but are less focused on the instrument than on the outcome.
- **Scope of savings:** Management and investors want material, durable cost reduction; labour and the state insist this must not come mainly from workers or be achieved through one-sided cuts.
- **Protection of guarantees:** Labour and the state want binding job, site, and co-determination protections; management is unwilling to preserve all historic commitments unchanged.
- **Form of restructuring:** The dispute remains whether savings can be achieved through negotiated alternatives or whether closure/footprint changes are required to make the economics work.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- How to achieve meaningful cost and capacity reduction **without** plant closures or compulsory redundancies.
- What level of employee contribution would be considered “fair” and “material” enough by management and investors.
- Whether management and ownership/governance will make visible contributions of their own, and if so in what form.
- How to balance short-term labour peace against the need for structural change.
- What binding investment and future-product commitments can realistically be offered in exchange for concessions.
- How to define a restructuring package that is both socially acceptable and financially sufficient.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package elements**: quantify savings targets, define possible alternatives to closures, and identify what each side can credibly concede.
- Test whether a **combined solution** is possible: working-time measures, productivity gains, voluntary exits, selective footprint changes, and governance/management contributions.
- Require each side to state its **minimum acceptable conditions** and the **maximum concession** it can make.
- Focus the discussion on whether there is a **single economically credible package** that can avoid closures and compulsory redundancies while still meeting competitiveness goals.
