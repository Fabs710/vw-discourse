We agree on the diagnosis: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic truce. We also agree that management, owners, and employees must all contribute, and that prolonged conflict would destroy value.

But we are not here to defend any one side’s comfort zone. We are here to back the option that most credibly restores **risk-adjusted value**. That means one thing above all: **the package must actually fix the cost base and margin profile on a fully loaded, recurring basis.**

## Where we agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the current German cost base is too high,
- cosmetic concessions are worthless,
- and a structural response is needed.

You are also right to insist on **quantified savings**, not rhetoric. We agree that the next discussion must be numbers-based.

### With the family holding
You are right that:
- a frozen cost structure is value destruction,
- the company must regain flexibility to invest,
- and a deal that only postpones pain is not acceptable.

### With the state government
You are right that:
- a destructive conflict would be costly,
- and the industrial base matters.

### With the workforce side
You are right that:
- management and owners must contribute,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, training, and voluntary exits are legitimate tools,
- and a one-sided workforce sacrifice is unstable.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” as a hard red line
We do **not** support an absolute no-closure stance.

That is not because we prefer closures for their own sake. It is because we will not back a settlement that leaves the company structurally weak just to preserve every existing site on paper. If non-closure measures truly close the gap, fine. If they do not, then refusing footprint adjustment is value-destructive.

### 2) “No forced redundancies” as an absolute
We understand the political and social resistance. But from a capital-market perspective, an absolute ban on structural workforce reduction is not credible if the economics do not work. Workforce adjustment may need to remain possible as a last resort.

### 3) Blanket job guarantees through 2030
A blanket guarantee is not credible before the economics are fixed. If the business model is still under water, pre-committing to full preservation just delays the adjustment and increases execution risk.

## What we need to see

The debate now needs a proper **fully loaded savings table**. Not broad claims, not bargaining positions, but a real comparison of:

- current fixed and variable cost by plant,
- savings from shift cuts and capacity consolidation,
- savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- savings from working-time and wage moderation,
- savings from management restraint and owner-side contribution,
- residual gap to target margin,
- and execution risk by scenario.

Without that, no one can responsibly say whether the non-closure route is enough.

## Our view on the compromise ideas

### 1) Time-limited no-closure freeze with review points
This is the most credible starting framework **if** it is hard-edged:
- a defined period,
- real quantitative milestones,
- clear capacity reduction,
- and automatic reopening if targets are missed.

We could support that only if it is not a disguised permanent guarantee.

### 2) Site preservation with capacity consolidation
This is preferable to closures **if** it actually removes enough fixed cost.
Keeping a plant legally open while cutting shifts, consolidating lines, moving volumes, and reducing headcount materially may be economically sufficient. But it has to be proven, not assumed.

### 3) Burden-sharing tied to investment and governance commitments
This is attractive only if the sacrifices are balanced:
- employees contribute,
- management restrains pay and payouts,
- owners contribute,
- investment is binding,
- and the savings are measurable.

If it is just labor giving ground while others offer promises, it is not credible.

## Our position, clearly

We will support the package that most credibly restores:
- **competitive margins**,
- **durable cost reduction**,
- **investable execution risk**,
- and **future product/investment capacity**.

That means we are open to a non-closure solution **if** it is genuinely sufficient.  
We are not open to a cosmetic deal that protects the footprint while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

## What we want next
Management should come back with a **numbers-based restructuring table**:
- required savings,
- savings by source,
- capacity reduction by site,
- labor contribution,
- management and owner contribution,
- and the margin impact.

If that table shows a non-closure package is enough, we can support it. If it does not, then the refusal to consider stronger structural measures is not a credible investment case.
