The discourse has now clearly plateaued because the parties agree on the diagnosis but disagree on the **acceptable mechanism of adjustment**.

## Core impasse
The central deadlock is this:

- **Management / family holding / investors** want a restructuring that delivers **material and durable cost and capacity reduction**, and they want the option of closures or compulsory redundancies to remain available if needed.
- **Works council / union / state government** reject closures and forced redundancies as the adjustment mechanism, and instead demand a **non-closure package** that preserves all German sites and job-security protections.

So the impasse is not over whether the company needs change. It is over:
1. **whether plant closures are a legitimate restructuring tool**,  
2. **whether compulsory redundancies can remain an option**, and  
3. **whether a non-closure package can be made numerically sufficient and enforceable**.

A second layer of impasse is procedural:
- one side demands **numbers before concessions**,
- the other demands **concessions before it will treat numbers as credible**.

---

## Concrete compromise positions that could bridge the divide

### Compromise 1: Time-limited non-closure freeze with hard review points
**Structure:**
- No German plant closures and no forced redundancies for a defined period, e.g. 18–24 months.
- In return, the workforce side accepts quantified working-time changes, voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, and temporary contributions.
- Management, owners, and investors accept restraint on payouts and compensation, plus binding investment commitments.
- A jointly agreed review mechanism measures savings, utilization, and margin improvement against milestones.

**Why this could bridge sides:**
- It gives labor and state a real no-closure window.
- It gives management and investors a defined test period instead of an open-ended prohibition.
- It reduces the “permanent guarantee” objection while avoiding immediate closure threats.

---

### Compromise 2: Site preservation with capacity consolidation across plants
**Structure:**
- All German sites remain legally open.
- Production is consolidated by removing shifts, lines, and duplicate activities.
- Headcount reduction occurs through voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and training-led mobility.
- Closure is replaced by **functional consolidation** inside the network.
- Savings are measured by fully loaded cost, not only wage savings.

**Why this could bridge sides:**
- It preserves the symbolic and industrial footprint that labor and the state want.
- It still allows management and investors to obtain real cost reduction.
- It shifts the argument from “close vs don’t close” to “how much capacity can be removed while keeping sites open.”

---

### Compromise 3: Burden-sharing package tied to binding investment and governance commitments
**Structure:**
- Employees accept temporary, limited contributions.
- Management accepts compensation restraint and reduced payouts.
- Owners/investors provide financial support or accept lower distributions.
- In exchange, there are binding commitments on:
  - job protection framework,
  - investment into German sites,
  - product allocation,
  - and governance safeguards.

**Why this could bridge sides:**
- It converts the dispute from a pure cost-cutting battle into a shared rescue package.
- It addresses the “workforce should not finance its own displacement” concern.
- It gives investors and management a credible path if the savings are numerically sufficient.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after you model a fully quantified non-closure package, and which specific cost items still require closures or compulsory redundancies to close it?**

### To the works council
**What level of quantified capacity reduction, labor contribution, and mobility flexibility would you accept as sufficient to replace closures while still preserving site viability?**

### To the union
**Are you prepared to accept temporary, measurable workforce concessions if they are matched by binding management/owner contributions and enforceable investment commitments?**

### To the state government
**Will you support a time-limited restructuring framework that preserves all German sites initially, even if it includes hard utilization cuts and workforce adjustments, provided the package is numerically credible?**

### To the family holding
**Would you accept a no-closure period with strict performance triggers if the package demonstrably improves margins, even if it limits your ability to use closures as leverage?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum level of recurring, fully loaded savings would make a non-closure package investable for you, and would you back it if those savings are independently verified?**
