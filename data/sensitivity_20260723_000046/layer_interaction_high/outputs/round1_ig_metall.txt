We are aligned on the core line: **no plant closures, no forced redundancies, and no unilateral dismantling of the collective framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: if management’s “solution” is simply to push the costs of the crisis onto the workforce while protecting investors, owners, and top management from any pain, then there is no basis for agreement. Our members will not accept being treated as the sole adjustment variable.

Where I agree with the works council:
- **Capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, training, and voluntary exits** are legitimate tools.
- A negotiated contribution from employees can be discussed **if it is temporary, fair, and tied to binding guarantees**.
- Any restructuring must preserve **real industrial viability**, not just short-term accounting fixes.

Where I draw the line:
- **Compulsory redundancies are unacceptable.**
- **Closures of German plants are unacceptable.**
- **Open-ended insecurity is unacceptable.**
- Any attempt to cut wages, abolish guarantees, or hollow out the collective agreement **without a balanced contribution from management and owners** will be met with resistance.

Our position is straightforward:
1. **Jobs and sites must be secured.**
2. **Any labour-cost contribution must be shared and limited in time.**
3. **Management and owners must also contribute materially** — through reduced payouts, foregone distributions, management restraint, and real investment commitments.
4. **There must be a credible industrial plan** for the future product portfolio and the affected sites.

If management wants a durable settlement, it needs to come back with something more than threats of closure. We are prepared to negotiate a package of shared sacrifices, but not a one-sided austerity programme disguised as restructuring.

And to be precise: if the board or management continues to insist on plant closures or forced layoffs, then the union will not sit quietly. We have already signaled willingness to escalate. That remains on the table.
