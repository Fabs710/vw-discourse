We are hearing the right diagnosis from several sides, but not yet a credible enough solution.

From the institutional investor perspective, the standard is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins, or does it merely postpone the problem?** If it is the latter, we will not support it.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and to a point the state government that:
- the current German cost base is too high,
- the status quo is not sustainable,
- cosmetic concessions are worthless,
- and prolonged conflict would destroy value.

We also agree that:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- training and reskilling,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and management/owner restraint

can all be part of a serious restructuring.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that keeps the current footprint intact while leaving the economics broken.

That means:
- **No blank check for “no closures” if the alternative does not produce equivalent savings.**
- **No blank check for “no forced redundancies” if workforce reduction is needed to restore competitiveness.**
- **No blank check for unconditional job guarantees through 2030 if they freeze an uncompetitive structure.**

At the same time, I also do **not** support a blunt closure-first strategy if it is likely to trigger prolonged industrial conflict, political deadlock, and execution risk that wipes out the financial benefit. Value destruction from bad execution is still value destruction.

## What matters to us
We need a package that is:
1. **quantified**
2. **durable**
3. **enforceable**
4. **margin-restoring**
5. **able to reduce structural capacity and fixed cost**

That is the test.

## On the works council and union
Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable as a bargaining position, but it is not acceptable if it results in a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. We do not invest to preserve every legacy arrangement. If your alternative cannot be shown in numbers to deliver real savings, then it is not a viable answer.

So if you want to avoid closures, you need to show:
- exactly how much capacity comes out,
- exactly how much cost is removed,
- exactly how employee contributions are sized and timed,
- exactly how management and owners are contributing,
- and exactly how the margin target is reached.

Without that, your “no closures” position is just a veto on adjustment.

## On management
We support the need for hard structural action, but you need to be careful not to turn “decisive” into “self-defeating.” A closure-heavy approach that triggers a strike and legal/political blocking could destroy more value than it creates.

So management should come back with a package that proves:
- the savings are real,
- the execution risk is controlled,
- and the company can actually get to target margins without a drawn-out war.

If closures are the only route, you must show why non-closure alternatives fail numerically, not rhetorically.

## On the state government
Your insistence on preserving the regional footprint is understandable, but regional protection is not a substitute for competitiveness. If the German base remains structurally expensive, then capital will move elsewhere over time. We will not support a solution that turns an industrial restructuring into a political freeze.

## On the family holding
We agree that governance stability matters, but it does not justify preserving a structurally weak cost base. The ownership side should also be willing to show discipline:
- reduced payouts,
- management restraint,
- and clear investment commitments tied to measurable restructuring.

That said, those items do not replace operational change. They just make the deal more credible.

## Our bottom line
We will support the package that most credibly delivers **risk-adjusted value** through:
- real and durable cost reduction,
- lower structural capacity,
- competitive margins,
- and minimal execution risk.

That could be:
- a negotiated alternative with equivalent savings, if it is truly sufficient, or
- stronger structural action, if that is the only way to achieve the necessary economics.

What we will **not** support is a cosmetic compromise that avoids pain but fixes nothing.

## What we want next
Bring us a **numbers-based package**:
- required savings,
- savings by source,
- capacity reduction by site,
- employee contribution,
- management/owner contribution,
- and a credible path to competitive margins.

If that package does not exist, then the current no-closure position is not credible. And if the only closure plan is likely to provoke prolonged conflict, then it is not credible either.

We are not attached to any one instrument. We are attached to **outcomes that restore value**.
