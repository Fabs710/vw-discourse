### Core impasse
The stalemate now sits on **one central fault line**:

- **Employee side / state government:** insist that German plant closures and forced redundancies are off the table, and that any restructuring must preserve sites, co-determination, and job security.
- **Management / investors / family holding:** insist that the company must achieve **real structural savings and capacity reduction**, and that they will not pre-commit to preserving the full current footprint if the numbers do not work.

So the deadlock is not about whether change is needed. It is about **which structural levers are permissible** and **what would count as an economically equivalent alternative to closures**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to explore

#### 1) “No closures now” with a quantified trigger framework
A possible bridge is:
- **freeze on closures and forced redundancies for a defined period**,
- paired with a **binding restructuring package** that includes capacity reduction, working-time adjustments, voluntary exits, reskilling, management restraint, and owner-side contributions,
- plus **pre-agreed numerical triggers** that would reopen the question if savings or margin targets are not met by specific dates.

This could address:
- the workforce’s demand for immediate protection,
- management’s demand for enforceable economics,
- and the investors’ demand for credibility.

#### 2) Site-preservation with capacity consolidation
Another compromise is:
- **keep all German sites legally open**,
- but **consolidate production volumes, shifts, and product lines** across fewer active lines,
- with temporary idling, reduced shifts, and internal transfer of roles,
- while bargaining a larger voluntary departure and retraining program.

This would attempt to separate:
- **site retention** from
- **full utilization of every line or every current role**.

It may be the most direct way to preserve the symbolic and regional value of sites while still delivering cost reduction.

#### 3) Burden-sharing package tied to binding investment and governance commitments
A third bridge is a full-package exchange:
- temporary employee concessions,
- management compensation restraint,
- reduced payouts,
- owner-side financial contribution,
- guaranteed investment in German plants,
- and future product allocation commitments,
- in exchange for **no immediate closures and no forced redundancies**.

This would only work if the package is **fully quantified, time-limited, and enforceable**, so that each side can show visible sacrifice and visible benefit.

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure arrangement if it were paired with hard numerical triggers, governance safeguards, and a verified capacity-reduction plan?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What level of quantified savings and margin improvement would make a non-closure restructuring credible enough for your support, and how much execution risk are you willing to tolerate?**
