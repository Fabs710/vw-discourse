I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me respond as the controlling family holding, through governance.

We are not indifferent to the social and political consequences here. But we are also not willing to preside over a slow erosion of the company’s value. The family holding’s responsibility is long-term stewardship, not short-term appeasement.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on the core diagnosis: **the company’s German footprint is too expensive, and structural change is necessary**. If we simply preserve the current setup and hope for better markets, we will destroy value over time. That is not acceptable.

I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state government on one important point: **a solution that produces open conflict, permanent labor war, or reputational damage would be value-destructive**. Stability matters. The company cannot be rebuilt on the basis of a prolonged confrontation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible path is plant closure and compulsory redundancies. That may be the easiest line to announce, but it is not the same as the best long-term outcome.

Nor do I accept an unconditional demand that every German site remain open regardless of economic logic. That would simply shift the adjustment burden into the future and make the eventual correction harsher.

So I reject both extremes:
- **No blank check for closures and forced layoffs**
- **No blank check for preserving every site and every legacy guarantee**

### What must be true for us to support a settlement
For the family holding, the bar is straightforward:

1. **Real and durable competitiveness gains**
   - We need a package that materially restores margins.
   - Cosmetic savings or short-lived concessions are not enough.

2. **No value-destroying escalation**
   - A prolonged strike or deadlock that harms customers, plants, and reputation would be unacceptable.
   - Stability is part of value creation.

3. **A negotiated rather than imposed restructuring**
   - We expect management to pursue a deal, not a public confrontation.
   - The workforce should contribute, but so should management and the ownership side.

4. **Preservation of governance control**
   - We will not support any arrangement that weakens the ownership and governance structure or creates a precedent of control being undermined through crisis tactics.

### What I can support
I am open to a package that includes:
- meaningful labor-cost relief,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- targeted redeployment and retraining,
- and a credible investment commitment for the future product mix.

I am also open to site-specific solutions if they produce the required savings without destroying the industrial base.

### What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a settlement that merely postpones the crisis,
- a deal that protects all legacy structures while leaving margins unrepaired,
- or a conflict strategy that puts the company’s future at risk.

### Bottom line
To the union and works council: your insistence on labor peace and site protection is understandable, but **it cannot amount to an absolute veto on structural change**. The company has to become competitive again.

To management: your restructuring has to be credible, proportional, and negotiated. If you come to the table with an all-or-nothing closure program, you will likely create resistance that is unnecessary and damaging.

To the state government: I understand your desire to preserve the regional footprint, but the company’s long-term competitiveness cannot be subordinated to regional optics alone.

So our position is this:
- **No outcome that permanently weakens the enterprise**
- **No outcome that destroys governance stability**
- **Yes to a serious restructuring package that restores competitiveness**
- **No to symbolic politics or maximalist red lines that make a solution impossible**

If a negotiated package can achieve comparable savings with fewer social costs, that is preferable. But if not, the company still needs a real restructuring, not a promise that everything can stay as it is.
