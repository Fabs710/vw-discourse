I will be direct.

## To management
You still have not answered the central question: **why should the workforce accept closures or compulsory redundancies when there is still no demonstrated exhaustion of non-closure options?**

You keep saying “durable restructuring,” “material savings,” and “capacity reduction.” I do not dispute the need for restructuring. I dispute your assumption that the only serious route is to sacrifice plants and jobs.

What you have not shown:
- a quantified non-closure package tested against the actual cost base,
- the savings from shift reduction, working-time changes, redeployment, and voluntary exits,
- the contribution from management restraint and owner-side sacrifice,
- and the gap that would remain after those measures.

Until that is on the table, closure talk is not a plan. It is a threat.

## To the family holding
I hear your concern about value destruction and I share one part of it: a frozen cost structure is not sustainable. But your conclusion remains too aggressive and too one-sided.

You say closures may remain on the table if a non-closure package does not close the gap. Fine — but you have not demonstrated that the workforce’s preferred route is infeasible. You are treating a plant closure option as if it were simply normal governance. It is not. It would break trust, trigger conflict, and permanently damage the industrial base.

If you want value protection, then support a binding non-closure framework first, instead of using closure as leverage from the start.

## To institutional investors
Your “risk-adjusted value” language is correct in principle, but I do not accept your implication that absolute no-closure and no-forced-layoff positions are somehow unrealistic by definition.

What is unrealistic is asking the workforce to accept pain without first proving the economic necessity of that pain.

If you want credibility, then demand the numbers — not the closure. Show us:
- what fully loaded savings are required,
- what non-closure measures deliver,
- and what is still missing.

That is the only rational basis for deciding whether more drastic measures are needed. Right now, you are still allowing the burden to default onto workers.

## To the state government
I support your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. That is the correct line.

But I need to be blunt: resistance alone is not enough if management is not forced to engage seriously with an alternative. Your blocking power is real. Use it to demand a **numbers-based non-closure package** and to prevent any fait accompli.

If necessary, insist that no closure logic enters the process until:
- all non-closure savings are quantified,
- the company’s own contributions are fully specified,
- and the investment commitments are binding.

## To the union
We remain aligned on the fundamentals. I support your refusal to let “structural change” become a euphemism for job destruction.

I also agree that temporary employee contributions can be discussed — but only if they are:
- time-limited,
- fair,
- matched by management and owner sacrifice,
- and linked to binding job and site protection.

What I will not accept is a deal where workers give up pay or conditions while closures stay on the table in the background as the next step.

## On the moderator’s compromise frames

### 1) Time-limited non-closure freeze with hard review points
This is only acceptable if the “review” is not a disguised closure trigger from day one. I do **not** accept a framework where concessions are made now and closures remain a pre-authorized punishment later.

If management wants a review, then the review must measure whether the agreed non-closure package has been fully and fairly implemented — not whether the workforce has quietly accepted its own displacement.

### 2) Site preservation with capacity consolidation
This is the only direction that is genuinely discussable, because it keeps every German site open while still allowing operational adjustment.

But I want to be clear:
- site preservation is not a slogan,
- it means legally open sites with real production and employment continuity,
- and it means no compulsory redundancies.

Capacity reduction can happen through:
- shift cuts,
- line consolidation,
- reallocation of models,
- less overtime,
- voluntary exits,
- and redeployment.

That is a real restructuring. Plant closure is not the only way to reduce capacity.

### 3) Burden-sharing tied to investment and governance commitments
Yes — but only if the burden-sharing is real and symmetrical.

I would consider a temporary employee contribution **only** if:
- management compensation is restrained,
- owner-side contribution is visible,
- investment commitments are binding,
- co-determination is preserved,
- and the job-security framework remains intact.

Without those protections, “burden-sharing” becomes a one-sided wage cut.
