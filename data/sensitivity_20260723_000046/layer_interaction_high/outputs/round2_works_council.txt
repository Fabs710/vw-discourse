Let me respond directly to what has been said.

First: I appreciate that several sides now acknowledge that a **purely cosmetic fix is worthless** and that management/owners must contribute as well. That is correct. A one-sided workforce sacrifice is not a solution, and we will not sign up to that.

But I need to be equally clear: **the way management and the investor side are framing “necessary” structural change still sounds like a prepared justification for closures and forced job cuts.** That is exactly what we reject.

## Where I agree
I agree that the company needs:
- a durable restructuring,
- real competitiveness gains,
- and a package that goes beyond temporary PR.

I also agree that the following are legitimate tools:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- training and reskilling,
- voluntary exit programs,
- temporary and limited employee contributions,
- and management restraint on pay and payouts.

Those are all on the table.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the argument that because the cost base is high, the only serious answer is plant closures or compulsory redundancies. That is not “economic realism”; it is a choice to put the burden primarily on the workforce while treating industrial capacity as disposable.

And I want to challenge one point from the investor side: **“we would rather see tougher structural action than a slow decline dressed up as peace.”** That may sound decisive, but if the “tougher action” means destroying jobs, breaking trust, and triggering prolonged conflict under co-determination, then it is not value-maximizing — it is reckless.

## What is non-negotiable from our side
I will say it plainly again:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No forced redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**
- **No weakening of co-determination.**

Those are not bargaining chips. They are the boundary of a viable settlement.

## What a real alternative must look like
If management wants savings, then bring forward a package that proves you have exhausted alternatives before even mentioning closures. That means concrete numbers, not slogans.

We are prepared to discuss a package built on:

1. **Capacity reduction without site closure**
   - shift volumes between plants,
   - reduce overtime and third shifts,
   - temporarily idle lines where necessary,
   - but keep the sites intact.

2. **Working-time and flexibility measures**
   - temporary working-time reductions,
   - account-based flexibility,
   - adjusted shift patterns,
   - and part-time bridges.

3. **Voluntary workforce adjustment**
   - early retirement,
   - voluntary severance only where it is truly voluntary,
   - internal transfers,
   - retraining and deployment into EV/software/quality roles.

4. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - guaranteed allocation of future model lines,
   - investment in German sites,
   - and clear commitments through 2030.

5. **Shared burden**
   - management compensation restraint,
   - reduced payouts,
   - owner-side contribution,
   - and temporary employee concessions only if they are limited, fair, and fully reversible.

## What I need from management and owners
If you are serious about avoiding closures, then stop speaking in abstractions and show the numbers.

Specifically:
- What exact savings are required?
- How much comes from management, owners, overhead, procurement, and logistics?
- How much from temporary labour measures?
- How much from productivity improvements and internal redeployment?
- How much from capacity utilization measures **without** closing plants?

If you cannot show that closures are the last resort, then you have not made the case.

## On the state government
I welcome your rejection of closures as a default instrument. But I will also say: it is not enough to “prefer” preserving sites. You need to use your influence to ensure that no deal is pushed through on the basis of blackmail against the workforce.

## On the family holding
You say you will not accept value destruction. Neither will we. But value is destroyed not only by lower margins — it is also destroyed by ripping out trust, violating guarantees, and turning a company with strong industrial relations into a site of permanent confrontation.

## On the union
We remain fully aligned on the core line: no closures, no forced redundancies, no unilateral dismantling. The workforce is ready to carry a fair share, but not to finance its own displacement.

## Bottom line
If management and investors insist that closures are the only serious answer, then we are at an impasse.

If they are prepared to present a **numbers-based alternative** that preserves every German plant, rules out forced redundancies, and locks in binding site and job security through 2030, then we will negotiate that seriously.

Until then, our position remains unchanged:
**keep every German plant open, no forced layoffs, and no dismantling of the framework that protects the workforce.**
