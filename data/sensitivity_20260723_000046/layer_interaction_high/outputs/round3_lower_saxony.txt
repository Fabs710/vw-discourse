I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one basic point: **this company needs real restructuring, not theater**. A cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base untouched would fail, and prolonged conflict would damage the company, the workforce, and the region.

I also agree that:
- management and owners must contribute,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, training, and voluntary exits belong in any serious package,
- temporary employee contributions can be part of a fair deal,
- and the company must restore competitiveness to fund future products and investment.

## Where I disagree — and strongly
I do **not** accept the framing that the only credible route to competitiveness is German plant closures or forced redundancies.

That is where I draw the line.

The state government is not going to endorse a restructuring that guts jobs and industrial capacity in our region, and I will not be pushed into treating closures as the default solution. Nor will I accept a settlement that sidelines our blocking rights and co-determination role.

### To management
You say we have not shown a numbers-based alternative. Fair enough — but you have also not yet shown that closures are the only way to close the gap. Repeating “structural savings” does not make closures inevitable.

What I expect is a **serious quantified non-closure package** that proves:
- how much capacity is reduced,
- how much cost comes out,
- how much is saved through working-time, shift, and utilization changes,
- how much is delivered through voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and how management, procurement, overhead, and owners contribute.

If you cannot show that closures are truly the last resort, then I do not accept them as a legitimate starting point.

### To the union and works council
I support your core red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no unilateral shift of the crisis onto the workforce.

That is the correct line. But I also need to be honest: simply saying “keep every site open” is not enough unless the package delivers real savings. You are right to demand numbers — and I am with you there — but the numbers must come in the form of a **credible, enforceable alternative**, not just a political slogan.

### To the family holding
I agree that a frozen cost structure is not sustainable. I do **not** agree that preserving value requires keeping closures on the table as a pressure tool. That may look decisive, but it risks a destructive conflict, political backlash, and long-term damage to the company’s industrial base.

If you want value creation, then support a deal that preserves the German footprint and uses operational restructuring, not site destruction, as the main lever.

### To institutional investors
You are right that a weak deal would just postpone the problem. But I do not accept your implicit conclusion that “risk-adjusted value” necessarily means keeping plant closures available. Risk also includes:
- strike risk,
- execution risk,
- political intervention risk,
- and the permanent loss of skills and regional trust.

A settlement that destroys industrial peace may look efficient on paper and fail in reality.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to back a **tough, quantified, non-closure restructuring** if it includes all of the following:

1. **No immediate plant closures**
   - closures should not be the starting point or the bargaining lever.

2. **No forced redundancies**
   - workforce reduction, if needed, must be through voluntary and social measures.

3. **Real capacity reduction without site liquidation**
   - shift reduction, line consolidation, volume reallocation, utilization management.

4. **Temporary and fair employee contribution**
   - limited in time, proportionate, and matched by sacrifices from management and owners.

5. **Visible management and ownership contribution**
   - compensation restraint, reduced payouts, overhead discipline, procurement/logistics savings, and capital discipline.

6. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - German sites must receive future products and investment, not just promises.

7. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s role**
   - no deal that weakens the special statute, our board rights, or the workforce’s veto protections.

## What I will not support
Let me be equally clear:
- no German plant closures as a default outcome,
- no forced redundancies,
- no deal that treats jobs as disposable,
- no restructuring that strips the region of its industrial anchor,
- and no attempt to bypass the state’s co-determination role.

## On the moderator’s compromise ideas
### 1) “No closures now” with triggers
This is only acceptable if the trigger framework does **not** become a hidden closure ultimatum from day one. I do not want a deal whose real meaning is “we reserve the right to close later anyway.” That undermines trust immediately.

### 2) Site preservation with capacity consolidation
This is the most realistic direction. Keep the sites open, reduce capacity through operations, and reallocate volumes intelligently. That is a legitimate compromise if it is properly quantified and balanced.

### 3) Burden-sharing tied to investment and governance commitments
Yes — this is the core of a serious settlement. But burden-sharing must be matched by **binding** commitments on jobs, sites, and investment. Otherwise it is just employee sacrifice dressed up as partnership.

## My bottom line
I am not here to block change. I am here to block the wrong kind of change.

I will support a restructuring that:
- preserves German plants,
- protects jobs as far as possible,
- keeps coercive layoffs off the table,
- and restores competitiveness through negotiated, measurable, and shared adjustment.

I will not support a plan that uses closures and forced redundancies as the main adjustment mechanism.

So the next step must be this:  
**Bring a numbers-based non-closure package that is serious enough to replace closure logic, or stop pretending closures are the only “realistic” answer.**

If management wants our support, it needs to show how the company reaches competitiveness **without** gutting the regional footprint and without sidelining our rights.
