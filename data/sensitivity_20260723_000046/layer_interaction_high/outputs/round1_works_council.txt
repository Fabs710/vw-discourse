We are in a crisis, but let me be absolutely clear from the outset: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no forced redundancies**.

The workforce has already shown willingness to carry a fair share of the burden, but not at the expense of destroying sites, breaking trust, and permanently weakening the company’s industrial base. Management cannot simply declare historic job guarantees void and hand the workforce a fait accompli.

Our priorities are clear:

1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **Exclude compulsory layoffs.**
3. **Secure binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Preserve co-determination and real worker influence over any restructuring.**
5. **Use alternatives to closures** — capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, internal reallocation, training, voluntary exits, and negotiated contributions only if they are fair, temporary, and tied to binding investment commitments.

If management wants a solution, it must be a negotiated one. We are prepared to discuss restructuring that protects jobs and sites, but not a strategy built on closures, mass layoffs, or the erosion of hard-won guarantees.

If the proposal still contains plant closures, forced job cuts, or a unilateral dismantling of the job-security framework, then I will say plainly: **that is unacceptable**.
