### 1) Agreed points
- All parties agree the company is in a serious crisis and needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad support for **negotiated solutions** rather than unilateral action.
- Several instruments are broadly accepted as possible elements of a package:
  - working-time flexibility
  - internal redeployment
  - training and reskilling
  - voluntary exit programs
  - temporary, limited employee contributions
- There is also shared recognition that **management/owners must contribute** in some form and that **prolonged conflict would damage the company**.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, and state government reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors see capacity reduction as necessary and do not rule out closures.
- **Forced redundancies:** Labour side and state government oppose compulsory layoffs; management and investors insist some form of structural workforce reduction may be necessary.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030:** Labour side wants binding guarantees; management and investors say blanket guarantees are not credible if the business case is weak.
- **Scope of concessions:** Labour side wants burden-sharing to be temporary, fair, and matched by management/ownership sacrifices; management and investors say employee contributions must be materially large enough to restore competitiveness.
- **Governance influence:** Labour side and state government emphasize co-determination and protective rights; management and investors stress the need for decisive restructuring without paralysis.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unresolved
- **Job protection vs. competitiveness:** how much employment/site security can be preserved without leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.
- **Closures vs. equivalent savings:** whether sufficient savings can be achieved through non-closure measures alone.
- **Employee concessions vs. management/owner contributions:** the size, duration, and enforceability of sacrifices on each side.
- **Stability vs. speed of restructuring:** whether a slower negotiated compromise can deliver enough savings, or whether faster structural action is needed.
- **Industrial future vs. immediate cost reduction:** how to balance long-term investment, product allocation, and regional footprint against short-term margin repair.

### 4) Brief refocus for next round
Please move from general red lines to **concrete package elements**: quantify required savings, specify which measures could deliver them, and clarify what each side is prepared to concede in exchange for binding commitments.
