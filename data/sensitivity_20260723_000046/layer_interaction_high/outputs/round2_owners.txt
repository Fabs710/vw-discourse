I will respond plainly.

The family holding is **not** prepared to back a settlement that preserves the current cost structure under the banner of “stability.” Stability is only valuable if the company is still competitive. If we protect every legacy arrangement and refuse all structural adjustment, we will merely guarantee a slower decline.

At the same time, I do **not** support a blunt closure-first strategy that triggers a prolonged industrial conflict, destroys trust, and risks execution failure. That would also destroy value. So I am rejecting both extremes.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the German cost base is too high,
- the company needs structural change,
- temporary or cosmetic measures will not be enough,
- and the business must regain margin to fund future investment.

That said, your argument still sounds too much like “closures or nothing.” I do not accept that framing.

### With the works council and union
You are right that:
- a one-sided workforce sacrifice is unacceptable,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, training, and voluntary exits should be part of the package,
- management and owners must also contribute,
- and a destructive strike would harm everyone.

But your insistence that **all plants remain open and no forced reductions can occur under any circumstance** is not a viable answer to a structural cost crisis.

### With the state government
You are right to insist that this cannot become a regional industrial retreat. A settlement that permanently weakens the German footprint and triggers social backlash would be value-destructive.

But I cannot accept the idea that the state can effectively pre-decide the outcome by ruling out the structural levers that may be needed. The company has to remain competitive first.

### With institutional investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is worthless and that value destruction through drift is unacceptable.

But I do not agree with the implication that the only way to prove seriousness is to force closures regardless of execution risk. A badly executed closure program can destroy more value than it creates.

## My position

The family holding will support a **credible, durable restructuring package** that does all of the following:

1. **Restores competitiveness materially**
   - not symbolically, not marginally.

2. **Delivers a meaningful capacity and cost reduction**
   - the current footprint cannot simply be frozen.

3. **Avoids value-destructive instability**
   - no prolonged conflict, no reputational self-harm, no uncontrolled escalation.

4. **Shares the burden**
   - management, owners, and employees all contribute.

5. **Preserves governance control and the company’s long-term industrial base**
   - we will not support any arrangement that weakens ownership control or permanently impairs the enterprise.

## What I can support

I am open to a package built around:
- working-time reductions and flexibility,
- targeted capacity reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining and reskilling,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- management compensation restraint,
- reduced payouts where appropriate,
- and binding investment commitments.

I am also open to **site-specific solutions** if they achieve the required savings without destroying the industrial footprint.

## What I cannot support

I cannot support:
- a deal that merely postpones the crisis,
- a blanket guarantee that locks in the entire current structure through 2030 regardless of economics,
- a settlement that shifts all adjustment onto management or owners while preserving every legacy protection,
- or a confrontation strategy that makes the company ungovernable.

## To management
If you want support from the family holding, bring me a package that proves the following:
- how much savings are required,
- how much comes from labour, management, procurement, overhead, and ownership-side restraint,
- how much structural capacity comes out of the system,
- and how the company returns to target margins without triggering a destructive conflict.

If your only answer is “closures,” then you have not yet shown me a governance-ready solution.

## To the works council and union
Your alternatives must be **numbers-based**, not just principled. If you want to keep German plants open, show how the economics work. Not in general terms, but in concrete terms:
- what capacity is reduced,
- what cost is taken out,
- what productivity gains are locked in,
- and what employee contribution is credible and durable.

If you cannot demonstrate equivalence, then you are asking the company to accept permanent weakness in exchange for temporary peace. I will not support that.

## To the state government
I respect the need to protect the regional footprint. But preserving jobs and preserving competitiveness are not the same thing. If the company becomes structurally uncompetitive, the region ultimately loses more.

Use your influence to push for a negotiated solution, yes. But do not confuse regional protection with blocking necessary change.

## Bottom line

The family holding’s line is this:

- **No value-destroying stalemate**
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No unconditional preservation of the current footprint**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that materially restores competitiveness**
- **No to a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance stability**

If the parties want our support, they need to come back with a serious, quantified alternative that delivers comparable savings and real competitiveness gains **without** simply freezing the problem in place.

If such a package cannot be produced, then stronger structural measures will remain on the table.
