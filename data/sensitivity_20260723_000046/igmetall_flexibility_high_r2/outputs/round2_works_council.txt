Management is still trying to frame this as if there are only two choices: either accept closures and forced layoffs, or accept a “cosmetic compromise.” I reject that framing.

Let me be direct:

**We will not accept plant closures in Germany.**  
**We will not accept compulsory redundancies.**  
**We will not accept a restructuring that tears up the historic job-security framework and treats the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort.**

That remains our line.

### Where I agree
I agree with one point from almost everyone here: this company needs a real, durable solution. No one benefits from a short-term patch that postpones the problem. And yes, the cost base must be addressed. But “addressed” does **not** mean closing German plants and dismissing thousands of workers against their will.

I also agree that industrial conflict is harmful. But the responsibility for that does not lie with the workforce. It lies with management, which has chosen to arrive at this table with a closure agenda and then present it as the only serious option.

### Where I disagree
#### To management
You keep saying structural savings require plant closures or mass compulsory redundancies. Prove it.

Right now, what you are presenting is not an unavoidable economic law. It is a strategic choice to push the burden onto workers and sites. We do not accept that this is the only path.

If you want us to discuss meaningful restructuring, then bring forward a **quantified alternative** that avoids closures and forced redundancies and shows:
- how much capacity is reduced,
- how costs are lowered durably,
- what investment is committed to German sites,
- and how jobs are secured through 2030.

Until then, your proposal is not a restructuring plan we can support; it is a threat.

#### To the institutional investors
You say you want “credible economics” and “structurally lower costs.” Fine. But you are acting as if social destruction has no cost. It does. A prolonged conflict, damage to industrial relations, loss of trust, and a weakened company culture are real economic risks. If you back a closure-first strategy, you are not choosing value creation; you are choosing a high-risk confrontation whose consequences will hit the company for years.

If you want durable value, then support a negotiated solution that protects the industrial base and preserves execution stability.

#### To the controlling family owners
You speak about long-term value, but value is not protected by sacrificing the workforce and destabilising the group’s most important domestic plants. A company that breaks its own job-security promise destroys trust that cannot be repaired quickly. That matters for future transformation, too.

If the ownership side wants legitimacy, it must back a solution that preserves German plants and avoids forced layoffs.

#### To the regional state
I appreciate your opposition to closures and mass redundancies. But that cannot remain a political statement only. We need you to use your blocking position accordingly. If there is no viable non-closure alternative, then the board should hear that clearly.

### On the numbers and scenarios
Management was asked to bring **one quantified closure/layoff option** and **one quantified alternative without them**. That is the right next step.

So here is our challenge back:

1. **Present the numbers for a non-closure restructuring.**  
   We want a serious plan with:
   - negotiated capacity adjustments,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - temporary or targeted employee contributions,
   - investment in German plants,
   - and binding job security through 2030.

2. **Show what savings you claim from closures and layoffs.**  
   If you want to argue they are necessary, put the figures on the table and compare them transparently to the non-closure option.

3. **Explain why alternatives are allegedly insufficient.**  
   We are not accepting vague claims about “competitiveness.” We want the actual gap, the actual measures, and the actual timeline.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary and negotiated employee contributions,
- working-time arrangements,
- capacity reductions **without** closures,
- reallocation of production,
- targeted efficiency measures,
- and investment commitments that secure future models for German plants.

We are **not** prepared to discuss:
- shutting German plants,
- forced redundancies,
- or using fear and blackmail to weaken co-determination and collective protections.

### Bottom line
If management comes with a plan that still depends on closures or compulsory layoffs, then there is no basis for agreement. If the shareholder side insists on that path, then it should understand this clearly: **it will face resistance, not consent.**

We are ready for a serious, quantified negotiation on a non-closure solution.  
We are not ready to negotiate away jobs, sites, or the workforce’s rights.
