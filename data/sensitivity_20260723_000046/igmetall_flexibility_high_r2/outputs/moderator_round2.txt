**Neutral moderator summary**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary one.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable restructuring** is needed, not a short-term patch.
- There is also agreement that **prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value** and should be avoided if possible.
- The economist’s intervention was accepted as a useful framing device: the key test is whether a **non-closure package can deliver savings comparable to a closure-led package**.
- Several parties agreed that the discussion now needs to move from principles to **quantified scenarios**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Management / investors / some owners**: argue that closures and/or compulsory redundancies may be necessary unless a non-closure plan can prove equivalent savings.
- **Works Council / union**: maintain firm red lines against **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies**, and reject the idea that workers should bear the main burden.
- **Regional state**: opposes closures and forced layoffs as a starting point, but has not yet endorsed a fully specified alternative.
- **Central dispute**: whether “no closures” can still produce **structurally sufficient, durable cost reductions**.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **exact savings gap** between:
  - a closure/layoff scenario, and
  - a negotiated non-closure scenario.
- The balance between:
  - **higher recurring savings** from closures,
  - versus **higher one-time costs, higher conflict risk, and social/industrial damage**.
- Whether a non-closure package can realistically include:
  - enough **capacity reduction**,
  - enough **labor-cost relief**,
  - enough **productivity improvement**,
  - and enough **durability through 2030**.
- How to compare:
  - financial savings,
  - execution risk,
  - industrial-relations stability,
  - and long-term investment confidence
  in one framework.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should be limited to **one quantified comparison**:
1. a closure-led restructuring model, and  
2. a non-closure negotiated model,

each showing:
- annual recurring savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- probability of implementation success,
- and net value over a defined time horizon.

The discussion should then focus on whether the non-closure option is merely politically preferable, or **actually economically equivalent**.
