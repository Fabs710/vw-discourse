I will be just as direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the owners on one essential point: **this is a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible**. The company needs a durable solution, not a short truce. I also agree that **industrial peace has economic value** and that a prolonged confrontation would hurt everyone.

I further agree with the economist’s core message: the question is not whether the company needs adjustment — it does — but whether a **non-closure package can deliver enough durable savings** to be credible.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept that plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies are the default answer.

That is the line management keeps pushing, and I am not persuaded. Not because restructuring is unnecessary, but because you have **not yet demonstrated that all viable alternatives have been exhausted**. For a regional anchor company with major co-determination protections and a special statute, that matters.

I also disagree with any framing that suggests the workforce should carry the main burden while ownership and management ask for patience. That is neither fair nor politically sustainable.

## To management
You say closures remain on the table unless the employee side produces a fully quantified alternative. Fine — then let’s be precise.

What I expect is not rhetoric, but a **real comparison**:
- plant-by-plant savings from a closure-led scenario,
- plant-by-plant savings from a negotiated non-closure scenario,
- one-time costs,
- implementation risk,
- and the timeline to get back to a competitive cost base.

Until that is on the table, I do **not** accept closures as justified.

And let me be clear: if your proposal depends on gutting German industrial capacity, then you are not just restructuring the company — you are weakening one of its core regional anchors. That is not acceptable to us.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your red lines on **no German plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies**.

But I also have to say this: red lines alone are not a restructuring plan. If the company is to be kept competitive without closures, then the non-closure package must be **deep, durable, and quantified**. That means real capacity reduction, real productivity gains, real labor-cost moderation, and binding investment commitments.

So I am not asking you to soften your core position. I am asking you to help force management to put a credible alternative on the table — one that protects sites and jobs **and** actually fixes the economics.

## To the controlling family owners
You are right that value destruction through conflict must be avoided. But value is not protected by reflexively accepting the harshest restructuring path either. A company that breaks trust in its domestic base and damages its social legitimacy may win a short-term cost reduction and lose long-term resilience.

So I would say this clearly: **support a negotiated restructuring, but do not use “competitiveness” as a blanket argument for closures before the numbers prove they are unavoidable.**

## To institutional investors
You are right to demand credibility. I share that demand. But credibility also requires recognizing the full cost of conflict, disruption, and weakened execution. If a negotiated alternative can achieve comparable savings with less damage, then that is not softness — it is good stewardship.

## My position
Let me state it plainly:

- **No blanket acceptance of German plant closures**
- **No acceptance of compulsory redundancies as the first resort**
- **Yes to a serious restructuring**
- **Yes to shared sacrifice**
- **Yes to a quantified, durable non-closure alternative**
- **No to a deal that guts regional employment and weakens the company’s domestic anchor role**

## What would move me
I would support a negotiated package if it includes:
- binding capacity reduction,
- durable cost relief,
- investment commitments for German sites,
- a credible employment perspective,
- and preservation of co-determination and our veto role.

That is the standard.

## Bottom line
Management must stop presenting closures as if they were an unavoidable fact of nature. They are not. They are a decision — and one with serious regional and political consequences.

I am open to a hard restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and jobs first and asks the region to applaud later.
