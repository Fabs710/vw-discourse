We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not yet on the standard for action.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary earnings dip.
- A **cosmetic compromise** would destroy value.
- **Prolonged industrial conflict** would also destroy value.
- Any acceptable path must produce **durable cost reduction** and a credible return to competitive margins.

That is the baseline. On that, we are not in dispute.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your red lines are understandable, but from a capital perspective they are **not yet a solution**.

Saying “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” may be legitimate as a negotiating position, but it does **not** answer the economic question:
- how much fixed cost comes out,
- how much capacity is removed,
- how headcount is aligned,
- and how the German cost base reaches competitive levels by 2030.

Right now, you are asking us to accept your constraints **before** showing an economically equivalent alternative. We will not support that.

What would move us is not rhetoric about trust or industrial peace alone. It is a package that is:
- **quantified**,
- **durable**,
- **enforceable**,
- and **close enough to the closure-led savings case** to restore margins.

If you can produce that without closures or forced layoffs, we are open to it. If you cannot, then your red lines are simply a veto on necessary restructuring.

### To Management
We agree that the company needs a hard reset. We also agree that the non-closure route must be judged against real economics, not sentiment.

But you still need to prove the case properly.

At the moment, “closures are necessary” is still a **claim**, not a demonstrated conclusion. We want the comparison done cleanly:
- closure-led scenario,
- deep non-closure scenario,
- recurring savings,
- one-time costs,
- implementation risk,
- and likely net value over 3–5 years.

And that comparison must include the real cost of conflict, delay, and execution damage. If a closure-led route sparks major industrial disruption, that has to be reflected in the economics.

So: we are not buying a closure-first argument on faith. Show the numbers.

### To the Regional State
We appreciate your opposition to closures and forced redundancies, but opposition alone does not create value.

If you want closures off the table, then use your leverage to force a **serious, quantified, binding alternative**. If management comes back without one, then the state should not simply object in principle and allow the burden to drift back onto capital.

### To the Controlling Family Holding
You are right that long-term value must be protected. But long-term value is not protected by preserving every legacy structure regardless of economics.

We agree that a closure-led solution should not be chosen lightly. But we also do **not** support treating “no closures” as a sacred principle if it leaves the cost base structurally impaired. That would be a transfer of value destruction into the future.

## Our position
We will support **whatever path credibly restores competitive margins** with durable savings and disciplined execution.

What we will **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- or a package that relies on political comfort while the operating economics remain weak.
