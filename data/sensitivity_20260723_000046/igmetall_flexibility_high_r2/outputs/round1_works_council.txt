As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of adjustment. Management cannot now demand that workers pay for strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, weakening co-determination, and breaking the historic job-security framework. A restructuring that relies on fear, forced layoffs, and shutting German sites is not a credible transformation plan — it is a social and industrial rupture.

What we are prepared to discuss is a different path:

- binding site and employment guarantees through 2030,
- no forced redundancies,
- future capacity adjustments only through negotiated, socially responsible measures,
- meaningful investment commitments for the German plants,
- and a genuine co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping the restructuring.

If management wants a sustainable solution, it must put forward an alternative that protects every German plant, secures jobs, and gives the workforce a real stake in the future. Cost reductions can be discussed, but not at the expense of closing sites or sacrificing thousands of livelihoods.

If that is not on the table, then I will oppose this plan with all means available to us, including mobilising the workforce.
