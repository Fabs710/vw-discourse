I appreciate that management is acknowledging the need for a durable solution rather than a short-lived truce. On that point, we agree: this company does need a credible path back to competitiveness, and I do not dismiss the cost problem.

But I need to be very clear where I draw the line as the regional shareholder and political actor:

**I do not accept a restructuring that is built around plant closures in Germany or mass compulsory redundancies.**  
That would be economically damaging, socially reckless, and politically unacceptable for a company that is a regional anchor employer.

What I do agree with is this:

- the company cannot simply leave its cost base untouched;
- capacity adjustments may be necessary;
- labour-cost contributions can be part of a package;
- and the status quo is not a solution.

But the burden must be shared fairly, and the solution must preserve the company’s role in the region. That means:

1. **No closure decision that permanently weakens the German industrial base unless every other viable option has genuinely been exhausted.**
2. **No forced redundancies as the main adjustment tool.**
3. **A negotiated restructuring with site protection, investment commitments, and long-term employment security.**
4. **A real contribution from management and owners as well, not just demands directed at workers.**

To management: saying “we need structural change” is not enough. You have to show that there is no alternative to destroying sites and jobs. Right now, I do not hear that case convincingly. I hear a preference for the most drastic option because it is easiest to execute on paper, not because it is the only viable path.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that the workforce should not pay alone. But we also need to be honest that some adjustment will be required if the brand is to remain competitive. If we refuse any meaningful contribution at all, we risk forcing management toward exactly the conflict and closure path we all want to avoid.

So my position is:

- **I am prepared to back a serious restructuring package.**
- **I am not prepared to back a closure-led restructuring.**
- **I want a negotiated solution that protects jobs, preserves sites, and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice and investment.**

If management comes back with a proposal that avoids plant closures, avoids compulsory redundancies, and delivers verifiable savings through negotiated measures, then I am ready to engage constructively. If the proposal still depends on shutting German plants, then I will oppose it.
