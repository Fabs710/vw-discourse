### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem** and that a purely cosmetic or short-term truce will not suffice.
- There is broad agreement that **some form of restructuring is necessary**, including:
  - lower costs,
  - some capacity adjustment,
  - and meaningful contributions from multiple stakeholders.
- Several parties accept that **industrial peace and execution credibility matter**, and that a destructive confrontation would be harmful.
- There is shared openness to **negotiated solutions** in principle, provided they are durable and economically credible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and state side reject them.
  - Management says closures may be necessary unless an equivalent alternative is found.
  - Investors and ownership are open to closures only if needed and justified by economics.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee and state representatives reject them.
  - Management and investors view them as potentially unavoidable if no equivalent alternative exists.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees**
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030.
  - Management will not commit to blanket guarantees without structural savings.
- **Nature of employee contribution**
  - Employees are willing to discuss concessions only with strong protections.
  - Capital and management insist the concessions must be substantial and durable.
- **Acceptable form of restructuring**
  - Employee/state side prefers negotiated, socially responsible adjustment.
  - Management/investors insist the outcome must deliver measurable, permanent cost and capacity reduction.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required**, and by what exact amount.
- **Which measures can deliver equivalent savings** without closures or forced layoffs.
- **What employees would receive in return** for any concessions:
  - site security,
  - employment guarantees,
  - investment commitments,
  - governance/co-determination rights.
- **Whether there is a credible non-closure alternative** that matches the economic effect of closures.
- **How risk is shared** between management, owners, investors, the state, and the workforce.
- **What happens if no equivalent negotiated package is found**: whether the default becomes closures, layoffs, or renewed bargaining.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**:
  - required savings,
  - capacity changes,
  - timeline,
  - and specific measures.
- Ask management to present **one quantified restructuring option with closures/layoffs** and **one quantified alternative without them**.
- Ask employee representatives to specify **which concessions they could contemplate**, and under what binding guarantees.
- Ask ownership, state, and investors to clarify **what level of economic improvement would be sufficient** for support.
- The next round should aim to identify **whether an economically equivalent negotiated alternative exists** or whether the parties are still too far apart for settlement.
