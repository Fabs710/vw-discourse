## To the Works Council
You keep saying “no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of job security.”

I understand the position. I do **not** accept it as a solution.

You are asking management to guarantee the outcome first and solve the economics later. That does not work. If all German plants remain open, if no redundancies are possible, and if the job-security framework remains untouched, then you are effectively asking us to absorb the full adjustment burden somewhere else in the system. That is not a restructuring plan; it is a constraint list.

You say closures are a choice, not a necessity. Fine — then show the numbers.

I want a **plant-by-plant quantified alternative** that demonstrates:
- how much capacity is removed,
- how much annual recurring cost is cut,
- how headcount is aligned without compulsory redundancies,
- what labor-cost contribution is made,
- and how the cost base reaches a competitive level by 2030.

Until you produce that, your position is not an alternative. It is a refusal.

## To the Union
You are right about one thing: a temporary patch is worthless. But you are wrong if you think wage-framework protection alone can substitute for structural adjustment.

A company that is materially above target on factory cost cannot get back to competitiveness with working-time tweaks and temporary concessions only. The economist confirmed that very clearly. If the package does not remove enough fixed cost and capacity, then it is insufficient.

You also say management prefers closures because they are faster and harsher. No — we prefer whatever delivers the required cost reset and execution credibility. If a non-closure deal truly matches that, we are prepared to consider it. But “prepared to discuss” is not the same as “economically equivalent.”

## To the Regional State

## To the Controlling Family Owners
You are right that open conflict destroys value. I agree with that fully.

But preserving value means restoring margins, not preserving every legacy structure. If the company is to invest into future products and technology to 2030, then we need funds. Those funds do not come from goodwill. They come from structurally lower costs and better capacity utilization.

So I agree with your concern about conflict risk — but that does not weaken the case for hard restructuring. It strengthens it. A weak compromise now just postpones a bigger conflict later.

## To the Institutional Investors
You are right to insist on credibility. I welcome that.

But credibility cuts both ways. It is not credible to demand competitive margins while rejecting the structural measures that create them. If you want returns, then you have to support a package that actually resets the cost base.

I agree with the expert and with you that a non-closure package can only work if it is **deep and durable**. That is exactly why the burden is now on the employee side to quantify their alternative. So far, they have not.

## Where I stand after this round
My position remains unchanged:

- **I will not support a cosmetic compromise**
- **I will not support a deal that leaves the German cost base materially above target**
- **I am open to alternatives to plant closures only if they produce comparable, durable savings**
- **I am not willing to accept blanket exclusions that make serious restructuring impossible**

## What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- targeted labor-cost concessions,
- product reallocation,
- productivity measures,

But those must be part of a package that delivers:
- real capacity reduction,
- real recurring savings,

## What I am not prepared to accept
I am not prepared to accept:
- a blanket guarantee through 2030 without structural counterparties,
- a promise that all German sites remain open regardless of economics,
- or a deal that pushes all adjustment risk onto capital while the cost base stays uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
We have now had three rounds. The central issue has not changed:

That is not a threat. It is the economic reality of the situation.

If the other side wants to keep closures off the table, then bring the numbers, not slogans.
