Facilitator summary of the plateau and next-step proposals:

## Core impasse
The central impasse is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the restructuring mechanism**:
- Management, investors, and owners are treating **closures / hard capacity cuts** as the most credible route to durable savings.
- Works council, union, and regional state are insisting that **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are unacceptable**.
- The expert view confirms that a **non-closure path can work**, but only if it is **deep, durable, enforceable, and risk-adjusted against closures**.

So the dispute is not “restructuring vs no restructuring,” but:
**“Can a non-closure package achieve sufficient risk-adjusted economic equivalence to a closure-led plan?”**

A second, related impasse is **trust and sequencing**:
- employee and state representatives do not accept closures as the default benchmark;
- management and capital do not accept red lines as a substitute for quantified savings;
- both sides are demanding proof first, while neither side is yet willing to move without it.

## Two or three concrete compromise positions
Below are facilitator proposals for bridging the most conflicting stakeholders:

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with conditional closure deferral
A negotiated package could:
- keep all German plants formally open for an initial period,
- deliver immediate labor-cost and capacity measures through working-time changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, and productivity commitments,
- and include a **conditional review trigger** after a defined period if targets are missed.

This could bridge:
- the works council / union red line against immediate closures,
- and management / investor demands for enforceable savings.

### 2) “No forced redundancies” combined with binding capacity reduction
A compromise could preserve the principle of **no compulsory redundancies**, while allowing:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- and site-by-site capacity reduction with measurable headcount targets.

This would create a route for labor-cost reduction without immediate forced layoffs, while still giving management a quantified adjustment path.

### 3) Non-closure package with hard enforcement and fallback
A serious alternative could be structured as:
- a non-closure package with binding investment commitments,
- specific annual savings targets,
- milestone-based monitoring,
- and pre-agreed fallback measures if milestones are missed.

This bridges:
- the workforce’s insistence on a genuine alternative,
- and capital’s demand for durability and enforceability.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
Can you present a plant-by-plant, risk-adjusted comparison that shows why a non-closure package cannot close the savings gap by 2030, including conflict and delay costs?

### To Works Council
What exact, quantified capacity and cost reductions are you prepared to accept under a non-closure model so that the package is not just a refusal, but a viable alternative?

### To Industrial Union
Which specific employee contributions are you willing to lock in for a multi-year horizon, and what enforcement mechanism would make them credible to capital?

### To Regional State Government
If management returns with a closure plan, what concrete blocking or leverage action are you prepared to use to force a negotiated alternative?

### To Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a time-limited non-closure solution if it came with hard triggers, measurable savings, and a credible fallback if targets are missed?

### To Institutional Investors
What minimum percentage of the closure-led risk-adjusted NPV would a non-closure package need to achieve for you to regard it as economically equivalent?

### To the Expert
What minimum set of conditions would make a non-closure plan sufficiently durable and enforceable to be judged comparable to closures in present-value terms?
