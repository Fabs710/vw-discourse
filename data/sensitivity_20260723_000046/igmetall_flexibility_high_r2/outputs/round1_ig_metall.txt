I agree with the core line the Works Council has set: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable “detail” — it is the boundary of any responsible settlement.

At the same time, I want to be equally clear about what is required from management and ownership if they want a durable deal:

- **Workers cannot be the only party paying for the restructuring.**
- **Cost contributions from employees are only discussable if they are matched by real contributions from management, owners, and the company itself.**
- **Any capacity reduction must be handled through socially responsible measures**, not through forced layoffs or site closures.
- **The collective wage framework must remain intact**; we will not accept a precedent that hollows out bargaining standards across the group.

What we brought forward already points to a possible route: negotiated labour-cost relief, temporary concessions, and capacity adjustments **in exchange for binding job and site security**, investment commitments, and a clear future for the German plants. That is the only kind of trade-off that can be called fair.

So to management: if your restructuring plan still depends on closing plants or cutting jobs by decree, then it is not a basis for negotiation. It will be met with resistance, including industrial action. If, however, you are prepared to come back with a package that shares the burden fairly and protects the workforce and sites, then we are ready to negotiate seriously and quickly.

To the shareholder side and state stakeholders: if you want stability, you should not back a “solutions” package that destroys industrial peace and social legitimacy. A carmaker of this size cannot be turned around by simply pushing risk onto employees. That approach will provoke conflict and damage the company’s future more than it helps.

So my position is straightforward:  
**We are open to contribution, not to sacrifice without protection.**  
**We will discuss savings, but not closures and forced redundancies.**  
**Any acceptable deal must preserve jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework while ensuring everyone contributes fairly.**
