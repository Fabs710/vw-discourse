## 1) Core economic principle

If the company has **structural overcapacity**, then a non-closure package must do more than reduce wages. It must also remove enough **fixed cost** and **semi-fixed capacity cost** to match what closures would otherwise eliminate.

That means the relevant comparison is not:

- “closures vs no closures”

but:

- **net present value of recurring savings**
  minus
- **one-time restructuring costs**
  adjusted for
- **probability of successful implementation**

A non-closure package is only economically equivalent if:

> **Expected NPV of non-closure savings ≥ expected NPV of closure-led savings**

---

## 2) What usually drives the gap

### Closure-led package
A plant closure usually removes:
- site fixed overhead,
- plant labor,
- support functions,
- maintenance and utilities,
- underabsorbed capacity cost.

But it also creates:
- severance and social plan costs,
- transfer/logistics costs,
- write-downs,
- ramp-up costs at receiving sites,
- potential disruption to supply.

### Non-closure package
A non-closure package can reduce:
- labor cost,
- working time,
- shift count,
- some overhead,
- some headcount through attrition,
- some capacity through partial line reduction.

But it usually leaves:
- most site fixed cost,
- more overhead leakage,
- more underutilization,
- more execution risk that savings will erode over time.

So the economic question is whether the non-closure package removes **enough recurring cost** to offset the fact that the plant footprint remains.

## 4) The exact savings gap: how to calculate it

The right calculation is:

### For each option:
**Expected NPV = Σ [Savings_t × Probability_t / (1+r)^t] – One-time costs**

Where:
- `Savings_t` = recurring annual savings in year t
- `Probability_t` = probability-adjusted realization of those savings
- `r` = discount rate
- One-time costs include severance, write-offs, transfer, retraining, logistics, consultants, etc.

### Savings gap:
**Gap = NPV(closure-led) – NPV(non-closure)**

If the gap is positive, closure is economically stronger.  
If the gap is near zero, non-closure may be equivalent.  
If the gap is negative, the non-closure package is better economically.

---

## 5) Probability adjustment matters

This is where many negotiations become misleading.

A non-closure package often looks attractive on paper, but its **realization probability** is lower because:
- wage concessions may expire,
- productivity measures may not stick,
- headcount reductions may be slower than planned,
- future bargaining can reopen the deal,
- underutilized capacity continues to create hidden cost.

A closure-led package has high certainty once executed, but with higher upfront cost and labor conflict risk.

### Practical probability ranges
For modeling purposes only:
- Closure implementation success: **80%–95%**
- Non-closure full-savings success: **50%–80%**

That difference can erase a large part of the apparent savings advantage of a non-closure deal.

Let’s compare two packages over five years.

Approximate 5-year undiscounted value:
- Savings: €200m × 5 = **€1.0bn**

- Approximate net value: **€400m**

### Non-closure package
- Annual net savings: **€170m**

Approximate 5-year undiscounted value:
- Savings: €170m × 5 = **€850m**
- Probability-adjusted savings: **€552.5m**
- Minus one-time cost: **€150m**
- Approximate net value: **€402.5m**

### Result
In this illustration, the two packages are nearly equal in gross economic value.

But if the non-closure package falls to:
- **€120m/year savings**

- 5-year savings = €600m
- probability-adjusted = €360m
- minus €150m cost = **€210m net**

Now the closure-led package is clearly superior by about **€190m**.

So the exact gap is highly sensitive to:
- annual recurring savings,
- durability,
- and execution confidence.

## 7) What usually makes the gap widen

The gap usually widens in favor of closure when:

1. **capacity utilization is already low**
2. **the plant’s product has weak margin**
3. **other German or European plants can absorb the volume**
4. **labor concessions are temporary or reversible**
5. **fixed overhead is large relative to labor**
6. **the company needs savings quickly, not over many years**

The gap narrows when:
1. the non-closure package includes **real footprint reduction**
2. the company can move a better product into the site
3. labor concessions are **durable and enforceable**
4. headcount can fall materially through natural attrition
5. investment raises output and margin enough to offset the fixed-cost burden
