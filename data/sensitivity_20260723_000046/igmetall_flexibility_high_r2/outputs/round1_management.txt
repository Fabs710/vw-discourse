I hear the line you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: **a deal that rules out closures and compulsory redundancies from the outset does not solve the crisis we are in.** It may sound socially easier, but it does not restore competitiveness, and that is the test this company must pass.

We are facing a structural problem, not a temporary downturn:

- German factory costs are materially above target.
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competitors are leaner, faster, and in some cases materially lower-cost.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- And we still have to fund the product and technology investment needed to 2030.

So I disagree with the premise that we can simply preserve every site and every job and still get to a durable solution. **That is not a restructuring; that is postponing the adjustment.**

At the same time, I am not saying the only answer is immediate plant closure by decree. What I am saying is this:

1. **We need a structural reduction in capacity and cost.**
   Cosmetic savings, temporary concessions, or purely voluntary measures will not be enough.
2. **A labour contribution is unavoidable.**
   If the workforce expects the company to protect every site, then employee-side cost relief has to be serious enough to matter.
3. **The burden cannot sit on the company alone.**
   Management, owners, and employees all have to contribute if we want a viable future.

Where I agree with you is that a purely destructive approach would be self-defeating. I do not want industrial chaos for its own sake. I want an agreement that is **durable**, not fragile. But durability requires credibility, and credibility requires hard measures.

So let me be precise about what I will and will not accept:

- **I will not accept a package that leaves the German cost base essentially unchanged.**
- **I will not accept a short-term truce that preserves all current structures but kicks the problem into 2027 or 2028.**
- **I am open to alternatives to outright closures** if, and only if, they produce a comparable reduction in capacity and fixed cost.
- **I am open to negotiated workforce measures** if they are meaningful, sustainable, and actually improve competitiveness.
- **I am not willing to give binding job security through 2030 as a blanket guarantee** unless the package delivers the structural savings this company needs.

On your point about co-determination and social responsibility: yes, those matter. But co-determination is not a veto on economic reality. If the company cannot compete, then no one is protected in the long run. The responsible path is to reshape the cost base now, not to defend every existing structure until the market forces a worse outcome later.

To be constructive: if the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it needs to help produce an alternative that is economically equivalent. That means real capacity reduction, real labour-cost reduction, and real flexibility. If that can be done through plant reallocation, working-time changes, targeted site adjustments, and negotiated cost relief, then we can discuss it. But I need something that actually closes the gap.

So my position is:

- **No to a cosmetic compromise.**
- **Yes to a serious, durable restructuring package.**
- **Open on the form, not open on the necessity of deep cuts.**

If you want, I am prepared to discuss a package that avoids the most disruptive outcomes — but only if it delivers a structural fix that restores margins and competitiveness.
