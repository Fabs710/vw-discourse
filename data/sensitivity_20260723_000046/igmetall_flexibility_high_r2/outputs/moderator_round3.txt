## Core impasse
The parties agree on the need for **durable restructuring** and on the need for **real, recurring savings**. They do **not** agree on the permissible means.

The conflict has narrowed to three linked questions:
1. **Are German plant closures necessary, or can equivalent savings be achieved without closures?**
2. **Can headcount and cost reduction occur without compulsory redundancies?**
3. **Can a non-closure package be made deep, binding, and durable enough to satisfy capital while preserving the red lines of labour and the state?**

At present:
- **Management, investors, and the owners** are asking for a quantified alternative that closes the savings gap.
- **Works Council, union, and regional state** are insisting that closures and forced layoffs are not acceptable starting points.
- The **expert input** indicates that a non-closure path is possible only if it is genuinely deep, durable, and enforceable.

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise paths
These are framed as procedural bridge options, not endorsements.

### 1) Two-track quantified comparison with a binding decision rule
The parties could agree to a short, fixed timetable to compare:
- a **closure-led scenario**, and
- a **deep non-closure scenario**,

using the same metrics:
- recurring savings,
- one-time costs,
- execution risk,
- employment impact,
- and timeline to competitiveness.

A pre-agreed rule would then determine which path proceeds if the savings gap in the non-closure scenario is not closed by a defined threshold.

**Bridge function:** gives capital the hard numbers it demands, while giving labour and the state a fair test before closures are treated as inevitable.

---

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” with a hard workforce-adjustment package
A compromise could be structured around:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- shift changes,
- temporary concessions,
- and targeted skills transfers,

combined with a **numerically binding headcount reduction target**.

This would preserve the red line against forced layoffs while still delivering measurable cost and capacity reduction.

**Bridge function:** addresses labour’s central protection while still offering management a real adjustment mechanism.

---

### 3) Site-preservation pact with conditional safeguards
A possible package could keep German plants open **initially** but make that commitment conditional on:
- meeting quarterly savings milestones,
- achieving agreed productivity targets,
- and implementing binding capacity reductions.

If milestones are missed, specific fallback measures would automatically activate, including more severe options.

**Bridge function:** allows the workforce and state to preserve sites now, while giving management and owners enforcement leverage if the negotiated route underdelivers.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings gap must be closed for you to rule out closures, and what verified non-closure measures would you accept as economically equivalent?**

### To the Works Council
**If closures are off the table, what binding and quantified capacity, cost, and headcount reductions are you prepared to commit to that would still reach the required savings?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which specific labour contributions are you prepared to make that are durable, measurable, and enforceable without crossing the line into compulsory redundancies or dismantling job security?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete use of your blocking or influence rights will you commit to if management brings back a closure-led plan without a fully tested non-closure alternative?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Under what quantified conditions would you support a negotiated non-closure package, even if it preserves all German sites, and what minimum return or competitiveness threshold would you require?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a settlement that avoids closures if it delivers the same recurring savings over 3–5 years, even if the one-time cost and implementation structure are more complex?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, labour-cost relief, and enforcement would make a non-closure package economically credible enough to compare directly with closures?**
