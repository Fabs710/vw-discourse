I agree with one central point from management and also from the state side: **this company does need a structural fix, not a cosmetic truce**. If the German cost base stays untouched, the company will keep bleeding competitiveness. That would be unacceptable for the owners and irresponsible toward the long-term survival of the enterprise.

But I also want to be equally clear: **I do not support a restructuring that turns into a prolonged industrial confrontation or destroys the company’s social legitimacy.** A value-preserving turnaround is not the same as a closure-led shock strategy.

My assessment is this:

- **Management is right** that the problem is structural and that labour-cost relief and capacity reduction must be meaningful.
- **The Works Council and union are right** that forced redundancies and a blunt closure-first approach would trigger conflict, undermine trust, and could damage the brand and execution.
- **The regional state is right** that this cannot become a socially and politically reckless hollowing-out of German industry.

Where I disagree with the employee side is on the line that appears to rule out closures and compulsory redundancies entirely. If that line is absolute, then it may simply block any credible restructuring. I cannot support a settlement that preserves every current structure while asking owners and management to absorb all the pain. That is not sustainable.

At the same time, I am not giving a blank cheque to management for the most drastic option. Plant closures are not a goal in themselves, and I would view a package built around them as justified only if there is a clear, convincing case that no alternative can deliver comparable savings and competitiveness.

So my position is:

- **Yes** to a durable restructuring that restores margins and competitiveness.
- **Yes** to serious employee contributions, capacity adjustments, and cost discipline.
- **Yes** to negotiated solutions that avoid unnecessary disruption.
- **No** to a shallow compromise that postpones the problem.
- **No** to an unnecessarily destructive closure strategy if a comparable alternative can be credibly delivered.
- **No** to any outcome that permanently weakens enterprise value or undermines governance stability.

If management can bring forward a package that produces real structural savings through negotiated measures — for example capacity rationalisation, working-time flexibility, labour-cost relief, and targeted site adjustments — then that deserves serious consideration. But I will not endorse any approach that leaves the underlying competitiveness problem unresolved.

In short: **the family ownership side supports tough restructuring, but only if it is genuinely durable and does not create long-term value destruction through open-ended conflict or a reckless assault on the company’s industrial base.**
