We are listening carefully, and we appreciate that management, the family owners, and the state shareholder all acknowledge the need for a real turnaround rather than a cosmetic one.

From the capital markets side, our test is simple: **does this plan credibly restore competitive margins, and does it do so durably?** If yes, we can support difficult restructuring. If not, we will not.

## Where we agree

We agree with management on the core diagnosis:
- the current cost base is too high,
- European demand is weaker,
- competition is tougher,
- and the company must regain room for investment and margin recovery.

We also agree with the family owners that:
- delaying hard decisions only compounds the problem,
- temporary patches are not enough,
- and the company needs a structurally viable industrial plan, not rhetoric.

And we agree with the works council, union, and state shareholder on one important point:
- a prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive,
- and a purely one-sided solution is unlikely to be durable.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be the starting premise
We cannot support a restructuring that rules out plant closures in advance if the economics require capacity exit.

If German operations are structurally overcapacity and some sites are uncompetitive, then preserving all sites as a principle would simply protect inefficiency. That is not acceptable to us as shareholders. It would leave the cost base structurally impaired and margins under pressure.

### 2) But closures alone are not a credible strategy either
At the same time, we do **not** support a blunt, conflict-heavy approach that destroys labour relations, triggers extended strikes, and wipes out operational value.

A headline closure that looks decisive but creates years of disruption is not value creation. We care about **risk-adjusted returns**, not just nominal cuts.

### 3) Temporary employee contributions are fine only if they are part of durable change
We are open to employee contributions, working-time flexibility, retirement schemes, voluntary departures, and similar tools.

But we would not back a package where:
- workers take a temporary hit,
- management declares victory,
- and the company then drifts back to the same cost structure.

That would be a cosmetic settlement, and it would not be enough.

### 4) Investment commitments must be tied to real restructuring, not used as a substitute
We welcome investment commitments in Germany, but only if they are backed by a competitive operating model.

We will not support committing capital into a structure that remains uncompetitive simply to buy peace. Investment without margin recovery is not a strategy.

## What would move us

A credible package would need to show, in concrete terms:
- substantial and durable cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- a clear path to return-on-sales improvement,
- limited and time-bound transition support from employees,
- and a settlement that avoids prolonged disruption.

That may include:
- selective plant consolidation where economically justified,
- voluntary exits and retirement pathways,
- working-time adjustments,
- targeted labor-cost relief,
- and binding product/investment commitments where they support future competitiveness.

What matters is not the label attached to the package, but whether the **end state** is competitive.

## Our limits

We will **not** support:
- a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- a settlement that preserves excess capacity indefinitely,
- a symbolic compromise that postpones restructuring,
- or an outcome where the company’s margins remain structurally impaired.

We also will not reward a process that produces prolonged conflict without a credible endpoint. That would destroy value and undermine confidence in management.

## To management
You need to come back with a package that is hard enough to restore competitiveness, but structured enough to be executable.

If you ask for maximal pain without enough certainty of delivery, you will lose support. Capital markets do not back uncertainty just because it is decisive.

## To the works council and union
Your refusal of forced redundancies and closures is understandable, but you cannot expect investors to underwrite a structure that fails to adapt.

If your position amounts to protecting every site and every legacy condition, then you are protecting the wrong thing. We care about the company’s long-term value, not legacy arrangements.

## To the state shareholder and family owners
You both speak about stewardship, and we agree with that framing. Stewardship means restoring competitiveness, not preserving comfort. If that requires difficult site decisions, then governance must not block them for political or emotional reasons.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to support a tough restructuring, but only if it is **credible, durable, and margin-accretive**.

What we will not support is:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a prolonged strike that erodes value,
- or a compromise that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

Bring a plan that clearly closes the competitiveness gap, and we can back it. Bring a compromise that merely delays the problem, and we will not.
