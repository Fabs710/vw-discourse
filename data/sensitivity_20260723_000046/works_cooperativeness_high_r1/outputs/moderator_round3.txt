### 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **real restructuring**; the status quo is not acceptable.
- **Cosmetic or purely symbolic measures** would not be sufficient.
- **Voluntary tools** are broadly accepted as part of the toolkit:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement / bridges,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - working-time flexibility.
- **Investment in future products and technology** is seen as necessary.
- There is shared recognition that **prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company**.
- An **independent expert assessment** was accepted as useful, especially its conclusion that a **no-closure package may be feasible in principle**, depending on the scale of the adjustment and the numbers.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state shareholder strongly reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain an option if needed economically.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour representatives reject them.
  - Management and capital representatives will not rule them out if voluntary tools prove insufficient.
- **Scope of employee contribution**
  - Labour is open only to **temporary, reversible, and fairly shared** contributions.
  - Management, owners, and investors want contributions that are **large enough to materially improve competitiveness**.
- **Guarantees**
  - Labour wants binding site and job guarantees first.
  - Management/owners want guarantees only if they fit a package that already restores competitiveness.
- **Role of the state shareholder**
  - The state wants to protect the industrial footprint and avoid hollowing out.
  - Others want the state to use its leverage more concretely, but not to block restructuring regardless of economics.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity and headcount must actually be removed** to close the cost gap.
- Whether that gap can be closed through:
  - voluntary exits,
  - attrition,
  - shift reductions,
  - internal reallocation,
  - and temporary labor-cost relief,
  **without closures**.
- The balance between:
  - **job and site security** for labour,
  - and **durable competitiveness and margin recovery** for management, owners, and investors.
- The **time horizon**:
  - labour seeks a phased, socially protected transition,
  - capital side wants a solution that is sufficiently fast and durable.
- The **fallback mechanism** if voluntary measures do not achieve the required scale.
- The lack of a shared **quantified plant-by-plant roadmap** showing:
  - headcount targets,
  - capacity changes,
  - volume allocation,
  - savings,
  - and investment commitments.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**. Specifically:
- present a **plant-by-plant restructuring model**,
- compare **no-closure**, **partial consolidation**, and **closure-based fallback** options,
- show the **headcount, capacity, cost, and timing implications** of each,
- and identify what combination of measures could realistically satisfy both:
  - the requirement for competitiveness, and
  - the demand for employment and site protection.
