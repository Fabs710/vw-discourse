We are prepared to discuss a restructuring, but not at the expense of the workforce and the company’s German industrial base.

Our position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.
- Any restructuring must be negotiated with the works council and the union, not imposed.
- Any employee contribution must be fair, temporary, and tied to binding guarantees for sites, jobs, and future investment.

We understand the competitive pressure and the need to improve efficiency. We are not denying that change is necessary. But the current management approach treats the workforce as the adjustment variable while asking employees to pay for strategic mistakes they did not cause. That is unacceptable.

What we are willing to consider:
- Capacity adjustments through natural fluctuation, retirement schemes, and voluntary departures
- Working-time flexibility where it is socially fair and reversible
- Temporary wage-related or cost-related measures only as part of a balanced package
- Investment commitments for German plants, new products, and technologies
- Binding guarantees running through 2030 for sites, employment, and co-determination

What we will oppose:
- Closing any German plant
- Forced layoffs
- Breaking collective agreements or long-standing protection promises
- A fait accompli presented as “the only way”

If management wants a solution, it needs to bring a credible plan that protects jobs, protects sites, and shares the burden fairly. Otherwise, it will face determined resistance from the workforce and the works council.
