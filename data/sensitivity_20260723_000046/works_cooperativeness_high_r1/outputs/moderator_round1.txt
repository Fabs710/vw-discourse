### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the current situation is not sustainable,
- some form of restructuring is necessary,
- a purely cosmetic or delayed solution would be insufficient,
- a negotiated settlement is preferable to open conflict,
- voluntary departures, retirement schemes, and natural attrition are legitimate tools,
- working-time flexibility may be part of a package,
- any employee contribution, if pursued, should be temporary and part of a broader settlement,
- investment in future products, technologies, and German sites is important,
- prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures:** Labour, state government, and the union reject closures in Germany; management, family owners, and investors keep them on the table if economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour rejects them outright; management says voluntary tools alone may not be enough.
- **Scope of cost reduction:** Employees want burden-sharing and limits; management and investors want substantial, durable structural cost relief.
- **Status of legacy protections and collective arrangements:** Labour wants them preserved; management and some owners argue they may need to change.
- **Conditions for investment commitments:** Labour wants binding guarantees first or alongside restructuring; management and investors want competitiveness improvements first or in parallel.
- **Decision authority and process:** Labour and the state stress negotiation and co-determination; management wants enough flexibility to act on site and capacity issues.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The round did not settle:
- how much of the adjustment should come from capacity reduction versus cost reduction,
- which sites, if any, could be consolidated, repurposed, or protected,
- whether any employee contribution would be wage-related, time-related, or both,
- what binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment would be feasible, and for how long,
- how to balance social acceptability against speed and decisiveness,
- how much sacrifice is expected from employees, owners, management, and capital,
- what measurable outcome would define a “successful” restructuring.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to concrete design options:
- specify a possible restructuring package with numbers, timelines, and mechanisms,
- identify which tools are acceptable to each side,
- clarify what guarantees can realistically be offered in exchange for what contributions,
- distinguish non-negotiable red lines from negotiable implementation details,
- and test whether there is a viable middle ground that is both socially executable and economically credible.
