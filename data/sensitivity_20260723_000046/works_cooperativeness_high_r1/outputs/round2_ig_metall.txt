We need to be very clear: the works council’s position is correct in substance, and the union stands with it. But I will also speak plainly to management, owners, and investors, because the current “all options on the table” rhetoric is not a neutral planning phrase — it is a threat to jobs, collective standards, and social peace.

## Where I agree

I agree that:
- the company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one;
- voluntary departures, retirement pathways, redeployment, and natural attrition are legitimate tools;
- working-time flexibility can be part of a fair package;
- investment in future products and technologies in Germany is necessary;
- prolonged conflict would damage the company and everyone’s interests.

I also acknowledge the basic economic pressure. We are not pretending that the current situation is comfortable or that nothing has to change.

## Where I disagree — sharply

### 1) Plant closures in Germany are not a negotiable starting point
Management, the family owners, and investors keep repeating that closures must remain “on the table.” From our side, that is not an acceptable framing. It is not “pragmatism”; it is a bargaining weapon.

We will not accept plant closures in Germany as a baseline assumption.

If management believes a site is uncompetitive, then it must first prove — transparently and in numbers — that all alternatives have been exhausted:
- capacity shifts,
- product allocation changes,
- working-time instruments,
- temporary cost measures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and conversion,
- voluntary exits and retirement options.

So far, we have not seen a credible case that the only answer is closure. And we will not let a closure logic become self-fulfilling through pressure.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are off limits
Management keeps implying that voluntary measures will not be enough. Then the company must redesign the restructuring, not force the workforce to absorb the gap through layoffs.

No compulsory redundancies.  
No “targeted” forced cuts.  
No euphemisms for job destruction.

If the cost base must come down, then the burden has to be shared through socially responsible tools, not through dismissals that break trust and destroy livelihoods.

### 3) Permanent wage erosion is unacceptable
I hear the calls for “durable” relief, but we will not accept a backdoor dismantling of the collective wage framework.

A temporary employee contribution can be discussed. A permanent wage cut cannot.

Any contribution from workers must be:
- temporary,
- limited,
- reversible,
- and tied to binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.

If employees give up income, then management, owners, and capital must also contribute — visibly and materially. The workforce will not pay alone for strategic errors made elsewhere.

## What would actually move us

A settlement is possible only if it contains all of the following:

### 1) Binding employment and site guarantees through 2030
- no plant closures in Germany;
- no compulsory redundancies;
- no hidden downsizing by outsourcing or production leakage;
- no unilateral dismantling of job-security standards.

### 2) A fair capacity adjustment
We are prepared to discuss capacity reduction through:
- natural fluctuation,
- early retirement,
- voluntary departure programs,
- redeployment,
- working-time reductions or flexibility where socially fair,
- temporary partial wage-related measures.

But the adjustment must not be achieved by fear.

### 3) Shared sacrifice
If employees contribute, then so must:
- management,
- owners,
- and capital.

That means real burden-sharing, not symbolic statements. Management costs, bonus structures, and capital allocation decisions must be part of the discussion.

### 4) Binding investment commitments
We need concrete commitments for:
- future products,
- EV and software-related investment,
- production allocation in Germany,
- qualification and transition measures for the workforce.

Not press releases. Not vague promises. Binding commitments.

### 5) A transparent industrial roadmap
We want plant-by-plant clarity on how competitiveness will be improved without closures or forced layoffs. If the company has a better plan than job destruction, show it.

## To management

You are right that the company needs structural improvement. But you are wrong if you think restructuring can be forced through by making closures and layoffs sound like the only serious option.

If you want a settlement, put forward a package that:
- preserves sites,
- protects employment,
- reduces costs fairly,
- and gives the workforce a real stake in the future.

If you insist on shifting the burden onto labour while keeping management and ownership insulated, then you will get resistance — and not just rhetorical resistance. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce is prepared to act.

## To the works council

We are aligned on the core principles:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective framework,
- and no unilateral restructuring.

I support the call for binding guarantees and a negotiated package. I would only add that if management refuses to move, the union will not hesitate to escalate industrial action in a targeted and strategic way.

## To the family owners

Long-term stewardship is not measured by how much restructuring pain you can impose on the workforce. It is measured by whether the company remains viable, socially legitimate, and capable of producing in Germany.

If you push closures as a governance reflex, you may win a short-term cost argument and lose the trust base that makes industrial recovery possible. We will not back that.

## To the state government

We appreciate your position and your blocking role. But this needs to be more than a general statement of concern. If you reject hollowing out the industrial base, then use that leverage to secure:
- site guarantees,
- employment protection,
- and a fair, negotiated transition.

Workers need that support to be real.

## To investors

We understand your focus on margins and credibility. But a settlement that detonates labour relations is not credible; it is fragile. A company cannot build durable returns on industrial conflict and social unrest.

If you want sustainable value, support a package that:
- improves competitiveness,
- avoids closures and forced layoffs,
- and preserves the collective bargaining framework that gives stability to the enterprise.

## Bottom line

The union is ready to negotiate seriously. But the terms are not negotiable in the way management seems to imagine.

Our framework is this:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of collective standards,
- fair, temporary employee contributions only within a balanced package,
- binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.

If that basis is accepted, we can work on the design.  
If not, then management is not proposing a restructuring — it is proposing that workers pay for the crisis alone. That will not be accepted.
