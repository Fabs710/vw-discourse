We are aligned with the works council on the core point: the workforce will not accept a restructuring that turns employees into the sole shock absorber for management’s strategic errors.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- No compulsory redundancies.
- No plant closures in Germany.
- No dismantling of the collective wage framework.
- No forced transfer of the crisis onto workers through one-sided wage cuts.

We recognise the pressure the company is under. We are not denying that costs must come down and capacity must be adjusted. But a durable solution cannot be built on threats, ultimatums, or making jobs disposable. If management wants social peace, it must negotiate a real package, not demand surrender.

Where we agree:
- Capacity reduction can be discussed if it is done through socially responsible tools.
- Voluntary departures, retirement schemes, and natural attrition are legitimate instruments.
- Working-time flexibility can be part of a broader settlement.
- A temporary employee contribution is conceivable if it is genuinely shared and tied to binding guarantees.

Where we disagree:
- Plant closures are a red line.
- Forced layoffs are a red line.
- Any attempt to weaken collective bargaining standards is a red line.
- A restructuring that asks workers to pay while ownership, management, and capital remain protected is not fair and will not be accepted.

If the company wants contributions from employees, then the burden must be shared. That means:
- Management must put real operational changes on the table.
- Owners and shareholders must also accept sacrifices.
- There must be binding commitments on sites, jobs, and future investment.
- Any employee contribution must be temporary, proportionate, and reversible.

The union is prepared to negotiate seriously, but we are also fully prepared to mobilise. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce expects action. If management continues down the path of closures and forced job cuts, it will face warning strikes and escalating industrial conflict.

So our message is straightforward: bring a fair package that protects jobs, protects sites, and shares the pain. Otherwise, there is no basis for agreement.
