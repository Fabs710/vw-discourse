I will speak plainly, because the situation demands it.

## First: what I accept
I accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**. I accept that:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement pathways,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and working-time flexibility

can all be part of a serious package.

I also accept that the company needs investment in future products and technology. And I agree that a prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company.

So yes: **we are prepared to negotiate a serious solution**.

## But our core line has not moved
And I want this understood clearly by management, owners, investors, and the state:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**
- **No restructuring by fait accompli.**
- **No bypassing of co-determination.**

These are not rhetorical preferences. They are the conditions under which a settlement is even possible.

## Where I disagree with management
Management keeps saying “all structural options must remain open.” That sounds neutral only if you ignore what it means in practice: it means keeping closures and forced layoffs as a threat over the workforce.

That is not how you build trust in a co-determined company.

You say the numbers require it. Then show the numbers in a way that proves:
- which alternatives were tested,
- why they were rejected,
- and why the workforce should believe that closing a German plant is truly unavoidable.

So far, I have not seen that proof. What I have seen is pressure to accept plant closures as a starting point. That is unacceptable.

## Where I disagree with owners and investors
The family holding and capital markets keep speaking about “durable” cost relief.

Let me be blunt: **durable for whom?**

If “durable” means permanently weakening wages, employment security, and site security so the company can preserve margins, then we reject that logic.

Employees are not here to permanently absorb the consequences of strategic missteps made elsewhere. We will not pay with jobs and rights so that others can call the result “discipline.”

A temporary employee contribution may be discussable — but only if:
- it is strictly time-limited,
- it is reversible,
- it is matched by real sacrifice from management and ownership,
- and it is tied to binding guarantees on sites, jobs, and investment.

## Where I disagree with the state shareholder
We appreciate that the state rejects hollowing out the industrial base. But support must become leverage.

If the state shareholder sees the social and regional damage of closures, then it should say clearly: **no plant closures in Germany**. Not “preferably avoid.” Not “only if possible.” If the special statute and blocking position mean anything, then they must be used to protect the industrial footprint.

Otherwise the workforce is being asked to trust speeches rather than power.

## What would actually move us
Not general assurances. Not vague talk about “balance.” We need binding commitments.

### We would consider a negotiated package only if it contains all of the following:
1. **Binding site guarantees through 2030**
   - no German plant closures,
   - no outsourcing that empties sites,
   - no covert downsizing through production relocation.

2. **No compulsory redundancies through 2030**
   - adjustment only through voluntary tools and socially responsible measures.

3. **A genuine co-determination role**
   - works council and union must shape the restructuring, not merely react to it.

4. **Investment commitments for German sites**
   - concrete allocation of future products, technologies, and production lines.

5. **A fair, temporary employee contribution only inside a balanced package**
   - if labour contributes, then management, owners, and capital must contribute materially too.

6. **A transparent plant-by-plant industrial roadmap**
   - not slogans, but numbers and mechanisms.

## On the claim that voluntary tools may not be enough
Management and investors keep implying that voluntary measures cannot deliver the necessary scale.

Then prove it. Put the numbers on the table:
- how many jobs must be reduced,
- by when,
- through which instruments,
- at which sites,
- and why no alternative combination can deliver the same result without closures or forced redundancies.

Without that, “voluntary tools are insufficient” is just a pretext for imposing the adjustment on the workforce.

If a specialist analysis is needed on whether a non-closure industrial roadmap can meet the cost targets, then we should use it.

## My response to each side

### To management
If you want a deal, stop presenting closures and forced layoffs as the benchmark of seriousness.

A serious restructuring can be achieved through:
- capacity shifts,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- early retirement,
- voluntary exits,
- product allocation changes,
- and fair temporary contributions.

If you insist that closures remain on the table, then you are making confrontation unavoidable.

### To the family owners
Long-term stewardship is not measured by how much social pain can be imposed quickly. It is measured by whether the company remains productive, legitimate, and able to invest in Germany.

A governance strategy that burns trust with the workforce may look efficient on paper and be destructive in reality.

### To the state shareholder
You have a blocking role. Use it. If you believe in regional responsibility, then back a settlement that protects all German plants and no compulsory redundancies.

If not, then the workforce will quite rightly ask what your special status is for.

### To investors
You want durable returns. So do we — but not at the price of destroying the social foundation of the company.

A company cannot generate durable value by turning industrial peace into a casualty.

## Bottom line
The works council is ready to negotiate a real restructuring.

But the framework is non-negotiable:
- **German plants stay open**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **co-determination respected**
- **job-security framework preserved**
- **burdens shared fairly**
- **investment secured**

If management can bring forward a credible package within those limits, we will engage seriously.

If not, then let there be no confusion: we will resist with every legitimate means available.
