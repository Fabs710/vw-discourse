## Core impasse

The central impasse is now narrow and specific:

1. **Management / owners / investors** want a restructuring that produces a **durable, quantified reduction in cost and capacity**, and they will not rule out closures or compulsory redundancies if the gap cannot otherwise be closed.

2. **Works council / union** insist on **binding no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy guarantees**, plus investment commitments, and they want the burden of proof on management to show why a closure-free path fails.

3. **State shareholder** is trying to hold a middle line: preserve the industrial base and regional legitimacy, while still accepting that the company needs real restructuring.

So the plateau is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **what kinds of restructuring are admissible before the numbers are shown**:
- Are closures and forced redundancies still “open” from the start?
- Or must the company first prove that a closure-free package is insufficient?

That sequencing dispute is the real blockage.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) **Conditional no-closure package with a hard review trigger**
A potential bridge is a package that begins with:
- no German plant closures for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- voluntary exits, retirement bridges, redeployment, shift changes, and working-time flexibility as the primary tools,
- plus binding investment commitments.

In return, the package would include:
- a **clear numeric review trigger** after a fixed period,
- and an agreed process for revisiting structural options if the savings, headcount reduction, or utilization targets are missed.

This allows labour to keep site-security as the starting rule, while management retains a structured path to revisit the question if the economics do not close.

---

### 2) **Partial consolidation inside the footprint, with site-specific specialization**
A second bridge is to avoid the binary “all sites stay unchanged vs. closures” framing by agreeing to:
- consolidate selected lines or functions within existing plants,
- reduce shifts at underutilized sites,
- transfer model allocation to fewer, better-utilized facilities,
- and centralize certain support functions.

This would aim to deliver real capacity reduction **without immediate plant closures**, while still giving management a meaningful cost-base improvement. It would also let the parties test whether the footprint can be made viable before any closure decision is considered.

---

### 3) **Tripartite burden-sharing package with temporary concessions and matched guarantees**
A third compromise is a balanced exchange:
- workforce: temporary, time-limited contribution;
- management/owners/investors: binding investment commitments and a quantified no-offshoring / German-site development roadmap;
- state shareholder: political and governance backing for the restructuring, conditioned on the agreed safeguards.

This would only work if the employee contribution is matched by visible commitments from the other sides, so that labour is not seen as the sole shock absorber.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To **Group & Brand Management**
**What specific headcount and capacity reduction numbers, by plant, would make you stop treating closures as necessary, and can you publish the threshold that would trigger a closure-free agreement?**

### To the **Works Council (Betriebsrat)**
**If the plant-by-plant model shows that a no-closure package still leaves a measurable cost gap, what additional operational measures inside the existing footprint would you be prepared to negotiate to close it?**

### To the **Industrial Union**
**What size, duration, and design of a temporary employee contribution would you regard as materially meaningful rather than symbolic, and what concrete reciprocal commitments would you require in return?**

### To the **Regional State Government**
**Will you commit to using your blocking role only after the plant-by-plant model is presented, and will you define in advance what minimum safeguards would make a restructuring acceptable to you?**

### To the **Controlling Family Holding**
**What level of quantified improvement in margins, utilization, and fixed-cost reduction would you require before you would accept a closure-free plan as durable?**

### To the **Institutional Investors**
**What financial metrics—margin, cash conversion, return threshold, or cost-base reduction—would be sufficient for you to support a non-closure settlement, provided the numbers are credible and enforced?**

### To the **Independent Expert**
**What are the minimum quantitative thresholds—on headcount reduction, utilization improvement, and fixed-cost savings—below which you would consider a no-closure solution economically implausible?**

## Facilitator next step

The most useful immediate next move would be:
**agree on the scope and format of the plant-by-plant model, the assumptions to be used, and the decision threshold that determines whether a closure-free package is still viable.**

That would convert the dispute from principle-based stalemate into a testable negotiation.
