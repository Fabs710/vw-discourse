Let me respond directly, because the situation is too serious for vague language.

## Our position remains unchanged on the core points

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**
- **No fait accompli.**
- **No restructuring imposed against co-determination.**

Management keeps saying “all options must be on the table.” From where the workforce stands, that is not a neutral statement. It is a threat to plants, jobs, and livelihoods. We will not normalise that.

## Where I agree with others

I agree with management, the owners, the state shareholder, and investors on one point: **the company needs a real solution, not a cosmetic one**. We also agree that:

- voluntary departures, retirement pathways, and natural attrition are legitimate tools;
- working-time flexibility can be discussed if it is socially fair and reversible;
- investment in future products and technologies is essential;
- prolonged conflict would damage the company.

So yes, we are prepared to negotiate a serious package.

But serious does **not** mean accepting closures or forced layoffs.

## Where I disagree, sharply

### 1) On plant closures
Management says closures may be necessary. The family owners and investors repeat that line.

We reject that premise.

German plants are not disposable line items. If there is overcapacity, then **reduce capacity through production shifts, working-time tools, allocation changes, and voluntary instruments**. Do not start from closures. Do not threaten closures as leverage. And do not pretend that closing a plant is the only “hard” option.

If a site is to be discussed at all, then the burden is on management to show, transparently and in numbers, that every alternative has been exhausted. So far, we have not seen that.

### 2) On compulsory redundancies
Management says voluntary tools may not be enough. That is precisely the point where we say: then the restructuring design is wrong.

If the company needs adjustment, it must be achieved by:
- natural fluctuation,
- voluntary exit programs,
- phased retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- shorter working time where necessary and agreed,
- and a fair temporary contribution.

What we will not accept is turning employees into the shock absorber through forced job cuts.

### 3) On “durable” cost reduction
I keep hearing the word “durable” used as a justification for permanent concessions by labour.

Durable for whom?

If the aim is to restore competitiveness, then the burden must also be durable on the side of management decisions, product strategy, procurement, and operational efficiency. Employees cannot permanently pay for strategic mistakes they did not make.

A temporary employee contribution may be discussable — but only if:
- it is strictly time-limited,
- it is reversible,
- it is paired with binding guarantees,
- and it does not become the back door to permanent wage erosion.

## What would actually move us

Not general assurances. Not “we hear your concerns.” We need **binding commitments**.

We would consider a negotiated package only if it includes all of the following:

1. **Binding site guarantees through 2030**
   - no German plant closures,
   - no outsourcing that empties sites,
   - no hidden downsizing by attrition plus production relocation.

2. **No compulsory redundancies through 2030**
   - adjustment only via voluntary instruments and socially responsible measures.

3. **A genuine co-determination role**
   - the works council and union must shape the restructuring, not just be informed after the fact.

4. **Investment commitments for German sites**
   - future products, technologies, and production lines must be allocated here, not merely promised in press statements.

5. **A fair, temporary employee contribution only within a balanced package**
   - if labour is expected to contribute, then owners, management, and capital must also contribute;
   - the contribution must be limited in time and tied to the guarantees above.

6. **A transparent industrial roadmap**
   - not slogans, but a plant-by-plant perspective showing how competitiveness will be improved without closures.

## On the arguments from management, owners, and investors

### To management
You say the company cannot preserve the current cost base. Fine. We are not defending the current cost base as a sacred object.

But you are trying to solve a strategic problem by attacking the workforce first. That is exactly what we reject.

If you want a viable package, bring us:
- concrete operational improvements,
- real burden-sharing from management and owners,
- and a restructuring plan that does not sacrifice sites and jobs.

### To the family owners
You say you support a real restructuring and cannot back “sentiment.” Neither can we back a strategy that destroys the company’s social foundation.

Long-term stewardship means preserving the productive base in Germany. If you push for closures now, you may win a short-term accounting gain and lose the company’s industrial legitimacy and workforce trust for years.

### To the state government
We appreciate your support for sites and employment. But support must become action. If plant closures are blocked in principle, then make that position count in governance. Workers need more than expressions of concern.

### To investors
You keep speaking about margins and credibility. We understand that. But a restructuring that detonates industrial peace is not credible; it is reckless.

If you want durable returns, support a package that:
- protects the sites,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- and gives the workforce a stake in the future instead of a bill for the past.

## What we are prepared to discuss

We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing **destructive restructuring**.

We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions or flexibility where socially balanced;
- redeployment and qualification measures;
- early retirement and voluntary departure schemes;
- temporary, fair employee contributions;
- productivity improvements agreed with the works council;
- investment and product commitments for German plants.

## What we are not prepared to discuss

- closures dressed up as “consolidation”;
- forced layoffs dressed up as “targeted adjustment”;
- permanent wage cuts;
- hollowing out the job-security framework;
- or a plan where labour pays and capital keeps all options open.

## Bottom line

If management wants a settlement, it must start from this reality:

**The German plants stay open. The workforce is not sacrificed. Co-determination is not bypassed.**

If that is accepted, then we can talk seriously about how to improve competitiveness through socially responsible instruments.

If it is not accepted, then there is no basis for agreement — and the workforce will continue to resist with all legitimate means at its disposal.
