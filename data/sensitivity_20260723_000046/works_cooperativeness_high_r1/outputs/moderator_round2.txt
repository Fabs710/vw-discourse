## Moderator Summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a symbolic one;
- **investment in future products, technologies, and production capability** is necessary;
- **voluntary tools** such as natural attrition, early retirement, redeployment, and voluntary exit programs are legitimate;
- **working-time flexibility** can be part of a negotiated package;
- a **prolonged industrial conflict** would be damaging for all sides;
- any employee contribution, if used, should be discussed as part of a broader package rather than in isolation.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures in Germany**: labour, and the state shareholder, reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain possible if economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: labour and the state reject them; management and owners say voluntary measures may be insufficient.
- **Scope of labour cost relief**: management, owners, and investors want a **durable structural cost reduction**; labour insists any contribution must be **temporary, limited, and reversible**.
- **Role of guarantees**: labour wants binding site and employment guarantees first; management and owners argue guarantees cannot freeze the current structure before the economics are fixed.
- **Decision logic**: labour and the state emphasize co-determination, social stability, and regional responsibility; management, owners, and investors emphasize competitiveness, capacity reduction, and margin restoration.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- **how much capacity reduction is actually required**, and at which sites;
- **whether voluntary measures alone can achieve the needed scale and speed**;
- **what level of temporary employee contribution would be meaningful enough** for management and investors, while still acceptable to labour;
- **what binding investment commitments can be made without pre-judging site decisions**;
- **how to reconcile site/job protection with the demand for credible competitiveness gains**;
- **what concrete evidence or numbers would justify or rule out closures**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
For the next round, participants should move from principles to a **joint structured package**:
- define the **required cost and capacity targets** in numbers;
- test whether **voluntary instruments alone** can reach them;
- identify which **site guarantees** are feasible, and under what conditions;
- specify any **temporary employee contribution** and matching burden-sharing from management/owners;
- outline a **plant-by-plant industrial roadmap** showing how competitiveness would improve without a purely destructive outcome.

A productive next step would be to ask each side to propose, in concrete terms, **one acceptable package element and one non-negotiable limit**, so the remaining gap can be narrowed.
