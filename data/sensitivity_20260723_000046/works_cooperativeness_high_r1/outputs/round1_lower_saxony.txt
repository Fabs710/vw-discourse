Thank you. I will be equally clear from the standpoint of the regional state government and shareholder.

We recognise the hard reality management describes: the market has changed, costs are under pressure, and the company needs a credible path to competitiveness. We are not denying that restructuring is necessary. But we are also not prepared to accept a solution that treats German plants and jobs as disposable.

### Where I agree
- The status quo is not sustainable.
- A purely cosmetic agreement would only delay the next crisis.
- The company needs a durable improvement in cost and capacity.
- A negotiated package is preferable to an uncontrolled conflict.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the idea that plant closures must be kept fully on the table as a default starting point. For us, closing German plants is not a neutral technical option. It has long-term consequences for the regional economy, for the industrial base, and for the company’s legitimacy as a German anchor employer.

I also disagree that employees alone should be asked to deliver the adjustment. If the company wants the workforce to contribute, then management and owners must show that they are also sharing responsibility through:
- operational improvements,
- investment commitments,
- and a restructuring design that protects the company’s future rather than simply shrinking it.

### Our position
As a shareholder with a blocking minority and special co-determination role, we will not support any solution that:
- guts regional employment,
- removes or hollow-outs key German sites,
- or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not a tactical posture. It reflects our responsibility to the regional economy and to social partnership.

### What we could support
We can support a restructuring if it is built around:
- capacity reduction through voluntary instruments, retirement schemes, and natural attrition,
- working-time flexibility where it is fair and reversible,
- a temporary and genuinely shared employee contribution,
- productivity and operational improvements,
- binding investment in German plants and future products,
- and site and employment guarantees for a defined period.

We are open to difficult trade-offs, but not to a one-sided solution.

### On management’s argument
Management says the numbers require decisive change. That may be true. But “decisive” does not automatically mean “closure first.” It also means designing a settlement that the workforce will accept and that the company can live with over the long term. A conflict that destroys trust, triggers strikes, and permanently weakens the company is not a success story.

### My message to all sides
If you want a durable settlement, bring forward a restructuring that preserves the company’s role in Germany, protects a meaningful share of jobs, and shares the burden fairly. If the proposal is essentially closures and forced cuts dressed up as necessity, then you should expect resistance from the state shareholder as well as from labour.

We are prepared to negotiate seriously. We are not prepared to endorse a hollowing-out of the company’s regional industrial base.
