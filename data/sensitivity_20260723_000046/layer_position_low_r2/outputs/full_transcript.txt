[Expert: Independent industrial restructuring aud — Round 3]
## Expert assessment: can a credible restructuring be done without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies?

Yes, **in principle** it can be done — but only under specific conditions, and not in every case.

From an industrial restructuring perspective, the key issue is not whether a company can reduce costs without closures. It is whether the **required savings and capacity reduction are large enough**, **fast enough**, and **durable enough** to restore competitiveness **while keeping the current footprint**. That depends on the actual gap between current German-site economics and the target cost base.

### What can be done without closures
A non-closure restructuring package can generate material savings through:
- **capacity reduction inside the network**: lower shifts, fewer lines, lower utilization
- **product allocation changes**: moving models to higher-efficiency sites or concentrating variants
- **working-time measures**: shorter hours, shift changes, temporary reductions
- **voluntary exits / natural attrition**
- **redeployment and reskilling**
- **temporary wage or bonus moderation**
- **process productivity gains and overhead reduction**
- **capex prioritization** and stricter investment discipline

These measures are real and can be significant. In large German automotive plants, labour and fixed-cost levers can produce meaningful relief if utilization is the problem.

### Where the constraint usually bites
However, non-closure restructuring has limits:

1. **If overcapacity is structural and large**, capacity cuts inside the existing network may not be enough.  
   You can lower output, but you still carry fixed-site costs.

2. **If German cost levels are materially above competitor benchmarks**, labour measures alone rarely close the entire gap.  
   Savings from concessions are often helpful, but not always sufficient to offset structural overhead and complexity.

3. **If product volume recovery is uncertain**, management may seek irreversible structural actions rather than temporary concessions.  
   Investors typically prefer actions that permanently lower the cost base.

4. **If the company needs cash and margin improvement quickly**, negotiated internal measures may take too long or deliver too little.

### On the stakeholder positions
- The **employee side** is correct that overcapacity does not automatically require closures. A company should first test internal alternatives.
- **Management and investors** are also correct that a restructuring is only credible if it closes the actual economic gap, not just postpones it.
- The dispute is therefore not about principle, but about **quantified feasibility**.

### What would make a non-closure deal credible
A non-closure solution would need all of the following:
- a **site-by-site economic model**
- an **independent capacity audit**
- a **quantified savings target**
- a **clear timeline**
- **binding workforce contributions**
- **management and owner concessions**
- **product and investment commitments**
- a mechanism for **re-opening talks if the gap remains**
