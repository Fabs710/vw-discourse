I hear the position from works council and union, but I need to be equally direct: **keeping every plant open and ruling out compulsory redundancies in advance is not compatible with the cost reality we face**.

We are not in a normal cyclical slowdown. We are dealing with a **structural competitiveness gap**:
- German factory costs are materially above target,
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we answer that with only working-time tweaks, voluntary exits, and symbolic contributions, we will **not** restore the company to a sustainable cost base. That would protect the status quo for a while and damage the business more deeply later.

### Where I agree
I agree that a solution must be:
- durable, not cosmetic,
- negotiated, not imposed by fiat,
- and capable of funding future products and technology.

I also agree that employees should not be asked to carry the burden alone. Management, owners, and the organisation as a whole have to contribute.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with three central points in your opening positions:

1. **No plant closures as a principle**
   We cannot pre-commit to keeping every German site open if the economics do not justify that footprint. Overcapacity is part of the problem. A restructuring that leaves all capacity intact is not a restructuring.

2. **No compulsory redundancies as an absolute**
   I would prefer to avoid forced layoffs where possible, but I am not prepared to rule them out categorically if we cannot achieve the necessary structural savings otherwise.

3. **Binding guarantees through 2030 without sufficient economic adjustment**
   We cannot offer long-term guarantees that freeze the current structure while the company remains uncompetitive. Guarantees have to be earned by a credible and sufficient cost contribution.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am not closing the door on alternatives. If you bring me a package that delivers **real, durable savings**, I am prepared to consider:
- capacity reductions instead of blanket site closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- a meaningful but temporary employee contribution,
- and investment commitments tied to the restructuring.

But I need to be clear: these measures must add up to **a structurally lower cost base**. A package that avoids closures on paper but leaves us with the same fixed costs and the same overcapacity will not be acceptable.

### My bottom line
If we want to protect the company’s future, we need to confront reality:
- the cost base must come down,
- capacity must be reduced,
- and labour costs must contribute in a material way.

If the employee side is willing to engage on that basis, we can negotiate seriously. If the only answer is “no closures, no redundancies, no fundamental change,” then there is no viable path forward.
