I appreciate the candor from management, works council, and the union. I will be equally direct.

The regional government is **not prepared to support a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany or a one-sided destruction of jobs**. That is not just a political preference; it would damage the regional economy, undermine trust in co-determination, and create long-term costs that are likely to outweigh any short-term balance-sheet relief.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **the company does need a durable restructuring**. Denial is not an option. The cost base must be addressed, and the company must regain competitiveness if it is to keep investing in future products and technologies.

I also agree with the works council and union that:
- employees should not carry the burden alone,
- any solution must be negotiated,
- and a social-partnership approach is preferable to a confrontation that destroys trust.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that the only credible path is plant closures and forced redundancies.

That is too narrow, too risky, and too damaging. A restructuring that gutting regional employment is not a serious solution from our perspective, because it would:
- weaken the industrial base in the state,
- create lasting political and social fallout,
- and risk hollowing out the company’s long-term capability.

I also cannot accept being put in a position where the state is expected to endorse a fait accompli. Under the governance structure here, the regional state is not a decorative shareholder. We have a legitimate co-determination role and a blocking minority for a reason, and we will use it if necessary to prevent irreversible damage.

### What I can support
I am open to a package that achieves real savings **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**, for example through:
- capacity reductions within the existing plant network,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and reskilling,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and investment commitments tied to job preservation.

But I am not interested in cosmetic measures. If management wants support, it must bring a plan that is credible **and** socially sustainable.

### My conditions for serious backing
For me, the following are essential:
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Protection of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
- **Binding assurances for sites and jobs**
- **A fair burden-sharing model involving management, owners, and workforce**
- **A restructuring path that preserves the company as a regional anchor**

### To management
If your proposal still depends on closures, then it will meet resistance from us. You have not convinced me that those are unavoidable, only that they are convenient from a managerial standpoint. That is not enough.

### To the works council and union
Your core line is understandable and, in important respects, justified. But I would also ask for realism on the need for change. If we want to protect sites and employment, we need a restructuring that genuinely improves competitiveness. That will require contributions from the workforce side too. The question is not whether there will be change, but **what kind of change**.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to engage constructively on a negotiated restructuring that protects jobs, sites, and co-determination while restoring competitiveness.

We are **not** prepared to endorse plant closures, forced layoffs, or a model that shifts the burden onto the workforce and the region.

If management wants a serious dialogue, it should present an alternative that preserves the industrial footprint and sets out how the needed savings will be achieved without crossing those red lines.
