Management, the owners, and the market may want to frame this as a simple cost story. It is not.

Let me be precise.

### Where I agree
I agree with one point: this company faces a structural challenge and cannot simply continue as before. We are not denying the pressure, and we are not pretending that no change is needed.

I also agree that any solution must be durable and must secure the company’s future investment capacity. That is exactly why we insist on a negotiated settlement that protects the workforce and the industrial base.

### Where I disagree
I disagree fundamentally with the idea that “credible restructuring” must mean **plant closures in Germany** or a **reserve right to compulsory redundancies**.

That is not a condition of competitiveness. It is a choice — and one we reject.

Management has still not demonstrated that closing German plants is unavoidable rather than simply the easiest way to shift the burden onto workers. And neither the investors nor the family holding have shown why the same savings could not be achieved through a real mix of:
- capacity reduction within the existing network,
- shorter or more flexible working time,
- voluntary instruments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and a fair contribution from management and capital.

If the argument is only “we need savings,” then show the savings path. Do not start by demanding irreversible destruction of sites and jobs.

### On compulsory redundancies
The employee side will not accept operational layoffs. That remains our line.

Why? Because once you open the door to forced redundancies, you are no longer talking about a fair restructuring — you are threatening livelihoods and breaking the trust that holds this company together. We are not prepared to negotiate the workforce into insecurity as the price of management’s strategic errors.

### On plant closures
We reject the claim that some German plant must be closed to make the numbers work. That has not been proven here.

If management believes otherwise, then put the numbers on the table:
- which capacity is supposedly surplus,
- what exact savings each option delivers,
- what happens to products, employment, and investment at each site,
- and why alternative measures are insufficient.

Until then, closure talk is not a plan. It is pressure tactics.

### On the round’s “trade-offs”
The moderator is right that we need numbers, not slogans. But numbers do not cancel red lines.

Our non-negotiable conditions remain:
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**

Those are not bargaining flourishes. They are the basis for any serious discussion.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not refusing change. We are prepared to discuss measures that genuinely protect sites and jobs, including:
- capacity adjustments within the current plant network,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary exit options only,
- redeployment across the group,
- qualification and reskilling,
- temporary employee contribution if it is fair and shared,
- productivity measures that do not destroy the workforce.

But let me be clear: these are options for protecting the company **without** crossing our red lines, not a path to get to closures by another name.

### To management
If you want serious negotiation, stop presenting closures and layoffs as inevitabilities. Come back with a credible non-closure scenario, quantified and transparent, and with binding guarantees attached.

### To the controlling family and investors
You talk about long-term value, but value is not created by hollowing out the German industrial base and breaking trust with the workforce. A company that tears up its own social contract will pay for that in conflict, instability, and lost capability.

### To the regional state government
You are right to resist closures, but resistance must not stop at words. Use your blocking position. We expect you to do so if management tries to force through plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a serious restructuring.
We are **not** ready to negotiate away plant security, job security, or co-determination.

If management wants savings, there are alternatives.
If management wants closures and layoffs, it will meet our full resistance.
