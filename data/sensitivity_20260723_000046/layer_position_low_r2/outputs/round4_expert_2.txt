As the independent restructuring auditor, I can respond to the questions aimed at my mandate.

## What I can assess
I can assess:
- site-by-site cost structures,
- utilization and overcapacity,
- fixed vs. variable cost behavior,
- closure vs. non-closure scenarios,
- credible ranges of savings,
- transition and one-off restructuring costs,
- and whether the stated restructuring logic is internally consistent.

I cannot validate labor-relations strategy, legal enforceability, political feasibility, or public messaging.

# Direct response to the round-4 questions

## 1) What minimum data set and methodology would you require to determine whether a non-closure restructuring is sufficient?
I would require, at minimum, for each German site and major production line:

### Required data
- annual and monthly output by model and line
- installed capacity and practical capacity
- current utilization by shift
- product allocation assumptions through the next product cycle
- direct labour hours per vehicle
- direct labour cost per vehicle
- fixed manufacturing overhead
- maintenance and utilities
- logistics and internal transport cost
- depreciation and site-specific fixed charges
- capex already committed and future capex required
- headcount by function and site
- labor flexibility constraints relevant to deployment and shift use
- one-time restructuring costs for each measure

### Methodology
1. Establish a baseline P&L for each site and line.
2. Split costs into:
   - truly variable,
   - semi-variable,
   - and fixed/step-fixed.
3. Quantify the effect of each measure separately:
   - shift reduction,
   - working-time changes,
   - voluntary exits,
   - redeployment,
   - product transfer,
   - overhead reduction,
   - purchasing/logistics simplification,
   - temporary compensation measures.
4. Test a **non-closure scenario** first.
5. Compare it with a **partial closure / consolidation scenario**.
6. Include:
   - implementation timing,
   - one-off costs,
   - labour fallout risk,
   - and sensitivity to volume and mix.

The key question is not whether savings exist in theory. It is whether the residual gap after realistic internal measures is small enough to be bridgeable without closing a plant.

---

## 2) Is a non-closure restructuring possible in principle?
Yes. That is economically plausible.

A non-closure package can work if enough of the following are real and sizeable:
- product reallocation,
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- redeployment,
- overhead reduction,
- logistics simplification,
- and durable labour-cost contribution.

However, “possible in principle” is not the same as “sufficient in practice.” That depends on the numbers.

---

## 3) What would determine whether it is sufficient in practice?
Four things:

1. **Size of the structural gap**
   - If the group’s German footprint is materially overcapacity, internal measures may not fully close it.

2. **How much of the gap is fixed-cost driven**
   - Plants with high fixed-cost absorption losses are harder to repair without footprint change.

3. **How much capacity can be removed without closure**
   - If excess capacity is mainly in underused lines or shifts, there is room.
   - If it is tied to whole-site economics, the options narrow.

4. **Durability**
   - Temporary wage or working-time concessions are not enough if the competitive problem persists for multiple years.

---

## 4) Is management’s claim that closures are necessary already proven?
No, not on the information provided.

What is present here is a diagnosis of structural overcapacity and high cost. That does **not** by itself prove closure is required.

To prove that, management would need to show:
- the site-by-site residual gap after internal measures,
- why that gap cannot be closed by capacity reduction inside the network,
- and why the one-off cost of closure is justified versus the ongoing cost of keeping the site.

Without that, closure is an option, not a demonstrated necessity.

---

## 5) Is the employee side’s claim that a non-closure solution is possible already proven?
Also no.

The employee side is economically credible in saying non-closure is possible **in principle**, but that is not yet proof that it is sufficient across the whole footprint.

That requires:
- detailed site economics,
- real capacity transfer options,
- and quantified burden-sharing that closes the gap.

Without those numbers, a blanket no-closure position is a preference, not a proven outcome.

---

## 6) What is the most likely economic conclusion, absent full data?
The only defensible conclusion is:

- A non-closure restructuring is plausible.
- It may be sufficient if the gap is moderate and much of the problem is utilization, overhead, and product mix.
- It may be insufficient if the gap is large, structural, and concentrated in one or more underperforming sites.

I cannot responsibly say which case applies without the site data.

---

# What I would flag as internally inconsistent
A few points are economically inconsistent or at least unproven:

## A) “No closures” plus “no compulsory redundancies” plus “full protection” plus “large durable cost reduction”
Those four together may be possible, but only if internal measures deliver unusually large savings. That is not impossible, but it is a high bar.

## B) “Conditional guarantees later” versus “guarantees first”
Both sides are partly talking past each other. Economically, guarantees can be meaningful only if they sit on top of a quantified package. But from a restructuring standpoint, employees will not treat them as credible if they are easily reversible. That is a sequencing issue, not a pure cost issue.

## C) “Temporary employee contributions” treated as durable restructuring
Temporary concessions improve short-term cost. They do not solve structural overcapacity unless they are paired with lasting capacity and overhead changes.

---

# Auditor’s bottom line
My neutral assessment is:

- **A non-closure restructuring is possible in principle.**
- **It is not yet proven sufficient.**
- **Closures are not yet proven necessary.**
- The decisive test is a **site-by-site residual gap analysis** after realistic internal measures and burden-sharing.
