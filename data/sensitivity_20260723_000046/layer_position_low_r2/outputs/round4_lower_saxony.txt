I will be very clear.

I agree with the expert and with the employee side on one crucial point: **a non-closure restructuring is possible in principle**, and the burden of proof is still on management to show why internal measures would not be enough. I also agree that the workforce should not bear the burden alone. Management, owners, and the company itself must contribute materially.

But I do **not** agree with management, the family holding, or investors that closures and compulsory redundancies should remain an open-ended fallback while employees are asked to give concessions first. That is not “execution credibility”; it is leverage against the workforce.

## Where I stand
From the state’s perspective, the line remains unchanged:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No hollowing out of the region’s industrial base**

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are the conditions for any responsible restructuring.

## On management’s argument
Management keeps saying the numbers may force closures. Perhaps. But so far I have not seen a **site-by-site, independently verified case** proving that internal measures cannot close the gap.

“Overcapacity” by itself does not justify closure. It just means the company needs to reduce capacity **somehow**. That “somehow” must be tested exhaustively inside the existing network first:
- product allocation changes
- shift reductions
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- temporary employee contributions
- management and owner contributions

If those measures are insufficient, then show it with hard data. Until then, closures are not a proven necessity.

## On the family holding
I understand the stewardship language, but stewardship is not just about cost discipline. It is also about preserving the company’s social capital, trust, and industrial base. A strategy that damages the workforce and region may look tough, but it can still destroy long-term value.

If you want to act like a long-term owner, then support a solution that protects the sites, jobs, and co-determination framework — not one that keeps closure threats alive as bargaining pressure.

## On institutional investors
You are right that cosmetic restructuring would be value-destructive. But you are wrong if you think the only way to restore value is to keep closure and layoff threats on the table.

Long-term value also comes from:
- industrial peace
- skill retention
- stable labour relations
- and a committed regional production base

A no-closure solution can be value-enhancing if it is quantified, serious, and burden-shared. What I will not support is a “credible” plan that is only credible because it threatens the workforce.

## On the works council and union
I support your core red lines:
- no closures
- no forced redundancies
- no erosion of the collective framework

I also support your insistence that any contribution from the workforce must be matched by real concessions from management and owners.

Where I would add one point: if we are going to keep the company competitive and rooted in the region, then we need a settlement that is not just defensive, but **constructive**:
- investment commitments
- product commitments
- retraining and redeployment
- productivity gains inside the network
- and a durable industrial strategy for the German sites

## On the expert assessment
The expert is right to say the real question is whether the verified savings gap can be closed internally. That is the question that must be answered — not whether closures are politically easier.

So I support a proper audit. But it must be an audit of **non-closure options first**, not a disguised pre-authorization for plant closures.

[SUMMON: independent site-by-site restructuring auditor with plant economics, capacity utilization, fixed-cost analysis, and comparison of non-closure versus closure scenarios]

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a tough restructuring package if it contains all of the following:
- binding no-closure commitment for all German sites
- binding no-compulsory-redundancy commitment
- preserved co-determination and job-security framework
- substantial capacity reduction **within** the existing network
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits and redeployment
- meaningful temporary employee contribution
- management and owner burden-sharing
- investment and product commitments through 2030

That is not softness. That is a serious restructuring that protects the company’s role as a regional anchor while restoring competitiveness.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closure as a standing option
- compulsory redundancies as a normal fallback
- conditional guarantees that can be withdrawn after workforce concessions
- an “exception process” that becomes a back door for plant closures
- a settlement that treats workers as the shock absorber while management and owners keep maximum freedom

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve everything unchanged. I am here to protect the region, the jobs, and the long-term health of the company.

So my message is simple:

1. Show us the numbers properly.
2. Test the non-closure path honestly.
3. Make management, owners, and employees all contribute.
4. Keep closures and forced redundancies off the table unless and until the evidence truly proves no alternative exists — and even then, the state will not accept a settlement that guts regional employment.

That is my position.
