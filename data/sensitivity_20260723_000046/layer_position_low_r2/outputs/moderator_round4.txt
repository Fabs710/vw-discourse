## Core impasse

The plateau appears to be driven by **sequencing and guarantees**, not just economics.

### The central conflict
- **Employee side / state** want a **binding no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy commitment first**, before any concessions.
- **Management / family / investors** want to **keep closures and forced redundancies available** until an audit proves they are unnecessary.

### Why this blocks progress
Each side views the other’s request as the risky move:
- Employees see “hard options kept open” as **bargaining pressure and a back door**.
- Management and investors see “no-closure first” as **freezing an uncompetitive structure** before the economics are settled.

So the impasse is not simply “whether restructuring is needed,” but **whether the restructuring framework must be fixed in advance or can remain conditional pending audit results**.

---

## Concrete compromise positions to bridge the conflict

### Compromise 1: Binding no-closure test phase with a defined re-opener standard
**Facilitator proposal:**  
A time-limited non-closure test phase is adopted, during which:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- and an independent site-by-site audit are in force.

At the end of the period, the parties do **not** automatically revert to closures. Instead, a **jointly defined re-opener standard** applies:
- only if the audit shows a specified residual gap above an agreed threshold,
- and only after a second round of internal measures has been attempted,
- can the parties reopen discussion of structural footprint changes.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It gives employees real protection during the test while giving management an evidence-based route if internal measures truly fail.

---

### Compromise 2: Tiered commitment structure
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Agree a three-tier framework:

1. **Tier 1: Guaranteed internal measures**
   - voluntary exits,
   - redeployment,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - product allocation changes,
   - overhead reduction.

2. **Tier 2: Binding workforce contribution only if Tier 1 savings are independently verified**
   - temporary wage moderation,
   - bonus suspension,
   - temporary working-time adjustment.

3. **Tier 3: No automatic closure, but a jointly governed escalation review**
   - closures are not pre-authorized,
   - but if the audited residual gap remains material, the parties must negotiate additional internal consolidation measures before any site decision is considered.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It avoids unconditional closure rights while also avoiding unconditional no-change guarantees.

---

### Compromise 3: Site-protection with narrowly defined exceptional review and veto safeguards
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Adopt site protection for all German plants, but allow an **exception review** only under strict safeguards:
- independent auditor confirms a site-specific residual gap,
- internal measures were exhausted,
- co-determination bodies have formal review rights,
- state and union receive advance notice and a structured intervention window,
- closure cannot occur until a supermajority governance process is completed.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
This softens the employee concern about a “back door” while preserving a narrowly controlled fallback if economics truly demand it.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
**Question:**  
What specific residual-gap threshold, if any, would you accept before concluding that the non-closure path has been genuinely exhausted?

### To Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What temporary workforce contribution would the union be willing to place on the table if it were matched by enforceable no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy guarantees for the audit period?

### To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Would you accept a binding no-closure test phase if the audit is time-limited, site-specific, and contains an agreed re-opener standard based on quantified residual gap?

### To Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to make your anti-closure position conditional on a verified audit outcome, or do you require a categorical no-closure settlement regardless of the numbers?

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What level of burden-sharing from ownership are you prepared to commit before asking the workforce to accept any temporary concessions?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
Would you support a no-closure restructuring if the audit demonstrates that it closes the gap with measurable and durable savings, even if that limits management’s fallback options?

### To Independent Auditor
**Question:**  
What decision threshold would you recommend for concluding that a non-closure restructuring is no longer sufficient and that structural footprint change must be considered?

---

## Facilitator summary
The discussion now appears to need:
1. a **defined test phase**,
2. an **agreed residual-gap threshold**,
3. and a **sequenced burden-sharing package**.

That is the most likely route to moving from principle-based deadlock to a negotiable framework.
