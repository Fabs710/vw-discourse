I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state government, and the expert on the key point: this company needs a **durable, economically credible restructuring**, not a cosmetic patch.

I also agree that the workforce should **not bear the burden alone**. Any serious package must include:
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and a fair employee contribution.

And I agree that a **site-by-site, quantified assessment** is necessary. We should not be arguing on slogans.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to keep **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** as routine fallback options while asking the workforce to make concessions first.

That is not balanced bargaining. It is leverage against employees.

If the company wants a fair settlement, then it must stop treating closures and forced layoffs as the default answer before the numbers have been properly tested. The burden of proof is on those who want irreversible damage to sites and jobs.

## On the “conditional” and “review” proposals
I reject the idea that worker concessions should be made under a framework where:
- security is conditional,
- guarantees can be withdrawn later,
- and closures return if management is still dissatisfied.

That is not a guarantee. That is a probation regime for jobs.

We are prepared to discuss **temporary employee contributions**, but only in a framework that genuinely protects:
- all German sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- the collective wage framework,
- and co-determination.

If management wants flexibility, it must give security in return. Not the other way around.

## On the independent audit
I support the audit — but only if its mandate is correct.

It must:
- test **non-closure measures first**,
- quantify savings from capacity reduction inside the network,
- assess product allocation, working time, redeployment, voluntary exits, qualification, and bonus moderation,
- and compare those outcomes against closure scenarios.

It must **not** be used to pre-legitimize closures.

[SUMMON: independent industrial restructuring auditor with site-by-site cost, capacity, and savings analysis]

## To management
You keep saying “show us the package that closes the gap.” Fine — but you have not shown the gap transparently enough yet.

So here is the sequence we require:
1. full site-by-site cost and capacity data,
2. verified savings potential from internal measures,
3. a clear management and owner contribution,
4. and only then a judgment on whether further measures are needed.

Until that is done, closures are not justified; they are only threatened.

And let me be absolutely clear: the workforce will not accept a settlement where employees give up wages, time, and protections while management keeps full strategic freedom and owners keep all the upside.

## To the controlling family
If you mean stewardship, then act like a long-term owner:
- protect the industrial base,
- protect the social fabric,
- and support a negotiated package rather than using closure threats as pressure.

A family owner should understand that trust, skill retention, and industrial peace are not soft issues. They are value drivers.

## To institutional investors
You are right that a cosmetic solution would be destructive. But you are wrong if you think the only credible route is to hold closures and layoffs over the workforce.

Execution credibility can also come from:
- independently verified savings,
- capacity rationalisation inside the network,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and binding management/owner concessions.

A hostile labour settlement may look decisive on paper and still destroy value in practice.

## To the regional state government
Your blocking position matters. Use it.

But do not ask workers to contribute while leaving the door open for closures later. That is not a fair settlement. If you want to support a deal, support one that is based on:
- binding site protection,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- and fair burden-sharing from management and owners.

## What the union is prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary wage moderation or bonus suspension,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- reskilling and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and capacity reduction **inside the current network**.

We are **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- permanent wage erosion,
- or a restructuring that leaves workers carrying the full adjustment burden.

## What would move us
Very simple:
1. **Binding no-closure commitment for all German sites**
2. **Binding no compulsory redundancy commitment**
3. **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
4. **A jointly agreed independent site-by-site audit**
5. **Material management and owner contributions**
6. **Investment and product commitments through 2030**

If those are on the table, then we can negotiate the shape of workforce contributions.

If they are not, then the company is not negotiating a settlement — it is trying to offload its crisis onto employees.

## Bottom line
We are not here to preserve everything unchanged. We accept restructuring.

But we will not accept:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or one-sided sacrifice by the workforce.

If management wants a durable solution, it must bring a genuine non-closure plan with real burden-sharing.  
If it cannot, then it should say so openly instead of hiding behind “credibility” as a pretext for job destruction.
