I will be direct.

We share the diagnosis that this company has a structural competitiveness problem, not just a temporary dip. The cost base must come down, and the restructuring cannot be purely cosmetic. I also agree that a durable solution has to preserve the ability to invest in future products and technology.

But I do **not** accept the premise that a credible restructuring must automatically mean plant closures in Germany. A plan that begins with closures and forced redundancies as the default answer risks destroying value faster than it creates it. For a long-term owner, that is not a serious first choice; it is a last resort.

### To management
You are right that the status quo is not an option. But you have not yet shown that closures are unavoidable rather than simply administratively convenient. Before asking for irreversible steps, I want to see whether you can achieve the required savings through:
- meaningful capacity reductions,
- shift and working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted cost concessions,
- and a tougher internal productivity agenda.

If those measures truly do not close the gap, then we can talk about more painful options. But you have not earned that conclusion yet.

### To the works council and union
I respect the insistence on social peace and the protection of jobs. That matters for the company’s stability and reputation. I also agree that employees should not carry the burden alone.

However, I cannot support an absolute line that rules out every plant closure and every compulsory redundancy in advance. If the result is a structure that remains permanently overcapacity and uncompetitive, then we are just deferring the problem and weakening the company further. That would be unfair to employees in the medium term as well.

So I agree with you on the need for negotiation, fairness, and burden-sharing. I do **not** agree with making the current footprint untouchable as a principle.

### To the regional state government
I understand the political and regional consequences. Stability matters, and this company is a major industrial anchor. But a governance veto cannot be used to preserve every existing site regardless of economics. If the business remains structurally uncompetitive, the long-term damage to the regional base will be worse.

I am open to a restructuring that avoids closures if — and only if — it delivers real, measurable, durable cost relief and productivity gains. If it does not, then protecting every site at all costs is not stewardship; it is avoidance.

### My position
The family holding supports a restructuring that:
- restores margins and competitiveness,
- preserves long-term industrial capability,
- and avoids destabilising conflict where possible.

We are **not** willing to endorse a weak compromise that leaves the company overstaffed, overpriced, and unable to invest.

So the test is simple:
- bring a credible package that produces durable savings and labour peace, and we will support it;
- bring a plan that merely postpones the necessary adjustment, and we will not.

I am prepared to back tough measures if they are economically necessary. I am not prepared to back damage to the enterprise under the banner of preserving comfort.
