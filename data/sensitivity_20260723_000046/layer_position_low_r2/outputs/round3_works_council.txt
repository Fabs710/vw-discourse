I will respond clearly: the latest “bridge” proposals still do **not** meet our core conditions. They may be useful as process suggestions, but they do not change the substance.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with the moderator on one point: we now have a clearer picture of the impasse.

I also agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one thing only: this company needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one. But durability does **not** require German plant closures or forced redundancies. That is the false premise I reject.

I further agree that we should not simply argue in the abstract forever. If management wants to claim that “no closures” cannot work, then it must prove that claim with numbers, site-by-site, product-by-product, and with all alternatives tested.

## Where I disagree — and strongly

### 1) “Structural overcapacity” is not a blank cheque for closures
Management keeps repeating that there is overcapacity. Fine. That may well be true.

But overcapacity does **not** automatically justify closing German plants. It only means the company must reduce capacity somehow. The question is **how**.

And here I draw a hard line:  
**capacity reduction must happen inside the existing network, not by destroying sites and livelihoods.**

That means:
- product allocation changes,
- shift reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal transfer,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification,
- and temporary employee contributions.

What it does **not** mean is:
- plant closures,
- forced redundancies,
- or turning “overcapacity” into a political excuse for irreversible cuts.

### 2) The “staged escalation” model is not neutral if the last stage includes closures
The moderator’s sequencing sounds balanced, but I need to be explicit: if Phase 3 includes discussion of closures or forced redundancies, then the model is only a delay mechanism for the same unacceptable outcome.

We are not prepared to accept a structure that says:
- first we make concessions,
- then we prove ourselves,
- and if management still wants, it can come back later and close plants.

That is not a compromise. That is coercion by stages.

### 3) Binding guarantees are not a reward; they are a prerequisite
The state government’s point about “conditional guarantees” still misses the mark.

If the company wants the workforce to contribute materially, then it must first commit to the basics:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of co-determination,
- preservation of the job-security framework.

Without those, temporary concessions just become a one-way street. We have seen enough of that.

## On the specific “bridge” proposals

### 1) Sequenced restructuring ladder
No, not acceptable if the ladder ends at closures or forced layoffs.

We are prepared to discuss a **non-closure restructuring ladder**, but only if the direction of travel is clear and irreversible on the core issue:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs.

That means the sequence can be:
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- reskilling
- working-time measures
- temporary employee contribution
- capacity reduction through internal allocation and shift changes

But not “phase 3 closure talk.”

### 2) Conditional site guarantees
This is closer, but still incomplete.

A guarantee package only has value if it is truly binding and covers:
- every German site,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closure threat by the back door,
- preserved co-determination,
- and a stable job-security framework through 2030.

If the “condition” is that the workforce must first give up its protections and then hope management behaves later, then we reject it.

### 3) Independent capacity audit
We have no objection to an independent audit. In fact, we welcome it — **if** it is genuinely independent and not an instrument to pre-legitimize a closure agenda.

But let me be precise:
- The audit must examine **non-closure options first**
- It must quantify what can be achieved through internal measures
- It must not assume closures as the baseline solution
- Its mandate must be transparent and agreed
- Its findings must be used to protect sites, not to target them

If management wants an audit, then fine. But do not use the audit to manufacture a conclusion you already decided on.
