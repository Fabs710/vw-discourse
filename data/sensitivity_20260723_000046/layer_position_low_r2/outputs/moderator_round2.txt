## Core impasse

The main deadlock is now very clear:

1. **Employee-side red lines**  
   Works council, union, and regional state government reject:
   - German plant closures
   - compulsory redundancies
   - binding 2030 guarantees being removed
   - weakening of co-determination / job-security framework

2. **Owner / management / investor demand**  
   Group management, controlling family, and institutional investors insist that:
   - structural overcapacity must be reduced
   - the cost base must come down materially and durably
   - closures and/or compulsory redundancies must remain available if needed
   - any guarantees must be conditional on credible savings

3. **Shared but unresolved requirement**  
   Almost everyone agrees restructuring must be:
   - durable
   - quantified
   - burden-shared
   - investment-preserving

So the impasse is not over whether change is needed, but **over whether credible restructuring can be achieved without closures and forced layoffs, and whether binding job/site guarantees can be made before the economics are settled**.

---

## Concrete compromise positions to bridge the gap

### 1) “No forced measures first” with staged escalation
A compromise could establish a **sequenced restructuring ladder**:

- Phase 1: voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time adjustments, temporary employee contributions
- Phase 2: capacity reductions inside the existing network, including shifts, product allocation changes, and partial consolidation
- Phase 3: only if a quantified gap remains after a defined review date, limited discussion of harder structural measures

This would not give either side everything:
- employees gain a real attempt to avoid closures and forced redundancies
- management and owners retain a fallback if the numbers still do not work

---

### 2) Conditional site guarantees tied to measurable milestones
A second bridge could be a **time-bound guarantee package**:

- no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
- in exchange, employees accept temporary and material concessions
- management/owners commit to investment, transparent reporting, and binding savings milestones
- if the company fails to meet agreed efficiency targets, all parties reopen negotiations on additional measures

This would turn the dispute from a binary “guarantees vs. no guarantees” into a **performance-based settlement**.

---

### 3) Non-closure restructuring with an independent capacity audit
A third compromise is a **third-party verified non-closure scenario**:

- an independent expert reviews actual overcapacity, site economics, and alternative measures
- the expert identifies whether savings can be achieved through internal capacity reduction, product allocation changes, and labor-cost measures
- all parties commit in advance to treating that audit as the basis for bargaining

This could reduce mistrust on both sides:
- employee representatives get proof before closures are considered
- management gets a credible test of whether a non-closure path is sufficient

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If a verified independent audit shows that the savings gap cannot be closed without any site reduction, what specific non-closure measures would you still be prepared to accept before your red lines are triggered?

### Industrial Union
What level of temporary employee contribution would you consider materially meaningful enough to support a non-closure deal, and how would you prevent it from becoming a permanent wage cut in practice?

### Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to a quantified, independently reviewed non-closure scenario before insisting on closures or compulsory redundancies, and if so, what deadline do you propose for that review?

### Regional State Government
If a negotiated package requires temporary concessions from workers but preserves all German sites, are you prepared to use your governance leverage to secure management and owner concessions as well?

### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a staged restructuring framework that delays closures and forced redundancies until after a third-party verification of all non-closure measures, even if that limits managerial freedom in the short term?

### Institutional Investors
What minimum set of quantified milestones would you need to see in order to accept a non-closure restructuring as credible and value-enhancing rather than merely postponing the problem?
