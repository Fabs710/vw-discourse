I will respond plainly.

The regional state government acknowledges the company’s competitiveness problem and does **not** deny that restructuring is necessary. But I do **not** accept management’s attempt to turn that into a justification for **German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or a one-sided dismantling of the industrial base**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. A durable solution must improve competitiveness, lower the cost base, and secure future investment.

I also agree with the works council and union that this must be a **negotiated** solution and that the workforce should not be asked to carry the burden alone.

## Where I disagree
### On plant closures
I reject the idea that closures are the default or necessary answer.  
Management still has not shown that closing German plants is the only credible route to savings. “Overcapacity” is not a magic word that makes closures justified. If there is a better way to reduce capacity **within the existing site network**, that must be exhausted first.

A restructuring that destroys regional employment and weakens the company’s role as an anchor employer is not responsible stewardship. It is a high-risk move with long-term political, social, and industrial damage.

### On compulsory redundancies
I also do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as a normal fallback.  
Once forced layoffs become the accepted tool, trust is broken and the co-determination model is damaged. That is not a side issue; it is central to the stability of this company and its workforce relations.

### On the “credible restructuring” argument
I disagree with the notion that credibility requires irreversibility.  
Credibility requires:
- measurable savings,
- real productivity improvements,
- durable labor-cost contributions,
- and a convincing path to competitiveness.

It does **not** require pre-announced closures before all alternatives are properly tested.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a restructuring package if it meets these conditions:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Substantial capacity reduction within the existing footprint**
- **Working-time flexibility and voluntary instruments**
- **Qualification, redeployment, and internal mobility**
- **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**
- **Management and owner contributions as part of the burden-sharing**
- **Binding guarantees for sites and jobs**
- **Protection of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is the basis on which we can act responsibly.

## To management
If you want our support, present a **quantified non-closure scenario**. Not slogans. Not general warnings. I want to see:
- which costs are to be reduced,
- through which measures,
- at which sites,
- with what employment impact,
- and what investment commitments accompany the package.

If the only answer remains “closures or nothing,” then I will say clearly: **that is not acceptable**.

## To the works council and union
I support your core red lines on site protection and no forced layoffs. That is not weakness; it is the right starting point for a social-partnership solution.

At the same time, I am telling you honestly: the state will expect a **real contribution** from the workforce side if it is to defend sites and jobs politically and in governance. That contribution can be fair and temporary, but it must be meaningful enough to help restore competitiveness.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that cosmetic restructuring will fail. But you are wrong if you believe that plant closures are the only way to force seriousness. A negotiated solution that preserves the industrial base while still delivering real savings is better for long-term value than a short-term shock that hollows out the company’s regional foundation.

## Bottom line
The regional state government will **not** back a restructuring built on plant closures and forced redundancies in Germany.

We are prepared to back a tough, negotiated, socially balanced restructuring that:
- preserves sites,
- protects employment,
- restores competitiveness,
- and keeps the company a regional industrial anchor.

If management wants a serious path forward, bring that package. If not, then do not expect the state to stand aside.
