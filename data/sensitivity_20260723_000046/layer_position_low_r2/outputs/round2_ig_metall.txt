I will respond directly.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no one-sided burden on the workforce**
- **binding job and site security**
- **real negotiation, not a fait accompli**

I support your insistence that management has not proven closures are unavoidable. That burden is still with them. If they want to claim that a German site must close, they need to show the numbers, the alternatives tested, and why less destructive measures do not work.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: this is not just about plant-level protection. It is also about **preserving the collective wage framework** and ensuring that any contribution from employees is **temporary, fair, and shared**. The workforce will not accept “security” that is just a slower path to wage erosion.

### To management
I disagree with your framing that closures or compulsory redundancies are simply “not ruled out” and therefore somehow pragmatic. At this stage, that is still a threat, not a solution.

You say the company needs a structurally lower cost base. Fine. But you have not yet shown why that necessarily requires:
- plant closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or the dismantling of long-standing protections.

If you believe the savings gap is that severe, then put forward a **quantified restructuring scenario** with:
- exact overcapacity assumptions,
- savings by measure,
- impact by site,
- investment commitments,
- and a full burden-sharing proposal that includes management and owners.

What I will not accept is a plan that starts from “jobs must go” and then asks the workforce to make it politically easier.

### To the controlling family
I hear your concern about long-term value, and I agree on one thing: a weak compromise that leaves the company permanently uncompetitive would be bad for everyone, including employees.

But I disagree with the suggestion that the path to value must begin with closures as a first-choice instrument. That is too crude and too destructive. Before irreversible cuts, we need to exhaust:
- capacity reduction within the network,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and management/owner contributions.

If those do not close the gap, then we can talk about further steps. But not before.

### To institutional investors
You are right about one thing: cosmetic restructuring will not restore margins.

Where I disagree is your assumption that “no closures” automatically means preserving the old cost structure. It does not. There are real alternatives that can deliver substantial savings without crossing red lines:
- reduced capacity,
- longer-term working-time adaptation,
- voluntary exit packages,
- temporary wage components,
- bonus moderation,
- and productivity improvements.

If you are serious about durable value creation, then pressure management to present those options honestly instead of treating closures as the only credible lever.

### To the regional state government
Your position is important and welcome: you reject plant closures and one-sided job destruction. Use that leverage.

But I also want to be clear: support for a negotiated solution cannot mean accepting wage concessions while management and owners keep the strategic upside. If the state wants social peace, it should back a package that protects:
- sites,
- jobs,
- the collective framework,
- and a fair distribution of sacrifice.

### Our position, plainly stated
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary employee contributions,
- reduction or suspension of bonuses,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and reskilling,
- voluntary exits,
- and capacity reduction **if it does not come through closures or forced layoffs**.

We are **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- permanent insecurity,
- or a restructuring where employees bear the pain alone.

### What management must do next
If management wants a serious settlement, bring:
1. a **quantified non-closure scenario**,
2. a clear **burden-sharing package** from management and owners,
3. binding **job and site guarantees**,
4. and explicit preservation of the **collective wage framework**.

If management cannot do that, then we are not in a serious bargaining phase — we are in a conflict phase.

And to be equally clear: if they try to force this through, the union will not stand aside. We have means, and members expect us to use them responsibly but decisively.
