### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem**, not just a temporary downturn.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **durable, negotiated, and economically credible**.
- All sides accept that the workforce should **not bear the burden alone**.
- There is openness, in principle, to discuss:
  - capacity reductions,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/reskilling,
  - temporary employee contributions,
  - and investment commitments tied to restructuring.
- There is shared interest in avoiding a destructive conflict if a workable compromise can be found.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, state government, and family holding reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management and institutional investors say closures cannot be ruled out if economics require them.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Works council, union, and state government reject them categorically.
  - Management and investors want them kept available as a fallback; family holding is less absolute but not fully aligned with a categorical ban.

- **Scope of guarantees**
  - Employee side and state government seek binding job and site guarantees.
  - Management and investors say such guarantees depend on sufficient economic adjustment and cannot freeze the current structure.

- **Meaning of “credible restructuring”**
  - Employee side and state government emphasize socially sustainable adjustments without closures.
  - Management, family holding, and investors emphasize that restructuring must produce **real, durable cost and capacity reduction**.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **substantial cost savings** without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- Whether capacity reductions can be large enough to satisfy management and investors while preserving the current footprint.
- What level of **employee contribution** is acceptable and how long it would last.
- How to balance **job/site security** against the need for **structural flexibility**.
- What governance or investment commitments could make any settlement credible to all sides.
- Which measures are truly **temporary** versus those that would permanently change the company’s structure.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**:
  - define the savings gap,
  - show what each option delivers,
  - and test whether non-closure alternatives can close the gap.
- Ask management to present **specific restructuring scenarios** with quantified effects.
- Ask the employee side to identify which **flexibility and contribution measures** they could support, and under what conditions.
- Ask the owners and state government what **binding assurances or governance terms** could support a negotiated package.
- Keep the next round focused on **tradeable concessions**, not repeated restatement of red lines.
