I’ll be equally direct.

The expert is helpful on one point: **yes, in principle a non-closure restructuring can work**. But “in principle” is not enough for this company, and it is not enough for me. We need a package that is **quantified, durable, and execution-credible**. So far, the employee side has given us red lines, not a cost solution.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert that the discussion must be evidence-based, not ideological. I also agree that internal measures can and should contribute:
- capacity rationalisation within the network,
- product allocation changes,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and temporary labour-cost relief.

I am not dismissing those tools. I am saying they must be **proven sufficient**.

I also agree with the family holding and investors on the essential point: a cosmetic arrangement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive is useless. It would only delay the problem and damage confidence further.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” as a principle is not a business case
The works council and union keep repeating that overcapacity can be handled without closures. That is not evidence; it is a preference.

If the network can genuinely absorb the adjustment, then show it.  
If it cannot, then insisting on “no closures” simply preserves overcapacity and pushes the cost of delay onto the business.

We are not talking about symbolism. We are talking about whether this brand can hit its return-on-sales target and fund its future product cycle to 2030.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute is not credible restructuring
The employee side wants to remove every hard fallback before the numbers are settled. I reject that.

Voluntary exits are welcome, but they are not always sufficient. Redeployment is useful, but not every job can be redeployed. Working-time measures help, but they do not permanently fix a structurally too-expensive footprint.

If the workforce insists on categorical exclusions, then the company is being asked to accept a restructuring without the ability to complete restructuring. That is not responsible governance.

### 3) Binding 2030 guarantees before a real cost reset are not acceptable
I reject the idea that we should bind the company into site and job guarantees through 2030 while the cost base remains uncompetitive.

A guarantee is only meaningful if it sits on top of a deal that materially lowers costs and capacity. Otherwise it becomes a straightjacket. And I will not accept a straightjacket that leaves the company unable to compete.

## On the expert assessment
The expert correctly says the real question is whether quantified non-closure measures can close the gap.

Good. That is exactly the question management has been asking from the start.

But let me be equally clear: if the non-closure package does **not** close the gap, then closures and tougher measures cannot be ruled out just because they are unpopular. Popularity is not our metric. Competitiveness is.

## What I take from the expert
The expert’s assessment does **not** support the employee side’s position that closures are unnecessary by definition. It says:
- non-closure restructuring is possible **if** the savings gap is moderate and the internal levers are sufficient;
- but if structural overcapacity and cost disadvantage are large, non-closure may be insufficient.

That is exactly why I am insisting on a real test, not a political declaration.

## What I am prepared to do
I am prepared to support a serious non-closure scenario if it includes:
- independently verified capacity reductions inside the network,
- meaningful and lasting labour-cost contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- clear productivity commitments,
- and a timeline that gets us back to target margins.

I am also prepared to accept investment commitments tied to the deal.

But I will **not** accept:
- a blanket ban on German plant closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a guarantee that freezes the current structure until 2030,
- or a compromise that relies on employee pain while avoiding real structural change.

## To the works council and union
You keep saying management has not proven closures are unavoidable. That is not the same as proving they are unnecessary.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring a package that actually closes the cost gap. Not a statement of principle. Not a demand for trust. A package.

And I want clarity on one issue: you say “temporary employee contribution.” Good. How large, how long, and how durable? A temporary wage gesture that evaporates after a year does not restructure anything.

## To the regional state government
Your position is politically understandable, but it does not answer the economics. A veto is not a business model.

If you use your blocking position to prevent any meaningful structural adjustment, then you are not protecting the regional industrial base; you are helping entrench a weak one.

If you want to be a responsible anchor shareholder, then help secure a package that materially lowers cost and capacity. That may require difficult decisions, not just protective language.

## To the family holding
You are right that value destruction must be avoided. But value destruction also comes from refusing to act decisively enough.

I would rather support a tough negotiated restructuring than a weak compromise that preserves the old footprint and condemns the company to underperformance.

## To investors
You want execution credibility. So do I. That is why I will not sign up to a deal that looks good politically but fails economically.

If you want credible capital allocation, then back a package that:
- materially reduces fixed cost,
- improves capacity utilization,
- and restores margin trajectory.

That may or may not include closures. I am not starting from a political taboo. I am starting from the business requirement.

## My bottom line
I am not here to preserve every current site at any cost.  
I am here to restore competitiveness.

That means:
- a real reduction in overcapacity,
- a real reduction in labour cost,
- a real improvement in margins,
- and a real funding base for future products and technology to 2030.

If that can be achieved without closures, I will evaluate it seriously.  
If it cannot, then the company must keep tougher options available.

So let’s stop repeating red lines and produce a package that actually closes the numbers.
