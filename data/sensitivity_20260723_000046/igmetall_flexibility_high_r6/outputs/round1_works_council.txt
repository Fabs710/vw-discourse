As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for management’s restructuring mistakes.

If the company wants a viable future, then it must be built on:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job security through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- real participation by employee representatives in shaping the transformation,
- and a fair distribution of the burden across management, owners, and the workforce.

We are not denying that the company faces serious pressure. But “competitiveness” cannot mean that workers pay alone for strategic failures, weak market positioning, and management decisions that were made over years. Any restructuring must be socially responsible, reversible where possible, and compatible with preserving industrial capability in Germany.

I will argue against any unilateral closure plan, any fait accompli, and any proposal that shifts the adjustment burden primarily onto employees through job cuts, pay reductions, or the dismantling of protections. If management wants cooperation, it needs to put genuine guarantees on the table first. Without that, there is nothing to negotiate.
