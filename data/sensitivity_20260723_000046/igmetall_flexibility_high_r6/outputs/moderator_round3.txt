## Core impasse

The discourse has now converged on a single central fault line:

**All sides agree that the status quo is unsustainable, but they disagree on whether competitiveness must be achieved with or without plant closures / forced redundancies.**

More specifically, the impasse has three linked components:

1. **Labour-side red lines**
   - No compulsory redundancies
   - No plant closures in Germany
   - Binding site/job security
   - Temporary, reversible concessions only

2. **Capital/management-side requirement**
   - Savings must be deep, durable, and numerically credible
   - Non-closure alternatives must match the savings of footprint reduction
   - Management and workforce contributions must both be real
   - Flexibility to adjust capacity and headcount cannot be precluded in advance

3. **Trust / sequencing problem**
   - Labour says: prove non-closure alternatives before invoking closures
   - Management/investors say: prove non-closure alternatives can actually replace closure-level savings
   - Result: both sides demand proof from the other side first, and neither accepts the other’s starting assumptions

So the deadlock is not over the need for restructuring, but over **what counts as a credible restructuring path** and **who must move first**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Non-closure test period” with quantified trigger points
A time-limited package could be negotiated in which:
- all parties commit to a **non-closure restructuring phase** for a defined period,
- the company implements a specified set of internal measures,
- and an independent joint review checks whether agreed savings milestones are met.

**If milestones are met,** the non-closure path continues.  
**If milestones are missed,** the parties reopen the question of deeper structural measures under predefined rules.

This does not settle the closure issue permanently, but it creates a **measured, auditable sequence** instead of an all-or-nothing standoff.

---

### Proposal 2: “No compulsory redundancies, but capacity reduction through voluntary and internal tools first”
A second bridge could combine:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no immediate closure decisions,
- and a priority order for adjustment tools:
  1. working-time changes
  2. shift / utilization optimization
  3. redeployment
  4. qualification and retirement/attrition measures
  5. voluntary severance / exit incentives
  6. only then, if still necessary, site-level decisions subject to renewed negotiation

This would preserve the labour-side demand for protection while giving management a **sequenced capacity-reduction pathway**.

---

### Proposal 3: “Guaranteed protections in exchange for measurable concessions”
A balanced package could be structured as follows:
- **Binding job/site protection for a defined period**
- **Temporary and reversible employee contribution**
- **Explicit management overhead cuts and owner contribution**
- **Investment commitments in Germany**
- **Joint governance over implementation**
- **A pre-agreed review mechanism** if the financial targets are not reached

This creates a trade:
- labour gets security and predictability,
- management gets measurable cost relief,
- investors get enforceability,
- the state gets a protected industrial base,
- and the family holding gets a governance-tested path rather than a symbolic compromise.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific, quantified savings would your non-closure package deliver by 2030, broken down by measure, so that stakeholders can compare it directly to the savings from plant closures?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What exact form of temporary and reversible employee contribution would you accept, and under what binding guarantees, so that it is seen as meaningful by investors but still defensible to members?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Which internal measures have you already quantified as exhausted or insufficient, and what exact savings gap remains after overhead, productivity, and allocation measures are applied?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete state action are you prepared to take to support a non-closure package, and under what conditions would you stop short of endorsing it?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What minimum financial and governance conditions would lead you to back a non-closure solution even if it preserves more sites than you would otherwise prefer?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**What level of quantified, durable margin improvement would you regard as sufficient to support a non-closure package, even if it relies on temporary labour concessions and internal restructuring rather than footprint reduction?**
