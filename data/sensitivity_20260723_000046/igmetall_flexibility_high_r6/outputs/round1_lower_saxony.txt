As the Regional State Government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: we recognize the economic pressure on the company, and we are not denying the need for a credible restructuring. But we will **not** support a course that solves a margin problem by creating a regional industrial and social crisis.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one central point: the status quo is not good enough. If the company’s German operations are structurally too expensive and under pressure from market change, then that reality has to be addressed. We are prepared to engage with a serious restructuring package, including:
- capacity adjustment,
- working-time solutions,
- selective efficiency gains,
- and negotiated employee contributions.

I also agree that a merely symbolic package will not restore competitiveness.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures and mass job losses are the default or inevitable answer.

This company is not just another financial asset. It is a regional industrial anchor with long-term responsibilities. German plants are not interchangeable line items. Once a site is closed, the damage is not easily reversed: skills are lost, supplier networks weaken, and regional economic stability is harmed for years.

Management is acting as if the only meaningful lever is to cut deep into jobs and sites. That is too narrow, and from our perspective, too risky.

### On closures
Plant closures in Germany are a red line for us unless there is truly no alternative and the burden-sharing is demonstrably fair. At this stage, I do not see a convincing case that every other route has been exhausted.

If there are alternatives that achieve comparable savings through:
- capacity consolidation without closure,
- reduced utilization,
- product and shift reallocation,
- temporary working-time measures,
- and negotiated cost relief,

then those alternatives must be pursued first.

### On guarantees
I also disagree with management’s framing that guarantees are somehow the obstacle. On the contrary, binding guarantees are what make a negotiated solution credible.

Without site and employment protection, workers are being asked to give concessions in exchange for trust that can be withdrawn later. That is not a sustainable social partnership. If we want a durable settlement, it has to include:
- protection of core sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a fair transition path for affected areas,
- and clear investment commitments for future products and technology.

### On burden-sharing
I support the union and works council in one key respect: if employees are asked to contribute, then management and owners must also contribute visibly.

That means:
- management must bring overhead reduction and real accountability,
- the ownership side must not expect the workforce to pay for strategic mistakes alone,
- and the company must invest in future competitiveness, not just cut its way through the crisis.

A deal that strips jobs while preserving every strategic privilege above the shop floor would be politically and economically unacceptable.

### Our position
So let me state the state’s position plainly:

1. We support a **negotiated restructuring**, not a unilateral closure agenda.
2. We will **not** support mass layoffs as the primary adjustment tool.
3. We will **not** accept plant closures in Germany as a first resort.
4. We are open to **temporary and reversible concessions** if they are tied to binding guarantees on sites, jobs, and investment.
5. We expect management and owners to present a solution that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor.

### To management
If you want our support, bring forward a package that proves:
- the savings are real,
- the burden is shared,
- and the industrial base in Germany is not being hollowed out.

### To employee representatives
If you want us to back a negotiated settlement, it will need to be economically credible as well as socially fair. We will not support a solution that simply postpones the problem.

But I am equally clear: we will use our rights and our voice to block any outcome that destroys regional employment, sidelines co-determination, or treats German plants as disposable.
