I hear the positions clearly, and I will respond just as clearly.

We, as the controlling family holding, share one fundamental premise with management: **the company must regain competitiveness and restore margins**. If the German cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, value will erode over time, and that is unacceptable from a stewardship perspective.

At the same time, I do **not** support a value-destructive confrontation that undermines labour peace, damages the brand, or creates a prolonged industrial conflict. That would also destroy value.

### Where I agree
- The status quo is not sustainable.
- A cosmetic solution is not enough.
- Any credible settlement must produce real savings, not just defer the problem.
- Employee contributions can be part of the answer if they are tied to a durable outcome.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only meaningful path is plant closures and a hard break with long-standing protections.

Why? Because from an ownership perspective, the issue is not just whether cost comes down on paper. It is whether the company emerges with:
- a viable industrial footprint,
- stable labour relations,
- continuing product and investment capability,
- and no permanent impairment of enterprise value.

A prolonged conflict over closures risks exactly the opposite: operational disruption, reputational damage, political escalation, and weakened execution. That is not a good trade if comparable savings can be achieved through a negotiated restructuring.

### My position
The family holding is prepared to back a serious restructuring, but it must meet three tests:

1. **It must be economically credible.**  
   The package has to deliver durable cost relief and capacity adjustment.

2. **It must be socially and industrially stable.**  
   We do not want a settlement that buys short-term savings at the price of years of conflict.

3. **It must preserve long-term control and the company’s strategic independence.**  
   We will not support any outcome that weakens governance control or leaves the enterprise structurally damaged.

### What this means in practice
I am open to:
- capacity reduction,
- shift and working-time adjustments,
- negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- overhead and complexity reduction,
- and targeted workforce adjustments where unavoidable,

**if** they are part of a durable package.

I am **not** open to a solution that simply freezes the current structure and pretends the problem is solved.

I am also **not** willing to endorse an approach that treats plant closures as the first and only serious lever without exhausting alternatives that preserve value and industrial capability.

### To management
Bring forward a restructuring that is hard enough to matter, but designed to hold.  
If the only plan is to force a confrontation, that is not stewardship. That is value risk.

### To the workforce side
If you want the owners to defend a negotiated solution, then it must be genuinely competitive. That means:
- real contribution,
- real flexibility,
- and acceptance that some adjustment is unavoidable.

But it also means the burden cannot fall solely on employees.

So my position is straightforward: **we support a durable, negotiated competitiveness package; we do not support a destructive closure-driven strategy unless there is no credible alternative that delivers equivalent savings and stability.**
