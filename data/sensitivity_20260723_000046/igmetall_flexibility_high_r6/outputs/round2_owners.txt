We appreciate the seriousness of the positions just laid out, and I will speak plainly.

## Where we agree
I agree with management, investors, and the state on one essential point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. The German cost base must come down, capacity has to be adjusted, and the company needs a durable path back to competitiveness. Cosmetic measures would be a disservice to the enterprise.

I also agree with the workforce side on one important point: **a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value**. That is not in the interest of the company, the employees, or the owners.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** support the idea that the only credible restructuring path is a closure-driven shock strategy. That would be too risky, too destabilizing, and potentially value-destructive.

At the same time, I do **not** accept the employee side’s position that broad guarantees through 2030, no closures, and no compulsory redundancies can simply be declared upfront and still leave a competitive business. That would freeze the problem rather than solve it.

So I am rejecting both extremes:
- **No** to a purely political defense of the current footprint.
- **No** to a confrontation strategy that treats closures and layoffs as the first and only real lever.

## What matters to us as controlling owners
Our test is straightforward: **does the package restore margins and competitiveness without permanently impairing the enterprise?**

That means:
- real and durable savings,
- a credible capacity adjustment,
- a settlement that can actually be implemented,
- and labour peace that supports execution rather than undermines it.

If a proposal cannot deliver that, we will not support it.

## To management
You are right to insist on real savings and structural correction. But you have not yet shown that closure-heavy options are the only way to get there.

Before moving to irreversible decisions, I expect a package that proves:
- what savings can be achieved through consolidation, working-time changes, redeployment, and overhead reduction,
- what the contribution from management will be,
- what the employee contribution would look like,
- and why those measures are insufficient if closures are being proposed.

If you cannot demonstrate that, then a closure-first approach is not yet justified from an ownership perspective.

## To the works council and union
I understand your insistence on social responsibility and job security. But you need to hear this clearly: **a settlement that preserves all guarantees and rejects meaningful adjustment is not a viable answer**.

The company cannot be asked to carry structural overcapacity indefinitely because it is politically uncomfortable to adjust it. That would weaken the business and, ultimately, everyone’s position.

I am open to:
- internal flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- shift and working-time changes,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and targeted workforce adjustment where unavoidable,

but only if these are part of a serious package that actually improves competitiveness.

What I am **not** open to is a deal that keeps the current structure intact in all material respects and simply calls that compromise.

## To the state government
Your concern for regional stability is legitimate, and I do not dismiss it. But regional stability is not preserved by protecting every site regardless of economics. If the company keeps losing competitiveness, the region will suffer anyway — just more slowly and with less clarity.

The right way to protect the region is to secure a viable company. That requires adjustment, not denial.

## My position
The family holding’s position is this:

1. **We support a serious restructuring.**
2. **We do not support a purely symbolic or cosmetic settlement.**
3. **We do not support a destabilizing confrontation that destroys value.**
4. **We are open to alternatives to closures if they deliver equivalent, durable savings.**
5. **We will not back a package that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.**

## What would move us
A credible package would need to show:
- quantified, enforceable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- a fair burden-sharing formula,
- management and ownership responsibility,
- and a stable implementation path.

That can include employee contributions, but those contributions must be matched by real concessions elsewhere and tied to an outcome that strengthens the company long term.

## Bottom line
We are **not** endorsing plant closures as the default answer.  
We are **not** endorsing blanket job security that prevents necessary change.

We are endorsing a **durable competitiveness package** — one that restores margins, protects the company’s industrial future, and avoids a value-destroying conflict.

If the next round is to be useful, I would ask the parties to move from principles to numbers:
- what savings are required,
- what each side is prepared to contribute,
- and which measures are truly off-limits versus negotiable.

Without that, we are still debating positions rather than solving the problem.
