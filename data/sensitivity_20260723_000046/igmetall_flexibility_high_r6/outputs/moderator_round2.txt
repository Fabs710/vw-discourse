## 1) What has been agreed
- The status quo is not sustainable and cosmetic measures are not sufficient.
- A prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company, employees, and value creation.
- Any solution must be durable and economically credible.
- There should be some form of burden-sharing rather than placing the full adjustment on one side alone.
- Internal measures such as productivity gains, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and qualification are broadly seen as part of the toolkit.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works Council, union, and state reject closures as a first resort or at all; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table if alternatives do not deliver sufficient savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side and state reject them; management does not accept an outright exclusion.
- **Job and site guarantees:**  
  Employee-side wants binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors say blanket guarantees would block necessary restructuring.
- **Extent of workforce contribution:**  
  All sides accept some employee contribution in principle, but disagree sharply on how large, how temporary, and under what conditions it should be.
- **Role of management/owners/state:**  
  Employee-side and state insist on visible contributions and binding commitments; management, family holding, and investors want stronger labour-cost relief and structural adjustment.
- **Preferred restructuring model:**  
  One camp prioritizes maximum site/job protection with internal savings; the other insists deeper structural adjustment must remain possible if needed.

## 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Savings versus job protection:** how much cost reduction can be achieved without closures or forced redundancies.
- **Temporary concessions versus binding guarantees:** what protections can realistically be made credible and reversible.
- **Internal efficiency measures versus structural footprint changes:** whether consolidation, shifts, and flexibility can substitute for site reduction.
- **Fair burden-sharing versus competitiveness:** how to divide the adjustment between labour, management, and owners without undermining implementation.
- **Short-term industrial peace versus long-term restructuring capacity:** how to avoid conflict while still delivering a package seen as economically real.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**:
  - required savings,
  - expected contribution by each side,
  - which measures are non-negotiable,
  - which are negotiable.
- Clarify whether a **non-closure package** can deliver savings comparable to deeper restructuring.
- Define the **minimum acceptable package** for each side in concrete terms.
- Test whether there is a **conditional deal structure**: employee concessions in exchange for binding protections, investment commitments, and management/owner contributions.
