We align on the core point that this crisis cannot be solved by simply forcing the workforce to absorb all the pain.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- We reject plant closures as a first resort.
- We reject compulsory redundancies.
- We reject dismantling the collective wage framework as if labor were the only cost factor that can be cut.
- We will not accept a restructuring that destroys trust and turns “transformation” into a one-sided wage and job-cut program.

Where I agree with the Works Council is that any viable path must protect industrial capability in Germany and must be socially responsible. That means real job security, not vague promises. It also means employee representation must be involved substantively, not just informed after decisions are already taken.

But I want to sharpen one point: if management argues that savings are unavoidable, then the burden has to be shared fairly and visibly. That includes:
- management and board contributions,
- owner contributions,
- a credible reduction in overhead and complexity,
- and, only if there is a genuine package for site and job security, an employee contribution that is limited, negotiated, and reversible.

What is not acceptable is a model where workers give up pay, guarantees, and jobs while management preserves all strategic freedom and ownership keeps its position untouched. That would not be a restructuring; it would be a transfer of crisis costs onto the workforce.

So our position is:

1. No forced layoffs.
2. No plant closures.
3. No unilateral wage destruction.
4. Yes to negotiated capacity adjustment, working-time solutions, and temporary employee contributions — but only in exchange for binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.
5. Yes to a durable settlement that protects the collective bargaining system.

If management and the owners want cooperation, they need to put a concrete shared-sacrifice package on the table. If they insist on closures or mass layoffs, then they should expect industrial resistance.
