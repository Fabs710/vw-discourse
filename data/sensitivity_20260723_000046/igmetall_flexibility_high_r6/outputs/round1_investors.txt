We, as dispersed institutional investors, are not here to defend any legacy structure for its own sake. We care about one thing: **risk-adjusted value creation**. On that basis, I see real merit in parts of what management and the controlling family are saying, but I am not prepared to endorse a solution that protects the current cost base and merely postpones the reckoning.

### Where I agree
I agree with management that:
- the current German cost base is not competitive enough,
- a cosmetic settlement would destroy value,
- and the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a temporary pause.

I also agree with the family holding that a prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive. A strike-heavy standoff, political escalation, or a settlement that leaves the business structurally impaired would be bad for shareholders.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support the workforce side’s attempt to make broad job and site guarantees the starting point for negotiations. If the business truly has structural overcapacity, then insisting in advance on no closures, no forced redundancies, and no meaningful labour-cost adjustment risks locking in an uncompetitive cost base.

That is not acceptable to us.

At the same time, I am also cautious about management’s framing if it turns into “closures first, bargaining later.” A confrontation that produces operational disruption, legal delay, or a permanently damaged labour relationship could wipe out the intended savings. So while I accept that closures and headcount reductions may be on the table, I would want to see whether comparable savings can be achieved through a credible negotiated package that does not destroy execution.

### What we need
From an investor perspective, the test is simple:

1. **Durable margin improvement**
   - We need a believable path back to competitive returns on sales.
   - Any deal that does not materially fix the cost base is insufficient.

2. **Real, not symbolic, capacity adjustment**
   - If there is overcapacity, it must be reduced somehow.
   - If that can be done without closures, fine.
   - If not, then closures cannot be ruled out just for political comfort.

3. **Labour-cost discipline**
   - Employees cannot be shielded entirely while shareholders absorb the downside.
   - We expect meaningful contributions from all parties, including labour.

4. **Deal certainty**
   - A prolonged conflict that drags on for months is a negative.
   - We would rather support a hard but credible settlement than an endless stalemate.

### On the red lines
What would be unacceptable to us is:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving margins structurally weak,
- or a prolonged strike that destroys near-term earnings and investor confidence without fixing the underlying economics.

### Bottom line
We are **open to either**:
- a negotiated restructuring with significant capacity and cost reduction, **or**
- more forceful measures if negotiation cannot deliver equivalent savings.

But we will only support an outcome that clearly restores competitiveness. If the final package still leaves the company too expensive, too complex, and too exposed to margin pressure, we will view it as a failure — regardless of how politically comfortable it may be.
