### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the status quo is not sustainable and that some form of restructuring is needed.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated** solution rather than unilateral action.
- Most parties accept that **real savings and capacity adjustment** are necessary.
- There is shared interest in preserving the company’s long-term competitiveness and industrial future in Germany.
- Several parties are open to **employee contributions**, provided they are part of a broader burden-sharing package.
- There is general recognition that a prolonged conflict would be value-destructive and damaging.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, state government, and family holding reject closures as a first resort; management and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side rejects them; management and investors view headcount reductions as potentially necessary.
- **Guarantees:** Employee side wants binding site and job security; management and investors say broad guarantees may block effective restructuring.
- **Burden-sharing:** Employee side wants management, owners, and workforce to share the burden fairly; management and investors want stronger labour-cost concessions; owners want shared adjustment but avoid value destruction.
- **Sequencing:** Workers want guarantees first, then contributions; management argues contributions and structural change must be negotiated together, not conditioned on prior guarantees.
- **Scope of restructuring:** Dispute remains over whether savings can come from efficiency, working-time, and consolidation measures alone, or whether closures and deeper job cuts are unavoidable.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is enough** to restore competitiveness without undermining industrial capability.
- **Which sites or functions, if any, could be adjusted** through consolidation rather than closure.
- **What employee contributions would be acceptable** and whether they should be temporary, reversible, or long-term.
- **What binding guarantees, if any, can be offered** without preventing necessary adaptation.
- **How to share pain credibly** among management, owners, and workforce.
- **How to prevent a negotiated deal from becoming either**:
  - too weak to fix the business, or
  - too harsh to secure labour peace and implementation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Next round should focus on a **specific package comparison**:  
- one scenario based on **maximal site/job protection with internal savings**, and  
- one scenario based on **deeper structural adjustment including possible closures**.  

Each side should state:
- the minimum savings or protections it requires,
- which concessions it can make,
- and what it would consider an acceptable burden-sharing formula.
