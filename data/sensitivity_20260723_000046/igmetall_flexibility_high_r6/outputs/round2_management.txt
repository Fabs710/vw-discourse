Let me respond directly.

I agree with everyone on one point: the status quo is not sustainable. The current cost base is too high, the market is weaker, and we do need a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one.

But I need to be equally clear: **a “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full guarantees through 2030” position is not a credible answer to the scale of this problem.** It may be understandable from your side, but it does not restore competitiveness.

## Where I agree
- We need a negotiated settlement, not chaos.
- A prolonged conflict would damage the company.
- Savings must be real and durable.
- The company must remain investable for future products and technology.

## Where I disagree
### 1) On closures and capacity adjustment
To the Works Council and union: you keep treating plant closures as if they were a political choice management is making for its own sake. They are not. They are one possible consequence of structural overcapacity and uncompetitive German factory costs.

I am not saying closures are automatically the first or only answer. But I **am** saying that if alternatives do not deliver equivalent, durable savings, then closures and deeper footprint changes stay on the table. I will not sign up to a settlement that preserves every site regardless of economics.

### 2) On compulsory redundancies
You reject compulsory redundancies outright. I understand that position, but I do not accept it as a binding precondition. If we need structural workforce reduction, then headcount must come down in a way that is economically effective. We can negotiate the instruments, but the outcome has to reduce cost and capacity materially.

### 3) On guarantees
Binding guarantees through 2030 across all German plants are not compatible with the kind of restructuring this company needs. Guarantees that freeze the structure and block adaptation would simply delay the problem and lock in the wrong cost base.

What I can consider is not blanket protection, but a **serious package with conditions**:
- measurable cost reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- labour-cost contributions,
- and clear implementation commitments.

## To the Works Council
You say we have not exhausted every alternative. That is simply not correct. We have spent years trying to improve productivity, align capacity, and adjust the cost base. We are now at the point where incremental measures are not enough.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that matches the scale of the problem. Not slogans, not generalities — a package:
- with quantified savings,
- with real labour-cost relief,
- with credible capacity reduction,
- and with enforceable implementation.

## To the union
I agree that burden-sharing has to be visible and fair. But burden-sharing does **not** mean that labour contributions are optional while management and owners carry the main load. That is not how a turnaround works.

If there is to be a durable settlement, then the workforce contribution must be real:
- less rigidity,
- lower labour costs,
- more flexibility,
- and adjustments to legacy protections.

I am open to structuring that in a negotiated way, but not to a model where the company keeps full cost pressure on itself while refusing the necessary changes.

## To the state government
You warn about regional damage, and I do not dismiss that. But protecting every site at any cost is not stewardship either. If the company cannot compete, it will suffer more — and so will the region.

So yes, we will look at alternatives to closures where they deliver equivalent savings. But if they do not, then blocking every hard decision is not a solution. It is a postponement.

## To the family holding and investors
I share your concern that a prolonged, unresolved conflict would be value-destructive. That is exactly why I am insisting on a hard but credible restructuring now.

But let me also be clear: a deal that avoids hard choices and leaves the business structurally expensive is also value-destructive. The market will not reward us for a compromise that preserves appearances and weakens the operating base.

## My position
Here it is plainly:

1. **We need deep, durable cost and capacity reductions.**
2. **We are open to negotiated alternatives to closures if they deliver equivalent savings.**
3. **We are not prepared to accept a settlement that preserves the full current footprint and cost structure.**
4. **We are not prepared to accept blanket guarantees that prevent necessary restructuring through 2030.**
5. **We need a meaningful labour-cost contribution, not a symbolic one.**

## What would move me
A credible package could move us if it includes:
- quantified and enforceable capacity reduction,
- a substantial labour-cost contribution,
- headcount reduction with real effect,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- and a settlement that restores margin and funds investment to 2030.

That is the standard. Anything below that is not a turnaround.

If you want to avoid closures, then show me a package that actually replaces the savings they would otherwise deliver. If you cannot, then you are asking management to accept an uncompetitive structure and call it compromise. I will not do that.
