## Facilitator readout: where the core impasse lies

The discussion is now blocked on **one central sequencing dispute**:

- **Works council / union / state** want **binding job and site protection first**, with any employee contribution strictly temporary, reversible, and conditional.
- **Management / family holding / investors** want **durable, numerically credible savings first**, and will not pre-rule-out closures or deeper structural adjustment if the non-closure path falls short.

### In practical terms, the impasse has three layers
1. **Sequence**
   - Is protection a precondition for contribution, or is contribution a precondition for protection?
2. **Fallback logic**
   - If milestones are missed, does that automatically reopen closures, or does management first have to present revised internal measures?
3. **Credibility of alternatives**
   - Can internal measures and utilization gains truly close the gap, or are closures/footprint reductions still needed for net savings?

The deepest conflict is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **whether the non-closure path is treated as a real solution or as a temporary holding pattern before closures**.

---

## Facilitator proposal 1: a “protected test period + defined escalation gate”
A compromise could be a **time-limited non-closure test period** with strong safeguards and a clearly defined review mechanism.

### Elements
- No plant closures during the test period.
- No compulsory redundancies during the test period.
- Employee contributions are permitted only if:
  - time-limited,
  - reversible,
  - and linked to measurable management/owner contributions.
- A jointly agreed independent panel benchmarks:
  - savings from internal measures,
  - utilization improvements,
  - and net savings versus closure scenarios.
- If targets are missed, the next step is **not automatic closure**, but a **mandatory revised management proposal** showing:
  - what additional internal measures were attempted,
  - what volume reallocation was changed,
  - what management/owner contributions were increased,
  - and what residual gap remains.

### Purpose
This could bridge the gap between:
- the workforce’s demand for protection, and
- management’s demand for credible consequences if the package underperforms.

---

## Facilitator proposal 2: “conditional non-closure deal with a protected fallback”
A second compromise could be a **conditional no-closure package** paired with a fallback that is not predetermined but is procedurally bounded.

### Elements
- The parties agree now on a non-closure package for a defined period.
- The package includes:
  - management overhead cuts,
  - procurement/sourcing savings,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - and investment commitments.
- If the package misses targets, then:
  - closures are not automatic,
  - but all sides must enter a second-round bargaining phase within a fixed deadline,
  - using the independent benchmark as the basis,
  - and with no unilateral action by any side during that interim.

### Purpose
This could give management and investors a credible path if the non-closure package underperforms, while avoiding the workforce fear that one missed metric immediately triggers plant shutdowns.

---

## Facilitator proposal 3: “site-by-site differentiation instead of all-or-nothing”
A third bridge could be to move away from a single all-plants-open vs. closure binary.

### Elements
- Treat sites separately based on utilization, cost per vehicle, and investment need.
- For sites that are benchmark-strong:
  - preserve and commit investment.
- For sites with weaker economics:
  - use a longer protected adaptation phase,
  - stronger internal measures,
  - and stricter review milestones.
- If adjustment is still needed later, it must be:
  - site-specific,
  - evidence-based,
  - and net-value positive after transition costs.

### Purpose
This may reduce the polarization between:
- “no closures anywhere,” and
- “closures remain on the table everywhere.”

It creates room for differentiated solutions without forcing immediate acceptance of broad plant exit logic.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**What specific internal savings, site-by-site, would you accept as sufficient evidence that a no-closure path is economically viable without reopening the closure question during the agreed period?**

### 2) Trade Union
**What temporary employee contribution measures would you regard as acceptable if they were paired with a binding no-compulsory-redundancy commitment and independently verified management/owner contributions?**

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**What exact non-closure savings gap, by plant and function, would still remain after all internal levers are applied, and at what point would you say the benchmark proves that closures are no longer avoidable?**

### 4) Regional State Government
**What enforceable conditions would you require to support a protected test period without triggering closures, while still ensuring the package cannot be used to delay necessary restructuring indefinitely?**

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**What concrete governance commitment would you make to signal that you are backing a real non-closure solution first, rather than keeping closures as the implicit preferred fallback?**

### 6) Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified savings and implementation safeguards would you need to see before accepting a non-closure package as margin-credible through 2030?**

### 7) Independent Expert / Benchmark Panel
**What single benchmark metric should be treated as decisive for determining whether internal restructuring has truly closed the gap: cost per vehicle, utilization, net present savings, or time-to-benefit?**
