### 1) What has been agreed
- **The status quo is not sustainable** and some form of restructuring is necessary.
- There is broad recognition that the company needs **durable competitiveness improvements**, not just temporary fixes.
- Several parties are open to a **negotiated settlement** rather than an imposed solution.
- There is shared interest in avoiding unnecessary damage from **prolonged conflict** and preserving the company’s long-term industrial future.

### 2) Core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works Council, union, and regional state reject closures; management, controlling family, and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour side and state oppose them; management does not rule out deeper structural workforce reductions.
- **Preservation of existing protections:** Labour and state want co-determination and job-security guarantees maintained; management says legacy guarantees must change materially.
- **How savings are achieved:** Labour-side proposals emphasize negotiated labor contributions, flexibility, and investment; capital-side actors insist on **durable structural cost cuts** and capacity reduction.
- **Role of industrial action:** Labour side signals strikes may be used; management and investors view this as value-destructive and ineffective.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to close the cost gap** without closures or forced layoffs, and whether that is economically sufficient.
- **What burden-sharing looks like** in concrete terms between workforce, management, owners, and possibly the state.
- **What level of capacity reduction is unavoidable**, and whether it can be achieved through redeployment, attrition, or working-time changes alone.
- **What binding guarantees** on jobs, sites, and investment can realistically be offered, and for how long.
- **Whether a package can be both socially acceptable and financially credible** to shareholders and markets.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **specific package elements**.
- Ask each side to name:
  1. **Non-negotiables**,  
  2. **Possible concessions**, and  
  3. **Quantified contributions** they are prepared to make.
- Require the parties to test one concrete question:  
  **Can a binding agreement deliver the required savings without plant closures and compulsory redundancies, and if so, what exact measures would make that possible?**
