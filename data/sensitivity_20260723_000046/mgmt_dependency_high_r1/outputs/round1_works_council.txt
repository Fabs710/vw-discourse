As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

The workforce did not create this crisis, and we will not accept that its solution is paid for by destroying jobs, closing sites, or breaking trust. The company has alternatives: capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, negotiated contributions, temporary measures, and investment commitments that secure competitiveness **without** sacrificing whole plants or forcing people out.

What we will argue for is a **binding, negotiated restructuring path** that:
1. **Keeps every German plant open**,
2. **Secures jobs through 2030** with a credible guarantee,
3. **Preserves co-determination and the existing labour protections**,
4. **Shares the burden fairly** across management, shareholders, and labour rather than loading it onto employees alone,
5. **Includes a real industrial strategy**: model allocation, product commitments, and investment into the German sites.

If management is serious about a future for this company, it must come with a solution that is **socially responsible, reversible where possible, and legally binding**. If the plan is still based on closures, forced layoffs, or breaking guarantees, then we are opposed to it. We will defend the plants, the workforce, and the company’s long-term industrial base with all available means.
