I will be equally direct.

We have now heard the same pattern from management and the owners again: **keep closures and compulsory redundancies on the table, then call it “responsible restructuring.”** I do not accept that framing.

## Where I agree
I agree with the basic diagnosis:
- the status quo is not sustainable,
- the company needs durable competitiveness improvements,
- and a negotiated settlement is better than a prolonged open conflict.

I also agree that labour must contribute. We have already said that publicly and in our counter-proposal. So there is no point pretending that the union is refusing reality.

## Where I disagree
### 1) No German plant closures
To management, the family, and investors: your insistence that closures must remain available is exactly what makes a fair settlement harder, not easier.

In this company, plant closures are not a harmless technical lever. They are a rupture of trust, a direct attack on the workforce, and given the governance structure, also a recipe for escalation and blockage. If you want a deal, then stop treating closures as the default answer.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies
The same applies to forced layoffs. Once compulsory redundancies are on the table, “negotiation” becomes pressure politics. That is unacceptable to us. We are prepared to reduce capacity and workforce through voluntary, negotiated, and socially responsible means. We are not prepared to endorse firing people against their will.

### 3) No dismantling of the collective wage framework
Management keeps implying that “meaningful” restructuring requires breaking the collective wage order. That is a line we will not cross. A contribution by labour is possible; the destruction of wage standards is not.

## On the expert assessment
The specialist has made one thing very clear: **a no-closure solution can work only if it is a real structural package**. That is not a problem for us. That is exactly what we have been calling for.

What we are not going to accept is the sleight of hand that says:
- keep the threat of closures alive,
- keep forced redundancies as fallback leverage,
- and then claim labour must prove the alternative.

We have already offered the alternative direction:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary labour-cost relief,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- specialization of sites,
- and an employee contribution tied to binding security.

That is not cosmetic. That is restructuring without social destruction.

## To management
You keep asking for a quantified package. Fine. Then put your own numbers on the table, transparently:
- which capacities are actually excessive,
- where the cost gap comes from,
- what overhead reductions you will take yourself,
- what management-level savings you will implement,
- what investment you are committing to,
- and how much workforce reduction you are still demanding.

Do not tell us that “structural change” means workers must absorb the pain while management retains every option against them.

## To the controlling family
If you truly want stewardship, then act like long-term owners. Stewardship means preserving value through industrial capability and social peace, not destabilizing the core workforce and hoping fear produces savings.

A real owner contribution is not just pressure on labour. It also means accepting that a durable settlement must protect jobs, sites, and the collective framework.

## To investors
You say you want risk-adjusted value. Then take the actual risks seriously:
- strike escalation,
- implementation failure,
- loss of trust,
- and a restructuring that may look neat on paper but cannot be executed without open conflict.

A deal built on closures and compulsory redundancies in this setting is not lower risk. It is more combustible.

## Our position
We are prepared to negotiate a binding package if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
4. **A time-bound labour contribution**
5. **Management and owner contributions**
6. **Clear investment and product commitments for Germany**
7. **A binding employment and site-security framework**

That is the minimum for a durable settlement.

## What we are prepared to discuss
- temporary wage moderation or deferred components,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal mobility,
- and site specialization.

But we will not sign up to open-ended sacrifice. Employees will contribute fairly — **not carry all the pain**.

## My bottom line
If management and owners are serious, then bring a quantified alternative that closes the gap **without closures and without forced redundancies**. If they cannot, then they should be honest that they are choosing conflict over compromise.

And if they choose conflict, then they should not expect the workforce to quietly finance a restructuring that destroys jobs, sites, and wage standards.
