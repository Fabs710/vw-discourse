Management, shareholders, and even parts of the state side keep saying the same thing: “structural change is unavoidable.” We hear that. What we do **not** accept is the leap from that premise to **German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and the tearing up of job-security guarantees**.

Let me be very direct:

- **No German plant will be closed with the support of the works council.**
- **No compulsory redundancies are acceptable.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of co-determination or historic employment protections.**

Those are not bargaining chips. They are the boundary lines.

Where I **do** agree:
- The company needs a durable solution, not cosmetic delay.
- The cost gap has to be addressed.
- Capacity can be adjusted.
- Labour can make a contribution if it is part of a fair, balanced package.
- A settlement must be binding and credible.

Where I **fundamentally disagree** is the assumption that “durable” automatically means “closures” or “mass workforce cuts.” That is a choice, not a law of nature. If management and owners are serious, then they must prove that they have exhausted the alternatives first.

### My non-negotiables
1. **Every German plant stays open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Job security through 2030, legally binding.**
4. **Preservation of co-determination and the collective protections framework.**
5. **Investment and model commitments for the German sites.**

### What we can discuss
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary or targeted labour-cost contributions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- internal capacity adjustments,
- natural attrition,
- shifting production volumes within Germany,
- temporary, reversible measures tied to investment.

### What is missing from management’s side
You speak about durability, but you still have not given a credible answer to this central question:

**How exactly do you close the economics gap without shutting plants and without forced job cuts?**

If the answer is “you can’t,” then what you are really saying is that workers must pay for management’s strategic failures by losing their jobs and sites. That is not a restructuring; it is a transfer of risk onto the workforce.

### To the controlling family and investors
You both talk about value preservation, but let’s be honest: **a strike cycle is not created by the workforce wanting conflict. It is created when management comes with a closure agenda and a fait accompli.** If you want stability and value, then stop insisting that closure threats are the only credible discipline.

A long-term owner should understand that destroying trust in the German industrial base also destroys value. So does breaking the social compact that has held this company together.

### To the regional state
Your concern for jobs and sites is right, and your rejection of closures is absolutely correct. But support for a “negotiated restructuring” must mean more than asking labour to absorb pain while management keeps closure options alive. If you stand with the industrial base, then stand with it clearly: **no closures, no forced redundancies, and binding site security.**

### On burden sharing
If you want a real package, then burden sharing must be real:
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- productivity and investment commitments,
- and a labour contribution that is **limited, negotiated, and conditional on security**.

What we will **not** do is accept an open-ended wage sacrifice in exchange for vague promises and “maybe” no closures.

So here is our position for this round:
- Bring a **quantified alternative** that keeps all German plants open.
- Put **binding job security to 2030** on the table.
- Commit to **no compulsory redundancies**.
- Preserve co-determination and the employment framework.
- Then we can discuss labour-side flexibility and contributions in detail.

If that is not possible, then let’s be honest about the conflict: the works council will defend the workforce and the sites with all means available, including coordinated resistance.
