We agree with management on the core diagnosis: **the current German cost base and capacity footprint cannot remain unchanged** if this business is to earn an acceptable return. From a capital-market perspective, “protecting every legacy structure” is not a solution if it leaves margins structurally impaired.

That said, we are **not** prepared to endorse a strategy that relies on blunt plant closures and prolonged industrial conflict as the default path. For shareholders, that is not discipline — it is a value-destroying detour.

Our position is straightforward:

- **We need a credible, durable path back to competitive margins.**
- **We need structural cost reduction, not temporary optics.**
- **We need deal certainty, because prolonged strikes and uncertainty will destroy value quickly.**

Where we agree:
- The status quo is not acceptable.
- Labour must make a meaningful contribution.
- Capacity has to come down.
- Temporary fixes alone will not restore competitiveness.

Where we disagree:
- We do **not** support a settlement that leaves the company with an uncompetitive cost base in exchange for preserving every plant at all costs.
- We also do **not** support a cosmetic “peace deal” that postpones the problem while margins continue to erode.
- A prolonged strike would be especially harmful; it would hit earnings, customer trust, and the investment case.

So the key test for us is not whether the solution is pleasant for any one stakeholder. It is whether it produces **verifiable, durable savings** and a credible return to target returns on sales.

That means we would look favorably on:
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- capacity reductions,
- workforce adjustments largely via natural attrition and redeployment where feasible,
- productivity improvements,
- and a package that is binding enough to give investors confidence.

But we would be sceptical of any package that:
- preserves all plants unchanged,
- avoids real structural savings,
- or relies on ongoing conflict as a bargaining tool.

To management: if you want support from capital markets, bring a plan that is **credible, quantified, and durable**.  
To labour and the state: if you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must still close the economics gap, not merely the headlines.

Our bottom line: **we will support the option that most credibly restores competitiveness and margins, even if that requires difficult structural change. We will not support a solution that leaves the company structurally impaired.**
