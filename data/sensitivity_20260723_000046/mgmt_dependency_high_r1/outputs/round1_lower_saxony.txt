Management is right on one point: **the status quo cannot simply be preserved unchanged**. The market reality is serious, and this company does need a credible restructuring that restores competitiveness and protects its future.

But I must be equally clear: **I do not accept closures in Germany as the default answer**, and I do not accept a restructuring that shifts the burden primarily onto the workforce or strips away the co-determination and site protections that are part of this company’s social contract.

From the perspective of the regional state and anchor shareholder, three things matter:

1. **Jobs and sites in Germany are not expendable.**  
   Plant closures would hit the regional economy, suppliers, and public confidence in a way that goes far beyond this company. That is not a sustainable path.

2. **This must be a negotiated restructuring, not a dictated dismantling.**  
   The workforce and its representatives have made clear they are prepared to discuss contribution and flexibility. That is the right starting point. But “contribution” cannot mean one-sided cuts while management and owners keep all options open for themselves.

3. **The company needs durable competitiveness, not a short-term accounting fix.**  
   I agree that temporary measures alone are insufficient. But durable savings can also come from a serious package of capacity adjustments, working-time changes, internal efficiency gains, product allocation, investment discipline, and a fair labour contribution — without closing plants.

So my position is this:

- **No German plant closures as a starting premise.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the existing co-determination balance.**
- **Yes to a binding restructuring agreement if it preserves sites and secures a viable industrial future.**

If management insists that the only path is closures and mass job cuts, then I will say plainly: **that is not acceptable to the state as shareholder or as political actor**. With our blocking position and supervisory-board seats, that is not a formality.

At the same time, I am not pretending the workforce can be asked for nothing. A fair settlement may require:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary or targeted wage moderation,
- internal redeployment,
- a limited, negotiated reduction in capacity,
- and investment commitments tied to future models and technologies.

But any such package must be **balanced** and **credible**. If management wants employee restraint, it must also commit to **site security, product allocation, and long-term investment in Germany**.

So I would challenge management directly:  
**Show us a restructuring that truly closes the cost gap without closing plants.** If you can do that, there is room for agreement. If not, then you are asking the regional state and the workforce to approve damage that we cannot and will not authorize.
