I will be very direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the controlling family, and the investors on one point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. This company does need a durable restructuring, and it does need to restore competitiveness by 2030. I also agree that temporary patches alone will not be enough.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only credible route is **German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or dismantling the existing co-determination and job-security framework**.

That is where I draw the line.

As the regional state and anchor shareholder, my responsibility is not just to financial metrics. It is to the **regional economy, the workforce, the supplier base, and the long-term industrial anchor role of this company**. Closing German plants would do lasting damage far beyond this balance sheet.

### To management
You keep saying the numbers don’t work without closures. Then show us the **actual quantified package** that proves that claim. So far, what I hear is a preference for the bluntest instrument.

If management wants support for restructuring, then bring a proposal that:
- preserves German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- produces verifiable, durable savings,
- and includes a real industrial plan for future products and investment in Germany.

What I will not support is a plan that treats closures as the default answer before the alternatives have been seriously exhausted.

### To the works council and union
I support your core objective: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no destruction of the social partnership**. That remains the right starting point.

But I also need to be honest: if the only answer is “nothing changes except management and owners should just accept it,” then you will not get a durable settlement either. A fair package will require **some** contribution from labour — but only in a way that is:
- negotiated,
- limited,
- time-bound where possible,
- and tied to binding site and job security.

That is the key condition. If workers give concessions, they must get **real guarantees** in return.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right that cosmetic compromise is not enough. But neither is a solution that destroys the company’s regional base in the name of discipline.

Value is not preserved by triggering a prolonged conflict or by hollowing out the company’s industrial footprint in Germany. A truly long-term owner should understand that **trust, skills, sites, and social stability are part of enterprise value**.

### My position
I am open to a **negotiated restructuring package** if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding employment and site security**
4. **Meaningful but fair labour contribution**
5. **Management and owner contribution as well**
6. **Investment and product commitments for German sites**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

If management cannot bring a package within those parameters, then I will oppose it.

### On burden sharing
Burden sharing must be real. That means:
- management must show operational discipline,
- owners must accept that value preservation is not achieved by simply shifting the pain to labour,
- and labour can be asked for flexibility only if jobs and sites are secured.

### Bottom line
I am not prepared to approve a restructuring that **guts regional employment** or treats the German industrial base as disposable.  
I am prepared to support a **serious negotiated alternative** — but only if it protects the company’s role as a regional anchor and keeps the workforce and sites viable long term.

If management wants my support, I expect a **concrete, quantified alternative** to closures in the next round.
