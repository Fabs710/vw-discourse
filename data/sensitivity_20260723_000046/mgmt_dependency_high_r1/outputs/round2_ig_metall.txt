Management, shareholders, and the state are all speaking as if the only meaningful restructuring is one that starts with **closures, forced redundancies, and pressure on wages**. I reject that framing.

Let me be precise.

### Where we agree
- The company has a **real competitiveness problem**.
- The cost base must come down.
- Capacity has to be adjusted.
- A durable settlement is better than endless conflict.
- Labour is prepared to make a **fair contribution** if the burden is shared.

### Where we do not agree
We do **not** accept that the only “durable” solution is to break the collective wage framework or to force people out.

If management’s real message is:  
“Accept closures or accept massive job losses, otherwise the economics won’t work,”  
then that is not a negotiation. It is an ultimatum.

And to be clear: **that will not be accepted.**

### Our non-negotiable line
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No open-ended insecurity dressed up as flexibility**

That is not rhetoric. That is the line that matters.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We have already shown that we are not pretending nothing must change. We can discuss:
- temporary or longer-term labour-cost relief,
- moderation of bonus components,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- redeployment,
- qualification and internal mobility,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

But that only works if the company side also contributes and if the package is tied to **site security and job security**.

### To management
You keep saying “structural change is unavoidable.” Fine. Then show us a **structural alternative that does not rely on forced layoffs or closures**.

We have been clear that labour can contribute. What we will not do is sign away wage standards and job security so that management can claim it has solved the problem by pushing all the pain onto the workforce.

If you want a serious deal, then put these elements on the table in concrete form:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A binding employment guarantee**
4. **A defined labour contribution with a time limit**
5. **Management and owner contributions**
6. **Investment and product commitments for Germany**

### To the controlling family and investors
You talk about value preservation, but value is not preserved by triggering a conflict that destroys trust, output, and the workforce’s willingness to cooperate.

If you insist on treating closures and layoffs as the default discipline mechanism, you will get resistance, not consent.

And let me add this: the market does not need a symbolic show of toughness. It needs a credible, durable industrial settlement. A strike is not our preferred outcome, but if the alternative is a settlement that makes workers carry the entire burden, then you should not expect quiet approval.

### To the regional state
You are right to reject closures as the starting point. But that position must be backed by a real commitment to a **fair restructuring without forcing workers to pay alone**. If the state wants social peace and industrial stability, then it should support a package that binds management, owners, and the company to real commitments — not just ask labour for concessions.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a **durable, fair, and binding package**. But the package must be built on:
- job security,
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a shared contribution from all sides.

If management cannot accept that, then it is not asking for restructuring. It is asking the workforce to underwrite its strategy failure. That is unacceptable.
