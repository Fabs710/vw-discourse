I agree with management on the **problem diagnosis**: this company cannot continue with a German cost base that is structurally above market reality while margins remain under pressure. If competitiveness is not restored, then long-term value will erode for everyone, including the workforce and the public shareholders.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only credible answer is plant closures or a sweeping dismantling of the existing framework. A restructuring that damages the industrial base in a way that is irreversible or permanently value-destructive would be unacceptable from an ownership perspective. We are long-term stewards, not short-term traders.

So my position is:

- **Yes** to a durable competitiveness plan.
- **Yes** to meaningful labour-cost and productivity contributions.
- **No** to a plan that treats closures as the default or only solution.
- **No** to avoidable instability that weakens the brand, disrupts production, or triggers a prolonged conflict.

To the works council and union: I hear the insistence on **no compulsory redundancies and no closures**. I understand why you draw that line, but I need to be equally direct: if you insist that every existing structure must remain untouched, then you are not solving the economic problem. The holding company will not support a settlement that preserves every legacy protection if the result is merely to postpone decline.

That said, I am open to a **serious negotiated package** if it delivers durable improvements in margins and competitiveness. That means:
- real labour-cost relief,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment and natural attrition where possible,
- and a credible investment path for the German sites.

To the regional state: I agree that Germany’s industrial base matters, and I share the concern about regional spillovers. But we should be careful not to turn “no closures” into a veto on all structural change. If the company needs capacity reduction to survive, then that must remain on the table. What matters to us is whether the final package preserves enterprise value and future viability, not whether every location remains untouched at any price.

So my message is simple:  
**I will support a negotiated settlement if it is economically real and durable.**  
I will **not** support cosmetic compromises that protect every site and every guarantee while leaving the company uncompetitive. And I will also not support a value-destructive escalation into prolonged conflict.

If the parties can bring forward an alternative that delivers comparable, lasting savings without a destructive strike cycle, I am prepared to examine it carefully. But it must be real, not symbolic.
