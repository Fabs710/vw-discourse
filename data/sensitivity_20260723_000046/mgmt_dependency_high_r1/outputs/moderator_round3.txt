## Core impasse

The plateau is centered on one issue:

**Whether structural savings can be achieved without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies.**

That single question is driving the deadlock because it combines four linked disputes:

1. **Management / owners / investors** want a restructuring that is clearly large enough to restore competitiveness and margin by 2030.
2. **Works council / union** reject closures and forced layoffs as red lines.
3. **Regional state** wants industrial stability and no closures, but also needs a credible economic plan.
4. **Expert** says a no-closure route may work only if it includes real internal footprint reduction, not just soft flexibility.

So the main impasse is not “change vs no change.” It is:

**What level of internal restructuring is sufficient, and what measures remain permissible if closure and forced redundancy are excluded?**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to explore

### 1) No immediate closures, but binding footprint reduction inside Germany
**Proposal frame:**  
Keep all German plants formally open for now, but agree to a binding internal consolidation plan that includes:
- site specialization,
- shift reductions,
- program transfers,
- capacity cuts,
- and a quantified headcount path via voluntary exits and attrition.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Meets the works council / union red line on no closures.
- Gives management / investors a structural-cost story rather than a cosmetic one.
- Gives the state a way to preserve the industrial base while still shrinking the footprint.

**Key condition:**  
This only works if the savings and capacity reductions are quantified and milestone-based.

---

### 2) Time-limited employment security in exchange for measurable employee contribution
**Proposal frame:**  
Offer a defined security period, potentially to 2030, but tied to a package of employee concessions such as:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification measures,
- and voluntary exit programs.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Addresses the workforce’s trust concern by removing immediate closure and layoff threats.
- Satisfies management / owners / investors only if the labour contribution is real and time-bound.
- Makes the state’s support more defensible if industrial stability is contractually protected.

**Key condition:**  
The guarantee must be paired with enforceable performance triggers and review points.

---

### 3) Conditional no-closure pact with a structured review trigger
**Proposal frame:**  
Agree now that there will be **no closures and no compulsory redundancies during an initial phase**, provided the company meets agreed milestones on:
- savings,
- productivity,
- capacity reduction,
- and investment.

If milestones are missed, the parties move to a predefined second-stage review.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Reduces immediate conflict.
- Gives management a governance path instead of an open-ended veto.
- Gives labour a concrete security window and a clearer incentive to engage.
- Gives investors a disciplined process with fallback logic.

**Key condition:**  
The review trigger must be objective, not discretionary.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management:
**What exact savings gap remains after you fully price a no-closure internal consolidation plan, and which specific capacity reductions would you accept in place of plant closures?**

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat):
**If all German plants stay open, what precise and quantified capacity, shift, and program reductions are you prepared to accept to make the economics work?**

### To the Industrial Union:
**What time-bound labour-cost contribution can you commit to that is large enough to be meaningful but still consistent with your red lines?**

### To the Regional State Government:
**What binding support or guarantees are you prepared to use to secure a no-closure settlement, and what conditions would you attach to that support?**

### To the Controlling Family:
**Would you back a no-closure package if it delivers verified structural savings through internal consolidation, and if so, what minimum economic thresholds must it meet?**

### To Institutional Investors:
**If the company presents a quantified no-closure restructuring with enforceable milestones, what minimum return and execution criteria would make it acceptable to you?**

### To the Expert:
**What are the minimum capacity-reduction and headcount-reduction thresholds that would make a no-closure plan economically comparable enough to a closure-based plan to be credible?**
