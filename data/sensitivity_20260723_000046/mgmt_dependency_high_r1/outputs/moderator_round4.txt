Facilitator assessment: the core impasse is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **what counts as a credible restructuring** and **which instruments are admissible before the economics are fully proven**.

## Core impasse

The deadlock sits on three linked issues:

1. **Plant closures**
   - Employee-side stakeholders and the state treat closures as a non-starter or first-resort taboo.
   - Management, owners, and investors treat closures as a necessary fallback if internal consolidation does not close the gap.

2. **Compulsory redundancies**
   - Employee-side stakeholders reject forced layoffs as incompatible with trust and a durable deal.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders insist a restructuring without any fallback enforcement mechanism may be non-credible.

3. **Proof standard**
   - Employee-side stakeholders want an independent, quantified test of a no-closure plan before conceding any site exit logic.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders want commitment on the table now, arguing that red lines must not preclude economically necessary measures.

So the dispute is not “restructure or not.” It is **whether a deep no-closure package can be made sufficiently structural, and what fallback language is allowed if it cannot**.

---

## Possible bridging compromise positions

### Compromise 1: Time-bounded no-closure covenant with a quantified trigger review
**Structure**
- All German plants remain open for a defined period, for example through 2030 or through an earlier review date.
- No compulsory redundancies during the initial phase.
- A binding internal consolidation package is implemented immediately.
- An independent modeller verifies whether the package closes the savings and margin gap.
- If agreed performance triggers are not met by a fixed milestone, a second-stage negotiation opens on additional measures.

**Why it could bridge**
- Gives employee-side stakeholders a real no-closure commitment.
- Gives management and investors a built-in economic safeguard.
- Converts the dispute from ideology to measurable milestones.

---

### Compromise 2: “No closure, no forced layoffs” now; fallback measures only as last resort and only after defined failure conditions
**Structure**
- The agreement explicitly bans closures and compulsory redundancies in the first phase.
- The package must include real capacity cuts, shift reductions, program consolidation, and labor-cost contribution.
- A predefined escalation ladder applies only if objective conditions are missed:
  1. extra voluntary exits
  2. additional internal transfers
  3. further working-time changes
  4. only then reconsideration of site measures

**Why it could bridge**
- Preserves the employee-side red lines in the immediate settlement.
- Addresses management’s concern that there must be an enforceable consequence if savings fail.
- Reduces fear that “no-closure” is merely rhetorical.

---

### Compromise 3: Site-security package with differentiated burden-sharing
**Structure**
- No plant closures during the restructuring period.
- No compulsory redundancies as a first resort.
- In exchange, all parties accept quantified contributions:
  - workforce: voluntary exits, flexibility, shift reductions, temporary wage moderation
  - management: overhead cuts, executive cost restraint, program simplification
  - owners: investment commitments and possible capital support
  - state: facilitation for retraining, redeployment, and industrial transition support
- A transparent scorecard tracks savings delivery by site and function.

**Why it could bridge**
- Makes the deal visibly shared rather than one-sided.
- Creates political cover for the state.
- Gives investors evidence of burden-sharing and monitoring.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What exact combination of capacity cuts, shift reductions, and headcount reduction would you accept as sufficient to make a no-closure package genuinely structural by 2030?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What time-bound labor-cost and flexibility contribution are you prepared to commit to in writing if management formally rules out closures and forced layoffs in the first phase?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What verified savings gap remains after internal consolidation, and at what quantified threshold would you say a no-closure option has actually failed?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you support a binding no-closure restructuring package only if it includes measurable capacity reduction and labor contribution, even if that requires politically difficult concessions?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Are you prepared to accept a binding moratorium on closures if independent modelling shows the internal package closes the gap within a defined tolerance band?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum evidence would make you regard a no-closure plan as financeable: verified run-rate savings, margin uplift, or a binding fallback mechanism if targets are missed?**

### To the Independent Expert
**What single metric should be treated as the decisive pass/fail test for a no-closure restructuring: annual run-rate savings, capacity reduction, or 2030 operating margin?**
