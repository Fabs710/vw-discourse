The works council is right on the core point: **Germany’s plants cannot be treated as disposable cost blocks**. We will not accept a restructuring that is built on **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or the destruction of the collective wage framework**. If management’s answer to a structural problem is simply to throw workers overboard, then it is not a restructuring plan — it is a one-sided transfer of crisis costs onto the workforce.

That said, we are not ignoring the competitive reality. We know the cost base must come down and capacity must be adjusted. The union has already shown willingness to negotiate a **fair contribution** from labour, including:
- temporary or permanent labor-cost relief,
- working-time and capacity adjustments,
- suspension or limitation of bonus elements,
- and other agreed measures that help stabilize the company.

But this can only be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**. What we will not accept is:
1. **Workers paying all the pain**, while owners and management remain untouched;
2. **Open-ended insecurity** presented as “flexibility”;
3. **Forced redundancies** disguised as inevitability;
4. **Closure threats** used as leverage to break bargaining standards.

If management wants serious talks, then the minimum conditions are clear:
- **No compulsory layoffs**
- **No plant closures**
- **A binding employment and site security agreement**
- **A negotiated labour contribution paired with management and owner contributions**
- **Credible investment and product commitments for the German sites**

To be equally direct: if the company insists that only closures and job cuts can solve the problem, then we will use the tools available to us, including **industrial action**. We are not going to sign off on a plan that sacrifices the workforce to preserve short-term margins.

So our position is simple: **we are ready to negotiate a durable restructuring, but only if it is fair, balanced, and protects the industrial base and the workforce’s future.**
