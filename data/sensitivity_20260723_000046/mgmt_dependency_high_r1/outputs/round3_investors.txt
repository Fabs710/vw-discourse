We agree on the diagnosis: the current structure is not acceptable, and the company needs a durable, not cosmetic, restructuring.

We also agree with the expert on one critical point: **“no closures” is not the same as “no structural change.”** If a no-closure package still delivers real capacity reduction, meaningful headcount reduction, lower labor cost, and simpler operations, then it can be evaluated on merit.

But we disagree with any plan that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies is understandable, but from a capital-markets perspective it only works if it is backed by a **quantified, durable savings package**. Right now, you have stated red lines more clearly than you have stated the numbers.

We need to see:
- how much capacity comes out,
- how many jobs come out,
- what labor-cost contribution is made,
- what overhead and management cuts are made,
- and how the company reaches target margins by 2030.

If the answer is “keep every site open and rely mainly on temporary flexibility,” that is **not enough**. That would leave the cost base impaired and the equity story weak.

### To management
You are right that symbolic compromise is unacceptable. You are also right that the economics must improve durably.

But saying “the numbers don’t work” is not sufficient. We need a **clear cost bridge** and a **comparative case**:
- closure-based plan,
- no-closure internal consolidation plan,
- expected annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- implementation risk,
- and timing to margin recovery.

Without that, the market cannot assess whether closures are truly unavoidable or merely the bluntest available option.

### To the regional state
We share your concern about prolonged conflict and the damage a strike cycle would do. But political opposition to closures is not, by itself, a substitute for a credible economic solution. If you want to rule out closures, then you need to support a package that still produces **hard structural savings**.

### To the controlling family
We agree that cosmetic compromise is not acceptable. We also agree that value cannot be preserved by protecting every legacy structure indefinitely.

But equally, if the ownership line remains “closures stay on the table no matter what,” that weakens the incentive to find a serious negotiated alternative. The right test is not ideological. It is economic:
**can a no-closure package deliver the required savings and execution certainty?**

## Our position
We would support the option that most credibly delivers:
1. durable margin recovery,
2. disciplined structural cost reduction,
3. quantified and enforceable savings,
4. implementation certainty,
5. and limited conflict duration.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that preserves the uncompetitive cost base,
- or a prolonged strike that destroys earnings and confidence.
