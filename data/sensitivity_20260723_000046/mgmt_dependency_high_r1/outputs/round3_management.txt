I’ll be blunt.

We have now heard a lot of principled language about “no closures,” “no compulsory redundancies,” and “job security through 2030.” None of that answers the central problem: **our German footprint is too expensive and too large for the market we are facing.** If the package does not produce a **durable structural cost and capacity reduction**, it is not a solution.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one point: the status quo is not sustainable.

I also agree that:
- this needs to be negotiated,
- a prolonged strike would damage the company,
- and a purely cosmetic deal would be unacceptable.

And I accept the expert’s core point: **a no-closure path can work only if it still removes real capacity, real cost, and real headcount.**

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute
The works council and union keep repeating “every German plant stays open.” I cannot accept that as a starting line.

Why? Because the numbers do not support preserving the footprint unchanged. We are dealing with:
- a shrinking European market,
- severe price pressure,
- too much fixed cost,
- and a brand that is missing its return target.

If every site stays in place simply because it is politically easier, we will end up protecting overcapacity and funding decline. I will not agree to that.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be a blanket veto
I understand the preference for voluntary measures. I prefer them too. But if the package does not deliver enough through attrition, redeployment, voluntary exits, and productivity measures, then **forced measures cannot be ruled out in advance**.

A restructuring without any fallback leverage is not credible. It is a wish list.

### 3) A 2030 security pledge is not enough by itself
A job-security promise only has value if the company remains competitive under it.

If the commitment locks in too much cost and too much capacity, then it merely delays the correction and makes the eventual adjustment harsher. That is not stewardship. That is postponement.

## On the expert assessment
The expert confirmed exactly what I have been saying:

- a no-closure plan is only plausible if it still brings **material capacity reduction**,
- **meaningful headcount reduction**,
- **labor-cost moderation**,
- and **site specialization / consolidation**.

That is the point.

So let me be clear:  
**If “no closures” really means “all sites remain open but several programs, shifts, and volumes are removed, overhead is cut, and headcount falls materially,” then we can evaluate it.**  
But if it means “preserve the whole footprint, preserve the protections, and ask management to hope for a better market,” then we are nowhere.

## To the works council
You say the burden of proof is on management. Fair enough. Here is the burden we place on you:

**Show us a quantified package that closes the economics gap without formal closures, but with real structural reductions.**

That means:
- how much capacity comes out,
- how many jobs come out,
- which sites are specialized,
- which shifts disappear,
- how much labor-cost relief is achieved,
- and how the company returns to target margins by 2030.

If you cannot quantify that, then “no closures” is not a plan. It is a refusal.

## To the union
You say you are prepared to contribute, but only with binding security. I hear that. But security cannot be unconditional.

If labour wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then the contribution must be **meaningful and durable**:
- real wage moderation,
- limits on bonus and special payments,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- voluntary exit mechanisms,
- and internal mobility.

Temporary, reversible, cosmetic measures do not move this company far enough.

## To the regional state
I respect your concern for jobs and the industrial base. But “no closures” cannot become a political shield for an uncompetitive structure.

If you want a no-closure path, then help make it economically real:
- support site specialization,
- support skills and redeployment,
- support a credible transition package,
- and accept that the footprint still has to shrink structurally.

Political opposition to closures is not enough if the economics remain broken.

## To the controlling family
You are right that cosmetic compromise is not enough. I agree.

But I also need to be explicit: if your position is simply “closures remain on the table no matter what,” then you raise the confrontation risk without making a deal easier. The useful question is not whether closures are ideologically available. It is whether a **credible, quantified alternative** can achieve the same structural effect.

If it can, I will assess it seriously. If it cannot, then the pressure for deeper measures remains justified.

## To investors
You are right to demand durable savings, not slogans. But I will also say this: the market does not reward a plan that looks hard but cannot be executed.

So I am not asking you to choose between “peace” and “discipline.” I am asking you to support the option that best delivers:
- durable margin recovery,
- structural cost reduction,
- implementation certainty,
- and a viable 2030 business.

If that is a no-closure package with real capacity and workforce reduction, fine.  
If not, then we should not pretend it is enough.

## My position
So let me define it precisely:

- I am open to alternatives to formal plant closures.
- I am **not** open to preserving the current footprint and cost structure.
- I am **not** open to a cosmetic settlement.
- I need a **genuine labour-cost contribution**, **capacity reduction**, and **workforce adjustment**.
- If those conditions are not met, then deeper structural measures, including plant rationalisation, remain on the table.

## What I want next
I want a concrete package, not more slogans.

Specifically:
1. **Quantified savings**
2. **Quantified capacity reduction**
3. **Quantified headcount reduction**
4. **Defined labour contribution**
5. **Management and owner contribution**
6. **Investment and product commitments**
7. **A credible implementation timeline to 2030**

If the employee side can produce a package that meets those criteria **without formal closures**, I will examine it seriously.

If not, then we should stop pretending that “no closures” is a sufficient answer.
