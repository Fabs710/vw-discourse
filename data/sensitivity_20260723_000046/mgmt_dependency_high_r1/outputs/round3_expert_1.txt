## Expert assessment: no-closure restructuring vs closure-based plan

Yes — a **no-closure restructuring can plausibly close a substantial part of the economics gap**, but only if it is **structural**, not cosmetic. In practice, it is usually **hard to fully match** the run-rate savings of a true plant-closure plan unless the company accepts **deep internal footprint reduction**.

### Core judgment
- **No-closure can work** if it includes:
  - meaningful **capacity cuts inside existing sites**
  - **shift reductions**
  - **program consolidation**
  - **site specialization**
  - **headcount reduction via attrition/voluntary exits**
  - **labor-cost moderation**
  - **management/overhead cuts**
- **No-closure is usually insufficient** if it means:
  - keeping all sites broadly intact
  - relying mainly on temporary flexibility
  - or avoiding hard volume/capacity decisions

---

## Economic comparison

### 1) Closure-based plan
**Typical effects**
- Highest recurring savings
- Removes fixed overhead, indirect labor, maintenance, and underutilized capacity permanently
- Strongest structural reset

**Typical downside**
- Large one-time charges
- High strike/implementation risk
- High political and governance friction in Germany

### 2) No-closure plan
**Typical effects**
- Lower one-time cost
- More executable socially
- Lower immediate conflict risk
- But residual fixed cost remains unless the footprint is materially simplified

## Indicative savings ranges
These are **scenario estimates**, not company-specific facts.

### No-closure plan: mature annual recurring savings
- **Low case:** €300m–€600m
- **Mid case:** €700m–€1.2bn
- **High case:** €1.3bn–€1.8bn

### Closure-based plan: mature annual recurring savings
- **Typical range:** €1.0bn–€2.5bn+
- Can exceed that if a full plant is closed and production is not simply reabsorbed elsewhere

---

## Headcount impact

### No-closure
Usually achieved through:
- hiring freezes
- attrition
- retirement
- voluntary exit programs
- redeployment
- reduced temporary labor

**Practical reduction range**
- **5%–15%**: commonly feasible
- **15%–25%**: possible, but usually requires aggressive working-time and role redesign

### Closure-based
Can produce faster and larger reductions.

**Practical reduction range**
- **15%–30%+** in the affected domestic footprint
- exact outcome depends on what is closed and what is relocated

---

## Capacity impact: the decisive issue
A no-closure plan only works if it **actually removes capacity**. Examples:
- one fewer shift at selected sites
- eliminating overlapping programs
- concentrating specialized production into fewer plants
- reducing annual output targets
- cutting internal logistics and support layers

If “no closures” means **no meaningful capacity reduction**, then the cost gap usually remains too large.

---

## Execution risk comparison

### Closure-based plan
**Pros**
- strongest structural savings
- clearer footprint rationalization

**Cons**
- high chance of delay, dispute, or partial blockage
- high transition risk
- expensive one-time execution

### No-closure plan
**Pros**
- lower industrial-relations risk
- faster to implement if trust exists
- less supply disruption

**Cons**
- savings can be diluted
- risk of “paper restructuring”
- requires strict monitoring and milestone enforcement

---

## What would make a no-closure plan credible?
I would require at minimum:

1. **Quantified 2030 run-rate savings target**
2. **Explicit capacity reduction by site/program**
3. **Headcount reduction path** with attrition and voluntary exit assumptions
4. **Time-bound labor-cost contribution**
5. **Management/overhead savings**
6. **Investment and product commitments**
7. **Fallback triggers** if savings/utilization milestones are missed

---

## Bottom line
### If the restructuring gap is moderate:
A **well-designed no-closure package can likely close most of the gap** and may be the more executable option.

### If the gap is large and structural:
A no-closure package is **unlikely to fully substitute** for closures unless the company accepts very substantial internal consolidation and labor-cost relief.

### Practical conclusion
The most realistic compromise is usually:
- **no immediate closures**
- **real capacity reduction inside Germany**
- **voluntary workforce reduction**
- **time-limited labor contribution**
- **management and owner contribution**
- **clear review/fallback mechanisms**
