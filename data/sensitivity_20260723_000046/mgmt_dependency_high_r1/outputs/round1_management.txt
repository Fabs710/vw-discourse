I appreciate the clarity, but I need to be equally clear: **a plan that excludes closures, excludes compulsory redundancies, and preserves the existing cost structure is not enough to solve this crisis**.

We are not dealing with a normal cyclical dip. We are facing:
- a **shrinking European market**,
- **intense low-cost competition**,
- and a **German cost base that is structurally too high**.

If we simply layer working-time tweaks and temporary measures on top of the current structure, we will not restore competitiveness. We will postpone the problem, drain cash, and weaken the company further. That is not a responsible path.

So let me state management’s position plainly:

1. **Structural capacity reduction is unavoidable.**  
   The company has too much production capacity in Germany for the market outlook ahead.

2. **Labour costs must come down materially.**  
   A meaningful contribution from the workforce is part of any viable settlement.

3. **The old guarantees cannot remain untouched.**  
   If we preserve every legacy protection exactly as-is, we will not reach the cost base required to fund future products and technology.

4. **We need durable, not temporary, savings.**  
   Short-term relief or reversible measures do not solve a structural overcapacity problem.

That said, I do hear one important point from both of you: a durable settlement has to be negotiated, and it has to preserve the company’s industrial core. I am not here to demolish the German base for its own sake. But I am also not prepared to sign up to a cosmetic agreement that leaves us uncompetitive in 2028, 2029, and 2030.

So the real question is not whether change happens, but **what kind of change**:
- If the employee side can bring forward a package that produces **comparable, durable savings** to closures and forced job reductions, then we can discuss it.
- If not, then management will continue to pursue deeper structural measures, including plant rationalization.

I will also say this directly: prolonged strikes would damage output, customers, and the brand, and they would not change the underlying economics. They would only make the adjustment harder and more painful.

So my message is simple: **bring a package that actually closes the cost gap in a durable way, or the pressure for closures and deeper cuts will remain on the table.**
