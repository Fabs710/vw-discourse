## Where we agree
### With management
You are right that:
- margin restoration cannot be postponed,
- the company needs a credible industrial plan,

### With the family owners
You are right that:
- the company cannot freeze itself into permanent decline,
- and governance cannot be used to block all structural change.

### With the expert
The expert confirms what capital markets need to hear:
- a non-closure package can work,
- but only if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity,
- and only if it is quantified site by site with enforceable milestones.

## Where we disagree
### With the works council and union
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and binding guarantees through 2030,

is **not acceptable as a starting package** if it means the company cannot achieve a real cost reset.

I understand the political logic, but from an equity-holder perspective, those red lines are only tolerable if they still allow:
- durable capacity reduction,
- material labor-cost moderation,
- and a credible path back to competitive margins.

If they block that, they become a value trap.

### With the state government
We respect the state’s role, but we do **not** support using statutory influence to preserve an uncompetitive structure indefinitely. A regional industrial anchor is only useful if it can compete. Protecting jobs by preserving a structurally weak footprint is not a sustainable solution for shareholders or, in the long run, for workers.

## Our position
We will support a negotiated package **only if it credibly delivers**:
1. **durable margin improvement,**
2. **quantified capacity reduction,**
3. **material labor-cost contribution,**
4. **clear implementation timing,**
5. **and a believable product/investment plan.**

We are **not** asking for closures as an ideology. We are asking for the economics to work.

That means:
- if non-closure measures can truly match the required savings, we can support them;
- if they cannot, then the parties need to stop pretending that a no-closure pledge is a solution.

## What we need next
The right next step is not another round of abstract red lines. It is a **site-by-site quantified restructuring proposal** comparing:
- closure,
- partial conversion/downsizing,
- and non-closure restructuring,

with:
- net present value of savings,
- one-off costs,
- timing,
- execution risk,
- and labor-relations risk.

If management cannot table that, then the proposal is under-specified.  
If labour cannot show equivalent economics without closures, then the no-closure position is not financeable.
