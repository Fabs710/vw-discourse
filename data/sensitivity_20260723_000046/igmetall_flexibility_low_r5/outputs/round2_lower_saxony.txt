I want to respond very plainly.

I agree with **one central point** raised by management, owners, and investors: **the current cost base is not competitive enough, and the company needs a durable industrial reset.** I do not dispute that. A cosmetic deal would be irresponsible, and delay would only deepen the problem.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that this necessarily means German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or a blank cheque to dismantle the state’s role and the co-determination balance.

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **real turnaround**, not postponement.
- There must be **durable savings**, not symbolic concessions.
- The future must include **investment, product allocation, and industrial modernization**.
- The burden cannot fall on employees alone; **owners, management, and labour must all contribute**.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not the default solution
Management keeps presenting closures as the implied fallback if negotiations do not deliver enough. I reject that framing.

A closure is not just a cost instrument. It destroys:
- jobs,
- regional industrial capacity,
- trust in the social partnership,
- and the company’s standing as an anchor employer.

Before anyone reaches for closures, I expect a **transparent site-by-site comparison** of alternatives. If there are non-closure measures that can deliver comparable structural effect, then closure is not justified.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
I do not support a restructuring strategy that relies on forced layoffs as a routine tool. That would shift the adjustment burden disproportionately onto the workforce and would be politically and economically destabilizing for the region.

If workforce reduction is truly unavoidable, then the first duty is to exhaust:
- natural attrition,
- internal transfers,
- early retirement,
- working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments,
- and negotiated, voluntary instruments.

### 3) The state’s co-determination and veto role is not up for removal
I need to say this clearly: **the state shareholder will not support an outcome that sidelines its statutory role or treats it as decorative capital.** Our blocking-minority position and board role exist to protect the regional interest and industrial stability. That is not something we will trade away.

## To the works council and union
I understand and respect the red lines you have stated:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of job security.

Those are serious positions, and I will not dismiss them. I am prepared to support them **if** the alternative package is credible.

But I also have to be candid: saying “no closures” is not enough on its own. We need a **serious operational alternative** that shows where the savings come from and how the company becomes competitive without gutting the region.

So I support your call for:
- binding site and employment safeguards,
- a fair but limited employee contribution,
- and a binding investment and product plan.

I would add: the package must be **measurable, staged, and enforceable**. Otherwise it becomes a promise, not a restructuring.

## To management
Management has a legitimate demand for structural savings, but I do not accept the tone that closures are the only serious path.

If you want support from the state shareholder, then bring:
- a quantified alternative scenario,
- clear savings targets,
- a realistic investment plan,
- and proof that your proposed measures can work without breaking the company’s social foundation.

I will not endorse a plan that “wins” on paper but loses the region, the workforce, and ultimately the company’s legitimacy.

## To the family holding and investors
I hear the concern about value destruction, and I share it. But short-term financial discipline is not the same thing as long-term value creation.

A package that triggers industrial conflict, damages trust, and tears up the company’s regional anchor role will also destroy value. So I would urge you not to treat job protection and competitiveness as opposites. In a company like this, they are linked.

## What I can support
I am prepared to back a negotiated restructuring if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting assumption**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A transparent site-by-site restructuring plan**
4. **Meaningful but limited employee contributions**
5. **Binding investment and product commitments**
6. **Preservation of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
7. **A credible path to durable cost reduction through reversible and negotiated measures**

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- a closure agenda,
- mass layoffs,
- a deal that guts regional employment,
- or an arrangement that weakens the state’s role in governance.

If management believes non-closure measures cannot work, then it has to prove that with facts, not assumptions. Until then, closures are not a necessity; they are a choice. And I do not accept that choice as the starting point.
