We are aligned on the need for a **real turnaround**, and I appreciate that several parties now explicitly reject a one-sided burden on workers. But I need to be very clear: **a restructuring built on closures, forced redundancies, and dismantling collective standards is not a turnaround we can support.**

## Where we agree
- The company does need **durable competitiveness**.
- The solution must include a **future industrial and investment plan**.
- The workforce can and should contribute, **if the burden is shared fairly**.
- Purely symbolic measures are not enough.

## Where we disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable default
Management, investors, and to a lesser extent the family owners keep treating closures as a legitimate fallback if negotiations do not move fast enough. For us, that is exactly the wrong logic.

Closing plants is not a neutral efficiency tool. It destroys trust, skills, regional industrial capacity, and the basis for a stable collective agreement. If you start from the assumption that closures remain on the table, you are making labour pay for management’s strategic mistakes.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are off the table
I will say it again: **no compulsory redundancies**.  
If this restructuring requires forced layoffs, then it is being designed to shift all adjustment risk onto employees. That is not a fair settlement, and it will not be accepted by the workforce.

### 3) The collective wage framework is not open for dismantling
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary pay restraint,
- foregone bonuses,
- working-time measures,
- capacity adjustments,
- productivity improvements,
- and limited, negotiated contributions.

But we are **not** prepared to accept a permanent erosion of the collective wage framework under the guise of competitiveness. A durable settlement has to preserve wage standards, not use this crisis to reset them downward forever.

## On management’s argument
You say alternatives must achieve the “same structural effect” as closures. That is too narrow.

Structural adjustment can be achieved through:
- shift reductions,
- capacity consolidation without closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- product and investment reallocation,
- targeted productivity commitments,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

What we will not accept is a demand that the workforce absorb open-ended insecurity so management can preserve maximum strategic freedom.

If you want contribution, then contribute yourselves:
- protect investment,
- give binding product commitments,
- and stop presenting closures as the only serious option before the alternatives have even been properly tested.

## To the family owners and investors
You keep speaking about value and margin restoration. Fine. Then understand this: **industrial conflict destroys value too**.

A package that triggers warning strikes, undermines trust, and weakens the social partnership may look hard-headed on paper, but it is economically reckless in practice. Long-term value comes from a stable, skilled workforce and a credible industrial settlement — not from forcing through a cost reset at any price.

If you want the workforce to accept sacrifice, then ownership and top management must also show sacrifice and commitment:
- investment,
- product allocation,
- and binding job and site security.

## To the regional state government
You are right not to be decorative capital. Use that position to prevent the company from turning this into a closure-and-layoff exercise. But I need to be equally frank: **conditional guarantees are not enough if they become a loophole for later job destruction**.

If employees are asked to contribute, they need real security in return:
- protection of German sites,
- no forced redundancies,
- and a clear future for the plants.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We remain open to a serious package if it contains all of the following:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and employment guarantees**
4. **A fair employee contribution that is temporary and reversible**
5. **Owner, management, and shareholder contributions as well**
6. **A credible investment and product plan for the German footprint**

## What we need next
If management wants seriousness, then bring a **site-by-site, transparent alternative scenario** showing:
- required savings,
- what capacity changes are actually needed,
- what contribution each side makes,
- and how jobs and sites are secured.

Without that, “closure if necessary” is not a plan. It is pressure politics.

We are ready to negotiate. But we are not ready to bargain away jobs, sites, and wage standards because management prefers the easiest route.
