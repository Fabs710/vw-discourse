From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the positions are now sufficiently clear.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, the state, and the expert on the core diagnosis:

- the German cost base is too high,
- margins are not competitive enough,
- and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value.

We also agree with the expert that **non-closure measures can be substantial**, but that they are **not automatically equivalent** to closure economics. That is exactly why we want a quantified, site-by-site comparison rather than slogans.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030,

is **not financeable as a starting position** unless you can show, with hard numbers, that the alternative package produces the same durable savings.

Right now, you have not done that. You have asserted that non-closure measures can work, but not proven they can close the structural gap. From a shareholder perspective, that is not enough.

I also reject the idea that closures are merely “pressure politics.” If one or more sites are genuinely redundant in the network, then refusing to acknowledge that before the economics are tested would just preserve overcapacity and delay the pain. That would be value-destructive.

### With the state government
We respect your role and your regional mandate, but we do **not** support using statutory influence to preserve an uncompetitive footprint indefinitely. Protecting jobs by preserving structurally weak capacity is not a sustainable solution for shareholders, and in the long run it is not a sustainable solution for workers either.

### With management
We agree with your need for structural savings, but we do **not** accept closures as a substitute for a proper analysis. Saying “closures remain on the table” is not a plan. You still need to prove, site by site, why non-closure measures do or do not work.

## Our position
We are prepared to support a negotiated restructuring **only if it credibly delivers**:

1. durable margin improvement,
2. quantified capacity reduction,
3. material labour-cost contribution,
4. a credible investment and product plan,
5. and implementation certainty.

We are **not** asking for closures as an ideology.  
We are asking for the economics to work.

## What must happen next
We want a **fully quantified site-by-site restructuring proposal** comparing:

- closure,
- downsizing / conversion,
- and non-closure restructuring,

including:

- NPV of savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- capacity effects,
- headcount impact,
- and execution risk.

If that analysis is not available, then it should be prepared immediately.

## Bottom line
We will support a tough deal if it restores competitiveness.  
We will **not** support a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base, blocks structural change, or substitutes political comfort for financial reality.
