### Core impasse
The dispute is no longer about whether the company needs restructuring. The impasse is about **the acceptable method of achieving structural savings**:

- **Labour / state**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding security and co-determination.
- **Management / family / investors**: no upfront exclusion of closures or deeper workforce reduction if the numbers require it.

So the deadlock is between:
1. **economic flexibility** versus **employment/site guarantees**, and  
2. **trust-building safeguards** versus **management’s demand for restructuring latitude**.

The expert position suggests a possible technical bridge: non-closure measures may be substantial, but their equivalence to closure economics must be proven site by site.

---

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise
These are not endorsements, but structured options that could bridge the most conflicting positions:

#### 1) Site-by-site decision ladder with thresholds
Adopt a formal sequence:
- first test non-closure measures,
- then downsizing/conversion,
- only then consider closure **if specific, quantified thresholds are not met**.

Possible threshold criteria:
- minimum fixed-cost reduction,
- minimum capacity reduction,
- minimum NPV improvement,
- and minimum execution feasibility.

This could partially bridge:
- labour’s demand for serious alternatives,
- and management’s insistence that closure not be ruled out in advance.

#### 2) Temporary protection with review gates
Agree to:
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined initial period,
- no site closure during that period,
- and a binding review after quantified milestones are tested.

If milestones are missed, the parties revisit the site-level options under a predefined process.

This could bridge:
- labour’s insistence on immediate security,
- and management’s need for a credible adjustment path.

#### 3) Conditional package: jobs preserved where savings are proven
Set up a package in which:
- sites with sufficient savings from non-closure measures receive stronger guarantees,
- sites where the gap remains after independent verification enter a different negotiated track.

That would separate:
- the sites where compromise is feasible,
- from the sites where the economics remain contested.

This may bridge:
- the state’s regional protection concerns,
- and investors’ demand for differentiated economics.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If an independent site-by-site analysis shows that one plant cannot reach required cost and capacity targets through non-closure measures alone, what adjustment path would you be prepared to negotiate instead of closure?

#### To the Industrial Union
What specific combination of voluntary exits, working-time measures, redeployment, and productivity changes would you consider sufficient to close the savings gap without compulsory redundancies?

#### To Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to a transparent, independently reviewed site-by-site model before any closure decision is taken, including a quantified non-closure option and the thresholds it must fail before closure is considered?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete conditions would you attach to your support so that protection of jobs and sites is tied to verifiable investment, utilization, and employment outcomes rather than open-ended assurances?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a structured process that postpones closure decisions until independent analysis has tested non-closure alternatives against agreed economic thresholds, even if that limits immediate strategic flexibility?

#### To the Institutional Investors
Would you accept a time-bound restructuring framework that prioritizes non-closure measures first, provided it includes hard triggers for escalation if the savings gap remains?

#### To the Expert
Can you specify the minimum data set and decision thresholds needed to determine when an open-but-downsized site is economically comparable to closure, and when it is not?
