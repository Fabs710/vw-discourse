From an industrial restructuring and workforce-planning perspective, the critical question is now well defined:

**Can a non-closure package remove enough fixed cost, capacity, and workforce expense to be economically comparable to a closure-based option at each site?**

## Technical assessment

### 1) What is likely true
- The company appears to need **structural adjustment**, not just temporary concessions.
- **Non-closure measures can be substantial** if they include:
  - shift elimination,
  - line consolidation,
  - product reallocation,
  - automation,
  - logistics simplification,
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - working-time changes,
  - wage moderation,
  - and management/owner contributions.
- But **non-closure is not automatically equivalent to closure**.

### 2) Why closure economics are different
A closure typically removes most site-level fixed cost in one step:
- plant overhead,
- maintenance,
- security,
- local administration,
- utilities and support functions,
- and stranded capacity cost.

A downsizing or conversion package may still leave:
- residual overhead,
- underused facilities,
- duplicated functions,
- and ongoing inefficiencies from operating a “half-empty” site.

### 3) When a non-closure package can be close to equivalent
A non-closure option can approach closure economics if it:
- removes a large share of excess capacity,
- materially cuts local overhead,
- reduces headcount substantially,
- eliminates underused shifts or lines,
- and avoids large ongoing stranded-cost burdens.

If those conditions are not met, the site often remains structurally expensive even after concessions.

## Practical benchmark
As a rule of thumb, an open-site restructuring has to do most of the following to rival a closure:
- cut enough volume/capacity to match realistic demand,
- remove a major portion of fixed cost,
- reduce staffing materially,
- simplify operations,
- and avoid persistent underutilization.

If the plant stays substantially underloaded, a non-closure package usually becomes only a **partial substitute**, not a full one.

## What a proper site comparison should include
For each site, compare:

- current and forecast utilization
- unit cost before and after
- fixed-cost removal
- headcount reduction
- one-off restructuring cost
- implementation timing
- execution risk
- NPV over 3–5 years
- strategic role of the site in future product allocation

## My expert conclusion
**A non-closure restructuring can sometimes match closure economics, but only if it meaningfully removes fixed cost and capacity at the site level.**  
Where a plant is fundamentally overcapacity relative to future volume, a non-closure package often cannot fully substitute for closure economics.
