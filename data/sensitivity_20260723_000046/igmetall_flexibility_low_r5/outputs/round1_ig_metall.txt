We share the core assessment that **the workforce must not be made to carry the entire burden of this crisis**. A restructuring that simply shifts the pain onto employees through closures, forced redundancies, and permanent erosion of wage standards is **not acceptable**.

Let me be equally clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

Those are not bargaining details; they are red lines.

That said, we have already shown that we are prepared to negotiate a **fair shared-sacrifice solution**. We are open to:

- labour-cost contributions,
- temporary pay restraint or foregone bonuses,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity measures,
- and other reversible tools,

**provided** they are tied to binding safeguards for jobs, sites, and future industrial investment.

What we will not accept is a scenario where management demands structural cuts from the workforce while owners, top management, and capital remain protected from any contribution. If there is to be a turnaround, then **everyone must contribute** — not just the people on the shop floor.

So our position is:

1. **A closure agenda is off the table.**
2. **Forced layoffs are off the table.**
3. **Any employee contribution must be matched by binding guarantees and a credible industrial future.**
4. **If management insists on making workers absorb all the pain, then industrial action remains on the table.**

We are ready for serious talks, but only on the basis of **job security, site security, and fairness**.
