I’ll be equally clear: I acknowledge the need for a credible industrial future, and I am prepared to negotiate serious alternative measures. But I cannot accept a response that preserves the current cost base and leaves us uncompetitive.

Let me respond directly.

### Where I agree
I agree that this crisis was not created by the workforce alone. I also agree that a solution based only on brute-force cuts, without a future product and investment plan, would be reckless. The company must invest into the next generation of products, technology, and industrial capability. That part is non-negotiable as well.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that:

- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and no material labour-cost contribution

can all be guaranteed upfront while still expecting management to restore competitiveness.

That combination is not a restructuring. It is a postponement.

German factory costs are structurally too high. Capacity is above what the market can sustain. Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our brand is missing its return-on-sales target. If we leave the current footprint and cost structure largely intact, we will simply burn cash and weaken the company further.

### What I need to see
If the workforce and union want alternatives to closures, then those alternatives must deliver **the same structural effect**:

- a real, durable reduction in capacity,
- a meaningful and lasting labour-cost contribution,
- and a credible path to restoring margins.

That means I am open to solutions such as:

- site-level capacity consolidation,
- shift reductions,
- working-time changes,
- productivity commitments,
- temporary or structural pay moderation,
- and other negotiated measures.

But I will not pretend that symbolic savings or short-term freezes are enough.

### On guarantees
I am not prepared to grant blanket site and employment guarantees through 2030 if they lock in an uncompetitive structure. Guarantees can only be part of the package if the package itself delivers lasting cost relief and real flexibility. Otherwise we are guaranteeing decline.

### On closures and layoffs
I hear your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. But I have to be honest: if we cannot secure a durable structural solution through negotiation, then deeper measures remain on the table. I would prefer to avoid forced conflict, but I will not rule out tough decisions simply to preserve every site unchanged.

### Bottom line
Management is ready for a negotiated settlement, but not for a cosmetic one.

If the employee side is prepared to contribute in a way that materially lowers the cost base and adapts capacity to market reality, then we can talk about investment commitments and a future industrial plan.

If the answer is “no closures, no layoffs, no real cost contribution,” then there is no serious restructuring path, and that is not acceptable to management, owners, or investors.
