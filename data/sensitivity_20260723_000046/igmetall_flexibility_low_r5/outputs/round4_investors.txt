We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **credible, durable turnaround**.
- A cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.
- The burden should not fall only on employees; **management, owners, and labour must all contribute**.
- The key question is rightly **site-by-site economics**, not slogans.
- The expert is also right that **non-closure measures can be substantial**.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be a starting rule
From a shareholder perspective, that would be a blank check against the very restructuring the company may need. If a site is structurally overcapacity and non-closure measures cannot remove enough fixed cost, then closure must remain available.

I do **not** support preserving every footprint regardless of economics.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” also cannot be absolute
I prefer voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and working-time changes. But if the quantified gap remains, the company cannot be forced to carry an uncompetitive cost base indefinitely. An absolute ban on workforce reduction would simply protect overcapacity and impair margins.

### 3) Blank guarantees through 2030 are not financeable upfront
I understand the workforce’s need for security, but unconditional guarantees before the economics are tested would freeze flexibility and undermine the turnaround. Any protection has to be **conditional, measurable, and tied to delivery**.

### 4) Co-determination and state rights are real, but they do not override economics
We respect the governance structure. We do not accept using it to entrench structural inefficiency. If the company cannot adapt, long-term value erodes for everyone, including employees.

## What we need now
The discussion must move from principle to numbers. We support an **independent, site-level model** comparing:
- closure,
- downsizing/conversion,
- and non-closure restructuring,

with:
- fixed-cost removal,
- capacity effects,
- workforce impact,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- execution risk,
- and 3–5 year NPV.

[SUMMON: an independent industrial network-planning and restructuring finance expert with full site-level access to compare closure, downsizing/conversion, and non-closure scenarios, including fixed-cost removal, capacity effects, headcount impact, one-off costs, execution risk, and 3–5 year NPV.]

## Our position
We will support a tough restructuring **if it demonstrably restores competitiveness**.  
We will **not** support a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive, blocks necessary capacity adjustment, or substitutes political comfort for financial reality.
