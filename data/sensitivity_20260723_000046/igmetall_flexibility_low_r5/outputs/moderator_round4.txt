Core impasse, as it stands, is procedural and substantive:

1. **Procedural impasse:**  
   Labour-side actors want the process to exclude closures and compulsory redundancies as acceptable end states.  
   Management, the family holding, and investors want those options to remain available if the quantified gap is not closed.

2. **Substantive impasse:**  
   The sides agree that non-closure measures can be substantial, but they disagree on whether they can be sufficient **site by site**.  
   The unresolved question is whether open-site restructuring can remove enough fixed cost and capacity to match the economics of closure.

3. **Trust / sequencing impasse:**  
   Labour and the state want a freeze on unilateral moves while analysis runs.  
   Management wants flexibility preserved during analysis.  
   This is blocking agreement on the data process itself.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal A: Temporary “standstill plus verification” framework
- Management agrees to **no unilateral closure announcements**, **no compulsory redundancies**, and **no site hollowing-out actions** during the analysis period.
- Labour agrees not to pre-reject closure as a theoretical outcome, but only to treat it as **unproven until the independent review reports**.
- The state or an agreed trustee body monitors compliance.

**Purpose:** preserves trust long enough for the site-by-site comparison to be decision-grade.

### Proposal B: Conditional restructuring corridor
- Parties define a **narrow corridor of acceptable outcomes** for each site:
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time changes,
  - line/shift reduction,
  - product reassignment,
  - and temporary contributions.
- Closure or compulsory redundancies may only be considered if the expert report shows that the corridor cannot achieve a predefined savings target.

**Purpose:** keeps closures from becoming the first bargaining option while still allowing a quantified fallback test.

### Proposal C: Package deal with reciprocal guarantees
- Labour accepts a limited, time-bound contribution package if paired with:
  - owner capital contribution,
  - management contribution,
  - binding investment/product commitments,
  - and a review-triggered site protection mechanism.
- In return, the company gets a negotiated path to cost reduction without immediate confrontation over closures.

**Purpose:** converts the dispute from “cuts vs no cuts” into “shared burden for defined protections.”

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
If the independent review shows that one site still has a large residual fixed-cost gap after all non-closure measures, what exact additional measure would you accept that preserves the site without calling closure a bargaining tool?

### To Union
What minimum combination of temporary labour-cost moderation, redeployment, and capacity reduction would you consider sufficient to support a site-specific deal without compulsory redundancies?

### To Management
Will you commit now to a formal standstill on unilateral closures and workforce reductions while the independent site-level analysis is being completed?

### To Regional State Government
How will you use your blocking and governance role so that the analysis cannot be bypassed by pressure, while still allowing a decision if a site genuinely fails the quantified test?

### To Controlling Family Holding
What concrete owner contribution are you prepared to place on the table now — capital, guarantees, or product allocation — before asking labour to accept restructuring risk?

### To Institutional Investors
If non-closure measures reduce most, but not all, of the gap versus closure, what residual difference would you tolerate in exchange for lower conflict, lower execution risk, and preserved industrial relations?

### To Independent Expert
What threshold would you use to judge that an open-site restructuring is “good enough” economically, even if it does not fully match closure savings?
