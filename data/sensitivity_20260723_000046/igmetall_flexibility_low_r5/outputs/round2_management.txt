I appreciate that the works council and the union are finally engaging with the need for a real industrial future, and I also note the state’s willingness to accept that the cost base must come down. But let me be equally direct: **none of the positions just tabled yet meets the scale of the problem.**

### Where I agree
I agree on three points:

1. **This cannot be a cosmetic deal.**  
   I do not want a short-lived peace that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

2. **A future product and investment plan is essential.**  
   Cutting costs without funding the next generation of products would be self-defeating.

3. **The burden cannot fall on employees alone.**  
   There must be a package that includes owners, management, and labour contributions.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) “No plant closures” is not a serious starting condition
The works council and union are trying to remove the only instrument that can create a decisive structural reset if negotiations fail. I understand why you do that, but I do not accept it.

If you want to rule out closures, then you must show, with numbers, how the same savings will be achieved through other measures. So far I have heard principles, not an equivalent economic plan.

#### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute if the cost base stays uncompetitive
I am not saying forced layoffs are my preferred route. They are not. But I will not pre-commit to preserving every job regardless of whether the business can sustain it. That would be irresponsible.

#### 3) Binding guarantees through 2030 are not acceptable if they freeze decline
A blanket guarantee before the restructuring is designed would lock us into the very cost structure we are trying to fix. That is not protection; it is postponement.

### On your claim that alternatives have not been proven
You are right that alternatives must be tested transparently. I am prepared to do that. But “tested” does not mean “politically comfortable.” It means site-by-site, line-by-line, with real savings, not aspirational ones.

So here is the standard I expect:

- **durable capacity reduction**, not just shifted utilization,
- **material labour-cost contribution**, not symbolic moderation,
- **measurable productivity gains**, not promises,
- **implementation that lowers the cost base for years**, not one budget cycle.

### To the works council
You say all German plants must remain open. I cannot accept that as a red line. If a site is structurally underutilized and its cost position cannot be brought in line, then the issue is not whether closure is emotionally difficult. The issue is whether the company survives competitively.

You also say the workforce did not create the crisis alone. I agree. But that does not mean the workforce can be insulated from the consequences of strategic correction. Everyone will have to contribute.

### To the union
You say compulsory redundancies are off the table. I hear you. But a restructuring that delivers real savings may still require workforce reduction. If you want to avoid forced measures, then the employee side has to bring alternatives that are economically equivalent and enforceable.

Temporary pay restraint, bonuses, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures may help. They do not automatically add up to the scale we need.

### To the state government
I respect your role and your responsibility for the region. But I must also say this clearly: the special statute is not a veto over reality. If we protect every site and every structure regardless of economics, then we will destroy competitiveness and, in the end, more jobs than we save.

A regional industrial anchor is only an anchor if it remains viable.

### To owners and investors
I agree that value destruction through prolonged conflict is unacceptable. That is exactly why we need a settlement that actually changes the economics. Market patience is not unlimited, and neither is management credibility.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a package that includes:

- capacity consolidation,
- shift and working-time changes,
- productivity commitments,
- temporary or structural labour-cost moderation,
- targeted job reductions where needed,
- investment commitments tied to the restructuring,
- and conditional guarantees that are earned by real savings.

### What I am not prepared to accept
I am **not** prepared to accept:

- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- guarantees that prevent structural adjustment,
- or a deal that leaves the cost base unchanged.

### Bottom line
If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then it must come forward with a **credible, quantified alternative** that delivers comparable, durable savings.

Until then, closures and tougher measures remain on the table because the company needs a solution that restores competitiveness, not just industrial peace.
