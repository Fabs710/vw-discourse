### Core impasse
The deadlock is now concentrated in **one central trade-off**:

- **Labour/state side** wants **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding site/job security, and preserved co-determination**.
- **Management/owners/investors** want **durable structural savings**, and insist that **closures and workforce reduction remain available** if non-closure measures do not close the gap.
- The **expert assessment** says non-closure measures can be substantial, but **may not fully match closure economics** unless they remove enough fixed cost and capacity site by site.

So the impasse is not over the need for a turnaround. It is over **the means of achieving it** and **what must be proven before closures become legitimate**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three concrete bridging options that could be tabled procedurally:

#### 1) Site-by-site “no decision before proof” package
- Immediate commitment to a **transparent site-by-site comparison** of:
  - closure,
  - downsizing/conversion,
  - and non-closure restructuring.
- For each site, the parties would jointly review:
  - savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - capacity effects,
  - employment impact,
  - execution risk.
- **No closure decision** would be taken until that analysis is completed and jointly reviewed.

**Purpose:** gives management the right to test economics, while giving labour/state a proof-based process before closures are used.

#### 2) Conditional job/site security with trigger-based review
- A **time-limited employment and site-security framework** could apply, but not as an absolute blanket guarantee.
- In parallel, the parties define:
  - savings milestones,
  - investment milestones,
  - product allocation milestones,
  - and review dates.
- If targets are met, protections continue or expand.
- If targets are missed, the parties move to a predefined escalation process rather than immediate unilateral action.

**Purpose:** gives workers real security, while giving owners/management an enforceable performance path.

#### 3) Mixed restructuring package with burden-sharing
- Combine:
  - shift and capacity adjustments,
  - temporary labour-cost moderation,
  - voluntary exits / attrition / retirement pathways,
  - product and investment commitments,
  - management and owner contributions.
- Add a rule that **forced redundancies and closures are postponed** while this package is implemented and assessed.
- If savings remain insufficient after a defined period, the parties return to the table with the quantified gap still open.

**Purpose:** preserves the possibility of structural savings without making closures the first move.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**What quantified savings gap remains after the best non-closure scenario, by site, and what specific evidence shows that gap cannot be closed through capacity reduction, product reallocation, or voluntary workforce measures?**

#### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**If closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded in advance, what exact operational measures would you accept to deliver the same structural savings, and how would you verify their sufficiency site by site?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**What temporary employee contribution are you prepared to put on the table, and under what measurable owner/management commitments would you agree that this contribution is fair and reversible?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**What form of state-backed conditional support can you offer that protects the industrial base without being interpreted as a veto against any restructuring outcome?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a staged process in which closures are not ruled out, but can only proceed after a jointly reviewed site-by-site comparison demonstrates that non-closure measures cannot achieve comparable NPV savings?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a restructuring that avoids closures if it produces comparable net present value savings, even if the implementation is slower and more negotiated than a closure-based plan?**

#### To the Independent Expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, fixed-cost removal, and workforce reduction would you require before labeling a non-closure package economically equivalent to a closure package at a given site?**
