### 1) Areas of partial agreement
- All parties agree the company needs a **credible turnaround** and **durable competitiveness**, not delay or cosmetic measures.
- There is broad agreement that the solution must include a **future industrial and investment plan**, not cost cuts alone.
- Most parties accept that the workforce should **not bear the full burden**; any employee contribution should be paired with contributions from owners and management.
- There is openness, in principle, to **negotiated measures** such as capacity adjustments, working-time changes, productivity measures, and some form of labour-cost moderation.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state reject closures; management and investors leave them open if alternatives fail.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and the state reject them; management and investors do not rule out tougher measures if negotiation fails.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour wants binding site and employment guarantees through 2030; management, family owners, and investors reject blanket guarantees and prefer conditional ones.
- **Extent of employee contribution:** Labour supports limited, negotiated contributions; management and investors want contributions that produce a substantial, durable cost reset.
- **Role of co-determination/state veto:** The state insists on a strong role and rejects outcomes that bypass it; management and owners warn against a framework that blocks meaningful restructuring.
- **Whether negotiated alternatives can match closure-level savings:** This is the central unresolved factual and strategic dispute.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Job security vs. structural cost reduction**
- **Preserving sites vs. reducing capacity to market level**
- **Binding long-term guarantees vs. flexibility to adapt**
- **Shared sacrifice vs. the risk of symbolic concessions**
- **Industrial peace vs. the possibility of strikes and escalation**
- **Political/regional stability vs. shareholder value restoration**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on **specific package design**, not positions in the abstract:
- What exact **capacity and cost reductions** are required?
- Which **sites, functions, or shifts** could be adjusted without closures?
- What **employee contributions** are acceptable, and for how long?
- What **guarantees** can be made conditional, measurable, and enforceable?
- What **investment and product commitments** would justify concessions?

A useful next step would be for management to table a **concrete restructuring scenario** with quantified savings and for labour/state/owners to respond with **alternative measures of equivalent effect**.
