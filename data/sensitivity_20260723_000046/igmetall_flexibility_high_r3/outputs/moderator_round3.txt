## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed; all sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the decision rule**:

- **Labour / works council / state** want a **quantified no-closure test first**, with binding protections, and treat closures as unacceptable unless proven unavoidable.
- **Management / investors / family** want to keep **closures and deeper site rationalization genuinely on the table** because they doubt a no-closure package can close the structural gap.
- The **expert** has confirmed both sides’ partial point: a no-closure package can be material, but may still be insufficient if the structural gap is large.

So the deadlock is:
> **Can the company be required to test a protected no-closure package before any closure decision, and if so, for how long and under what measurable failure criteria?**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Protected no-closure trial with hard review gate
A time-limited package is agreed that includes:
- no German plant closures during the trial period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- voluntary exits, working-time measures, productivity actions, redeployment, and temporary employee contributions,
- binding site and investment commitments,
- monthly or quarterly reporting.

At the end of the trial, the parties use **pre-agreed financial triggers**:
- if recurring savings hit a defined threshold and utilization stabilizes, the no-closure path continues;
- if not, a second-stage discussion on selective rationalization begins.

**Why this bridges:** it gives labour protection now and gives management/investors a real failure test later.

---

### 2) Site-by-site differentiated outcome, not one blanket rule
Instead of one company-wide rule on closures, parties agree to classify plants into three buckets:
- **Protected sites** with viable future model allocation and acceptable utilization,
- **Conditional sites** that must hit specific savings and utilization milestones,
- **Structural problem sites** that may be candidates for conversion, partial downsizing, or other non-blanket rationalization measures.

This avoids a single binary “all sites stay open” vs “closures everywhere” framing.

**Why this bridges:** it allows economic differentiation while preserving maximum possible footprint.

---

### 3) Compensation package for any site reduction
If any site reduction, conversion, or partial exit is considered later, it must be paired with:
- a stronger redeployment and qualification fund,
- enhanced voluntary exit terms,
- investment commitments for remaining sites,
- a regional transition plan with state involvement,
- and a formal co-determination process.

**Why this bridges:** it makes any structural change conditional on visible mitigation, which may reduce resistance from labour and state actors.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific recurring annual savings threshold, by site and in total, would you accept as evidence that a protected no-closure package has genuinely failed?**

### Trade Union
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure restructuring with binding milestone reviews, if compulsory redundancies remained prohibited during the trial period?**

### Management
**Will you publish a site-by-site bridge showing the exact savings from each lever before asking for any closure mandate?**

### Regional State Government
**Will you commit to using your blocking rights if the board attempts closures before the no-closure package has been tested against agreed milestones?**

### Controlling Family
**Are you prepared to formally back a no-closure trial period, or do you insist that closure authority remains available from day one?**

### Institutional Investors
**What residual-gap level, after a no-closure package, would you consider acceptable enough to avoid site rationalization?**

### Expert
**What minimum utilization and recurring savings thresholds would make a no-closure outcome operationally credible on a site-by-site basis?**
