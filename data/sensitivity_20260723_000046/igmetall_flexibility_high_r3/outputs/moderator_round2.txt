## Core impasse

The central blockage is not about whether restructuring is needed; nearly all parties accept that it is. The impasse is over **how much structural change is required and who must bear the risk**.

More specifically, the split is between:

- **Labour/state side:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, strong job and site guarantees, preservation of co-determination.
- **Management/shareholder side:** structural cost and capacity reduction, with closures and workforce reductions remaining available if needed.
- **Investors/family:** require a quantified, durable savings path but differ on how much conflict or site reduction is acceptable.

The unresolved issue is whether a **credible no-closure package** can close the competitiveness gap, or whether the numbers require **site rationalization and deeper workforce reduction**. At present, the discussion contains positions and counterpositions, but not yet a shared quantified bridge.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise paths

### 1) Quantified no-closure test package
A time-limited package could be negotiated in which the company accepts:
- no German plant closures for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- and binding investment and product allocation commitments.

In return, labour would provide:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- voluntary exits,
- wage or bonus moderation,
- redeployment and productivity measures.

**Condition:** management and the experts would need to produce a site-by-site savings test showing that this package closes a defined percentage of the gap within a fixed deadline. If the test is not met, parties would reopen the question of site rationalization.

---

### 2) Conditional protection with a fallback mechanism
A second option is a **protection-first agreement** with a built-in fallback:

- Sites receive temporary protection from closure and compulsory redundancies.
- The workforce accepts measurable contributions now.
- Management commits to transparent milestones on cost, utilization, and capex.
- An independent review is triggered after a set period.

**Condition:** if the milestones are missed, the parties pre-agree a second-round process that may include limited site rationalization, but only after the quantified failure of the protected scenario has been established.

This preserves labour’s red lines for now while giving management and investors a credible enforcement path.

---

### 3) Selective rationalization instead of blanket closure
A third compromise is to shift from the binary “all sites protected vs closures on the table” to a narrower model:

- No broad closure programme.
- But one or more sites may be converted, merged, or reduced in scope.
- Workforce impacts are managed primarily through voluntary exits, transfers, and early retirement.
- Remaining German sites receive future-model allocation and capex commitments.

This would aim to satisfy the demand for structural savings without a full closure-driven restructuring, while still allowing a measurable reduction in footprint and fixed cost.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**Which specific, quantified combination of working-time, voluntary exits, redeployment, and productivity measures would you accept as sufficient to close the gap without closures, and by when?**

### Industrial Union
**What minimum annual savings and workforce reduction would you consider compatible with your red lines, and how would you verify that the package is structurally durable rather than temporary?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target do you require by site and by year, and what quantified threshold would make you conclude that a no-closure package is genuinely insufficient?**

### Regional State Government
**If the numbers show that limited site rationalization is necessary, what specific forms of adjustment would you be prepared to tolerate without withdrawing support?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What concrete evidence would persuade you that closures should truly remain only a last resort, rather than a bargaining tool used too early?**

### Institutional Investors
**Would you support a no-closure package if it delivers a verified multi-year margin improvement of a defined size, even if it takes longer to achieve than a closure-led plan?**

### Automotive Restructuring Expert
**What single quantified scenario should be treated as the benchmark for deciding whether the no-closure option is genuinely viable or economically exhausted?**
