I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with several other parties on one essential point: **this must be a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.  
I also agree that the company needs real savings, future investment, and a path back to competitiveness. Nobody benefits from endless drift.

I also acknowledge the hard operational reality: if a site is badly underutilized, that creates a cost problem that cannot be ignored.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the leap that because a strong no-closure package may be difficult, plant closures and compulsory redundancies therefore become the “economically honest” answer.

That is exactly the kind of logic that makes workers pay first and asks for proof later. I reject that.

The expert has not said closures are inevitable. The expert has said:
- a no-closure package can still deliver **€300m–€700m per year**
- it may be enough if the gap is moderate
- it may not be enough if the gap is very large

That is a **test condition**, not a mandate for closures.

## To management
You keep saying the restructuring must materially change the economics. Fine. Then show us the actual bridge, site by site:
- what savings come from voluntary exits,
- what comes from working-time measures,
- what comes from productivity,
- what comes from product allocation,
- what comes from internal redeployment,
- and what residual gap remains.

Until that is shown, “all options remain open” is not a plan. It is pressure.

And I need to be very clear:  
**I will not support plant closures or compulsory redundancies as a bargaining instrument.**

If you want cooperation, then bring a real no-closure package with:
- binding job security,
- binding site security,
- fair employee contribution,
- management and owner contributions,
- and serious investment commitments.

Without that, there is no fair settlement.

## To the works council
I support your red lines:
- no plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- no dismantling of co-determination
- no destruction of the collective framework

That remains the correct line.

But I also think we should be disciplined in how we use the expert’s numbers. The point is not to turn “€300m–€700m” into a pretext for forced closure later. The point is to test whether a strong social restructuring can close enough of the gap to make closure unnecessary.

That means we need a concrete no-closure package, not just principles. I support your defence of jobs — but it has to be matched by an operationally serious proposal.

## To the state government
I welcome your rejection of blanket closure strategies. But your support has to be real, not rhetorical. If this goes to the board, the state shareholder must use its blocking position against any closure-led solution that sacrifices whole sites and shifts all the pain onto the workforce.

If the state truly wants to protect regional employment, then it cannot leave workers alone when the pressure rises.

## To the family holding
You say closures are a last resort. Then treat them like a last resort, not a bargaining weapon.

If you insist on keeping closures on the table from the start, that destroys trust and pushes the talks toward confrontation. A “last resort” should only come after a genuine, quantified no-closure alternative has been tested and shown insufficient.

So I ask you plainly:  
Will you support a protected no-closure trial package long enough to test whether it closes the gap?

## To the investors
You are right that a cosmetic deal is useless.  
You are wrong if you think the only alternative to cosmetic is closure.

The expert’s assessment shows a serious no-closure path can still produce major recurring savings. That means the question is not “closures or nothing.” The question is whether management is prepared to pursue a real restructuring that shares the burden fairly and preserves industrial capacity.

## My position
Let me state it without ambiguity:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No one-sided burden on employees**
- **No weakening of the collective wage framework**

I remain open to:
- working-time reductions
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- temporary wage moderation
- bonus restraint
- productivity measures

But only if they are paired with:
- **binding site guarantees**
- **binding job security**
- **investment commitments**
- **future product allocation**
- **fair contributions from management and owners**

## What would move me
A serious package would need to show:
1. the exact annual savings target
2. the residual gap after a strong no-closure package
3. why that package is not enough, if management claims it isn’t
4. what management and owners themselves are contributing
5. binding protection for jobs and sites during the restructuring period

If the no-closure package is genuinely tested and still falls short, then we can discuss additional social measures. But I will not start from closure and redundancy. That is not acceptable.

## Bottom line
The burden is still on those demanding closures to prove they are unavoidable.  
Until that proof is made with site-by-site numbers, the union will continue to oppose closures and compulsory layoffs, and we will mobilize accordingly if necessary.

If management wants an agreement, bring forward a **quantified no-closure proposal with binding protections**. Otherwise, there is no basis for a fair settlement.
