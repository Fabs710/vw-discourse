I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the owners on one point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**.  
The company does need a durable cost and productivity reset, and it must become more competitive if it is to remain an industrial anchor in the long term.

I also agree with labour that **industrial peace, skills retention, and investment matter**. If this becomes a prolonged destructive confrontation, everyone loses — including the company, the workforce, and the region.

So yes: the status quo is not acceptable.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that the answer must include **German plant closures** or treat them as a normal bargaining lever.

The expert has said something more nuanced than management is now implying:
- a strong no-closure package can still produce roughly **€300m–€700m per year**
- that may be enough if the gap is moderate
- it may not be enough if the gap is very large

That means the question is not “closures or nothing.”  
The real question is: **what is the residual gap after the strongest no-closure package is fully tested site by site?**

Until that is answered with real numbers, closure is not an honest inevitability — it is a preference.

## To management
You say the company needs a structural reduction in cost and capacity. I do not dispute that. But you still have not shown, in a sufficiently concrete way, that this requires plant closures in Germany.

What I want is the bridge:
- current gap to target
- savings from voluntary exits
- savings from working-time changes
- savings from redeployment and qualification
- savings from productivity improvements
- savings from product allocation and capex
- residual gap after the strongest no-closure package

Without that, “all options remain open” sounds less like strategy and more like pressure.

And I will be very clear: **I will not support a restructuring that guts regional employment or removes meaningful site protection simply to create management flexibility.**

That is not acceptable.

## To the Works Council
I support your refusal of compulsory redundancies and your insistence that plant closures are not a default answer. That is a legitimate line.

But I also need to be frank: a successful no-closure package must be **serious and quantified**.  
It cannot just be a list of good intentions.

If the company is to avoid closures, then the workforce side must be prepared to discuss:
- working-time adjustments
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- temporary but real employee contributions
- productivity and flexibility improvements

I can support that — **if** it is matched by:
- binding site security
- binding job security for a meaningful period
- investment commitments
- and real future product allocation to the German sites

What I cannot support is asking workers to sacrifice while management and owners keep closure pressure permanently on the table.

## To the Union
You are right that trust is destroyed if closures and forced layoffs are used as a threat from the start. I share that concern.

But I also need to say this: we should not mistake a range of possible savings for proof that a no-closure package will automatically be sufficient.

The expert’s numbers show a real possibility of success — but not a guarantee.  
So the union should not just say “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” and stop there. It should help produce a credible restructuring that proves those red lines are compatible with competitiveness.

I am prepared to back a fair, socially managed adjustment.  
I am **not** prepared to back a one-sided burden on employees.

## To the Controlling Family
You say closures are a last resort. Then act like it.

A last resort is not a bargaining club to be waved around from the outset.  
If you truly mean “last resort,” then you should support a protected no-closure test period with enforceable commitments, not keep the workforce under permanent threat.

I can support difficult change, but not a governance culture that normalizes closure pressure in a company that is central to the state’s economy.

## To Investors
You are right to insist on a credible margin path. That is legitimate. But I want to challenge one assumption: **the most value-maximizing solution is not automatically the harshest one**.

If a closure-led route creates prolonged conflict, damages labor relations, weakens know-how, and disrupts execution, then some of the headline savings can be offset by real operational loss. That risk is not theoretical.

So yes, I understand your call for quantified discipline. But I will not back a solution that improves the spreadsheet while undermining the company’s industrial base.

## My position
Let me state it plainly:

- **No blanket plant-closure strategy**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No outcome that destroys the company’s role as a regional anchor**

At the same time, I do not support freezing the structure in place. There must be real restructuring.

So the path I can support is:
- quantified savings
- socially managed capacity reduction
- voluntary exits and redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- productivity improvements
- targeted investment commitments
- and a genuine no-closure test

## What would move me
I could support a negotiated restructuring if it includes all of the following:
1. **A quantified no-closure savings bridge**
2. **Binding site guarantees for a defined period**
3. **No compulsory redundancies during that period**
4. **A fair employee contribution**
5. **Matching management and owner contributions**
6. **Clear investment and product commitments for German sites**
7. **Preservation of the state’s co-determination and veto role**

If the numbers then show that the no-closure route truly cannot close the gap, we can discuss additional measures. But I will not begin by conceding closures as the presumed answer.

- what each German plant contributes today,
- what a no-closure package delivers at each site,
- what residual gap remains,

Without that, the debate is still too abstract to justify irreversible decisions.

## Bottom line
I support a serious restructuring.  
I do **not** support a restructuring that sacrifices regional employment or treats German plant closures as an acceptable first-order solution.

If management wants my support, it must prove — with hard, site-specific numbers — that a protected no-closure package is insufficient. Until then, my position remains:
**protect the sites, protect the jobs, and restructure in a way that preserves the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.**
