I agree with the Works Council on the core point: **a restructuring that relies on plant closures and compulsory redundancies is not acceptable**.

The burden cannot fall one-sidedly on the workforce. If management says the company needs a durable turnaround, then that has to be proven through a **fair shared-sacrifice package** — not through shutting sites, tearing up collective standards, and making employees pay for strategic mistakes at the top.

Our position is clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **Any labour contribution must be matched by management and owners**
- **Any capacity reduction must be handled through negotiated, socially responsible instruments**

We are prepared to talk about:
- temporary or staged working-time reductions
- voluntary exit options
- redeployment and qualification
- waiving bonuses or other negotiated employee contributions
- productivity and flexibility measures
- a temporary cost contribution if there is a credible, binding package in return

But we will not accept a model where workers are asked to absorb permanent pain while management keeps all strategic options open. That is not a restructuring; that is a transfer of risk onto employees.

If the company truly wants a durable solution, then it needs to put concrete commitments on the table:
- binding site security
- clear job-security guarantees
- investment in future products and technologies
- a fair contribution from shareholders and management
- a path that preserves the industrial base rather than hollowing it out

If those conditions are not met, then we will not give consent lightly. We have already shown willingness to negotiate constructively, but we are equally prepared to escalate industrial action if necessary.

So my message to management and the owners is simple: **we are ready for a serious settlement, but not for forced layoffs or closures dressed up as inevitability.**
