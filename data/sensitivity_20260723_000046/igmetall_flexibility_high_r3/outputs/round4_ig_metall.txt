I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council on the central point: **this must be a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.  
I also agree that the company needs durable savings, and that industrial peace, skills retention, and investment matter.

And I agree with the expert that a **strong no-closure package can still produce several hundred million euros per year** in recurring savings. That is serious. It deserves to be treated as a real option, not dismissed as a slogan.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt by management, investors, and the family to make closures sound “economically honest” before the no-closure route has been fully tested.

That logic is too convenient:
- workforce contribution first,
- pressure on jobs second,
- closures kept available as the final threat.

I reject that sequencing.

A range of possible savings is not proof that closures are necessary. It is proof that the parties must test the strongest no-closure package properly, with hard numbers and binding commitments.

## To management
You still have not given a convincing **site-by-site bridge** that proves the no-closure path is insufficient.

If you want the union to consider deeper sacrifice, then show:
- starting gap to target,
- savings from voluntary exits,
- savings from working-time changes,
- savings from productivity and overhead reduction,
- savings from product allocation and capex,
- residual gap after the strongest no-closure package.

Until that is on the table, “all options remain open” is not a plan. It is a threat.

And let me be very clear: **no compulsory redundancies** and **no plant closures** are still my red lines. That has not changed.

## To the works council
I support your insistence on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- no one-sided burden on employees.

That is the right line.

But I also want to be direct: a serious no-closure proposal must be more than principle. It has to be operationally credible. That means the employee side should be prepared to discuss:
- working-time reductions,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- productivity improvements.

I support that — **if and only if** it is matched by binding job and site security.

## To the state government
You are right to reject blanket closure strategies. But if that is your position, then it must be backed by action, not just language.

If the company tries to force closures before a protected no-closure option has been tested, the state should use its blocking position. Otherwise your “regional stability” stance will be seen as rhetorical rather than protective.

## To the controlling family
You say closures are a last resort. Then treat them like a last resort, not as bargaining leverage from day one.

If you want legitimacy, support a **time-limited protected no-closure trial** with binding commitments.  
If you refuse that, then you are not defending long-term value — you are giving management a license to shift the burden onto the workforce.

## To investors
You are right that a cosmetic deal destroys value.  
You are wrong if you think the only alternative to cosmetics is closure.

The expert has confirmed that a no-closure package can still deliver substantial savings. So the real question is not “closures or nothing.” The real question is whether management is willing to pursue a fair, quantified restructuring that shares the burden.

## On the expert’s benchmark
The expert’s benchmark is useful: roughly **70–80% of the structural gap** should be closed by a no-closure package for it to be credible.

I can live with that as an analytical test.

What I do **not** accept is turning that test into a pretext for closure if management still prefers a harsher route. The test must be used honestly: if no-closure is sufficient, closures are off the table.

## What would move us
A settlement could move the union only if it includes all of these:
- no German plant closures during the restructuring period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site guarantees,
- fair and time-limited employee contribution,
- real management and owner contributions,
- investment and future product commitments,
- full transparency on site economics.

If those conditions are not met, there is no fair settlement.

## Bottom line
We are not moving from the core union position:
- **protect jobs**
- **protect the collective wage framework**
- **avoid forced layoffs**
- **no plant closures**

If management wants cooperation, it must prove with hard numbers that a protected no-closure restructuring cannot work. So far, that proof has not been made.

If the company refuses to engage on that basis, then we will continue to oppose closures and forced redundancies — including through industrial action if necessary.
