As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable to break the historic job-security commitments and then present closures and forced layoffs as if there were no alternative. There are alternatives — but they must be negotiated seriously and fairly.

Our priorities are:

- Keep every German plant open
- Secure all jobs through 2030
- Exclude compulsory layoffs
- Preserve co-determination and meaningful worker influence over any restructuring
- Ensure that any employee contribution is matched by binding investment and future-product commitments
- Avoid one-sided cost-cutting at the expense of the workforce while protecting the company’s long-term viability

We are prepared to discuss a restructuring path that avoids closures and forced redundancies, including capacity adjustments, working-time measures, internal redeployment, voluntary instruments, productivity improvements, and a fair negotiated contribution from management, owners, and workforce alike. But that must be on the basis of binding site guarantees, job guarantees, and respect for the people who built this company.

If management is coming here with a fait accompli — closures, mass job cuts, and the dismantling of guarantees — then you should expect determined resistance. We are not here to rubber-stamp a plan that sacrifices workers to fix a strategic failure at the top.

So my message is simple: no closures, no forced layoffs, no weakening of co-determination. If there is to be a real solution, it must protect jobs and sites first, and only then discuss how to make the company competitive.
