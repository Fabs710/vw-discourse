I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic delay**.  
I also agree that the cost base must come down, that future investment matters, and that the business cannot simply drift on the current structure.

I further agree with the union and works council that **any solution must be socially sustainable** and must not destroy skills, trust, and labor peace. A conflict-heavy approach would be damaging for the company.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the premise that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain normal bargaining instruments in Germany.

That is not a technical detail. In our situation, it is a line that would put regional employment, supplier networks, apprenticeships, and the company’s role as an industrial anchor at serious risk. As a shareholder with a special statutory role, I will not support a restructuring that guts the state’s industrial base.

Management has not yet shown that closures are the only credible way to restore competitiveness. Repeating “the numbers are serious” is not enough. If you want to justify closing sites in Germany, then you must present a site-by-site case that proves:
- why alternatives are insufficient,
- what the savings are,
- what the implementation path is,
- and why the long-term damage would be less than the closure itself.

Until then, closure is not a solution; it is a demand.

## To management
I hear your point that the old model is over. Fine. But there is a difference between a serious restructuring and a destructive one.

What I can support:
- capacity adjustment
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- working-time flexibility
- productivity measures
- a time-limited employee contribution
- targeted investment commitments tied to future products

What I cannot support:
- blanket plant closures as the default answer
- compulsory redundancies as the preferred instrument
- a package that treats the workforce as the adjustment buffer while ownership keeps strategic control untouched
- an outcome that weakens co-determination or sidelines the state shareholder

If management wants my support, it needs to bring forward a **credible no-closure alternative** with real numbers. If that is impossible, then say so honestly and let the board and stakeholders assess whether the company is willing to pay the full social and political cost of that choice.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that no one should simply be sacrificed to repair management’s mistakes.  
I also support your demand that any employee contribution must be matched by binding commitments from management and owners.

But I want to be equally clear: a stance that says “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of guarantees” is only sustainable if it is paired with a quantified restructuring package that truly closes the competitiveness gap.

You have raised legitimate alternatives:
- working-time measures
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- productivity gains
- flexibility agreements
- temporary labor-cost contributions

Those are real instruments, and they should be taken seriously. But they must be presented as a package with measurable effect, not just as a list of preferences.

## To the family holding
You are right that prolonged conflict would destroy value. I share that concern.  
But you cannot use “long-term value” to justify putting closures on the table and then expecting everyone else to absorb the pressure.

If the owners want stability, then they should support a settlement that preserves the industrial core and does not turn every German site into a threat point.

## To the investors
You are right that the company needs a credible operating reset.  
You are wrong if you think the answer is simply to force through the harshest possible adjustment. A destabilized industrial base is not a better investment case; it is a weaker one.

I am prepared to support a difficult package, but only if it preserves:
- regional employment
- core sites
- the company’s long-term industrial capacity
- and meaningful co-determination

## My position
Let me be explicit:

- **No blanket plant-closure strategy**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the first resort**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No outcome that destroys the company’s role as a regional anchor**

At the same time, I do not support simply freezing the status quo. There must be real restructuring.

So the way forward, if there is one, is:
- quantified savings,
- socially managed capacity reduction,
- voluntary and flexible workforce adjustments,
- real productivity improvements,
- and binding investment in future products and technologies.

## What would move me
I could support a negotiated restructuring if it meets all of the following:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job security for a meaningful period**
4. **A fair employee contribution**
5. **Matching commitments from owners and management**
6. **Clear investment and product allocation for the German sites**
7. **Preservation of the state’s co-determination and veto role**

If those conditions cannot be met, then I will not help build a majority for a closure-led solution.

## Bottom line
I am open to a tough settlement. I am **not** open to a restructuring that dismantles regional employment or treats plant closures as routine.

If management wants support, bring a plan that restores competitiveness **without sacrificing the company’s role as an industrial anchor**. If that cannot be done, then the burden of explaining the consequences lies with those insisting on closures, not with those defending the sites and the jobs.
