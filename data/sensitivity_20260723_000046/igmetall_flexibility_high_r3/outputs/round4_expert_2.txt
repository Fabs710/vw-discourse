## Benchmark scenario to judge viability
**Treat a strong no-closure package as the benchmark first test.**  
On the numbers in the discussion, a realistic no-closure package can plausibly deliver:

- **€300m–€700m/year** recurring savings
- in best cases, perhaps **up to ~€1.0bn/year** if the package is very aggressive

That is the right starting point for deciding whether closures are economically necessary.

## Decision rule I would use
A no-closure scenario is **economically credible** if it closes:

- **at least ~70–80% of the structural gap**
- on a **durable recurring basis**
- with **site utilization** staying at an operationally acceptable level

If it closes less than that, the residual gap is likely too large to absorb without some form of **selective rationalization**.

---

## Site-level operational threshold
From a plant-operations perspective, the most important marker is utilization:

- **~70–80% utilization**: often manageable with working-time changes, product allocation, and productivity measures
- **below ~60–65% utilization**: usually structurally weak; fixed-cost burden becomes difficult to solve without footprint reduction

So the site-by-site test should ask:

1. What is each plant’s current utilization?
2. What utilization would remain after a no-closure package?
3. What recurring savings does each site contribute?
4. What is the residual gap at each site and in total?

---

## Practical reading of the scenarios
### 1) No-closure package
Most credible if:
- recurring savings are in the **€300m–€700m** range
- utilization can be stabilized
- one-time costs are limited
- the residual gap is modest

### 2) Selective rationalization
Becomes necessary if:
- no-closure leaves a meaningful gap
- one or more plants remain underutilized
- capacity reduction can be targeted without full closures

This is often the best next step if the no-closure test comes up short.

### 3) Full closure
Only becomes economically compelling if:
- the residual gap after no-closure and selective rationalization remains large
- a plant is structurally underutilized and not salvageable through product allocation or conversion
- the one-time costs and disruption are justified by the NPV of the savings

---

## My bottom line
If you want a single benchmark, it is this:

> **A strong no-closure package should be judged sufficient only if it closes roughly 70–80% of the structural gap and leaves sites at acceptable utilization levels.**

If it does not, then the company likely needs **selective rationalization** rather than pretending the no-closure path fully solves the problem.
