I will be equally direct.

The state government understands the pressure on management. We do **not** deny that the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness. But I must also be clear: **a restructuring that relies on German plant closures, mass job losses, and the hollowing-out of regional industrial capacity is not acceptable to us**.

### Where I agree with management
I agree that:
- the cost base must come down,
- the company needs durable savings, not just accounting moves,
- and the transition to future products must be financed.

I also agree that simply preserving the status quo is not a solution. The business must become stronger.

### Where I disagree
But I do **not** accept the implication that the only serious path is closing sites and forcing large-scale layoffs.

That is too narrow, too risky, and too destructive.

This company is not an abstract balance sheet. It is a central industrial anchor for entire regions, and it carries obligations beyond the next quarterly target. A solution that permanently damages employment, supplier networks, apprenticeship pipelines, and local economic stability is not a “turnaround” in the public interest — it is a short-term fix with long-term damage.

And I will say something very plainly: **the state’s co-determination and blocking position are not decorative.** We are not here to be informed after the fact while major decisions are already settled elsewhere. Any path that sidelines the state, the workforce, or the special statutory safeguards will face resistance.

### To the works council and union
I support your core rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. That position is legitimate. But I would also urge that we stay focused on a **negotiated industrial solution**, not just a defensive line.

The public will support a restructuring only if it is credible and fair:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no blanket plant closures,
- strong investment commitments,
- serious productivity measures,
- and a shared contribution from all sides, including owners and management.

### To management
If you want support for restructuring, then bring forward an alternative package that is genuinely capable of restoring competitiveness **without** destroying the industrial base. That means:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time and flexibility arrangements,
- voluntary instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted cost contributions,
- and binding future-product commitments.

But I am not prepared to endorse a package where the workforce gives ground, the regions absorb the pain, and ownership keeps strategic control without matching responsibility.

### My bottom line
The state is open to a difficult restructuring. We are **not** open to a closure-led solution that treats jobs and sites as disposable.

If management insists that plant closures are unavoidable, then you should be under no illusion: **that will not be a simple governance decision.** It will face serious opposition from us.

A viable outcome must protect:
- regional employment,
- key sites,
- co-determination,
- and the company’s long-term role as an industrial anchor.

If those conditions cannot be met, then we do not have a solution — we have a conflict.
