### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring** and **improved competitiveness**.
- There is broad support for **negotiation rather than unilateral action**.
- Several instruments are seen as acceptable in principle:
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - working-time adjustments
  - flexibility measures
  - targeted investment commitments
- There is general recognition that a solution must be **socially sustainable** and should avoid prolonged conflict if possible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state reject them; management, family holding, and investors say they must remain possible.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour side rejects them; management and investors want workforce reductions at a scale that materially changes the cost base.
- **Job guarantees / site guarantees:** Labour and state want binding protections; management, family holding, and investors say blanket guarantees are incompatible with competitiveness.
- **Scale of cost reduction:** Management, family holding, and investors insist on substantial structural savings; labour and state insist these must not come through closures or forced layoffs.
- **Role of co-determination and political oversight:** Labour and state insist these must remain meaningful; management and capital side want enough flexibility to execute a decisive turnaround.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve major savings without closures or forced layoffs**
- **How much job security can be offered in exchange for workforce concessions**
- **What level of employee contribution is acceptable and for how long**
- **What binding investment and product commitments can be credibly made in return**
- **How to balance short-term competitiveness with long-term industrial presence**
- **How to reduce overcapacity while preserving labour peace and governance legitimacy**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **quantified proposals**:
  - required savings
  - number and timing of job reductions
  - cost of voluntary instruments
  - investment amounts and timing
  - site-specific implications
- Ask each side to specify:
  - **non-negotiables**
  - **possible concessions**
  - **minimum acceptable package**
- Focus next round on whether there is a **credible no-closure alternative** that still closes the competitiveness gap, or whether the gap can only be closed with measures the labour and state sides currently reject.
