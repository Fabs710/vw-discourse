From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the issue is not ideological. It is whether this restructuring produces a **credible, durable improvement in risk-adjusted value** or simply locks in a weaker cost base while conflict drags on.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, and to some extent with the controlling family, on the core diagnosis: **the current German cost base and capacity are not competitive enough**. If margins are already below target and the company is facing a tougher European market and intense low-cost competition, then inaction is not a neutral option — it is value destruction.

I also agree with the state government and employee representatives on one point: **a prolonged, disorderly conflict would be bad for everyone, including shareholders**. Strike risk, customer uncertainty, execution delay, and management distraction all hit the share price and the cost of capital.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept an outcome that preserves all existing structures while claiming competitiveness has been restored. If the proposal is merely:
- symbolic cost cutting,
- temporary concessions,
- or a cosmetic agreement that avoids hard decisions,

then we would see that as insufficient. That would leave the cost base structurally impaired and margins vulnerable.

At the same time, I am also not prepared to endorse **value-destructive brinkmanship**. Plant closures and compulsory redundancies may be economically justified in principle if they are necessary to restore competitiveness, but they are only acceptable if they are part of a **credible, executable package** that actually delivers the savings and does not trigger an open-ended industrial conflict.

### What matters to us
We need to see four things:

1. **Verified, durable cost reduction**  
   Not promises, but quantified savings that close the margin gap.

2. **Clear capacity adjustment**  
   If there is overcapacity, it must be removed in a way that is operationally real.

3. **Execution certainty**  
   A deal that is likely to hold is more valuable than a theoretically cleaner but politically impossible plan.

4. **Protection of medium-term investment capacity**  
   Cost cuts matter because they fund future product cycles and technology. If the package undermines that, it fails the test.

### On the positions of the other stakeholders
- **Works council / union:** Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable politically, but as a shareholder I cannot support a line that would prevent meaningful restructuring if the economics require it. If you want no closures, then you need to bring an alternative with equivalent savings and enforceability. Otherwise you are asking investors to accept structurally lower returns.

- **State government:** Your concern for regional employment is legitimate, but shareholder value cannot be subordinated indefinitely to local politics. Protecting every site regardless of economics would be a recipe for underperformance and eventually a worse outcome for the region as well.

- **Controlling family:** You are right that the company cannot drift. But “support restructuring” has to mean more than endorsing tough rhetoric. Shareholders will judge you on whether the final package actually restores margins, not on whether it sounds decisive.

- **Management:** You have the burden of proof. If you are asking for support for closures, layoffs, or labor-cost changes, you need to show that the package is sufficient, durable, and executable. If not, the market will discount it as conflict without resolution.

### Our bottom line
We are **not** aligned with any outcome that leaves the company with:
- structurally uncompetitive costs,
- continued overcapacity,
- or a settlement that postpones the problem.

We are open to either:
- a deep restructuring with difficult capacity and employment measures, **or**
- a negotiated alternative that achieves **comparable, durable savings** with enough certainty to restore margins.

What we will not support is a half-measure that buys short-term peace at the expense of long-term returns.

If management believes closures or workforce reductions are necessary, then present a package that is financially compelling and executable. If the unions and works council want to avoid that, then they need to produce a credible alternative that truly closes the gap — not just a softer version of the same problem.
