From the perspective of the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **a restructuring that destroys regional jobs and closes German plants is not acceptable as a starting point**.

I do not dispute that the company faces real competitive pressure. I also do not deny that the cost base must improve. On that point, I agree with management that doing nothing is not an option. But I strongly disagree with the idea that the only credible route is plant closure and compulsory redundancies.

### Where I agree
- The company needs a **durable industrial solution**, not cosmetic savings.
- We need to **restore competitiveness** and preserve investment capacity for future products.
- A **negotiated settlement** is preferable to escalation and open conflict.
- The burden must be **shared**, not carried by one side alone.

### Where I disagree
- I do **not** accept closure of German plants as the default answer.
- I do **not** accept mass forced layoffs.
- I do **not** accept that short-term cost targets can override the long-term role of this company as a regional economic anchor.

This company is not just an accounting problem. It is a central industrial structure in the region, with thousands of direct and indirect jobs. A decision that tears out plant capacity would have consequences far beyond the balance sheet: for suppliers, local employment, training pipelines, municipal finances, and social stability. Those costs do not disappear — they are simply shifted onto the region and the public.

### What I would support instead
I am prepared to support a restructuring if it is built around:
- **site protection in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **internal capacity adjustment instead of closures**
- **working-time and flexibility measures**
- **voluntary and socially responsible workforce reductions if absolutely necessary**
- **qualification and redeployment**
- **clear management and owner contributions**
- **a credible investment plan tied to future products and technologies**

If management can demonstrate that savings of comparable scale are achievable without closing plants, then that is the route we should pursue. If there are structural inefficiencies, address them — but do it in a way that preserves the company’s long-term role in the region and respects the social partnership that has made this company resilient.

### A clear warning
If the restructuring package is framed as “closures first, negotiations later,” then I will not be a bystander. We have a legitimate blocking position, and we will use it to prevent any outcome that guts regional employment or sidelines co-determination. The state will not endorse a strategy that treats German sites as disposable.

So my message is simple:  
**Bring forward a credible alternative that reduces cost, preserves sites, protects jobs, and secures the company’s future.**  
That is the only basis on which I can engage constructively.
