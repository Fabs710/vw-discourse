## Core impasse
The parties are not disagreeing about whether restructuring is needed. They are disagreeing about **the instrument and the threshold**:

- **Employee side**: no closures, no forced redundancies, binding job/site guarantees.
- **Management / investors / controlling family**: willingness to consider internal measures, but only if they are large enough to remove the structural cost burden; closures remain on the table if they are not.
- **State government**: wants a serious alternative tested first, and does not want closures treated as the starting assumption.

So the deadlock is between:
1. **protecting employment and sites upfront**, and  
2. **preserving enough restructuring freedom to guarantee durability and competitiveness**.

The specialist has narrowed the issue further: a no-closure package appears capable of closing **a meaningful share** of the gap, but possibly not all of it. That means the unresolved point is not “whether to act,” but **what residual gap is still acceptable without structural reductions**.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### 1) Sequenced “No-closure first, conditional review” package
A first phase would adopt a serious no-closure package with:
- voluntary exits at scale
- working-time flexibility
- productivity measures
- management/owner contributions
- redeployment and qualification
- investment commitments for the German footprint

Then, after a fixed review point, the parties would assess:
- actual savings achieved
- residual gap
- utilization and demand development

Only if the agreed targets are missed would deeper structural measures be revisited.

**Purpose:** bridges the gap between “no closures now” and “management cannot be forced to rule out all structural options forever.”

---

### 2) “No closures during the agreement” with a hard fallback mechanism
The parties could agree:
- no plant closures during the agreement term
- no compulsory redundancies during the same term
- but with a jointly defined fallback clause if pre-set financial or volume triggers are not met

That fallback would not predetermine closures, but it would preserve credibility for the capital side.

**Purpose:** gives the employee side protection in the near term while giving management/investors an enforceable path if the internal package fails.

---

### 3) Differentiated site protection with selective flexibility
Instead of a blanket promise for all sites, the parties could discuss:
- full protection for strategically critical sites
- stronger adjustment measures at weaker or more underutilized sites
- a commitment to avoid closures unless a site-specific review shows no viable alternative

This would allow the parties to preserve most of the workforce guarantee while acknowledging that not every location may require identical treatment.

**Purpose:** may bridge the conflict between total site protection and the demand for economically targeted restructuring.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
What exact residual recurring gap remains **after** the strongest feasible no-closure package, and what specific threshold would make you conclude that closures are still necessary?

### To the Works Council
What level of quantified savings and what binding protections would make a no-closure package acceptable as a serious restructuring rather than a temporary delay?

### To the Industrial Union
Which mix of voluntary exits, working-time changes, and productivity measures would you treat as sufficient to justify a no-closure settlement, and what duration would you require?

### To the Controlling Family
Would you support a sequenced deal that excludes closures for a defined period, provided that it contains clear trigger points and a credible fallback if targets are missed?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you accept a no-closure package if the residual gap is reduced materially and the agreement contains measurable triggers, or do you require capacity reduction regardless of the alternative package’s performance?

### To the Regional State Government
What concrete support can you offer for a no-closure solution—financial, political, or regulatory—if the package is quantified and time-bound but still leaves some residual risk?
