As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of this transformation. If management now tries to solve strategic and financial problems by destroying jobs, breaking long-standing protections, and handing the workforce a fait accompli, then it will meet decisive resistance — in the plants, at the bargaining table, and on the supervisory board.

What we will argue for instead is:

- binding guarantees that all German plants remain open,
- no forced layoffs,
- a secure framework for jobs and sites through 2030,
- real co-determination in shaping any restructuring,
- and a credible industrial plan that protects the future of the locations, not just short-term accounting targets.

We are not denying that the company faces pressure. But the answer cannot be to make workers pay for management mistakes, failed planning, or underinvestment through closures and mass job cuts. If savings are needed, then management must present alternatives that avoid breaking the social contract: working-time measures, internal redeployment, qualification, voluntary solutions, and a fair burden-sharing arrangement — but always under the condition that sites and jobs are secured.

Let me also be plain: if this is being presented as a closed package with plant closures and layoffs already decided, then there is no basis for agreement. We are prepared to fight that course with all the means available to us.
