I will be equally direct.

The specialist has confirmed two things at once:
1. the company likely needs **substantial structural savings**, and  
2. a **no-closure package can still deliver material savings** — often enough to be a serious first step, not a symbolic one.

That matters. Because it means the employee side is right that closures are **not automatically necessary**, and management is wrong if it presents plant shutdowns as the default answer before exhausting the alternative package in a serious, quantified way.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and investors that:
- the cost gap is real,
- doing nothing is not an option,
- and any deal must be durable, not cosmetic.

I also agree with the works council and union that:
- the workforce cannot be the only party paying,
- and a settlement based on open-ended conflict would damage the company.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You have not yet shown that closures are unavoidable. The specialist’s assessment says a no-closure package can plausibly deliver **€400m–€900m annually** if it is serious. That is not trivial.

So I reject any attempt to make plant closures the starting premise. If management wants support for deeper cuts, it must first show:
- the exact residual gap after a strong internal package,
- why that gap cannot be closed through voluntary exits, working-time changes, productivity, and overhead reduction,
- and why any closure would not create disproportionate political and operational damage.

I will not support a process that jumps straight to dismantling jobs and sites.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right to insist on hard numbers and durability. But the specialist also shows that closures carry very high one-off costs, long lead times, and major execution risk. That means “harder” is not automatically “better.”

A package that produces social conflict, ramp-down disruption, and political blowback may destroy the value you are trying to protect. So I will not accept a stance that treats closure as the clean, disciplined solution by default.

### To the works council and union
I agree with your rejection of closures and forced redundancies as the first answer. But I will be equally frank with you: saying “no closures” is not enough by itself.

The specialist is clear that a no-closure package only works if it is:
- broad,
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- and large enough to remove a meaningful part of the cost burden.

That means you will need to engage seriously on:
- voluntary exits at scale,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification and redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- and a time-limited employee contribution.

What I will not accept is a package that simply preserves every legacy arrangement and calls that a restructuring.

## My position
As regional state government and anchor shareholder, my line remains unchanged:

- **No plant closures in Germany as the starting point**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **A serious alternative package must be tested first**
- **Binding protection for the regional footprint where possible**
- **Shared burden from management, owners, and workforce**
- **Investment commitments for future products and transformation**

That is not a refusal to restructure. It is a refusal to let restructuring become a euphemism for gutting the state’s industrial base.

## What the expert means for the next step
The specialist has effectively set the task:
1. build the strongest possible **no-closure package**,
2. quantify the residual gap,
3. only then discuss whether any further structural measures are truly necessary.

That is the correct sequence. Not closures first, alternatives later.

## What I want on the table now
I want both scenarios presented side by side:
- a **no-closure package** with hard numbers on annual savings, one-off costs, headcount effects, and timing,
- and any **structural adjustment scenario** with the same metrics.

Then we can judge whether the alleged need for closures is real or exaggerated.

But I will say this plainly:  
if a no-closure package can close the gap materially, then closures are **not justified as a first resort**. They become a last resort only if the residual gap remains large and proven after serious negotiation.

Until then, I will defend the state’s co-determination role, the sites, and the jobs.
