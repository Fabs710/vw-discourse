Facilitator assessment: the discourse has plateaued because the parties are no longer disputing the existence of a restructuring problem; they are disputing **sequencing, proof threshold, and non-negotiable safeguards**.

## Core impasse
The main block is this:

1. **Employee side / state government** want a **no-closure-first** settlement, with no compulsory redundancies and strong site protection.
2. **Management / controlling family / investors** accept a serious no-closure package, but only if they retain **structural fallback options** if the residual gap remains material.
3. The **expert** has clarified that a no-closure package is economically real, but may still leave a **residual gap** large enough to justify deeper measures.
4. The discussion is therefore stuck on **what must be proven, by whom, and at what stage** before structural measures can be discussed.

So the deadlock is not about whether savings are needed. It is about **whether closure-related options must be excluded up front, or only after a quantified test of the strongest internal package**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise
Below are three concrete bridging positions that could be tested.

### Proposal 1: “No-closure first, with objective review gate”
A settlement built in two phases:
- **Phase 1:** implement the strongest feasible no-closure package for a defined period.
- **Phase 2:** only if a jointly verified residual gap remains above a pre-agreed threshold, trigger a second review of structural options.

Possible elements:
- no closures during Phase 1
- no compulsory redundancies during Phase 1
- joint verification by an independent expert
- a pre-agreed residual-gap threshold that must be met before any structural option is reopened

This may bridge:
- employee/state demand for no-closure sequencing
- management/investor demand for not precluding future structural tools

---

### Proposal 2: “Hard job protection now, conditional structural review later”
A package that combines:
- a binding **moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies** for the agreement period
- strong voluntary exit and redeployment measures
- a **conditional review clause** at a fixed date or savings milestone
- if the target is missed, parties reopen talks on additional measures

This may bridge:
- workforce certainty in the near term
- management’s insistence on not locking in permanent inflexibility

Key point: the review trigger must be **objective and pre-defined**, not discretionary.

---

### Proposal 3: “Shared burden package with explicit side conditions”
A no-closure package is accepted if all sides contribute in parallel:
- workforce: voluntary exits, flexibility, qualification, temporary concessions
- management: overhead cuts, compensation restraint, governance commitments
- owners/investors: capital or dividend restraint
- state: political support and investment mediation

In exchange:
- no plant closures during the package term
- no compulsory redundancies
- a jointly agreed sustainability check after implementation

This may bridge:
- the works council and union’s insistence on shared sacrifice
- the capital side’s insistence that the deal be economically credible

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
What exact residual-gap threshold, if any, would you accept before structural options could be reconsidered, and what objective verification mechanism would you accept to establish that gap?

### To the Union
Would you accept a time-limited no-closure moratorium if it were paired with a jointly verified review clause and a binding management/owner contribution?

### To Group & Brand Management
What is the smallest no-closure package you would consider sufficient to suspend closure discussions for a defined period, and what measurable trigger would you accept before reopening them?

### To the Regional State Government
Are you prepared to underwrite a no-closure-first sequence politically and financially, including by supporting an independent verification process and a review threshold?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a temporary no-closure commitment if it were paired with a hard performance review and preserved governance flexibility after the review point?

### To Institutional Investors
What combination of recurring savings, timing, and residual-gap reduction would make a no-closure package acceptable to you without immediate structural measures?

### To the Expert
What residual-gap threshold would, in your professional view, make a no-closure settlement economically sustainable for at least the next agreement cycle?
