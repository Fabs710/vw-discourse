The latest specialist assessment is helpful, but it does **not** change the core conclusion for me: this company needs **durable structural savings**, not a symbolic peace, and not a settlement that preserves every legacy claim at the expense of competitiveness.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the company has a real structural cost problem and that doing nothing would be value-destructive. You are also right that a weak compromise would simply postpone the pain and leave the business exposed again.

### With the expert
The specialist has confirmed the key economic point: a no-closure package can generate **material savings**, but likely not always enough if overcapacity is truly substantial. That is exactly why I am not willing to rule out structural measures in advance.

### With investors
You are right that the final package must be **quantified, durable, and enforceable**. A cosmetic deal that buys temporary calm while leaving the cost base uncompetitive would destroy value.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
Your position is understandable politically, but it is not sufficient as a governing answer.

- “No plant closures”
- “No compulsory redundancies”
- “Binding guarantees through 2030”
- “All German sites remain open”

That is not a restructuring framework. It is a demand that management and owners absorb the full adjustment risk while keeping all strategic flexibility off the table.

The expert did **not** prove that closures are necessary. But the expert also did **not** prove that your preferred package can fully close the gap. What was shown is that a no-closure package may close a **meaningful part** of the problem. That is not the same as solving it.

So I reject the idea that plant closures can be categorically excluded before the residual gap is known.

### With the regional state government
You are right to insist that the company is a regional anchor and not a spreadsheet. But “no closures as a starting point” cannot become a political veto on structural adjustment.

If the business remains uncompetitive, the region suffers more over time: lower investment, weaker margins, and eventual job erosion through decline rather than restructuring. So I will not support a stance that effectively freezes the industrial footprint regardless of economics.

## What the specialist actually means for governance
The expert’s numbers support a **sequenced approach**, but not an ideological one.

A no-closure package may plausibly deliver:
- around **€400m–€900m annually**
- with lower execution risk and lower social damage
- but it may still leave a residual gap if overcapacity is deep

A closure/capacity-reduction package may deliver:
- around **€700m–€1.6bn annually**
- with stronger long-run economics
- but much higher one-off cost and execution risk

So the real question is not whether closures are “nice” or “harsh.” It is whether the no-closure package, once fully quantified, actually closes the remaining gap **after** realistic productivity, voluntary exit, and flexibility measures.

## My position as controlling shareholder

I will support a restructuring only if it does all of the following:
1. restores margins and competitiveness on a durable basis,
2. reduces capacity and cost materially enough,
3. avoids prolonged conflict that destroys operational value,
4. preserves the company’s ability to invest in future products,
5. protects the strategically important footprint where possible.

That means I am open to a serious no-closure package — but only if it is **large enough** and **durable enough**. If it is not, then structural measures remain on the table.

## What I do not accept
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket no-closure promise,
- a blanket no-redundancy promise,
- a settlement that relies on temporary concessions with no structural reset,
- or a deal that preserves all sites while leaving the company unable to compete.

## What I expect next
Before anyone asks me to rule out structural measures, I want the alternative package shown in hard numbers:
- annual savings
- one-off costs
- headcount effects
- capacity effects
- timing
- durability beyond the first agreement cycle

If the no-closure package really closes the gap, fine — then we can assess it seriously. But if it only softens the problem, then it is not enough to protect the long-term value of this company.

### Bottom line
I support **competitive restructuring**, not symbolic compromise.  
I support **durable value creation**, not frozen guarantees.  
And I will not accept a process in which plant closures and meaningful workforce reduction are ruled out before the economics have been fully tested.

If useful, the next step should be a side-by-side board-level comparison of:
- the strongest possible **no-closure package**, and
- a **structural adjustment package**,

with the same metrics and the same discipline.
