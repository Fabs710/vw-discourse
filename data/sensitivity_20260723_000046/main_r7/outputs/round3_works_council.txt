## What I agree with
I agree on one point: the company has a genuine structural problem and a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict. I also agree that any serious package must be quantified, enforceable, and durable.

But agreement ends there if management and capital side still insist on keeping plant closures and forced redundancies as the default threat.

## Where I disagree — sharply

### To management
You keep saying that alternatives may help, but not enough. That is still not a case for closures. It is an argument for **doing the hard work of building a real alternative**.

You have not proven that:
- every German plant must remain “expendable,”
- compulsory redundancies are unavoidable,
- or that the only way to lower costs is to destroy sites and break guarantees.

What you have shown is that you prefer the fastest transfer of risk onto the workforce.

That is not acceptable.

If you want credibility, then put forward a package that is:
- fully quantified,
- based on internal adjustment,
- built on voluntary measures,
- and designed to preserve all German sites and jobs.

Anything else is not a restructuring proposal. It is a pressure campaign.

### To the controlling family
You speak about long-term value, but you are treating the workforce as the variable to be cut first and hardest. That is not stewardship. It is short-termism dressed up as discipline.

Long-term value in an industrial company depends on:
- skilled workers,
- stable production,
- trust,
- and co-determination that is actually respected.

If you blow up that trust now, you will not get it back with a spreadsheet. You will get warning strikes, falling morale, output disruption, and long-term damage to execution.

### To investors
You keep repeating that “competitiveness first” is the only serious logic. No. A company that destroys its own industrial base to satisfy a cost narrative is not serious. It is reckless.

You ask for durable savings. Fine. Then support a package that gets them **without** closures and **without** forced redundancies. If you cannot accept that, then you are admitting that your priority is not durability but coercive restructuring.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate that you are not endorsing closures as a starting point. But I need to be blunt: “please examine alternatives” is not enough if management keeps closures on the table as a veiled ultimatum.

If the state wants to act as a responsible anchor shareholder, then it must do more than express concern. It must support:
- a binding no-closure commitment,
- no forced redundancies,
- investment in all German sites,
- and protection of the co-determination framework.

A vague promise of “regional sensitivity” is not protection.

## On the economist’s assessment
The specialist said alternatives can reduce the gap materially, and that is exactly why management’s argument is still incomplete.

The expert did **not** prove that closures are necessary. The expert said:
- alternatives can deliver meaningful savings,
- voluntary exits, working-time changes, productivity measures, and contributions can materially reduce costs,
- and only if the gap is truly large and persistent do closures become the preferred capital-side instrument.

That means the burden remains on management to prove necessity — not on workers to accept destruction in advance.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**

- forced layoffs in the name of “balance”
- making workers pay for strategic errors they did not create
- weakening guarantees so management can later reintroduce cuts at will
- a settlement that preserves shareholder optionality at the cost of employee certainty

If management claims a no-closure package cannot work, then it must show:
- the exact savings gap,
- the exact measures already exhausted,
- the exact reason voluntary and internal measures fail,
- and why the company supposedly cannot accept a genuine co-determined restructuring.

Until that is shown, closures and forced redundancies remain a choice, not a necessity.
