### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All stakeholders agree the company faces a **serious competitiveness and cost-base problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **negotiated solution is preferable** to unilateral action or open conflict.
- There is common ground on the need for:
  - a **credible industrial/future product plan**,
  - **durable cost reduction**,
  - **investment capacity for the future**,
  - and, to varying degrees, **avoiding unnecessary social damage**.
- Several parties accept that **alternatives to immediate closures and layoffs should be examined**.

#### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and state government reject them as a starting point; management, controlling family, and investors keep them on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies / workforce reductions:**  
  Employee-side actors reject compulsory layoffs; management and capital-side actors say workforce reduction may be necessary.
- **Scope of site and job guarantees:**  
  Employee-side and state-side actors want binding protection; management and capital-side actors say guarantees are only meaningful if the business remains competitively viable.
- **How to define “credible restructuring”:**  
  One side emphasizes social protection and internal adjustment; the other emphasizes durable capacity reduction and verified savings, even if that requires closures or job cuts.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Social protection vs. structural adjustment:**  
  No shared formula yet for preserving jobs and sites while still closing the competitiveness gap.
- **Short-term labor peace vs. long-term viability:**  
  Stakeholders differ on whether temporary concessions are enough or whether deeper changes are unavoidable.
- **Who bears the burden:**  
  The discussion has not resolved the balance between employee contributions, management responsibility, owner contribution, and possible state sensitivity.
- **Evidence threshold for alternatives:**  
  Employee-side actors seek alternatives to closures; capital-side actors demand quantified, durable, executable savings. The standard for proving equivalence is still undefined.
- **Regional impact vs. enterprise economics:**  
  The state highlights wider regional consequences, while capital-side actors stress that avoiding difficult changes may worsen the eventual outcome.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **verifiable options**: ask each side to specify what savings, capacity changes, and protections it can actually support.
- Identify a **small set of package elements** that could be combined into a compromise, such as:
  - internal redeployment,
  - voluntary exits,
  - working-time measures,
  - qualification,
  - targeted site protection,
  - management/owner contributions,
  - and clearly defined structural savings.
- Clarify the **minimum conditions** under which each stakeholder would consider a deal possible.
- Keep the next round focused on **numbers, enforceability, and sequencing** rather than general statements of principle.
