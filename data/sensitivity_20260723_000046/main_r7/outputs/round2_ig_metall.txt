I will be equally direct.

I agree with the works council on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. I also agree that the workforce cannot be asked to absorb the entire burden of the crisis while management, owners, and capital market stakeholders insist on “competitiveness” as a one-way street. That is not a settlement; that is a transfer of pain.

At the same time, I want to be precise about what a responsible trade-union position is here. We are **not** defending the status quo at any price. We are prepared to negotiate a real contribution from employees — but only in a package that is fair, time-limited, and matched by equivalent contributions from management and owners.

### Where I agree
- The company has a real structural problem and needs a durable solution.
- A negotiated path is better than escalation.
- Productivity, flexibility, qualification, redeployment, and voluntary measures all have a place.
- Any solution must preserve the company’s long-term industrial base and future investment capacity.

### Where I disagree
#### To management
You keep saying closures and workforce reductions may be necessary, but you have not shown that they are the only way to achieve the needed savings. Saying “the economics require it” is not enough when the burden falls almost entirely on employees.

If you want our cooperation, then show us a package that is:
- quantified,
- verifiable,
- reversible where possible,
- and socially balanced.

That means:
- no forced layoffs,
- no closure of German sites,
- no dismantling of the collective wage framework,
- and no permanent erosion of standards under the label of restructuring.

#### To the controlling family and investors
You both sound as if the only acceptable restructuring is one that proves toughness to the market. That is exactly the wrong test. A “hard” package that triggers prolonged strikes, output losses, and a destruction of trust is not value-creating. It is reckless.

If you want durable value, then you need:
- labour peace,
- retained skills,
- operational continuity,
- and a workforce that sees the deal as fair.

#### To the state government
I appreciate the political and regional responsibility. But concern alone will not secure jobs. We need enforceable commitments, not general expressions of support. If the state wants to preserve this industrial base, it should back a binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy settlement, not remain vague about “internal adjustment.”

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are ready to negotiate a package that includes:
- temporary working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and reskilling,
- voluntary departure programmes,
- targeted capacity reduction through non-coercive means,
- and a fair employee contribution.

But that employee contribution must be matched by:
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and real investment commitments for future products and locations.

### What would be unacceptable
Let me state the limit plainly:
- **forced redundancies are unacceptable**
- **plant closures are unacceptable**
- **a settlement that shifts the crisis onto employees alone is unacceptable**
- **any weakening of the collective bargaining framework as a price of peace is unacceptable**

### On the next step
If management is serious, then bring forward a **full alternative package** that demonstrates how the required savings can be achieved **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**. Not slogans — numbers, sequencing, and enforceability.

If such a package is impossible, then management should say so honestly and explain why. But we will not accept a scenario where employees are told to sacrifice jobs and protections simply because that is the easiest route for capital.

So my position is clear:
- We are open to shared sacrifice.
- We are not open to one-sided sacrifice.
- We will defend jobs, sites, and collective standards.
- If necessary, we will escalate industrial action to make that point unmistakable.
