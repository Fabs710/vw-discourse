## Where I agree
### With management
I agree that the business problem is real and that doing nothing is not an option. If German operations remain structurally above competitive cost levels, then shareholder value keeps eroding. That is unacceptable.

I also agree that a weak compromise would be value-destructive. A deal that preserves the current cost base and simply postpones the adjustment will be punished by the market.

### With the controlling family
I agree that the final package must restore long-term value and not just create the appearance of discipline. You are right that this cannot be theater.

### With the expert
I agree with the economic framing: a no-closure package can be meaningful, but if overcapacity is substantial it may only be a partial solution. That is important.

## Where I disagree
### With the works council and union
Your position remains too rigid for me.

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**
- **All German sites open**

That is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand that the company preserve every legacy claim while expecting the capital providers to accept continued underperformance.

The specialist has not shown that your preferred package is sufficient. It may reduce a large share of the gap, but that is not the same as closing it. If you want investors to back a no-closure solution, then it must be **quantified, enforceable, and large enough to restore competitiveness** — not merely morally preferable.

### With the regional state government
I respect the political constraints, but I do not accept site protection as the lead criterion. If the company remains uncompetitive, the region loses anyway through weaker investment, lower margins, and eventually worse employment outcomes. Protecting every site regardless of economics is not responsible stewardship.

## What I need to see
I am not ruling out a no-closure package. But I need the following compared side by side:

1. **Strong no-closure alternative**
   - annual savings
   - one-off costs
   - headcount impact
   - durability
   - residual gap

2. **Structural adjustment package**
   - annual savings
   - one-off costs
   - headcount impact
   - capacity reduction
   - timing and execution risk

If the no-closure option can genuinely close the gap and do so durably, I would support it. But if it only softens the problem while leaving margins structurally impaired, then it is not enough.

## My position
I will back **whichever package credibly restores competitive margins and disciplined cost reduction**. That means:
- durable savings,
- real capacity adjustment where needed,
- no cosmetic guarantees,
- and no settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

What I will not support is:
- a symbolic compromise,
- a time-limited bridge with no structural reset,
- or a deal that asks capital to finance indefinite underperformance.
