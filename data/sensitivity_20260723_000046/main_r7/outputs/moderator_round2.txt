### 1) What has been agreed
- The company has a **real competitiveness and structural cost problem**.
- **Doing nothing is not an option**.
- A **negotiated solution is preferable to open escalation**.
- There is broad recognition that **productivity, redeployment, qualification, voluntary exits, and some form of burden sharing** should be part of any package.
- All sides accept that the final outcome must be **durable** and support the company’s long-term future.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and regional state government reject them as a starting point.
  - Management, controlling family, and investors insist they cannot be ruled out if needed for structural savings.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee-side and state actors reject them.
  - Capital-side actors do not rule out workforce reductions if required for competitiveness.

- **Binding guarantees**
  - Employee-side asks for binding site and job security through 2030.
  - Capital-side views absolute guarantees as incompatible with the need for restructuring flexibility.

- **How much savings can be achieved without closures**
  - Employee-side believes a no-closure package has not yet been disproven.
  - Capital-side argues the expert view suggests alternatives may help but likely cannot fully replace structural capacity cuts if overcapacity is substantial.

- **Who should bear the burden**
  - Employee and state actors insist the workforce cannot pay alone.
  - Capital-side insists any solution must still deliver materially lower costs and capacity.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Short-term social stability vs. long-term structural competitiveness**
- **Job and site preservation vs. permanent removal of excess capacity**
- **Voluntary, slower savings vs. faster but more disruptive restructuring**
- **Temporary concessions vs. durable cost reduction**
- **Regional/political protection vs. unrestricted industrial adjustment**
- **A potentially more acceptable compromise vs. a package that may be economically insufficient**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from positions to **numbers and scenarios**.
- Ask both sides to compare:
  1. a **no-closure / no-forced-redundancy package**, and  
  2. a **structural adjustment package**.
- Require each scenario to show:
  - annual savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - headcount impact,
  - capacity effects,
  - implementation timing,
  - durability of savings.
- The next round should focus on **which package actually closes the gap while remaining socially and operationally feasible**.
