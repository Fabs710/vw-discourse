## Executive view

For a large German automotive operation with structural overcapacity, a **strong no-closure package** can usually deliver **material recurring savings**, but it typically **does not fully eliminate** the cost gap if fixed-cost absorption and site duplication are the core issue.

A **closure / capacity-reduction package** is more expensive and slower to execute, but it can deliver **larger durable savings** because it attacks the fixed-cost base and utilization problem directly.

---

## 1) Side-by-side economics

### A. Strong no-closure / no-forced-redundancy package
Typical levers:
- voluntary exits / early retirement
- partial retirement
- internal redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- temporary wage or bonus moderation
- management overhead cuts
- overtime / agency reduction
- productivity gains
- qualification and multi-skilling

#### Realistic recurring savings
- **€500m to €900m per year**

#### Headcount impact
- **2,500 to 6,000 FTE**

#### One-off costs
- Severance / exit incentives: **€250m to €700m**
- Qualification / redeployment / transition: **€50m to €150m**
- Temporary backfill / ramp management: **€50m to €150m**
- Management system / overhead change costs: **€25m to €100m**

**Total one-off cost:** roughly **€400m to €1.2bn**

#### Timing
- Some savings start in **0–6 months**:
  - overtime cuts
  - hiring freeze
  - agency reduction
  - management overhead
- Material labor-cost savings arrive over **6–24 months**
- Full run-rate usually takes **18–36 months**

#### Durability
- Good on labor and overhead
- Weaker on fixed plant-cost structure
- Savings are durable only if redeployment and productivity gains stick

---

### B. Closure / capacity-reduction package
Typical levers:
- one or more plant closures or partial closures
- model-line consolidation
- elimination of excess shifts / capacity
- deeper headcount reduction
- relocation of selected production
- permanent overhead rationalization

#### Realistic recurring savings
- **€700m to €1.6bn per year**

#### Headcount impact
- **4,000 to 10,000+ FTE**

#### One-off costs
- Severance / social plan costs: **€600m to €1.8bn**
- Write-downs on plant, machinery, tooling: **€300m to €1.0bn**
- Decommissioning / environmental remediation: **€100m to €300m**
- Relocation / transfer / launch duplication: **€100m to €400m**
- Ramp-down and transition production losses: **€100m to €500m**

**Total one-off cost:** roughly **€1.5bn to €4.0bn+**

#### Timing
- Planning and consultation: **6–18 months**
- Ramp-down / transfer: **12–36 months**
- Full savings realization: often **24–48 months**

#### Durability
- Stronger than no-closure because it removes fixed costs and structural overcapacity
- But execution risk is materially higher

---

## 2) Residual gap after a strong no-closure package

If the German footprint really has structural overcapacity, a strong no-closure package often still leaves a residual gap of about:

- **€200m to €700m per year**

That residual is usually driven by:
- underutilized plant overhead
- duplicated logistics and support functions
- fixed maintenance and utilities
- weak capacity absorption
- residual labor-cost competitiveness gap

This is the key point economically:  
a no-closure package can improve the income statement substantially, but it may not fully fix the **fixed-cost dilution problem**.

---

## 3) When no-closure is enough, and when it is not

### No-closure may be enough if:
- residual gap is only **€100m–€200m/year**
- demand is recovering
- utilization can improve materially
- product mix improves
- management is willing to live with a lower near-term margin in exchange for labor peace

### No-closure is probably not enough if:
- residual gap remains **€300m–€700m/year**
- plant utilization stays structurally weak
- cost disadvantage persists versus peers
- future product investment needs higher margins
- the company needs a permanent reset, not just temporary relief

---

## 4) Cash vs accounting timing

### No-closure package
- **Cash impact:** mostly severance and exit payments up front
- **Accounting impact:** savings can appear quickly through reduced payroll and overhead
- Cash savings are often more gradual than the P&L effect because exit costs and transition payments offset the early years

### Closure package
- **Cash impact:** large up-front outflows for severance, write-downs, decommissioning, relocation
- **Accounting impact:** asset impairments may hit immediately, but P&L savings only phase in as sites ramp down
- Often the **first 1–2 years look worse** before the structural savings emerge

---

## 5) Major execution risks

### No-closure package risks
- voluntary exit uptake lower than planned
- redeployment capacity insufficient
- working-time concessions smaller or temporary
- labor peace breaks down if burden sharing is seen as one-sided
- productivity gains fail to stick

### Closure package risks
- industrial action and launch disruption
- production transfer losses
- delayed ramp-down, so savings arrive later than planned
- tooling relocation and quality issues
- legal/works-council complexity around implementation
- higher reputational and morale damage, which can hurt execution elsewhere

---

## 6) Economic conclusion

### Strong no-closure package
A credible first-stage solution.  
It can deliver **large savings** and buy time, but it is **not automatically a full structural fix** if overcapacity is real.

### Closure / capacity-reduction package
More painful and slower, but it is the **stronger instrument** for permanently removing fixed cost and excess capacity.

### Practical judgment
If the company’s true residual gap after maximal internal measures is still in the **hundreds of millions annually**, then **some capacity reduction is economically justified** on a pure restructuring basis.

If the residual gap is only modest, a no-closure settlement may be the better economic trade-off once execution risk, industrial peace, and timing are fully priced in.
