I will answer very clearly.

We agree on one point: the company faces a real competitiveness problem and cannot simply continue as before. But that is where agreement ends if management, the owners, or investors believe the solution is to sacrifice German plants and break the job-security framework.

### Our position remains unchanged
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or the job-security framework**
- **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**

That is not a negotiating “preference.” It is the minimum basis for any serious discussion.

### To management
You say the cost base is too high and capacity must be reduced. I hear that. But you have not demonstrated that closures and forced layoffs are the only way to achieve savings. You are presenting the workforce with a pressure tactic, not a balanced restructuring plan.

If you want credibility, then put forward a package that is:
- fully quantified,
- enforceable,
- socially responsible,
- and demonstrably sufficient **without** closures or compulsory redundancies.

That means looking at:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- voluntary programmes,
- targeted productivity improvements,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

What you cannot do is demand that workers pay for the strategic mistakes of others by accepting the destruction of sites and the loss of guaranteed jobs.

### To the controlling family and investors
You both keep saying that “if the economics require it,” closures must remain possible. That may sound disciplined from a capital perspective, but it ignores what this company is: a major industrial employer with a social and regional responsibility. A strategy that breaks the trust of the workforce and tears up the historic social contract is not “value creation.” It is escalation.

You want durable value? Then durable value requires:
- labour peace,
- retained skills,
- stable industrial relations,
- and production locations that remain viable because they are modernized, not abandoned.

A solution that provokes conflict, warning strikes, and long-term hostility is not a credible value-maximizing plan. It damages output, reputation, delivery reliability, and the future ability to implement change.

### To the regional state government
We are aligned on the central point: German plants must not be treated as disposable. But I would add this: a vague call for “internal adjustment” is not enough unless it is backed by binding commitments. The workforce cannot be asked to accept sacrifices on faith while management keeps closures in reserve.

So if the state wants to play a constructive role, it should support:
- a binding no-closure commitment,
- no forced redundancies,
- investment into German sites,
- and a genuine transformation agreement with clear safeguards.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not refusing change. We are refusing unilateral destruction.

If management wants a real solution, then the discussion must start from these conditions:
1. **All German plants remain open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Security framework extended to 2030**
4. **Meaningful co-determination in restructuring design**
5. **Shared burden, not one-sided labour cuts**
6. **Investment and product commitments for the German footprint**

### What we reject outright
- closure by stealth or by “economic necessity” as a pretext
- mass layoffs dressed up as restructuring
- guarantees that disappear the moment pressure rises
- asking the workforce to finance the entire correction alone

And let me be equally blunt: if the proposal still depends on plant closures or forced layoffs, then it is not a compromise. It is a confrontation. We are prepared for that confrontation, and we will use every legitimate instrument at our disposal to defend the workforce and the sites.

If anyone wants to move this forward, then bring concrete numbers for a restructuring **without** closures and **without** compulsory redundancies. Until then, the burden is on management and the capital side to prove that their demands are necessary, not on us to accept them by default.
