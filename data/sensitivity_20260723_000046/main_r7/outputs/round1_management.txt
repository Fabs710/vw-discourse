I hear the line you are both drawing: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and a binding security framework. I understand why you say that. But I need to be equally clear from management’s side: **the current cost base and capacity profile are not viable**. If we simply paper over that reality with guarantees and internal shifts, we will not restore this business to competitiveness.

Where I agree:
- We do need a **negotiated solution**, not a unilateral diktat.
- We do need to **avoid unnecessary social damage** if there is a workable alternative.
- We do need a **credible industrial plan** with investment and future products, not just cuts.

Where I disagree fundamentally:
- **A blanket exclusion of plant closures is not something I can accept upfront.**
- **A blanket exclusion of workforce reductions is not realistic** if we are serious about closing the gap to target.
- A “security agreement through 2030” has no value if it simply locks in an uncompetitive structure and pushes the crisis into the future.

Let me be direct: we are not talking about marginal efficiency here. We are talking about a structural problem in German operations. European demand is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our domestic manufacturing costs are materially above target. If we fail to act now, we will destroy far more value later — including the investment capacity needed for the next product cycle.

So my position is this:

1. **We need durable capacity reduction.**  
   That can include plant consolidation or closure if that is what the economics require. I am not going to rule that out.

2. **We need a meaningful labour-cost contribution.**  
   Voluntary measures alone will not be enough. We need structural savings, not a temporary pause.

3. **We need flexibility on employment instruments.**  
   Redeployment, qualifications, and voluntary departures can help, but they cannot be the only tools.

4. **We need a settlement that restores margins and funds future investment.**  
   If the package does not move us decisively toward the return-on-sales target, then it is not sufficient.

What I am prepared to consider is a package that combines:
- significant capacity reduction,
- deep cost savings,
- protection for the most strategic parts of the footprint,
- and a negotiated transition for affected employees.

But I will not pretend that we can secure the future of the brand by promising that everything stays as it is. That is exactly the kind of compromise that looks comfortable now and fails the company later.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring me an alternative that delivers **comparable, verified, durable savings** and a real reduction in overcapacity. Not slogans. Not vague assurances. A hard package that closes the competitiveness gap.
