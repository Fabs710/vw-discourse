We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the clear starting point for the works council. The workforce has already delivered decades of productivity, flexibility, and sacrifice. Now management is trying to solve a strategic mistake by shifting the full burden onto employees and entire regions. That will not stand.

Our priorities are straightforward:

- Keep every German plant open
- Exclude forced layoffs
- Secure binding job guarantees through 2030
- Protect co-determination and the existing job-security framework
- Shape any restructuring through negotiated, reversible measures rather than irreversible closures

If the company truly needs savings, then management must first look at alternatives that do not destroy sites and livelihoods: capacity adjustments, temporary working-time measures, internal redeployment, reduced outsourcing, investment commitments, and a fair contribution from capital and management. But any “solution” built on plant closure, mass dismissals, or breaking historic guarantees is unacceptable.

We are ready to talk about competitiveness. We are not ready to bargain away the future of German sites or make workers pay for a crisis they did not create. If management comes with a fait accompli, we will resist it with every legitimate means available, including industrial action and our rights in co-determination.
