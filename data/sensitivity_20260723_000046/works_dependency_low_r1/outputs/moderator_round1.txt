### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company faces a serious competitiveness and cost challenge.
- All sides prefer a negotiated settlement over unilateral action or prolonged conflict.
- There is broad support for:
  - investment in future products and sites,
  - qualification and redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - some form of shared contribution,
  - and avoiding value-destroying escalation.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures:** Employee side and state reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state reject them; management will not exclude headcount reduction; shareholders want economically effective restructuring.
- **Job guarantees vs. structural change:** Employee side seeks binding protections through 2030; management and capital sides say old guarantees cannot remain unchanged if economics require adjustment.
- **Depth of cost reduction:** Management and capital insist on durable, structural savings; employee side favors reversible, negotiated measures and says workers should not bear the full burden.
- **Governance leverage:** State emphasizes co-determination and veto rights; management and some capital voices warn against a rigid veto that blocks restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- What level of cost reduction is required to restore competitiveness, and how much of it can come from non-closure measures?
- What combination of wage restraint, working-time changes, capacity reduction, and site adjustments would be considered durable enough by management and investors?
- What specific protections, guarantees, and investment commitments would justify employee concessions?
- How can restructuring be made credible without triggering industrial conflict or governance deadlock?
- Which sites, functions, or cost categories are actually in scope for adjustment, and what is the timeline?

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers, scenarios, and package design**.
- Ask management to specify the minimum required savings and capacity changes.
- Ask the employee side to identify which reversible measures and concessions could realistically close part of the gap.
- Ask shareholders and the state what outcome thresholds they would view as acceptable.
- Focus on a **sequenced package**: immediate relief, medium-term restructuring, and binding investment/job-security conditions.
