I appreciate the expert input, because it confirms what I have been saying: **the closure case has not been proven**. At the same time, it also confirms what management keeps stressing: **temporary measures alone are not enough** if the structural gap is large. So we are not at a point where anyone can claim a simple victory.

## My position

The state will **not** support a restructuring that uses plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the default answer.

That is not a symbolic line. It is a substantive one:
- the company is a regional economic anchor,
- jobs and sites in our state matter,
- co-determination is part of the governance reality,
- and the state’s veto role cannot be bypassed by pressure tactics.

I can support a serious restructuring. I cannot support the dismantling of the industrial base in the region.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the company needs a durable reset,
- cosmetic savings are worthless,
- and a conflict spiral would destroy value.

I also accept the expert’s point that **voluntary measures must be large enough and recurring enough** to matter. I am not pretending that a few temporary concessions will fix a structural problem.

### With the works council and union
You are right that:
- numbers must be put on the table,
- management must justify its claims,
- and a closure threat is not proof of necessity.

I also agree that workers should not bear the whole burden. That would be politically and economically wrong.

### With the family holding and investors
You are right that a hollow compromise is no solution. The company does need a package that improves competitiveness in a durable way.

## Where I disagree

### Against closures as the starting point
Management and capital keep speaking as if closures are the inevitable endpoint unless workers surrender. That is not established.

The expert was clear: **the right conclusion today is “undetermined pending quantified gap analysis.”** That is exactly the point. No one has yet demonstrated that the non-closure path cannot work.

So I reject any attempt to make closures the default assumption.

### Against a “review gate” that is really a trapdoor
I do not accept a phased approach if Phase 1 is merely a delay mechanism that sets up later shutdowns.

If we do phases, then the first phase must be real and the review must be honest:
- audited numbers,
- clear savings targets,
- no hidden pre-commitment to closures,
- and no automatic escalation into plant shutdowns if the first round falls short for reasons not fully tested.

### Against forcing workers to pay for strategic underperformance
If management wants contribution from the workforce, then management and owners must also contribute visibly:
- overhead cuts,
- management cost reductions,
- procurement discipline,
- product allocation commitments,
- and investment in German sites.

The burden cannot simply be shifted downward.

## What I can support

I am prepared to support a **non-closure restructuring package** if it is genuinely economically credible and protects the regional base. That means:

1. **A quantified cost-gap model**
   - per site
   - per cost category
   - with recurring savings and one-offs clearly separated

2. **A serious non-closure package**
   - working-time flexibility
   - temporary wage restraint
   - bonus restraint or deferral
   - reduced outsourcing
   - overtime reduction
   - internal redeployment
   - qualification
   - voluntary severance / early retirement
   - management-side cost cuts

3. **Binding protections**
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - no German plant closures in the first phase
   - preservation of co-determination
   - protection of the state’s veto role

4. **Investment and product commitments**
   - not as decoration,
   - but as part of a real industrial future for the state’s sites

5. **An independent verification process**
   - the expert should be used again once management puts the numbers on the table
   - because right now, neither side has proved its full case

## What I will not accept

I will not support:
- a cosmetic peace deal,
- a closure-first strategy,
- compulsory redundancies as the default instrument,
- or a settlement that weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor.

And I will be explicit: if management comes back with numbers showing that a limited, carefully designed capacity adjustment is truly unavoidable, I will listen. But that is very different from accepting closures as the first move or the only credible one.

## My request now

Before anyone escalates further, I want three things on the table:

1. **Management:** a board-usable savings bridge, broken down by labor, overhead, procurement/logistics, and capacity.
2. **Employee side:** a concrete non-closure package with quantified contributions and duration.
3. **Expert verification:** a scenario test of whether the voluntary package closes the structural gap, and if not, how large the remaining gap really is.

## Bottom line

The state’s line remains:

- **yes** to serious restructuring,
- **yes** to shared sacrifice,
- **yes** to investment in the future,
- **yes** to independent verification,
- **no** to plant closures as the starting point,
- **no** to compulsory redundancies as the default answer,
- **no** to sidelining co-determination or the state’s co-determination rights.

If the numbers eventually show that further adjustment is needed, that discussion must happen on verified facts, not on pressure and assumption. Until then, I will not give a blank cheque for closures.
