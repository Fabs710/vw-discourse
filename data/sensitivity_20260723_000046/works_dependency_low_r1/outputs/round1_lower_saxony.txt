I will be very clear: I agree that the company needs a durable competitiveness solution. I do **not** agree that the only credible route is plant closure and compulsory redundancies.

As the regional state government and an anchor shareholder, my priority is not a short-term accounting fix. It is the long-term viability of the company **and** the preservation of the industrial base in our state. That means:

- no unilateral fait accompli,
- no stripping the region of jobs and industrial capability,
- no sidestepping our co-determination and veto rights,
- and no restructuring that simply exports the burden onto workers and communities.

To management: I understand the pressure on margins, demand, and costs. I am not dismissing the seriousness of the situation. But your framing is too narrow. If you present closures as the default answer before exhausting negotiated alternatives, you are choosing a path that is politically explosive, socially destructive, and—given the governance structure—highly unlikely to deliver a stable result.

If you want the state to support a restructuring, then it must meet basic conditions:
- preserve German sites wherever possible,
- avoid compulsory redundancies,
- use reversible and negotiated measures first,
- protect core co-determination and the statutory balance of power,
- and attach any employee contribution to credible investment and future-product commitments.

To the works council and the union: I support the line that closures and mass layoffs are not the starting point. But I also have to be honest: a settlement that preserves every current arrangement unchanged will not be enough if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. We should not pretend that cost and capacity issues do not exist.

So I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** if it is built on:
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction through redeployment and natural attrition,
- temporary rather than irreversible measures,
- fair but limited labor concessions,
- management contribution on overheads and execution,
- and binding commitments on investment, new products, and site development.

What I will not support is:
- plant closures in the state as a first resort,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or any outcome that weakens the state’s veto and co-determination role.

In short: I am open to hard negotiations, but not to a solution that sacrifices our region to repair management’s strategic mistakes. If there is a serious alternative that protects jobs and sites while restoring competitiveness, I am ready to engage constructively. If not, you should expect the state to use every legitimate right it has.
