## Core impasse

The central impasse is now very clear:

**Management, family holding, and investors want a restructuring that is demonstrably durable in economic terms, while works council, union, and state want binding protection against closures and compulsory redundancies.**  
The dispute is no longer about whether the company needs a reset. It is about **what level of irreversible adjustment is economically necessary, and whether that can be achieved without plant closures or forced layoffs**.

The expert’s key finding is procedural but important: **the record is still “undetermined pending quantified gap analysis.”**  
So the conflict currently rests on a missing bridge:
- no agreed savings gap,
- no agreed site-by-site cost model,
- no agreed proof that voluntary/non-closure measures either do or do not close the gap.

That is why the sides keep talking past one another:
- one side treats closures as a necessary instrument,
- the other treats closures as unacceptable absent proof,
- and no common quantified baseline has yet been accepted.

## Possible compromise positions

### 1) Quantified phase-1 package with a protected review window
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Adopt a first-phase restructuring package that contains:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits / early retirement,
- redeployment,
- overhead cuts,
- procurement/logistics savings,

**paired with**:
- a defined protection window in which no closures and no compulsory redundancies occur,
- independent verification of savings delivery,
- and a pre-agreed review date based on audited numbers.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
- Gives management and investors measurable early savings.
- Gives works council, union, and state a real non-closure test.
- Avoids locking in either side before the numbers are verified.

---

### 2) “No forced redundancies” plus conditional capacity adjustment by voluntary means first
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Agree that the first toolset must be:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- reduced overtime,
- shift and working-time changes,
- qualification and redeployment.

Only if these measures fail to close the quantified residual gap would any further capacity adjustment be considered, and even then only after renewed consultation and independent review.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
- Preserves the red line against compulsory redundancies for the employee side.
- Gives management a pathway to deeper adjustment if voluntary tools prove insufficient.
- Keeps the decision tied to evidence rather than threat.

---

### 3) Site guarantees tied to measurable investment and productivity milestones
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Offer binding site guarantees for a defined period, but condition the continuation of those guarantees on:
- specific investment commitments,
- product allocation,
- measurable productivity improvements,
- and verified implementation milestones.

This would require:
- transparent KPI tracking,
- union/works council participation in oversight,
- and an independent audit of whether targets are being met.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
- Gives workers and the state a real protection framework.
- Gives management and investors a performance-based structure rather than an unconditional freeze.
- Converts the dispute from “closure yes/no” into “what verified performance triggers what adjustment.”

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the exact residual annual savings gap after every non-closure measure you are prepared to test, and can you show it site by site, with recurring versus one-off effects separated?**

### To the works council
**Would you accept a defined, independently audited review process if phase 1 includes binding non-closure protections but also binding quantified savings commitments from the workforce side?**

### To the union
**Which specific voluntary measures are you prepared to quantify as sufficient in aggregate, and what minimum duration of labor contribution would you accept in exchange for no compulsory redundancies?**

### To the state government
**If the numbers show a residual gap remains after a genuine non-closure package, what precise conditions would you require before reconsidering your no-closure position?**

### To the controlling family holding
**What level of verified recurring savings would be enough for you to support a non-closure settlement, and are you prepared to accept that threshold if it is met through voluntary and investment-led measures?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you support a settlement that preserves sites and avoids forced layoffs if an independent bridge shows the gap is closed through durable, recurring savings rather than closures?**

### To the expert
**Can you define the minimum data set and decision threshold needed to determine whether the residual gap after non-closure measures is materially closed or still structurally significant?**
