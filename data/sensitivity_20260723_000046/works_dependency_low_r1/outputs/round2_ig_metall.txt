We are aligned with the works council on the core red lines: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures as the default solution, and no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**.

But I want to be equally clear: if management keeps treating those red lines as mere “preferences,” then it is not negotiating in good faith. And if the burden keeps being pushed one-sidedly onto employees, then there will be industrial resistance.

## Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: a purely cosmetic package will not solve the problem. If there is a real competitiveness gap, then there must be real measures.

I also agree with the family holding, the state, and investors on one important issue: **the company needs a durable settlement, not an endless conflict spiral**. A strike is a tool, not a goal. We want a result that lasts.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the idea that “durable” automatically means **closures and layoffs**.

That is not an economic law; it is one restructuring choice. And because this company has co-determination, a blocking state stake, and a workforce with legitimate bargaining power, management cannot simply declare closure to be the only credible path.

I also reject the framing that workers must prove they are “serious” by accepting open-ended insecurity first. That reverses responsibility. Management must first show that it is prepared to share the burden fairly.

## To management
You keep asking for “comparable, durable savings,” but you have not yet shown that closures are unavoidable.

So here is the test:
- What is the exact savings gap?
- What share comes from labour cost?
- What share comes from overhead, product mix, procurement, management structure, and capital allocation?
- Which specific non-closure measures have been ruled out after serious analysis, not just after convenience?

If you want employee concessions, bring a package that is concrete and balanced:
- **binding site guarantees**
- **no forced redundancies**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **temporary wage restraint or bonus sacrifice**
- **internal redeployment and qualification**
- **a real management and owner contribution**
- **investment commitments for future products**

That is a negotiated restructuring. A one-sided cost-cutting push is not.

## To the works council
I support your insistence on protecting sites and rejecting forced layoffs. But I need to draw one distinction.

Saying “no closures” is a legitimate bargaining position. Saying “everything stays exactly as it is” is not a solution if the company genuinely needs a structural adjustment. So if we want to avoid irreversible measures, then we need an alternative that actually closes the gap.

That means we should be prepared to discuss:
- temporary or phased working-time reductions
- deferred or forgone bonuses
- reduced tariff additions for a limited period
- capacity adjustments through voluntary and natural attrition
- redeployment across the group
- qualification and transformation agreements

But these can only happen **with binding job and site security**. Not as a blank cheque.

## To the state government
We appreciate your support against a fait accompli. But “site rationalisation” cannot be a code word for later closures and job cuts.

If the state wants a stable compromise, then it should be explicit:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- and a negotiated transformation plan with investment commitments.

If you stand for that, we will treat you as a serious partner. If not, workers will assume the state is simply asking them to absorb the damage more politely.

## To the family holding and investors
Long-term value is not protected by forcing through a settlement that destroys trust, wages, and industrial peace. You talk about durability; we agree. But durability also requires legitimacy.

A package that gets accepted only under pressure and then explodes in the next dispute is not durable. If you want stability, then you need to support a settlement that employees can actually live with.

## What we are prepared to do
We remain open to a **shared-sacrifice package** if, and only if, it meets three conditions:

1. **Jobs and sites are protected**
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - no plant closures as the restructuring path

2. **Employees contribute fairly**
   - temporary wage restraint
   - bonus renunciation or similar contributions
   - working-time flexibility
   - possible fund or transition contribution

3. **Management and owners also contribute**
   - overhead and execution cuts
   - investment commitments
   - credible changes on the company side, not just pressure on labour

That is the only basis on which we can discuss concessions without undermining the collective wage framework.

## Bottom line
We are not here to block every change. We are here to block a restructuring that makes workers absorb all the pain while management and owners preserve their options.

If management comes back with a quantified, balanced package that protects jobs, keeps German sites open, and shares the burden fairly, then we are ready to negotiate seriously.

If it insists on closures, compulsory redundancies, or breaking the wage framework, then it should expect strong resistance — including industrial action.
