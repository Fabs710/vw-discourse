## My position
The family holding supports a **serious, durable restructuring**. We do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves weak margins and punts the problem into the next cycle.

At the same time, I do **not** accept a blanket “closures at any cost” posture either. If closures or compulsory redundancies are to be part of the final package, they must be **proven necessary by numbers**, not assumed by habit or used as leverage.

So my threshold is clear:
- **durable competitiveness improvement**
- **measurable structural savings**
- **preserved enterprise value**
- **no value-destructive conflict**
- **no permanent impairment of the ownership position or governance control**

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the cost base is too high,
- temporary optics are not enough,
- and the company needs a structural reset.

You are also right that voluntary-only measures may be too small if the gap is large.

### With investors
You are right to insist on:
- durable savings,
- credibility,
- and margin recovery.

A deal that simply buys peace while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive would destroy value.

### With the state
You are right that a strike spiral or governance deadlock would be harmful. The company cannot afford prolonged paralysis.

### With the works council and union
You are right that management must show the numbers. No one should accept irreversible measures on assertion alone.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
I do **not** accept the position that:
- every German plant must stay open,
- no compulsory redundancies may ever be considered,
- and no capacity reduction can be contemplated.

That is not a serious economic principle. It is a red line built for industrial defense. I understand why you hold it, but I do not accept it as governing logic for a company that must restore competitiveness.

If the economics require it, then **some site or capacity adjustment may ultimately be unavoidable**. Refusing even to test that possibility would be irresponsible.

### With the state
I do **not** accept the idea that the state’s blocking position can be used to freeze restructuring indefinitely. Protecting the regional base is important, but preserving every structure unchanged is not protection if the business keeps eroding.

### With management
I also do **not** accept a closure-first reflex. If management wants support from the owners, it must show:
- the exact savings gap,
- what is structural and recurring,
- what non-closure measures were genuinely tested,
- and why they fall short.

You have not yet earned a blank cheque for irreversible cuts.

## What I need before I move

The expert’s intervention was useful: the correct judgment right now is still **undetermined pending quantified gap analysis**.

So I want a board-grade bridge with:
1. **current German cost base**
2. **site-by-site fixed and variable cost data**
3. **capacity and utilization assumptions**
4. **target margin / return-on-sales**
5. **a full savings bridge by category**
   - labor
   - overhead
   - procurement/logistics
   - capacity/fixed cost
   - capex / investment allocation
6. **a clear separation of one-off vs recurring savings**

Without that, neither the closure camp nor the no-closure camp has proved its case.

## My governing principle
We will back the solution that best preserves and grows long-term enterprise value.

That means:
- if a serious non-closure package can close the gap, we favor it;
- if it cannot, then harder measures may become necessary;
- but either way, the package must be economically real, durable, and implementable.

## What I will support
I am prepared to support a settlement that includes:
- temporary wage restraint or bonus moderation
- working-time flexibility
- reduced outsourcing
- redeployment and qualification
- management-side cost cuts
- voluntary exits / early retirement
- investment commitments
- and a verified review gate

I am **not** prepared to support a package that:
- leaves the cost base structurally unchanged,
- rules out any site adjustment in advance,
- or simply transfers the burden onto the owners through lower value without fixing the business.

## My red lines
My own unacceptable outcomes are simple:
- permanent impairment of enterprise value
- erosion of governance control
- a long, destructive conflict
- and a hollow compromise that looks humane but keeps the company weak
