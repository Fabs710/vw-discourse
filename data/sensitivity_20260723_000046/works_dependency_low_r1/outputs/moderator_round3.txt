## Core impasse

The central blockage is this:

- **Employee-side actors** insist on **binding no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy protections** and want management to prove that irreversible cuts are unavoidable.
- **Management and capital-side actors** insist on **structural, durable cost reduction** and will not accept a package that preserves all German sites and current capacity if the economics remain weak.

So the dispute is not about whether change is needed; it is about **whether durability can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs**, and if so, **what binding guarantees and quantified savings would make that credible**.

A second, related impasse is **sequencing and trust**:
- the employee side wants numbers and guarantees first,
- management wants concessions and flexibility first,
- both sides doubt the other will honor commitments once signed.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### 1) Phased restructuring with a binding protection window
**Structure**
- A first phase of 12–18 months with:
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - no German plant closures,
  - no weakening of co-determination,
  - temporary wage/bonus restraint,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - reduced outsourcing,
  - management-side cost cuts,
  - redeployment and qualification measures.
- A jointly agreed review gate at the end of Phase 1, based on audited financial and operational metrics.

**Purpose**
- Gives management immediate cost relief and implementation certainty.
- Gives employee representatives binding near-term protection.
- Moves the closure question from assertion to measured review.

**Key condition**
- The review gate must be tied to **predefined, audited metrics** and **cannot be used as an automatic trapdoor**.

---

### 2) “No forced redundancies” package with voluntary capacity reduction and owner commitment
**Structure**
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Voluntary tools only:
  - early retirement,
  - severance on voluntary basis,
  - internal transfer,
  - reduced overtime,
  - voluntary hours reductions.
- In parallel:
  - owner and management contributions,
  - visible overhead cuts,
  - procurement/logistics savings,
  - investment commitments for German sites,
  - product allocation commitments where feasible.

**Purpose**
- Preserves the core employee red lines while still allowing structural adjustment.
- Forces all sides, including owners and management, to contribute materially.
- Tests whether a voluntary path can close enough of the gap before any harder decisions are revisited.

**Key condition**
- A quantified savings target must be agreed up front, with an independent audit of whether voluntary measures are sufficient.

---

### 3) Site guarantees tied to investment and productivity milestones, with a non-closure floor
**Structure**
- Each German site receives a binding baseline guarantee through 2030.
- Closures are off the table unless jointly verified milestone failures occur after full alternative measures have been exhausted.
- In exchange:
  - productivity commitments,
  - investment commitments,
  - transformation/qualification programs,
  - temporary labour-cost measures,
  - and agreed capacity adjustments where operationally justified.

**Purpose**
- Gives the state and employee side a credible industrial-base protection.
- Gives investors and management a measurable framework rather than open-ended protection.
- Converts the dispute into a performance-based arrangement instead of a binary closure threat.

**Key condition**
- Milestones must be specific, measurable, and jointly monitored; they should not function as a disguised pretext for unilateral shutdowns.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
What exact savings gap remains after you have modelled **all** non-closure measures, and what independent verification can you provide that those measures are insufficient?

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
Under what precise conditions, if any, would you accept temporary concessions from employees, and what binding governance guarantees would you require in return?

### To the Industrial Union
Which voluntary or temporary labour measures are you prepared to place on the table, and what minimum job and site protections must be legally or collectively binding for you to endorse them?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you explicitly define the state’s supportable restructuring corridor in measurable terms, including what it would accept as a non-closure settlement and what would trigger opposition?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
What concrete owner-level commitments are you prepared to make—capital, investment, governance assurances, or patient capital—to make a non-closure solution economically credible?

### To Institutional Investors
What minimum combination of durable savings, capacity adjustment, and implementation certainty would you accept as sufficient even if it does not include immediate closures or compulsory redundancies?
