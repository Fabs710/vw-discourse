I appreciate the clarity from all sides. From the perspective of the controlling family, our lens is simple: we need a restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins **durably**, protects the long-term value of the enterprise, and avoids a spiral of conflict that destroys value faster than any cost base can.

So let me be equally clear.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on the core diagnosis: this company cannot carry a structurally uncompetitive cost base indefinitely. If German operations remain above market cost while demand is under pressure, then value erodes, investment capacity shrinks, and the brand weakens. That is unacceptable for a long-term owner.

I also agree with the employee side and the state on one crucial point: a solution that triggers prolonged industrial war, reputational damage, and governance deadlock is not a solution. The company needs labour peace and operational stability, not a headline-driven showdown.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures or compulsory redundancies must be the default or first resort.

Management is right that a cosmetic package is not enough. But it does not follow that the only serious answer is irreversible closures. In a company with co-determination, a blocking state shareholder, and a controlling owner focused on long-term value, a confrontational “take it or leave it” strategy is not governance; it is a recipe for stalemate and value destruction.

Likewise, I cannot support any arrangement that simply preserves every current structure, fully protects all existing guarantees, and shifts the adjustment burden entirely into the future. That would also be a form of denial, and it would damage the business.

### What would move us
We would support a negotiated package if it meets three tests:

1. **Durable cost improvement**
   - Real, measurable savings that go beyond temporary symbolism.
   - Structural labour-cost relief.
   - Capacity and overhead reduction that actually changes the economics.

2. **Stability and deliverability**
   - No endless conflict.
   - A settlement that can actually be implemented at plant level.
   - Predictability for investment and product planning.

3. **Protection of long-term value**
   - No outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise’s core industrial base unnecessarily.
   - No governance escalation that weakens the company’s ability to act.
   - No settlement that creates a false peace while storing up a bigger crisis.

### On the current positions
To management: if you want support for hard restructuring, you will need to show that alternatives to closures can deliver **comparable durable effect**. Not marginal savings, not vague promises, but a package with substance. If that cannot be done, then you should not be surprised that resistance intensifies. But equally, you should not be forced into a dead end by rigid taboos if the economics truly do not work.

To the works council and union: I respect the insistence on dignity, co-determination, and avoiding unnecessary forced layoffs. But “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and all guarantees intact” is not a sustainable economic answer by itself. If you want to avoid irreversible measures, you need to bring forward a credible alternative that actually closes the cost gap and allows investment. That will require real concessions, not just objections.

To the state government: I understand the regional and political responsibility, and I agree that the industrial base matters. But the state should not turn this into a rigid veto exercise that prevents any meaningful restructuring. If we protect every site and every legacy arrangement regardless of economics, we will simply weaken the company until the problem returns in worse form.

### Bottom line
The family holding will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** that restores competitiveness, even if it includes difficult measures. But we will not back an outcome that is either:

- a cosmetic compromise that leaves the cost base fundamentally broken, or
- a destructive confrontation that undermines value, stability, and governance control.

If the parties can produce a package with durable savings, labor peace, and credible investment into the future, we are prepared to consider it. If not, then management will have to continue pursuing structural remedies, because preserving the status quo is not an option.
