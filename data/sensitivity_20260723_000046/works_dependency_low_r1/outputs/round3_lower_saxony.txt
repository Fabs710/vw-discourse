I will be very direct.

## My position
The state will not support a restructuring that is effectively a **code for plant closures, forced redundancies, and permanent weakening of the company’s regional footprint**.

I accept the need for a durable competitiveness solution. I do **not** accept the claim that durability requires sacrificing the industrial base in our state or sidelining co-determination. Those are not acceptable trade-offs from our side.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **cosmetic measures are not enough**.

I also agree with the works council and union that a destructive conflict would be value-destroying. A strike spiral, a broken trust relationship, and a governance deadlock would hurt the company and the region. None of us should want that.

So yes: the company needs a serious package, and it needs to be durable.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You keep saying that closures or irreversible cuts are necessary because the cost base is too high. That may be your conclusion, but it is **not yet proven** as the only viable conclusion.

Before you ask the board and stakeholders to accept closures or compulsory redundancies, I expect:
- a quantified savings gap,
- a breakdown by cost category,
- and a serious comparison of closure versus non-closure measures.

If you have not exhausted:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus restraint,
- reduced outsourcing,
- internal redeployment,
- management-side cost cuts,
- productivity improvements,
- and targeted investment reallocation,

then closures are not the first answer. They are the last resort.

And let me be equally clear: I will not support a fait accompli dressed up as urgency.

### To the works council and union
I understand — and share — your rejection of compulsory redundancies as a first response. I also understand your refusal to let workers carry the whole burden.

But I need to say this plainly: a blanket “no closures, no layoffs, no changes” is not enough on its own if the company truly needs a structural reset. If you want to rule out closures, then you must help produce a **credible alternative that actually closes the gap**.

That means discussing:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus deferral or sacrifice,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary hours reductions,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and voluntary capacity reduction.

I am open to those measures. I am **not** open to a package that simply shifts the pain onto workers while giving them no real security in return.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right to insist on long-term value. But long-term value is not protected by a scorched-earth restructuring that damages trust, triggers years of industrial conflict, and undermines the company’s legitimacy in the region.

If you want my support for hard measures, then they must be:
- economically credible,
- socially sustainable,
- and consistent with the company’s role as a regional anchor.

A solution that leaves permanent political and social damage is not value-preserving. It is just a different kind of cost.

## What I can support
I am prepared to back a negotiated package if it meets these conditions:

1. **Real competitiveness gains**
   - measurable structural savings
   - lower fixed costs
   - improved capacity utilization
   - a credible path to margin recovery

2. **Protection of the regional base**
   - no plant closures in Germany as a first resort
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - binding site and job protections where possible
   - no weakening of co-determination

3. **Fair burden sharing**
   - employees contribute
   - management contributes
   - owners contribute
   - investment and future products are secured

## What I will not accept
I will not support:
- a cosmetic peace deal,
- a restructuring that uses closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as the default instrument,
- or a settlement that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not where the state stands.

## On the bridge formulas
### 1) Phased restructuring with a review gate
This is the only serious starting point, but only if the first phase includes **binding protection**:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- no hidden pre-commitment to shutdowns.

The review must be genuine, not a trapdoor.

### 2) No forced redundancies, voluntary capacity reduction
This is the most workable route if management is serious. Voluntary severance, early retirement, internal transfer, reduced overtime, and natural attrition can be part of the solution. But they must be sufficient in aggregate and paired with investment and site commitments.

### 3) Site guarantees tied to productivity and investment milestones
I can support productivity targets and investment commitments. I cannot support a mechanism that simply postpones the closure decision until the next pressure point. Milestones must be used to secure the future, not to manufacture a later shutdown.

## My threshold
I can support a settlement if it delivers all of the following:
- measurable savings,
- industrial peace,
- preserved regional industrial strength,
- binding investment commitments,
- and no compelled destruction of jobs and sites.

If the package does not protect the state’s industrial base, then I will not support it.

## My request to management
Bring us the numbers:
- What is the exact savings gap?
- What comes from labor, overhead, procurement, and capacity?
- What non-closure package has been fully tested?
- What does “equivalent” really mean in measurable terms?

## My request to the employee side
Identify which concessions can be made **without** surrendering the company’s regional footprint:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus sacrifice,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary capacity adjustment.

If you want to avoid closures, we need a concrete package that can actually do the job.

## My request to the family holding and investors
Be explicit about what you would accept as a durable, value-preserving compromise that does **not** dismantle the regional anchor role of the company.

## Bottom line
The state will help solve this crisis. We will not help dismantle the company’s industrial base in the process.

So my line is this:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to shared sacrifice.
- **Yes** to binding investment and transformation commitments.
- **No** to closures as a first solution.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies as the default answer.
- **No** to weakening co-determination or sidelining the state’s role.

If management wants our support, it must come back with a quantified, genuinely negotiated package that protects the company’s long-term health **and** its regional anchor function.
