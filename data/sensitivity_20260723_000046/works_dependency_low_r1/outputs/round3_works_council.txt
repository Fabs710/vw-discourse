Let me be absolutely clear in round 3:

## Our position has not changed
The works council will not accept:
- **German plant closures**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **the dismantling of the job-security framework**
- **any weakening of co-determination**

Those are hard red lines. Not “starting points.” Not “preferences.” Red lines.

## Where I agree with others
I do agree on one thing: the company needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.

I also agree that mere slogans about flexibility are not enough. If management claims there is a structural problem, then it must show the numbers. I welcome that part of the discussion.

But that is where the agreement ends.

## To management
You keep saying closures or layoffs are “necessary.” I do not accept that claim unless you prove that **every serious non-closure alternative has been exhausted**.

So here is what I want, in concrete terms:
- the exact savings gap
- a split between labor cost, overhead, procurement, logistics, and management costs
- a breakdown by site
- a serious comparison of closure versus non-closure options

Until then, your closure threat is just leverage, not necessity.

And let me remind you: you damaged trust when the historic job-security pledge was broken. You do not get to demand blind confidence after that.

### What we will consider
We are prepared to discuss a real restructuring package built on:
- working-time reductions and flexibility
- temporary wage restraint
- bonus sacrifice
- reduced outsourcing
- redeployment across the group
- qualification and transformation work
- investment commitments to German sites

Those are real tools. They are not “cosmetic” unless management refuses to use them seriously.

What we will **not** accept is a plan that starts from closures and then calls employee concessions “shared sacrifice.”

## To the union
We are aligned on the core red lines. I support your willingness to mobilize, because management only understands pressure when workers show it is real.

But I want to be equally firm: employee sacrifice is only legitimate if it protects **all German plants and all jobs from forced destruction**. If there is to be any contribution, it must be tied to:
- binding site guarantees
- no compulsory redundancies
- no plant closures
- a genuine role for worker representation in implementation

Without that, there is no basis for concessions.

## To the state government
I hear your concern about value destruction and regional stability. I share that concern.

But I do not accept a vague “site future” language that later becomes closures through the back door. The state must be explicit:
- no plant closures in Germany
- no compulsory redundancies
- no weakening of co-determination
- no use of “milestones” as a delayed closure mechanism

If the state wants to be a credible anchor shareholder, it must defend the industrial base in practice, not only in speeches.

## To the family holding
You say you want a serious restructuring and fear inertia. Fine. But I reject the idea that only irreversible cuts can create value.

A destructive confrontation would not be “discipline”; it would be self-harm. It would damage trust, productivity, industrial peace, and the company’s reputation for years.

If you want long-term value, then support a package that keeps every German plant open and avoids forced layoffs. That is the stable path. Anything else risks trading short-term accounting gains for long-term industrial damage.

## To investors
You want measurable, durable savings. Understood.

But let me be blunt: if your only definition of durability is “someone loses a plant or a job permanently,” then you are ignoring the real risks of conflict and implementation failure.

A package is durable if it is:
- negotiated
- enforceable
- socially legitimate
- and capable of being implemented without years of resistance

A settlement built on closures and compulsory redundancies may look clean on paper and fail in practice.

## On the proposed bridge formulas
### 1) Phased restructuring with a review gate
This only becomes acceptable if the first phase contains **binding protection**:
- no closures
- no forced layoffs
- no hidden pre-commitment to later closures

A review gate cannot be a trapdoor.

### 2) No forced redundancies, voluntary capacity reduction
This is closer to reality, but only if it remains truly voluntary and does not become pressure by attrition. Voluntary tools are acceptable. Compulsory layoffs are not.

### 3) Site guarantees tied to productivity and investment milestones
We can discuss productivity and investment, but not as a mechanism to reopen closure threats later. Site guarantees must be real and time-bound, not conditional promises that vanish at the first accounting miss.

## My answer to the pointed questions

### To management
Show the numbers first. Until then, no plant closures, no layoffs.

### To the union
Temporary wage restraint, bonus sacrifice, working-time flexibility, and redeployment can be discussed **only if** jobs and sites are protected through 2030 and no forced redundancies occur.

### To the state
You should explicitly support:
- no closures
- no forced redundancies
- investment commitments
- qualification and redeployment
- reduced outsourcing
- management-side cost cuts

Your red line must be any restructuring that transfers the burden onto workers through site destruction.

### To the family holding
If you want employee concessions, then authorize:
- binding site guarantees through 2030
- no compulsory redundancies
- no closures
- a genuine co-determination role in implementation
- management and owner contributions that are visible and real

### To investors
A credible package must show:
- real savings, yes
- but also preserved labor peace
- intact industrial capacity
- protected German sites
- and no governance crisis

## Bottom line
We are not here to bargain over whether workers should accept plant closures or forced redundancies. They will not.

If management wants cooperation, it must come back with a package that:
1. keeps every German plant open,
2. excludes compulsory redundancies,
3. preserves co-determination,
4. and secures jobs and sites through 2030.

Without that, the works council will continue to resist — politically, industrially, and through the full leverage that co-determination gives us.
