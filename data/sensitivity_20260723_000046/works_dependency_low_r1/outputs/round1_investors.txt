As a dispersed institutional shareholder, my position is straightforward: **we need a restructuring that credibly restores competitive margins, not a symbolic settlement or a prolonged conflict that keeps eroding value**.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and the state on the core diagnosis: the current German cost base is not competitive enough, and the company cannot keep missing its return-on-sales target while demand is weak and competition is intensifying. If that is the reality, then **some form of durable cost and capacity reduction is necessary**.

I also agree that a long strike or a governance deadlock would be value-destructive. Every month of uncertainty, lost production, and reputational damage hits earnings and undermines investor confidence.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that simply preserves the current structure and calls it a compromise. If the result is:
- no meaningful capacity reduction,
- no durable labour-cost improvement,
- no credible improvement in margins,
then it is not a restructuring from our perspective.

At the same time, I am not automatically in favour of plant closures as the default answer. What matters to us is not ideology, but **economic effect**. If comparable savings can be achieved through negotiated measures that are durable and enforceable, then that is preferable to a disruptive closure program that creates execution risk and industrial conflict.

### What would make us support a deal
We would back an agreement if it delivers all of the following:
- **credible, durable cost reduction**
- **measurable capacity adjustment**
- **a clear path to margin improvement**
- **implementation certainty**
- **limited disruption compared with open conflict**

That can include a mix of:
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- wage restraint or other employee contributions,
- targeted capacity reduction,
- selective site rationalisation if truly necessary,
- overhead reduction,
- and investment commitments tied to restructuring milestones.

But we will not endorse a package that keeps every legacy commitment intact while leaving the business structurally uncompetitive.

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no closures is understandable, but from a capital-market perspective it becomes unacceptable if it blocks real restructuring. **Jobs cannot be protected indefinitely by destroying the economics of the company**. If you want support for avoiding closures, then the alternative must still produce the same financial outcome.

### To management
You need to show that your plan is not just a blunt cut, but a credible route back to competitive margins. If you ask investors to tolerate conflict and restructuring pain, then we need confidence that the end state is materially better. Otherwise, capital will continue to be priced against execution risk and weak returns.

### Bottom line
We are **not** aligned with a cosmetic peace deal. We are **not** aligned with indefinite preservation of uncompetitive structures. We are **open to a negotiated alternative to closures only if it is genuinely durable, measurable, and margin-accretive**.

If the final package does not clearly close the cost gap, then it does not deserve investor support.
