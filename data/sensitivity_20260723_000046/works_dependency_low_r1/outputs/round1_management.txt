I hear the position, but I need to be equally clear: **a solution that preserves every German plant unchanged, rules out compulsory redundancies entirely, and extends job guarantees through 2030 without a meaningful structural cost reduction is not credible**.

We are in an acute competitiveness crisis, not a normal bargaining round. German factory costs are too high, European demand is shrinking, and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target. If we respond with only temporary measures, internal redeployment, or cosmetic savings, we will still be carrying an uncompetitive cost base into 2030. That is unacceptable.

Where I agree:
- We do need a negotiated settlement, not a unilateral diktat.
- We do need to avoid destroying value through chaos or prolonged industrial conflict.
- We do need investment, qualification, and a path to future products.

Where I disagree:
- **No plant closures as a blanket taboo.** If the capacity base is structurally too large, then closures must remain on the table.
- **No absolute exclusion of workforce reductions.** I am not seeking indiscriminate layoffs, but the company cannot rule out headcount reduction as part of a durable restructuring.
- **No extension of old guarantees without a serious countervailing contribution.** Job security is not free if the economics no longer support it.

To the union: I am not dismissing shared sacrifice. But “fair contribution” cannot mean only modest wage restraint while the company continues to absorb excess capacity and cost. A real deal must produce:
- a **durable labour-cost contribution**,
- a **structural capacity reduction**,
- and a **credible path to margin improvement**.

To be specific, management is prepared to consider alternatives to outright closures **only if** they deliver comparable, lasting savings. That could include:
- significant working-time and pay measures,
- materially lower labour costs,
- binding capacity reductions,
- site rationalisation,
- and a verifiable reduction in overheads and fixed cost.

But if the package merely postpones the problem, then it fails. We need an outcome that restores competitiveness and frees resources for technology and product investment through 2030.

So my message is straightforward:
- I am open to negotiated solutions.
- I am not open to a cosmetic peace settlement.
- I am not open to preserving the current cost structure and calling that restructuring.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it needs to bring a **serious, durable alternative** that gets the same economic result. Otherwise, we will have to continue to pursue structural cuts.
