I will respond plainly.

## My position

I agree that the company needs a **durable restructuring**. I do **not** agree that durability automatically requires plant closures or compulsory redundancies in Germany.

As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I am not here to defend the status quo at any price, but I am equally not prepared to let a restructuring destroy the industrial base in our state or sideline our co-determination rights. That is a line I will not cross.

## Where I agree with management, family, and investors
You are right on the diagnosis that the company has a real competitiveness problem:
- German cost levels are too high,
- capacity is under pressure,
- the return-on-sales target is being missed,
- and a purely cosmetic package would be irresponsible.

I also agree that a prolonged conflict would damage the company, the workforce, and the region. No one benefits from a strike spiral or governance deadlock.

## Where I disagree strongly
I do **not** accept the framing that:
- closures are the “only serious” answer,
- compulsory redundancies must remain on the table as a normal instrument,
- or that the only credible restructuring is one that breaks site security and weakens trust.

That is too narrow and too risky.

The works council and union are right about one central issue: workers cannot be asked to absorb the full adjustment while management and owners preserve all options. And the state is right that closures in Germany would be politically and economically explosive, especially given the governance structure and the company’s role in the region.

## To management
You keep asking for “comparable, durable savings.” Then be specific enough for the board and shareholders to assess.

What I need to see is:
- a quantified savings gap,
- a breakdown of where savings are expected to come from,
- and a serious comparison of closure versus non-closure measures.

If non-closure measures truly cannot close the gap, then show that transparently. But if the closure option is being used mainly because it is administratively simple or strategically convenient, I will not support it.

Before any closure discussion, management must show it has exhausted:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint,
- internal redeployment,
- reduction of outsourcing,
- overhead cuts at management level,
- investment reallocation,
- and site-by-site capacity balancing.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence on protecting jobs, sites, and co-determination. I also support your rejection of compulsory redundancies as a first response.

But I need to be equally direct: saying “no closures, no layoffs” is not, by itself, a complete restructuring plan. If the company truly needs structural change, then labor will have to contribute in a way that is real, but it must be **fair, reversible where possible, and tied to guarantees**.

That means I would consider:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus reductions or deferred variable pay,
- working-time adjustments,
- flexibility agreements,
- natural attrition,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and possibly a jointly financed transformation contribution.

What I will **not** support is a package where employees give up income and security while the company gives them no binding future in return.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right that long-term value matters. But long-term value is not protected by a scorched-earth restructuring that weakens the company’s social foundation and triggers years of conflict.

A settlement that destroys trust, undermines labor peace, and damages the regional ecosystem will create its own costs:
- lower morale,
- weaker productivity,
- political backlash,
- and implementation risk.

So if you want my support for hard restructuring, it must be one that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable.

## My threshold
I can support a negotiated package if it meets all three of these conditions:

1. **Real competitiveness gains**
   - measurable structural savings
   - lower fixed and labor costs
   - credible capacity adjustment

2. **Protection of regional industrial strength**
   - no plant closures in the state as a first resort
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - site and job security wherever possible

3. **A fair burden-sharing deal**
   - management contributes
   - owners contribute
   - employees contribute
   - investment and future products are secured

## What I will not accept
I will not support:
- a fait accompli,
- closures presented as unavoidable before alternatives are fully tested,
- a deal that guts jobs in the state,
- or any restructuring that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not negotiable from my side.

## Where I am prepared to move
I am prepared to support a **serious negotiated restructuring** if it is built on:
- reversible and phased measures,
- clear numbers,
- binding investment commitments,
- a credible path to competitiveness,
- and protection of the regional industrial base.

But if management insists that closures and compulsory redundancies are the starting point, then it should expect resistance from the state as shareholder and political actor.

We are ready to help solve the problem. We are not ready to approve a solution that creates a bigger one for the region.
