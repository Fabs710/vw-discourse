Let me be equally direct in round 4.

I have listened carefully, and I agree with one important point from the employee side: this cannot be a cosmetic deal. A short-term pause that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive would be unacceptable to me as well.

But I disagree with the core premise you keep repeating: that **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and intact job guarantees through 2030** can coexist with a genuinely durable competitiveness reset. At this stage, you have not shown that.

## Where I agree

I agree that:
- the company needs hard numbers, not slogans;
- trust was damaged when the old guarantees ended;
- a destructive conflict would harm value;
- employee contributions should not be one-sided;
- and any settlement must be implementable, not just politically convenient.

I also agree that a review gate must not be a sham. If we use phases, they must be real and measurable.

## Where I disagree strongly

### To the works council
You say “every German plant must stay open” and “no compulsory redundancies” are red lines.

I understand that position. I do **not** accept it as the basis for a viable restructuring.

If every site is automatically protected regardless of economics, then we are not doing restructuring — we are preserving overcapacity and hoping the market fixes itself. It will not.

You keep asking me to prove that closures are necessary. Fair enough. But you have not yet produced a non-closure alternative that is economically equivalent. Temporary wage restraint, bonus sacrifice, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and reduced outsourcing may help, but they do not automatically close a structural gap in a company with too much fixed cost and too much capacity.

### To the union
I hear your willingness to discuss temporary concessions. That is welcome.

But temporary concessions are not enough if the underlying problem is structural. If the package does not deliver lasting labor-cost reduction, lasting capacity reduction, and lower fixed costs, then it will simply buy time.

And let me be blunt: saying “voluntary only” does not answer the economic question. Voluntary exits, early retirement, and attrition are useful tools, but they are often too slow and too small if the company needs a real reset now.

### To the state government
I respect the state’s role and its co-determination position. But the state cannot turn its political leverage into a veto against necessary restructuring.

You say closures are not first resort. I agree. But if the economics still do not work after tested alternatives, then they cannot be ruled out in advance. That would be governance by slogan, not responsibility.

### To the family holding and investors
I agree with you on the need for durability and on the danger of a hollow compromise.

Where I push back is the idea that management must first prove closures are the only path before anyone accepts hard measures. In a restructuring crisis, management is allowed to act on credible economic judgment. We are not required to wait until the cost problem becomes irreversible.

That said, I am still prepared to do exactly what I said before: put the numbers on the table and test the non-closure options seriously.

## The real issue: economic equivalence

This is the threshold that matters.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then I need a package that is **economically equivalent**, not just morally preferable.

That means all of the following:
- durable structural labor-cost reduction;
- measurable capacity reduction;
- lower fixed costs;
- higher utilization;
- credible implementation beyond one bargaining round;
- and a path back to margin recovery.

Anything less is not enough.

## What I need next

I want a board-usable package with the following elements:

1. **Exact savings gap**
   - labor cost
   - overhead
   - procurement/logistics
   - capacity/utilization
   - site-by-site impact

2. **Non-closure measures quantified**
   - what they save
   - how long they last
   - and why they are sufficient or insufficient

3. **Structural measures quantified**
   - voluntary exits
   - early retirement
   - internal transfer
   - working-time changes
   - outsourcing reduction
   - overhead cuts
   - site adjustments

4. **A realistic timeline**
   - what happens in the first 12–18 months
   - what is fixed for 2030
   - what triggers a review

5. **A credible commitment from the employee side**
   - not just “no closures” and “no layoffs”
   - but a concrete labor-cost contribution of sufficient scale

## On the proposed compromise formulas

### 1) Phased restructuring with a binding protection window
I can support a phase if it produces immediate and measurable savings.

But I will not support a phase that locks in all sites and all current structures while asking the company to wait and hope. A review gate must be genuine, and it must allow harder action if the numbers fail.

### 2) No forced redundancies, voluntary capacity reduction
This is acceptable only if it is sufficient in aggregate.

If voluntary tools really can close the gap, then show it. If they cannot, then “voluntary only” is not a solution — it is a refusal to confront the economics.

### 3) Site guarantees tied to productivity and investment
I am open to binding investment commitments and product allocation.

I am **not** open to site guarantees that make restructuring impossible regardless of performance. If milestones are real, they must mean something. If they never permit adjustment, then they are just another freeze.

## Bottom line

Here is my position, unchanged:

- I support a negotiated settlement.
- I support employee contribution.
- I support management and owner contribution.
- I support investment in future products and technology.
- I do **not** support a deal that preserves every German site unchanged and leaves the cost base structurally broken.
- I do **not** accept “no closures, no forced redundancies” as an absolute answer.
- I do **not** accept a cosmetic peace deal.

So let me be clear: if the employee side wants to rule out closures, it must bring a package that closes the gap in economic terms. Not symbolic terms. Not political terms. Economic terms.

That is the only basis on which we can proceed.
