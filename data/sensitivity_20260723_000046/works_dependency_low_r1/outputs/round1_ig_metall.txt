We agree with the works council on the essentials: plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable starting point.

The burden of a strategic and management failure cannot simply be handed to the workforce. Workers have already absorbed years of pressure, productivity demands, and uncertainty. If the company now needs a restructuring, then it must be a shared-sacrifice settlement, not a unilateral cutback program.

Our position is:

- No compulsory redundancies
- No plant closures
- No dismantling of the collective wage framework
- No open-ended job insecurity
- Any cost contribution must be fairly shared across labor, management, and owners

We are prepared to discuss real contribution from employees, but only if it is part of a balanced package. That could include:
- temporary wage restraint or partial renunciation of bonuses
- working-time flexibility
- capacity reduction through negotiated, reversible measures
- internal redeployment and qualification
- a jointly financed transition or future-investment contribution

What we will not accept is a deal where employees give up pay, protections, and jobs while management preserves all its leeway and ownership is spared. That is not restructuring; that is shifting the crisis downward.

If management wants durability, it needs to bring something credible in return:
- binding site guarantees
- protection against forced layoffs
- clear investment commitments for the German plants
- a real contribution from the company side, not just rhetoric about “competitiveness”

We are aligned with the works council that negotiations must focus on alternatives to closures. If management insists on plant shutdowns or mass layoffs as the first resort, then it should expect strong resistance from us, including industrial action. The peace obligation is over, and our members expect us to defend their interests decisively.

So our message is clear: we are ready for a tough settlement, but only one that protects jobs, sites, and the collective bargaining framework while sharing the pain fairly.
