### Core impasse
The central impasse is between **job/site preservation as a hard red line** on one side and **management/investor demands for structurally durable cost and capacity reduction** on the other.

More specifically:
- **Works council / union / state**: insist on no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies, with strong site and job guarantees.
- **Management / investors / family**: insist that temporary flexibility alone is insufficient and that the gap requires irreversible structural savings, likely including capacity reduction and possibly closures or layoffs.
- **Shared blockage**: no side currently accepts the other side’s definition of what counts as “durable” restructuring.

### Three possible compromise positions
These are not endorsements, but potential bridge formulas for further negotiation:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a hard review gate
- Immediate package of temporary labor measures: working-time reductions, bonus restraint, redeployment, overtime reduction, outsourcing cuts.
- A jointly agreed savings target and a defined review date.
- Only if the target is not met by the review date do more intrusive measures come back into scope.
- In exchange, employee-side gets **binding interim site and employment protection** during the first phase.

#### 2) No forced redundancies, but voluntary capacity reduction
- Commit to **no compulsory redundancies** for a fixed period.
- Capacity reduction is handled through **voluntary severance, early retirement, internal transfer, natural attrition, and retirement-linked measures**.
- Plant-level adjustments are allowed only if paired with **investment commitments** and a negotiated “site future” package.

#### 3) Site guarantees tied to productivity and investment milestones
- German plants remain open for a defined horizon, but each site accepts **measurable productivity and cost targets**.
- Management and owners commit to **specific investment and product-allocation promises**.
- If milestones are met, sites receive extended protection; if not, the parties reopen bargaining over limited structural adjustments.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the works council
If management proves, with numbers, that temporary wage restraint and working-time measures still leave a significant structural gap, **what specific non-closure measures would you accept that are durable enough to close that gap without compulsory redundancies?**

#### To the union
If the company requires a lasting cost reset, **which concrete employee-side concessions are you prepared to trade for binding job and site guarantees, and for how long?**

#### To group and brand management
Before insisting on closures, **what exact savings gap remains after you have quantified labor cost, overhead, procurement, capacity, and investment-allocation measures separately?**

#### To the regional state government
If you reject closures and forced redundancies, **what restructuring measures would you explicitly support as economically credible, and what would your red line be if those measures are insufficient?**

#### To the controlling family holding
If you want durability but also want to avoid a destructive conflict, **what level of binding job, site, and investment security are you prepared to authorize in exchange for employee concessions?**

#### To institutional investors
If a negotiated package delivers measurable savings but avoids closures, **what minimum economic indicators would you require to consider the deal credible and value-protective?**
