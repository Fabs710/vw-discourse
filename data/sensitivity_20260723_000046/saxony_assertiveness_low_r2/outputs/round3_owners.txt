We can now be candid.

## My position
The family holding supports a **serious restructuring**, not a symbolic one. We do **not** support preserving an uncompetitive cost base simply to avoid hard decisions. That would destroy long-term value.

At the same time, we do **not** support a value-destructive industrial war. A prolonged conflict, strike escalation, or reputational damage would hurt the company, the workforce, and the owners. So the only acceptable path is a **durable, economically credible settlement**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the expert on the core diagnosis:
- German costs are too high.
- Overcapacity is real.
- The current margin problem cannot be solved by slogans or cosmetic compromise.
- Voluntary tools, redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility should be part of the package.
- Management, owners, and employees all need to contribute.

I also agree with the employee side on one important point:
- a solution that detonates labour peace and trust would be deeply value-destructive.

## Where I disagree

### To the works council
Your position is too absolute.

You say:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- full guarantees through 2030,
- all German sites must remain open.

That is not yet an economically credible answer. It may be understandable from a workforce perspective, but it does not show how the company restores competitiveness if the gap is truly structural.

The expert was clear: internal measures can work **only if** they remove enough fixed cost and capacity. You have not yet demonstrated that your preferred package does that at the necessary scale and speed.

So I do **not** accept a blanket no-closure rule as a governance position.

### To the union
Your willingness to discuss temporary concessions is more constructive, but temporary measures alone are not enough if they do not translate into durable savings.

Deferred pay, bonus moderation, and working-time flexibility can help. But if the company remains structurally overloaded and underutilized, then the problem remains. A real contribution must be **material**, not merely rhetorical.

### To management
I support your insistence that the company needs a real cost reset. But I will not back a strategy that treats closures and compulsory redundancies as the only serious answer without proving why internal measures are insufficient.

You still need to present a **site-by-site quantified plan**:
- current utilization,
- savings from internal measures,
- savings from consolidation or shift reduction,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and the recurring margin uplift.

Without that, “closures are on the table” is leverage, not strategy.

### To the regional state government
I respect the state’s role and the importance of the industrial base. But “no closures ever” is not a workable governance principle if the economics prove otherwise.

The state should support a durable settlement, not lock the company into a frozen footprint regardless of economics. That would protect appearance, not value.

## What would move us
The family holding can support a package if it meets three tests:

1. **It restores margins and competitiveness in a durable way**
2. **It avoids prolonged instability and labour conflict**
3. **It is economically quantified, site by site**

That means we need an actual comparison of scenarios:
- no closure plus internal measures,
- consolidation/shift reduction plus voluntary exits,
- closure or site rationalization.

Not general statements. Numbers.

## My view on closures
Let me be plain:
- I do **not** want closures as the first answer.
- I do **not** want them used as a bargaining reflex.
- But I will **not rule them out in advance** if the numbers show they are necessary to remove durable overcapacity and restore competitiveness.

That is the hard truth. Sentiment cannot substitute for arithmetic.

## My view on guarantees
Blanket guarantees through 2030 are too rigid if they block necessary adjustment. A guarantee can only be supported if it is part of a package that truly fixes the cost base.

## My view on employee contribution
A fair contribution from the workforce is possible, but it must be meaningful:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary pay moderation,
- internal redeployment,
- and capacity reduction.

What is not acceptable is a package where labour gives nothing material while asking owners to absorb the entire burden.

## What I need next
We are now at the point where the discussion needs **hard numbers**, not further repetition of positions.

## Bottom line
The family holding is prepared to support a serious restructuring path. But it must be:
- economically credible,
- durable,
- and not a disguised preservation of the status quo.

If the no-closure route can truly restore competitive margins, prove it.  
If it cannot, then we will not block necessary structural action merely because it is unpopular.
