1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties agree the company faces a serious competitiveness and margin problem.
- There is broad support for a negotiated solution rather than open-ended conflict.
- Voluntary instruments are preferred over compulsory measures where possible.
- Qualification, redeployment, and internal adjustment measures are seen as part of the toolbox.
- There is shared recognition that any solution must be durable and economically credible.
- Most sides accept that management, owners, and other stakeholders should contribute, not only employees.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, and regional state government reject them; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state oppose them; management and investors do not rule them out if savings are insufficient.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030:** Employee side wants binding guarantees; management and investors say blanket guarantees may block needed adjustment.
- **Scope of employee contribution:** Management and capital side want a substantial, structural contribution; employee side accepts only a balanced package with strong protections and limits.
- **Role of co-determination/state influence:** State and workforce want a strong role; management and investors want flexibility to implement restructuring.

3) **Key trade-offs still unaddressed**
- How much cost reduction is required, and from which levers it will come.
- Whether equivalent savings can be achieved through working-time, pay, voluntary exits, and redeployment instead of closures.
- What level of site protection is compatible with meaningful capacity reduction.
- How to balance short-term labor peace and political stability against long-term competitiveness.
- What concrete investment commitments or future product allocations can accompany concessions.
- What would count as a sufficiently “balanced” package for each side.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from position statements to numbers and scenarios: quantify the savings target, identify which measures produce them, and show what is being exchanged for what.
- Ask each side to specify its minimum acceptable package and its non-negotiables in operational terms.
- Test alternative combinations: voluntary exits, working-time adjustments, internal redeployment, pay elements, and targeted capacity changes.
- Clarify whether a package can preserve major German sites while still delivering the required competitiveness gains.
