## Our position
As capital-market shareholders, we support a **serious restructuring that restores competitive margins**. We do **not** support a cosmetic compromise, and we do **not** support a deal that leaves the cost base structurally impaired.

What matters to us is simple:
- durable fixed-cost reduction,
- better capacity utilization,
- credible savings,
- and a clear path back to target margins.

If the package does not do that, we will not back it.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the specialist on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and the current margin problem is structural, not temporary.

We also agree with the union and works council on one important point:
- prolonged conflict is value-destructive.

A strike, escalation, or endless uncertainty would hurt earnings, delay investment, and depress valuation. So yes, we want a negotiated settlement.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full guarantees through 2030” position is **not acceptable to us as an endpoint** unless you can prove the same economic result through other means.

The specialist was clear: a no-closure package can work only if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity. You have not yet shown that your preferred framework can do that at the required scale.

So we do **not** accept:
- an absolute ban on closures,
- a blanket rejection of compulsory redundancies in all circumstances,
- or guarantees that freeze the company into an uncompetitive structure.

Those positions may be understandable socially, but they are not enough financially.

### To the union
Your willingness to discuss temporary concessions is more constructive. But temporary measures alone do not solve a structural cost problem.

Bonus moderation, deferred pay, and working-time flexibility are useful only if they translate into:
- lower recurring cost,
- lower headcount where needed,
- and better utilization.

If they merely postpone the problem, we will not support them.

### To management
We agree that you need real restructuring, not symbolism. But you still have not provided the one thing investors need: a **site-by-site quantified case**.

We want to see:
- current utilization,
- the savings from internal measures,
- the savings from consolidation or shift removal,
- the one-time restructuring costs,
- and the recurring margin uplift.

Until that is on the table, “closures remain possible” is not a strategy; it is leverage.

### To the regional state government
We respect your role, but we do not accept a governance position that effectively makes closures impossible regardless of economics.

Protecting the industrial base is not the same as preserving every current site structure. If a site is structurally uneconomic, then keeping it open to avoid political discomfort destroys value.

### To the controlling family
We agree that cosmetic compromise is worthless. But the family should be careful not to endorse a restructuring that is too soft to work.

Long-term value is not protected by avoiding hard decisions. It is protected by making the right hard decisions early enough.

## What we need
We need a package that delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Meaningful fixed-cost removal**
3. **A real employee contribution**
4. **Clear, recurring savings**
5. **No open-ended delay**
6. **A credible margin recovery path**

That does **not** mean we insist on plant closures. It does mean we insist on an outcome that is economically equivalent to the problem.

## Our practical view
A no-closure solution is acceptable **only if** it can be shown to:
- remove enough fixed cost,
- reduce capacity enough,
- and restore utilization enough
to close the margin gap.

If it cannot, then consolidation, rationalization, or closure must remain on the table.

## Our bottom line
We are not on the side of labour versus management. We are on the side of **value creation**.

So our position is:
- **Yes** to a negotiated settlement,
- **Yes** to voluntary measures if they are large enough,
- **Yes** to internal capacity reduction if it is real,
- **No** to a cosmetic social compromise,
- **No** to any package that leaves margins structurally impaired.
