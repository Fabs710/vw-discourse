We have enough to move beyond slogans.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the state on the core diagnosis: this company has a real competitiveness problem, and “business as usual” is not a strategy. German fixed costs, overcapacity, and weak margins cannot be waved away. If we do nothing material, we will destroy value over time.

I also agree with the employee side on one important point: a prolonged industrial conflict would be deeply value-destructive. It would hurt earnings, delay investment, damage the brand, and poison labour relations for years. That is not in the interest of the owners either.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the current employee position as a sufficient answer.

### To the Works Council
You are demanding:
- every German plant stays open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- job security through 2030,
- and only voluntary or reversible instruments.

That may be understandable politically, but economically it is not yet a plan. If it blocks the company from achieving the necessary structural savings, then it merely preserves the problem.

I also do not accept the implication that closures are never legitimate under any circumstances. If a site is genuinely subscale and the savings gap cannot otherwise be closed, then closures cannot be ruled out in advance by fiat.

### To the Union
Your willingness to discuss temporary concessions is useful, but temporary measures alone are not enough if the company needs durable structural relief. Deferred bonuses and short-term moderation may help the optics, but they do not necessarily solve the unit-cost problem.

If labour is serious about a balanced package, then the contribution must be real:
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reduction,
- meaningful cost moderation,
- voluntary exits where needed,
- and a willingness to accept that some structures may need to change permanently.

### To Management
I support your insistence that the company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one. But I also want to be clear: simply keeping closures and compulsory redundancies on the table is not yet a solution. It is leverage, not a settlement.

If management wants owner backing, it must show a quantified plan that distinguishes:
- what savings come from labour cost,
- what savings come from utilization improvement,
- what savings come from simplification,
- and what the consequences are site by site.

Threats without a credible operating model will not get my support.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect your stewardship role and your concern for the industrial base. But I cannot support a position that effectively turns the state’s veto into a permanent freeze on economically necessary change.

Protecting industrial capability is not the same as protecting every current site structure. The company’s long-term value matters too, and it must be able to adapt.

## What would move us
The family holding is prepared to support a package only if it is both economically meaningful and socially containable.

That means:

1. **A quantified savings target**
   We need an explicit number, not generalities. How much annual cost reduction is required to restore margins?

2. **A site-by-site utilisation and cost case**
   If a closure is being discussed, management must demonstrate why internal alternatives do not suffice.

3. **A material employee contribution**
   Not symbolic. Real labour-cost and capacity relief.

4. **Priority for voluntary measures**
   Redeployment, attrition, working-time changes, qualification, and voluntary exits first.

5. **No open-ended instability**
   A deal that simply postpones the fight is not acceptable.

6. **Investment and future model commitments**
   If labour is asked to contribute, the company must commit to future industrial allocation in Germany.

## My position on closures
I do not want closures as the first answer. A closure is a blunt instrument and can destroy know-how, provoke conflict, and create reputational damage.

But I will also say plainly: I will not rule them out if the numbers show that they are the only credible way to restore competitiveness. The key is whether management can prove that equivalent savings cannot be achieved by internal restructuring.

## My position on job guarantees
Blanket guarantees through 2030 are too rigid if they prevent necessary adjustment. I can support site stability and protection of the industrial base, but not an unconditional freeze of the company’s ability to restructure.

## My bottom line
The family holding will back a serious restructuring, but not one of these two extremes:
- not a cosmetic compromise that changes nothing,
- and not a reckless conflict that destroys value.

So the test is simple:
- show us the numbers,
- show us the site-specific economics,
- show us the savings path,
- and show us that the workforce contribution is real.

If that can be done without closures, good. If not, then we will not block necessary structural action merely because it is unpopular.
