## Neutral moderator summary — end of Round 3

### 1) What has been agreed
Across the parties, there is broad agreement that:

- the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**
- a **cosmetic compromise** would not be sufficient
- **prolonged conflict** would harm value, operations, and employment
- any solution should involve a mix of:
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - working-time flexibility
  - overhead simplification
  - management/owner/shareholder contribution
- the discussion now needs to move from general positions to **quantified, site-level analysis**

There is also agreement that the expert’s framing is relevant: a no-closure route is only viable if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity to restore margins.

---

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The central unresolved disputes are:

- **Plant closures**
  - Employee-side actors and the state reject closures as a first answer and, in some cases, as a category.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out in advance if the economics require them.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee-side actors oppose them categorically.
  - Management and capital-side actors want them to remain available if voluntary measures are insufficient.

- **Job-security guarantees through 2030**
  - Employee-side actors view binding guarantees as essential.
  - Management, family holding, and investors view unconditional guarantees as too restrictive if they block restructuring.

- **Scope of employee contribution**
  - Employee-side actors are willing to discuss temporary and reversible concessions.
  - Capital-side actors insist the contribution must be materially large and recurring enough to deliver durable savings.

- **Whether internal measures are sufficient**
  - Employee-side actors say this has not been disproven and that closures are therefore not justified.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say the burden remains on the employee side to show that a no-closure package can deliver the required economics.

---

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved the following trade-offs:

- **Economic credibility vs. social protection**
  - How to achieve enough recurring savings without undermining job security and trust.

- **Speed vs. acceptability**
  - Whether a slower, more consensual adjustment can deliver savings fast enough.

- **Site preservation vs. footprint optimization**
  - Whether all German sites can remain open while still achieving the needed margin improvement.

- **Temporary concessions vs. durable structural change**
  - Whether labor-side concessions are sufficient if they are limited to temporary measures.

- **One-time restructuring cost vs. long-term savings**
  - The parties agree this matters, but no scenario comparison has yet been agreed.

- **Governance and co-determination vs. executive flexibility**
  - The state and employee side want stronger binding role protections; management and investors want more latitude to act on economics.

---

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should narrow to a **scenario-based comparison**, not general argument.

Specifically, the parties should be asked to compare three options:

1. **No-closure internal measures**
2. **Consolidation / shift reduction + voluntary exits**
3. **Closure / rationalization**

For each option, the discussion should focus on:

- annual recurring savings
- one-time restructuring costs
- utilization effects
- headcount effects
- impact on the margin target through 2030
- conditions under which the option would be viable

### Procedural guidance for next round
A useful next-step question would be:

> “Under realistic volume and utilization assumptions, which scenario actually closes the margin gap, and what concessions or guarantees would each side need to accept for it to be implementable?”

If you want, I can also turn this into a **one-paragraph chair’s statement** or a **bullet-point agenda for Round 4**.
