From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the key test is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins and protect risk-adjusted equity value?** If yes, we can support difficult measures. If not, we will not back a cosmetic settlement.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, and to a degree with the family, on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- the brand is under margin pressure,
- and a return to competitive profitability requires structural action, not slogans.

We also agree that **prolonged conflict is value-destructive**. A drawn-out strike, a stalemate, or a half-measure that merely defers the problem would be damaging for earnings, cash flow, and the valuation multiple.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a “restructure in principle, but not in practice” approach.

To the works council and union: insisting on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no meaningful labour-cost adjustment** is not a credible answer if the current structure is genuinely uncompetitive. If the result is that margins remain structurally impaired, that is not a workable outcome for shareholders. We cannot endorse preserving every site and every guarantee if the company continues to miss its return-on-sales target.

To management: we also will not support a strategy that relies on confrontation as a substitute for a disciplined plan. Simply threatening closures and job cuts is not enough. If management wants investor support, it must show:
- quantified savings,
- a clear capacity rationalization plan,
- a realistic implementation timeline,
- and evidence that the restructuring will actually translate into margin improvement, not just one-off disruption.

To the regional state government and family: we understand the desire to avoid industrial damage, but that cannot become a veto over necessary restructuring. Any solution that protects headlines while leaving the cost base intact would destroy long-term shareholder value.

### What we need to see
We would support a package only if it contains all of the following:
1. **Durable structural cost reduction**
2. **Credible capacity cuts**
3. **A meaningful employee contribution**
4. **No open-ended delay**
5. **Clear delivery of margin improvement within a defined timeframe**

We do not insist that every adjustment must come through plant closures. If comparable savings can be achieved through working-time changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, pay moderation, and capacity consolidation, that is fine. But the economics must be real. The burden cannot sit entirely on shareholders through lower returns while labor protections remain unchanged.

### Our bottom line
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to voluntary instruments if they deliver enough savings.
- **Yes** to site protections only if they do not prevent necessary capacity rationalization.
- **No** to a cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that erodes margins further.

We are flexible on the method, not on the outcome. The outcome must restore competitiveness, improve cash generation, and support a credible return to target margins.
