I agree with the works council on the core red lines: no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral breaking of hard-won job security. Management cannot solve a strategic problem by pushing the whole burden onto the workforce.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- We will defend the collective wage framework.
- We will defend jobs and sites.
- We will not accept a restructuring that turns employees into the sole shock absorber while owners and management remain untouched.
- And we are prepared to use industrial action if that is what it takes.

That said, we are not refusing change. We already signaled openness to a fair contribution from employees if, and only if, it comes as part of a balanced package. That can include:
- temporary wage moderation or waived bonuses,
- working-time and capacity adjustments,
- voluntary instruments rather than forced layoffs,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a binding commitment to future investment and model allocation.

What would be unacceptable is a “restructuring” that means:
- closures first, talks later;
- layoffs presented as inevitable;
- wage cuts without guarantees;
- or open-ended insecurity dressed up as competitiveness.

If management genuinely wants a durable solution, then it must put something real on the table:
1. binding site guarantees,
2. no compulsory redundancies,
3. a credible industrial plan for the German plants,
4. and shared sacrifice from management, owners, and shareholders, not just from workers.

We are not here to save a failed strategy at workers’ expense. We are here to force a fair settlement that protects industrial jobs and gives the company a future. If that is not the basis, then there will be resistance.
