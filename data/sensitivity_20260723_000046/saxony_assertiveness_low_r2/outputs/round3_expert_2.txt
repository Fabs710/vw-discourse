## My assessment, in plain terms
- **No-closure + internal measures:** plausible, but often only enough for a **moderate** cost gap.
- **Consolidation / shift reduction + voluntary exits:** usually the **most realistic compromise** if the objective is material savings without full plant closure.
- **Closure / rationalization:** delivers the **largest recurring savings**, but also the highest one-time cost and execution risk.

## What “enough” looks like
For a large carmaker under this kind of pressure:

- **Recurring savings below ~€250m/year:** usually too small
- **€250m–€500m/year:** borderline; depends on the gap to target
- **€500m–€800m/year:** potentially sufficient for a meaningful turnaround
- **Above ~€800m/year:** likely needs footprint rationalization or very deep consolidation

## Practical conclusion
If the company’s margin gap is truly structural and large, then:

- a **pure no-closure package** is **unlikely** to be enough unless it includes:
  - substantial headcount reduction,
  - shift elimination,
  - overhead cuts,
  - and real utilization improvement.
