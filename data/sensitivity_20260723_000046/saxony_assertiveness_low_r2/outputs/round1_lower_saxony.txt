As the regional state government and an anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: we do not support a restructuring strategy that solves competitiveness by hollowing out the company’s German footprint.

I do agree on one point with management: the company clearly needs a credible path back to competitiveness. Ignoring cost pressure, overcapacity, or the investment needs of the transition would be irresponsible. We are not here to defend inertia.

But I strongly disagree with the idea that plant closures, forced redundancies, or the erosion of long-standing job-security commitments are the default answer. For us, this is not just a labor issue; it is a regional economic and political issue. These plants are anchors for entire value chains, supplier networks, and communities. Once lost, that damage is hard to reverse.

So my position is:

- **No outcome that shuts German plants lightly or uses closures as the first tool**
- **No compulsory redundancies if alternatives exist**
- **No sidelining of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **Yes to a serious restructuring, but only if it protects sites, jobs, and the company’s long-term industrial base in Germany**

I also want to respond to the employee side. Your insistence on social responsibility, no forced layoffs, and a negotiated path is legitimate. I share that basic direction. But I would caution against treating the current structure as untouchable. If the company’s economics have genuinely deteriorated, then some adjustment in capacity, working time, internal deployment, or pay components may be necessary. The key question is whether that adjustment is designed to **preserve the industrial core** or to dismantle it.

What would move us is a package that includes:
- binding commitments to keep the major German sites open,
- a negotiated reduction in overcapacity through internal measures before any closure is contemplated,
- voluntary rather than compulsory workforce reductions,
- targeted labor-cost contributions that are time-limited and reversible,
- concrete investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany,
- and a restructuring framework that preserves the state’s role as a co-determining shareholder.

What will not move us is a proposal that says “accept closures now, and trust us on the rest later.” That is not a credible basis for agreement.

So I am not giving management a blank check. If the company wants the state’s support, it must present a restructuring that is economically serious **and** socially and regionally responsible. If it does not, we will use the leverage we have.
