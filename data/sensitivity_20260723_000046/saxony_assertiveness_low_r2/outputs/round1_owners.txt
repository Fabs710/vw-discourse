We are aligned on one central point: the company needs a credible, durable path back to competitiveness. If the German cost base stays structurally out of line, then the long-term value of the enterprise is damaged. That is not acceptable to us as controlling owners.

But I want to be equally clear about the boundary conditions:

- We do **not** support a value-destroying strategy of prolonged conflict.
- We do **not** support a cosmetic deal that preserves every structure unchanged while delaying the problem.
- We do **not** support unnecessary plant closures if a credible alternative can achieve the same economics.
- And we do **not** accept any outcome that permanently weakens the company’s governance control or industrial base.

So, responding directly:

### To management
You are right that “business as usual” is not an option. I agree that overcapacity, high fixed costs, and weak margins require real action.

Where I disagree is the implication that closures and forced redundancies should be treated as the default starting point. As controlling owners, we judge this by long-term value, not by the shortest path to headline savings. A closure may save cost on paper, but if it triggers durable labor conflict, reputational damage, political backlash, and erosion of know-how, then it can destroy more value than it creates.

What I would need to see from management is:
- a credible quantified restructuring plan,
- a clear demonstration that alternatives to closure were genuinely exhausted,
- and a package that restores margins without triggering a self-inflicted industrial war.

If management cannot show that, then I will not back a hard line simply because it is hard.

### To the works council
I respect the red lines you have set, especially on social stability and no reckless destruction of productive capacity. But I cannot endorse a position that effectively says every German site must remain untouched regardless of economics.

That would not be stewardship. It would be denial.

If the company is to remain strong, the workforce will need to contribute in a meaningful way. That can include working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, temporary concessions, and internal capacity adjustments. But it must be negotiated in a way that preserves the core industrial base and protects the company’s future.

### To the union
I hear your willingness to discuss a fair contribution if the package is balanced. That is the right framework. But “balanced” must mean more than symbolic management statements. It must produce real structural relief.

We would support:
- voluntary instruments before compulsory ones,
- redeployment and qualification,
- time-limited labor-cost measures,
- and investment commitments tied to measurable competitiveness gains.

We would not support indefinite guarantees that eliminate all adjustment capacity while expecting owners to absorb the economic consequences.

### To the regional state government
Your role as anchor shareholder and regional steward is important, and I understand the political sensitivity. But the company cannot be governed as though preservation of every current location were the only criterion.

We agree that German industrial capability matters. However, that capability is preserved by a company that can earn its cost of capital, not by protecting every structure from change. If the state wants to protect the industrial base, it should support a solution that restores competitiveness, not block all hard decisions in advance.

### Our position
The family holding is prepared to support a serious restructuring, but only if it meets these conditions:

1. **Durable margin improvement**
2. **A meaningful employee contribution**
3. **Priority to voluntary and negotiated measures**
4. **Protection of the company’s core industrial capabilities in Germany**
5. **No open-ended instability or governance erosion**

We are not here to force a particular tactic. We are here to ensure the outcome restores the company’s competitiveness and long-term value.

So I will say plainly: if the choice is between a weak compromise that preserves every site on paper but leaves the company uncompetitive, and a reckless closure strategy that creates lasting conflict and value destruction, we reject both extremes. We want a restructuring that is economically real, socially containable, and durable.

That is the standard against which we will judge any proposal.
