We appreciate the added clarity from the specialist. It sharpens the real issue: this is not about slogans, but about whether the package removes enough fixed cost and underutilized capacity to restore margins.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, and the expert on the diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and a cosmetic deal would be value-destructive.

We also agree with the union and works council on one practical point:
- a prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would hurt earnings, cash flow, and valuation.

So yes, we want a settlement. But it must be a **real restructuring**, not just labor peace at any price.

## Where we disagree
### To the works council
Your absolute position — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full guarantees through 2030, only voluntary and reversible measures — is not financially credible unless you can show the same savings and utilization improvement through other means.

The expert made that clear: if internal measures only shift the pain around without removing enough fixed cost, the company stays uncompetitive. That is unacceptable to us.

We will not support a package that:
- leaves the cost base structurally impaired,
- preserves every site regardless of utilization,
- and turns “no closures” into a veto on necessary economics.

### To the union
Your willingness to discuss concessions is more constructive, but temporary moderation alone is not enough. Deferred bonuses and reversible flexibility may buy time, but they do not necessarily deliver durable margin improvement.

If the package does not produce a material and lasting cost reset, it will just postpone the problem. We will not back that.

### To management
We also are not signing a blank check for confrontation. Threatening closures is not the same as proving that they are needed. Management must quantify:
- how much savings are required,
- what each site contributes,
- what can be removed internally,
- and why a closure or consolidation is or is not necessary.

The expert’s framework makes that mandatory. If management cannot show the numbers, then closure threats are just leverage, not strategy.

### To the regional state government
We respect the state’s role and the value of the industrial base. But the state cannot define success as “no closures ever” if that leaves the enterprise structurally uncompetitive. Protecting industrial capability means backing a company that can earn returns, not freezing a suboptimal footprint.

### To the controlling family
We agree with your core point that cosmetic compromise is worthless. But the family must also support a package that is hard enough to fix the economics. Avoiding conflict is not enough; value is also destroyed if the company exits this process still too expensive.

## What we need to see
We can support a deal only if it delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable structural cost reduction**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **A material employee contribution**
4. **Clear savings within a defined timeframe**
5. **No open-ended delay**
6. **A credible path back to competitive margins**

That does **not** mean we insist on closures as the only acceptable tool. It means the outcome must be economically equivalent to the problem.

## Our position on the no-closure route
The expert said a no-closure package can work if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity. We accept that.

But the burden is now on management and labor to show, with numbers, that a no-closure package can realistically deliver:
- sufficient headcount reduction,
- shift and overhead simplification,
- and a genuine improvement in utilization.

If they can do that, we will consider it.

If they cannot, then the company should stop pretending that a closure-free solution exists at the necessary scale. In that case, some combination of consolidation, site rationalization, or closure will be necessary, and we would not oppose that simply to preserve appearances.

## Our bottom line
We are not aligned with either extreme:
- not a cosmetic social compromise that leaves the company uncompetitive,
- and not a reckless all-out confrontation that destroys value.

We are aligned with **economic reality**.

So our stance is:
- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring,
- **Yes** to voluntary instruments if they are large enough,
- **Yes** to internal capacity reductions if they genuinely reduce fixed cost,
- **No** to a deal that preserves the current structure and calls it reform,
- **No** to prolonged conflict,
- **No** to any outcome that fails to restore margins.
