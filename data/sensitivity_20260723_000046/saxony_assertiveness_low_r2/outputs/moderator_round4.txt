## Where the discussion is stuck
The conflict is no longer about whether the company needs restructuring. All sides now accept that it does.

The impasse is about **proof and sequencing**:

- **Management / capital side** says: internal measures may not be enough, so closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available.
- **Works council / union / state** says: closures and forced layoffs cannot be accepted unless management proves, site by site, that no-closure options fail.
- **Expert** says: the economics must be quantified, and the no-closure route is only viable if it removes enough fixed cost and capacity.

So the real deadlock is:

> **Who must move first: the workforce on protection, or management on proof?**

And beneath that:

> **Can the required savings be achieved without plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or not?**

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible bridging positions

### 1) A phased “proof-first, action-second” restructuring process
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a two-step process with a short, binding data phase followed by a decision phase.

- Phase 1: management provides site-level numbers for all German sites.
- Phase 2: all parties negotiate against those numbers, with closures and forced redundancies not implemented during the proof phase.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It gives the employee side the proof they demand, while allowing management to keep the full toolbox available until the analysis is complete.

**Potential boundary:**  
A clear deadline and an agreed consequence if the numbers show the no-closure path is insufficient.

---

### 2) A “no closure unless threshold is missed” conditional package
**Proposal:**  
Set a predefined economic threshold for the no-closure path.

- If internal measures plus consolidation achieve the target savings and utilization improvements, all sites remain open.
- If the threshold is not met, then a narrower set of fallback measures becomes negotiable.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It converts the closure debate from ideology into a measurable test.

**Potential boundary:**  
The threshold must be agreed in advance and independently verified, otherwise both sides will reject the result.

---

### 3) A protected employment package with deeper internal restructuring
**Proposal:**  
Combine:
- voluntary exits,
- shift reduction,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary compensation measures,

with a temporary moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It gives the workforce security while allowing management to test whether internal measures can close the gap.

**Potential boundary:**  
The package would need strong enforcement and a review clause so it does not become a permanent delay tactic.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a site-by-site quantified plan within a fixed deadline, and suspend closure decisions until that plan has been jointly reviewed?

### To the works council
**Question:**  
If the numbers show that internal measures still leave a material gap, what specific additional adjustment would you accept that does not involve plant closure or compulsory redundancies?

### To the union
**Question:**  
What level of temporary employee contribution would you consider sufficient if it is tied to binding job-security and no-closure guarantees?

### To the regional state government
**Question:**  
Will the state define in advance the conditions under which it would accept consolidation measures, even if they reduce footprint without full plant closures?

### To the controlling family
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to support a binding no-closure review period, even if that delays the option of using closures as leverage?

### To the institutional investors
**Question:**  
Would you accept a consolidation-heavy solution if it delivers the same recurring savings and utilization improvements as closures, but with lower implementation risk?
