I will respond plainly.

I hear the employee side repeating the same red lines: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full job guarantees through 2030, and no structural weakening of protections. I understand why you want that. But I need to be equally clear: **those demands, taken together, do not solve the company’s cost and capacity problem**.

We are not dealing with a temporary dip. We are dealing with a structural competitiveness gap:
- factory costs in Germany are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competitors are faster and cheaper,
- and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we come out of this process with “all sites open, no forced reductions, no real labor-cost contribution, and guarantees to 2030,” then we will have protected the status quo at the expense of the company’s future. I will not accept that.

## Where I agree
I agree on several points:
- A **cosmetic compromise** would be worthless.
- Voluntary tools are preferable to blunt forced measures.
- Qualification, redeployment, and internal adjustment should be part of the package.
- Prolonged conflict is damaging and should be avoided if possible.
- Management, owners, and the company leadership must also contribute.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No plant closures” as an absolute
That is not acceptable. I am not saying closures are the first choice. I am saying they remain on the table if they are needed to remove durable overcapacity and fix a structurally uncompetitive footprint.

You keep speaking as though a closure is merely a political choice. It is not only that. It can be an economic necessity when a site no longer fits the cost base and production structure we need for 2030.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I prefer voluntary solutions. But I will not rule out compulsory measures in advance if the numbers do not work otherwise. If the savings required are substantial, then the instrument must be judged by whether it delivers the necessary structural effect, not by whether it is comfortable.

### 3) Blanket guarantees through 2030
A full, unconditional guarantee through 2030 would freeze the adjustment we need. I cannot sign up to that. A guarantee can only exist if it is tied to a package that produces real and durable cost relief.

### 4) Treating labour contribution as optional or symbolic
The union says a contribution is possible if the package is balanced. Fine. But “balanced” must mean **material savings**, not only deferred bonuses or soft language.

We need a genuine contribution to the cost base:
- on pay,
- on working time,
- on flexibility,
- and on capacity.

If the workforce contribution is too small, then the company still loses.

## To the works council
You say closures are always destructive and should never be used. I reject that. Preserving every German site regardless of economics is not responsible stewardship; it is denial of the scale of the problem.

You also demand that management first exhaust all alternatives. That is exactly what we are trying to do in this process — but alternatives must be measured against a savings target, not just listed in principle. If alternatives do not deliver enough, then they are not a substitute for structural change.

## To the union
You are right that a deal has to be defendable to the workforce. But a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive is not defendable to anyone — including your members, because it endangers the future of their jobs.

I hear your willingness to consider temporary concessions, deferred wage elements, and working-time flexibility. That is the right direction. But I will say directly: **temporary concessions alone are not enough** if they do not translate into durable cost improvement.

## To the regional state government
I respect your role and your concern for the regional industrial base. But your position cannot be that every German site must remain open and then expect the company to magically recover competitiveness.

A strong industrial base is preserved by a company that can invest, innovate, and earn returns. If we avoid hard decisions now, we risk weakening the entire base later. I need the state to help enable restructuring, not merely to constrain it.

## To the controlling family
I welcome your recognition that cosmetic deals are useless. But I want to be clear: supporting “durable competitiveness” means supporting real capacity and cost reduction, not just hoping for improvement while avoiding difficult decisions.

If we cannot deliver enough savings through negotiated means, then we need the freedom to use tougher structural tools. I do not ask for a blank cheque; I ask for alignment around the necessity of real change.

## To investors
You are correct that prolonged conflict destroys value. That is why we need a settlement with substance. But let me stress: value is also destroyed if we stop short of the structural measures required.

So I will not back a package that:
- keeps all sites untouched,
- avoids meaningful labor-cost adjustment,
- relies only on vague promises of future efficiency,
- and leaves the company below target in 2026, 2027, and beyond.

## What management will insist on
We need a package that includes:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **A meaningful labor-cost contribution**
3. **Structural simplification**
4. **Flexible deployment and working-time instruments**
5. **A credible path back to target margins**
6. **Investment capacity for future products and technology to 2030**

I am open on the method. I am not open on the outcome. The outcome must materially reduce the cost base.

## On the numbers
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it needs to show how the same savings are achieved through other means. Not in general terms — in real terms:
- how much cost is saved,
- how much capacity is removed,
- what labor-cost contribution is delivered,
- and how quickly it takes effect.

Without that, “no closures” is simply a veto without an alternative.

## Bottom line
Let me be blunt: I will not sign a deal that preserves the current footprint, protects every guarantee, and leaves the company uncompetitive. That would be a failure of management responsibility.

But I am still prepared to negotiate a serious package if it delivers:
- real structural savings,
- real capacity reduction,
- a real employee contribution,
- and a credible future for the company.

If the employee side wants to avoid plant closures, then it must help produce an equally durable economic answer. Otherwise, the harder route remains unavoidable.
