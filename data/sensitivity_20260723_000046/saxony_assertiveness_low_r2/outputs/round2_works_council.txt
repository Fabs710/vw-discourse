I will respond very directly.

We agree on one thing only: the company faces a serious competitiveness problem and a durable solution is needed. Beyond that, there is a hard line the workforce will not cross.

## What we reject
### 1) Plant closures in Germany
We do **not** accept plant closures as a restructuring tool. Not “as a last resort,” not “for credibility,” not “to show resolve.” Once management normalizes closures, the entire social and industrial fabric is put at risk.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies
We do **not** accept forced layoffs. If the business is being restructured, then the adjustment must come through voluntary and internally managed measures, not through dismissals that destroy trust and security.

### 3) Breaking job-security guarantees
We do **not** accept the dismantling of the historic job-security framework as the entry ticket to talks. Management cannot ask workers to pay for a strategy failure by tearing up protections that were the basis of industrial peace.

## Where we are prepared to talk
We are not saying “nothing can change.” We are saying the burden must be shared fairly and the industrial base must be preserved.

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exits only,
- temporary and reversible measures,
- capacity balancing within the existing German footprint,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

If savings are required, then management must first show that it has exhausted non-destructive alternatives. That means:
- better plant loading,
- product and allocation decisions,
- internal efficiency measures,
- supply-chain and overhead reductions,
- management compensation restraint,
- and genuine owner contribution.

## On the arguments from the other sides

### To management
You keep speaking as if closures are simply the logical consequence of market pressure. They are not. They are a choice. And it is a choice we will resist with all available means.

You also speak as if a “meaningful employee contribution” must mean wage cuts and loss of guarantees. That is not a serious starting point. Workers did not create the strategic missteps, and they will not be the sole shock absorber.

If management wants credibility, then put forward a plan that protects all German sites, excludes compulsory redundancies, and keeps the job-security framework intact. Then we can discuss how to make the company fit for the future.

### To the union
We are aligned on the red lines. But I want to be very clear: any labor contribution must remain limited, temporary, and tied to binding guarantees. No open-ended concessions. No blank cheque. No trading away core protections for promises.

### To the regional state government
Your role matters, but it is not enough to say you oppose closures while leaving the door open to “adjustment” that eventually amounts to the same thing. We need a binding commitment to preserve the sites, not political caution language.

### To the controlling family
You say you want long-term value and want to avoid industrial war. Then use your control to stop management from turning restructuring into a social rupture. The best way to protect value is to keep the workforce, the know-how, and the sites intact. A closure strategy may look clean in a spreadsheet, but it will poison labor relations and damage the company for years.

### To investors
You demand “durable structural cost reduction.” We reject the implication that the only durable solution is to cut jobs and close plants. Durable competitiveness can also come from:
- better product allocation,
- investment in future models,
- smarter industrial planning,
- internal reorganization,
- and a stable, committed workforce.

A package that destroys trust will not restore value; it will create new risks and new costs.

## Our minimum acceptable package
If anyone expects the works council to move, the package must include all of the following:
1. **Binding guarantees that all German plants remain open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Job-security guarantees through 2030**
4. **A real co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
5. **Investment and product commitments for the German sites**
6. **Voluntary and reversible adjustment instruments only**
7. **A fair contribution from management, owners, and shareholders**

Without those elements, there is no basis for agreement.

## On numbers and scenarios
If management wants us to discuss quantified measures, then start with a transparent industrial plan showing:
- how much savings are needed,
- where exactly they come from,
- what is being cut at management level,
- what future investment is secured for each site,
- and why closures would supposedly be necessary when alternatives have not been exhausted.

Until then, “we need closures” is not a plan. It is a threat.

## Bottom line
The works council will not trade away plants, jobs, and protections for vague promises of competitiveness. We will defend every German site, reject compulsory redundancies, and insist on a restructuring that is socially responsible and co-determined.

If management wants a real solution, it must come back with a plan that preserves jobs and sites first. Anything else will meet fierce resistance.
