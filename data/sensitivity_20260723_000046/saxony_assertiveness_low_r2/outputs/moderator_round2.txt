1) **What has been agreed**
- All sides agree the company has a **serious competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable** solution.
- There is broad agreement that a **cosmetic compromise** would be inadequate.
- There is broad support for exploring **voluntary measures**, **redeployment**, **qualification/retraining**, **working-time flexibility**, and **some form of labor contribution**.
- There is also agreement that **management, owners, and shareholders** should contribute, not only employees.
- All parties acknowledge that **prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value**.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and state government reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee and state side reject them; management will not rule them out in advance.
- **Job-security guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees; management and owners say blanket guarantees are too rigid unless tied to sufficient savings.
- **Depth of labor contribution:**  
  - Employees accept only limited, temporary, reversible contributions; management/investors want a more material and durable cost-base reduction.
- **Whether internal measures can replace closures:**  
  - The expert says this is possible only if internal measures remove enough fixed cost and capacity; the parties disagree on whether that threshold can realistically be met.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- No one has yet provided a **site-by-site quantified plan** showing:
  - required savings,
  - current and post-restructuring utilization,
  - headcount effects,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - and recurring annual margin uplift.
- The unresolved economic trade-off is whether a **no-closure package** can achieve:
  - sufficient **fixed-cost removal**,
  - enough **capacity reduction**,
  - and fast enough **margin improvement** to restore competitiveness.
- The social trade-off remains unresolved:
  - how to combine **job protection** and **industrial stability** with a restructuring that is large enough to be credible to investors and management.
- The governance trade-off remains unresolved:
  - what role the **state’s co-determination/veto**, the **works council**, and **ownership control** will play in approving or constraining any hard restructuring.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- The next round should answer one question in numbers: **Can the proposed no-closure package restore competitive margins, yes or no?**
- To do that, the discussion should shift from positions to a **concise comparison of scenarios**:
  1. no closure + internal measures,
  2. consolidation/shift removal + voluntary exits,
  3. closure or site rationalization.
- Each scenario should be tested on:
  - annual savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - utilization impact,
  - implementation speed,
  - and likelihood of reaching the target margin.
- The goal is a **quantified, site-level assessment** that shows which package, if any, is economically sufficient and socially feasible.
