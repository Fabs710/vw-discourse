## 1) What has been agreed
- **A restructuring is necessary**; the current cost/capacity situation is not sustainable.
- **A negotiated solution is preferred** over unilateral action.
- **The burden should not fall only on employees**; management, owners, and the company itself are expected to contribute.
- **Future investment in Germany matters** and should be tied to any settlement.
- **Prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging** for both the company and stakeholders.

## 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures**: Works council, union, and state government reject them as a starting point; management, family owners, and investors keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Labor-side actors reject them; management and owners will not rule them out.
- **Scope of job/site guarantees**: Labor and state want binding guarantees; management and investors want flexibility for structural change.
- **Co-determination / governance limits**: The state and labor want strong protection; investors and owners warn against governance becoming a barrier to restructuring.
- **Depth of cost reduction**: Management and investors want durable, material savings; labor side wants savings without permanent insecurity or one-sided sacrifice.

## 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is required** versus **how much can be achieved through working-time, redeployment, or attrition**.
- **What employee contribution is acceptable** and whether it is **temporary or structural**.
- **Which sites/products are strategically protected** and on what criteria.
- **What concrete management/owner concessions** will accompany workforce sacrifices.
- **How to balance investment commitments with near-term savings**.
- **What happens if negotiations fail** and whether escalation can still be avoided.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to a **structured package discussion**:
- identify the **minimum required savings/capacity reduction**,
- specify **which measures are negotiable** and which are not,
- define **what guarantees can be offered** on sites, jobs, and investment,
- and present **shared contributions** from all sides in a comparable, quantified form.
