I will be equally direct.

I agree with everyone on one central point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, and a prolonged conflict would destroy value.** That is not in the family owners’ interest, and it is not in the company’s interest.

But I do **not** agree that the answer is to freeze the footprint, rule out hard measures in advance, and then hope temporary concessions magically fix a structural cost problem. That would be a value-destructive compromise, not stewardship.

## Where I agree
- **With management:** the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable.
- **With labor and the state:** the burden cannot fall only on employees.
- **With investors:** savings must be real, durable, and measurable, not symbolic.
- **With the state and works council:** a settlement that triggers a prolonged industrial war would be worse than the problem.

## Where I disagree
### To the works council and union
I respect the insistence on avoiding compulsory redundancies and preserving jobs where possible. But I cannot accept **“no plant closures ever”** or **“no structural job reduction under any circumstances”** as absolute principles.

Why? Because if a site is structurally underutilized and permanently too expensive, then preserving it unchanged is not social responsibility — it is postponing a larger loss later. That is bad for workers, bad for the company, and bad for long-term ownership value.

I also do not accept the implication that any mention of closures is blackmail. Sometimes it is simply the economic reality being stated honestly. What would be unacceptable is using closures as a bargaining trick without first exhausting serious alternatives. But they cannot be removed from consideration before the numbers are tested.

### To management
I support the diagnosis, but I will not endorse a plan that looks like a blunt-force escalation. If you want ownership support, you need to show:
- that alternatives to closures were genuinely examined,
- that the savings are quantified,
- that the package is durable,
- and that the company will still have investment capacity for future products.

You cannot just say “all options are on the table” and expect us to sign off on a disruptive outcome without a credible industrial logic.

### To the state government
I understand your political constraints and the company’s regional role. But the blocking position cannot become a de facto guarantee that every German site survives in current form regardless of economics. That would preserve symbolism and destroy competitiveness.

### To investors
You are right to demand margin repair and structural savings. But value is also destroyed by a settlement that creates permanent labor conflict. So your standard must be: durable savings **and** durable industrial peace. One without the other is not enough.

## What I would support
I am open to a package that:
- delivers **material and permanent capacity reduction**,
- achieves **measurable labour-cost improvement**,
- includes **temporary, fair employee contributions**,
- preserves **critical investment in Germany**,
- and avoids unnecessary conflict through a negotiated path.

That package does **not** have to mean immediate closures, but it does have to mean real structural change. If the labor side can produce a combination of working-time flexibility, attrition, redeployment, productivity gains, and cost contributions that achieves the necessary economics, I will support it. If not, then harder measures remain legitimate.

## My bottom line
I will support a solution only if it restores competitiveness **for the long term**, not just the next quarter.

So my position is:
1. **No cosmetic compromise**
2. **No blanket veto on structural change**
3. **No one-sided burden on employees**
4. **No prolonged conflict**
5. **A quantified, durable restructuring with shared sacrifice**

That is the standard the family holding will apply through governance. If a package meets it, we can support it. If it does not, we will not pretend it is a solution simply because it is politically comfortable.
