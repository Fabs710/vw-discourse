## Core impasse

The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is about **means and guarantees**:

1. **Labor side red line:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no hidden closure triggers.  
2. **Management / investors / owners concern:** the package must deliver **durable, quantified, structural cost and capacity reduction** sufficient to restore competitiveness.  
3. **State role:** wants restructuring, but with a binding moratorium and no one-sided employee sacrifice.  
4. **Expert position:** a closure-free path is possible **only if** it still removes enough permanent capacity and fixed cost.

So the real clash is this:
- **Labor wants certainty of protection.**
- **Management and capital want certainty of economics.**
- **Neither side yet has a formula that gives both at once.**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Binding no-closure phase with quantified restructuring duties
**Proposal:**  
A time-limited agreement of, for example, 12–18 months with:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no trigger mechanism for later closures,
- independent plant-by-plant verification,
- and mandatory delivery of quantified savings measures.

**Safeguard:**  
If targets are not met, the consequence is not automatic closure. Instead, the parties must enter a second-round renegotiation with pre-agreed escalation steps.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It gives labor protection now, while giving management and investors a disciplined implementation framework.

---

### 2) Shared-sacrifice package with asymmetrical protection removed from the table
**Proposal:**  
A package combining:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- management overhead cuts,
- executive compensation restraint,
- owner-side payout restraint,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- and investment commitments.

In exchange:
- no closures during the agreement period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a jointly governed capacity plan.

**Safeguard:**  
All concessions expire automatically unless the company delivers matched contributions from management and ownership.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It addresses the investor demand for structural savings while meeting labor’s insistence that it not bear the burden alone.

---

### 3) Strategic-site specialization with network-wide employment guarantees
**Proposal:**  
Instead of debating all sites equally, designate:
- some sites as protected strategic centers,
- others for specialization and volume reallocation,
- with binding employment, training, and investment commitments for each site.

**Safeguard:**  
No site can be downgraded without:
- prior independent validation,
- a redeployment plan,
- and state/works council review.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It gives management a route to permanent capacity reduction without immediate closures, while reducing labor’s fear of arbitrary site sacrifice.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
Can you provide a **site-by-site table** showing which savings depend on closures, which can be achieved by shift reduction, redeployment, specialization, and overhead cuts, and what the **residual gap** is after those measures?

### To the works council
If closures and forced redundancies remain off the table, what **specific quantified capacity reductions** and structural measures are you prepared to accept so that the package is not only defensive but economically credible?

### To the union
What is the minimum **time limit, burden-sharing formula, and enforcement mechanism** under which you would endorse temporary concessions without them becoming a permanent wage or rights erosion?

### To the controlling family holding
Are you prepared to commit to **owner-side restraint** — including payout discipline and investment support — as a binding part of any deal, rather than keeping closures in reserve as a bargaining tool?

### To the regional state government
Will you support a **binding moratorium with enforceable conditions**, even if that requires accepting some specialization or capacity reduction within the network, so long as no plant closures occur during the agreement period?

### To institutional investors
Would you accept a restructuring package that avoids closures **if** it delivers independently verified, permanent fixed-cost and capacity reductions equivalent to a closure-led plan?

### To the independent expert
What minimum combination of **shift reduction, specialization, indirect-cost cuts, and voluntary headcount reduction** would you consider sufficient to credibly substitute for closures at the current cost gap?
