I will be very direct.

I agree with management, owners, and investors on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not symbolic delay**. I also agree that a one-sided employee sacrifice would be unacceptable and unstable. And I agree with the moderator that the discussion has to move from principles to a quantified package.

But I do **not** accept the core implication that has been running through the other side’s arguments: that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the natural or inevitable answer.

As the regional state shareholder, I will not support any outcome that **guts regional employment** or **treats German sites as disposable**. That is not only politically unacceptable; it would also damage the company’s long-term legitimacy, industrial base, and ability to operate as a regional anchor.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the current cost base is too high,
- the status quo is not sustainable,
- temporary fixes alone are not enough,
- and future investment in Germany must be part of the package.

### With the union and works council
You are right that:
- the burden cannot fall only on employees,
- industrial peace matters,
- and any restructuring must preserve skills, trust, and co-determination.

### With the family owners and investors
You are right that:
- the solution must be durable,
- savings must be real,
- and the company cannot simply preserve the current footprint unchanged if that footprint is structurally uncompetitive.

## Where I disagree

### On plant closures
I reject the idea that closures should be treated as a starting point.  
If closures are placed on the table immediately, then management is not negotiating from necessity; it is using existential pressure to force acceptance.

I will not endorse that method.

If management believes a site change is truly unavoidable, then it must first prove that:
1. alternatives have been seriously exhausted,
2. the savings cannot be achieved otherwise,
3. and the social and regional costs are genuinely proportionate.

That burden of proof has not been met.

### On compulsory redundancies
I do not accept compulsory redundancies as a default instrument.  
We need a restructuring that uses:
- redeployment,
- attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- and targeted productivity measures first.

If the company cannot make a negotiated package work without forced layoffs, that is a failure of strategy and bargaining, not proof that layoffs are the right answer.

### On co-determination and the state’s role
I also reject any outcome that sidelines the state’s blocking role or weakens co-determination in practice.  
Those are not obstacles to be bypassed. They are part of the governance architecture of this company.

If owners or management want to override that reality, they are inviting a much deeper confrontation than they seem to recognize.

## My position on the compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: No closure now, trigger-based review
This is the only proposal that is even moving in the right direction, but I would tighten it.

I would support:
- a **binding moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period**,
- in exchange for a serious shared-sacrifice package,
- with strict review of implementation.

But I do **not** accept a trigger mechanism that quietly reopens closures as an automatic fallback.  
A review must measure whether all parties have delivered on their obligations — not create a pre-authorized path to shutting plants later.

### Proposal 2: Shared-sacrifice package
This is necessary. But “shared sacrifice” must mean exactly that.

I would support:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary programs,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- management overhead restraint,
- payout discipline where needed,
- and binding investment commitments.

I will **not** support:
- employees giving up pay and guarantees,
- while ownership and top management remain insulated,
- and while closure threats stay in the background.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is one-sided erosion.

### Proposal 3: Site-specific restructuring with protected strategic sites
This can be discussed, but only if it strengthens all German sites rather than quietly writing some off.

If site specialization is part of the solution, then it must come with:
- product allocation,
- investment,
- training,
- and employment perspectives.

It must not become a code word for a two-tier company where some locations are protected and others are left to wither.

## What I can support

I am prepared to support a package with the following features:

1. **No plant closures in Germany during the agreement period**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the default restructuring tool**
3. **A quantified employee contribution that is temporary and limited**
4. **Management-side cost restraint and governance discipline**
5. **Owner-side payout and capital discipline**
6. **Binding investment commitments for German future products**
7. **A measurable path to capacity adjustment through redeployment, attrition, and working-time instruments**
8. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s effective blocking role**

## What I cannot accept

I cannot accept:
- a closure-led restructuring,
- a deal that shifts the major burden onto workers,
- a settlement that weakens job security permanently,
- or a package that turns the company away from its role as a regional economic anchor.

## Bottom line

I am open to a tough restructuring.  
I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and regional employment as the price of management’s strategic errors.

If management, owners, and investors want my support, they must bring a package that:
- protects sites and jobs,
- delivers real savings,
- and secures the company’s future investment base in Germany.

If they do not, then they should not expect the state shareholder to help them push through closures by the back door.
