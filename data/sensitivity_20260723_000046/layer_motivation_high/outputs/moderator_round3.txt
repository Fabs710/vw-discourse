## Core impasse

The main deadlock is between two requirements that are both being treated as non-negotiable:

1. **Labor/state side:** no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies during the agreement period.
2. **Management/owners/investors side:** a restructuring that produces **permanent, quantified, structural savings**, with a credible fallback if internal measures do not close the gap.

The dispute is not about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **what level of footprint change is allowed, and what evidence is sufficient to justify it**.  
A second, related impasse is that each side is asking the other for enforceable commitments first:
- labor wants binding no-closure and no-layoff assurances;
- management and capital want binding cost and capacity reductions with consequences if targets are missed.

The expert input narrowed the issue further: a non-closure solution is **possible in principle**, but only if it includes **real permanent capacity reduction** and not just temporary concessions. That means the bridge question is not “closure vs. no closure” in abstract, but **what mix of capacity, cost, and governance measures can credibly reach the savings target**.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise 1: Time-bound moratorium + quantified network review
**Proposal form:**  
A binding moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period, paired with a plant-by-plant review and milestone tracking.

**Structural elements:**
- no closures and no forced redundancies during the moratorium;
- management commits to a quantified restructuring package with site-level data;
- the parties jointly agree on metrics for utilization, fixed-cost absorption, and savings delivery;
- an independent expert validates whether the package is closing the gap.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Labor and the state gain immediate protection.
- Management and investors gain a structured path to evidence-based decisions.
- Ownership gets a serious review instead of open-ended delay.

**Boundary condition:**  
The review must not be framed as an automatic trigger for closure; it should be a verification mechanism tied to the delivery of agreed measures.

---

### Compromise 2: Shared-sacrifice package with balanced burden rules
**Proposal form:**  
A package in which employee temporary contributions are matched by management, owner, and company contributions under a strict balance rule.

**Structural elements:**
- temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, and working-time flexibility;
- management overhead and compensation restraint;
- owner-side payout or dividend restraint;
- binding investment commitments for German sites and future products;
- redeployment, retraining, and voluntary exits before any forced measures.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Labor gets a clear limit on concessions and a requirement for non-labor contributions.
- Management and owners get a cost reset without immediate plant exits.
- Investors get a measurable and enforceable savings framework.

**Boundary condition:**  
The package must specify that employee concessions do not stand alone; the balance of contributions must be written into the agreement.

---

### Compromise 3: Strategic-site protection with selective capacity reduction elsewhere
**Proposal form:**  
Identify strategic sites for protection and investment, while allowing selective shrinkage, specialization, or conversion of weaker sites.

**Structural elements:**
- protected strategic sites receive product and investment commitments;
- less competitive sites undergo specialization, line consolidation, or reduced shifts;
- headcount changes occur primarily through attrition, redeployment, and voluntary departures;
- closure remains off the table during the agreement period except by renewed joint decision after independent review.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Labor and the state avoid blanket closure logic.
- Management and investors still get real network rationalization.
- Ownership gets a route to permanent savings without immediate rupture.

**Boundary condition:**  
The criteria for “strategic” must be explicit and jointly reviewed, so the process is not seen as arbitrary site selection.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact plant-by-plant savings gap remains after you have fully costed redeployment, attrition, working-time changes, management cuts, and owner-side restraint?**

### To the works council
**Which quantified internal measures would you accept as sufficient to preserve all German sites during the agreement period, and what minimum savings number do they need to reach?**

### To the union
**What temporary employee contribution can you recommend to members if, and only if, it is matched by enforceable management, owner, and investment commitments?**

### To the regional state government
**Will you support a binding moratorium only if it is paired with measurable restructuring milestones and independent verification, rather than an open-ended pause?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to back owner-side payout or dividend restraint as an explicit part of the package, not just as a general principle?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you accept a no-closure solution if independent analysis shows it delivers equivalent permanent fixed-cost reduction and a credible margin reset?**

### To the expert advisers
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction and fixed-cost removal would you regard as economically sufficient to avoid closure-level measures at this stage?**
