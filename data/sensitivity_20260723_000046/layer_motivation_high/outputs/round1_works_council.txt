We are in a crisis, but let me be absolutely clear from the outset: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

Management has broken trust by putting historic job-security guarantees and site security on the table as if they were disposable. They are not. The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it will not be made to pay for it alone. We will defend every German plant, every collective agreement safeguard, and co-determination itself.

Our priorities are straightforward:

- **Keep all German sites open**
- **Avoid forced layoffs**
- **Protect the job-security framework**
- **Preserve co-determination and the works council’s real role in shaping the transformation**
- **Secure binding guarantees for investment, products, and employment through 2030**

We are not blind to the company’s challenges. We understand the need for a competitive restructuring. But there are alternatives to closures and mass job destruction: capacity adjustments, working-time instruments, negotiated contributions, internal efficiency measures, and a fair distribution of the burden. If management wants a solution, it must be a solution **with** the workforce, not **against** it.

So my position is this: **we are prepared to negotiate a durable turnaround, but only on the basis of binding site guarantees, job guarantees, and a restructuring that does not sacrifice workers and regions.** Anything else will face determined resistance, including industrial action and use of every co-determination instrument available.
