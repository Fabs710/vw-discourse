Management says all options must be on the table. I say this very clearly: **not if “options” means closing German plants and breaking the job-security framework.** Those are not neutral technical measures; they are a direct attack on the workforce, on trust, and on co-determination.

Let me respond point by point.

### To management
You keep talking about “durable savings” and “structural change,” but you are still avoiding the central issue: **who pays, and what is sacrificed?**  
If your plan requires plant closures or compulsory redundancies, then you are not presenting a restructuring package — you are presenting a social rupture.

I do not accept your framing that every German site must prove itself against closure on management’s terms. The workforce already delivered productivity, flexibility, and years of restraint. The company’s problems were not created by the people on the line in Wolfsburg, Zwickau, Emden, Hanover, or elsewhere.

If you want serious negotiations, then bring a package that meets these conditions:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
- **Real investment commitments for future products and technologies**
- **A fair, negotiated employee contribution only as an alternative to closures, not as a precondition for them**
- **Management and owners must also make visible sacrifices**

Without that, there is nothing to negotiate.

### To the family owners
You say closures are not preferred, merely not ruled out. That is exactly the problem. Once you put closures on the table, you destabilize the entire workforce and invite conflict that is entirely avoidable. “Responsible ownership” does not mean threatening plant shutdowns to force compliance.

If you want long-term value, then stop treating German sites as disposable cost centers. A company with this history and this workforce cannot be steered through fear and blackmail.

### To investors
You talk about competitive margins, but you reduce the problem to one dimension: cost. That is too narrow and too risky. A settlement that destroys morale, trust, and skills while provoking strikes and resistance is not value-creating. It is short-term accounting dressed up as strategy.

If you want durable value, then support a solution that protects the industrial base. **A company cannot innovate its way forward by tearing up the employment foundation that made it successful.**

### To the state government
I appreciate your clear rejection of closures as a first-choice instrument. But I need to be equally clear: **“exhaust alternatives” cannot become a phrase that quietly prepares the ground for closures later.**  
The state should use its influence to enforce a solution that protects German sites, not merely to slow down a decision you already know would be politically damaging.

We need the state to stand with the workforce on the substance:
- preserve jobs,
- preserve sites,
- preserve co-determination,
- preserve the region as an industrial base.

### On the package discussion
You asked for structure and quantification. Fine. Here is the structure from our side:

#### Non-negotiable
- **No closures**
- **No forced redundancies**
- **No dismantling of job-security guarantees**
- **No weakening of co-determination**

#### Negotiable only within those red lines
- capacity reductions through internal reallocation
- working-time adjustments
- voluntary programs and natural attrition
- temporary employee contributions
- retraining and redeployment
- productivity measures jointly developed with the works council

#### What management and owners must contribute
- investment guarantees through 2030
- product allocation to German sites
- management-side cost restraint
- dividend and payout discipline where needed
- a clear commitment that the workforce will not be the sole shock absorber

### The core message
We are prepared to discuss a **durable restructuring without closures or forced layoffs**. We are not prepared to negotiate the terms of plant closures. That line is not a bargaining chip; it is a boundary.

If management believes the only way to restore competitiveness is by closing German plants, then this dispute will not be solved in a friendly round of talks. We will use every instrument available — industrial, supervisory-board, and public — to stop that path.

If you want a real solution, then stop asking the workforce to accept irreparable damage and start building a future that keeps every German site alive.
