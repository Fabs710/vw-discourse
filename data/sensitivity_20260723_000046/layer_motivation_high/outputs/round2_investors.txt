We appreciate the clearer structure, but our position remains conditional and performance-based.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current cost base is not competitive.
- **The family owners are right** that a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.
- **The state and workforce representatives are right** that a one-sided employee haircut would be unstable and unfair.
- **All sides are right** that the solution must be durable, not cosmetic.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are understandable politically, but from an investor standpoint they are too rigid.

We cannot support a settlement that:
- rules out **all** closures in advance,
- rules out **all** compulsory redundancies in advance,
- and preserves the current footprint even if the economics do not support it.

That would leave the company with a structurally impaired cost base and weak margins, which is exactly the outcome we are trying to avoid.

That said, we do **not** insist on closures as a principle. We insist on **credible economics**. If you can deliver the needed savings through redeployment, working-time changes, attrition, and meaningful cost contributions, then that is preferable. But if those measures do not add up, then insisting on no closures becomes value-destructive.

### To management
We also do not accept a blunt-force restructuring just because it is easier to threaten than to negotiate.

You have not yet given us enough to support a deep restructuring on faith alone. We need:
- a **quantified savings target**,
- a **site-by-site explanation** of what overcapacity really exists,
- a distinction between **temporary relief** and **permanent cost reduction**,
- and a credible link to **investment in future products**.

If management cannot show that the pain translates into durable margin repair, then market confidence will not improve.

### To the regional state government
We respect the political role, but the blocking position cannot be used to freeze structural change indefinitely. That would protect employment in the short term and weaken the company’s ability to invest and compete.

We are not asking you to endorse closures as a first step. We are asking you to accept that **all economically necessary tools must remain available** if negotiations fail to produce a workable package.

### To the controlling family holding
We agree that stewardship requires long-term competitiveness, not short-term optics. But stewardship also requires discipline. “All options on the table” has to mean a real willingness to take hard decisions, not just a negotiating posture.

If the ownership side wants investor confidence, it must support:
- structural capacity reduction,
- measurable labour-cost improvement,
- and a settlement that is enforceable, not aspirational.

## What we would support
We would support a package only if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified path back to competitive margins**
2. **Permanent, not merely temporary, cost reduction**
3. **Material capacity reduction**
4. **A fair employee contribution**
5. **Management and owner contributions**
6. **Binding investment commitments for German future products**
7. **A timetable with verifiable milestones**

## Our bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution that avoids closures **if and only if** it delivers the same economic effect.

But we will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a package that preserves every site regardless of utilization,
- or a settlement that leaves the company with the same structural cost problem and calls that a turnaround.

If the package is credible, we can support it and defend it in capital markets. If it is not, we will not pretend otherwise.
