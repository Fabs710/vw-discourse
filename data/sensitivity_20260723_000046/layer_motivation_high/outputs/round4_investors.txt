### To the controlling family holding

## What would move us

We would support a no-closure restructuring if it includes all of the following:

1. **Real permanent capacity reduction**
2. **Material fixed-cost savings**
3. **Balanced burden-sharing**
4. **Binding investment commitments**
5. **Clear milestones and enforceability**
6. **A credible fallback if the package fails**

If those are in place, we can support a negotiated deal and defend it as value-preserving.

## What we will not support

- a cosmetic truce
- a deal that leaves overcapacity intact
- a package where employees carry the main burden
- a permanent closure taboo regardless of economics
- a settlement that restores peace briefly but leaves the share price and margins structurally impaired

## Bottom line

We are open to a non-closure solution **only if it achieves the same economic effect** as closure would: permanent, quantified, structural cost reduction.

If management and labor can produce that, we will back it.

If they cannot, then we will not pretend a weak compromise is a turnaround.

If the parties want market confidence, the next step is not more principle statements. It is a **site-by-site quantified package** showing:
- utilization,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- savings by measure,
- transition costs,
- and the residual gap.
