I will be direct.

I agree with management on one point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company does need a serious restructuring, and it needs durable savings, not symbolism.

But I disagree sharply with the idea that **plant closures or compulsory redundancies are the natural starting point**. For this company, in this region, with this governance structure, that is not just a technical measure. It is a break with the social and industrial compact that has made the company strong. I will not support a solution that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s veto and co-determination role.

### To management
You are right that temporary fixes alone are not enough. But you are still framing the problem too narrowly as a footprint-cutting exercise. If you want support from the state shareholder, you need to show a **credible restructuring without closure as the first lever**.

What I would consider supportable is a package built around:
- real capacity reduction through internal reallocation and attrition,
- working-time instruments,
- targeted and temporary employee contributions,
- management-side cost restraint,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites and future products.

What is not acceptable is a plan that treats German plants as disposable just because cost targets are missed. If closures are your preferred outcome, you will face strong resistance from me.

### To the works council and union
I support your refusal to accept compulsory redundancies and unilateral sacrifice. That is legitimate. But I also need to be frank: if the answer is simply “no closures, no cuts, no change,” then we will not solve the company’s problems.

The better argument is not a blanket veto. It is a **serious alternative restructuring** that protects sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness. If you can help produce that, I will back it. If not, I will not pretend the company can be frozen in place.

### To the family owners
You are correct that long-term value matters and that prolonged conflict would be damaging. But “all options on the table” cannot become a polite way of forcing through social damage first and calling it stewardship later.

Responsible ownership means exhausting alternatives to closures before treating them as inevitable. It also means recognizing that this company is not only a balance sheet asset; it is a regional industrial anchor.

### To investors
You are right to demand durable, quantified savings. I do not dispute that. But you also need to understand that **value destruction does not only come from cost levels**; it also comes from blowing up trust, skills, and industrial peace.

A solution that preserves every German footprint while fixing nothing is unacceptable. But a solution that creates a deep political and labor rupture is not value-creating either. The right answer is a negotiated restructuring with measurable savings, not a scorched-earth approach.

## My position in concrete terms
I would support a package only if it includes:

1. **No mass layoffs as the default**
2. **No plant closures unless all less destructive alternatives are proven insufficient**
3. **Binding job, site, and investment commitments for Germany**
4. **A fair employee contribution that is temporary or targeted, not a permanent dismantling of standards**
5. **Management and owner contributions on costs, governance discipline, and capital allocation**
6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s effective blocking role**

### Bottom line
I am open to a tough restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices regional employment and long-term industrial legitimacy.

If management wants my support, bring me a package that protects sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness. Otherwise, I will use the state’s leverage to block a solution that crosses those lines.
