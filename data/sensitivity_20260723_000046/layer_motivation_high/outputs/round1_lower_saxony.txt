I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally direct from the perspective of the regional state government and anchor shareholder.

We recognize the economic reality management is describing: the company does need a credible restructuring, and it cannot simply carry on with an uncompetitive cost base. We also recognize that employees cannot be asked to bear the full burden alone. A durable solution will require sacrifices and changes from several sides.

But I need to set a clear boundary:

- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable first-choice instrument**
- **Mass job destruction is not an acceptable restructuring method**
- **Any deal that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking role is unacceptable**
- **A solution that weakens the company’s regional anchor function is not a solution we can support**

So where do I agree?

I agree with management that **the status quo is not sustainable**. I agree that the company needs **durable savings**, not symbolic gestures. I also agree with the union and works council that this cannot be a one-sided labor sacrifice. A serious package must include **contributions from management, owners, and the company itself**, alongside a credible industrial future for German sites.

Where I disagree is on the starting premise that closures must be treated as a normal or unavoidable lever. In this company, with its history, its regional importance, and the governance structure it has, closures are not just a technical cost decision. They are a social, political, and strategic rupture. If management insists on that path, it should expect maximal resistance from the state shareholder.

What I would consider constructive is a restructuring package built around:
- **capacity reductions without closures where possible**
- **internal reallocation and working-time instruments**
- **natural attrition instead of forced redundancies**
- **temporary but meaningful employee contributions**
- **management-side concessions on costs and governance discipline**
- **binding investment commitments for product, battery, and future vehicle programs in Germany**
- **site and employment guarantees where credible**

That is the route that can preserve the company as a regional economic anchor while restoring competitiveness.

To management: if you want support, you will need to show that you have exhausted alternatives to closures and that the burden is being shared fairly. If your real plan is to force through plant shutdowns and permanent erosion of German employment, then you will run straight into the state’s veto position and our political opposition.

To the works council and union: I support your refusal to accept compulsory redundancies as the default answer. But I would also ask you to keep the door open to a serious negotiated restructuring. The company does need change. The question is not whether, but how. If we can secure jobs, preserve sites, and still achieve competitiveness through a social-partnership solution, that is the outcome we should pursue.

So my position is this:
**No blank check for closures. No empty promises either. We are prepared to back a tough restructuring only if it protects German jobs and sites, preserves co-determination, and creates a credible long-term industrial future.**

If management wants a deal, bring a package that meets those conditions. Otherwise, I am not prepared to move.
