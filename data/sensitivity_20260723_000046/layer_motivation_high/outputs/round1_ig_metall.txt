I agree with the works council on the core point: **this crisis cannot be solved by making the workforce carry the full burden**. Plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points, and they would destroy trust in any “transformation” management claims to be pursuing.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided wage sacrifice by employees while management and owners remain untouched**

That said, I am not ignoring the economic reality. We know the company needs lower costs and more flexibility. We are prepared to discuss a **fair, negotiated contribution** from employees — but only as part of a package that also includes real contributions from management, owners, and the company itself.

What could be on the table:
- temporary or targeted wage restraint
- working-time reductions or flexibilities
- waiving bonuses or special payments
- an employee contribution fund or solidarity mechanism
- capacity reductions through natural attrition, redeployment, and internal adjustments
- binding investment commitments for German sites and future products

What is **not** on the table:
- forced layoffs
- closure of German plants
- open-ended “rightsizing” that leaves workers permanently insecure
- breaking the collective bargaining system to shift all risks onto employees

If management says competitiveness requires sacrifice, then the burden must be **shared**. That means:
1. management and owners must contribute materially,
2. sites and jobs must be secured,
3. any reductions must be negotiated and socially responsible,
4. the company must give a credible industrial future, not just a cost-cutting plan.

We have leverage, and we will use it if necessary. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect action. Warning strikes are not a rhetorical threat; they are a real option if the company insists on closures or compulsory job cuts.

So I support a **durable restructuring agreement**, but only if it protects jobs, preserves the collective framework, and ensures that employees are not asked to pay for management’s mistakes alone.
