I will be direct.

We agree on the diagnosis: **the company needs a durable restructuring**, and **a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value**. We also agree that **the burden cannot fall only on employees** and that **future investment in Germany is essential**.

But I do **not** accept the central labor-side premise that the answer must be a restructuring **without closures and without the credible possibility of deeper structural adjustment**.

## Where I agree
- **With management:** the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable.
- **With labor and the state:** a one-sided employee sacrifice would be unstable and unfair.
- **With investors:** the savings must be real, durable, and measurable.
- **With everyone:** a settlement that triggers a long conflict would be value-destructive.

## Where I disagree

### To the works council and union
You keep treating **“no closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** as if they were self-evidently compatible with a serious turnaround. They are not, unless you can show a **quantified alternative** that actually closes the gap.

I have not yet seen that alternative.

Temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, working-time flexibility, attrition, and redeployment are useful, but I do not accept the claim that these measures automatically substitute for structural capacity reduction if the economics still do not work. If they do not deliver the required permanent effect, then they are not enough.

I also reject the idea that even discussing closures is “blackmail.” In a company with overcapacity and weak margins, refusing to consider all economically relevant tools would be irresponsible stewardship.

### To management
I support the need for hard decisions, but I do **not** support blunt-force escalation. If you want ownership backing, you must show:
- which savings are achievable without closures,
- which are not,
- and why.

You have to make the case plant by plant and measure by measure. “All options are on the table” is not sufficient governance language if it is not backed by a credible industrial logic.

### To the state government
I respect the state’s role and the special governance structure. But the blocking position cannot become a de facto guarantee that the footprint never changes. That would protect employment in the short term while eroding competitiveness and future investment.

### To investors
You are right to insist on quantified, enforceable savings. But you also need to recognize that a settlement that preserves every site in current form, regardless of utilization, is not a solution. It is a delay.

## My position on the compromise ideas

### 1) No closure now, trigger-based review
A **limited moratorium** can be discussed.  
A **permanent closure taboo** cannot.

I would only consider a moratorium if it is tied to:
- a quantified restructuring package,
- clear delivery milestones,
- and a credible review of whether the measures actually reduce the cost base.

I do **not** accept a trigger mechanism that becomes a hidden promise never to restructure later. If the package fails, the company must still retain the ability to act.

### 2) Shared-sacrifice package
This is the right direction, but only if the sacrifice is truly shared.

That means:
- employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- owner payout discipline,
- and capital support for the future.

What it does **not** mean is:
- employees make concessions,
- management keeps full flexibility,
- and ownership only speaks in generalities.

### 3) Site-specific restructuring
This is the most realistic approach, but I will not pretend every site can remain unchanged. Some sites may be strategic and protected. Others may need to shrink, specialize, or change materially.

That is not ideological. It is industrial reality.

## What I would support
I would support a package if it delivers all of the following:

1. **Permanent capacity reduction**
2. **Material labour-cost improvement**
3. **Visible management and owner contributions**
4. **Binding investment commitments for German future products**
5. **A timetable with verifiable milestones**
6. **A negotiated path that avoids unnecessary conflict**

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that preserves every site regardless of economics,
- a package that relies only on temporary employee concessions,
- or a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
I am open to a negotiated restructuring.  
I am **not** open to a compromise that merely postpones the problem while protecting every existing arrangement.

If the labor side wants to avoid harder measures, then it must bring a **numerically credible alternative** that achieves the same economic effect. Until then, closures and other structural measures remain legitimate instruments, not forbidden words.
