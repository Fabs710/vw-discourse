I’ll be direct.

I agree with the works council on the core red lines: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, no dismantling of the collective wage framework**. And I agree with the state shareholder that this cannot become a one-sided employee sacrifice. If management wants a turnaround, then the burden has to be shared.

But I do **not** accept management’s repeated attempt to turn “structural change” into a justification for breaking the workforce and threatening German sites as leverage.

## To management
You keep saying “all options must be on the table.” That is not a neutral statement here. In practice it means: put closures and forced job cuts on the table first, and then demand that workers accept worse terms to avoid them.

That is not a fair restructuring. It is coercion.

If you want my side to engage seriously, then bring a package that achieves the required savings **without**:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or permanent destruction of the collective agreement.

I am prepared to discuss:
- temporary wage restraint,
- waiver of bonuses or special payments,
- working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary departure programs,
- an employee contribution fund,
- and capacity reductions through attrition and reallocation.

But that only works if management and owners also contribute visibly. The workforce will not be the only shock absorber while executive pay, governance power, and capital returns remain untouched.

## To the family owners
Your “closures are not preferred, but not ruled out” position is exactly what escalates this conflict. Once you normalize closures as a negotiating instrument, you poison trust across the plants.

If you want long-term value, then stop treating the workforce as the variable cost in your portfolio logic. This company’s strength has always depended on skilled labor, local legitimacy, and industrial peace. You do not preserve that by threatening to rip up sites whenever margins tighten.

## To investors
I understand the demand for durable margin improvement. But if your solution destroys morale, triggers strikes, and breaks the collective bargaining order, then you are not creating durable value — you are creating a deeper conflict and a weaker company.

You are right about one thing: **cosmetic fixes are not enough**. But you are wrong if you think the only real fix is closure pressure and wage extraction. A serious restructuring can be done through negotiated capacity adjustment, fair contributions, and investment commitments.

## To the state government
You are right to reject closures as a first-choice instrument. But I need more than “we prefer alternatives.” I need the state to use its blocking position to make sure the company actually pursues those alternatives seriously.

That means:
- no premature closure planning,
- no forced redundancies by stealth,
- no hollow site guarantees,
- and no deal where the workforce pays first and others only later, if at all.

## On the package
Here is where I stand in concrete terms:

### Not acceptable
- plant closures in Germany
- compulsory redundancies
- permanent weakening of collective bargaining standards
- a settlement that shifts all pain to employees

### Negotiable
- temporary pay restraint
- waiver of bonuses/special payments
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary programs
- redeployment and retraining
- capacity reduction through natural attrition
- an employee solidarity contribution, **if** matched by management and owner contributions

### Required from the other side
- binding investment commitments for German sites and future products
- meaningful management-side cost restraint
- owner-level discipline on payouts and governance
- credible employment and site security for the duration of the agreement

## Bottom line
I am willing to negotiate a tough, fair restructuring. I am **not** willing to negotiate the dismantling of German plants or the collective wage framework.

If management wants a deal, then stop framing closure threats as unavoidable and start putting a quantified, shared-sacrifice package on the table. If you refuse that, then we will use our leverage — including warning strikes — to defend jobs, sites, and the collective agreement.
