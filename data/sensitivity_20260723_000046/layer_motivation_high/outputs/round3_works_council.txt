## On the compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “No closure now, but binding trigger-based review”
This is better than immediate closure threats, but I do **not** accept a trigger mechanism that becomes a hidden backdoor to closures. If you want a review, then the review must be about:
- investment delivery,
- productivity actions,
- internal reallocation,
- and management obligations.

It must **not** be a pre-authorized path to shutting plants if management decides targets were missed.

So: moratorium yes, closure trigger no.

### Proposal 2: “Shared-sacrifice package”
I can discuss management overhead cuts, executive restraint, and owner-side payout discipline. Those are overdue. But I reject any package that uses employee concessions as the main source of savings while leaving the structural footprint unresolved at the expense of jobs.

If management and owners want credibility, they must put real money and real authority on the table:
- top-management cost reduction,
- dividend restraint,
- investment guarantees,
- and binding no-layoff commitments.

### Proposal 3: “Site-specific restructuring with protected strategic sites”
This is the most dangerous one, because “strategic sites” often becomes code for sacrificing the rest. I will not accept a model that divides the workforce into winners and losers and quietly prepares some plants for decline.

Every German site has value, skills, and a workforce that contributed to this company. If there is to be specialization, it must strengthen all sites, not preselect some for long-term insecurity.
