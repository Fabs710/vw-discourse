I’ll be equally clear: **I do not accept a cosmetic solution that leaves our German cost base structurally uncompetitive.** If we are serious about the brand’s future, then the restructuring must deliver **durable, material savings in cost and capacity** — not just delay the problem.

Where I agree:
- We do need a **negotiated** solution.
- We do need to protect the company’s industrial future in Germany.
- We do need a **fair distribution of burden**.

Where I disagree sharply:
- **No plant closures as a starting point** is not a credible position if a plant is structurally underutilized and too expensive to sustain.
- **No compulsory redundancies** cannot become a blanket veto on real adjustment.
- Protecting every site in its current form is not the same as securing the company’s future.

The reality is simple:
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competition is intense.
- Our factory costs are far above target.
- The brand is not hitting its return-on-sales goal.
- We need funds for future products and technology through 2030.

That means we need a package that changes the structure, not just the mood.

So my position is:
1. **All options must be on the table**, including plant closures, capacity reductions, and job reductions.
2. The employee side must make a **genuine labour-cost contribution**.
3. Any agreement must produce **permanent savings**, not temporary waivers.
4. We will support **investment commitments for future products**, but only if they are matched by credible cost relief.
5. If there is resistance to structural change, then we will have no choice but to escalate, including confronting industrial action.

To be very explicit: I am not looking for conflict for its own sake. But I will not sign up to a deal that preserves every German footprint while leaving us with an uncompetitive base and no room to invest in the future.

If the union and works council want to avoid closures, then they need to bring forward a package that achieves the same economic effect:
- substantial capacity reduction,
- meaningful wage and cost adjustment,
- increased flexibility,
- and a credible path to profitability.

That is the standard I will hold this discussion to.
