### Core impasse
The main deadlock is between:

1. **Labor/state side red lines**: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of job-security/co-determination.
2. **Management/investor/ownership requirement**: the restructuring must produce **permanent, quantified, structural savings**, including capacity reduction if needed.

In short:  
**One side is insisting on preserving sites and employment protections; the other is insisting that meaningful restructuring must still be economically binding and durable.**  
The disagreement is not over whether change is needed, but **what kinds of change are still admissible** and **how much risk each side must absorb**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: “No closure now, but binding trigger-based review”
A compromise could be:

- **Immediate moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period**  
- In exchange, labor accepts:
  - temporary wage restraint,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment and retraining,
  - productivity commitments.
- Management receives:
  - a **quantified savings package**,
  - a **site-by-site performance review**,
  - and a **pre-agreed trigger mechanism** if targets are missed.

**Purpose:**  
This bridges the gap by replacing immediate closure threats with a **measurable correction mechanism** later, rather than an open-ended guarantee or an immediate shutdown path.

---

#### Proposal 2: “Shared-sacrifice package with ownership and management contributions made explicit”
A second bridge would be a package in which all sides contribute in visible, quantified ways:

- Employees:
  - temporary pay moderation,
  - bonus/special-payment waiver,
  - flexibility measures.
- Management:
  - executive compensation restraint,
  - overhead reductions,
  - governance discipline.
- Owners/investors:
  - dividend/payout restraint,
  - commitment to capital support,
  - funding for product and site investment.
- State:
  - support for a negotiated solution, conditional on no forced redundancies during the agreement period.

**Purpose:**  
This addresses the labor objection that employees are being asked to carry the entire burden, while still giving management a durable savings package.

---

#### Proposal 3: “Site-specific restructuring with protected strategic sites”
A third option is to distinguish between sites:

- Identify **strategic core sites** that receive:
  - binding product allocation,
  - investment commitments,
  - employment protection for a defined term.
- At less strategic or underutilized sites:
  - use natural attrition,
  - redeployment,
  - partial capacity reduction,
  - voluntary exits,
  - conversion or specialization.

**Purpose:**  
This offers management a real structural adjustment while avoiding a blanket closure agenda and preserving industrial legitimacy where it matters most.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
Can you specify **which savings are truly dependent on closures or forced redundancies**, and which savings could still be achieved through redeployment, attrition, working-time flexibility, and shared contributions?

#### To the Works Council
If closures are off the table, what is your **numerically credible alternative** for delivering permanent capacity and cost reduction at the scale management says is required?

#### To the Union
What exact combination of **temporary concessions and structural measures** would you endorse as sufficient to avoid closures while still producing durable savings?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage will you use to ensure negotiations produce an enforceable agreement, rather than simply delaying a closure decision?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you commit to a **no-closure moratorium during a defined negotiation period** if labor accepts a quantified shared-sacrifice package?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What level of savings, by what deadline, would make you support a restructuring **without** closures, and what evidence would you require that it is durable?
