We are going to be blunt.

## Our position
We support a restructuring that **credibly restores competitive margins** and **disciplined cost reduction**. We will not support an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive or preserves a margin problem under a nicer label.

That means:
- **no cosmetic deal**
- **no open-ended delay**
- **no settlement that protects every legacy feature while eroding equity value**
- **yes to whatever package actually resets the economics on a durable basis**

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert on the central diagnosis:
- this is a **structural** competitiveness problem
- the company’s German cost base is too high
- capacity has to come down
- symbolic concessions are not enough
- industrial conflict itself destroys value, so execution risk matters

We also agree with the expert that a **closure-led** solution is not automatically superior if it creates major delay, legal risk, industrial action, and ramp-up losses.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of guarantees” line is not acceptable **unless** you can show a package that closes the savings gap in hard numbers and on a durable basis.

So far, you have given a political red line, not an economic answer.

We do **not** accept:
- blanket site guarantees to 2030 if they freeze an uncompetitive footprint
- temporary contributions that simply defer the problem
- internal redeployment and working-time changes that do not materially reduce fixed cost
- a deal that preserves all sites and all jobs while margins stay impaired

If your non-closure alternative is real, show the numbers. If it is not, then it is not a serious alternative.

### To the union
We agree that symbolic concessions are worthless. We also agree that a deal must be durable.

But your insistence that closures and compulsory redundancies should be ruled out in advance is too rigid **unless** you can prove equivalent savings. At present, that proof has not been made.

Employee contribution can be part of the answer, but it must be:
- material
- structural
- durable
- and large enough to matter

Temporary bonus waivers and working-time flexibility alone are not enough if the footprint remains too heavy.

### To the state government
We respect the political and regional concerns, but they do not override economic reality.

If the current footprint is structurally too expensive, then blocking closures by principle risks leaving shareholders with a weaker business and a permanently impaired equity case.

You are asking for a non-closure route. Fair enough. But then **quantify it**:
- what fixed-cost reduction is achieved?
- how quickly?
- with what capex?
- with what headcount change?
- and with what assurance the savings are durable?

Without that, your position is understandable politically but not sufficient economically.

### To the family holding
We agree with you more than with labour on the need for a real competitiveness reset. We also agree that a purely cosmetic compromise would be value destructive.

Where we push back is on any suggestion that “hybrid” can be left vague. Hybrid is only acceptable if it is genuinely tough:
- real capacity reduction
- real labour-cost improvement
- real simplification
- credible implementation certainty

If hybrid becomes a euphemism for slow-motion postponement, we will not support it.

### To management
You are right to insist that the current state is unsustainable. But you still need to prove the chosen path is **risk-adjusted optimal**, not just theoretically tough.

Closure may be the cleanest structural lever, but if it triggers prolonged conflict and destroys present value, it is not automatically the best shareholder outcome.

So we want the scenario comparison:
1. closure-led
2. hybrid
3. no-closure

with hard numbers on:
- savings
- timing
- capex
- employment
- execution risk
- and expected margin recovery

## Our bottom line
We are open on the **method**, not on the **result**.

The result must be:
- restored competitiveness
- durable margin improvement
- disciplined cost base
- credible path to value creation

If a no-closure or hybrid package can deliver that on a durable, risk-adjusted basis, we will support it.

If not, then we will not support a compromise that merely preserves the current cost structure and calls it a settlement.

## What would move us
Only one thing:
a **quantified, credible, risk-adjusted restructuring plan** that demonstrates the company can return to competitive margins without hidden or deferred cost.  

If management can show that closures are necessary and superior after accounting for execution risk, then that case should be made plainly. If not, then the burden is on the other parties to prove their alternatives are economically equivalent.

At this stage, we are not choosing between “hard” and “soft.”  
We are choosing between **real restructuring** and **value erosion**.
