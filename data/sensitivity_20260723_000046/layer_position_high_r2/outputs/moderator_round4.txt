## Core impasse

The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that the cost base must come down.

The impasse is about **how much restructuring can be achieved without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, and whether the parties can agree on a package that is:
- economically credible,
- implementable in this governance structure,
- and durable enough to survive to 2030.

In practical terms, the conflict is between two hard requirements:
1. **Labor and the state** want binding site and job protection.
2. **Management, ownership, and investors** want a structural reset that materially lowers fixed cost and capacity.

The expert assessment suggests the unresolved question is whether a **hybrid package** can close enough of the savings gap on a risk-adjusted basis to satisfy both sides.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridging positions

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with binding site protection
A compromise could combine:
- no formal plant closures in Germany for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies in the same period,
- targeted capacity reduction through shift cuts, line consolidation, and specialization,
- voluntary exits and retraining,
- a time-limited employee contribution,
- and explicit management/owner contributions.

This would give management real structural change while giving labor and the state the security that there is no immediate closure path.

**Bridge logic:** preserves sites while still reducing complexity and cost.

---

### 2) Conditional no-closure package with economic triggers
A second compromise could be:
- all German plants remain open for a fixed horizon,
- but the company gets a pre-agreed restructuring toolbox,
- including shift changes, internal redeployment, and voluntary exit programs,
- with a jointly reviewed savings target and a trigger mechanism.

If agreed savings are not achieved by a set checkpoint, the parties return to the table rather than immediately defaulting to closure or layoffs.

**Bridge logic:** gives management a credible path to savings while avoiding premature closure decisions.

---

### 3) Phased settlement with independent verification
A third option is a phased deal:
- Phase 1: immediate labor-cost and productivity measures,
- Phase 2: capacity rationalization and specialization,
- Phase 3: independent review of whether the savings gap is actually closed.

An independent specialist would verify whether the package is meeting the risk-adjusted savings target before any further step is discussed.

**Bridge logic:** reduces mistrust by making the economics measurable rather than ideological.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Which exact combination of measures, quantified by annual savings and headcount impact, would you accept as sufficient to rule out plant closures for the next restructuring phase?**

### To the works council
**What concrete, time-bound operational concessions are you prepared to accept if they are the condition for binding no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy guarantees?**

### To the union
**Would you support a multi-year negotiated contribution from employees if it were paired with enforceable site protection and independent verification of savings delivery?**

### To the state government
**What specific leverage will you use to convert your opposition to closures into a binding restructuring framework rather than a political preference?**

### To the family holding
**Would you support a hybrid package if independent analysis showed it produced slightly lower static savings than closures but a better risk-adjusted present value?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum savings threshold, on a risk-adjusted basis, would make a no-closure or hybrid package acceptable to you?**

### To the expert
**What would be your threshold test for concluding that a no-closure package is genuinely equivalent on a risk-adjusted basis, rather than merely politically attractive?**
