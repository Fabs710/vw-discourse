### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that **internal redeployment, qualification, productivity improvements, and working-time flexibility** should be part of the toolkit.
- Most parties accept that **all sides may need to contribute**, including employees, management, owners, and possibly the state/shareholders, rather than burdening workers alone.
- There is general agreement that the outcome should preserve **long-term value**, **industrial capability**, and **investment capacity**.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and state government reject them.  
  - Management says they remain on the table.  
  - Owners/investors say closures are acceptable if needed to achieve durable savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour side and state reject them.  
  - Management and investors say they may become unavoidable if no sufficient alternative emerges.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Labour and state want firm job/site/investment guarantees.  
  - Management will not commit to blanket guarantees before restructuring.  
  - Owners/investors want commitments only if the economics are credible.
- **Scope of employee contribution:**  
  - Labour is open to limited, time-bound, negotiated contributions.  
  - Management and investors want material, structural savings.  
  - No shared definition yet of what would count as “equivalent” savings.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve the required savings without closures or layoffs**, if that remains the preferred route for some parties.
- **How much employee sacrifice is acceptable** in exchange for site and job security.
- **What management, owners, and the state are prepared to give up or commit** in return for workforce concessions.
- **What “durable” means in practice**: temporary measures versus permanent structural changes.
- **How to balance speed of implementation with social and political acceptability**.
- **What form of investment commitment can be credibly offered now**, and under what conditions.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on **comparing concrete restructuring packages**, not just positions. Each side should specify:
- the minimum acceptable outcome on **sites, jobs, and cost reduction**,
- which measures they are willing to concede,
- which measures are non-negotiable,
- and what would count as an **equivalent alternative** to closures or layoffs.

A useful next step would be for management to present **one or more quantified scenarios**, and for labour, the state, and owners to respond to the **specific trade-offs** in each scenario.
