I will respond directly and clearly.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one. The market situation, the cost base, and the investment pressure are real.

But I **do not accept** your framing that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain normal tools on the table as if they were equivalent to other options. For us, they are not. A restructuring that destroys key German sites or turns mass layoffs into the default solution would damage the company’s role as a regional anchor and would face serious resistance from our side at board level.

You have not yet demonstrated that closures are the only way to achieve the required savings. If you want support, bring **quantified alternatives** that can credibly deliver the same durable effect:
- meaningful capacity reductions,
- productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- and a real employee contribution.

But the burden is on management to prove that these do not merely defer the problem. I am open to tough measures. I am not open to hollowing out the German industrial footprint.

## To the works council and union
I hear your red lines, and in important respects I share them: **no closure as a first resort, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral dismantling of co-determination or the collective framework**.

Where I need to be careful is this: if the company truly needs a structural reset, then we cannot simply say “no” to every hard change and expect the economics to fix themselves. A credible alternative to closure must still deliver real savings and not just preserve the status quo.

So I support:
- capacity reduction without closures where possible,
- internal transfers and qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary instruments,
- and a limited, negotiated employee contribution.

But I would expect that contribution to be matched by:
- management restraint,
- owner responsibility,
- and clear investment commitments.

What I will not support is a package where the workforce carries the main burden while the company’s future remains uncertain. Nor will I accept any solution that undermines the state’s and workforce’s co-determination role.

## To the family owners
I appreciate your long-term stewardship language, but stewardship also means protecting the industrial base that gives this company its strength. A solution that is “stable” only because it is imposed at the expense of plants and jobs in Germany is not a good long-term solution.

I do agree with you that prolonged conflict would destroy value. That is exactly why I am pushing for a **negotiated, socially balanced restructuring** rather than a clash over closures. But that negotiated path must be genuine, not a prelude to a fait accompli.

If there are credible alternatives that achieve comparable savings without closing German plants, I expect them to be seriously pursued before irreversible decisions are made.

## To the institutional investors
You are right that the company needs a credible route back to margins. But credibility is not the same as the harshest headline. A plan that looks clean in a spreadsheet but triggers industrial conflict, political backlash, governance deadlock, and execution delays is not truly credible.

So I agree with your demand for:
- durable cost reduction,
- a real capacity reset,
- and a measurable path to profitability.

Where I disagree is your assumption that closures or forced redundancies are acceptable just because they are economically neat. In this company, they carry major governance, political, and social costs. Those costs matter, and they matter materially.

## My position
Let me be explicit:

- I support a **serious restructuring**.
- I support **capacity reduction**, if needed.
- I support **negotiated labour contributions**, if they are fair and limited.
- I support **productivity and working-time measures**.
- I support **investment commitments** tied to the restructuring.
- I do **not** support German plant closures as the default answer.
- I do **not** support compulsory redundancies as a bargaining instrument.
- I do **not** support any solution that weakens our co-determination or blocking role.

## What would move me
A settlement becomes realistic if it contains all of the following:
1. **Preservation of the German industrial base**
   - no closure-driven hollowing out of the state’s sites.

2. **A credible, quantified savings package**
   - not symbolic, not open-ended, not deferred.

3. **A fair distribution of burden**
   - employees, management, and owners all contribute.

4. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - so restructuring does not become mere downsizing.

5. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
   - because that is part of the governance legitimacy of this company.

## What I suggest next
Before anyone talks about irreversible plant decisions, management should present **two or three quantified restructuring scenarios**:
- one with closures,
- one without closures,
- and one hybrid.

Then the parties should compare:
- employment effects,
- savings,
- implementation risk,
- capex needs,
- and regional impact.

That is the only serious basis for decision. Right now, we have assertions; we do not yet have a sufficiently tested comparison.

If management insists that closures are unavoidable, then it must prove that no other package can deliver the same durable effect. Until then, I will continue to oppose closure-driven restructuring in Germany.
