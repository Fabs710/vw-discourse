As the regional state government and an anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: we do **not** accept a restructuring that solves the company’s problems by hollowing out the German industrial base.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **the situation is serious and a durable solution is needed**. Nobody here benefits from denial. The cost base, the market pressure, and the need for investment are real.

I also agree with the works council and the union that any solution must be **socially responsible** and must not simply dump the entire burden on the workforce.

### Where I disagree
I disagree fundamentally with the idea that **plant closures and forced redundancies are the default answer**.

That is not acceptable to us:
- because it would damage regional employment,
- because it would weaken the company as a long-term industrial anchor,
- and because it would undermine the co-determination and veto structures that are part of this company’s governance and legitimacy.

This is not just a political preference; it is a core condition for our continued support.

### To management
If your position is that closures “remain on the table” as a first-class option, then you should understand that this will meet resistance from us at board level. A deep restructuring that destroys jobs and sites in Germany is not a plan we can simply wave through.

What I will support is a **serious negotiated restructuring** that:
- preserves the German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- includes capacity reductions where needed,
- and improves competitiveness through operational changes, productivity measures, and a fair contribution from all sides.

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are understandable and, in important parts, legitimate. But I also need to be candid: a credible solution cannot be built on slogans alone. If the company truly needs durable savings, then there may need to be:
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary wage restraint or other employee contributions,
- redeployment and qualification measures,
- and changes in internal processes.

What I cannot support is a stance that protects every existing arrangement unchanged and expects the company to absorb the cost somehow elsewhere. That would not be sustainable either.

### What would move us
A settlement becomes realistic if it meets these conditions:
1. **No plant closures in Germany.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **A negotiated package of capacity and cost reductions** that is durable and credible.
4. **Binding investment commitments** for future products and technology.
5. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s role** as an anchor shareholder.

If management can bring forward a plan that achieves real savings without closures, then we are prepared to work constructively. But if the proposal remains centered on closures and mass layoffs, then I will not consider that an acceptable basis for agreement.

### Bottom line
We are not here to protect the status quo at any price. We are here to protect the company’s future **without sacrificing the regional economy and the workforce that built it**.

So my message is:
- **to management:** bring a restructuring that preserves sites and jobs, or you will not have our support;
- **to labour:** be prepared for real, negotiated contributions if you want a durable settlement;
- **to all sides:** the path forward must be socially balanced, economically credible, and consistent with the company’s long-term role in the region.
