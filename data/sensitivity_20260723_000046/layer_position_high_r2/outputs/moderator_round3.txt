## Core impasse

The deadlock is now concentrated in one issue:

**Whether restructuring can be made acceptable without any German plant closures or compulsory redundancies.**

This splits the parties into two blocks:

- **Labour / state side:** insists on binding site security, no forced layoffs, no closures, and protection of co-determination.
- **Management / investors / family holding:** insist that the cost base must be reduced materially, and that closures remain available if alternatives do not close the economic gap.

A second, related impasse is procedural:

**No shared definition yet exists for what counts as an “economically equivalent” non-closure alternative.**

That means the conversation is stuck between:
- a demand for guarantees first, and
- a demand for structural savings first.

## Concrete compromise positions

### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional site security
A possible bridge is a **time-limited no-closure commitment** paired with measurable operational milestones.

**Elements:**
- German plants remain open for a defined period, e.g. through 2027 or 2028, instead of 2030.
- In return, labour accepts a quantified cost-reduction package:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - temporary wage restraint / bonus reduction,
  - productivity measures,
  - internal redeployment.
- Management receives a **review trigger**: if agreed savings are not met by set dates, a renegotiation mechanism opens.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**
- Labour gets immediate protection against abrupt closures.
- Management gets a real, measurable restructuring path.
- Investors get a mechanism that is not purely symbolic.

---

### 2) Hybrid network rationalization without plant closures
A second compromise is to keep all German plants formally open but reduce the footprint through **specialization and volume reallocation**.

**Elements:**
- No plant shutdowns.
- Some plants lose certain product lines or shifts.
- Production is consolidated across sites.
- Voluntary severance and retraining handle headcount reduction.
- Management commits to site-specific investment and modernization.
- Employee contribution is time-bound and paired with job-security guarantees.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**
- It preserves the legal and symbolic red line of no closures.
- It still delivers real savings through lower complexity and capacity use.
- It gives management a structural reset without crossing the most contested line.

---

### 3) Binding social-contract package with external oversight
A third compromise is a **governed restructuring pact** with independent monitoring.

**Elements:**
- No compulsory redundancies for a defined period.
- No closures during that period.
- Measurable savings targets agreed in advance.
- A joint oversight committee including management, works council, union, and state.
- Independent verification of whether savings are being achieved.
- If targets are missed, the parties revisit the package before any unilateral escalation.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**
- It builds trust through transparency.
- It reduces fear of hidden closure-by-attrition.
- It gives management a route to enforce discipline without immediate shutdown threats.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What exact concessions, quantified in annual savings and headcount effect, would you accept in exchange for a binding no-closure period?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-limited wage and flexibility package if it were tied to enforceable site-security guarantees and independent monitoring?**

### To Management
**Can you specify the minimum savings threshold below which closures are truly unavoidable, and separate that threshold from bargaining pressure?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to use your governance leverage to back a hybrid package even if it requires accepting some site specialization and reduced employment, provided closures are avoided?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you commit to evaluating the hybrid option on a risk-adjusted basis before treating closure as the default fallback?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What level of durable savings would persuade you that a no-closure or hybrid package is economically sufficient, even if it is slower and more politically constrained than closures?**
