From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the only acceptable outcome is one that **credibly restores competitiveness and margins**. We are not attached to any particular method, but we are very attached to the result. Right now, the company’s share price and cost of capital are telling you the market does not believe the current cost base is sustainable.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family owners, and the state on one core point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company faces a structural problem, not a temporary one. If German operations remain overcapacity, expensive, and unable to hit return-on-sales targets, then the equity story deteriorates further. That is unacceptable.

We also agree that:
- productivity must improve,
- capacity must come down,
- labour costs must be addressed,
- and the solution must be durable, not a one-off accounting fix.

On that, the works council and union are right only insofar as they accept that **some form of real sacrifice is unavoidable**. If everyone simply preserves their current position, there is no solution.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement whose main achievement is to preserve legacy employment structures while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.

That means:
- **No cosmetic deal.**
- **No agreement that postpones the adjustment.**
- **No package that relies on vague promises, temporary deferrals, or voluntary measures that do not add up.**

To the works council and union: your red lines on plant closures and compulsory redundancies are understandable politically, but from a shareholder-value perspective they are not credible unless you can offer **equivalent, measurable, and durable savings**. If you cannot, then those red lines become a demand that shareholders absorb the full burden while margins remain impaired. We will not support that.

To the state government: preserving the regional base matters, but not if the result is a company that continues to underperform and destroy value. Political constraints cannot substitute for economic reality.

To the family holding: we agree that prolonged conflict would destroy value, but that cuts both ways. Avoiding disruption cannot mean accepting an underpowered restructuring. A stable bad deal is still a bad deal.

### What would move us
We would support a deal if it clearly delivers all three of the following:

1. **A durable reduction in the cost base**
   - not temporary savings,
   - not accounting effects,
   - not a mere delay of the problem.

2. **A credible capacity reset**
   - whether through plant closures, site consolidation, or other hard measures,
   - but it has to be enough to match demand and restore competitiveness.

3. **A clear path back to competitive margins**
   - with measurable milestones,
   - management accountability,
   - and enough flexibility to protect future investment in EVs and technology.

### On plant closures and layoffs
We are not ideologically attached to closures, but we also will not pretend they are off the table if they are required to reach a viable structure. If alternative measures can genuinely produce the same savings, then fine — use them. But if not, then the business should not be held hostage to a promise that makes the restructuring impossible.

Likewise on compulsory redundancies: they are undesirable, but if the alternative is a permanently bloated cost base, then investors will judge management on value destruction, not on how gently the pain was distributed.

### On employee contributions
A negotiated employee contribution can be acceptable only if it is:
- material,
- time-bound if temporary,
- and tied to binding structural changes.

We would view open-ended wage restraint or symbolic concessions as insufficient unless they are paired with firm capacity and overhead reductions.

### Bottom line
Our position is simple:

- **We support decisive restructuring.**
- **We do not support a settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.**
- **We will back whichever path is most credible in restoring competitiveness, whether that includes closures or a genuinely equivalent alternative.**
- **We will not reward delay, half-measures, or conflict that preserves value for one stakeholder group while eroding it for shareholders.**

If the parties can produce a package that is economically credible, durable, and implementable, we will support it. If not, we would regard the outcome as value-destructive and would say so clearly.
