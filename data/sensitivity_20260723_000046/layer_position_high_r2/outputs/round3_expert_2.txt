# Scenario comparison: relative assessment

## 1) Closure-led restructuring
**Description:** Close one or more German plants, reduce footprint materially, cut headcount sharply, consolidate production.

### Industrial effect
- **Savings potential:** High
- **Fixed-cost reduction:** High
- **Capacity reduction:** High
- **Employment impact:** High
- **Capex needs:** Medium to high during transition
- **Timing:** Slower to realize than headline announcements suggest
- **Execution risk:** High
- **Industrial-relations risk:** Very high

### What it tends to deliver
- Best chance of a **permanent structural reset**
- Strongest lever if the issue is genuine overcapacity
- Usually the cleanest answer on paper for a cost base problem

### Main constraints
- In this governance setup, closures face:
  - works council veto pressure,
  - union resistance,
  - state shareholder resistance,
  - likely strike action,
  - implementation delay.
- Those risks can substantially reduce the **present value** of the savings.

### Industrial judgment
This is the **strongest structural** option, but not necessarily the best **risk-adjusted** option here.

---

## 2) Hybrid restructuring
**Description:** Keep all or most German plants open, but reduce capacity through shifts, line changes, product reassignment, voluntary exits, internal transfers, selective specialization, and negotiated employee contributions.

### Industrial effect
- **Savings potential:** Medium to high
- **Fixed-cost reduction:** Medium to high
- **Capacity reduction:** Medium
- **Employment impact:** Medium
- **Capex needs:** Medium
- **Timing:** Moderate
- **Execution risk:** Medium
- **Industrial-relations risk:** Medium to high

### What it tends to deliver
- Often the **best compromise** between economic effect and feasibility
- Can preserve production footprint while still reducing structural cost
- More likely to survive governance constraints than outright closures

### Main constraints
- Risk of becoming a **soft compromise** if capacity reduction is not real
- Requires disciplined design:
  - fewer product lines,
  - fewer shifts,
  - tighter site specialization,
  - measurable labour-cost contribution,
  - strict headcount reduction pathway.
- Can fail if it relies too much on temporary concessions or goodwill

### Industrial judgment
This is probably the **most plausible implementable path** in the described governance environment, but only if it is materially tough.

---

## 3) No-closure restructuring
**Description:** No plant closures, no forced redundancies, rely on productivity, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, internal redeployment, wage restraint, bonuses, and other negotiated cost measures.

### Industrial effect
- **Savings potential:** Low to medium
- **Fixed-cost reduction:** Low to medium
- **Capacity reduction:** Low to medium
- **Employment impact:** Low to medium
- **Capex needs:** Medium
- **Timing:** Faster initially, but savings may be less durable
- **Execution risk:** Low to medium
- **Industrial-relations risk:** Low initially, but risk rises if savings disappoint

### What it tends to deliver
- Best chance of preserving industrial peace
- Lowest direct social disruption
- Easiest to announce politically

### Main constraints
- Often **insufficient** if the overcapacity problem is structural
- Temporary wage restraint and flexibility rarely close a large cost gap on their own
- If the footprint remains too broad, the company may simply defer the problem

### Industrial judgment
Viable only if the company can extract **deep, durable concessions** and still materially simplify operations. Otherwise it is likely underpowered.

# What matters most economically

The decision is not just “how much cost is cut,” but:

- **How permanent are the savings?**
- **How much transition cost is incurred?**
- **How much output is lost during execution?**
- **What is the strike / delay probability?**
- **How much capex is needed to stabilize the remaining footprint?**
- **Does the package improve utilization enough to matter?**

In this type of OEM restructuring, a closure can look superior in static cost terms but still underperform on a **risk-adjusted net present value** basis if:
- industrial action is prolonged,
- product migration slips,
- remaining plants are underutilized,
- or political/governance blockages delay implementation.
