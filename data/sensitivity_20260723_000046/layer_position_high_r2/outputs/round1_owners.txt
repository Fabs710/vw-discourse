The family holding shares the assessment that this company needs a **durable competitiveness reset**, not a cosmetic fix. On that point I align with management: if the cost base and capacity situation are structurally out of line, the business will continue to erode value, and that would be unacceptable from a long-term ownership perspective.

But I also want to be equally clear: **value destruction through uncontrolled conflict, reputational damage, or a settlement that permanently weakens the enterprise is not acceptable either**. We are long-term owners, not short-term maximizers. We need a solution that restores margins **and** preserves the company’s industrial strength.

### Where I agree
I agree with management that:
- the market reality is harsh,
- Germany’s cost position must be improved,
- and a structural answer is needed, not temporary delay.

I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state government that:
- trust has been damaged,
- the burden cannot fall on employees alone,
- and any solution must be stable enough to survive implementation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support a position that says “no meaningful structural change” while expecting the company to regain competitiveness. If the alternatives do not deliver **equivalent, durable savings**, then they are not a real solution.

At the same time, I do **not** support treating plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the automatic first move. Those are not value-neutral tools. They carry major risks:
- industrial conflict,
- execution delays,
- loss of know-how,
- political escalation,
- and long-term damage to the brand and the region.

So from the family owners’ perspective, the issue is not “closures versus no closures” as an ideological question. The question is: **what combination of measures actually restores competitiveness without destroying the franchise?**

### What I need to see
I would support a package only if it is:
1. **economically credible** — the numbers must add up and be durable;
2. **implementable** — not dependent on hopes or one-off effects;
3. **stable** — capable of delivering labour peace and avoiding prolonged escalation;
4. **protective of long-term value** — no permanent impairment of the core asset.

That means I am open to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary or negotiated employee contributions,
- and other structural efficiency measures.

But I am **not** open to a deal that preserves every legacy arrangement and simply postpones the reckoning. Nor am I open to a settlement that triggers a prolonged confrontation and weakens the company’s governance or strategic coherence.

### To management
Your case for a lower cost base is understood. But if you want support at governance level, you need to show that the proposed path is not just tough, but **smart**. If there are alternative measures that can truly match the savings of closures, I would expect you to pursue them first. If you insist on closures, you must demonstrate clearly why no other package can reach the same durable effect.

### To the works council and union
I hear your refusal of closures and forced redundancies. I do not dismiss that lightly. But you should also understand that **blanket guarantees without meaningful contributions are not realistic**. If the company must regain competitiveness, labour will need to participate in that effort in a concrete way.

What I would regard as constructive is:
- a negotiated contribution that is limited and time-bound,
- serious flexibility on working arrangements,
- productivity and redeployment measures,
- and a willingness to discuss a package that delivers real savings without destabilising the company.

What I would regard as unacceptable is a stance that blocks all structural adjustment while demanding full investment certainty and employment protection. That would not be stewardship; it would be avoidance.

### To the state government
Your insistence on preserving the industrial base and social stability is understandable and, in part, aligned with our own long-term view. But the company also needs **economic realism**. A political veto cannot become a substitute for a viable business model. If the alternatives are not sufficient, then the problem does not disappear because closures are politically difficult.

### Bottom line
The family holding’s position is this:

- **We support a decisive restructuring.**
- **We do not want value-destructive plant closures if credible alternatives can deliver the same durable outcome.**
- **We do not support permanent impairment of ownership control or governance effectiveness.**
- **We want a negotiated solution that restores competitiveness, protects long-term value, and avoids a drawn-out conflict.**

So I would urge the parties to focus on a package that is genuinely structural, not symbolic. If management can demonstrate that there is no other path to the required savings, then difficult decisions will have to be faced. But if labour and the state can help deliver equivalent durability through a serious negotiated contribution, that would be the preferable route from the owners’ perspective.
