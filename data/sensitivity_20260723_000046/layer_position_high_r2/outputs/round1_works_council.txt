As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the red line. Any restructuring that starts from closing sites, breaking the historic job-security pledge, or pushing the burden entirely onto the workforce will face our full resistance — in the boardroom, in the plants, and publicly.

What we are prepared to discuss is a different path: a negotiated restructuring that protects all German plants, secures jobs through 2030, and preserves co-determination and the existing job-security framework. If management truly needs savings, then we will examine alternatives such as:
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary instruments only,
- and a fair contribution from management and shareholders as well, not just employees.

But any credible solution must meet three conditions:
1. No site closures in Germany.
2. No forced layoffs.
3. Binding guarantees for jobs and investment through 2030, with genuine employee co-determination in shaping the restructuring.

Management created this crisis by abandoning trust. You do not restore competitiveness by destroying the workforce and the industrial base. We are ready to negotiate seriously, but only on a solution that protects people, plants, and the long-term future of the company.
