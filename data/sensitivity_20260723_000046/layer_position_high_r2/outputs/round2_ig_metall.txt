I’ll respond directly.

First, to the works council: **yes, we are aligned on the non-negotiables** — no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework. I support that line fully.

Second, to management: your answer still does **not** satisfy the condition under which the union can move. You keep describing closures and layoffs as “on the table” and framing wage concessions as the price of survival. That is not a shared-sacrifice strategy; it is a pressure strategy aimed at shifting the burden onto employees first and asking everyone else to wait and see. That will not work.

### What I agree with
I do agree with the expert on one important point: a no-closure solution only works if it is **serious, quantified, and durable**. I also agree that “symbolic” concessions are not enough. If we are going to discuss an alternative to closure, then it must include:
- real capacity reduction,
- meaningful productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and a material employee contribution.

That is exactly why I already put a counter-proposal on the table.

### Where I disagree
I **do not accept** the implication that because a no-closure solution is harder, it therefore becomes impossible or automatically inferior.

The expert is right that closures can be a cleaner accounting solution. But in a co-determined German industrial company, a “cleaner” financial answer that detonates industrial peace, destroys trust, and triggers long conflict is not automatically the better answer. **Execution matters.** A closure-led package that cannot be implemented smoothly is not credible.

And I reject the idea that compulsory redundancies are somehow the natural fallback if employees do not immediately accept management’s framing. That is exactly the kind of threat that makes negotiations unstable.

### What the union can support
We remain open to a **hard but fair negotiated package** that avoids closures and forced layoffs, if it contains all of the following:

1. **Binding no-closure commitment for German sites**
   - not a vague intent,
   - not “for now,”
   - but a real agreement.

2. **Binding no-compulsory-redundancy commitment**
   - workforce reduction only through voluntary exits, retirement paths, redeployment, and working-time measures.

3. **A limited employee contribution**
   - time-bound,
   - proportionate,
   - and tied to job and site security.

4. **Management and owner contribution**
   - no pretending that labour alone should finance the restructuring,
   - no protected distributions while workers absorb all the pain.

5. **Investment and product commitments**
   - not as vague promises,
   - but as part of the bargain.

### What is acceptable as employee contribution
We are not refusing contribution. That would be dishonest. What we will discuss includes:
- temporary wage restraint or bonus waivers,
- working-time adjustments,
- flexibility instruments,
- possibly a solidarity contribution,
- and internal restructuring measures.

What we will **not** accept:
- permanent wage dumping,
- open-ended cuts,
- hidden erosion of the collective agreement,
- or concessions that become one-way once management has the leverage.

### On the expert’s “hybrid compromise”
A hybrid package is only acceptable if the “hybrid” part does **not** become a euphemism for:
- slow plant closure by attrition,
- creeping outsourcing,
- or forced job cuts under another label.

If the company wants to reduce capacity, then it should do so through:
- line adjustments,
- shift reductions,
- model allocation changes,
- and negotiated internal transitions.

That is the industrially honest route.

### To the state government
Your support matters, but it must become clearer than statements of concern. If you oppose hollowing out the industrial base, then use your leverage to stop closures and forced redundancies. The state cannot be a rhetorical ally while leaving management free to impose the same outcome in practice.

### To the family owners and investors
You are right that a deal must restore competitiveness. But you are wrong if you think competitiveness can be bought by making the workforce absorb the pain alone.

A durable solution requires:
- lower cost,
- yes,
- but also social legitimacy and implementation stability.

If you force a confrontation, you may get a “harder” plan on paper and a weaker company in reality.

## Union position, clearly stated
- **No plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- **Yes, to a fair, limited, negotiated contribution by employees if — and only if — management, owners, and the state also contribute and job/site security is guaranteed.**

If management can bring a quantified package that meets those conditions, we will negotiate seriously. If not, then there is no basis for the workforce to pay for a crisis it did not create.
