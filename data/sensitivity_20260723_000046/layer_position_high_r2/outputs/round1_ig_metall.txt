I can support the works council’s basic line: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear, though: if management is asking the workforce to make a contribution, then that contribution must be **fair, limited, and matched by sacrifices elsewhere**. We will not accept a restructuring where employees are expected to pay for the whole crisis while management, the owners, and capital are insulated.

Where we agree:
- **Capacity reductions and internal adjustments** can be discussed.
- **Working-time flexibility, qualification, redeployment, and voluntary instruments** are legitimate alternatives.
- A **negotiated employee contribution** is possible if it is tied to **site security, job security, and investment commitments**.

Where we disagree:
- **Plant closures are not an acceptable starting point.** Once a site is closed, the jobs, skills, and regional industrial base are gone.
- **Forced layoffs are off the table.** They would destroy trust and make any settlement unstable.
- **Open-ended wage cuts or permanent erosion of standards** are unacceptable. If labour is asked to contribute, it must be time-limited, proportionate, and part of a balanced package.

So the union’s position is this:

1. **No closures and no compulsory redundancies.**
2. **A shared-sacrifice package is possible** — for example through temporary wage restraint, bonus waivers, working-time measures, or a solidarity contribution.
3. **Management and owners must contribute too** — through reduced distributions, internal restructuring, productivity commitments at the top, and a credible industrial plan.
4. **There must be binding guarantees** for sites, jobs, and future investment, not just verbal assurances.

If management insists on “deep structural cuts” meaning closures and layoffs, then there is no basis for agreement, and we will respond with the full range of industrial action available to us. The workforce is not prepared to bear all the pain while others preserve their positions.
