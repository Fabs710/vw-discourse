The regional state government takes this seriously, and I want to be equally clear from our side.

We **do** agree that the company needs a durable restructuring. We are not here to defend inefficiency for its own sake, and we understand that the current cost base and market pressure are real. A credible future for the brand matters to the region, to the workforce, and to the company as a whole.

But we **do not** accept the framing that plant closures in Germany are the necessary or acceptable starting point. Nor do we accept compulsory redundancies as the default solution. The state is a shareholder and a political actor with a legitimate responsibility to protect regional economic stability, and that means we will not support a restructuring that destroys industrial capacity and jobs in our state when alternatives have not been exhausted.

### Where we agree
- The status quo is not sustainable.
- The company needs competitiveness, not slogans.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to a destructive confrontation.
- Voluntary instruments, redeployment, training, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures should all be on the table.
- Management and owners must contribute visibly; this cannot be treated as an employee-only adjustment.

### Where we disagree
We fundamentally disagree with the idea that **closure must remain on the table as an ordinary tool** simply because it is the easiest way to force savings. That may be a management preference, but it is not yet a justified necessity. Once a plant is closed, the damage is largely irreversible: jobs disappear, supplier networks weaken, regional value creation is lost, and trust in co-determination is badly damaged. That is not a cautious or responsible path.

We also reject any approach that treats the workforce as the only side expected to accept real pain. If labour is asked to contribute, then capital and management must do so in a fair and measurable way too — not only with words, but through concrete commitments on investment, product allocation, and restraint where appropriate.

### Our position
We are prepared to support a restructuring **if and only if** it is based on:
- **no plant closures in Germany as the default outcome,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **socially responsible capacity adjustment,**
- **fair and limited employee contributions,**
- **management and owner contributions,**
- **binding investment and future-product commitments for the German sites,**
- and a solution that preserves the company’s long-term role as a regional industrial anchor.

If management can produce an alternative package that achieves the necessary savings without closing plants and without forcing layoffs, then we are ready to engage seriously and quickly. But if the proposal remains “closures first, jobs later,” then I must say plainly: the state will use its rights, including its blocking position, to prevent a outcome that is economically shortsighted and politically unacceptable.

We are not here to block restructuring. We are here to insist that restructuring be done **responsibly**, with fairness, with a long-term industrial strategy, and with respect for the workers and regions that have made this company strong.
