Management, shareholders, and even the investors keep repeating the same formula: “structural problem, therefore closures and forced redundancies must stay on the table.” I reject that logic as the starting point.

### Let me be clear on what we accept
We accept that the company needs a durable restructuring.
We accept that there must be real productivity improvements.
We accept that all sides must contribute, including management and owners.
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- redeployment and retraining,
- capacity adjustments through better utilization,
- and product and investment commitments for German sites.

### What we do not accept
We do **not** accept:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- the breaking of historic job-security commitments,
- or a restructuring that treats the workforce as the sole adjustment variable.

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are red lines.

### On management’s argument
You say closures must remain “available” because alternatives must be equally credible. But you have not shown that closures are the only way to achieve savings. You have shown a desire for maximal flexibility. That is not the same thing.

If management claims the cost gap is so severe, then put the numbers on the table:
- what exact savings are required,
- by when,
- from which plants,
- and how much comes from labor costs, logistics, overhead, and management compensation.

So far, what we hear is a demand for a permanent weakening of job security without a transparent proof that every non-closure alternative has been exhausted.

### On the union’s position
We agree with the union that there must be a shared-sacrifice settlement and that management and owners must contribute visibly. We also agree that temporary labor-cost contributions can be discussed if they are limited, fair, and tied to binding future commitments.

But I want to be equally direct: “no closures” cannot be turned into a vague slogan and then left unsupported. We need a concrete alternative package that protects every German plant and every job as far as possible. That means using the industrial-relations tools available to us, not simply appealing to goodwill.

### On the regional state
We appreciate the state’s clear stance against closures and forced layoffs. But political support alone will not protect sites if we do not jointly define a credible industrial plan. The state should use its influence to push management and the owners toward:
- investment commitments,
- model allocation for German plants,
- training and technology programs,
- and a fair internal redistribution of work.

### On the family holding
You say you do not support a scorched-earth approach. Good. Then do not back management into using closure threats as a negotiating weapon. That destroys trust and makes any “durable” settlement harder, not easier.

If the family holding wants a responsible turnaround, then it must back:
- a no-closure framework,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring.

### On investors
You speak about “risk-adjusted equity value,” but the workforce is not a disposable cost item. A deal that undermines trust, breaches guarantees, and triggers industrial conflict is not “value creation”; it is value destruction. If you want durable competitiveness, you need industrial peace, skilled labor retention, and stable governance.

### Our minimum acceptable package
If you want an actual settlement, then it must include:
1. **Binding no-closure commitment for all German plants through 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of co-determination and core job-security protections**
4. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution only if matched by management and owners**
5. **Concrete investment and product commitments for German sites**
6. **Transparent joint review of productivity, logistics, and overhead reductions before any structural workforce reduction is even discussed**

### Our credible alternative to closures
Here is the alternative we insist on:
- capacity reduction through working-time measures and better utilization,
- voluntary retirement and departure programs only,
- internal redeployment across plants,
- training and qualification to shift workers into future products,
- a temporary solidarity contribution from labor,
- management pay restraint and shareholder contribution,
- and binding allocation of future models to German sites.

That is a credible path if management is serious about partnership. What is not credible is demanding plant closure authority upfront and then calling every other instrument “insufficient” before they have even been properly deployed.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate fast, but not on the premise that German plants must be sacrificed to prove seriousness. The workforce built this company, and it will not accept being used as the adjustment buffer for strategic errors.

If management, shareholders, and the state want a durable solution, then they must commit now to **no closures, no forced layoffs, and real future guarantees**. Anything less will meet resistance in the boardroom and in the plants.
