**Neutral moderator summary — Round 3**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a short-term disturbance.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **durable through 2030**, not merely temporary.
- Most parties accept that some combination of:
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - training/reskilling,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary labor-cost contributions,
  - management restraint,
  - productivity/logistics improvements,
  - and product or volume reallocation
  should be part of the discussion.
- There is shared support for an **independent plant-by-plant cost and capacity audit** and a comparative scenario review.
- There is common rejection of a **cosmetic deal** that leaves the underlying cost problem unresolved.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Closures and compulsory redundancies:**  
  The employee side and state government reject them as an accepted outcome; management, investors, and the family holding want them to remain available if alternatives fail.
- **Sequencing:**  
  Employee and state voices insist alternatives must be fully tested before closures are pre-authorized; management and investors say closure options cannot be ruled out in advance before the economics are proven.
- **Standard of equivalence:**  
  Management/investors define success as a package that removes enough **permanent fixed cost and capacity**.  
  Employee/state voices define success as a package that preserves sites and jobs while still achieving viability.
- **Governance constraints:**  
  The state insists its special statutory role and blocking power must not be weakened; management/investors want enough flexibility to act if the non-closure package is insufficient.
- **Bargaining baseline:**  
  Some treat closure as the benchmark instrument for savings; others reject that framing and argue it creates undue pressure on workers.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Magnitude vs. preservation:** How much permanent cost/capacity reduction can be achieved without closures, and what residual overcapacity would remain?
- **Speed vs. legitimacy:** Whether a faster closure-led solution is preferable to a slower but more acceptable non-closure package.
- **Certainty vs. flexibility:** Whether management can accept binding no-closure protections, or must retain fallback options if targets are missed.
- **Temporary relief vs. structural change:** Which proposed measures are truly durable and which only postpone the problem.
- **Regional employment vs. enterprise value:** How to balance plant/site protection with the need for a competitive cost base.
- **Enforceability:** How any compromise would be monitored, audited, and triggered if agreed savings are not achieved.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Keep the discussion centered on a **plant-by-plant comparison** of:
  1. closure,
  2. partial footprint reduction,
  3. and non-closure restructuring.
- Require each side to specify:
  - which costs are permanently removed,
  - which remain,
  - what capacity is actually taken out,
  - and what the payback/breakeven is through 2030.
- Use the audit to test whether a **credible non-closure package** can close the gap, rather than debating closure in the abstract.
- Clarify the decision rule for the next phase: **what evidence would be sufficient to keep closures off the table, and what evidence would make them unavoidable**.
