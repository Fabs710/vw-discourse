### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable restructuring**.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated solution** rather than a prolonged open conflict.
- Most actors accept that a package should include some mix of:
  - voluntary measures,
  - redeployment/retraining,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - productivity improvements,
  - and some form of contribution from management/owners as well as employees.
- There is shared recognition that the company must retain the ability to **invest in future products and technology through 2030**.
- All sides agree that a purely symbolic or cosmetic deal would not be sufficient.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures as the starting point and want them excluded as far as possible. Management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject them; management and investors keep them as a possible instrument if alternatives do not deliver enough savings.
- **Scale and permanence of labour-cost reductions:**  
  Management and investors want material, lasting labour-cost relief. Employee side is open to limited, negotiated contributions but rejects open-ended or structurally weakening cuts.
- **Role of collective guarantees/co-determination:**  
  Employee representatives want existing protections preserved; management wants flexibility to adjust legacy structures; shareholder-side actors support change if needed for competitiveness.
- **How to measure “necessity”:**  
  The state and employees argue alternatives to closures have not been exhausted; management, shareholders, and investors argue alternatives must match closures in scale and certainty, otherwise they are insufficient.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** how much employment protection can be retained while still reaching the required cost base.
- **Short-term social protection vs. long-term restructuring:** whether to prioritize avoiding immediate site/job losses or securing deeper structural savings now.
- **Voluntary vs. compulsory instruments:** whether voluntary exits, redeployment, and flexibility can deliver enough, or whether compulsory measures become unavoidable.
- **Employee concessions vs. investment commitments:** what level of workforce contribution would be matched by binding product, investment, and site guarantees.
- **Political constraints vs. market logic:** the extent to which public/ownership influence can limit closures without undermining financial viability.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from general red lines to a **comparative package discussion**:
- quantify the savings needed,
- specify which measures could deliver them,
- distinguish between **temporary** and **permanent** contributions,
- and identify what binding commitments each side would require in return.

A useful next step would be for each side to table:
1. its **minimum acceptable package**,  
2. its **non-negotiable red lines**, and  
3. one **credible alternative** to closures or forced redundancies, with numbers attached.
