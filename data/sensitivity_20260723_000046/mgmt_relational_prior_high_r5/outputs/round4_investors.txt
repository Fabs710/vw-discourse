## To management

## To the works council
I understand your resistance to closures and forced layoffs. From a shareholder perspective, I also want to avoid a prolonged conflict, because strikes and uncertainty destroy value.

But your position is still too absolute.

A blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not acceptable **unless** the non-closure package demonstrably restores competitiveness with the same permanence and certainty as hard measures. You have not shown that yet.

Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, retraining, and temporary wage moderation are useful. But they are not automatically enough. If the residual fixed-cost base remains uncompetitive, then preserving every site in its current form is just delaying the problem.

So I agree with you on sequencing — test alternatives properly first — but I do **not** agree that the outcome is pre-decided in advance.

## To the industrial union
You are right to insist that cosmetic concessions are worthless. I agree.

Where I disagree is your refusal to allow any fallback if the numbers do not work. A negotiated package can include employee contributions, but only if it also delivers:
- permanent cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- and execution certainty.

Temporary wage moderation or bonus restraint alone is not enough. If the non-closure package cannot close the gap, then the company must retain the ability to act. That is not anti-worker ideology; it is financial discipline.

## To the regional state government

If you want a no-closure path, then support a **verified** one:
- quantified savings,
- binding capacity reductions,
- and an enforceable timetable.

## To the controlling family holding

I am not asking for a closure-first approach. I am asking for a **credible fallback** if the non-closure package proves insufficient. That distinction matters.

## To the independent auditor

## My position
I can support a negotiated restructuring if it includes:
1. a serious independent plant-by-plant audit,
2. voluntary exits, redeployment, and retraining,
3. working-time flexibility and time-limited employee contributions,
4. management restraint and some capital-side discipline,
5. and binding investment/product commitments.

But I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that preserves structurally uncompetitive cost,
- or a deal that rules out every hard instrument before the audit proves the alternatives work.

If it does not, then management and owners must retain the ability to pursue harder measures.
