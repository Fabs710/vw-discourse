### Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **sequencing and instrument set**:

- **Employee side**: closures and compulsory redundancies must be excluded now; alternatives must be tested first and must be binding.
- **Management / investors / auditor logic**: alternatives may be accepted, but only if they can be shown to remove enough **permanent fixed cost** and **capacity**; closures must remain available if they cannot.
- **State / family holding**: want a durable restructuring, but also want to avoid irreversible damage; they are in the middle, but have not yet resolved whether “closures as fallback” is acceptable.
  
So the conflict is:
1. **Whether closures and compulsory redundancies are negotiable instruments or prohibited outcomes**
2. **What level of proof is required before hard measures can remain on the table**
3. **Whether a non-closure package can be made sufficiently permanent, enforceable, and large enough**

---

### Facilitator compromise options

#### 1) “Audit-first, decision-later” package with temporary standstill
A structured compromise could be:

- Management commits to **no closure announcement and no compulsory redundancy action** during the audit period.
- Employee side commits to **rapid disclosure of plant-level data and operational access** for the independent audit.
- All parties accept that the audit must compare:
  - closure,
  - partial footprint reduction,
  - non-closure restructuring,
  through 2030.

This does **not** decide the end state now, but it removes immediate closure pressure while the proof is built.

#### 2) “No-closure unless proven insufficient” conditional framework
A second compromise could be:

- The parties agree that the **preferred baseline** is a no-closure restructuring.
- Closures and compulsory redundancies are not used unless the independent audit shows, by plant, that:
  - the non-closure package leaves a defined residual cost gap,
  - volume cannot be transferred sufficiently,
  - and the gap cannot be closed by further permanent non-closure measures.

This bridges the gap by making closure a **conditional exception with explicit proof criteria**, not an opening demand.

#### 3) “Permanent non-closure package with predefined fallback triggers”
A third compromise could be:

- The employee side accepts that some **permanent** measures may be necessary:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - overhead removal,
  - model concentration,
  - footprint consolidation short of closure.
- Management accepts that closures and compulsory redundancies are **pre-excluded unless objective trigger thresholds are reached**.
- Those triggers would be predefined in the audit, such as:
  - minimum savings threshold,
  - minimum utilization threshold,
  - minimum payback period,
  - minimum transferability of volume.

This creates a rule-based path instead of an open-ended threat.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What specific plant-by-plant threshold would have to be missed before you would agree that closures and compulsory redundancies are no longer justified, and can you define that threshold now in writing?**

#### To the works council
**Would you accept a binding package of permanent non-closure measures if the independent audit proves it achieves the same 2030 cost reduction as a closure scenario, even if that requires significant site-level change and permanent capacity reduction short of closure?**

#### To the industrial union
**Will you commit to negotiate quantified permanent measures immediately if the counterpart accepts a formal no-closure standstill during the audit, or do you require closures to be removed from the table entirely before any data-sharing begins?**

#### To the regional state government
**Will you use your blocking position to prevent any closure decision until the plant-by-plant audit is completed, and if so, what exact conditions must be met for you to consider lifting that block later?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to support a framework that rules out closures unless and until the audit shows that no-closure restructuring cannot meet a predefined savings benchmark, or do you want closures to remain available without such a benchmark?**

#### To institutional investors
**Would you accept a non-closure restructuring if the audit shows it delivers equivalent permanent fixed-cost reduction and execution certainty by 2030, even if the route is operationally more complex than closure?**

#### To the independent auditor
**Can you define, in measurable terms, what level of residual cost and capacity shortfall would make a non-closure package materially inferior to closure, so the parties have a shared test rather than a vague debate?**
