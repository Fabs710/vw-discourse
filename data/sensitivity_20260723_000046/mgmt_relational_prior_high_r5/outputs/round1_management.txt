We hear the positions clearly, and I will be equally clear from management’s side.

I agree on one point: **we do need a durable, negotiated solution.** We cannot afford a protracted conflict that paralyses output. But let me reject the core premise of what you are proposing: **preserving every site, every job, and every historic guarantee regardless of economics is not a restructuring plan.** It is a recipe for continued decline.

The company is facing a structural cost crisis, not a temporary wobble.

- German factory costs are materially above target.
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competitors are leaner, faster, and often lower-cost.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- And we need investment capacity for future products and technology through 2030.

That means we need **real and lasting** change in both **capacity** and **labour cost**. Anything cosmetic will fail.

### Where I agree
I do agree that:
- purely punitive cuts would be counterproductive,
- we need a negotiated settlement,
- voluntary instruments, redeployment, and working-time flexibility should be part of the toolbox,
- and the company must keep investing in the future.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket refusal of plant closures,
- an absolute veto on compulsory reductions in workforce where alternatives are insufficient,
- or a position that demands management carry the adjustment alone while labour costs stay structurally unchanged.

If the cost base remains uncompetitive, then no amount of rhetoric about trust will save jobs in the medium term. In fact, failing to act now would put even more jobs and sites at risk later.

### On the union’s request for “shared sacrifice”
I am open to a genuine shared-sacrifice package. But it must be material, not symbolic.

That means:
- a **substantial labour-cost contribution**,
- a **meaningful reduction in overcapacity**,
- and a structure that delivers savings we can actually rely on through 2030.

A temporary bonus waiver alone will not do it. A small working-time tweak alone will not do it. A promise to “revisit later” will not do it.

### On closures
I am not declaring closures as an ideological goal. I am saying plainly that **the option must remain on the table** because we need structural savings of sufficient scale. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it needs to put forward an alternative that is equally credible, equally durable, and comparable in financial effect.

That would need to include, at minimum:
- deep capacity reductions,
- clearly quantified labour-cost relief,
- site-level productivity commitments,
- and enforceable adjustments to legacy guarantees that are now out of line with reality.

### On investment and future products
You are right that any restructuring must secure an industrial future. But that future will not be financed by a cost structure that destroys margins. We need to free up resources now, or we will not have the means to invest in 2030 products later.

### Bottom line
So my position is this:

- **No cosmetic deal.**
- **No preservation of the status quo.**
- **Yes to a negotiated package only if it delivers durable capacity reduction and a real labour-cost contribution.**
- **If that cannot be achieved, then deeper structural measures including closures remain necessary.**

I am prepared to continue discussions, but I will not sign up to a settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive and merely postpones the crisis.
