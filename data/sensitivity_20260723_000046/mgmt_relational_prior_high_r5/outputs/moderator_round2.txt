### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one central unresolved issue:

**Whether the restructuring can be made credible without pre-authorizing plant closures and compulsory redundancies.**

More precisely, the sides are split on three linked questions:
1. **Permanence:** Are non-closure measures capable of delivering durable savings through 2030?
2. **Equivalence:** Are they economically comparable to closures in scale, timing, and certainty?
3. **Commitment structure:** Can the employee side accept meaningful concessions only if management, owners, and investors make binding counter-commitments?

At present:
- the **employee/state side** wants a binding no-closure framework,
- **management/investors** want closures or equivalent hard measures to remain available,
- the **family holding** wants a durable turnaround but not a scorched-earth approach,
- and the **union/works council** want protections tied to concrete alternatives.

So the plateau is not about whether change is needed; it is about **which restructuring tools are admissible before the numbers are jointly validated**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options that could move the process forward.

#### 1) Conditional no-closure framework with a verified fallback
A temporary settlement could state:
- **No closures and no compulsory redundancies during an initial defined phase**;
- a **jointly audited financial bridge** must be completed within a fixed period;
- if the bridge shows that non-closure measures do not reach agreed savings thresholds, then **specific fallback options** are pre-defined for later negotiation.

This gives the employee side a protected period and gives management/investors a route to revisit hard measures if the numbers do not close.

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring package with plant-specific role changes
Instead of preserving all sites in full current form, the parties could explore:
- **no full closures initially**,
- but **site role changes**, model concentration, shift reductions, and partial footprint consolidation,
- combined with voluntary exits, redeployment, and temporary labor-cost contributions.

This may bridge the gap between “full no-closure” and “closure as default” by allowing structural capacity reduction without immediate shutdowns.

#### 3) Binding exchange deal: labor concessions for binding capital commitments
A structured exchange could link:
- employee concessions on working time, bonuses, and temporary pay moderation,
to
- **binding investment commitments, product allocation, training funding, and management/owner restraint**.

This would require:
- measurable savings,
- a timetable,
- and enforcement clauses so that neither side gives up leverage without reciprocal commitments.

This option does not resolve the closure question on its own, but it can create the trust needed to test whether closures are actually necessary.
