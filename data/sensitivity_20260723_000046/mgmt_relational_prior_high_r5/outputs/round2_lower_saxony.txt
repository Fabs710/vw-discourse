I will be equally direct.

The state government agrees that the company needs a durable restructuring and that the status quo is not an option. But I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to make plant closures and compulsory redundancies the default benchmark for seriousness.

### To management
You keep asking for “numbers,” and that is fair. Then let us speak concretely:

- If the issue is **overcapacity**, show us the full capacity-utilization picture plant by plant.
- If the issue is **labour cost**, show us the bridge: management compensation, overhead, procurement, logistics, working time, and employee contribution.
- If the issue is **competitiveness**, prove that non-closure measures cannot deliver the needed effect before demanding irreversible site decisions.

What I will not accept is a strategy that starts with closures because it is administratively simple and politically convenient. That is not the same as necessity.

You say temporary measures are insufficient. Possibly. But that does **not** automatically justify closures. It means we need a deeper negotiated package. I am open to one — but only if it preserves industrial capacity in Germany and does not gut regional employment.

### To the works council and union
I agree with you on the core principle: the workforce cannot be asked to bear the whole burden. And I support the insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no unilateral dismantling of protections.

Where I want to sharpen the discussion is this: “no closures” must not remain only a slogan. It needs to be backed by a credible industrial alternative that delivers savings and future competitiveness. The alternative package you have described is directionally right:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures only,
- redeployment,
- training,
- productivity gains,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and binding future-product commitments.

That is the right framework. But it must now be quantified and made enforceable.

### To the family holding
I welcome your rejection of a scorched-earth approach. But that must also mean rejecting closure threats as a routine bargaining device. If closures are used too early, they poison trust and make a durable settlement harder, not easier.

You are right that value must be restored. But value is not restored by destroying the company’s regional anchor function or by forcing a confrontation that weakens co-determination. Long-term value requires a stable industrial base and social peace.

### To the investors
I understand the demand for durability. I do not accept, however, the implication that any solution without closures is “cosmetic.” That is simply not true. A serious package can include:
- permanent capacity reductions through working-time and utilization measures,
- targeted productivity improvements,
- a limited, time-bound employee contribution,
- management pay restraint,
- and owner-side contribution through dividend discipline or capital support if needed.

If those measures are real, they can be economically meaningful without sacrificing sites.

### The state’s minimum line
Let me be unambiguous:  
- **No plant closures in Germany as the preferred or default solution.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No outcome that strips the state of its legitimate co-determination and blocking role.**
- **No restructuring that permanently damages regional employment and supplier networks.**

That does not mean “no change.” It means change must be socially responsible and economically credible.

### What I could support
I could support a package if it includes:
1. **Binding no-closure commitment unless all alternatives are demonstrably exhausted and proven insufficient**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Temporary, fair employee contribution**
4. **Visible management and owner contribution**
5. **Concrete investment and model commitments for German sites**
6. **Verified productivity, logistics, and overhead savings**
7. **A jointly monitored implementation path through 2030**

### What I cannot support
I will not support a deal that:
- makes closures the first resort,
- turns the workforce into the sole adjustment variable,
- uses “competitiveness” as a blank cheque for plant destruction,
- or weakens the state’s ability to protect regional economic stability.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to keep pushing for a negotiated restructuring. But I will only do so on the basis of a solution that preserves the company as an industrial anchor in this region.

If management wants my support, bring a package that protects sites, protects jobs, and restores competitiveness without sacrificing the company’s social foundation. If not, then I will use every right the special statute gives me to block an unacceptable outcome.
