I’ll respond directly and plainly.

## To the Works Council
We are aligned on the essential point: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. I support that line, and I also support a real industrial future for the German sites, not just a freeze that slowly erodes them.

Where I think we need to sharpen the position is this: it is not enough to say “no closures” and wait for management to accept it. We need to **back that demand with an alternative package that is concrete, measurable, and enforceable**. Otherwise management will keep claiming there is no credible path except cuts.

So I support your red lines, but I want us to be tougher on the numbers:
- what savings are required,
- which costs can be reduced instead,
- and what binding commitments must come with employee concessions.

## To Management
Your central argument is still the same: “we need durability, therefore closures and forced layoffs must remain on the table.” I do **not** accept that as a starting premise.

You have not shown that:
- all non-closure options have been exhausted,
- voluntary instruments cannot achieve enough,
- or that the workforce must bear the adjustment through permanent job destruction.

You keep saying “material and lasting” savings are needed. Fine. Then show the full cost bridge:
- how much comes from management pay,
- how much from dividends or shareholder contributions,
- how much from overhead and logistics,
- how much from capacity/utilization changes,
- and how much would still be needed from labor.

Right now, your position reads as: “give us the right to close plants, and we’ll decide later if we need it.” That is not a negotiated solution; it is a threat.

### My explicit line
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures as part of the settlement.**
- **No breaking of the collective wage framework as the price for management’s restructuring mistakes.**

If you want a deal, then it has to be built around **shared sacrifice**, not unilateral labor pain.

## To the Regional State Government
I appreciate your clear opposition to closures and forced redundancies. That is important and consistent with protecting the industrial base.

But I also want to be direct: political support must translate into pressure on **all** sides, not only moral statements. If the state wants a no-closure settlement, then it should help force the rest of the package into place:
- investment commitments,
- future-model allocation,
- training and qualification support,
- and visible contributions from management and owners.

A no-closure line is only credible if the rest of the deal is made real.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You say you do not want a scorched-earth approach. Good. Then do not back management into using closure threats as the default bargaining tactic.

If the family holding wants long-term value, then it should support:
- a no-closure framework,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a real contribution from capital, not just pressure on labor.

You cannot ask the workforce to pay, through wages and jobs, while capital is protected from meaningful contribution. That would not be a durable settlement; it would be imposed cost shifting.

## To Institutional Investors
You keep saying “value,” “certainty,” and “durability.” I agree with the need for durability. But you are missing the industrial reality: **a settlement that destroys trust and triggers conflict is not value creation**.

You are right that symbolic gestures are not enough. But you are wrong if you think the only serious alternative to closures is a cosmetic compromise. There is a serious alternative:
- working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- temporary employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- and binding investment and product commitments.

That is not soft; it is a restructuring that preserves the company’s industrial base.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to negotiate quickly on:
- temporary wage moderation or a solidarity contribution,
- forgoing bonuses,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reduction through utilization changes,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and training,
- and targeted productivity measures.

But only on the condition that:
- **German sites remain open,**
- **there are no forced layoffs,**
- **the collective bargaining framework remains intact,**
- and management/owners make measurable contributions too.

## What is unacceptable
Let me be unambiguous:
- **closures**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **open-ended wage cuts**
- **dismantling collective standards**
- **workers bearing the full burden while top management and owners stay insulated**

If that is the direction, then the peace obligation is already over, and the workforce will respond accordingly.

## Our challenge to management
If your numbers are real, then put them on the table in full. We are ready to deal with facts. But facts cut both ways:
- How much can be saved through management and overhead?
- How much through production redesign and productivity?
- How much through reduced executive remuneration and owner contribution?
- How much through flexible working time and temporary contributions?

If, after exhausting those measures, you still claim you need closures, then we will say no. Because if the company can only “save” itself by breaking the workforce and permanently damaging German industrial capacity, then the restructuring is wrong.

## Bottom line
Our position is unchanged and reinforced:

1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No weakening of collective wage standards as the main solution**
4. **Yes to a fair, shared-sacrifice package**
5. **Yes to temporary employee contributions if matched by management and owners**
6. **Yes to binding investment, product, and site commitments**

We are not here to protect everything for free. We are here to prevent the company from solving a structural problem by dumping it onto the workforce alone.

If management wants a settlement, bring a real alternative package with numbers. If not, then this will be settled not by goodwill, but by industrial power.
