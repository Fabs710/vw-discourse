1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real, durable restructuring** to restore competitiveness.
- There is broad agreement that **cosmetic or delayed measures are insufficient**.
- Most parties accept that **some form of labour contribution and capacity adjustment** will likely be necessary.
- There is shared concern that **prolonged conflict, uncertainty, or strikes would destroy value**.
- All sides acknowledge that **future investment capacity and long-term competitiveness** matter.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures vs. preservation of sites:** Management, investors, and the family see closures as an available option if needed; the state, union, and works council reject closures as a first resort or at all.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Management has not ruled out hard structural change; the union and works council reject forced redundancies.
- **How to define an “acceptable alternative”:** Investors/family/management want alternatives to be economically equivalent to closures; labour-side actors argue that benchmark is too narrow and should include job, site, and social protections.
- **Burden-sharing:** Labour-side actors insist management, owners, and capital must also contribute; management and investors focus primarily on achieving the required savings.
- **Governance and regional role:** The state and works council emphasize co-determination, site security, and regional industrial capacity; management and shareholders emphasize execution, cost reduction, and margin recovery.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- The parties have not yet quantified **how much savings are required** and what mix of measures would be sufficient.
- There is no agreed comparison between **closure-based savings** and **non-closure alternatives** in timing, enforceability, and permanence.
- The discussion has not resolved the balance between **job security, site security, and competitiveness**.
- No one has yet specified which concessions would be exchanged for which protections in a **balanced package**.
- The relationship between **investment commitments** and **employee concessions** remains undefined.
- It is still unclear whether a deal can be reached that avoids closures while still delivering **structural cost reduction** at the required scale.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Shift from positions to **numbers and package design**: what savings are required, by when, and through which measures.
- Test specific alternatives against a shared standard: **durability, enforceability, timing, and economic equivalence**.
- Clarify what each side is prepared to give and receive on:
  - capacity reduction,
  - labour cost,
  - site security,
  - investment commitments,
  - and governance safeguards.
- Aim to identify whether there is a **single negotiated package** that can restore competitiveness while limiting social and regional damage.
