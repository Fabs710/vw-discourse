### Core impasse
The central impasse is **not about whether restructuring is needed**; all parties now accept that it is. The impasse is about **the means and distribution of pain**:

1. **Management / investors / controlling family** want a package that is **economically equivalent to closures**: permanent savings, real capacity reduction, enforceability, and quick impact.
2. **Union / works council / regional state** require that restructuring **does not cross red lines**: no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, preserved sites, and a socially balanced burden-sharing model.

So the conflict is essentially:
- **economic equivalence vs. social red lines**
- **hard capacity removal vs. site preservation**
- **speed/durability vs. workforce protection and governance constraints**

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three possible bridge positions for structured negotiation:

#### 1) Mixed restructuring package with verified savings
A package combining:
- targeted capacity reduction through shift consolidation, product reallocation, and line rationalization,
- temporary pay restraint and bonus reductions,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- working-time adjustments and productivity measures,
- and only a limited number of site-specific measures if the savings target is not met.

**Bridge value:**  
This allows labour to avoid immediate closures while giving capital stakeholders a measurable, enforceable savings path.

---

#### 2) Phased conditionality model
Agree now on:
- a first-phase package with site preservation, labour contributions, and operational changes,
- plus a pre-defined second-phase trigger mechanism if savings or demand thresholds are missed.

For example:
- if agreed annual run-rate savings are not achieved by a fixed date, then additional capacity measures automatically activate.

**Bridge value:**  
This gives investors and management enforceability while giving unions and the state a chance to prove non-closure measures can work first.

---

#### 3) Site protection in exchange for stronger structural concessions
A bargain in which:
- German sites remain open for a defined period,
- but employees accept deeper, time-bound concessions in wages, hours, bonuses, and flexibility,
- while owners and management provide investment commitments and governance assurances.

**Bridge value:**  
This is a “jobs now, concessions now, investment later” structure that may align regional and workforce concerns with capital’s demand for real savings.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Institutional Investors
**What minimum annual run-rate savings and capacity reduction would you accept as genuinely equivalent to closures, and by what date must they be in place?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Which specific non-closure measures would you accept as sufficient to justify withholding support for plant closures, and what governance safeguards would you require in return?**

#### Regional State Government
**Which quantified restructuring package would you be prepared to support even if it included significant site specialization, shift reductions, or limited site restructuring short of full closures?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What exact combination of labour-cost relief, capacity reduction, and timing would meet your internal threshold for a durable turnaround without requiring plant closures?**

#### Industrial Union
**Which permanent labour contributions are you prepared to put on the table that would be large enough to satisfy the savings requirement while still preserving your red lines?**

#### Works Council
**If closures remain excluded, what specific operational changes at plant level are you willing to endorse that would deliver measurable and enforceable savings?**
