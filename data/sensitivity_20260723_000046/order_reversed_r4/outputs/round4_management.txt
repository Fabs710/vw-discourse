Let me be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central point: **the company needs a real restructuring now**.  
I also agree that:
- cosmetic measures are worthless,
- delay destroys value,
- prolonged strikes would be damaging,
- and any final package must be **durable, enforceable, and reflected in the P&L**.

So the diagnosis is not in dispute.

## Where I disagree
What I **do not** accept is the attempt to turn “site preservation” into a principle that overrides the economics of the business.

We are not facing a theoretical debate. We are facing:
- a shrinking European market,
- brutal competition,
- a cost base that is too high,
- and a brand that is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we do not reduce structural cost and capacity, then we will not restore competitiveness. That is the reality.

### To the union and works council
You keep repeating:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no breaking of guarantees,
- no real job losses.

I understand the political logic. I do **not** accept it as an economic answer.

A package built only on:
- working-time tweaks,
- voluntary exits,
- bonus restraint,
- productivity promises,

is **not automatically enough**. If the fixed cost base stays too high, then we have not solved the problem — we have postponed it.

If you want to rule out closures, then you must show a **quantified, durable, enforceable alternative** that closes the savings gap. Not slogans. Not principles. Numbers.

### To the regional state government
I respect the regional responsibility, but I reject the suggestion that preserving every site is the test of responsible stewardship.

A company that remains structurally uncompetitive will not be a reliable employer in the region.  
So the state should not ask us to protect legacy structures at the expense of the company’s future.

If you want site preservation, then help produce a package that is economically equivalent. Otherwise, you are asking us to subsidize excess capacity indefinitely.

### To the controlling family
You are right about one thing: a brittle compromise is not a solution.  
But that cuts both ways.

A package that preserves everything and leaves the cost base broken is also brittle. It merely delays the harder decision and risks an even worse one later.

I am prepared to support a negotiated solution if it is truly equivalent. I am **not** prepared to support a settlement that looks socially balanced but leaves the business structurally weak.

### To the institutional investors
You are right to insist on:
- annual run-rate savings,
- timing,
- permanence,
- enforceability,
- and margin recovery.

That is exactly the lens management is using.

But I will say this bluntly: if you want a non-closure route, then “good intentions” are not enough. The savings must be:
- large,
- durable,
- and credible enough to restore investor confidence.

If not, then we are merely avoiding a confrontation while preserving an uncompetitive footprint. That is not value creation.

## What I actually need
What I need from the labour side and the state is not another assertion that closures are undesirable.

What I need is one of two things:

1. **A quantified non-closure package** that is genuinely closure-equivalent on savings, timing, permanence, and enforceability; or  
2. **Acceptance that hard structural measures, including closures, remain necessary** if the numbers cannot be closed otherwise.

There is no third option that restores competitiveness.

## On the expert view
The expert has said the decisive issue is equivalence:
- closures are strongest on permanence and enforceability;
- non-closure packages are only viable if they are unusually deep and tightly designed.

That is exactly my position.

So let’s stop pretending that a soft package will do. It will not.

## My boundary
I am prepared to negotiate on the **instruments**.  
I am **not** prepared to negotiate away the objective.

That means:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to a hybrid package if it delivers real structural savings.
- **Yes** to a non-closure package only if it is proven equivalent.
- **No** to symbolic site protection.
- **No** to preserving excess capacity.
- **No** to a deal that leaves the German cost base uncompetitive.

## What I want next
I want management to bring back a **single quantified comparison**:
- closure-based package,
- hybrid package,
- non-closure package,

with:
- annual run-rate savings,
- one-time costs,
- timing to full effect,
- enforceability,
- and employment/site impact.

If a non-closure or hybrid package really matches the economics, I will prefer that.  
If it does not, then we should not pretend otherwise.
