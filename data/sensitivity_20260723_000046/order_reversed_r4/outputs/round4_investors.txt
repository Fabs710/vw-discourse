We are still at the same decision point: **competitiveness first, optics second**. From our side, nothing has changed.

## Where we agree
- We agree with management that the German cost base is too high.
- We agree with the family that stability without competitiveness is worthless.
- We agree with the expert that **closures are the strongest instrument** for permanent savings.
- We agree with the union and works council that a prolonged conflict destroys value.

So yes: **a real restructuring is necessary**.

## Where we disagree
What we do **not** accept is the attempt to treat a site-preserving deal as inherently good enough just because it is politically easier.

That is not our standard. Our standard is:
- **annual run-rate savings**
- **timing**
- **permanence**
- **enforceability**
- **margin recovery**

If a non-closure package cannot match that, then it is not a serious alternative. It is a delay mechanism.

## To the union and works council
You keep saying closures and compulsory redundancies are non-starters. That may be your bargaining position, but it does not answer the economics.

You have offered:
- working-time changes,
- bonus restraint,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity measures,
- capacity reduction without closures.

That may contribute. But unless you can show the **quantified P&L effect** and explain why it is durable, it remains incomplete.

We do not need ideological assurances. We need:
- how many euros of savings,
- by when,
- for how long,
- and what stops the cost base from drifting back.

If you cannot produce that, then your package is not equivalent to closures.

## To the state government
We understand the regional role. But preserving industrial presence is not the same as preserving every current site and structure.

If you want us to support a non-closure solution, then it must be more than a political compromise. It must be a **business solution**.

That means:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- lower labor costs,
- clear execution milestones,
- and no ambiguity about the margin outcome.

If not, you are effectively asking shareholders to subsidize excess capacity in the hope that conflict avoidance will somehow create competitiveness. It will not.

## To the controlling family
You say you are pro-economic reality. Then the only useful question is whether the alternative really matches the economics.

We are open to a hybrid or site-preserving package **only if** it is independently validated as economically equivalent. If it is weaker, then supporting it would be a failure of stewardship, not prudence.

## To management
You need to stop speaking in generalities and bring a single quantified package.

Not:
- “closures remain on the table,”
- “we need more flexibility,”
- “we need shared sacrifice.”

We need:
1. **Required annual savings**
2. **Source of savings by measure**
3. **Timing to full run-rate**
4. **Permanence / duration**
5. **Fallback if targets are missed**
6. **Effect on return-on-sales**

Until you provide that, you have not shown that the non-closure route can restore competitiveness.

## Our position on the compromise paths
### 1) Hybrid restructuring with a hard savings floor
This is the only non-closure route we would even consider. But the floor has to be real, independently verified, and tied to credible fallback action. If the fallback is weak, the whole package is weak.

### 2) Phased conditionality with automatic trigger
Potentially acceptable, but only if the trigger is automatic, credible, and not subject to future bargaining. If “phase 2” is just another negotiation, it is worthless.

### 3) Site protection in exchange for concessions
Acceptable only if the concessions are **large enough to be materially closure-like** in the P&L. Otherwise it is a cosmetic settlement.

## What we need to hear next
If the labour side wants to avoid closures, then show us:
- the **minimum annual savings** you can commit to,
- the **exact measures** producing them,
- whether they are **permanent**,
- and the **enforcement mechanism** if delivery slips.

If you can’t, then we should not pretend a non-closure package is sufficient.

## Bottom line
We are not attached to closures as a principle. We are attached to **returns and competitiveness**.

So our position remains:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to a non-closure package **only if** it is truly equivalent.
- **No** to symbolic settlements.
- **No** to preserving excess capacity while margins stay impaired.
- **No** to a deal that protects appearances and weakens the business.

If management, the union, and the state want capital-market support, they need to bring **numbers, enforceability, and a credible margin path**. Otherwise, we will regard the outcome as insufficient.
