I will be equally direct.

We all agree on one thing: the company needs a **real** restructuring, not cosmetics. But that is where the agreement ends. The current management line still treats **plant closures and forced job destruction** as the default answer. That is unacceptable.

### To management
You say alternatives only matter if they are “economically equivalent.” Fine. Then prove it. But do not hide behind that phrase to force through closures as a fait accompli. If you want the works council to consider a serious settlement, it must contain:

- **binding site guarantees**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no closure of German plants**
- **real investment commitments**
- **co-determination in shaping the restructuring**

Without that, you are not asking for a negotiation; you are asking the workforce to sign off on dismantling its own future.

### To the controlling family and institutional investors
You speak about durable competitiveness, and I do not dispute that the company must remain competitive. But your language about “capital reallocation” and “value creation” cannot be allowed to override the social and legal reality of this company. We are not a disposable cost item.

You also say you want execution certainty. So do we. But certainty is impossible if management keeps threatening closures and breaking historic guarantees. The instability is being created from the top.

If you want returns, then the company needs a solution that is **stable, lawful, and socially sustainable**, not one that provokes industrial conflict across German sites.

### To the regional state government
I welcome your clear rejection of closures as a first resort and your insistence on preserving industrial capacity. On that, we are aligned. But your support must be more than political language. If the state says it will not accept hollowing-out, then it should use its role to block any plan that destroys sites or imposes forced layoffs.

### On the substance
We are prepared to talk about:
- working-time measures
- temporary sacrifices
- productivity improvements
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- capacity adjustments **without closures**
- negotiated employee contributions

But we are **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures
- compulsory redundancies
- the breaking of job-security commitments
- one-sided cost cuts imposed on workers alone

### My message is simple
If management wants a durable solution, it must come forward with a package that keeps all German plants open, protects employment, and respects co-determination. Anything less will be met with resistance, including mobilisation of the workforce.

We are not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that sacrifices jobs to make the numbers look better for a quarter. We are here to defend the workforce and the company’s long-term industrial base.
