We appreciate the moderator’s framing, but we need to be explicit: **a package without hard, durable savings is not acceptable**, even if it is socially smoother.

## Our position
We are prepared to support a **non-closure solution** only if it is genuinely **economically equivalent** to closures in:
- annual run-rate savings,
- timing,
- permanence,
- and enforceability.

If not, then it is a cosmetic settlement that preserves the appearance of peace while leaving the cost base broken. That would be value-destructive.

---

## To management
You are right that the German cost base is too high and that the footprint must change. We agree.

But saying “closures remain on the table” is not enough. The market will not reward threats; it will reward results.

What we need from you is a **numbers-first package**:
- quantified savings,
- clear capacity reductions,
- a binding timetable,
- and a mechanism that prevents costs from creeping back.

If you cannot show that a non-closure route gets you to competitive margins quickly and durably, then you are asking shareholders to accept a weaker business for the sake of avoiding confrontation. We will not endorse that.

---

## To the union
We hear your red lines, but they do not override the economics of the business.

A package that says:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no real job losses,
- but still somehow achieves full competitiveness,

is not credible unless the labour-side contribution is substantial and permanent. Working-time tweaks, voluntary exits, and productivity pledges may help, but by themselves they are often too soft, too slow, and too reversible.

If you want us to accept site preservation, then you must demonstrate a **material P&L impact**. Otherwise you are asking capital to finance an uncompetitive structure indefinitely.

---

## To the works council
We respect co-determination, but co-determination is not a veto over economic reality.

We do not accept the argument that because closures are destructive, they should therefore be ruled out categorically. The question is not whether closures are pleasant; it is whether the alternative is strong enough.

If plant-level measures can deliver the same savings, fine. But if the answer is only “keep the plants open and trust us,” that is not a restructuring. That is a deferral.

---

## To the regional state government
We understand the regional importance of the plants and the political sensitivity. But the state’s role cannot be to lock in excess capacity.

A company that never gets back to competitive margins will not remain a strong employer in the region. So preserving every site at all costs is not a success metric; it is a slow decline strategy.

If you want us to support a site-preserving solution, then it must include:
- credible capacity reduction,
- visible labour-cost relief,
- and a genuine return to profitability.

---

## To the controlling family
We agree that stability without competitiveness is meaningless. But ownership support should be conditional on a package that actually fixes the economics.

If the family wants to avoid closures, then it should back a hard alternative with enforceable savings rather than a politically comfortable compromise. The capital markets will not give credit for symbolism.

---

## What would move us
We would support a negotiated package if it includes all of the following:
1. **A quantified annual savings target** that is large enough to restore competitiveness.
2. **Real capacity reduction**, not just reallocation of labels.
3. **Permanent labor-cost relief** that shows up in the P&L.
4. **A binding delivery timeline** with consequences if missed.
5. **Protection against rebound** in the cost base.
6. **A credible path back to margin target and investment capacity**.

---

## What we reject
We will not support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a delay tactic,
- a package that keeps excess capacity intact,
- or a settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the only way to reach the required economics is through closures, then we accept that as a legitimate tool. We are not ideologically attached to closures, but we are attached to returns and competitiveness.

---

## Response to the moderator’s bridge options
- **Mixed restructuring package:** acceptable only if the savings are real and verified.
- **Phased conditionality:** potentially acceptable, but only if the first phase is substantial and the trigger mechanism is automatic and credible, not a future bargaining loophole.
- **Site protection in exchange for stronger concessions:** acceptable only if the concessions are large enough to offset the loss of closure-level savings.

So our bottom line is unchanged: **show us the numbers, show us enforceability, and show us a durable path to competitive margins.** Without that, we will regard the outcome as insufficient and value-destructive.
