Yes. The core question is whether a **non-closure package can be made credibly equivalent to closures** on savings, timing, permanence, and enforceability.

## Short answer
It can be **partly equivalent**, but in large German automotive restructurings it is usually **harder to make fully equivalent** than a closure-based package.

### Why closures are stronger
Closures score best on:
- **permanence**: capacity is removed outright
- **enforceability**: savings are not dependent on future bargaining
- **speed of structural effect**: once executed, fixed cost base falls materially
- **credibility with capital markets**: less execution risk

### Why non-closure packages can still work
A non-closure package can be credible if it combines several deep measures at once:
- **shift / line consolidation**
- **product reallocation**
- **headcount reduction via voluntary exits + natural attrition**
- **working-time reductions**
- **temporary or permanent wage restraint**
- **bonus / allowance cuts**
- **staffing norm tightening**
- **overhead reduction**
- **binding fallback triggers**

But to be closure-equivalent, it must close the savings gap on a **run-rate basis**, not just in one-off accounting terms.

## Minimum elements for a credible non-closure package
To be taken seriously as a substitute for closures, I would expect:

1. **Quantified annual run-rate savings**
   - a hard euro target by measure and by site
   - not just “productivity improvements”

2. **Fast implementation**
   - first savings in the current year
   - near-full run-rate within 12–24 months, depending on contract and notice periods

3. **Durability**
   - wage and hour concessions lasting multiple years
   - staffing rules changed in a way that cannot easily revert

4. **Enforceability**
   - automatic fallback if targets are missed
   - no open-ended renegotiation

5. **Real capacity reduction**
   - fewer shifts, fewer lines, lower output capacity
   - otherwise fixed-cost savings will be too small

6. **Clear employer-side contribution**
   - overhead cuts, investment discipline, product simplification
   - otherwise labour bears the whole burden

## Practical assessment
### If the target is mainly short-term cash relief
A non-closure deal can often do that.

### If the target is durable structural cost reset
A non-closure deal must be unusually deep, and even then it may fall short unless it includes:
- substantial capacity rationalization
- permanent labour-cost relief
- and a strict fallback mechanism

## What would make it credible in this case
Given your scenario, I would treat a non-closure package as credible only if it includes:
- **material annual savings**
- **explicit capacity reduction**
- **multi-year labour concessions**
- **automatic trigger clauses**
- **independent verification**
- and ideally some **site specialization / footprint reduction**

## Bottom line
A non-closure restructuring can be a real turnaround tool, but in a large German carmaker it is only equivalent to closures if it is:
- **deep enough**
- **fast enough**
- **durable enough**
- and **enforceable enough**

Absent that, it is a delay mechanism rather than a true substitute.
