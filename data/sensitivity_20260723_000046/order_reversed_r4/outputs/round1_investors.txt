Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable, and enforceable return to competitive margins**.

What we care about is not preserving every plant or every legacy guarantee. We care about **risk-adjusted value creation**. If German operations remain structurally overcosted, margins will stay weak, the market will keep discounting the stock, and capital should be reallocated elsewhere.

So our priorities are:

- **Meaningful, permanent cost reduction**
- **Capacity aligned with realistic demand**
- **A clear path back to the return-on-sales target**
- **Execution certainty, not endless conflict**
- **Protection of future investment capacity through higher competitiveness**

What we would support:
- A package that achieves savings quickly and durably
- Workforce and labor contributions that are material, not symbolic
- Capacity reductions if that is what is required to restore competitiveness
- A negotiated solution if it is genuinely comparable to plant closures in financial effect

What we would not support:
- A cosmetic compromise that preserves excess capacity
- Delayed action that only pushes the problem forward
- Any deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired
- Prolonged strikes or uncertainty that destroy value without solving the underlying issue

We are open to alternatives to plant closures, but only if they are **economically equivalent** in terms of savings, structural discipline, and timing. If the final outcome is just a way to avoid hard decisions while keeping the same cost problem, we will oppose it.
