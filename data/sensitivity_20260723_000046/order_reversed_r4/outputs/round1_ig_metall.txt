We agree on one point: **the company needs a real solution, not a cosmetic one**. If the German cost base stays structurally too high, then competitiveness, investment capacity, and ultimately jobs are all at risk. On that, we are not naive.

But I need to be equally clear: **we will not accept a restructuring that makes employees bear all the pain while management, owners, and capital get to declare victory**.

### Where we agree
- The company needs **durable competitiveness**.
- There has to be **meaningful structural adjustment**.
- Pure delay, soft promises, or symbolic measures are not enough.
- Future investment and product allocation matter.

### Where we disagree
We fundamentally reject the idea that the only serious path is **plant closures and forced redundancies**. That is not a “hard truth”; it is a choice.

We also reject the framing that workers must first give up wages, guarantees, jobs, and sites before others contribute. A deal that preserves ownership returns while shifting the burden onto employees is **not fair and will not be accepted**.

### Our bottom lines
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

Those are not bargaining gestures. They are the limits of what is acceptable.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We already signaled openness to a **shared-sacrifice package**:
- labor-cost contributions,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction through non-compulsory means,
- forgoing bonuses or other employee contributions,
- and other reversible, negotiated measures.

But these only make sense if they are paired with:
- **site security,**
- **job security,**
- **investment commitments,**
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

If you want the workforce to contribute, then the burden must be shared. That means:
- management must also give on costs and flexibility,
- owners must accept restraint on payouts and pressure for short-term returns,
- and the company must commit to securing industrial sites and future products.

### To management
You say closures may be necessary if alternatives are not “economically equivalent.” We are saying the benchmark cannot be defined so narrowly that only closures qualify. If you want savings, then negotiate them seriously:
- through capacity planning,
- redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- temporary sacrifices,
- and productivity measures.

But do not present plant closure as the default and then call everything else “insufficient” by definition.

### To the shareholders and state
You are right that the company needs competitiveness. But competitiveness cannot be built on social destruction. A durable settlement will be stronger if it preserves skills, trust, and industrial capability instead of provoking open conflict.

### Our position in one sentence
We are ready for **substantial employee contribution**, but only in a package that **protects jobs, protects sites, and ensures that management and owners carry their share**.

If that is on the table, we can negotiate seriously.  
If the demand remains “jobs and plants must go first,” then you should be under no illusion: **the union will resist with all available means**.
