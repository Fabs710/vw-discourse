I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family on one essential point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic settlement**. The cost base is too high, the competitiveness problem is real, and delay would only make the eventual adjustment harsher.

I also agree with the union, works council, and the expert that **prolonged conflict would destroy value**. A strike spiral or governance deadlock would hurt the company, the workforce, suppliers, and the region.

So yes: the status quo is not an option.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated framing that plant closures are the default benchmark for seriousness.

That is too narrow, and for this company and this region it is also too risky. A closure may be economically clean in a model, but it is not automatically the right answer if it destroys regional industrial capacity, damages trust, and weakens the company’s long-term role as an anchor employer.

I am also not prepared to endorse a package that shifts the decisive burden onto workers while treating owners, management, and capital as passive observers.

## To management
You are right that a non-closure package must be **durable, enforceable, and material**. But you have not yet shown that closures are unavoidable.

What I need from you is not rhetoric about “clean” solutions, but a **quantified comparison**:
- closure package,
- hybrid package,
- non-closure package,

with:
1. annual run-rate savings,
2. one-time costs,
3. timing to full effect,
4. permanence,
5. and site/employment impact.

Until that is on the table, I will not treat closures as justified.

## To the investors
You are right to insist on numbers and enforcement. I support that.  
But I reject the idea that only closure-level economics count as “real.”

A **hybrid package** with real capacity reduction, labor-cost relief, and binding fallback clauses may be sufficient if it protects core sites and preserves the company’s regional function. That is not softness; that is responsible stewardship.

What I will not support is using the language of equivalence to force through irreversible site destruction as the first resort.

## To the controlling family
I agree with you that stability without competitiveness is worthless. But I also think you are too quick to treat closures as the benchmark of credibility.

If you truly want long-term stewardship, then support a package that:
- protects viable sites,
- preserves the state’s co-determination role,
- delivers real savings,
- and avoids unnecessary regional damage.

A brittle solution that wins the short-term cost fight but breaks industrial trust is not good ownership.

## To the union and works council
I support your insistence on:
- no compulsory redundancies as a first resort,
- no unnecessary closures,
- and genuine burden-sharing.

That said, I will be candid: if you want the state to defend site preservation, you must be prepared to make **material contributions** that are visible in the P&L and durable over time.

What I cannot support is a package that preserves every structure unchanged and simply renames delay as social balance.

## My position as anchor shareholder
My bottom line is unchanged:

- **No** to plant closures as the automatic first answer.
- **No** to forced redundancies as a default instrument.
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to labor contributions, if they are part of a balanced package.
- **Yes** to targeted capacity reduction, if it preserves core sites and the region’s industrial base.
- **Yes** to enforceable savings, if they are paired with investment and governance security.

I will support a negotiated solution **only if** it protects the company’s role as a regional economic anchor and preserves meaningful co-determination. That is not a side issue; it is part of what makes this company viable in the long run.

Before anyone pushes closures as the “necessary” option, management should present a fully quantified comparison of:
- what closures would save,
- what a hybrid package would save,
- what a non-closure package would save,
- and what each means for jobs, plants, suppliers, and future investment.
