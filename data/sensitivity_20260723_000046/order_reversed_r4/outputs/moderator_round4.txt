### Core impasse
The impasse now appears to be **not over whether restructuring is needed, but over what counts as a credible restructuring**.

More specifically, the deadlock sits on four linked questions:

1. **Equivalence standard**  
   Investors, management, and the family want a package that matches closure economics on a quantified basis.  
   The union, works council, and state reject closures as the default benchmark and want non-closure solutions treated as genuine alternatives.

2. **Capacity versus site preservation**  
   Management and capital want structural capacity reduction, including possible closures.  
   Labour and the state want site protection and no compulsory redundancies.  
   The unresolved issue is how much capacity reduction can be achieved without closing plants.

3. **Enforceability and fallback**  
   All sides say they want enforceable savings, but they differ on whether automatic fallback clauses can be made strong enough without future renegotiation.

4. **Burden sharing**  
   Labour is offering concessions, but other stakeholders are unconvinced they are large or durable enough.  
   Labour, in turn, says owners, management, and capital are not sufficiently sharing the adjustment burden.

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled for discussion.

#### 1) Quantified hybrid package with automatic escalation
A package combining:
- working-time changes,
- bonus and allowance restraint,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- productivity and staffing-norm changes,
- selective line or shift consolidation,
- and targeted product/volume reallocation.

**Feature:** If verified run-rate savings fall short at a fixed checkpoint, pre-agreed fallback measures automatically activate.  
**Purpose:** Gives investors enforceability and gives labour a route to avoid immediate closures.

---

#### 2) Phased restructuring with independent verification gates
A two-step model:
- **Phase 1:** implement all non-closure measures immediately and verify savings within a set period.
- **Phase 2:** only if the savings gap remains material, activate further capacity measures, potentially including closures or deeper footprint reduction.

**Feature:** Phase 2 is not reopened to bargaining; it is triggered mechanically by the verification outcome.  
**Purpose:** Reduces the risk of a premature closure decision while preserving a credible backstop.

---

#### 3) Site-preservation package with closure-like economics in selected functions
Instead of closing whole plants, the parties could agree to:
- remove specific low-value functions,
- consolidate selected production lines,
- specialize remaining sites,
- and permanently reduce overhead layers and support capacity.

**Feature:** This preserves core sites while still delivering structural savings and capacity reduction.  
**Purpose:** Gives the state and works council a stronger site-preservation basis while trying to satisfy capital’s demand for material savings.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Institutional Investors
What minimum verified annual run-rate saving, by date, would make a non-closure or hybrid package acceptable to you without requiring immediate plant closures?

#### Controlling Family
Would you support a site-preserving restructuring if an independent model showed it closes most of the savings gap, even if it does not fully replicate closure economics?

#### Regional State Government
What specific capacity reductions and labour-cost concessions are you prepared to endorse publicly if they are the price of avoiding plant closures?

#### Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to publishing a single side-by-side financial comparison of closure, hybrid, and non-closure packages, including one-time costs, timing, enforceability, and return-on-sales impact?

#### Industrial Union
What concrete, quantified package of labour concessions are you prepared to bind into a multi-year agreement so that the savings can be independently verified?

#### Works Council
Which site-level changes, if any, would you accept as part of a non-closure compromise, provided that compulsory redundancies are excluded and employment guarantees remain intact?

#### Expert
What exact combination of labour relief, capacity reduction, and fallback triggers would you define as the minimum threshold for a non-closure package to be considered closure-equivalent?
