### Core impasse

The central impasse is **not** over the need to restructure; all parties accept that a restructuring is necessary. The impasse is over **instrument choice and evidentiary standard**:

- **Management / investors / family** say the package must deliver **closure-level, durable, enforceable savings**, and they view closures as the benchmark if alternatives cannot prove equivalence.
- **Union / works council** reject closures and compulsory redundancies as non-starters, and they insist that a serious non-closure package can still be structurally effective.
- **State government** wants restructuring, but only if it preserves regional industrial capacity and avoids irreversible site destruction.

So the deadlock is: **what counts as sufficient proof that a non-closure package is “real” restructuring, versus a cosmetic compromise?**

---

### Possible compromise positions

#### 1) Hybrid restructuring with a hard savings floor
A compromise package could combine:
- **limited, targeted capacity reduction**,
- **working-time and bonus adjustments**,
- **voluntary exits / attrition / redeployment**,
- **product or line consolidation**,
- and **binding labor-cost relief**.

Key feature: the agreement would include a **minimum quantified annual run-rate savings target** that must be met, with a **fallback mechanism** if it is not achieved.

This could bridge:
- investors’ demand for real savings,
- management’s need for enforceability,
- union/work council resistance to blanket closures,
- and the state’s desire to preserve core sites.

#### 2) Phased conditionality with automatic trigger
A second option is a **two-step deal**:
- **Phase 1:** immediate site-preserving measures with measurable savings and a short implementation window.
- **Phase 2:** an automatic trigger for harder measures, including selective closures or deeper capacity cuts, **only if** the phase-1 savings do not materialize on schedule.

This may bridge the divide by:
- giving labor a chance to prove a non-closure alternative,
- giving capital a credible fallback,
- and avoiding an upfront commitment to either full closure or full preservation.

#### 3) Site protection in exchange for stronger structural concessions
A third option is a **site-preservation pact** in which the company keeps core plants open, but in return labor accepts a more substantial package:
- longer-duration wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- permanent shift simplification,
- tighter staffing norms,
- productivity commitments,
- and stronger management discipline on overhead and investment allocation.

This can work only if the concessions are large enough to be **material in the P&L** and are locked in for a sufficiently long period.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact annual run-rate savings, by measure and by site, are you willing to commit to as the minimum acceptable outcome, and what fallback action is automatic if the target is missed?**

#### To institutional investors
**Would you accept a non-closure package if it is independently verified to deliver closure-level savings and enforceability, even if it preserves more sites than a closure-based plan?**

#### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to back a hybrid deal that protects some sites, even if it requires accepting a smaller, more conditional near-term margin improvement than a full closure program would deliver?**

#### To the regional state government
**What specific capacity reduction and labor-cost relief would you regard as sufficient to support a site-preserving deal, and what are you prepared to do if those conditions are not met?**

#### To the industrial union
**What permanent, quantified cost relief are you willing to place on the table so that the package can be judged economically credible without plant closures or forced redundancies?**

#### To the works council
**If an independently verified package shows that one or more sites can remain open only with substantial internal reconfiguration, are you prepared to negotiate site-level changes, or is no closure-related adjustment acceptable under any circumstance?**

#### To the expert
**Can you define the minimum combination of labor-cost relief, capacity reduction, and enforceable fallback clauses that would make a non-closure package credibly equivalent to closures in this case?**
