I will respond directly.

I agree with the diagnosis shared by management, investors, and the family: **this company needs a real restructuring, and delay would be harmful**. I also agree that any solution must be **durable, enforceable, and economically credible**. A cosmetic compromise would help no one.

But I have to be equally clear: **I do not accept that plant closures or forced redundancies are the default answer**. As regional anchor shareholder, my responsibility is not only to margins on paper, but to the company’s role in the state’s industrial base, employment, apprenticeships, suppliers, and long-term social stability. If a restructuring destroys that foundation, it may “solve” a short-term cost problem while creating a much larger long-term one.

### Where I agree
- The cost base is too high.
- Capacity must be adjusted to demand.
- Employee contributions will likely be necessary.
- Management, owners, and labour all have to contribute.
- Prolonged strikes would damage the company and the region.
- A deal must be quantified, not just announced.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the logic that only closures can be “real.” That is too narrow and too convenient. Before anyone turns to closures, I want to see a serious comparison of alternatives that preserve sites and jobs while still delivering structural savings.

Those alternatives are not fantasy. They include:
- shift consolidation,
- product and line reallocation,
- working-time reductions,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- temporary pay restraint,
- bonus restraint,
- productivity gains,
- and targeted capacity reduction without shutting plants.

If those measures are properly combined, they may not be painless, but they can still be a real restructuring. The burden is on management to prove that they are not enough — not merely to assert it.

### On the investors’ position
You are right to insist on numbers, permanence, and enforceability. But you are too quick to treat closure as the benchmark of seriousness. The benchmark should be **whether the company restores competitiveness without unnecessary destruction of regional industrial capacity**. If a non-closure package reaches the savings target, then it is superior precisely because it avoids irreversible damage.

### On the family’s position
I agree that stability without competitiveness is not acceptable. But stability is not the enemy here; instability is. A plan that tears apart sites and triggers a permanent conflict with workforce and region may improve a spreadsheet in the short run while weakening the company’s strategic freedom in the long run. I am not prepared to support that.

### On management
You keep saying the operating footprint must change. Fine — but changing the footprint is not the same as closing plants. If you want my support, come with a package that shows:
1. the required annual savings,
2. the exact sources of those savings,
3. which measures are permanent,
4. how quickly they take effect,
5. and how the company avoids a rebound into the old cost structure.

If you cannot make the case that alternatives are insufficient, then closures should remain the last resort, not the first.

### On the union and works council
I support your insistence that the burden cannot fall only on the workforce. That is legitimate. But I also need you to engage the economic reality, not just the political red lines. If you want to avoid closures, you must be prepared to make a meaningful contribution that is visible in the P&L and durable over time.

What I cannot support is a package that merely preserves the status quo and renames it “socially balanced.” Preservation without adaptation is not a strategy.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring **if and only if** it:
- protects core sites where possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- delivers real and measurable savings,
- preserves the company’s role as a regional anchor,
- and includes binding investment commitments for the future.

I am **not** prepared to support a plan whose practical effect is to trade away jobs and sites as the price of competitiveness. That crosses a line I will not cross.

### What I want next
Before this discussion goes any further, I want management to bring a **quantified comparison**:
- what closures would save,
- what a non-closure package would save,
- over what time horizon,
- and with what effect on employment, sites, and future investment.

Without that, we are not deciding; we are only choosing which constituency bears the pain.

If needed, I would support bringing in an independent industrial restructuring expert to validate the savings comparison and test whether the non-closure path is truly insufficient.
