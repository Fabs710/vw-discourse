We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not yet on the solution.

## Where we agree
- The current German cost base is too high.
- Capacity is too large for the market reality.
- A cosmetic settlement would destroy value.
- A prolonged strike or industrial war would also destroy value.
- Any package must be durable, measurable, and enforceable.

That is the right frame. We will not support a deal that leaves margins structurally impaired or merely postpones the adjustment.

## Where we disagree
The works council’s and, to a lesser extent, the union’s insistence on **absolute no-closure / no-forced-redundancy red lines** is not financially credible unless someone can prove that the gap is modest and fully bridgeable through other measures. So far, I have not seen that proof.

Likewise, management is not entitled to use “competitiveness” as a slogan for a default closure strategy without showing the actual gap and the savings comparison. We need numbers, not posture.

The state and family are right to reject a value-destructive conflict, but they should not confuse that with an open-ended guarantee of all sites. Preserving the industrial base matters only if it is economically sustainable.

## What we require
For capital markets, the only acceptable outcome is one that clearly demonstrates:
1. **Annual durable savings at the scale needed to restore return-on-sales**
2. **Upfront transition costs that are financeable**
3. **No cosmetic substitution of temporary concessions for structural change**
4. **A credible implementation path with milestones**
5. **Protection of future investment capacity**

If a non-closure package can genuinely deliver equivalent savings with enforceable discipline, we would prefer that to closures. But the burden of proof is on the proponents of that alternative.

## Our view on the current bridge proposals
### 1) No forced redundancies + limited capacity adjustment
This is only acceptable if “limited capacity adjustment” is real and quantified. If it is just closure by stealth avoided through language, that will not pass.

### 2) Time-bound site security with conditional review
This is potentially workable, but only if the review is based on hard savings and hard metrics, not a political escape hatch that guarantees later deadlock.

### 3) Shared-contribution package
This is the most promising direction, but only if contributions are meaningful across all parties:
- labor: wage restraint, flexibility, voluntary exits
- management: overhead and pay discipline
- owners: capital support for transition
- company: permanent simplification of the footprint and cost base

## Our bottom line
We will back **whatever package credibly restores competitive margins and lowers structural costs**. That could include a negotiated non-closure solution, but only if it is economically equivalent and enforceable. If not, then deeper footprint reduction — including closures — remains on the table.

What we will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a freeze of structural inefficiency,
- or a settlement that buys peace by sacrificing long-term value.
