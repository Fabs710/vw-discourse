I agree with the Works Council on the core line: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable side issue; it is the basic condition for any credible settlement.

Management’s framing is too one-sided if it treats the workforce as the only place to absorb the entire crisis. Workers did not create the market downturn, the cost gap, or the strategic delays. So we will not accept a restructuring that shifts all pain onto employees through layoffs, wage cuts, and the dismantling of long-standing protections while management and owners remain insulated.

That said, I do not deny that the company needs a serious response. If there is structural overcapacity, then the answer must be **shared sacrifice and reversible measures**, not closures. We are prepared to discuss:
- a temporary labour-cost contribution,
- working-time reductions or flexibility,
- waiver or suspension of certain bonuses,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- capacity reduction through non-coercive means,
- and an industrial-policy-style package tied to investment and future product commitments.

But this only becomes discussable if the package includes:
1. **binding site security for the German plants**,  
2. **no forced layoffs**,  
3. **preservation of the collective wage framework**, and  
4. **clear contributions from management, owners, and capital as well**.

If management insists on plant closures or open-ended job insecurity, then there is no common basis. In that case, we will use the instruments available to us, including industrial action and our bargaining power in the co-determination process.

So my message is simple: **we are ready to help build a durable solution, but not one that sacrifices jobs, sites, and wage standards as the price of management’s strategic mistakes.**
