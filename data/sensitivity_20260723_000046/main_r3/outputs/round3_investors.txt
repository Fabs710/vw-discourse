We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on a financeable solution.

## Where we agree
- The current German cost base is too high.
- Capacity is too large for the demand outlook.
- A cosmetic settlement would destroy value.
- A prolonged strike would also destroy value.
- Any outcome must be durable, quantified, and enforceable.

That is the right framing, and it matters. We are not interested in preserving inefficiency for sentiment’s sake.

## Where we disagree
### On the works council
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is understandable politically, but it is not sufficient economically. If the company’s fixed-cost overhang is large, then refusing footprint reduction in advance is not a restructuring position; it is a veto on the mechanism that may be needed to restore margins.

What I do **not** accept:
- a package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- a settlement that preserves all guarantees while shifting the burden elsewhere,
- or a deal that relies on temporary measures that unwind before competitiveness is restored.

If you want us to support a non-closure route, it must be backed by hard numbers showing that the residual gap is genuinely bridgeable. So far, that proof is not there.

### On the union
Your willingness to discuss temporary wage restraint, flexibility, and voluntary exits is more constructive. That is the right direction. But temporary measures are only valuable if they lead to a permanent reset.

For capital markets, what matters is:
- annual durable savings,
- not symbolic sacrifice,
- and not a two-year bridge back to the same structural problem.

So yes, labour contribution can be part of the answer, but only if it is paired with real capacity reduction and measurable long-run savings.

### On management
Management is right that it cannot accept a cosmetic compromise. But management also has to stop treating closures as the default answer before the alternatives are fully quantified.

We need a proper side-by-side comparison:
- non-closure package,
- hybrid downsizing,
- closure-heavy option,

with:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- liquidity impact,
- strike/disruption risk,
- and timing to margin improvement.

Without that, “closures are necessary” remains an assertion, not a case.

### On the state and family
You are both right to worry about value-destroying conflict. But that does **not** justify freezing the company’s footprint in place. The question is not whether closures are politically ugly; it is whether the company can hit its return-on-sales target without them.

If the answer is yes, we would prefer that. If the answer is no, then the governance blocks cannot be used to preserve an uneconomic structure indefinitely.

## What we require
For investors, the acceptable solution must show:
1. **Durable margin improvement**
2. **Structural, not temporary, cost reduction**
3. **Credible implementation certainty**
4. **Financeable transition costs**
5. **No cosmetic substitution of concessions for actual restructuring**

That means a non-closure settlement is acceptable only if it is economically equivalent in effect, not just politically more comfortable.

## What would move us
We are open to a negotiated package if it contains:
- quantified headcount reduction,
- real fixed-cost removal,
- management overhead and executive restraint,
- owner-side capital support for restructuring,
- and a binding timetable with milestones.

We would also accept a phased solution if the upfront transition cost is high but the long-run savings are truly equivalent.

## What will not move us
- a blanket promise to keep every site open no matter what,
- vague “shared sacrifice” rhetoric,
- temporary concessions without permanent savings,
- or a settlement that avoids difficult decisions while margins remain impaired.

## Bottom line
We will support whatever credibly restores competitiveness and protects the risk-adjusted value of our holdings. That can include a non-closure package, but only if the numbers are real and the savings are durable.

If the alternative package does not close the gap, then deeper footprint reduction stays on the table. We will not support a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.
