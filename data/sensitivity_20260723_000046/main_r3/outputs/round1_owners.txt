I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally clear from the controlling shareholder perspective.

We are not indifferent to jobs, sites, or the social role of this company. But we are also not prepared to preside over a slow erosion of the core asset. If competitiveness is not restored, then neither employment nor regional responsibility will be sustainable for long.

So I agree with Management on the central diagnosis: **the status quo is not acceptable, and structural savings must be real, not cosmetic**. I also agree with the State Government and the employee side on one important point: **a solution that simply imposes pain on the workforce while avoiding hard commitments from management and owners will not hold**.

Where I disagree is on any framing that treats the current German footprint as untouchable. If the numbers require durable capacity reduction, then we should not pretend that every site can be insulated from change. That would be irresponsible. The market will not be negotiated away.

At the same time, I do **not** support a crude closure strategy that creates a prolonged industrial war, damages the brand, and destroys value faster than it creates savings. That would be unacceptable from an ownership standpoint. A drawn-out conflict, reputational harm, or a solution that permanently weakens the company’s industrial base would fail the test of stewardship.

So my position is this:

- **No empty guarantees that block any restructuring.**
- **No forced collapse of the workforce settlement.**
- **No prolonged confrontation that bleeds value.**
- **Yes to a credible package that restores margins and competitiveness durably.**

What would move us is a package with all of the following:
1. **Material, durable cost reduction** — not just temporary optics.
2. **A negotiated labour contribution** that is meaningful but socially balanced.
3. **Capacity adjustment** that is real, but not necessarily limited to outright closures if equivalent savings can be proven.
4. **Protection of future investment** in products and technology.
5. **Stability and labour peace** for a defined period, so the company can execute.

To the Works Council and Union: your insistence on no forced redundancies is understandable, but an absolute rejection of site change or capacity reduction is not a serious answer to structural overcapacity. If you want to avoid closures, you need to show a **credible alternative that closes the cost gap at the same scale and with enforceable discipline**. If that exists, we will take it seriously. If it does not, then you are asking owners to finance decline.

To Management: you have our backing only if you are prepared to pursue a disciplined settlement, not just a confrontation. If closures are used, they must be the result of necessity and negotiation, not a first reflex. And if an alternative package can deliver comparable savings with less destruction, you should be ready to accept it.

To the State Government: I understand your concern about the regional anchor role, and I share the view that this company matters beyond quarterly earnings. But stewardship also means avoiding a false preservation of the status quo. Your veto power is not a guarantee that every site will survive unchanged; it is a responsibility to force a sustainable outcome, not to entrench inefficiency.

My bottom line: **we support a restructuring that restores competitiveness and protects long-term enterprise value. We do not support a settlement that either freezes structural problems or detonates the company’s industrial peace.** Bring forward a package that is durable, enforceable, and value-creating — then we can support it through governance.
