I hear the line you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: **a solution with binding guarantees for every German site through 2030, no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no meaningful labour-cost contribution is not a credible restructuring package.** It does not restore competitiveness; it freezes the problem in place.

I agree with one point: this cannot be a crude cut-and-run exercise. We do need investment in future products, qualification, internal transfers, and measures that preserve know-how where possible. And yes, management cannot expect employees alone to carry the burden. Owners, capital, and management credibility all matter.

But the core issue remains unchanged: **our German cost base is structurally too high, and the current capacity footprint is too large for the market we face.** Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and the brand is missing its return target. If we do not make a durable reduction in cost and capacity now, we will simply be back here later with worse numbers and fewer options.

So let me state the management position plainly:

- **Plant closures remain on the table.**
- **Structural capacity reductions are required.**
- **Labour costs must come down materially.**
- **The old guarantees cannot survive unchanged if they block the company’s survival and future investment.**

That said, I am not blind to the fact that execution requires a settlement. We are prepared to consider **how** the savings are achieved, provided the outcome is real and durable. That means we can discuss:
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary concessions where they create permanent savings,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and investment commitments linked to the restructuring.

What I cannot accept is a cosmetic deal that swaps one set of headlines for another while leaving the cost base uncompetitive. Nor can I accept a package where management is expected to pretend that no hard choices are needed while the business continues to lose ground.

So my counterposition is this: **if you want to avoid closures, then you must bring an alternative that delivers comparable structural savings in capacity and labour cost, with binding force and not just promises.** If that package is credible, we can talk. If it is simply a demand to preserve all sites and all guarantees, then it is not a restructuring proposal — it is a delay tactic.

We need a settlement that restores the brand’s competitiveness to 2030. Anything less is not enough.
