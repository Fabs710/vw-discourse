## To institutional investors
You are right to demand durable savings. You are wrong if you assume that only closures can deliver them.

Your own expert says the decisive variable is the residual gap after alternatives. Fine. Then let’s actually quantify those alternatives site by site:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- shift reductions,
- product concentration,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner support.

Until that is done, your closure preference is not a financial conclusion; it is a judgment call. And given the industrial conflict, legal risk, and strike risk, it is not even obviously the best one.

## My position on the three compromise ideas

### 1) Independent “bridge-to-gap” package
I support the independent accounting exercise, but not as a pretext to soften our red lines. It must test whether the company can close the gap **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**.

If the analysis is honest, it may well show that a combination of voluntary exits, working-time measures, and burden-sharing is enough. That is the path we want.

### 2) Time-bound site security with objective escape triggers
I do **not** accept any clause that creates a later trapdoor to closures. A “review” that ends in shutdowns is not a guarantee. It is just deferred conflict.

If management wants a review, then the first and only permissible responses are:
- non-closure measures,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- and additional burden-sharing by management and owners.

Closures are not an acceptable next step.

### 3) Hybrid downsizing without immediate closures
This is still too close to the logic of slicing away the industrial base. If “hybrid downsizing” means shifting work out of plants and hollowing them out until closure becomes a formality, then we oppose it.

If it means reorganizing production while keeping every German plant open and fully viable, then that is a different discussion. But I am not signing up to a slow-motion closure process.

## What I will accept
I will support a serious restructuring only if it contains all of the following:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job security through 2030**
4. **Internal redeployment and voluntary instruments only**
5. **Management overhead and executive restraint**
6. **Owner-side contribution**
7. **Real product and investment commitments for German sites**
8. **A genuine co-determination role in implementation**

Anything short of that is not a solution for the workforce.

## What I will not accept
- closure-heavy restructuring
- forced layoffs
- a “shared sacrifice” package where labour gives and capital keeps the decisive options
- a trapdoor review mechanism
- any attempt to turn temporary flexibility into permanent job erosion
- any weakening of co-determination or the job-security framework

## Bottom line
The company needs restructuring. That much is clear.

But the restructuring must be done **without German plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**. That is the line. Management has not shown a sufficient case to cross it, and the expert inputs do not justify crossing it yet.

If you want a serious next step, bring the plant-by-plant numbers, the quantified alternatives, and the binding 2030 guarantees. Until then, we are not negotiating the surrender of our sites and jobs.
