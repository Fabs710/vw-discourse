## My position remains unchanged

I do **not** accept:
- German plant closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as a restructuring instrument,
- or any outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not bargaining theater. It is the condition for any settlement I can support.

## To management
You are right about one thing: the company needs a **durable** restructuring. I do not dispute that.

What I **do** dispute is your repeated attempt to turn “durable” into an argument for closures before the alternatives are fully and transparently quantified. You still have not shown that:
- voluntary exits,
- internal transfers,
- early retirement,
- working-time changes,
- product concentration,
- management overhead cuts,
- executive restraint,
- and owner-side support

cannot close the gap to a sufficient degree.

Until that is shown, closures are not “necessary”; they are your preferred instrument.

And I will say this plainly: if the analysis shows that the remaining gap is only modest, then there is no justification for irreversible plant damage. In that case, the socially and economically responsible route is a negotiated non-closure package.

## To the works council
I agree with your core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no dismantling of job-security or co-determination.

That is the correct starting point.

Where I want to be equally clear is that the alternative package must be **real**, not rhetorical. If we want to preserve all German sites, then we need a credible package that actually reduces cost and capacity through reversible and voluntary means. Otherwise management will use the missing numbers to force the debate back toward closures.

So yes: I support the demand for full plant-by-plant transparency. But that transparency must be used to build a defensible non-closure settlement, not to create a pretext for retreat.

## To the union
Your willingness to discuss temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, and flexibility is constructive. That matters.

But I do not accept a model in which labour contributes and management then treats closures as the final trump card anyway. Shared sacrifice must mean **shared**:
- labour contributes temporarily,
- management contributes materially,
- owners contribute materially,
- and the company commits to the regional industrial base.

If that is not true, then “shared sacrifice” is just a polite way of shifting the burden onto the workforce.

## To the controlling family
If you mean stewardship, then stewardship must include protecting the company’s regional anchor role and the governance settlement that has sustained it.

I do not see stewardship in a posture that says: “We will accept closures if the numbers justify them” without first exhausting the broader social and industrial alternatives. That is too easy. It is also too damaging to trust.

If the family wants legitimacy, it should support:
- a binding non-closure restructuring window,
- serious owner participation,
- and a solution that preserves the German industrial core.

## To institutional investors
Your insistence on durability and implementability is legitimate. I agree with that.

What I do **not** accept is the idea that only footprint reduction can produce credible savings. That is not a financial law; it is a conclusion you have not yet proven at this point in the process.

You want quantified, durable improvement. Fine. Then support the full comparison and do not jump to the most destructive instrument before the numbers are in.

## What I will support
I can support a serious restructuring if it includes:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures in Germany during the protected period**
3. **Binding site and job security**
4. **Voluntary and reversible workforce adjustment tools**
5. **Management overhead and executive restraint**
6. **Owner-side capital and transition support**
7. **Transparent monitoring of savings and utilization**
8. **A genuine co-determination role in implementation**

I am not asking for a cosmetic deal. I am asking for one that preserves jobs, protects the regional economy, and still restores competitiveness.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a closure-heavy package,
- a trapdoor review clause that simply defers shutdowns,
- a deal where labour pays and capital keeps the decisive options,
- or any outcome that erodes our veto role and regional responsibility.

## Bottom line
If the numbers show that a non-closure or hybrid package can close the gap, preserve the sites, and restore competitiveness, then that is the route I will back.

If the numbers are used instead to justify closures as a first resort, then I will oppose that plainly and with the leverage I have.

Bring the plant-by-plant accounting. Bring the quantified alternatives. Bring the burden-sharing. Then we can talk seriously. Until then, closures are not acceptable.
