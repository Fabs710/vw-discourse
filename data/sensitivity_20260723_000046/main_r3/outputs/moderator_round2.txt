**Core impasse**

The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **what counts as a legitimate restructuring instrument**.

The core deadlock is between:

1. **Management, investors, and the controlling family**, who require a **durable, quantified reduction in fixed cost and capacity** and regard closures as a necessary option if alternatives fall short; and
2. **The works council, union, and state government**, who treat **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as unacceptable starting points** and want any adjustment to occur through voluntary, internal, and temporary measures while preserving site security and co-determination.

In practical terms, the impasse is over three linked questions:
- **How much structural capacity reduction is required?**
- **Can it be achieved without closures or forced redundancies?**
- **What level of binding site protection can coexist with a credible savings plan?**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) “No forced redundancies” with a quantified, capped capacity adjustment
A possible bridge is:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No immediate plant closures**
- But a **predefined, quantified capacity reduction target** that can be achieved through:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - redeployment,
  - single-shift conversion,
  - product concentration,
  - and site-specific efficiency measures.

This would require:
- explicit numerical savings targets,
- a cap on involuntary measures,
- and a clear rule that any footprint change must be reviewed against agreed alternatives first.

**Purpose:** gives management structural movement while preserving the employee-side red line against forced layoffs.

---

### 2) Time-bound site security with a mandatory re-opener only on objective triggers
A second bridge is:
- **Binding site security for a defined period**
- **No closures during that period**
- A **mandatory review only if agreed triggers are met**, such as:
  - volume falling below a defined threshold,
  - savings shortfall against milestones,
  - liquidity deterioration,
  - or failure to execute agreed productivity measures.

To make this acceptable, the review clause would need:
- objective metrics,
- independent verification,
- and pre-agreed next steps that start with non-closure remedies before any footprint action.

**Purpose:** gives labour and the state protection against open-ended closure threats, while giving management a structured exit if the plan fails.

---

### 3) Shared-contribution package with matched stakeholder commitments
A third bridge is a **matched burden-sharing deal** in which:
- labour provides temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, and flexibility;
- management cuts overhead and executive compensation;
- owners provide liquidity or transition capital;
- the company commits to investment, retraining, and product allocation for German sites;
- and any site reduction is limited to the amount strictly needed after all other measures are quantified.

This could be paired with:
- a joint monitoring committee,
- independent monthly reporting,
- and milestone-based release of concessions.

**Purpose:** turns the dispute from “who sacrifices first” into a staged, measurable package.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**If independent numbers show that a limited, quantified capacity reduction is necessary to preserve the remaining German sites, what specific non-closure measures would you accept, and what numeric savings threshold would make a no-closure package credible to you?**

### Industrial Union
**What exact combination of temporary wage restraint, voluntary exits, flexibility, and redeployment would you be prepared to support, and how large must management and owner contributions be for the package to remain fair?**

### Expert: Industrial restructuring finance specialist
**What is the smallest annual savings gap that a no-closure package can realistically bridge before footprint reduction becomes financially unavoidable, and what are the one-time cash costs of that alternative?**

### Management / Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to presenting a side-by-side quantified comparison of non-closure, hybrid downsizing, and closure-heavy options before invoking closures as necessary, and will you agree to a sequencing rule that exhausts voluntary and internal measures first?**

### Expert: Industrial operations and capacity model specialist
**What utilization threshold and plant-fixed-cost profile would justify major downsizing rather than closure, and which sites or functions appear most vulnerable under that model?**

### Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a time-bound site-security arrangement with objective review triggers if it includes binding investment commitments and no compulsory redundancies during the protected period?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support owner-side capital support or governance backing for a negotiated non-closure package if it demonstrably closes most of the gap, even if it requires temporary returns restraint?**

### Institutional Investors
**What level of quantified durable savings and implementation certainty would be sufficient for you to endorse a non-closure settlement, and would you accept a phased plan if the upfront cost is high but the long-run margin impact is equivalent?**
