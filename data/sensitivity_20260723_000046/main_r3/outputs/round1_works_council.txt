As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable to break the historic job-security commitments and hand employees a fait accompli after years of loyalty and sacrifice. If management wants a credible path forward, it must come with:

- binding guarantees for all German sites through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and a restructuring that relies on alternative measures rather than closures: capacity adjustments, internal transfers, working-time solutions, qualification, and a fair negotiated contribution from management, owners, and capital.

We are willing to discuss how to secure competitiveness, but not by destroying jobs and tearing up trust. If the plan is built on closures or forced cuts, we will oppose it with all the tools at our disposal, including industrial action and our veto rights in the supervisory board.

If management is serious about the company’s future, then it must come to the table with a solution that protects sites, protects jobs, and gives the workforce a real role in shaping the transition.
