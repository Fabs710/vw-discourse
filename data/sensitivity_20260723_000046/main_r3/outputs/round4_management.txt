## My position on the compromise concepts

### 1) Independent “bridge-to-gap” package
I support the independent analysis. I do **not** support using it as a way to pre-bind management to no closures regardless of findings.

The analysis must be genuinely open:
- non-closure,
- hybrid downsizing,
- closure-heavy restructuring.

If it shows that non-closure closes the gap, I’ll say so. If it does not, then we need to move.

### 2) Time-bound site security with objective escape triggers
I can accept a time-bound arrangement only if it is not a trap for management.

That means:
- objective triggers,
- independent verification,
- and a meaningful path to footprint adjustment if the savings gap persists.

What I cannot accept is a clause that effectively immunizes every site until 2030 and turns review into a dead end.

### 3) Hybrid downsizing without immediate closures
This is the most plausible bridge if the numbers support it. But it must be real hybrid downsizing — not a cosmetic reshuffling that leaves the fixed-cost base intact.

That means:
- product concentration,
- shift rationalization,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- and actual site-specific cost removal.

If “hybrid” just means delay, then it is not acceptable.

## What would move me
I remain open to:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- management overhead cuts,
- executive restraint,
- owner support,

**if and only if** they produce a durable structural cost reduction and do not leave the company structurally uncompetitive.

I am also open to keeping sites open where the numbers justify it. That is not the issue. The issue is that no one gets to declare all German sites untouchable before the plant-by-plant economics are known.

## What will not move me
- a blanket no-closure rule
- compulsory redundancies ruled out before the analysis
- a 2030 security framework that freezes the footprint
- shared sacrifice that does not materially reduce fixed cost
- a review clause that removes management’s ability to act if the restructuring fails

## Bottom line
We are not here to preserve every existing site at any cost.

We are here to restore competitiveness, hit the return-on-sales target, and free funds for the future product and technology cycle to 2030.

If the independent numbers show that we can do that without closures, then fine. If they do not, then closures or equivalent permanent footprint reduction remain on the table — and I will not pretend otherwise.

Bring the plant-by-plant numbers. Then we can stop arguing in abstractions and decide what is economically unavoidable.
