I’ll be direct.

I agree with the **shared diagnosis** from management, investors, and the state: the current cost base and capacity are not sustainable, and pretending otherwise would damage the company’s long-term value. I also agree with labour and the state that a prolonged confrontation would be value-destructive and must be avoided if at all possible.

But I do **not** accept the notion that the company can preserve all German sites, preserve all guarantees, and still achieve the required competitiveness reset. That is not stewardship; it is denial.

## Where I stand

### On management’s position
Management is right that structural savings must be real, durable, and measurable. If the alternative package does not materially reduce fixed cost and capacity, then it is not enough.

Where I disagree is on turning closures into the default political threat before the alternatives are fully tested. A closure-first approach risks a long industrial conflict, reputational damage, and value destruction that could outweigh the savings. I will not support a course that destroys the enterprise to “save” it.

### On the works council
I respect the insistence on no compulsory redundancies and the concern for trust. But a blanket “no closures, no cuts, no weakening of guarantees” is not a viable answer to structural overcapacity.

If the works council wants to avoid closures, then it needs to engage on a package that actually closes the gap:
- voluntary departures,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- and a meaningful labour-cost contribution.

Anything less simply pushes the problem into the future.

### On the union
The union is more constructive because it recognizes shared sacrifice. That is the right instinct. But “temporary” measures are only acceptable if they lead to a durable reset. A concession that disappears in two years and leaves the fixed-cost base unchanged is not enough.

I would support:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- and redeployment,
**if** these are tied to measurable permanent savings and a credible capacity adjustment.

### On the state government
I agree that the state has a legitimate role in protecting the regional anchor function and in preventing a reckless destruction of industrial capability.

But the state’s veto power should be used to force a sustainable outcome, not to freeze the status quo. If the alternatives do not close the competitiveness gap, then blocking adjustment simply stores up a larger crisis.

### On investors
You are right to demand quantifiable, durable savings. You are also right that cosmetic compromises are unacceptable.

What I would add is this: investors should not assume that only closures can deliver the necessary result. The burden is on management to prove the residual gap after all other measures. If a non-closure package can close that gap, it is preferable. If it cannot, then further footprint reduction becomes unavoidable.

## My bottom line

I am prepared to support a restructuring only if it contains all of the following:

1. **Material, durable cost reduction**
2. **A real capacity adjustment**
3. **A fair but meaningful labour contribution**
4. **Protection of future investment and product allocation**
5. **A stable governance settlement and industrial peace**

What I am **not** prepared to support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a prolonged strike war,
- or any outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control.

So my position is clear:

- I do **not** back empty guarantees that block restructuring.
- I do **not** back a crude closure campaign that destroys value.
- I do back a disciplined, quantified settlement that restores competitiveness and preserves the company’s long-term strength.

If management and labour want a viable path, bring forward a package that proves the economics and the durability. If the numbers do not work, then sentiment will not change that.

If needed, I would support a **[SUMMON: independent industrial restructuring mediator to quantify the residual savings gap after voluntary exits, flexibility, overhead cuts, and owner contributions]** so the board can separate real economics from negotiating positions.
