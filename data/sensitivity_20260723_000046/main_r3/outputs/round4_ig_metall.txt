I’ll respond clearly and directly.

## To the works council
I agree with your core line:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures as the default response**
- **no dismantling of co-determination**
- and the burden must not fall on labour alone.

That said, I want to correct one point in the way you are framing this. If we want to **protect jobs and the collective wage framework**, we cannot rely on slogans alone. We need a **quantified alternative package** that is serious enough to withstand management pressure and investor scrutiny. Otherwise they will keep using the threat of closures to divide the employee side.

So yes: your red lines are right. But they have to be backed by a binding, credible counterproposal:
- voluntary exits only,
- early retirement / partial retirement,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- product concentration,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner contributions,
- and transparent monitoring.

I support that path. What I do **not** support is a package that quietly becomes a closure program by attrition.

## To management
I hear your insistence on durability and measurable savings. That is legitimate. What is **not** legitimate is treating closures as the starting point before the alternatives are fully and transparently tested.

You still have not shown that closures are unavoidable. You have shown that you **prefer** the strongest instrument. That is not the same as proving necessity.

If you want a real settlement, then put the numbers on the table:
1. savings from voluntary departures,
2. savings from early retirement and internal redeployment,
3. savings from shift and working-time changes,
4. savings from overhead and executive restraint,
5. owner-side support,
6. then the **residual gap**.

Until that is done, closure is a threat, not a demonstrated necessity.

And let me be explicit:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures as the first response**
- **No deal where labour carries the pain while management and ownership keep their options intact**

If management refuses to work on that basis, then we are not in a negotiation; we are in a confrontation.

- non-closure packages can work if the gap is modest,
- hybrid solutions may work if there is real capacity rationalization,
- closures become more likely only if the gap is large and persistent.

## To the regional state government
You are right to reject closures and forced redundancies. But that position matters only if you actually use your blocking power when it counts.

I would expect the state to support:
- site security,
- industrial stability,
- investment commitments,
- retraining and transition finance,

What I do **not** want is polite language about balance while management pushes a closure-heavy package through the back door.

## To the controlling family
If you mean stewardship, then show it in practice.

Stewardship means:
- not forcing the workforce to absorb the whole cost of strategic failure,
- not using family control to impose the harshest option first,
- and not treating social conflict as a side effect to be managed later.

If you want trust from labour, then support a package that:
- keeps sites open,
- protects jobs,
- and shares the burden fairly.

If you insist on closures, then you should expect open conflict. That is not responsible ownership; that is escalation.

## To institutional investors
You are right about one thing: the deal must be durable and financially credible. I agree.

But durability does **not** automatically mean plant closures. It means a settlement that actually holds. If a non-closure package can close most of the gap with real structural measures, it is likely the better long-term answer than a strike, legal conflict, and reputational damage.

If you want to support a non-closure solution, then support the measures that make it real:
- voluntary exits,
- internal transfers,
- early retirement,
- working-time flexibility,
- management cuts,
- owner support,
- and binding site security.

If you want only the easiest accounting answer, then you are shifting risk onto the workforce and the region.

## My position on the three compromise ideas

### 1) Independent “bridge-to-gap” package
I support this — **if** it is used to test whether the gap can be closed **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**.

That is the only acceptable purpose of the exercise. It must not become a staging ground for later shutdowns.

### 2) Time-bound site security with objective escape triggers
I can discuss objective triggers, but I will not accept a trapdoor to closures. A review clause is only acceptable if the first responses are:
- non-closure measures,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- and additional burden-sharing by management and owners.

If closure is the automatic next step, then the “security” promise is worthless.

### 3) Hybrid downsizing without immediate closures
This is only acceptable if it truly preserves every German site and does not hollow out plants until shutdown becomes inevitable.

If it means:
- product concentration,
- shift rationalization,
- capacity reduction through voluntary and internal means,
- and fair burden-sharing,

then it is discussable.

If it means a slow-motion closure strategy, then I reject it.

## What would move me
I remain open to a **temporary labour contribution** if — and only if — it is tied to:
- binding job and site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closures,
- management and owner contributions,
- and a quantified savings plan with transparent monitoring.

That is the condition. Not a phrase, not a promise — a binding package.

## What will not move me
- vague claims that closures are “necessary”
- a review clause that is really a trapdoor
- “shared sacrifice” where workers pay and everyone else stays protected
- pressure to surrender the collective wage framework without equivalent counter-concessions

## Bottom line
I am ready for a serious deal. But the deal must be:
- **fair**
- **durable**
- **shared**
- and **non-destructive to jobs and sites**

Management has not yet proven that closures are unavoidable. Until it does, they are unacceptable.

Bring the plant-by-plant numbers. Bring the residual gap. Bring the burden-sharing. Then we can negotiate seriously. Without that, there is no basis for asking workers to carry the pain alone.
