### Core impasse

The central deadlock is between **labor/region** and **management/capital** on the method of restructuring:

- **Works Council, Union, and State Government** reject:
  - plant closures in Germany,
  - compulsory redundancies,
  - and any settlement that breaks existing job-security/co-determination arrangements.

- **Management, Family Holding, and Institutional Investors** insist:
  - the current cost base and capacity are structurally too high,
  - savings must be durable and measurable,
  - and some combination of capacity reduction, labor-cost relief, or site change is unavoidable.

So the impasse is not over whether the company needs restructuring — all sides accept that it does — but over **whether restructuring must include closures/layoffs, or whether an equivalent savings package can be built through negotiated alternatives**.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

#### 1) “No forced redundancies” + “limited capacity adjustment” package
A compromise could preserve the line against compulsory redundancies while allowing:
- voluntary departure programs,
- internal transfers across sites,
- early retirement / partial retirement,
- reduced working time,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and, if necessary, selective capacity reductions through non-coercive means.

**Purpose:** This would let management and investors obtain real headcount/capacity reduction without immediate forced layoffs, while allowing labor to preserve the principle that jobs are not cut by compulsion.

---

#### 2) Time-bound site security with conditional review
A second bridge would be:
- a **time-limited site-security commitment** for German plants,
- paired with **clear performance and savings milestones**,
- after which parties jointly review whether additional measures are needed.

This would not be a blank guarantee through 2030, but it could provide:
- a protected restructuring window,
- labor peace,
- and a mechanism for management to prove whether the alternative measures are sufficient.

**Purpose:** This gives labor and the state a buffer against immediate closures, while giving management and owners an enforceable path if the savings gap remains.

---

#### 3) Shared-contribution restructuring package
A third compromise position would distribute the burden across all stakeholders:
- workforce: temporary wage restraint, working-time flexibility, bonus suspension, redeployment support,
- management: reduced executive compensation and stronger execution commitments,
- owners: capital support for investment and transition costs,
- company: guaranteed future product and investment allocation to German sites where viable.

**Purpose:** This would answer the criticism that only labor is being asked to adjust, while still producing a measurable cost and capacity reset.

---

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If compulsory redundancies and closures are ruled out, **what quantified alternative package can you accept that closes the cost and capacity gap at the same scale and on a binding timeline?**

#### Industrial Union
What specific combination of wage, working-time, and flexibility measures would you put on the table so that “no closures” is backed by a credible economic counterproposal rather than a political red line?

#### Group & Brand Management
Before insisting on closures, **what exact alternative measures have you tested, and what savings gap remains after those alternatives are applied?**

#### Regional State Government
How far are you prepared to go in supporting a negotiated restructuring — including temporary concessions from labor — if that is the price of preserving sites and avoiding closures?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a time-bound restructuring moratorium if it were paired with measurable savings, governance discipline, and a credible fallback mechanism should targets not be met?

#### Institutional Investors
What level of quantified savings and what durability threshold would be sufficient for you to support a non-closure restructuring package instead of demanding direct site reductions?
