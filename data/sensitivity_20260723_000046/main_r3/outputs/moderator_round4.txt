## 1) What has been agreed

- All parties agree the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic solution.
- There is broad agreement that any settlement must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.
- Most parties accept that the next step should be an **independent plant-by-plant analysis** comparing:
  - no-closure measures,
  - hybrid downsizing,
  - and closure scenarios.
- There is also broad agreement that the analysis must include:
  - fixed costs,
  - utilization,
  - one-time transition costs,
  - annual savings,
  - and the remaining savings gap.
- Several parties accept that **voluntary exits, early retirement, internal transfers, working-time measures, management restraint, and owner contributions** should be part of the examined options.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain

- **Plant closures in Germany**:
  - Works council, union, and regional state government reject closures as a default or acceptable starting point.
  - Management and investors keep closures open as a legitimate outcome if the numbers justify them.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:
  - Employee side and state reject them.
  - Management and investors will not rule them out in advance.
- **Site/job security through 2030**:
  - Employee side and state want binding protection.
  - Management and investors view a hard freeze as potentially incompatible with competitiveness.
- **Review / escape mechanisms**:
  - Employee side sees “escape triggers” as a possible trapdoor to closures.
  - Management, investors, and family want review mechanisms to preserve restructuring flexibility.
- **Meaning of “shared sacrifice”**:
  - Labour representatives insist it must include material contributions from management and owners.
  - Capital-side actors say contributions must also produce durable structural cost reduction, not just delay adjustment.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed

- **How much of the gap can realistically be closed without site closures?**
  - No site-specific evidence has yet been presented.
- **Temporary relief vs. durable restructuring**
  - Employee-side proposals emphasize preserving sites and jobs.
  - Management/investors emphasize long-term cost-base reduction.
- **Political/gov ernance protection vs. economic flexibility**
  - State and co-determination constraints may block some options.
  - Management and investors argue this cannot freeze the footprint indefinitely.
- **One-time cost vs. recurring savings**
  - Closure-heavy options likely generate higher long-run savings but also higher upfront cash, legal, and disruption costs.
- **Industrial conflict risk**
  - A harsh restructuring may trigger strikes or delays.
  - A softer package may fail to restore competitiveness.
- **What counts as an acceptable “hybrid”**
  - Parties disagree on whether hybrid downsizing is a genuine compromise or a slow-motion closure.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round

- Keep the discussion centered on the **independent plant-by-plant comparison**.
- Require the analysis to show, for each site, the **residual gap after non-closure measures** and how that changes under hybrid or closure scenarios.
- Ask each stakeholder to state, in writing:
  - which trade-offs they would accept,
  - which they would not,
  - and what minimum conditions would move them.
- Avoid revisiting general positions unless tied to a **site-specific number** or a **binding condition**.
- The next round should aim to narrow the dispute from abstract principles to **quantified options and decision thresholds**.
