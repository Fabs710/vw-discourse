## Procedural summary of the impasse

The core impasse is now narrow and explicit:

1. **Employee-side stakeholders** require:
   - no plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - binding guarantees through 2030,
   - and no weakening of co-determination.

2. **Management, investors, and the family holding** require:
   - durable, structural cost reduction,
   - meaningful and permanent capacity reduction,
   - and a package that closes the competitiveness gap, not just postpones it.

3. **The state government** is trying to reconcile both:
   - protect regional stability,
   - but still support a credible restructuring.

4. **The expert view** indicates:
   - pure no-closure is unlikely to be sufficient unless the internal cuts are unusually deep,
   - hybrid is the most plausible route,
   - closure-led is strongest economically but hardest institutionally.

So the conflict is not simply “jobs vs. costs.”  
It is: **what level of permanent structural change is required, and by what means can it be achieved without breaking the governance settlement?**

---

## Facilitator proposals: 3 compromise positions

### Proposal 1: “No-closure, deep hybrid” package
A package with:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- but real permanent capacity reduction through:
  - shift elimination,
  - line consolidation,
  - product reassignment,
  - functional centralization,
  - and site specialization.

Additional elements:
- temporary employee contribution,
- management compensation restraint,
- owner-side contribution,
- binding employment guarantee through 2030,
- and co-determined implementation.

**Purpose:**  
Bridge the works council and union’s red lines with the investors’ demand for structural savings.

---

### Proposal 2: “Closure moratorium + quantified trigger” package
A binding moratorium on closures for a defined period, paired with:
- a plant-by-plant restructuring plan,
- measurable savings milestones,
- and a jointly agreed trigger mechanism.

If milestones are missed by a fixed date, the parties reopen the question of deeper structural measures.

**Purpose:**  
Give employee-side stakeholders protection from immediate closure while giving management and capital a path to enforceability if internal measures fail.

---

### Proposal 3: “Employment guarantee for sacrifice” package
A binding employment and site guarantee through 2030, in exchange for:
- limited, time-bound worker concessions,
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- productivity measures,
- and a defined amount of permanent capacity reduction via non-closure methods.

**Purpose:**  
Create a trade structure where workers contribute only if all other stakeholder groups also contribute visibly and measurably.

## Pointed questions for each stakeholder

### 1. Works Council
**Question:**  
If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what exact plant-by-plant capacity reductions are you prepared to accept that would still deliver a structurally lower cost base through 2030?

---

### 2. Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What level of temporary worker contribution is acceptable to you if, and only if, management, owners, and top leadership also commit to quantified burden-sharing and binding guarantees through 2030?

---

### 3. Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Which specific cost and capacity reductions, short of plant closure, would you accept as economically equivalent to a closure-led plan, and by when must they be implemented?

---

### 4. Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What enforceable commitments from management and owners would you require before you would support a no-closure restructuring package?

---

### 5. Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to accept a permanent capacity-reduction package that avoids plant closures if it can be shown to deliver a comparable margin improvement and preserve governance stability?

---

### 6. Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum level of recurring annual savings and fixed-cost reduction would make a no-closure or hybrid package acceptable to you instead of a closure-led solution?

---

### 7. Expert / Independent Specialist
**Question:**  
Which specific configuration of line, shift, and function reductions would you test first as the most credible no-closure alternative, and what savings threshold would determine whether it is sufficient?
