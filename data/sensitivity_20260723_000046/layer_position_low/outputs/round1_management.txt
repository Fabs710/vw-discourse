I hear the red lines you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: the current cost base and capacity footprint are not compatible with this company’s competitive reality.

We are not talking about a cosmetic adjustment. We are talking about restoring a structurally uncompetitive German operation so that the brand can survive and invest through 2030. If we do not reduce costs and capacity now, we will simply preserve inefficiency, weaken margins further, and end up with even harsher choices later.

Where I agree:
- Change must be negotiated, not imposed by fantasy.
- Industrial peace is desirable.
- Any restructuring must preserve the company’s ability to invest in future products, software, battery, and platform development.

Where I disagree fundamentally:
- No plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no material labour-cost contribution are not a viable starting point.
- “Alternative measures” only matter if they deliver the same structural effect as closures and workforce reduction.
- A temporary or symbolic contribution from employees will not solve a structural cost gap.

Let me be explicit about management’s position.

We need:
- durable reduction in German capacity,
- meaningful headcount reduction,
- a sustained labour-cost contribution,
- removal of legacy guarantees that no longer fit the business reality,
- and a package that brings the brand back to target returns.

I am open on the form:
- closures,
- partial site consolidation,
- reallocations,
- productivity agreements,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exit packages,
- and a negotiated transformation contribution.

But I am not open on the outcome being preserved at current scale with only short-term concessions. That would leave us uncompetitive.

To the Works Council: a blanket refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies may be understandable politically, but it does not answer the cost problem. If you insist that every German plant must stay open regardless of economics, then you are asking management to sign up to an unsustainable structure. That I cannot do.

To the Union: I appreciate that you distinguish between permanent damage and a fair contribution. But “temporary” is not enough unless the total package is large enough to close the structural gap. A wage contribution, shorter working hours, and bonuses alone will not be sufficient unless they produce real, durable savings at scale.

So here is the reality check:
- If there is no credible structural package, management will continue to push for closures and stronger measures.
- If there is a credible package that materially reduces capacity and labour costs, I am prepared to discuss how it is implemented, including how to avoid unnecessary disruption.
- But there must be a genuine economic result, not just a promise of future goodwill.

The burden is not just on management to justify change. It is also on the employee side to propose a solution that actually fixes the business. If you want to avoid closures, then bring me something that delivers equivalent, durable savings. Otherwise, we will not get to a sustainable settlement.
