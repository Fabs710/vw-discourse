I will be direct.

The family holding supports a **durable competitiveness reset**. We do **not** support a solution that merely preserves the status quo under a different label, and we do **not** support open-ended conflict that destroys value. At the same time, we are **not** prepared to treat plant closures or forced redundancies as the automatic starting point.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, investors, and the expert that:

- the cost gap is real,
- the current footprint is too expensive for the market environment,
- cosmetic measures will fail,
- and the company needs **durable** savings, not a temporary pause.

I also agree with the works council, union, and state that:

- industrial peace has value,
- a one-sided solution would be destructive,
- and any employee contribution must be **temporary, limited, and matched by broader burden-sharing**.

## Where I disagree

### With management
You are right that the business needs structural improvement. You are **not** yet right to present closures and compulsory redundancies as the implied baseline.

The expert made the important point: closures are usually the strongest instrument for removing fixed cost and capacity, but they are also disruptive and not automatically the best outcome in this governance structure. Given co-determination, the two-thirds requirement, and the blocking positions in the system, a closure-first strategy is not just confrontational — it is institutionally fragile and value-risky.

So I reject any attempt to force the discussion into “closures or decline.”  
That is too simplistic and too destructive.

What I need from management is a **quantified restructuring bridge**:
- annual savings by measure,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- capacity removed,
- impact on headcount,
- and the margin effect through 2030.

If management cannot show that a non-closure or hybrid package closes the gap, then say so plainly. But do not start from the conclusion that closures are unavoidable before the alternatives have been properly tested.

### With the works council and union
I understand the defense of jobs, sites, and co-determination. I respect that. But I will not support a blanket “no closures, no redundancies” position if it amounts to freezing a structurally oversized network in place.

That is not stewardship either.

If the company’s footprint is too large, then real capacity must come out somehow. That may be through closures, or it may be through equivalent permanent reductions elsewhere. But it cannot be only words, temporary relief, and deferred pain.

At the same time, I agree with you that the workforce should not bear the whole burden. A temporary labor contribution is only acceptable if it is:
- meaningful,
- reversible,
- time-limited,
- and paired with binding guarantees and visible contributions from management and owners.

### With the state government
Your concern for the regional base is legitimate. We share it. But protecting long-term value does not mean preserving every existing structure. It means ensuring the company remains viable enough to invest, compete, and employ people in Germany over the long term.

A settlement that avoids conflict but leaves the business uncompetitive is not a victory for regional stability. It is deferred decline.

## What the family holding will support

We are prepared to back a package built around:

- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- temporary and limited employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and binding product and investment commitments.

We are **not** prepared to back:

- a symbolic compromise,
- a one-sided wage cut,
- or a package that preserves the old cost structure while postponing the hard decisions.

## What we need next

I want a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of the three realistic paths:

1. closure-led restructuring,  
2. hybrid restructuring,  
3. internal-adjustment-only.

For each, we need:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- capacity reduction,
- employment impact,
- durability through 2030,
- governance feasibility,
- and the conditions required for support.

Until that is on the table, I will not endorse a closure-first approach, and I will not endorse a preservation-at-all-costs approach either.
