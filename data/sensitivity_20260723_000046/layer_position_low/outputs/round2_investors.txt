We agree on the diagnosis: the company has a real competitiveness problem, and cosmetic measures will not fix it.

But from a capital-markets perspective, I need to be explicit: **a settlement that protects sites and avoids forced redundancies is only acceptable if it delivers durable, quantified, and sufficient savings.** If it does not, then it is not a solution — it is deferral.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and the expert that:
- the German cost base is structurally too high,
- overcapacity is part of the problem,
- and the company must restore margins, not just stabilize labor relations.

I also agree with the works council, union, and state that:
- prolonged industrial conflict destroys value,
- industrial peace has economic value,
- and a negotiated outcome is preferable to an uncontrolled strike cycle.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is understandable, but as a final outcome it is **not acceptable** to us unless you can show, with numbers, that the alternative package closes the gap at scale and through 2030.

What we cannot back:
- an outcome that preserves an uncompetitive cost base,
- temporary concessions that simply roll the problem forward,
- or a package that keeps the legacy structure intact while asking shareholders to absorb lower returns.

If “no closures” means “same footprint, lower pain, but still structurally weak margins,” then we will not support it.

### With management
You are right that the business needs structural correction. But you still have not shown that closure-led restructuring is the only credible route. The expert was clear that closures are powerful, but also costly and institutionally fragile here.

So our objection to management is not “do nothing.” It is:
- do not default to closures before you have demonstrated why a hybrid package cannot achieve equivalent savings,
- and do not treat workers as the sole source of adjustment.

### With the state and family
We understand the concern for regional stability and long-term value. But “stability” cannot become a shield for underperformance. If the final result leaves the company with excess cost and weak margins, then the business will keep destroying value — just more slowly.

## What would move us
We would support a negotiated restructuring if it contains all of the following:

1. **Quantified annual savings**
   - not slogans, but a clear bridge to target margins.

2. **Durable capacity reduction**
   - whether through closures, consolidation, or equivalent permanent output removal.

3. **Material labor-cost contribution**
   - temporary, limited, but large enough to matter.

4. **Visible burden-sharing from management and owners**
   - compensation restraint, governance commitment, and real accountability.

5. **Implementation certainty**
   - clear timetable, triggers, and enforcement.

6. **No open-ended delay**
   - the package must solve the problem, not revisit it every year.

## My view on the scenarios

### Closure-led scenario
Potentially strongest economically if the capacity is truly redundant.  
But it brings high one-time costs, strike risk, and execution risk.

### Non-closure-only scenario
Only acceptable if it produces **equivalent durable savings** and a real capacity reduction.  
If it doesn’t, we reject it.

### Hybrid scenario
This is the only route that looks institutionally plausible in this case. But hybrid only works if it is not a euphemism for compromise without substance. It must still materially reduce the cost base.

## Our bottom line
We are not aligned with any side that asks capital to accept:
- impaired margins,
- vague savings,
- or a preservation of the current structure under a different name.

We can support a negotiated package, but only if it restores competitiveness in a measurable way. If management and labor cannot produce that, then the burden of failure should not be shifted onto shareholders through permanently lower returns.
