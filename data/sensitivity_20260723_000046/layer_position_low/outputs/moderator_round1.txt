**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

**1) Points of agreement**
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable improvement in competitiveness**.
- There is broad agreement that a **purely symbolic or cosmetic solution is insufficient**.
- Most parties prefer a **negotiated settlement** over imposed measures and prolonged conflict.
- There is shared openness to some combination of:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - transfers/redeployment,
  - qualification measures,
  - voluntary exits,
  - temporary employee contributions,
  - and investment/product commitments.
- Several actors agree that any restructuring should avoid unnecessary damage to **industrial peace, value, and regional stability**.

**2) Core disagreements that remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works Council, union, state government, and family holding reject closures as the default or acceptable starting point; management and investors say closures may be necessary if alternatives do not close the cost gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Employee-side and state/family actors oppose forced layoffs; management and investors have not accepted this as a firm constraint.
- **Scope of employee contributions**: Labour side accepts only temporary, limited contributions tied to binding guarantees; management and investors want contributions large enough to produce durable savings.
- **Burden-sharing**: Employee side and state/family insist management, owners, and governance must also contribute visibly; management is focused on operational savings and capacity reduction, with no commitment yet to equivalent contributions from top stakeholders.
- **Extent of restructuring**: Management and investors want material, lasting reduction in capacity and labour costs; other parties want restructuring that preserves sites, employment, and co-determination as far as possible.

**3) Key trade-offs still unresolved**
- How to reconcile **competitive cost reduction** with **site and employment protection**.
- Whether the required savings can be achieved through **internal measures** alone, or whether closures/reductions are unavoidable.
- What level and duration of **worker concessions** would be acceptable, and what **binding commitments** would be provided in return.
- How to distribute the burden across:
  - workforce,
  - management,
  - owners,
  - and capital markets.
- Which commitments can be made **credibly and enforceably** through 2030.
- Whether a package can deliver both **industrial peace** and **measurable financial impact**.

**4) Refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: what savings each proposal delivers, by when, and with what impact on sites and jobs.
- Ask management to specify which alternatives to closures have been tested and why they are insufficient.
- Ask labour-side actors to identify which **capacity, working-time, and productivity measures** could be accepted if paired with binding guarantees.
- Require all parties to state what they would contribute to a **balanced package**, not just what they reject.
- Focus next round on a **side-by-side comparison of restructuring scenarios**: closures/reductions versus negotiated internal adjustments, including costs, risks, and safeguards.
