I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a symbolic one. I also agree that a settlement which simply postpones the problem would be irresponsible. But I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures and mass job destruction are the default answer, nor do I accept that the workforce should carry the main burden while the rest of the governance structure limits its own contribution.

## Where I agree
- The cost gap is real.
- The company must regain competitiveness.
- A purely cosmetic package would fail.
- Industrial peace matters.
- Any solution must be measurable, durable, and enforceable through 2030.

## Where I disagree

### 1) Plant closures are not the starting point
Management keeps treating closures as the “realistic” instrument and everything else as secondary. That is not acceptable from the state’s perspective.

Closing German plants would not just affect one balance sheet line. It would:
- cut skilled employment,
- damage supplier networks,
- weaken regional value creation,
- trigger political and social backlash,
- and risk lasting damage to the company’s role as an industrial anchor.

That is not a responsible default. It may be a last resort, but only after alternatives have been tested seriously and proven insufficient. We are not there.

### 2) Forced redundancies are not acceptable
I support restructuring, but not compulsory layoffs as the mechanism of choice. This company has built its competitiveness partly on social partnership and co-determination. If management now wants to discard that model, it will face resistance from the state.

### 3) The burden must be shared
I agree with the union and works council that employees cannot be the only ones making concessions. If there is to be a contribution from labour, then it must be:
- temporary,
- limited,
- tied to binding guarantees,
- and matched by visible contributions from management, owners, and governance.

That means I want to see:
- restraint in management compensation,
- real owner-side support where appropriate,
- investment commitments,
- and a credible product and platform plan for German sites.

## On the expert’s assessment
The specialist is right about one thing: internal adjustment only works if it is large enough and durable enough. But that does **not** automatically prove closures are necessary.

What the expert also showed is that the decisive question is **not ideology**, but whether a non-closure package can remove enough fixed cost, capacity, and overcapacity risk. That is exactly why I want the numbers.

So I am not rejecting restructuring. I am rejecting a closure-led solution as the first move.

## What I would support
I would support a package if it includes:
- no forced redundancies,
- no German plant closures in the agreed horizon,
- capacity reduction through internal means,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit arrangements,
- a temporary employee contribution,
- management and owner contributions,
- and binding investment/product commitments for the German sites.

That is the kind of package that could protect the company’s long-term health **and** preserve its regional anchor role.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a fait accompli on closures,
- a restructuring that permanently guts regional employment,
- a one-sided labor cost cut,
- or a settlement that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking rights.

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are lines I will defend.

## To management
You say you need structural savings. Fine. Then present a credible restructuring plan that shows:
- what savings come from internal capacity reduction,
- what savings come from labor measures,
- what contributions come from management and owners,
- and why closure is truly unavoidable if you still insist on it.

Right now, I see a lot of pressure on the workforce and too little evidence that the company has exhausted the less destructive options.

## To the works council and union
Your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures as the starting point is legitimate. But I also need you to engage with the cost problem seriously and in numbers, not only principles. If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be credible at scale.

## To the controlling family and investors
Long-term enterprise value is not served by hollowing out the company’s regional base. If this becomes a conflict in which the workforce is weakened, industrial peace is broken, and the company’s legitimacy erodes, then that is not a victory for value creation.

## My position
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring, but only one that:
- protects German sites as far as possible,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- preserves co-determination,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and restores competitiveness without destroying the company’s regional anchor function.

If management wants my support, it should come back with a quantified alternative package before pushing closures as inevitable.
