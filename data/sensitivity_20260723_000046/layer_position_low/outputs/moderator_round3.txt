## Core impasse

The central deadlock is not over whether the company needs restructuring. It is over **what counts as an acceptable source of savings and capacity reduction**.

More specifically:

1. **Management / investors** want a package that is clearly large enough to restore competitiveness, and they remain unwilling to rule out closures or stronger structural measures.
2. **Works council / union** insist that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are off-limits, and they want binding guarantees through 2030.
3. **State government / family holding** reject a closure-first logic but also do not want a preserve-everything outcome.
4. The **expert assessment** says internal measures alone are likely insufficient, but a full closure-led approach is institutionally hard to execute.

So the impasse is:  
**Everyone agrees on the need for material savings; nobody agrees on the acceptable mechanism for achieving them.**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Hybrid package with quantified site-by-site capacity reduction
A possible bridge would be a **hybrid restructuring** that includes:

- no compulsory redundancies,
- voluntary exits only,
- temporary employee contribution,
- management compensation restraint,
- owner-side contribution,
- targeted productivity and working-time measures,
- **plus limited, quantified capacity reduction by site or line**, without immediately closing whole plants unless a jointly verified threshold is reached.

This would allow management and investors to get permanent structural savings, while giving labour a pathway that does not begin with full closures.

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional no-closure moratorium with trigger-based review
A second compromise could be a **time-limited moratorium on closures** through a defined review period, for example 12–18 months, during which:

- internal measures are implemented first,
- savings are measured transparently,
- capacity reduction targets are monitored quarterly,
- and an agreed trigger mechanism is used if targets are missed.

This would give the employee side protection against premature closures, while giving management a structured escape clause if the non-closure package underperforms.

---

### Proposal 3: Employment guarantee linked to enforceable savings package
A third bridge could be a **package deal**:

- binding employment and site guarantees through 2030,
- in exchange for a defined temporary concession from labour,
- paired with management restraint and owner contribution,
- and a jointly audited savings bridge.

This would not resolve every disagreement, but it could shift the discussion from abstract red lines to a measurable exchange of commitments.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after internal measures, and what verified threshold would make you rule out plant closures for the duration of the agreement?**

### To the works council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what specific site-by-site measures are you prepared to accept that permanently remove enough capacity and fixed cost to be financially credible?**

### To the union
**What temporary employee contribution, in concrete terms, would you accept if it is matched by enforceable management restraint, owner support, and binding job guarantees through 2030?**

### To the state government
**Will you support a quantified hybrid package even if it includes partial capacity reduction, provided that German sites remain open and employment is protected as far as possible?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to back a no-closure settlement if it is independently verified to deliver the same long-term savings as a closure-led plan, or do you require structural site reduction regardless of the alternative?**

### To the institutional investors
**What minimum annual savings and margin improvement would make a hybrid, non-closure-first package acceptable to you, and what evidence would you require to trust those figures?**

### To the expert
**Which specific combination of temporary wage restraint, voluntary exits, productivity gains, and targeted capacity reduction would most plausibly close the gap without triggering the governance blockade?**
