I agree with one basic point made by management, and I want to say that clearly: this company does need a durable improvement in competitiveness, and a merely symbolic adjustment will not solve the problem. If the German cost base and capacity structure are out of line with the market, then pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

But I do not agree with the framing that closures and forced reductions are the default or that the burden should be pushed primarily onto the workforce.

From the perspective of the controlling family holding, the priority is long-term enterprise value, not short-term headline savings. That means two things at once:
- the business must become competitive again,
- and the company must not be destabilized in a way that permanently damages value, capability, or governance.

On that basis, I can support a serious restructuring. I cannot support a value-destructive confrontation or a package that simply burns down industrial peace without delivering a credible operational result.

To the Works Council and the Union: your insistence on avoiding compulsory redundancies is understandable, and I agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to imposed cuts. But I do not support a blanket position that every German plant must remain untouched regardless of economics. If the final package cannot produce real structural savings, then it is not a solution; it is postponement.

At the same time, I also do not accept management’s implication that the only credible route is immediate closures. That would be too blunt an instrument unless every alternative has been seriously tested and shown insufficient. A company with this ownership structure, this workforce, and this reputation should first exhaust options that preserve industrial capability:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- transfers and internal redeployment,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and a clear investment and product commitment.

To the State Government: I share your concern about regional stability and the value of the industrial base. That concern is legitimate. But I would caution against turning that into a veto on any meaningful restructuring. A protected status quo that leaves the company uncompetitive would ultimately harm the same sites and jobs you want to protect. Stability has to be earned by restoring performance, not by freezing the structure.

What I need to see before I support any final package is simple:
1. a credible savings plan that materially closes the competitiveness gap,
2. avoidance of permanent damage where possible,
3. industrial peace and implementation certainty,
4. and a fair distribution of the burden, including a visible contribution from employees, management, and the wider governance structure.

I am not interested in endless debate, and I am not looking for a cosmetic compromise. But I am also not prepared to endorse a course that sacrifices long-term value through either unilateral cuts or an immovable refusal to change. The company must become leaner and more competitive, yes. It must do so in a way that preserves the core asset and avoids an open-ended conflict. That is the standard I will apply.
