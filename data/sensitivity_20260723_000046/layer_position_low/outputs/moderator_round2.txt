**Moderator summary — Round 2**

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic fix;
- the current cost/capacity structure is not sustainable as-is;
- industrial peace and trust matter economically;
- any employee contribution must be **temporary, limited, and matched by broader burden-sharing**;
- a purely one-sided solution would be unacceptable;
- quantified scenarios are needed before final decisions.

There is also broad recognition that:
- management must show numbers, not just principles;
- employee-side alternatives must be credible at scale if plant closures are to be avoided;
- governance constraints make an imposed solution difficult.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved dispute is whether **plant closures and forced redundancies** are legitimate and necessary instruments.

- **Works council, union, and state government** reject closures as a starting point and oppose compulsory redundancies.
- **Management and investors** argue that meaningful structural capacity reduction and cost cutting are necessary, and that closures may be required if alternatives do not achieve enough savings.
- **Family holding** opposes a closure-first approach but also rejects preserving the status quo at all costs.

A second disagreement concerns the **burden-sharing balance**:
- labor representatives say workers should not carry the main burden for a crisis they did not create;
- management and investors insist the package must still produce material labor-cost and capacity relief.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- whether a **non-closure package** can remove enough fixed cost and capacity to match a closure-led outcome;
- how much savings each measure would deliver, and for how long;
- the size of **one-time restructuring costs** versus the value of permanent savings;
- the employment, site, and regional impacts of each path;
- how to reconcile **co-determination, veto constraints, and stakeholder consent** with the need for rapid restructuring;
- what level of management, owner, and investor contribution would make a compromise credible.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on a **side-by-side quantified comparison** of three options:
1. closure-led restructuring,
2. non-closure internal adjustment,
3. hybrid package.

For each, parties should specify:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- capacity reduction,
- employment impact,
- durability through 2030,
- governance feasibility,
- and the conditions required for support.

The discussion should move from general positions to a **measurable cost-capacity-employment bridge** that identifies which package can close the competitiveness gap while remaining institutionally viable.
