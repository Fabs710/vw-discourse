We agree with the diagnosis, but not with any solution that fails to restore margins decisively.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **durable** restructuring.
- Cosmetic measures are unacceptable.
- Industrial conflict is value-destructive.
- Any labor contribution must be **temporary, limited, and matched by broader burden-sharing**.
- Management must present **numbers**, not slogans.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
Your insistence on **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies”** is not acceptable unless you can show, with hard numbers, that the alternative package removes enough **fixed cost and capacity** to restore competitiveness through 2030.

Right now, I have not seen that proof.

If “no closures” means:
- preserving underutilized capacity,
- leaving fixed overhead largely intact,
- and relying mainly on temporary concessions,

then that is not restructuring. That is postponement.

We will not back a package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive just to avoid difficult decisions.

### With management
You are correct that the company needs structural correction. But you still have not made the case that closures are **necessary**, rather than merely the strongest bargaining instrument.

If you want capital markets support, show the comparison clearly:
- no-closure package,
- hybrid package,
- closure-led package,

with:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- capacity removed,
- headcount impact,
- and ROS effect through 2030.

Until then, closures remain a **claim**, not a proven necessity.

### With the state government and family holding
We understand the concern for regional stability and governance feasibility. But stability is not value if it locks in a weak business.

If the final settlement avoids closures but leaves the company:
- overcapacitated,
- under-margined,
- and dependent on repeated concessions,

then shareholders will continue to price in weak returns. That is not acceptable.

## Our position
We can support a negotiated restructuring only if it delivers:

1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **Material fixed-cost removal**
3. **Meaningful labor-cost contribution**
4. **Visible management and owner-side restraint**
5. **Implementation certainty**
6. **No open-ended delay**

What we cannot support:
- a “no closures” deal that preserves too much capacity,
- temporary concessions that expire before the economics improve,
- or a cosmetic compromise dressed up as industrial peace.

## What we need next
We want the independent specialist to produce the requested **plant-by-plant quantified comparison**. Without that, this discussion is stuck between:
- a closure-first threat, and
- a no-closure promise.

Neither is enough. We need to know which package actually closes the gap on a risk-adjusted basis.
