We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the starting point.

The workforce has already carried a huge burden through transformation, investment, and repeated promises that jobs and sites would be secured. Management cannot now come forward with a fait accompli that breaks those commitments and asks employees to pay the price for strategic mistakes and a cost problem that was not created by the people on the shop floor.

Our priorities are clear:

- keep every German plant open
- rule out forced layoffs
- protect the historic job-security framework
- secure binding guarantees for sites and employment through 2030
- preserve real co-determination in shaping any restructuring
- ensure that any contribution from employees is fair, temporary, and matched by binding commitments from management

We are not refusing change. We are refusing a restructuring that destroys jobs and closes sites. There are alternatives that must be explored first: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, transfers, qualification, internal reallocation, product commitments, and negotiated cost measures that do not break the workforce.

If management wants responsibility from employees, then it must show responsibility too:
- no unilateral closure plans
- no forced redundancies
- no erosion of collective protections
- no decisions over workers’ heads

If those conditions are not met, we will resist with all means available under co-determination and industrial relations, including mobilising the workforce. The burden of proving necessity lies with management, and at this point we do not see that burden met.
