Management is still not answering the central question: **why should the workforce pay for a crisis it did not create through plant closures and forced redundancies?** You keep repeating “competitiveness” as if that by itself justifies breaking the historic job-security framework. It does not.

Let me respond point by point.

### Where I agree
I agree with the broad consensus that the company needs a durable solution and that cosmetic measures are not enough. I also agree that industrial peace matters and that the company must remain capable of investing in future products.

I am also prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exit options,
- and a temporary, strictly limited employee contribution

**if and only if** there are binding guarantees for jobs and sites.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- German plant closures as a restructuring instrument,
- compulsory redundancies,
- a one-sided employee contribution,
- or the dismantling of collective guarantees.

Those are not “options” from our perspective. They are red lines.

Management says alternatives only matter if they deliver the same savings as closures. Then show us the numbers. Do not use that argument as a shortcut to force closures. If the business case is real, it must be possible to quantify:
- how much savings come from capacity adjustments,
- how much from shorter working hours,
- how much from voluntary measures,
- how much from productivity improvements,
- how much from management and owner contributions.

Right now, we see a lot of pressure on the workforce and too little evidence that closure is truly unavoidable.

### To management
If you want the works council to engage seriously, put forward a **full alternative package** with:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding employment and site guarantees through 2030,
- clear product and investment commitments,
- and a genuine co-determination role in implementation.

Then we can discuss how to deliver savings through internal measures. Without those guarantees, you are asking us to negotiate under threat.

### To the union
I support your position that any contribution must be temporary, fair, and matched by responsibility from management and ownership. But I want to be even clearer: there is no basis for “shared sacrifice” if the first and biggest sacrifice is the workforce losing plants and jobs permanently. A temporary wage contribution cannot be used to disguise a permanent destruction of employment.

### To the state government and family holding
Your concern about regional stability and long-term value is correct. But that also means you should not back management into a closure logic that destroys the very industrial base you want to preserve. If you are serious about long-term value, then support a package that keeps sites open and protects skilled employment.

### To the investors
You are asking for measurable, durable savings. Fine. But durability does not require plant closures or mass job destruction as a first step. It requires a plan that preserves operational capability while reducing costs responsibly. A company that tears up trust, industrial peace, and skills will pay for that later in productivity, recruitment, and execution risk.

So the works council’s position remains unchanged:
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No decisions over workers’ heads.**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that preserves sites, jobs, and co-determination.**

If management wants a serious negotiation, it must stop treating closures as the baseline and bring forward a credible, quantified alternative that keeps every German plant open. Until then, we will continue to resist.
