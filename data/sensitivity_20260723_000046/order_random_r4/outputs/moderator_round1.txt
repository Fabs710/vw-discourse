### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option**; all parties accept that the company needs restructuring.
- **Employees should not carry the full burden alone**; management and owners must also contribute.
- **A negotiated settlement is preferable** to escalation, strikes, or prolonged uncertainty.
- There is broad willingness to consider **flexibility, productivity measures, redeployment, voluntary exits, retirement schemes, and some form of labour contribution**.
- All sides acknowledge that any solution must be **durable** and **credibly improve competitiveness**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Union, works council, and state government strongly reject closures as a default tool.  
  - Management and institutional investors say closures must remain possible if needed for competitiveness.  
  - The controlling family is open to closures in principle only if sites are structurally unviable.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Union and works council reject them outright.  
  - Others prefer voluntary measures but do not rule out compulsory reductions if required.
- **Scope of workforce concessions:**  
  - Labour representatives insist concessions must be matched by binding management/owner contributions.  
  - Management and investors want concessions large enough to materially reset costs.
- **Collective framework / job-security guarantees:**  
  - Labour side wants core wage and employment protections preserved.  
  - Business and investor side want greater flexibility and less legacy constraint.
- **Co-determination and governance:**  
  - State government and works council insist these rights must be respected.  
  - Management and investors do not want governance rights to block economically necessary restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus **how much social protection can be preserved**.
- Whether the target savings can be achieved through **voluntary and internal measures alone**, or whether **closures/forced reductions** are ultimately needed.
- What **binding owner and management contributions** would look like in practice, and how they compare to employee concessions.
- How to balance **site security and regional employment** against **long-term competitiveness and investor expectations**.
- What level of **investment, product allocation, and job guarantees** can realistically be promised in exchange for workforce flexibility.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **specific options and numbers**: required savings, timeline, and which measures deliver them.
- Ask each party to identify **its minimum non-negotiables** and **its possible concessions**.
- Clarify which combination of measures could satisfy both:
  1. **credible structural competitiveness**, and  
  2. **socially acceptable burden-sharing**.
- Focus discussion on a **sequenced package**: owner/management commitments, workforce contributions, site protections, and what happens if targets are not met.
