## Core impasse

The central deadlock is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. Nearly all parties accept that it is.

The impasse is over **sequencing and fallback**:

- **Labour, works council, and the state** want a **site-preserving test first**, with no closures or compulsory redundancies as the initial or default fallback.
- **Management, the controlling family, and investors** want to preserve **economic flexibility**, including the ability to close sites or reduce headcount if the quantified savings gap remains too large.
- The disagreement is therefore about whether closures/forced redundancies are:
  1. **a last-resort option after alternatives fail**, or
  2. **a necessary live fallback from the start** to make the restructuring credible.

A second, related impasse is **evidence burden**:
- Some parties say closure is being treated as a default without proof.
- Others say they cannot give up closure leverage until the numbers prove a site can survive.

So the dispute is really about **what must be proven, by whom, and before which decision becomes available**.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise 1: Time-limited moratorium + independent plant audit
**Proposal**
- Agree a **defined moratorium period** during which:
  - no plant closures are announced,
  - no compulsory redundancies are triggered,
  - no side uses closure as bargaining leverage.
- In parallel, commission an **independent plant-by-plant operating audit** with a fixed deadline.

**Purpose**
- This gives labour and the state the site-preserving test they want.
- It gives management, owners, and investors the quantified decision basis they require.

**Condition**
- The moratorium ends automatically when the audit is delivered and reviewed.
- At that point, all options return to the table if the numbers require it.

---

### Compromise 2: “Alternatives first” with a quantified residual-gap trigger
**Proposal**
- The parties pre-commit to a structured sequence:
  1. voluntary exits / retirement,
  2. working-time and productivity measures,
  3. temporary labour-cost contribution,
  4. redeployment and internal capacity measures,
  5. owner and management contributions,
  6. only then a review of whether any site-specific residual gap remains.

**Purpose**
- This preserves management’s right to act if the gap remains.
- It also ensures closures and forced redundancies are not treated as the first-line response.

**Condition**
- Closure or compulsory redundancy can only be considered if the audit shows a **documented residual gap** after the agreed measures are implemented and measured.

---

### Compromise 3: Site preservation for viable plants, controlled restructuring for non-viable ones
**Proposal**
- Classify plants into three categories based on the independent audit:
  - **Viable with measures**
  - **Viable only with additional investment/product allocation**
  - **Structurally non-viable**
- For the first two categories, pursue site-preserving restructuring.
- For the third category, allow a separate decision process with enhanced protections, consultation, and social mitigation.

**Purpose**
- This avoids an all-or-nothing fight over every site.
- It allows labour and the state to protect viable plants.
- It gives management and owners a pathway for sites that genuinely cannot be stabilized.

**Condition**
- No plant may be placed in the “non-viable” category without the independent audit and documented alternatives test.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a fixed-date, independent plant-by-plant audit and suspend any closure or compulsory redundancy announcements until that audit is completed and reviewed?

### To the controlling family
**Question:**  
What binding owner contribution are you prepared to make—capital, investment, or guarantees—to support a site-preserving restructuring before any closure is pursued?

### To institutional investors
**Question:**  
If the independent audit shows that a site-preserving package closes the quantified savings gap, will you support it even if it leaves closure off the table?

### To the state government
**Question:**  
Will you use your blocking and co-determination powers to require an independent alternatives test before any closure or forced layoff is formally pursued?

### To the works council
**Question:**  
If the audit shows a specific site cannot be made viable even after all agreed alternatives, what restructuring path would you accept that still protects employment as far as possible?

### To the industrial union
**Question:**  
What exact level of temporary labour-cost contribution and flexibility are you prepared to put on the table if it is paired with binding site and employment guarantees?

### To the independent expert
**Question:**  
What minimum data set and decision threshold do you need to determine whether the residual gap after alternatives is small enough for site preservation or large enough to justify deeper restructuring?

### To management, investors, family, and the state jointly
**Question:**  
Can you agree that no side will treat closure or no-closure as predetermined until the independent audit has produced a plant-by-plant viability assessment?
