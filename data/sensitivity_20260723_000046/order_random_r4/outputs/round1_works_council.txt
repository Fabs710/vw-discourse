I will be very clear: **the workforce will not accept closures, forced redundancies, or the tearing up of the historic job-security framework as the price of management’s failed strategy.** That is not an opening bargaining position to be “nudged” away from — it is the line that protects tens of thousands of livelihoods and the industrial base in Germany.

### Where I agree
I agree with the basic diagnosis that the company needs a credible restructuring and that the burden cannot fall on employees alone. I also agree that cosmetic measures are useless. If the plan does not restore competitiveness, it is not a plan.

I further agree that:
- management must contribute,
- owners must contribute,
- and any settlement has to be durable, not just a pause before the next attack on jobs.

### Where I fundamentally disagree
#### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable “tool”
To management and the investors: your repeated framing of closures as an ordinary restructuring option is exactly what makes this confrontation so serious. A closure is not a neutral efficiency measure. It destroys sites, supplier networks, skills, and trust. Once you break that barrier, you damage the whole group’s social and industrial legitimacy.

The works council will not accept a settlement that normalizes closures in Germany.

#### 2) “Structural” cannot mean “employees pay, everyone else waits”
I hear the language about “durable cost reduction” and “credible capacity reduction.” But too often that is code for:
- wage cuts,
- job cuts,
- loss of guarantees,
- and pressure on the workforce,
while management autonomy and owner expectations remain untouched.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is a transfer of risk onto employees.

#### 3) No compulsory redundancies
To the investor side: if your support for a deal depends on compulsory layoffs, then you are asking for a settlement that will trigger massive resistance and industrial conflict. That is not “value protection”; that is conflict buying. We know exactly what that produces: warning strikes, plant-level mobilisation, political escalation, and a much weaker company.

### What would be a serious basis for discussion
If you want our cooperation, then we need binding commitments, not vague assurances:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Site and employment guarantees through 2030**
- **Capacity reductions only through socially responsible means**
  - internal redeployment
  - voluntary exit programmes
  - retirement solutions
  - working-time arrangements
- **Management restraint**
  - real contribution from leadership
  - no one-sided demands on labour
- **Owner contribution**
  - capital support and investment commitment
  - not just pressure from the top
- **Genuine co-determination**
  - the workforce must have a real role in shaping the restructuring, not just absorbing its costs

### To the state government
You are right to insist that closures cannot be the default. But I would go further: the government should use its position to defend the industrial base, not merely “moderate” the damage. Co-determination and the special governance structure exist for exactly this reason: to prevent a short-term market logic from gutting viable industrial capacity.

### To the controlling family
You say you do not want a one-sided workforce solution. Fine. Then prove it. If you want the workforce to contribute, the company must make equally binding commitments on jobs, sites, and investment. A vague promise that you will “support a turnaround” is not enough after the trust breach we have already experienced.

### To management
Your argument still starts from the assumption that closure and layoffs are the benchmark and everything else is secondary. That is precisely why we are in conflict. You do not get to present a fait accompli and then ask the workforce to socialise the pain. If management wants an agreement, it must come with:
- a real industrial plan,
- a commitment to German sites,
- and an end to unilateral threats.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate **a fair, socially responsible restructuring**. We are **not** prepared to negotiate away the core guarantees that protect the workforce from dispossession.

If the company’s answer is “closures, layoffs, and wage sacrifice,” then the answer from the works council will be resistance — in the boardroom, in the plants, and in public. If the company is serious about a future in Germany, then it must put **sites, jobs, and co-determination** at the centre of that future.
