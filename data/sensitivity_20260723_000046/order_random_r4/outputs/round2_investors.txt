We are listening for one thing above all: **a credible, quantified path back to competitive margins**. Not a political slogan, not a temporary truce, and not a restructuring that leaves the German cost base structurally too high.

## Where we agree

### To the union and works council
You are right that employees should not carry the full burden alone. A one-sided solution would be unstable, and instability destroys value. We also agree that cosmetic fixes are useless. If the package does not materially improve competitiveness, it will not be acceptable to capital markets.

### To the controlling family and state government
You are right that a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict. Prolonged strikes and governance paralysis would be value-destructive. We also agree that management must present the economics clearly and that any burden-sharing must include management and owners.

### To management
You are right on the central point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The brand’s margin problem is real, and capital markets will not reward delay.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No plant closures” cannot be a principle
This is the key issue. We do **not** support a settlement that rules out closures in advance if closures are required to reset capacity and cost. If the company’s footprint is structurally too large, then protecting every site as a matter of principle is not responsible stewardship — it is value destruction by another name.

That said, closure should not be a slogan either. It must be justified by:
- a clear cost gap,
- a transparent site-by-site business case,
- and proof that less damaging options are insufficient.

But if that proof exists, then closures must remain available.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not acceptable as an absolute
We prefer voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, and phased reductions. But if the required savings cannot be delivered otherwise, the company must retain flexibility. A hard absolute on compulsory layoffs risks making the restructuring non-credible.

### 3) “Shared sacrifice” must be real, not symbolic
We are not interested in a deal where:
- employees give up materially,
- management offers modest restraint,
- owners provide bridge support,
- and the operating structure remains unchanged.

That would buy peace briefly and leave margins impaired. Capital markets will not treat that as a solution.

## What would move us

We would support a package if it includes all of the following:

- **quantified savings** with clear timing
- **durable capacity reduction**
- **meaningful labour-cost reset**
- **management and owner contributions**
- **a measurable path to competitive return-on-sales**
- **execution certainty**
- **no ambiguity about what happens if targets are missed**

## On the current positions

### Union / works council
We respect the insistence on social responsibility, but your current “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” position is too rigid unless you can show us a package that closes the economics without them. So far, we have not seen that evidence.

### State government
We understand the regional stakes, but regional preservation cannot override competitiveness indefinitely. If the company stays uncompetitive, employment and investment will erode anyway — just more slowly and with less control.

### Controlling family
You are right that one-sided workforce cuts are unacceptable. But “supporting a turnaround” is not enough unless it translates into hard structural action, not just capital support.

### Management
You are closest to our view on the need for hard action, but you still need to do the work of proving the savings gap and the impact of each measure. Without that, market confidence will remain weak.

## Bottom line
We can back **a disciplined, durable restructuring that restores competitiveness**. We cannot back a cosmetic settlement or a package that permanently protects overcapacity. If the company can show that a site-preserving alternative truly delivers the same savings, then we will examine it. If not, then closures and workforce reductions must remain on the table.

That is the standard capital markets will apply.
