We are now at the point where vague principles are no longer enough.

I agree with several things the union has said:

- Doing nothing is not an option.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to open conflict.
- Employees should not carry the full burden alone.
- Voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and productivity measures should all be part of the toolbox.

But I do **not** agree with turning these into absolute vetoes that make a real restructuring impossible.

### Where I disagree, directly

#### 1) “No plant closures” as a fixed rule
I cannot accept that as a governing principle. If a site is structurally uncompetitive, then refusing even to consider closure means the company is being asked to preserve capacity for political comfort rather than economic reality.

That is not a sustainable basis for a long-term industrial settlement. It would protect the appearance of stability while storing up a larger crisis later.

#### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I understand why that is your line, but I will not endorse any formula that makes the workforce’s position untouchable regardless of business conditions. If voluntary measures and internal redeployment are sufficient, excellent. If not, then the company must retain the ability to act.

I am not asking for mass layoffs as a first resort. I am saying you cannot rule out every hard instrument before the numbers are even on the table.

#### 3) Binding guarantees without binding economic realism
You ask for site and employment guarantees, protection of the collective framework, and negotiated employee contributions. That can only work if the package also produces a **real and durable** cost reset. Otherwise we are not restructuring; we are buying time.

The family holding will not support a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value by freezing an uncompetitive structure in place.

### What I need to hear from the union and works council

If you want our support for a negotiated package, then it must include:

- a **credible and quantified savings target**
- a **clear breakdown** of what savings come from labour, capacity, and productivity
- a **time-bound implementation plan**
- a **real management contribution**
- a **real owner contribution**, but tied to structural reform
- and a mechanism that allows the company to act if targets are missed

That is the difference between a solution and a delay.

### On the state government
I respect the regional responsibility, but a blocking-minority holder cannot be used to freeze the company’s industrial structure forever. Governance rights exist to protect the long-term enterprise, not to prevent any difficult adjustment.

### On management
I share the view that management must be specific. “Hard decisions” is not enough. The company should put forward the exact cost gap, the measures that close it, and the sequencing. If management cannot quantify the gap, then no one else can be asked to sign up to painful concessions.

### My bottom line
I am open to a socially responsible restructuring, but not to one that is defined by prohibitions on closures and redundancies regardless of economic necessity.

If there is a package that:
- materially restores competitiveness,
- shares the burden fairly,
- avoids unnecessary instability,
- and preserves the company’s long-term value,

then I am prepared to support it through governance.

If the package instead amounts to “keep everything open, protect every guarantee, and hope the market improves,” then I will not support it.
