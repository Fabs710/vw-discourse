### Core impasse
The central impasse is **not over whether restructuring is needed**. Most parties now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **sequencing and fallback**:

- **Labour, state government, and parts of the family** want a **site-preserving, alternatives-first process** with no closure/layoff threat embedded from the start.
- **Management and institutional investors** want to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies available as credible fallback options** if the residual gap remains too large after alternatives.
- The **expert** has confirmed that the missing piece is a **plant-by-plant quantified residual-gap analysis**, but that data is not yet in hand.

So the live conflict is:
1. **Whether closures/forced redundancies may remain on the table now**, or only after alternatives are tested.
2. **Who gets to define “non-viable”** and by what evidence.
3. **Whether a moratorium is a real pause or a disguised countdown to closure.**

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Sequenced alternatives-first package with a hard audit gate
**Structure**
- Immediate time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies.
- Independent plant-by-plant audit within a fixed deadline.
- Concurrent implementation of first-wave measures:
  - voluntary exits
  - retirement
  - redeployment
  - working-time flexibility
  - productivity measures
  - temporary labour-cost moderation
  - management and owner contributions
- A residual-gap threshold reviewed only after the audit.

**Why this may bridge**
- Gives labour and the state the real alternatives-first process they want.
- Gives management and investors a quantified basis for any later deeper action.
- Preserves the option of tougher measures **only after** evidence, rather than as a starting threat.

---

#### Proposal 2: Site-specific deal classification with three bins
**Structure**
After the audit, each plant is placed into one of three categories:

1. **Protected viability sites**  
   Sites that can be preserved through the agreed package.

2. **Conditional sites**  
   Sites kept open only if specific volume, product, or capex conditions are met.

3. **Structural exception sites**  
   Sites where, and only where, the audit shows the residual gap remains too large even after full alternatives.

**Why this may bridge**
- Avoids a blanket “all sites saved” or “closures from day one” posture.
- Lets different stakeholders see that not every site is treated identically.
- Creates a narrower and more evidence-based route for any deeper restructuring.

---

#### Proposal 3: Binding social contract in exchange for bounded management flexibility
**Structure**
Labour and government accept a meaningful restructuring package if management and owners agree to:
- binding investment commitments,
- transparent site economics,
- no closure or redundancy announcements before audit completion,
- defined consultation rights,
- and a social mitigation framework.

In return, management and investors retain **bounded** flexibility to propose site-specific measures later if the audit proves a persistent gap.

**Why this may bridge**
- Addresses labour’s demand for guarantees and owner contribution.
- Addresses management and investor concern that restructuring cannot be permanently frozen.
- Turns the conflict from “yes/no closures” into “what evidence and what process are required before any deeper action.”

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**What exact residual savings gap do you believe remains after voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, productivity gains, and management/owner contributions—and will you commit to withholding any closure or compulsory redundancy announcements until an independent audit tests that gap site by site?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**If the independent audit shows that one or more plants still have a material residual gap after all reasonable alternatives, what specific site-preserving measures would you accept before concluding that any deeper restructuring is unavoidable?**

#### To the Works Council
**What concrete conditions would you require for a moratorium to count as a genuine protection rather than a delay tactic, and how would you verify that those conditions are being met during the audit period?**

#### To the Controlling Family
**What binding owner contributions and investment commitments are you prepared to place on the table to demonstrate stewardship before any discussion of site reductions becomes legitimate?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**Will you define in advance the decision rule that determines whether a plant remains open after the audit, so that your blocking power supports a real alternatives test rather than an open-ended stalemate?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What quantified margin or return threshold would make a site-preserving solution acceptable to you, and are you prepared to endorse a temporary moratorium if that threshold is tested through an independent audit?**

#### To the Expert
**Can you specify the exact data fields and threshold tests the audit must include so that all parties agree in advance what would count as a proven residual gap?**
