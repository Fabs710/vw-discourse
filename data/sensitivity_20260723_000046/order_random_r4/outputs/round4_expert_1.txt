  - voluntary exits / retirement
  - working-time changes
  - productivity gains
  - internal redeployment
  - temporary labour-cost moderation
  - management overhead cuts
  - owner support and investment commitment
- If the gap is **material and persistent**, then a site-preserving solution becomes progressively less credible, and deeper restructuring options may become economically necessary.

## What must be quantified
To determine whether closures or compulsory redundancies are economically necessary, I would need for each German site:

1. **Current production volumes and capacity utilization**
2. **Fixed cost base** by site
3. **Variable conversion cost** per unit
4. **Labor cost per vehicle**
5. **Break-even volume**
6. **Product allocation and model mix**
7. **Logistics and complexity costs**
8. **Capex needed to keep the site competitive**
9. **Savings from each proposed alternative measure**
10. **Residual gap after first-wave measures**

Without this, any “closure required” claim is incomplete.

## What a defensible decision framework looks like
The right sequence is:

1. **Independent plant-by-plant audit**
2. **Implement alternatives first**
   - voluntary exits
   - retirement
   - redeployment
   - working-time measures
   - productivity improvements
   - temporary labour-cost contribution
   - management/owner contribution
3. **Measure the residual gap**
4. **Only then assess whether any site remains structurally non-viable**

That is the only way to separate genuine economics from bargaining leverage.

## Feasibility of alternatives
A site-preserving turnaround is most plausible if:
- utilization can be lifted materially,
- labor and overhead costs can be reduced enough to move break-even,
- product allocation can be improved,
- capex is sufficient to avoid underinvestment,
- and labor concessions are durable, not temporary.

It is less plausible if:
- the company has **chronic overcapacity**
- one or more plants have **persistent low utilization**
- logistics and complexity costs are structurally high
- the cost base remains above peers even after concessions

## Risk statement
The main risk on the labour-preserving route is that it produces **delay without sufficient structural change**.  
The main risk on the closure route is that it destroys capacity and trust before proving that alternatives cannot close the gap.
