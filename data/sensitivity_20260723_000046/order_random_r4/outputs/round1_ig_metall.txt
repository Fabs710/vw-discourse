We recognise the company is under real pressure, and we are prepared to contribute to a viable solution. But our starting point is clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No destruction of the collective wage framework**
- **No restructuring where employees carry the whole burden while management and owners are spared**

What we will argue for is a **fair shared-sacrifice package** that secures the sites and the workforce while restoring competitiveness through other means. That means:

- capacity reductions and efficiency measures **without closing plants**
- flexible working-time and production adjustments
- a **negotiated employee contribution** only if it is matched by real contributions from management and owners
- binding commitments on **site security, investment, and future product allocation**
- a durable collective agreement that preserves bargaining standards

We are not denying the need for change. But any solution must be **socially responsible, reversible where possible, and balanced**. If the company wants workforce concessions, it must also put real skin in the game: management restraint, owner support, and a credible industrial strategy.

If the proposal is simply “jobs and sites down, wages down, employees pay,” then we will reject it. If there is a genuine compromise that protects employment and spreads the burden fairly, we are ready to negotiate.
