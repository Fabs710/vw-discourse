We are now at the point where we must separate **economic necessity** from **management preference**.

My position remains clear:

- I agree that the company needs restructuring.
- I agree that employees cannot be asked to carry the full burden.
- I agree that the package must be durable and competitive.
- I agree that management and owners must contribute visibly.

But I do **not** accept that plant closures or compulsory redundancies are the default answer, or that they should be treated as an ordinary fallback before the alternatives have been fully tested.

## To management
You still have not provided the decisive evidence.

The expert is right about one thing: the burden of proof is on management. If you want us to consider any closure at all, you must present a **site-by-site operating case** with:
- the exact savings gap,
- fixed and variable costs by plant,
- utilization rates,
- logistics and complexity costs,
- and the savings from each alternative measure.

Until that is on the table, “closures may be necessary” is not a business case. It is a demand for trust without evidence.

And I will say plainly: I do not grant blank checks when jobs, sites, and regional value creation are at stake.

## To the union and works council
I support your insistence that:
- workers should not pay alone,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and productivity measures should come first,
- and any employee contribution must be matched by management and owners.

That is legitimate.

Where I stop with you is the idea that **no closure and no compulsory redundancy can be absolute under any circumstances**. I understand the political and social logic, but as a shareholder with responsibility for the enterprise’s long-term health, I cannot endorse a formula that makes economically necessary action impossible forever.

So yes, I support a **site-preserving alternatives test first**. No, I do not support writing off all future flexibility before we know the numbers.

## To the controlling family
You are right that enterprise value matters, and that cosmetic compromise is not enough. But your side also cannot use “competitiveness” as a general justification for shutting sites without exhausting alternatives and showing the figures.

If you want my support for difficult restructuring, then the package must include:
- binding investment commitments in Germany,
- a real owner contribution,
- protection of the industrial base as far as economically possible,
- and an explicit preference for internal and voluntary measures before any closure decision.

That is not sentimentality. That is how you preserve the company’s long-term legitimacy and workforce trust.

## To institutional investors
I understand your demand for a credible path to margins. That is reasonable.

What I reject is the implied assumption that seriousness only exists if plant closures remain permanently on the table from day one. The expert has said clearly that a site-preserving deal can work if the savings gap is modest enough or if the company is willing to use the full range of internal levers. That is exactly why we need the numbers before making irreversible decisions.

So I would say this directly: if a site-preserving package can close the gap, it deserves serious consideration. If it cannot, then management must prove that with data, not pressure.

## On the expert assessment
The expert’s contribution is useful because it confirms what I have been saying all along:

- closures are a last resort, not a starting point;
- the site-by-site economics have not yet been shown;
- alternatives are feasible and should be tested rigorously;
- the real question is the size of the residual savings gap.

That is precisely why I will not accept a closure-first logic.

## My practical position
I am prepared to support a restructuring package that includes:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- and binding management/owner contributions,

**provided that** it is tied to:
- a genuine site-by-site alternatives test,
- binding site and employment protections for the state,
- preservation of co-determination,
- and a clear rule that closures are only examined after alternatives have demonstrably failed.

## What I will not accept
I will not support:
- closures as a default tool,
- compulsory redundancies as a first response,
- any deal that sidelines the state’s veto role,
- or any restructuring that turns the regional workforce into the adjustment reserve while management and owners remain insulated.

That would be politically unacceptable and economically short-sighted.

## [SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant restructuring and valuation audit]
I want an independent, plant-by-plant restructuring and valuation audit that tests:
- whether site-preserving measures can close the savings gap,
- what residual gap remains after voluntary and internal measures,
- and at what point, if any, a closure becomes economically unavoidable.

Until that audit exists, I will not be pushed into accepting plant closures as “common sense.”

## Bottom line
My support is available for a **socially responsible, site-preserving restructuring** that restores competitiveness and shares the burden fairly.

My support is **not** available for a package that begins with closures and layoffs and only later asks whether alternatives existed. In our state, and in this company, that order matters.
