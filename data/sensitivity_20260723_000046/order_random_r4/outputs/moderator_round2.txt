### Core impasse

The central deadlock is over **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies are legitimate restructuring tools at all**.

- **Union / Works Council / State Government**: treat closures and forced layoffs as non-starters, and demand binding site and employment guarantees.
- **Management / Investors / Controlling Family**: insist those tools must remain available if quantified savings cannot otherwise be achieved.
- **Common ground** exists on the need for restructuring, shared burden, and management/owner contribution — but there is no agreement yet on the **fallback mechanism** if voluntary and internal measures do not close the gap.

A second, related impasse is **credibility and sequencing**:
- employee side wants a proven, site-preserving alternative first;
- capital/management side wants permission to keep closure/redundancy options open before committing to full guarantees.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: “No immediate closure / no immediate compulsory redundancy” with a review trigger
A possible bridge is a **time-bound restructuring moratorium**:
- no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies **during an initial negotiation and implementation period**;
- in that period, parties implement:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - productivity measures,
  - temporary labour-cost contribution;
- if agreed savings targets are **not met by a defined date**, the parties trigger a **joint review** of deeper measures.

This preserves the employee-side demand for immediate protection while allowing management/investors a contingent fallback rather than an open-ended veto.

#### Proposal 2: Site-specific package with “preserve sites unless hard threshold is missed”
A second bridge is a **site-by-site approach**:
- each plant gets a quantified business case and a tailored rescue plan;
- closures are excluded **unless a site-specific economic threshold is demonstrably missed** after exhausting agreed alternatives;
- any such decision would require:
  - documented evidence,
  - consultation,
  - a social mitigation package,
  - and governance approval.

This narrows the dispute from “closures in principle” to “closure only after verified failure of alternatives.”

#### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing matrix with binding equalization
A third compromise is a **burden-sharing matrix**:
- every concession from employees must be matched by defined contributions from management and owners;
- owner side: investment commitments, site-security commitments, and possibly capital support;
- management side: compensation restraint, overhead reduction, operational restructuring;
- workforce side: temporary labor-cost moderation, flexibility, and voluntary measures.

This does not settle the closure issue by itself, but it can make any package more acceptable by proving the burden is not one-sided.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
Can you specify, by site and by measure, the exact savings gap and show which combination of voluntary/internal measures would close it **before** asking for closures or compulsory redundancies?

#### To the Union / Works Council
Would you accept a **time-limited, quantified trial period** in which closures and compulsory redundancies are paused, provided there is a binding review trigger if savings targets are missed?

#### To the Controlling Family
Are you prepared to commit to **binding site and investment guarantees** in exchange for a labor-cost and productivity package, even if that constrains your future governance flexibility?

#### To the State Government
Will you use your governance position to insist on a **site-preserving alternatives test** before any closure decision, and if so, what threshold would you require for that test to be considered passed?

#### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a restructuring package that preserves sites and avoids compulsory redundancies **if** it delivers the same quantified savings and margin improvement through other means, and what evidence would you require to accept that equivalence?

#### To the Works Council
If a package includes binding employment and site commitments plus measurable savings targets, what specific fallback mechanism would you accept if the targets are missed, short of immediate closure or layoffs?
