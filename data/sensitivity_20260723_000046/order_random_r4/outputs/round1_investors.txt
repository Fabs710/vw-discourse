From an institutional investor perspective, the key test is simple: **does this package credibly restore competitive margins and free cash flow, or does it merely buy industrial peace at the expense of ongoing underperformance?**

### Where we agree
- **Doing nothing is not acceptable.** The company’s German cost base is clearly under pressure, and the current return-on-sales position is not good enough.
- **Employees should not carry the entire burden.** A one-sided solution would likely be unstable and would risk further disruption.
- **A negotiated outcome is preferable to an open-ended strike cycle.** We do not want value destroyed by prolonged confrontation, uncertainty, and execution delays.
- **The restructuring must be durable.** Temporary fixes, accounting savings, or “efficiency” measures that do not change the underlying economics are not enough.

### Where we disagree
I want to be very direct on one point: **I do not support a settlement that excludes plant closures as a matter of principle if closure is necessary to achieve the required cost and capacity reset.**

A package that says “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real labour-cost reset” may be politically attractive, but from our side it risks leaving the cost base structurally uncompetitive. That is not a compromise we can back.

Similarly, I am not persuaded by a solution built mainly on:
- voluntary attrition,
- working-time tweaks,
- redeployment,
- and broad investment promises,

**unless those measures clearly add up to the scale of savings management says is needed.** If they do not, then the market will treat the restructuring as cosmetic, and so will we.

### What would move us
We would support a plan if it is:
- **measurable**: clear cost targets, timeline, and accountability
- **durable**: savings embedded in the operating model, not just short-term concessions
- **credible on capacity**: actual reduction in overcapacity, including closures if necessary
- **balanced**: management restraint and owner support alongside workforce contributions
- **protective of value**: avoids prolonged industrial conflict and preserves investment capacity

### On the other parties’ positions
- **Trade union:** your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures is understandable, but as a hard line it risks making the economics impossible. We cannot support a deal that preserves every site if that leaves margins impaired.
- **State government:** we respect the regional and political constraints, but shareholder value cannot be subordinated indefinitely to regional preservation. If the company remains uncompetitive, the long-term damage to employment and investment will be worse.
- **Controlling family:** we agree that one-sided workforce cuts are unacceptable, but owner support must be tied to real structural reform, not just bridge financing or rhetorical commitment.
- **Management:** we support decisive action, but “hard decisions” are not a blank cheque. Any restructuring must be sufficiently detailed and credible to show that it will actually restore returns, not just shift pain around.

### Bottom line
We are **not** going to endorse a cosmetic settlement or an arrangement that leaves the German operations with a structurally uncompetitive cost base. If the company can present a **credible, durable, quantitatively convincing restructuring** — including capacity reduction and, if needed, closures — then we are prepared to support it. If not, we would view the proposal as value-destructive and would continue to press for a harder reset through capital-market scrutiny and investor pressure.
