To the Works Council: we are aligned on the essentials, and I support the clarity of your red lines.

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No unilateral destruction of the job-security framework**

That remains non-negotiable from the union side as well.

Where I want to sharpen the argument is this: we should not let management turn “competitiveness” into a blank cheque for intimidation. If they want change, they need to show a **credible alternative** that protects jobs and sites while still delivering real savings. Otherwise, they are not restructuring; they are transferring risk onto the workforce.

### To management
You are still framing this as if the only serious option is to keep closures and forced layoffs on the table. I reject that logic.

If your plan requires:
- closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the permanent weakening of collective protections,

then it is not yet a plan we can negotiate on. It is a threat.

You say savings must be durable. I agree. But durability does **not** automatically mean destruction of sites or forced cuts. Durable savings can also come from:
- working-time reductions,
- shifts and capacity adjustments,
- natural attrition,
- retirement pathways,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification for future products,
- and a negotiated, time-limited employee contribution.

What we will **not** accept is a deal where employees give up wages, security, and guarantees while management keeps all strategic freedom and ownership avoids its share. That is one-sided and unacceptable.

If you want the union to help stabilize the company, then we need:
1. a **binding job guarantee**,
2. **no closures**,
3. **no compulsory redundancies**,
4. a **fair, limited, time-bound contribution** from labor,
5. and **binding investment/product commitments** for German sites.

Without that, there is no basis for labour peace.

### To the family owners
You speak of long-term value, and that is precisely why I urge you not to back a closure-first approach. A company’s long-term value is not protected by breaking trust with its workforce and undermining the collective bargaining architecture that has sustained this firm for decades.

If you want a durable turnaround, then support a settlement in which:
- the workforce contributes fairly,
- management commits to real industrial planning,
- and the owners also accept responsibility for securing future work.

Long-term owners should prefer a solution that preserves the company’s social fabric, not one that burns it down in the name of short-term correction.

### To the regional state government
Your blocking rights matter, and they should be used responsibly. On this issue, that means not allowing plant closures or forced redundancies to become the default outcome.

You are right that the company needs competitiveness. But competitiveness achieved by hollowing out regional employment and breaking the wage framework is not sustainable. It will trigger conflict, weaken trust, and create more risk for the company and the region.

The state should support a negotiated alternative that:
- preserves German sites,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- includes fair employee contributions,
- and locks in future investment.

### To the investors
I hear the demand for credibility and quantified savings. That is fair. But let’s be equally precise: credibility is not the same as maximal pressure on labor.

If you want equivalent savings without closures, then you need to recognize that a negotiated package can still be economically serious. What matters is whether the measures are:
- durable,
- enforceable,
- and balanced across stakeholders.

A settlement that gets cost down through working-time flexibility, labor-cost contributions, redeployment, and product commitments is not “cosmetic” if it actually reduces fixed costs and preserves production capacity. What would be cosmetic is a closure announcement that triggers conflict, damages output, and destroys trust.

### Our union position, plainly
We remain prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package**, but only under conditions that protect the workforce from being the sole shock absorber.

That package can include:
- temporary wage or bonus moderation,
- working-time adjustments,
- flexibility instruments,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a limited employee financial contribution,

**if and only if** these are matched by:
- **no closures**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **binding site and job security**,
- and **serious commitments from management and owners**.

If management refuses that framework, then the conflict will escalate. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce will not be expected to absorb the entire cost of a restructuring they did not cause.

### Bottom line
We are not rejecting restructuring. We are rejecting a restructuring that is built on:
- forced layoffs,
- plant closures,
- and one-sided employee sacrifice.

Bring us a fair, durable, and balanced package, and we can negotiate seriously. Otherwise, we will defend the workforce with all available means.
