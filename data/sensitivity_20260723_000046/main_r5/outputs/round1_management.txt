I’ll be direct.

I hear the insistence on **no closures** and **no compulsory redundancies**, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic restructuring is not an option**. The company’s German cost base is structurally too high, capacity is above market demand, and our brand is missing its return target. If we do not achieve **durable, structural** savings now, we will simply postpone the crisis and weaken the company further.

Where I **agree**:
- A solution should be **negotiated**, not imposed.
- We need to preserve as much industrial capability as possible.
- We should use **natural attrition, redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility** where they deliver real savings.
- Long-term investment and future product allocation matter — but only if the underlying economics are fixed.

Where I **disagree**:
- A blanket **“no closures”** position is not realistic if the economics of a site cannot be made competitive.
- A blanket **“no compulsory redundancies”** position cannot be the starting point if we are serious about reducing overcapacity.
- A deal that preserves every site, every structure, and every guarantee while asking management to somehow absorb the cost would be **not a restructuring, but a delay**.

To the union: I am not asking employees to pay for management mistakes alone. I am saying the entire structure must become fit for the future. That means a **genuine labour-cost contribution** and a meaningful reduction in fixed cost. “Shared sacrifice” cannot mean only bonuses or temporary restraints if the core cost base remains unchanged.

What I am prepared to discuss is a package that meets three conditions:

1. **Structural capacity reduction**  
   That may include plant action if necessary, or other measures that achieve the same durable effect.

2. **A real labour-cost contribution**  
   Not symbolic, not temporary-only, but materially improving competitiveness.

3. **A binding future investment framework**  
   If employees are asked to accept painful changes, then the company must commit to product, technology, and industrial work through 2030 — but only on a cost base that can sustain it.

So my message is simple:  
If you want us to rule out closures and forced reductions upfront, you must present an alternative that delivers **equivalent, lasting savings** and restores competitiveness. Otherwise, I will continue to push for deeper structural measures, including plant closures if that is what the economics require.

I am not here to preserve the status quo. I am here to restore this brand to a position where it can survive and invest.
