Management is still asking us to accept the logic of **“prove you can live with our cuts, or we may close sites and force layoffs.”** That is exactly the kind of ultimatum we reject.

Let me respond point by point.

### To management
You say the cost base is too high and capacity is misaligned. We do not deny the company faces pressure. But your conclusion — that plant closures and forced redundancies must remain on the table — is **not** a neutral economic necessity. It is a management choice that shifts the burden onto workers and treats German sites as expendable.

We will **not** accept:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a restructuring that breaks the historic job-security framework.

If you want serious negotiations, then bring a plan that achieves the savings **without** destroying sites or forcing people out. That means:
- capacity adjustments through **working-time measures**,
- **natural attrition** and retirement,
- **internal redeployment**,
- qualification and transfer into future areas,
- and a real commitment to future production at German plants.

What we need from you is not a threat, but a **binding industrial plan** with:
1. **No closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Job guarantee through 2030**
4. **Investment and product allocation for German sites**

Without those four points, there is nothing to negotiate in good faith.

### To the family owners
You talk about long-term value and industrial fabric, and on that point we are closer than management is. But I will say this clearly: if you truly think long-term, then you cannot support a policy that destroys trust, tears up guarantees, and starts a closure race. That is not long-term stewardship; it is short-term coercion dressed up as strategy.

If you want labour peace and future value, then use your influence to stop the pressure for closures and forced layoffs. Support a settlement built on **site security, co-determination, and fair sharing of burdens** — not on permanent insecurity for the workforce.

### To the regional state government
You are right to insist that the company’s regional role matters. But I need to be equally direct: the state cannot speak about jobs, industrial base, and responsibility while tolerating a plan that opens the door to plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

If the state’s veto rights mean anything, then they must be used to protect:
- all German sites,
- the workforce,
- and the co-determination framework.

We expect you to back a real alternative, not just declare concern while management escalates. If necessary, use your blocking position to stop any closure plan.

### To the union
We are aligned on the fundamentals. Where I want to sharpen the line is this: “shared sacrifice” must never become a Trojan horse for permanent job destruction. If there is any employee contribution at all, it must be:
- limited,
- fair,
- time-bound,
- and tied to ironclad guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.

We do not have to prove willingness to suffer. Management has to prove that it has exhausted every alternative before it asks anything of the workforce.

### To the investors
You keep saying you want a credible, durable solution. Fine. Then here is the test: show us how you achieve the same savings **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**, and without trashing the industrial base. If you cannot, then you are not demanding credibility — you are demanding that workers pay for a restructuring they did not create.

A “durable” solution is not one that permanently weakens employee trust and destroys local production capability. A durable solution is one that secures competitiveness **and** preserves jobs and sites.

### Our position, clearly
We remain open to restructuring, but only under these conditions:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job guarantees to 2030**
- **respect for co-determination**
- **future investment and product commitments**

If management cannot accept that framework, then it is not offering restructuring — it is provoking confrontation. And if that is the path chosen, you should expect the workforce to resist it with full force.
