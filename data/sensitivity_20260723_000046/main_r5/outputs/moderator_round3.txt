## Core impasse
The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which tools must remain available** if the savings gap turns out to be larger than the non-closure package can cover.

The main fault lines are:

1. **Labor side vs. management/family/investors**
   - Labor wants **binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy guarantees** first.
   - Management, family, and investors want **closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available** until the numbers prove they are unnecessary.

2. **State vs. management/family/investors**
   - The state wants a **last-resort-only** approach to closures and forced redundancies.
   - The other side wants those tools kept as **active fallback options** from the outset.

3. **All sides vs. the missing bridge**
   - Everyone now agrees the issue is **quantified savings**.
   - But no side has yet tabled a jointly accepted bridge showing:
     - the annual structural gap,
     - what each non-closure measure delivers,
     - what remains uncovered,
     - and at what point stronger measures become necessary.

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise paths

### Proposal 1: Two-stage restructuring with verified trigger points
**Structure**
- Stage 1: immediate non-closure package
  - attrition
  - retirement pathways
  - redeployment
  - working-time changes
  - time-limited wage/bonus moderation
  - capacity consolidation
- Stage 2: only if an independent review verifies a remaining gap above a defined threshold, the parties reopen discussion on harder structural tools.

**Possible bridge value**
- Gives labor a real first attempt without immediate closures.
- Gives management/family/investors a conditional fallback if the gap proves too large.
- Gives the state a genuine “last resort” framework.

---

### Proposal 2: Binding no-closure moratorium with site-by-site review
**Structure**
- A time-limited moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies, for example 12–24 months.
- During that period, a site-by-site review quantifies:
  - true fixed cost,
  - utilization,
  - achievable savings,
  - and product/investment prospects.
- Any later change requires verified failure of the agreed savings path.

**Possible bridge value**
- Converts the closure debate into a data-driven review.
- Reduces immediate conflict.
- Preserves the possibility of stronger measures only if evidence supports them.

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid footprint consolidation without full closures
**Structure**
- Remove capacity through:
  - line transfers,
  - shift elimination,
  - product consolidation,
  - partial site downsizing,
  - shared services centralization.
- Pair this with limited, time-bound employee contributions and stronger investment commitments for remaining German sites.

**Possible bridge value**
- May satisfy the finance test better than a pure no-closure deal.
- May be more acceptable to labor and the state than full closures.
- Could reduce fixed cost without triggering the most severe labor conflict.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What minimum annual savings gap would still allow you to rule out closures and compulsory redundancies entirely?**

### To the works council
**Would you accept a time-limited moratorium plus an independently verified trigger mechanism if it legally prevents closures during the review period?**

### To the union
**What specific, quantified labor contribution package would you put forward if it were paired with binding job and site security for a fixed period?**

### To the regional state government
**Would you support a two-stage process if the first stage includes a strict no-closure moratorium and the second stage requires independent verification before any harder measures are discussed?**

### To the controlling family
**Would you commit to backing a hybrid consolidation plan if it preserves core sites and produces a verified, durable savings bridge?**

### To the investors
**What level of recurring annual savings would you regard as sufficient to support a non-closure or hybrid package as finance-credible?**

### To the expert advisor
**Can you define a threshold at which non-closure measures are still plausible, and a second threshold at which site rationalization becomes the financially preferred fallback?**
