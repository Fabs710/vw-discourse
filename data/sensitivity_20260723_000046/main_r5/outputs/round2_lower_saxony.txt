I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic delay**. The cost gap is real, and pretending otherwise would be irresponsible. I also agree that any solution has to restore competitiveness and secure future investment.

But I do **not** accept the logic that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are simply the default tools and that the rest of us should adjust around them.

### To management
You are right that the current structure cannot continue unchanged. Where I disagree is with your insistence that closures and forced redundancies must remain broadly available as a first-line option.

That is not a neutral technical choice for us. In this state, plant closure is a **permanent regional loss**. It destroys jobs, supplier networks, skills, and political trust. Once gone, that capacity does not easily return. So if you want my support, you must show that you have **genuinely exhausted alternatives first**.

Those alternatives are not vague:
- natural attrition,
- retirement pathways,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions without closure,
- and a fair, time-limited employee contribution.

If those measures, taken seriously and quantified properly, still do not close the gap, then we can discuss the next step. But I will not start from a premise that treats closures as just another management instrument.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce should not be made the sole shock absorber. I also support your demand that co-determination remain real. On that, we are aligned.

However, I need to be equally clear: a purely categorical “no change, no compromise” position will not hold if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. Protecting every current structure at any price is not a solution either.

What I can support is a **socially responsible restructuring** with:
- no forced redundancies as the first resort,
- no closure as the first resort,
- binding site and job-security commitments where there is a credible industrial future,
- and a fair employee contribution matched by commitments from management and owners.

But that must be tied to real investment, product allocation, and a plan that actually secures work in Germany. If those elements are missing, then labor is being asked to give up security without receiving a durable future in return — and I will not back that.

### To the controlling family
I hear your focus on long-term value, and I share the concern that a permanently weakened company helps no one. But long-term value is not protected by making drastic cuts without regard to regional consequences. It is protected by a solution that keeps the company strong **and** keeps its industrial base intact where possible.

I will not support a course that treats the state’s governance role as an obstacle to be bypassed. The special statute and our board presence are there for a reason: to ensure that long-term corporate decisions do not ignore regional responsibility.

### To the investors
You are right to demand credibility and quantified savings. I agree that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. But credibility is not measured only by the severity of cuts; it is measured by whether the package is **deliverable, durable, and socially stable**.

A strike-heavy, closure-driven confrontation can also destroy value very quickly. If there is a negotiated path that achieves comparable savings without tearing up the company’s social fabric, that is the better economic outcome.

### My position
So my position is this:

1. **No blanket acceptance of closures.**
2. **No acceptance of forced redundancies as the starting point.**
3. **Yes to a serious, quantified restructuring that uses reversible and negotiated measures first.**
4. **Yes to a fair employee contribution if it is time-limited and tied to binding future commitments.**
5. **No outcome that guts employment in this state or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.**

If management can demonstrate, with numbers, that a non-closure package cannot achieve the required savings, then we will have a harder discussion. But until then, I will not endorse shutting plants or forcing job cuts in a company that still has alternatives worth exhausting.

If needed, I am prepared to use the state’s blocking rights to prevent a premature or excessive closure decision. I would prefer a negotiated solution. But it has to be a real restructuring with real protection for jobs, sites, and the company’s role as a regional anchor — not a one-sided correction imposed on the workforce and the region.
