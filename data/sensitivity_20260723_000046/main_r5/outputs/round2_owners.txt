I will respond plainly.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the **diagnosis**:
- the cost base is too high,
- capacity is misaligned,
- and a cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.

I also agree that a prolonged strike or an open-ended confrontation would damage the company, and by extension all shareholders, including the family holding. That is not a path I support.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the union and works council position that:
- **no closures** can be a categorical red line,
- **no compulsory redundancies** can be ruled out absolutely,
- and **job guarantees to 2030** can be demanded as a starting point regardless of economics.

That is not stewardship. That is trying to freeze the current structure while expecting owners and management to absorb the consequences. If the alternative measures do not produce the necessary durable savings, then those red lines are not credible.

I also do **not** accept the state government’s implication that its blocking rights can be used to prevent economically necessary correction indefinitely. Regional responsibility matters, but so does the survival and competitiveness of the company itself. Protecting every current site at any cost is not long-term regional policy; it is deferred decline.

### To management
You are correct that structural change is required. But I want to be equally clear: **closure pressure alone is not a strategy**. If you cannot quantify precisely:
1. the savings required,
2. the savings delivered by each measure,
3. the time profile,
4. and the operational risk,

then you will not have my support for drastic action.

I want a **credible, quantified plan** that shows:
- how much cost reduction is achieved,
- how durable it is,
- what mix of labor contribution, capacity reduction, and structural change is needed,
- and why the outcome restores return on sales.

If closures are truly necessary, prove it. If they are not necessary, stop using them as leverage to force a weak deal.

### To the works council and union
You keep asking for “no closures” and “no forced redundancies” without yet showing an alternative of sufficient scale. I am open to alternatives, but only if they are **material** and **durable**.

A package built on:
- attrition,
- retirement,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- and a limited employee contribution

can be part of the answer. But if it does not close the gap, then it is not enough.

What I would not support is a deal where:
- the workforce gives up guarantees,
- but the company still remains structurally uncompetitive,
- while management is deprived of the tools needed to correct the situation.

That would simply postpone the next crisis.

### To the regional state government
I respect your position, but I need to be direct: the state should not turn its governance role into a permanent veto against necessary restructuring. If you insist on preserving capacity regardless of economics, you will eventually weaken the whole enterprise, including the jobs you want to protect.

If you want my support, then support a package that:
- first exhausts reversible measures,
- uses closures only if truly unavoidable,
- but does not rule them out in advance.

That is the only responsible stance.

### My position
The family holding’s position is this:

1. **We support a real restructuring, not a cosmetic compromise.**
2. **We do not support blanket prohibitions on closures or compulsory redundancies.**
3. **We prefer negotiated, reversible, and socially stable measures first.**
4. **We will only support deeper structural action if it is clearly necessary and properly quantified.**
5. **We will not accept any outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control.**

So the question is not whether the company must change. It must. The question is whether the other parties are willing to present a solution that is economically credible enough to avoid the harshest measures.

If they can do that, I will listen. If they cannot, then I will not back a deal that preserves an uncompetitive structure and calls it peace.
