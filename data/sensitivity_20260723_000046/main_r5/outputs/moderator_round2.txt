### Core impasse

The central deadlock is **not** over whether the company needs restructuring. All sides now accept that some form of durable cost and capacity correction is required.

The impasse is over **the mechanism and limits of that restructuring**:

- **Works Council / Union / State** want a package that **rules out plant closures and compulsory redundancies up front** and preserves site security and co-determination.
- **Management / Family / Investors** want **closures and forced redundancies to remain available** if the non-closure package does not close the quantified cost gap.
- The **finance expert** has narrowed the dispute to a factual test: whether a non-closure package can deliver savings of sufficient **scale, durability, and enforceability**.

So the unresolved issue is: **Can the parties agree on a quantified, time-bound alternative package that is large enough to avoid closures, or are closures and forced redundancies still needed as fallback tools?**

---

### Facilitator proposals for compromise

#### Proposal 1: “Conditional no-closure pact” with review trigger
A possible bridge is a **time-limited no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitment** that applies only if the parties agree to:
- a quantified savings target,
- specific headcount and capacity reduction measures,
- binding employee contributions,
- and quarterly milestone reviews.

If the package misses agreed milestones, the parties would reopen the question of more structural measures.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives labor and the state a real period of site security, while giving management, family, and investors a performance-based route to more drastic action if the numbers do not materialize.

---

#### Proposal 2: “Hybrid restructuring” with capacity consolidation instead of full closure
A second path is to replace the binary choice of “open vs. close” with **partial consolidation**:
- product reassignment,
- line removal,
- shift reduction,
- transfer of selected volumes to stronger plants,
- and controlled workforce reduction through attrition, retirement, and redeployment.

This would require a **site-by-site map** identifying which plants are protected, which are reduced, and which receive future product commitments.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It can deliver permanent fixed-cost reduction without an immediate full closure decision, while still giving management and investors real structural savings.

---

#### Proposal 3: “Two-stage agreement” with independent verification
A third compromise is a **two-stage deal**:
1. Stage one: immediate labor and working-time measures, redeployment, qualification, and investment commitments.
2. Stage two: an independent advisor verifies after a fixed period whether the savings gap has truly been closed.

If the verified gap remains large, the parties enter a second round on further structural measures, including possible site rationalization.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It separates the urgent need for action from the politically sensitive question of closures, while anchoring the process in audited numbers.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact annual savings target must be met for you to remove closures and compulsory redundancies from the table, and what quantified measures would satisfy that threshold?**

#### To the works council
**Which specific, measurable workforce contributions are you prepared to put on the table for a fixed term if they are matched by binding job, site, and investment guarantees?**

#### To the industrial union
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure agreement if an independent review mechanism is built in and the company commits to quantified product and investment allocations for German sites?**

#### To the regional state government
**Are you prepared to define the conditions under which the state would allow restructuring to proceed, even if those conditions fall short of a full no-closure guarantee?**

#### To the controlling family
**What level of quantified improvement and governance assurance would be sufficient for you to back a non-closure solution instead of insisting that closures remain available?**

#### To the institutional investors
**Would you accept a non-closure package if it is independently verified to close the structural cost gap and improve return on sales within a defined time frame?**

#### To the independent restructuring expert
**What is the minimum combination of labor contribution and capacity reduction that would make a no-closure scenario finance-credible at the company’s current cost gap?**
