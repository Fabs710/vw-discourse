The trade union’s position is aligned with the core message from the works council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no shifting the full burden of restructuring onto employees**.

Let me be equally clear on what we can and cannot accept:

- **We will not accept a restructuring that destroys jobs to fix management’s mistakes.**
- **We will not accept open-ended insecurity for the workforce.**
- **We will not accept the collective wage framework being broken unilaterally.**

What we are prepared to discuss is a **serious shared-sacrifice package** that protects the industrial base and creates a durable path forward. That can include:

- temporary or negotiated **labour-cost contributions**,
- working-time adjustments,
- restraint on bonuses or other non-base pay elements,
- qualification and redeployment,
- capacity reductions that do **not** mean site closure,
- and a binding commitment that management, owners, and shareholders also contribute.

But that only works if the deal is fair. If employees are asked to give up wages, guarantees, and security while management keeps all strategic room and ownership avoids real pain, then there is no basis for agreement.

So my position is:

1. **No closures.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No one-sided employee sacrifice.**
4. **A negotiated settlement is possible only with binding job, site, and investment commitments.**

If management wants peace, it needs to put something real on the table: not slogans about competitiveness, but a credible industrial plan with shared burdens and future work for German sites. If not, the union will use all available means, including industrial action, to defend our members.
