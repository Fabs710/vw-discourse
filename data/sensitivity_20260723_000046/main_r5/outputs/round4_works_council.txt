## On the expert
The expert was helpful, but the interpretation is being stretched by management and investors.

What the expert actually said was:
- non-closure packages can close **moderate** gaps,
- capacity consolidation without full closure is often the best midpoint,
- closures become relevant when the gap is **large and persistent**.

That is **not** a green light for closure threats. It is a demand for a proper test of the gap and the alternatives.

So here is the test we support:
- full transparency on the gap,
- site-by-site savings potential,
- independent verification,
- and a real comparison of non-closure options against closure options.

What we do **not** accept is using the expert’s caution as an excuse to keep closures permanently available from day one.

## Our position on the so-called “hybrid” and “two-stage” ideas
We are not interested in labels. We are interested in substance.

- no plant shutdowns,
- no forced exits,
- no permanent dismantling of the job-security framework.

Likewise, a “two-stage” process is only acceptable if stage one is not a trapdoor into stage two. We will not agree to a process that grants concessions now while preserving the right to come back later and close plants if management still wants more.

If the company truly needs savings, then it should quantify what can be achieved through:
- working-time changes,
- natural attrition,
- retirement pathways,
- redeployment,
- training,
- limited and fair employee contributions,
- and internal capacity reductions.

If that is not enough, then management must explain why it failed to plan earlier — not ask workers to pay for it by losing plants.
