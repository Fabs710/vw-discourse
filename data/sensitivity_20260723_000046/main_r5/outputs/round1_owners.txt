I will be direct as well.

I agree with management on the core diagnosis: **this company needs a structural reset, not a cosmetic adjustment**. If the German cost base remains above peers, if capacity stays misaligned with demand, and if margins remain under pressure, then we will all be discussing a weaker company in a few years instead of a stronger one. That would be unacceptable for long-term owners.

I also agree that **shared sacrifice** must be part of the answer. It cannot be a one-sided story where only capital, only management, or only a political body pays lip service while the underlying economics remain untouched.

Where I disagree is on the notion that **plant closures or compulsory redundancies are the starting point**. They are not. They are the most destructive instruments, and once you cross that line, you risk lasting damage to the company’s industrial fabric, morale, reputation, and labour relations. For a family owner with a long horizon, that matters. A permanently weakened enterprise is not an acceptable “solution.”

To the works council and union: I understand the red lines, but I do not accept red lines that make the company uncompetitive by default. A blanket “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” stance is only credible if you are prepared to deliver **equivalent, durable savings** through other means. If that cannot be done, then you are not defending the company; you are defending the current structure at the expense of future value.

What I could support is a package built around these principles:

- **Durable structural savings**, not temporary postponement.
- **Maximum use of natural attrition, retirement, internal mobility, and qualification**.
- **Capacity adjustments and working-time flexibility** where they produce real and lasting cost improvement.
- **A meaningful employee contribution**, but only if it is balanced and tied to a broader industrial plan.
- **Binding investment and product commitments** for German sites, so the restructuring is tied to future work, not just cuts.

To the regional state government: I recognize your role and the importance of regional stability. But a blocking position cannot become a veto on necessary economic correction. If the company is forced to carry excess structure for political reasons, the long-term price will be paid by the same region through weaker investment and less secure employment later. Protecting every site at any cost is not stewardship; it is deferral.

So my position is this:

1. **No blind rush to closures**, but no taboo on structural change either.
2. **No permanent preservation of uncompetitive structures**.
3. **No forced outcome that permanently damages the enterprise or governance stability**.
4. **A negotiated solution is preferable only if it delivers real competitiveness gains and labour peace**.

I am not here to declare a compromise for the sake of peace. I am here to protect the long-term value of the core asset. If the alternative measures on offer do not achieve that, then they are not enough. If they do achieve it, then I would prefer them over a destructive closure battle.

So the test is simple:  
**Show me a credible, durable plan that restores margins and competitiveness without creating permanent instability.**  
If that exists, I am open. If not, I will not support an arrangement that merely delays the necessary restructuring.
