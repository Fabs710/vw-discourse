Below is a neutral facilitator summary of where the process is stuck, followed by compromise pathways and one pointed question for each stakeholder.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is not mainly about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **the sequencing and credibility of fallback options**.

Two positions are clashing:

1. **Labor / state / co-determination side**
   - Closure threats and compulsory redundancies should not be on the table from day one.
   - First, the company must prove the gap and fully test non-closure alternatives.
   - Plant closures are seen as leverage that distorts bargaining.

2. **Management / family / investors side**
   - A restructuring without closure/forced-redundancy fallback may not be credible if the gap is large.
   - Non-closure measures may be insufficient unless they deliver durable fixed-cost reduction.
   - Removing fallback tools in advance may undermine finance credibility.

So the core impasse is:
**Should closures and forced redundancies be excluded up front, or retained as conditional fallback tools pending a quantified test?**

A second, related impasse is:
**Who must prove what, and when?**
- Labor says management must first prove that non-closure is insufficient.
- Management says labor must first prove that non-closure is sufficient.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Quantified no-closure first, with an independently triggered fallback review
**Structure**
- Stage 1: A binding no-closure package for a fixed period, including:
  - attrition,
  - retirement pathways,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - temporary labor contribution,
  - partial consolidation where savings are measurable,
  - investment and product commitments.
- Stage 2: An independent review after implementation, using agreed metrics:
  - annual gap,
  - recurring savings,
  - fixed-cost reduction,
  - utilization,
  - residual shortfall.
- Only if the residual gap remains above a defined threshold does a second-stage discussion open.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- Addresses labor’s demand for a genuine test before closures.
- Addresses management/investor demand for a contingency if savings prove insufficient.
- Replaces open-ended closure threat with a rules-based trigger.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid consolidation instead of full closures
**Structure**
- No immediate plant closures.
- Real capacity reduction through:
  - shift cuts,
  - line removals,
  - product reassignment,
  - overhead centralization,
  - possible partial downsizing of selected sites.
- No compulsory redundancies in the first phase; use voluntary exits and redeployment first.
- Explicit review of whether hybrid measures close the gap.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- Preserves more of the industrial base and avoids full shutdowns.
- Delivers stronger cost reduction than symbolic labor concessions.
- Gives management and investors a material fixed-cost reduction without forcing an all-or-nothing closure decision.

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional safeguarded fallback, not open-ended leverage
**Structure**
- The parties agree that closures and compulsory redundancies are **not** the first resort.
- They remain legally/politically possible only if:
  - the company publishes a site-by-site bridge,
  - independent verification confirms the residual gap,
  - a defined non-closure package has been implemented,
  - and a supermajority or special governance step authorizes any fallback move.

**Why this may bridge sides**
- Gives management and capital a credible fallback.
- Gives labor and the state protection against premature or tactical closure threats.
- Converts “closures on the table” into a tightly governed last-resort mechanism.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**Question:**  
If an independent site-by-site model shows that non-closure measures close, for example, 80–90% of the gap but leave a smaller residual shortfall, would you accept a pre-agreed review mechanism that may open a second-stage discussion without predetermining closures?

### 2) Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What minimum package of recurring savings and capacity reduction would you consider sufficient to support a time-limited no-closure agreement without treating it as a temporary pause?

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Will you table a site-by-site savings bridge with assumptions, timing, and one-off costs, and agree to separate “verified residual gap” from “asserted structural gap” in the negotiations?

### 4) Regional State Government
**Question:**  
If the independent model shows that a hybrid consolidation package materially reduces fixed cost but does not fully eliminate the gap, what exact governance conditions would you require before considering any fallback measure?

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Would you support a staged framework in which closures remain legally possible only after independent verification confirms that a quantified no-closure package cannot close the gap?

### 6) Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum level of recurring annual savings and fixed-cost reduction would you require to regard a no-closure or hybrid package as finance-credible?

### 7) Independent Expert
**Question:**  
What threshold should be used to distinguish a “moderate” gap that a no-closure package can plausibly close from a “large” gap where hybrid consolidation becomes the more likely midpoint?

2. hybrid consolidation,

using the same assumptions for:
- annual gap,
- recurring savings,
- one-off costs,
- implementation risk,
