As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be equally clear: **we will not accept a restructuring that destroys regional jobs, weakens the company’s industrial base in the state, or sidelines our co-determination and veto rights**.

I do recognize the core economic problem management is pointing to. A cost base that is structurally too high cannot simply be ignored, and I am not interested in preserving inefficiency for its own sake. So I agree on one point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness solution, not another short-term postponement**.

But I must disagree sharply with the implication that plant closures or forced redundancies should be treated as the default answer. For a company of this strategic importance, that is not a neutral technical choice. It has **lasting regional, social, and political consequences**. Once a site is closed, that capability is usually gone for good. That is not a reversible restructuring move; it is a permanent wound to the regional economy.

To management: if you are serious about long-term competitiveness, then present a plan that proves you have exhausted the alternatives first. That means:
- capacity reduction through **working-time measures, reallocation, internal mobility, and retirement pathways**,
- a **negotiated employee contribution** that is fair and time-limited,
- management and ownership also taking responsibility, not only the workforce,
- and a **binding investment and product commitment** for German sites.

What I will not support is a “solution” that simply shifts the burden onto workers and the state while preserving strategic room for management and capital. That is neither fair nor sustainable.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that there must be **no blanket destruction of jobs** and that co-determination must remain real, not symbolic. I also think it is important that any labor contribution be tied to a credible industrial plan and be distributed fairly. But I would caution against setting the table in a way that makes any change impossible. If we want to protect the company as a regional anchor, we need a restructuring that is tough but socially responsible.

So my position is:

1. **No outcome that guts employment in the state.**
2. **No outcome that removes or sidesteps the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
3. **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it preserves sites, uses reversible measures first, and includes real investment commitments.**
4. **No to closures as the first or automatic answer.**

I am prepared to use my blocking rights if necessary. But I would prefer a deal that strengthens the company without sacrificing the region that has supported it for decades. If management wants support, it must come to the table with something better than closure pressure and labor-cost demands.
