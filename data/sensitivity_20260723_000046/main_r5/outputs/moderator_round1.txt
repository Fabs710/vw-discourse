**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- **All sides accept that the current cost base/capacity is not sustainable** and that the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad, though not identical, support for using **reversible or less disruptive measures first** where possible:
  - natural attrition / retirement,
  - redeployment / internal transfers,
  - qualification and retraining,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - some form of labor-cost contribution.
- There is also broad recognition that any solution should include **future investment, product allocation, and a credible long-term industrial plan**.
- Most parties agree that **negotiated settlement is preferable** to open-ended conflict, provided it is economically credible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures as an acceptable default and in some cases categorically.
  - Management says closures cannot be ruled out if needed for competitiveness.
  - Family holding and investors do not insist on closures, but will accept them if they are necessary for durable savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council and union reject them.
  - Management says they cannot be excluded if overcapacity must be reduced.
  - Other stakeholders prefer avoiding them but have not ruled them out absolutely.
- **Scope and burden of sacrifice:**  
  - Labor representatives and the state insist burdens must be **fairly shared** and not fall mainly on employees.
  - Management and investors want **material, durable savings** and view temporary concessions as insufficient.
  - The family wants structural savings but is wary of measures that permanently damage the enterprise.
- **Co-determination / political veto rights:**  
  - The state insists its governance role must be respected.
  - Management and investors caution against political blocking that prevents necessary economic adjustment.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus **how much job/site protection can realistically be preserved**.
- Whether the same financial outcome can be achieved through **non-closure alternatives** with sufficient durability.
- The division between:
  - **temporary concessions** vs. **permanent structural measures**,
  - **site guarantees** vs. **operational flexibility**,
  - **job security** vs. **competitiveness and margin restoration**.
- What level of **binding investment/product commitment** is possible if labor accepts concessions.
- What would count as an **“equivalent” alternative** to closures from a financial and operational standpoint.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**: specify required savings, timelines, and effects of each option.
- Ask each side to identify **which measures they can actually accept** and **which they cannot**, in ranked order.
- Test whether a package exists that is:
  1. **durably cost-saving**,  
  2. **operationally credible**,  
  3. **socially and politically workable**,  
  4. and **supported by governance stakeholders**.
- The next round should aim to compare **specific restructuring packages**, rather than repeating general red lines.
