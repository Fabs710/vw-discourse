### 1) What has been agreed
- The company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable restructuring**.
- A **cosmetic or short-term deal** is widely rejected.
- All sides now accept the need for a **quantified, scenario-based comparison** rather than argument by assertion.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should examine:
  - management/owner contribution,
  - employee contribution,
  - investment commitments,
  - capacity adjustment,
  - and the impact on margins/return on sales.
- There is support for **independent review/audit** of the restructuring options.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Employee-side actors and the state oppose closures as a first or default option; management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side actors seek to exclude them; management and capital-side actors say they cannot be ruled out in advance if alternatives do not close the gap.
- **Burden of proof:**  
  Management argues no-closure advocates must prove viability; others argue management must prove that closures are necessary.
- **Acceptable end state:**  
  Worker/state side prioritizes site and job security; management/investors prioritize restoring competitiveness even if footprint reduction is needed.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **No-closure vs. closure-led savings:**  
  The parties have not yet produced a decision-grade comparison of recurring savings, one-time costs, and execution risk.
- **Short-term cash cost vs. long-term cost relief:**  
  The upfront cost of restructuring measures versus their longer-term effect on margins and liquidity remains unresolved.
- **Employment protection vs. economic sufficiency:**  
  The extent to which job/security guarantees can coexist with the required permanent cost reduction is still unclear.
- **Regional/industrial base vs. corporate competitiveness:**  
  The implications of footprint reduction for regional employment and supplier networks have not been reconciled with return-on-sales targets.
- **Contribution balance:**  
  The precise mix of management, owner, and workforce contributions has not been defined.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to a **single audited scenario table** comparing:
  1. no-closure,
  2. minimal-closure,
  3. closure-led restructuring.
- For each, specify:
  - recurring savings,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - cash timing,
  - margin/ROS impact,
  - implementation risk,
  - and investment implications.
- The next round should focus on **plant-by-plant data and the savings bridge**, so the group can test whether a no-closure solution is economically sufficient or not.
