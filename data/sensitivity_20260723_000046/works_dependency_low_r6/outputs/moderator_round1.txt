**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs a **durable solution**, not a temporary patch.
- All sides recognize the need for:
  - **room for future investment**
  - **capacity alignment with market demand**
  - **some form of restructuring or adjustment**
  - **a negotiated outcome if possible**
- There is also shared openness to **non-closure alternatives** such as:
  - internal redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - working-time flexibility
  - capacity reductions
  - limited employee contributions
  - cost restraint in other areas

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and regional state strongly reject them as a default path.
  - Management says they must remain on the table.
  - Family holding and investors do not endorse closures as first choice but do not rule them out if needed.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee representatives and regional state reject them.
  - Management says it cannot rule them out absolutely.
  - Shareholders want flexibility if no equivalent savings can be achieved otherwise.

- **Scope of employee contribution**
  - Union/works council accept only negotiated, limited, proportionate burden-sharing.
  - Management and shareholders want a **durable and material** labour-cost reduction.
  - The political side wants contributions to be matched by owner/management responsibility.

- **Binding guarantees**
  - Employee side wants strong job and site guarantees, including through 2030.
  - Management and shareholders say overly rigid guarantees could block necessary restructuring.
  - Regional state and family holding are open to commitments, but not to freezing the structure.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are needed**, and what level of savings is sufficient.
- **Which measures are temporary vs. permanent**.
- **Whether the required cost reduction can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs**.
- **How burden-sharing will be distributed** among workers, management, owners, and sites.
- **What investment commitments are credible and enforceable** if employees accept concessions.
- **How to preserve future industrial capacity while reducing the current footprint**.
- **What level of certainty can be offered** without removing management’s ability to act if the situation worsens.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and options**:
  - required savings gap
  - possible savings by measure
  - impact on sites, jobs, and investment
- Ask each side to present:
  - **their minimum acceptable package**
  - **their non-negotiable red lines**
  - **what they are prepared to trade**
- Focus next round on **comparing specific restructuring scenarios**, including one with no closures and one with limited closures, so the parties can test whether equivalent economic outcomes are actually possible.
