I will be equally direct.

## To management
You keep saying “economic credibility” while still refusing to provide the **decision-grade evidence** that would justify irreversible damage to jobs and sites.

So far, what you have offered is a general story:
- cost pressure,
- weak demand,
- margin targets,
- and the claim that closures must remain possible.

That is not enough.

You have **not** shown, plant by plant, that:
1. every German plant is unviable,
2. compulsory redundancies are unavoidable,
3. and the same savings cannot be achieved through a no-closure package.

Until that proof exists, closures are not necessity. They are a choice. And we reject that choice.

## To the family holding and investors
You are trying to turn “flexibility” into permission to break the historic job-security framework. I reject that completely.

Yes, long-term competitiveness matters. But long-term value is not protected by destroying trust, hollowing out industrial capability, and forcing the workforce to pay for management’s failure.

If you want a serious outcome, then back:
- all German plants remaining open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- real management and owner contribution,
- and binding guarantees through 2030.

Anything less is not a solution; it is a transfer of risk onto workers.

## To the regional state
You are right to insist that closures and forced redundancies are not the first instruments. But I need to be blunt: political concern is not enough if it does not translate into hard resistance against a closure-driven package.

If management returns with a proposal that breaks sites and jobs, then I expect the state to use every lever available — not just urge “balance.”

That said, I agree with you on one point: burden sharing must be real. Workers cannot be the only side making sacrifices. Any contribution from the workforce must be matched by management restraint and owner responsibility.

## To the union
We are aligned on the essential line:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no one-sided crisis burden on workers.

Where I differ is that I do **not** accept any softening of that line before management has proven its case. We should not “pre-compromise” in order to look reasonable. They need to show why their preferred path is necessary.

A no-closure package is not a slogan. It must be tested properly. But it must be tested **before** closures are treated as legitimate.

## To the auditors and finance modelers
Your contribution is useful, but the question is being framed too narrowly.

You are comparing:
- no-closure,
- minimal-closure,
- closure-led restructuring.

That is fine as an analytical exercise. But the benchmark cannot be “what gets management closest to its preferred margin target.” The benchmark must include:
- preserving every German plant,
- avoiding forced layoffs,
- preserving co-determination,
- and maintaining the job-security framework.

If a scenario only works by destroying those protections, then it is not a valid success case. It may be a spreadsheet win and an industrial loss.

## On the actual decision
Let me state our position again, clearly and without ambiguity:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No dismantling of job-security guarantees**
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- capacity reductions through non-closure measures,
- and fair, limited workforce contributions,

but only within a framework that protects every German site and every job.

## What management still needs to answer
If you claim there is no alternative, then answer these questions:
- What exactly is the savings gap?
- What savings come from management cuts?
- What savings come from owner contribution?
- What savings come from work-time and internal flexibility?
- What is the plant-by-plant contribution under a no-closure plan?
- Why are those measures insufficient?

Until that is answered, closure talk is not analysis. It is pressure.

## Final word
We are not refusing restructuring.  
We are refusing a restructuring that sacrifices plants, jobs, and trust.

If management wants a durable solution, then it must stop using closures as leverage and prove that the company can be made competitive **without** violating the workforce’s core protections.

Until then, the works council will continue to resist — in the plants, in the supervisory board, and publicly.
