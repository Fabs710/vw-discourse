We agree with the central diagnosis: **the current cost base is too high, and a cosmetic deal would destroy value**. From a capital-markets perspective, that is the only starting point that matters.

### Where we agree
- Management is right that the company needs **durable** cost reduction and not a temporary truce.
- The family is right that this is about **long-term value**, not just this year’s optics.
- The regional state is right that a prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.
- The employee side is right that the company needs an industrial future, not just cuts for their own sake.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support turning “no closures” or “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute red lines **if** they prevent the company from restoring competitive margins.

That said, we also do **not** accept management’s position that closures are the default answer simply because they are available. The burden is on management to show, with numbers, that any chosen package actually closes the cost gap. If a no-closure alternative can deliver equivalent, permanent savings, that is preferable. If it cannot, then the company should not be boxed in.

### Our bottom line
We will support **the package that most credibly restores return on sales and competitive economics**. That means:

- **material and permanent cost reduction**
- **capacity aligned with demand**
- **implementation certainty**
- **no purely symbolic settlement**
- **a credible path back to target margins**

### To the works council and union
Your refusal of closures and forced redundancies is understandable, but from an investor standpoint it is only acceptable if you can show **equivalent savings**. “Protect every site” is not a financial strategy unless the economics still work.

If you want market support, bring a quantified package:
- how much cost comes out,
- from which levers,
- on what timeline,
- and how it improves margins.

### To management
You have to do better than “closures are on the table.” We need a **clear scenario comparison**:
1. no closures, real structural savings;
2. limited closures, if necessary;
3. deeper structural measures only if the first two fail.

Tell us:
- exact savings gap,
- site-by-site economics,
- permanent versus temporary savings,
- and the impact on return on sales.

If you cannot demonstrate that, the market will treat this as bluff, and the share price will reflect that.

### To the regional state and family
We support your insistence on a serious, negotiated solution, but not at the cost of preserving an uncompetitive structure. Political and governance constraints cannot become a shield for underperformance.

### Our position
We are **not** committed to closures as such. We are committed to **economic credibility**.

If the no-closure route truly delivers durable competitiveness, we support it. If it does not, then management must be able to take the necessary structural steps. What we will not support is a deal that leaves the company structurally impaired and simply postpones the reckoning.
