From an industrial restructuring audit perspective, the immediate issue is not whether restructuring is needed — that is already broadly accepted — but **whether management has demonstrated, with sufficient operational and financial rigor, that closures and compulsory redundancies are necessary rather than optional**.

## Expert assessment

### 1) What is factually clear
Based on the scenario provided, the company appears to face a **real structural gap**:
- high fixed cost base,
- weaker European demand,
- pressure from lower-cost competitors,
- EV transition capital demands,
- and underperformance against return-on-sales targets.

That is a credible restructuring trigger.

### 2) What is not yet proven
What is **not yet demonstrated** is the key claim underpinning closure pressure: that a **no-closure or minimal-closure package cannot close the gap permanently**.

To justify plant closure, management would normally need to show:
- plant-level contribution margins and fixed-cost absorption,
- the size of the overcapacity by product line and site,
- the permanent savings from closure versus non-closure measures,
- redeployment limits,
- the cost and timing of severance versus alternative workforce measures,
- and the impact on future investment capacity.

From the conversation, those data have **not yet been presented in a decision-grade form**.

### 3) On the specific question: are closures already justified?
**No, not on the evidence described so far.**

That does **not** mean closures are off the table. It means they are **not yet substantiated as necessary**. In restructuring terms, closure is usually the last-resort structural option after testing:
- capacity reduction,
- shift and working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transfer,
- wage moderation or temporary labour-cost concessions,
- management overhead reduction,
- procurement and non-labour savings,
- and phased footprint optimization.

If those instruments cannot mathematically bridge the savings gap, then closure may become justified. But that proof has not been shown.

### 4) Main audit finding
The stakeholder positions currently reflect a classic restructuring deadlock:
- management is asserting **necessity**,
- employee-side actors are asserting **avoidable harm**,
- and neither side has yet put a fully auditable scenario comparison on the table.

So the current state is best described as:

> **A structurally justified need for cost reduction, but an insufficiently evidenced case for German plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the required means.**

### 5) What would resolve the question
An independent comparison should be built on:
1. **No-closure scenario**  
   Savings from capacity reduction, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and negotiated contributions.

2. **Minimal-closure scenario**  
   Only the smallest footprint reduction necessary if the no-closure package fails.

3. **Financial bridge**  
   Delta versus required savings target, over a defined horizon.

4. **Implementation risk**  
   Probability-adjusted savings, not headline savings only.

5. **Investment linkage**  
   Whether a lower-cost settlement actually funds future product and platform investment.

### 6) Bottom line
My expert view is:

- **Restructuring is necessary.**
- **A cost reduction package is necessary.**
- **But plant closures and compulsory redundancies have not yet been proven necessary on the evidence provided.**

So the works council and state are correct to demand a **site-by-site, independently reviewable comparison** before closures are treated as required rather than discretionary.
