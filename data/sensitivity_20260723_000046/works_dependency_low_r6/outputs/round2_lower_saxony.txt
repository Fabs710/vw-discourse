I will be very clear.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one fundamental point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness fix, not a temporary patch**. The cost problem is real, and the German operations must become more efficient if the brand is to have a future.

I also agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to a drawn-out conflict that damages the company, the workforce, and the region.

### Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the way management keeps presenting plant closures and compulsory redundancies as if they were just neutral tools.

For us, they are not abstract instruments. They would mean:
- lasting damage to regional employment,
- erosion of supplier networks,
- political and social instability in the state,
- and a weakening of the company’s role as an industrial anchor.

That is not a direction we can support as a shareholder and public actor.

I also do **not** accept the idea that the only serious answer is to make employees bear the burden first. If workers are expected to contribute, then management, owners, and the company itself must also show restraint and commitment. Otherwise this becomes a one-sided adjustment.

### To management
You have made the case that the cost base is too high. Understood. But you have **not yet proven** that the only way to close the gap is through closures or forced redundancies.

So my question is direct:
- What is the exact savings gap?
- What savings come from non-wage measures?
- What savings come from management and owner contributions?
- What savings come from capacity reduction **without** plant closure?
- What investment and product commitments accompany the package?

If you want state support, bring a package that protects the industrial base, not one that weakens it by default.

I will also say this plainly: **if the restructuring proposal is built around closing German plants, you should expect resistance from us under the special statute and in the supervisory board**. We are not a passive investor, and we do not hold our blocking rights for decoration.

### To the works council
I agree with you that the company cannot be allowed to solve this crisis by sacrificing German plants and jobs. Your insistence on protecting sites is legitimate.

That said, I would caution against framing “every plant stays open” as a slogan without presenting a workable industrial path. If the company is to remain competitive, we need a package that is not just defensive. I would like to see a concrete alternative that includes:
- capacity reduction,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a fair, limited employee contribution,
all tied to binding investment commitments.

If such a package can preserve sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, I will support it. If not, we cannot simply pretend the problem does not exist.

### To the union
I share your rejection of a one-sided transfer of crisis costs onto employees. That is not a socially responsible solution.

But I also cannot support an approach that rules out structural change entirely. If there is a way to avoid closures while still achieving real, permanent savings, then that is the route we should pursue. If not, then the company needs to show me a credible alternative before anyone asks the state to endorse closures.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that the company must restore margins. But long-term value is not protected by destroying the regional foundation of the business. A settlement that blows up labour peace, strips co-determination of substance, or hollowes out key German sites may look efficient on paper and destructive in reality.

### My bottom line
The regional state is prepared to support a **serious restructuring**, but not a demolition of German sites.

We can consider:
- negotiated capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- limited employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- and binding future investment.

What we cannot accept is:
- plant closures as the default answer,
- compulsory redundancies as the first instrument,
- or a “restructuring” that simply shifts the cost of correction onto workers and the region.

So the next step must be concrete:
**Show us a no-closure or minimal-closure package with quantified savings and binding commitments.**
If that package is credible, we can talk. If it is not, then management should not expect political or shareholder backing for a destructive course.
