### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company has a **serious competitiveness and cost problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable, not temporary, solution** is needed.
- Most parties agree that **prolonged conflict would be harmful**.
- There is shared willingness to discuss a **negotiated package** rather than a purely imposed outcome.
- Several parties are open, in principle, to a mix of:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - capacity reductions,
  - redeployment,
  - training/qualification,
  - limited employee contributions,
  - management/owner restraint,
  - and future investment commitments.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and regional state reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors insist closures must remain possible if required by the economics.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side and regional state reject them.  
  - Capital-side actors do not accept ruling them out categorically.
- **Scope of burden-sharing:**  
  - Employee side says workers must not bear the adjustment alone and wants management/owners to contribute materially.  
  - Management and investors say those contributions may help but are not yet shown to be sufficient.
- **Starting point for negotiations:**  
  - Employee and public actors want a **no-closure or minimal-closure baseline**.  
  - Management and capital-side actors want a **scenario comparison** that includes closures as a legitimate option.
- **Co-determination / protection agreements:**  
  - Works council insists these must not be weakened.  
  - Management has not accepted that constraint as absolute.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **No-closure versus economic sufficiency:** whether the cost gap can be closed permanently without plant closures.
- **Job security versus restructuring depth:** how far employment guarantees can go while still achieving the required savings.
- **Employee contributions versus owner/management contributions:** what each side is prepared to contribute, and in what quantified amount.
- **Capacity reduction versus site preservation:** how much reduction is possible without shutting sites.
- **Short-term savings versus long-term industrial strategy:** whether the package is only a cost-cutting exercise or also includes investment, products, and future competitiveness.
- **Benchmark for “equivalent savings”:** no shared definition yet of equivalent to what, over what horizon, and measured against which target.
- **Implementation certainty:** no agreed mechanism yet for ensuring any non-closure package actually delivers the promised savings.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **quantified scenario comparison**.
- Ask management to present:
  - the exact savings gap,
  - a no-closure scenario,
  - a minimal-closure scenario,
  - and the impact of each on margins and return on sales.
- Ask employee representatives to specify:
  - what capacity, working-time, redeployment, and contribution measures they would accept,
  - and how these would produce permanent savings.
- Ask owners, investors, and the state to clarify:
  - what management/owner contributions,
  - investment commitments,
  - and governance constraints they are prepared to put behind a deal.
- Keep the next round focused on **numbers, timelines, and implementation mechanisms**, not repeated restatements of red lines.
