We share the same starting point as the works council: this company needs a viable industrial future, not a restructuring that simply exports the pain to the workforce.

But I need to be equally clear: **“every German plant stays open” is a political line I understand, but it is not enough on its own to close the economics unless management also accepts a real burden-sharing package and a credible industrial plan.** If the discussion stays at slogans, we will all fail.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company faces a serious competitiveness problem,
- a durable solution is needed,
- capacity must be aligned with market demand,
- and any adjustment must be negotiated, not dictated.

I also agree that **workers cannot be the only ones paying for the crisis**. If management and owners want wage restraint and flexibility, then they must bring binding commitments on investment, products, and site security.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s logic that closures and forced redundancies are simply the “hard truth” and that everyone else must adapt to that premise.

You have not demonstrated that you need to start with closures. You have stated that they are on the table. That is a negotiating position, not proof.

And I want to say this directly to the works council: if the company’s answer is a permanent threat of closures, then trust will keep collapsing and the workforce will not accept “flexibility” as a euphemism for insecurity.

### Our position remains firm
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided transfer of crisis costs onto employees**

That is not negotiable as a principle. If management wants movement, it must come through a **shared-sacrifice package** that is fair and durable.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We remain open to:
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction without closures,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- temporary or limited employee contributions,
- bonus restraint and other cost elements,
- and a binding investment and product roadmap.

But those measures only make sense if they are paired with:
- **site guarantees**,
- **job security**,
- and a clear commitment that employees are not simply being used to bridge a management failure.

### To management
If you want a serious answer from us, then stop talking in abstractions and put numbers on the table:
- What is the exact savings gap?
- What savings come from management cuts, owner contributions, and non-wage measures?
- What savings come from labour-cost moderation?
- What is the investment commitment in return?
- What does the no-closure alternative actually look like in operational terms?

If you cannot show that, then your closure threat is not a solution; it is pressure.

### To the family holding and institutional investors
You say you want margin discipline. Fine. Then show discipline where it belongs as well:
- not only on wages,
- but also on management costs,
- capital allocation,
- and the willingness to back a long-term industrial base in Germany.

If you insist on a settlement that preserves value, then support a package that preserves jobs and sites. Otherwise, you are asking the workforce to absorb all the downside while capital stays protected. That is not a fair bargain.

### To the regional state
We welcome your rejection of plant closures and forced redundancies. But that has to mean more than a political statement. If a package emerges that protects sites and jobs, we expect you to use your influence to secure it. If management pushes a destructive course, we expect active resistance, not neutrality.

### Bottom line
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing **socially one-sided restructuring**.

If management comes with:
- a transparent savings gap,
- a no-closure alternative,
- limited and fair employee contribution,
- binding investment,
- and site/job guarantees,

then there is room for a serious settlement.

If not, then the conflict will continue, and the workforce has the means to make that costly.
