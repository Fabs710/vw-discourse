Thank you. I want to be equally direct from the perspective of the regional state and long-term shareholder.

I **do** agree with management on one point: the company cannot simply drift and hope the cost problem disappears. The structural pressure is real, and a credible competitiveness strategy is necessary.

But I **do not** accept the implication that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only serious path forward. For us, that is not a neutral technical question. It has direct consequences for tens of thousands of families, supplier networks, and the economic stability of this region. A solution that permanently weakens the company’s German base is not a solution we can support.

### Where I agree
- The current cost base must be reduced.
- The company needs room to invest in future products and technology.
- A negotiated outcome is better than an open conflict that damages everyone.
- Capacity has to be aligned with market reality.

### Where I disagree
- **Plant closures in Germany are not a default option.** They would only be acceptable if there were no other credible way to secure the company’s future, and that bar is very high.
- **Forced redundancies are unacceptable as a primary instrument.** They would undermine social trust and trigger political and industrial consequences that would hurt the company further.
- **A one-sided labour-cost diktat is not a sustainable answer.** If employees are expected to contribute, then owners and management must also take responsibility.

### To management
If you want the state’s support, then bring a package that is economically serious **and** socially defensible. That means:
- verifiable savings, not just announcements,
- a clear investment commitment for German sites,
- a plan that protects core competencies and regional value creation,
- and a burden-sharing model that is fair.

If the plan is essentially “close sites, cut jobs, and call it transformation,” then you should expect resistance from us in the supervisory board and under the special statute. We do not hold a blocking minority for decoration.

### To the works council and union
Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable and legitimate. I share the core concern: we must not hollow out the company’s regional industrial base.

At the same time, I have to be honest: if we insist that **nothing** changes, we risk pushing the company into a worse crisis later, which would threaten even more jobs. So I would encourage you to stay open to a negotiated restructuring that:
- avoids closures,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- uses working-time and capacity instruments,
- includes internal redeployment and qualification,
- and asks employees for a fair but limited contribution.

That is the kind of compromise that can protect both employment and competitiveness.

### My bottom line
The regional state will support a **socially responsible restructuring**, not a demolition of German sites. I am prepared to back a settlement that preserves plants, preserves co-determination, and creates a durable path back to competitiveness.

But I am **not** prepared to endorse a course that makes closure and mass redundancies the price of management’s strategic reset. If that is the direction, we will oppose it.
