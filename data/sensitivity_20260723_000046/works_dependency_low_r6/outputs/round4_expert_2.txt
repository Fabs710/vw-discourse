Yes — within restructuring finance and scenario modeling, the next useful step is a **decision framework template** for the scenario table.

## 1) Core question to answer
For each restructuring path, does the company achieve:

- **sufficient permanent annual savings**
- **acceptable one-time cash cost**
- **manageable execution risk**
- **adequate liquidity runway**
- **acceptable impact on ROS / margin**

All scenarios should be judged on the **same baseline** and the **same timing horizon**.

---

## 2) Recommended scenario set
Use three cases:

1. **No-closure scenario**
   - no German plant closures
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - savings from work-time, redeployment, productivity, management cuts, wage/cost concessions

2. **Minimal-closure scenario**
   - one limited closure or partial footprint reduction
   - preserves most sites
   - combines structural capacity removal with labor measures

3. **Closure-led scenario**
   - more aggressive footprint reduction
   - highest structural savings
   - highest one-time cost and execution risk

---

## 3) Table structure
For each scenario, show:

### A. Operating bridge
- Current annual operating cost base
- Less: permanent labor savings
- Less: plant/fixed-cost savings
- Less: overhead / management savings
- Less: procurement / other SG&A savings
- Less: productivity / utilization gains
- Equals: post-restructuring run-rate cost base

### B. One-time costs
- Severance / termination cost
- Closure / decommissioning cost
- Relocation / transfer cost
- Training / redeployment cost
- Contractual / settlement cost
- Consulting / advisory / implementation cost
- Equals: total restructuring cash cost

### C. Cash timing
- Year 1 cash cost
- Year 2 cash cost
- Year 3 cash cost
- Savings ramp by year
- Net cumulative cash effect by year

### D. Margin impact
- Revenue baseline
- EBITDA / EBIT improvement
- Return on sales before and after
- Break-even volume shift, if relevant

### E. Risk assessment
- Labor-relations risk
- Supervisory board approval risk
- Implementation delay risk
- Demand / utilization risk
- Savings leakage risk

### F. Strategic / investment effects
- Capex freed up or required
- EV / future platform investment capacity
- Supplier / logistics effects
- Site concentration effects

---

## 4) Minimum decision-grade metrics
At a minimum, each scenario should report:

- **Annual recurring savings**
- **One-time restructuring cost**
- **Net present value of savings**
- **Payback period**
- **Cash breakeven year**
- **EBIT / ROS uplift**
- **Headcount impact**
- **Capacity removed**
- **Implementation risk score**

---

## 5) Sensitivity cases
Every scenario should be stress-tested against:

- volume down 10%
- volume flat
- volume up modestly
- wage inflation above base
- slower-than-planned headcount exit
- delayed site closure / implementation slippage
- lower-than-expected productivity gains

This matters because a scenario can look good on paper but fail under downside volume or delayed execution.

---

## 6) Simple evaluation rule
A useful restructuring finance test is:

**A scenario is credible only if its risk-adjusted NPV of savings exceeds its one-time cost by a clear margin, and liquidity remains adequate during the implementation period.**

If two scenarios are close on NPV, then the one with:
- lower execution risk,
- lower cash drain,
- and faster payback  
is generally stronger financially.

---

## 7) What I would ask management to disclose
To make the comparison real, management should provide:

- current plant-level fixed and variable cost
- utilization by site
- line / product profitability
- labor cost per unit
- overhead allocation methodology
- planned annual savings by measure
- one-time cost assumptions by measure
- timing of cash outflow and savings ramp
- capex implications of each scenario

Without this, the debate remains rhetorical.

---

## 8) What the employee-side package must quantify
If the no-closure case is being proposed seriously, it should quantify:

- hours reduction / flexible working-time effect
- redeployment capacity by site
- attrition assumptions
- training cost and timing
- management overhead cuts
- wage / allowance / bonus restraint
- owner or company-funded bridging support
- probability that savings are permanent

---

## 9) Practical output format
A good board-level appendix would look like this:

| Headcount impact | h1 | h2 | h3 |
