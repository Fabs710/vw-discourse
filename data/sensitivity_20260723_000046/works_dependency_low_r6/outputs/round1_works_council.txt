We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the clear starting point of the works council.

Management has broken trust by turning a historic job-security framework into a cost-cutting exercise at the expense of the workforce. We do not recognise a “restructuring” that shifts the burden onto employees while leaving the company’s long-term commitment to German sites vague or conditional.

Our priorities are clear:

- every German plant stays open
- no forced layoffs
- binding job and site guarantees through 2030
- no unilateral dismantling of co-determination or existing protection agreements
- a real industrial strategy for future products and investment, not just cuts

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that secure competitiveness without destroying jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, training, efficiency measures, and a fair employee contribution — but only if they are negotiated, proportionate, and backed by binding guarantees.

If management comes with closures, mass redundancies, or a fait accompli, we will resist it politically, industrially, and in the supervisory board. We have already shown that the workforce is prepared to stand together, and we will use every co-determination right available to defend jobs and locations.

So my message is simple: save the company, yes — but not by sacrificing its people and its German plants.
