We are aligned with the need for **durable restructuring**, but we are not aligned with any outcome that merely preserves the current cost base under a different label.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- A **cosmetic settlement** would destroy value.
- We need **quantified, scenario-based analysis**.
- Any credible deal must include:
  - management contribution,
  - owner contribution,
  - employee contribution,
  - and a clear link to future investment and margin recovery.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and union
We respect the refusal to accept a one-sided burden on workers. That is legitimate. But we do **not** support turning:
- “no plant closures,”
- “no compulsory redundancies,”
- and “binding guarantees through 2030”

into absolute preconditions.

Why? Because from a shareholder perspective, that can easily become a **recipe for structural underperformance** if the no-closure package does not fully close the gap. We are not interested in protecting every site as a principle if the result is a permanently impaired cost base and continued margin erosion.

At the same time, we also do **not** support closures as a default reflex. The audit input is right: management has **not yet proven** in decision-grade form that closures are necessary. So the burden is now on management to show, with data, that the non-closure path is insufficient.

### To management
We agree with your diagnosis, but not yet with your evidentiary standard.

You have not yet shown:
- the exact savings gap,
- plant-by-plant economics,
- recurring vs temporary savings,
- and why non-closure measures are insufficient.

That is not a minor detail. It is the whole case. If you want capital markets to support difficult restructuring, bring a **decision-grade bridge**, not just a narrative that closures must remain available.

### To the regional state and family holding
We agree that prolonged conflict and a cosmetic compromise are both bad outcomes. We also agree that burden-sharing should not fall only on labor.

But we do **not** support using governance or political constraints to lock in an uncompetitive footprint. If the audited scenarios show that a smaller footprint is required, then the company must be allowed to take that step. Long-term value cannot be protected by preserving every structure regardless of economics.

## Our position
We will support the package that **most credibly restores return on sales, margin discipline, and durable competitiveness**.

That means:
- material permanent cost reduction,
- capacity aligned to demand,
- implementation certainty,
- and future investment capacity.

We are open on instruments. We are **not** open to preserving the existing structure by default.

## What we want next
The next round must produce a proper comparison of:
1. **no-closure scenario**
2. **minimal-closure scenario**
3. **closure-led scenario**

For each, we need:
- recurring savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- cash timing,
- margin/ROS impact,
- implementation risk,
- and investment implications.

We also want:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- a clear management contribution,
- a clear owner contribution,
- and a quantified employee contribution.

## Bottom line
We are not on the side of closures for their own sake. We are on the side of **financially credible restructuring**.

If the no-closure route truly restores competitiveness, we will support it. If it does not, then the company must retain the ability to take the necessary structural steps, including closures where unavoidable.

That is our limit and our position.
