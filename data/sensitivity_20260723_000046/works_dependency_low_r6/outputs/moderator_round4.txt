Core impasse: the parties are no longer disagreeing about whether restructuring is needed; they are disagreeing about **what counts as proof** that closures are necessary, and whether **site preservation** can be the benchmark before a full audited scenario comparison exists.  
In practical terms, the deadlock is between:

- **Management / investors:** want flexibility, including closures, if needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Works council / union:** insist that closures and compulsory redundancies are not legitimate unless a no-closure route is proven insufficient.
- **State / family holding:** want credible restructuring but are not willing to pre-endorse irreversible site closures without decision-grade evidence.

## Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the gap

### 1) Time-limited “no-closure first” testing phase
**Proposal:** Agree to a defined short period for a fully audited no-closure package to be quantified and tested first, using the same assumptions as the closure scenarios.

**Features:**
- Plant-by-plant scenario table
- Independent audit of recurring savings, one-time costs, and risk-adjusted NPV
- Explicit deadline for results
- Management retains fallback options, but cannot invoke them before the test is complete

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Gives the works council and union the proof standard they demand
- Gives management and investors a disciplined process rather than an open-ended veto
- Gives the state and family holding a decision basis before endorsing irreversible steps

---

### 2) Conditional restructuring with protected fallback
**Proposal:** A staged package in which non-closure measures are implemented immediately, but a limited set of fallback measures is pre-negotiated and only activated if quantified milestones are missed.

**Features:**
- Immediate use of working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, management cuts
- Agreed trigger points tied to savings, cash, and utilization metrics
- Only if triggers fail: limited closures or deeper structural measures become eligible
- No compulsory redundancies as first-line measure; use attrition and redeployment first

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Protects workers from premature irreversible action
- Satisfies management and investors that the plan has enforcement mechanisms
- Allows the state and family holding to support a credible, conditional process

---

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with binding burden split
**Proposal:** Tie any workforce contribution to equal or visible contributions from management and owners, with the package expressly limited to measures that preserve all current German sites during the initial implementation period.

**Features:**
- Workforce: limited negotiated contribution in hours, flexibility, or pay components
- Management: overhead reduction, leadership cuts, bonus restraint
- Owners/investors: capital support, investment commitments, or bridge financing
- Protected commitments through a fixed period, with review based on audited outcomes

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**
- Addresses the “one-sided burden” objection
- Gives investors evidence of seriousness and commitment
- Preserves a path to competitiveness without immediate closure decisions

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact recurring savings gap remains after management cuts, owner contribution, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and qualification measures are fully quantified plant by plant?**

### To the works council
**Would you accept a time-limited audited no-closure test if it is paired with transparent trigger points that permit limited fallback measures only if the no-closure package fails to meet agreed savings targets?**

### To the union
**What specific employee-side measures are you prepared to quantify now, in writing, so that the no-closure scenario can be tested as a real industrial package rather than as a principle statement?**

### To the regional state
**Will you commit to backing the audited comparison process only if it includes a clear deadline and a decision rule that prevents indefinite delay on either side?**

### To the controlling family holding
**What concrete owner contribution are you prepared to place on the table now—capital support, governance restraint, or investment commitment—to show that burden sharing is not being asked of labor alone?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum financial thresholds must the no-closure scenario meet for you to support it over a more aggressive closure-led plan?**

### To the independent auditor
**Can you define the exact data inputs and risk-adjustment method that will determine whether a no-closure package is genuinely sufficient, rather than only theoretically attractive?**

### To the finance modeler
**Will you produce side-by-side sensitivity cases showing when the no-closure path fails under downside volume or delayed implementation, so that fallback measures can be tied to objective triggers?**
