I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one core point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness fix**. The German cost base is too high, the market is tougher, and the current margin situation is not acceptable for a core asset of this size. A cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.

I also agree that **prolonged conflict is bad**. A strike spiral, reputational damage, and a drawn-out public fight would hurt the business, the workforce, and long-term owners alike.

And I agree that **a negotiated solution is preferable** if it can actually deliver the economics.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** agree with the attempt to turn “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute starting points. If those are hard red lines before the numbers are even tested, then we are not negotiating a restructuring; we are negotiating how to avoid it.

That is not acceptable if the company truly needs a structural reset.

I also do **not** accept the implication that management’s only realistic path must be closures. Closures may be necessary in the end, but they are not the first reflex. Before that, I expect a credible test of alternatives:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and productivity measures,
- a real and durable labour-cost contribution,
- and management restraint.

## To management
You are right that the company needs more than symbolism. But I want to challenge you on one thing: you still need to prove, in a disciplined and quantified way, that the savings gap cannot be closed by non-closure measures.

So I want a clear comparison of scenarios:
1. no closures, but real capacity and labour-cost adjustments;
2. minimal closures, if needed;
3. deeper structural measures, only if the first two fail.

Not slogans. Numbers.

If closures are to remain on the table, then they must be justified by:
- site-by-site economics,
- permanent savings,
- and a clear link to restoring return on sales.

What I will not back is a rushed “hard truth” narrative used to force a weak settlement.

## To the works council
I understand your distrust. But your position also has a weak point: saying “every German plant stays open” may be politically powerful, but by itself it does not answer the economics.

If you want to avoid closures, you need to show a package that achieves the same structural effect. That means real savings, not just postponement.

I am open to a no-closure path **if** it delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- actual capacity alignment,
- meaningful labour-cost contribution,
- and investment commitments that are credible.

What I will not support is a deal that preserves everything in place and calls that a solution.

## To the union
You are right that the burden cannot fall only on workers. I support that principle.

But I do not accept “no closures, no redundancies, no dismantling of the wage framework” as a complete answer unless you can show how the economics still work. The company cannot be asked to carry structural overcapacity out of sentiment.

If you want owner support, show us how the package restores competitiveness while preserving labour peace. I am willing to support limited, negotiated, fair contributions from employees — but only if they are part of a package that genuinely restores margin discipline.

## To the regional state
I take your political and regional concerns seriously. A settlement that destroys the industrial base would be value-destructive and socially damaging.

But I also need to be frank: a blocking posture that makes any hard restructuring impossible would not protect the region either. It would just delay the adjustment until the company is weaker.

So I support your call for:
- binding investment commitments,
- protection of core competencies,
- and negotiated capacity adjustment.

I do **not** support using the special statute as a shield against necessary change if the economics require it. That would put the company’s future at risk.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to back a settlement if it has all of the following:
- a durable improvement in margins and competitiveness,
- measurable and permanent cost reduction,
- capacity aligned to demand,
- a fair and negotiated burden-sharing package,
- and a credible investment path for future products.

I am open on the instrument mix. I am **not** open on preserving the current structure by default.

## My bottom line
The family holding will support a serious restructuring. But “serious” means economically real, not politically comfortable.

If there is a no-closure package that truly closes the gap, I will listen. If there is not, then management must have the freedom to take the necessary structural steps, including closures where unavoidable.

What I will not support is a settlement that leaves the company structurally weak while everyone declares victory. That would protect no one in the long run.
