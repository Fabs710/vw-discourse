Let me be clear: the moderator’s summary is useful, but it must not be used to blur the central issue.

**We do agree** that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem and that a durable solution is needed.  
**We do not agree** that this gives management a free hand to impose plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

### Our position remains unchanged
- **Every German plant must stay open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **No erosion of co-determination or historic protection agreements**
- **A real industrial plan, not a cuts-only program**

### To management
You keep saying closures “must remain on the table.” That is exactly the problem. Once you put closures on the table, trust is destroyed, and the burden of your strategic failure is shifted onto the workforce.

You have not shown that plant closures are the only way to secure competitiveness. You have asserted it. That is not the same thing.

If you want us to take your restructuring seriously, then bring:
- a transparent savings gap,
- a site-by-site alternative comparison,
- concrete investment commitments,
- and a package that proves the same economic result can be reached **without** shutting German plants or forcing people out.

Until then, closures are not a “tool.” They are a threat.

### To the family holding and institutional investors
You say you are not committed to closures as such, only to restoring margin discipline. Then do not back a strategy that demands the destruction of sites and jobs as the price of that discipline.

If equivalent savings are possible through:
- working-time changes,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- capacity reductions,
- wage moderation negotiated with the union,
- and management and shareholder contributions,

then that route must be taken first. That is the responsible order of priorities.

What we will not accept is this logic:  
“because the cost gap exists, therefore the workforce must pay with jobs and plants.”  
That is neither fair nor necessary unless proven otherwise.

### To the regional state
We appreciate your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But that has to translate into real board-level resistance, not just political concern. If management comes with a closure package, we expect you to use every lever available under the special statute and your shareholder position.

### On “equivalent savings”
This phrase keeps appearing, and it needs to be treated honestly.

Equivalent to what?
- Equivalent to plant closure plus mass redundancies?
- Equivalent to management’s target margin?
- Equivalent to short-term accounting relief?

If the benchmark is simply “whatever management prefers,” then the discussion is tilted from the start. We will not accept a moving target used to justify already-decided cuts.

A proper comparison must include:
1. **No-closure scenario**
2. **Limited capacity adjustment without forced layoffs**
3. **Management / owner contribution**
4. **Investment and product commitments**
5. **Job and site guarantees to 2030**

If management claims no-closure options are insufficient, then prove it with numbers, not pressure.

### Our red lines
These are not bargaining chips:
- no plant closures in Germany
- no compulsory redundancies
- no dismantling of co-determination
- no “restructuring” that is really just a transfer of risk to workers

If those red lines are crossed, then you should assume confrontation, not cooperation.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to negotiate a serious alternative package, but only if it starts from protecting sites and employment:
- capacity adjustment through existing instruments
- working-time flexibility
- redeployment across the group
- training and qualification
- fair and limited employee contribution
- commitments on future products and investment

That is a viable industrial strategy. Closing plants is not.

So my message is simple:  
**Bring us a plan that saves the company without sacrificing its German workforce. Otherwise, we will continue to resist it in the plants, in the public arena, and in the supervisory board.**
