## Core impasse

The central blockage is this:

- **Works Council / Union / State** want a **binding no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy, no-outsourcing framework** with temporary, reversible concessions only.
- **Management / Investors / Controlling Family** want **credible structural cost and capacity reduction** and are not willing to rule out closures or redundancies unless the alternative package is proven sufficient.

So the dispute is not mainly about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **whether hard instruments must remain available as fallback tools**, or whether the parties can agree to a **time-limited protected restructuring period** in exchange for temporary employee contributions.

A second, linked impasse is **proof sequencing**:
- labour side says: *show necessity first, then discuss concessions*;
- capital/management side says: *show a package that actually closes the gap, otherwise hard measures must remain available*.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Time-limited stability pact with explicit protections
A fixed-term pact could be considered, with:
- no plant closures during the pact period,
- no compulsory redundancies during the pact period,
- no outsourcing / relocation by the back door,
- preservation of co-determination,
- temporary, reversible employee contributions,
- independent verification of savings and capacity effects,
- automatic reversion at expiry.

**Purpose:** bridge trust and give management a protected window to implement restructuring through internal means first.

---

### 2) Sequenced restructuring: internal measures first, hard measures only under verified trigger conditions
A second option is a staged model:
- Phase 1: redeployment, qualification, working-time tools, voluntary exits, bonus moderation, internal consolidation.
- Phase 2: independent review of whether the remaining gap still exists.
- Phase 3: only if predefined, jointly verified thresholds are missed, the parties revisit the question of deeper structural measures.

**Purpose:** give management a credible path without immediately normalizing closures, while giving labour a structured safeguard against premature escalation.

---

### 3) Site-and-investment guarantee in exchange for bounded labour contributions
A third compromise is a package deal:
- binding site guarantees,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- defined employment protection for a fixed term,
- temporary labour contributions that are clearly limited and reversible,
- transparent milestones and monitoring.

**Purpose:** align the financial and industrial logic by trading near-term flexibility for concrete long-term commitments.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If management accepts a fixed-term no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy pact with automatic reversion and independent verification, **what specific temporary contribution package are you prepared to quantify now?**

### Industrial Union
If the parties draft a legally safe temporary contribution package, **what exact guardrails do you require so that it cannot later be used as a precedent for permanent cuts?**

### Regional State Government
If you want to prevent destructive restructuring, **what concrete leverage will you use to secure a binding compromise rather than merely urging both sides toward agreement?**

### Controlling Family Holding
If you say you are stewarding long-term value, **what minimum site, employment, and investment guarantees are you prepared to endorse before hard measures become legitimate again?**

### Institutional Investors
If you want a market-credible restructuring, **what evidence would convince you that a non-closure package truly closes the savings and capacity gap?**

### Group & Brand Management
If closures and redundancies remain your fallback, **what exact quantified gap remains after the best internal and labour-side measures, and by when can you present it?**

### Expert
If the parties want temporary concessions without precedent risk, **which two or three drafting clauses are absolutely non-negotiable to preserve co-determination and automatic reversion?**
