We are aligned on one essential point: **this cannot be a cosmetic deal**. If the outcome leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive, or simply defers the pain, then it will not be supported by capital markets.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current structure is too costly and capacity is misaligned.
- **The family holding is right** that prolonged conflict destroys value.
- **The state is right** that a purely unilateral diktat would be slow, risky, and value-destructive.
- **Labour is right** that hidden closures, outsourcing-by-stealth, and open-ended insecurity are unacceptable.

## Where we disagree
### On absolute no-closure / no-redundancy red lines
The works council and union are asking for **hard guarantees first** and structural adjustment second. From an investor perspective, that is not enough.

If the package does not deliver:
- durable margin improvement,
- verifiable capacity reduction,
- and a materially lower cost base,

then we will not support it — regardless of how much job protection language it contains.

That said, we also do **not** want closures or forced redundancies used as the default answer if a credible alternative can achieve the same economics. The test is not ideology; it is whether the numbers work.

### On management’s framing
Management is right to insist on structural savings, but it still has to **prove necessity** measure by measure. “We need flexibility” is not a sufficient business case for the most disruptive instruments. If closures or compulsory redundancies are truly needed, show the comparative economics, the implementation risk, and why alternatives fall short.

## What would be acceptable to us
We could support a negotiated package if it includes all of the following:

1. **Material, quantified labour-cost relief**
   - temporary bonus restraint
   - working-time flexibility
   - limited pay moderation or deferred components
   - but with a clear end point and no permanent erosion disguised as a crisis measure

2. **Real capacity alignment**
   - not just reshuffling output
   - not accounting tricks
   - an actual reduction in structural overcapacity

3. **No immediate compulsory redundancies**
   - if and only if a credible, jointly verified alternative produces equivalent savings

4. **Independent verification and hard milestones**
   - clear metrics on margins, costs, and capacity
   - a review point
   - and a re-opener if targets are missed

5. **Investment tied to restructuring**
   - not blank-cheque promises
   - but real commitments linked to improved returns and future products

## What we will not support
- a “peaceful” agreement that preserves all structures and leaves margins impaired
- permanent job guarantees that freeze an uncompetitive cost base
- symbolic employee contributions that do not move the economics
- open-ended conflict that burns cash and confidence
- a settlement where investors fund delay while operational problems remain unresolved

## To each stakeholder
### To labour
If you want to avoid closures and forced layoffs, then the alternative must be **material and durable enough** to close the competitiveness gap. If it is not, then you are asking shareholders to accept lower future returns for the sake of preserving an unsustainable structure. We will not do that.

### To management
Bring a real comparison:
- what savings come from the current plan,
- what savings come from alternatives,
- what the execution risk is,
- and what each path does to margins over the next 2–3 years.

Without that, you are asking capital to support assertions, not strategy.

### To the state and family owners
We support a compromise only if it produces **genuine economic improvement**, not just social calm. Industrial peace matters, but it is not a substitute for competitiveness.

## Bottom line
We can support a negotiated restructuring that avoids immediate closures and forced redundancies **only if** it delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- measurable capacity cuts,
- credible margin recovery,
- and implementation certainty.

If those conditions are not met, we will not back a deal that merely postpones the problem.
