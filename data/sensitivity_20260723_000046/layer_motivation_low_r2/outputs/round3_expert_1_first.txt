Yes — the key legal-industrial-relations question is whether a package of **“temporary” concessions** can be drafted so that it **does not erode co-determination, weaken binding site guarantees, or create an unintended precedent**.

## Short answer

**Yes, partly — but only with careful drafting and with limits.**  
In German industrial relations, “temporary” concessions can be made legally effective **without creating a permanent precedent** if they are:

- **clearly time-limited**
- **expressly non-prejudicial**
- **tied to defined economic triggers or expiry dates**
- **documented as an exceptional crisis measure**
- **subject to reversion to the prior baseline**
- **without waiving statutory co-determination rights**
- and **without language that can be read as an implicit acceptance of structural weakening**

But there is an important limit:

> You can temporarily suspend or modulate certain contractual or collective protections; you cannot, by private agreement, contract out of mandatory co-determination rights or permanently neutralize them.

So the drafting must distinguish between:
1. **temporary concessions in wages, working time, staffing processes, or capacity tools**, and
2. **non-waivable statutory rights and binding site protections**.

## 1) What can be made temporary

From an industrial-relations perspective, the following are commonly capable of temporary design:

- bonus moderation / temporary pay freezes
- temporary reduction of certain allowances
- working-time flexibility, including temporary short-time-style arrangements if lawful and agreed
- temporary redeployment obligations
- voluntary exit programs
- temporary suspension of agreed increases
- temporary capacity adjustments
- temporary consultation or review mechanisms
- temporary commitment not to pursue certain structural measures

These can be framed as:
- **“for the duration of the crisis measure”**
- **“until date X”**
- **“until trigger Y is met”**
- **“subject to automatic reversion”**

## 2) What cannot be effectively “made temporary” in a way that erodes core protections

### A. Co-determination rights
Statutory co-determination rights under the **BetrVG** and, where relevant, the **Mitbestimmungsgesetz** are not something parties can simply waive away for convenience.

In practice:
- the works council’s information, consultation, and participation rights remain intact
- the supervisory-board employee side’s rights remain intact
- temporary concessions must not be drafted as if they authorize management to bypass future participation

So you should avoid wording like:
- “the works council agrees not to assert its rights”
- “employees waive co-determination for the duration”
- “the parties accept that management may implement measures unilaterally”

Those formulations are legally and industrial-relationally dangerous.

### B. Binding site guarantees
A site guarantee can be made **time-limited**, but if it is to retain value it must be drafted with precision.

A weak guarantee says:
- “the company intends to preserve the site”

A stronger guarantee says:
- “no closure, no transfer of core production, no material downsizing, no outsourcing of defined functions until date X, except in defined extraordinary circumstances and only after specified procedures.”

If you want to avoid erosion, the guarantee must specify:
- covered sites
- covered functions
- prohibited acts
- duration
- exceptions
- consequences of breach
- dispute-resolution mechanism

Without that, “temporary” often becomes a political promise rather than a binding industrial instrument.

## 3) How to avoid unintended precedent

This is the central drafting issue.

A temporary concession becomes precedent when the text or conduct implies:
- a general acceptance that the concession level is the new normal
- an admission that the employer may later demand the same again
- a concession for one site that can be exported to others
- a concession framed as a “first step” rather than an exceptional crisis measure

### Drafting techniques to avoid precedent
Use language such as:

- **“without prejudice”**
- **“exceptional and time-limited”**
- **“no admission of a permanent change in the contractual baseline”**
- **“no waiver of rights for future negotiations”**
- **“no precedent for other sites, periods, or agreements”**
- **“automatic expiry and reversion”**
- **“subject to express renewal only by written agreement”**

Also include a clause stating that:
- the measure is adopted **solely to bridge an exceptional economic situation**
- it does **not** modify the parties’ baseline positions for future bargaining
- it cannot be cited as evidence of an accepted permanent restructuring model

That said, a disclaimer is not magic. If the actual conduct shows repeated renewals or partial permanent retention, a de facto precedent can still arise industrially even if the text says otherwise.

## 4) The legal danger of “temporary” concessions: mission creep

The main practical risk is not that the first agreement is invalid. The risk is that the temporary arrangement becomes the **new bargaining floor** through:
- repeated extensions
- ambiguous reopener language
- vague economic triggers
- lack of an exit path
- one-sided management discretion
- pressure to re-accept the same sacrifice later

To reduce this risk:
- make the end date fixed
- define objective triggers narrowly
- require joint verification, not unilateral assessment
- specify the automatic restoration of prior conditions
- require fresh bargaining for any extension
- avoid open-ended “as long as necessary” language

## 5) Co-determination-safe drafting architecture

A legally and industrial-relationally safer structure is:

### Layer 1: Framework agreement
States:
- exceptional crisis basis
- objectives
- duration
- no-waiver / no-prejudice clause
- governance structure
- dispute mechanism

### Layer 2: Site-specific agreements
Define:
- site guarantees
- staffing rules
- capacity adjustments
- investment commitments
- implementation timetable

### Layer 3: Temporary concessions package
Defines:
- exact concessions
- exact offsets
- end date
- review point
- automatic reversion
- breach consequences

### Layer 4: Co-determination reservation
Explicitly states:
- nothing in the agreement limits statutory participation rights
- all implementation steps remain subject to applicable works-council procedures
- the agreement does not authorize bypassing future consultation/consent where required

That is the most defensible structure if the goal is to preserve legitimacy and avoid legal ambiguity.

## 6) A word on collective bargaining and peace obligations

If the union is party to the agreement, one must separate:
- **collective-bargaining content** handled in a tariff agreement or tariff-like package, from
- **company-level operational implementation** handled through works agreements and operational commitments

This matters because:
- some matters are tariff-matter dominated
- some are works-council matters
- some are mixed and require careful allocation

If the drafting crosses those lines badly, you create:
- enforceability problems
- competence disputes
- and a risk that the agreement is attacked as ultra vires or internally inconsistent

## 7) The bottom line

### Can temporary concessions be drafted safely?
**Yes, if they are genuinely temporary, tightly defined, and expressly non-prejudicial.**

### Can they preserve co-determination?
**Yes, but only if they expressly reserve statutory rights and do not purport to waive them.**

### Can they protect a site guarantee?
**Yes, if the guarantee is concrete, time-bound, and backed by clear prohibitions and remedies.**

### Can they avoid creating precedent?
**Mostly yes, but only if the agreement avoids ambiguous language and avoids repeated extensions that create de facto precedent.**

## Practical conclusion

If the parties want a credible compromise, the drafting should do four things:

1. **Name the arrangement as exceptional and temporary**
2. **State that all statutory co-determination rights remain untouched**
3. **Define a hard expiry and automatic reversion**
4. **Expressly say there is no precedent or baseline shift**
