We are in the same place on the core red lines: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

I want to be equally clear about what that means in practice: if management is trying to force a restructuring by making employees carry all the pain while owners, capital and management remain protected, then we will not accept it. That is not a “solution”; it is a transfer of the crisis onto the workforce.

Where I agree with the works council:
- **Capacity has to be adjusted** if the market is really smaller.
- **Efficiency measures and working-time solutions** are legitimate discussion points.
- **Internal redeployment, qualification, and temporary flexibility** are better than layoffs.
- A **shared-sacrifice package** can be part of a deal, but only if it is genuinely shared.

Where I disagree or need to sharpen the point:
- Saying “we will discuss alternatives” is not enough unless management also accepts that **closures and forced layoffs are off the table**.
- A “labor contribution” cannot mean open-ended wage cuts or the erosion of the collective agreement.
- Any reduction in capacity must be handled in a way that **preserves sites and gives workers a real future**, not a slow-motion shutdown.

So our position is this:

1. **No closures, no compulsory redundancies.**
2. **Any restructuring must be negotiated, not imposed.**
3. **Employees can contribute**, but only fairly and temporarily:
   - possible waiver of bonuses,
   - temporary adjustments to working time,
   - qualification and transfer measures,
   - possibly a crisis fund or similar instrument.
4. In return, we need:
   - **binding site guarantees,**
   - **job security,**
   - **clear investment commitments,**
   - and **a time-limited, reversible framework**, not a permanent lowering of standards.

If management wants lasting cost relief, then they must also put something substantial on the table: investment, product commitment, and a credible strategy that does not turn German workers into the sole shock absorber.

And to the other stakeholders: if there is talk of “necessary cuts,” we need to be honest about who pays. If the answer is “the workforce alone,” then there will be conflict. If the answer is “all sides contribute, and jobs/sites are secured,” then we can talk.

That is the only basis on which we can move.
