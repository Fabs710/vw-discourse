## Core impasse

The central impasse is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All stakeholders now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **what counts as an acceptable restructuring instrument** and **who bears the primary adjustment burden**:

- **Management, family holding, and investors** require a package that produces **durable, material cost reduction and capacity alignment**.
- **Works council, union, and state** insist that this must **not** be achieved through plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or an open-ended weakening of job and site protections.
- The disagreement is therefore between:
  1. **structural flexibility and economic credibility**, and  
  2. **binding employment and site protection**.

A second, related impasse is that the sides differ on sequencing:
- Labour wants **guarantees first, contributions second**.
- Management/capital want **savings first, protections only where economics permit**.

## Facilitation proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited stability pact with quantified labour contribution
A negotiated package could include:
- a **temporary labour contribution** that is quantified, bounded, and reversible;
- **working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and bonus moderation** as the main adjustment tools;
- **no compulsory redundancies during the pact period**;
- **site protection for a defined period**;
- and a **jointly verified savings target**.

This would try to bridge:
- labour’s demand for protection and reversibility, and
- management/capital’s demand for measurable structural relief.

### Proposal 2: “No closures unless jointly verified necessity” mechanism
A second option is a staged process:
- an initial phase focused on **internal restructuring only**;
- a joint review using **objective economic criteria** and **independent verification**;
- closures or deeper measures only if it is **demonstrated that alternatives do not close the gap**.

This could bridge:
- labour’s demand to prevent premature closure logic, and
- management/investor insistence that fallback instruments remain available if needed.

### Proposal 3: Site guarantees tied to investment and capacity commitments
A third compromise could link:
- **binding site guarantees**,
- **investment commitments for future products/technology**,
- **measurable productivity and capacity adjustments**,
- and **temporary labour concessions**.

This would aim to align:
- workforce security,
- industrial modernization,
- and capital’s requirement for credible medium-term competitiveness.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management / group and brand management:
**What precise savings gap remains after all non-closure, non-compulsory-redundancy measures are applied, and what evidence shows that this gap cannot be closed through a jointly verified internal restructuring package?**

### To the works council:
**Which temporary and quantified labour measures, if paired with binding site and job protections through 2030, would you consider sufficient to enter a binding negotiation framework?**

### To the industrial union:
**What minimum package of reversible labour contributions could you credibly recommend to your members if it were guaranteed to prevent compulsory redundancies during the agreement period?**

### To the regional state government:
**Will you commit to using your governance leverage to block any plan that relies on closures or compulsory redundancies before an independent verification has shown that alternatives are insufficient?**

### To the controlling family holding:
**What specific conditions would you require before accepting a time-limited moratorium on closures, even if the package includes measurable labour-cost relief and capacity reduction?**

### To institutional investors:
**What exact combination of cost reduction, capacity alignment, and verification would be enough for you to support a deal that avoids immediate closures and forced redundancies?**
