We hear the red lines from labour clearly. But I need to be equally clear from management’s side:

**A restructuring that leaves the current cost and capacity structure intact is not a solution.**  
If we merely defer the problem with temporary measures, we will fail on competitiveness, fail on margins, and fail on the investment needed for the next product cycle through 2030.

I agree on one point: **a purely unilateral approach will not work**. We need a negotiated outcome. But negotiation is not the same as preserving the status quo.

What I accept:
- **No cosmetic deal.**
- **No short-term patch that avoids hard decisions while keeping the cost base uncompetitive.**
- **No open-ended conflict that destroys value for everyone.**
- **A negotiated package with real labour contribution, capacity adjustment, and durable cost reduction.**

What I do not accept:
- **A blanket rejection of plant closures as a principle.**
- **A veto on compulsory redundancies if there is no credible alternative to achieve sufficient structural savings.**
- **A guarantee that every site remains untouched regardless of market reality.**

Let me be very direct: the company is facing a structural problem, not a temporary dip. German factory costs are too high, European demand is shrinking, and we are under pressure from lower-cost competitors. If we do not reduce overcapacity and labour costs meaningfully, we will not have the resources to invest in future products and technology.

So if the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward something that is **equally structural**:
- permanent and significant capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- credible productivity improvements,
- and a real change in the cost base, not just delayed pain.

I am open to **alternatives to closures** if they deliver the same economic result. That could include:
- capacity consolidation,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted pay moderation,
- removal or redesign of legacy guarantees,
- and other measures that produce verifiable, lasting savings.

But I will not endorse a package that sounds cooperative and humane while leaving us in the same uncompetitive position two years from now.

To the works council and union: if you insist that **all sites remain open and there are no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances**, then you need to explain how we achieve a structurally lower cost base. If you cannot, then you are not protecting the company — you are protecting a cost structure that the market will simply punish.

To the regional state and owners: this is the moment to back a real turnaround, not a symbolic one. Investors and the market will not reward hesitation.

My bottom line is simple:  
**We need a durable restructuring with measurable, lasting savings.**  
If the workforce side is prepared to help deliver that, we can talk. If not, then we will have a much harder confrontation ahead.
