## Core impasse

The central blockage is a **sequencing and credibility dispute**:

- **Works council / union / state / family** want **binding protection first**:
  - no plant closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - strong site guarantees,
  - preservation of co-determination,
  - before any labour concessions.

- **Management / investors** want **material restructuring first**:
  - durable cost and capacity reduction,
  - with closures and redundancies remaining as fallback instruments if needed.

So the dispute is not simply “save jobs vs. save costs.”  
It is **whether the restructuring can be made credible without keeping closure and layoff threats alive**.

A second fault line is **how “temporary” concessions are made safe**:
- labour side wants them strictly reversible and non-prejudicial,
- capital/management wants enough flexibility that the package actually changes the economics.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Fixed-term stability pact with hard protections and hard metrics
A time-limited pact could be structured as follows:
- **No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period**,
- **temporary, quantified labour contributions**,
- **automatic reversion** at expiry,
- **joint monitoring of savings and capacity targets**,
- **re-opener only if predefined metrics are missed**.

This would aim to give the workforce real protection while giving management and investors measurable restructuring output.

---

### 2) Conditional no-closure moratorium linked to verified restructuring milestones
A second bridge could be:
- a **moratorium on closures** during the pact period,
- with **jointly verified milestones** on:
  - cost reduction,
  - productivity,
  - redeployment,
  - voluntary exits,
  - investment commitments,
  - capacity alignment.

If milestones are met, the moratorium continues or becomes permanent for the term.  
If milestones are not met, the parties re-enter structured talks before any hard measures are triggered.

This keeps closure as a contingency only after verified failure, rather than as an active bargaining weapon.

---

### 3) Site-and-investment guarantee in exchange for bounded labour contributions
A third option is a package exchange:
- labour accepts **temporary, reversible concessions**,
- management and owners provide **binding site protection**, product commitments, and investment commitments,
- the state supports the package politically if co-determination and regional industrial capacity are preserved,
- independent verification is used to confirm both savings and commitments.

This tries to anchor the compromise around **future industrial presence**, not around a permanent threat of exit.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If temporary concessions are fully time-limited, automatically reversible, and explicitly non-prejudicial, what specific wording would you require to treat them as compatible with your red lines?

### Industrial Union
What minimum savings and capacity-reduction figures would you accept as evidence that a no-closure package is economically credible?

### Group & Brand Management
What precise comparative evidence can you provide that non-closure measures are insufficient before closures and compulsory redundancies are kept on the table?

### Regional State Government
Will you specify the exact political or governance levers you are prepared to use to secure binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitments?

### Controlling Family Holding
What concrete stewardship conditions would make you support a binding no-closure settlement through 2030?

### Institutional Investors
What verification standard and milestone framework would you require before you would accept a time-limited pact without closure as sufficiently credible?
