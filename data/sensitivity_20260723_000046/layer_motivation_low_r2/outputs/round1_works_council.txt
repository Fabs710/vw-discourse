We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the starting point, and it is non-negotiable for the works council. The workforce has already delivered flexibility, productivity and sacrifices over many years. What we cannot accept now is that management tries to solve strategic mistakes and market pressure by destroying jobs, breaking the historic job-security framework, and handing sites a death sentence.

Our priorities are clear:

- Keep every German plant open.
- Secure all existing jobs as far as possible.
- Exclude forced layoffs.
- Preserve co-determination and real involvement in any restructuring.
- Protect long-term industrial capacity in Germany, not just short-term financial targets.

If the company needs adjustment, then we are prepared to discuss alternatives that avoid closures and forced redundancies: capacity reductions spread fairly, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification, and a negotiated contribution from labour — but only if there is a binding guarantee for sites and jobs through 2030 and a genuine role for employee representatives in shaping the plan.

What we will oppose firmly:
- unilateral closure announcements,
- breaking the job-security pledge,
- outsourcing or relocation that amounts to hidden closure,
- any “restructuring” that shifts the burden almost entirely onto employees.

Management must come to the table with a credible plan that protects the German industrial base and the people who built this company’s success. Without that, there will be resistance — in the supervisory board, at the plants, and in public.
