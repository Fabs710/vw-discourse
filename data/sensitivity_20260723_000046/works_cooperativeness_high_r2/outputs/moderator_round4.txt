### Core impasse
The main deadlock is **not over whether restructuring is needed**; all sides accept that it is. The impasse is over **the form of restructuring and the burden of proof**:

- **Employee side / state**: closures and compulsory redundancies must not be assumed; management must prove site-by-site why they are unavoidable.
- **Management / investors / family**: the footprint may be structurally too large; a solution must deliver durable savings and may require consolidation.
- **Shared sticking point**: no side is yet working from a jointly accepted, plant-by-plant evidence base that ties utilization, capex, savings, and employment impact to each scenario.

In short: **the dispute is about whether the company can be made viable without closing plants or forcing layoffs, and whether that claim can be demonstrated with site-specific numbers.**

---

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise
Below are **procedural bridge options** that could narrow the gap without taking a substantive position.

#### 1) Time-limited “no irreversible step” phase
A temporary moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period, paired with:
- full site-by-site disclosure,
- independent industrial/financial review,
- and a binding timetable for decision-making.

This gives the employee side reassurance that no irreversible step happens before evidence is tested, while giving management a structured path to prove or disprove viability.

#### 2) Tiered restructuring package
A staged agreement in which the parties first implement the least destructive measures:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- shift consolidation,
- targeted investment and repurposing tests.

Only if those measures fail to close the gap would the parties reopen discussion on more difficult measures, under pre-agreed criteria.

#### 3) Site-by-site “traffic light” framework
Each German site is classified jointly into:
- **Green**: viable with modest optimization,
- **Amber**: viable only with repurposing or volume reassignment,
- **Red**: structurally at risk unless a credible plan is found.

This could reduce the all-or-nothing dynamic and allow negotiation plant by plant rather than as a single package.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If an independent site-by-site analysis shows that one or more plants cannot be kept viable without major repurposing or consolidation, **what specific alternative adjustment measures would you accept before any discussion of closures?**

#### Industrial Union
What level of **durable labor contribution** would you consider acceptable if it were explicitly linked to binding site protection, transparent investment commitments, and no compulsory redundancies?

#### Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to publishing a **plant-by-plant case** that clearly separates:  
1) what can be fixed through optimization,  
2) what requires repurposing, and  
3) what, if anything, is genuinely non-viable?

#### Regional State Government
What concrete leverage will you use to ensure that “no closures as a starting point” becomes a **real negotiation constraint**, not just a political statement?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a temporary framework that preserves every German site during the review period, even if it limits management’s freedom to act immediately?

#### Institutional Investors
If the analysis shows that site-preserving restructuring can deliver a competitive cost base, will you accept a settlement that prioritizes jobs and site continuity over footprint reduction?

#### Expert
Can you produce a **standardized threshold table** for each site showing the minimum viable utilization, likely repurposing capex, and estimated cash impact under both scenarios?
