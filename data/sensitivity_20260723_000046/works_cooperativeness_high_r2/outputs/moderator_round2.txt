### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad support for **voluntary measures**, including:
  - redeployment,
  - retraining,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - internal transfers,
  - productivity improvements,
  - and, where feasible, capacity optimization.
- There is shared concern that a **prolonged conflict** would harm the company.
- All parties ask for **site-by-site transparency** on overcapacity, costs, and savings assumptions.
- There is general recognition that any settlement should be **durable**, not a short-term fix.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**
  - Employee side, regional state, and in part the family strongly resist closures as a starting point.
  - Management and investors say closures may be necessary if the economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Works council, union, and state reject forced layoffs if avoidable.
  - Management and investors refuse to rule them out categorically.
- **Scope of job/site guarantees**
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030, including no closures.
  - Management and investors say such guarantees are only credible if the package delivers sufficient structural savings.
- **Burden-sharing**
  - Employees want shared sacrifice with contributions from management, owners, and investors.
  - Management and investors say labour must make a **material, durable** contribution; they view temporary concessions as insufficient on their own.
- **Governance/co-determination**
  - Employee side and state insist co-determination must remain substantive.
  - Management and investors want restructuring flexibility and do not accept governance constraints that prevent economically needed change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- How to reconcile **job and site security** with the need for **durable cost reduction**.
- Whether the required savings can be achieved through **alternatives to closures and layoffs**, and if so, on what scale.
- What mix of measures can deliver both:
  - **operational competitiveness**, and
  - **social/political acceptability**.
- How much of the adjustment should come from:
  - labour-cost concessions,
  - capacity reduction,
  - management restraint,
  - owner commitment,
  - and potential consolidation of uneconomic capacity.
- What future **investment and product commitments** would make any restructuring credible beyond the immediate bargaining round.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **quantified options**:
- Present a **site-by-site picture** of overcapacity, costs, and savings.
- Identify which measures are **necessary**, which are **optional**, and which are **red lines** for each side.
- Compare at least two restructuring scenarios:
  1. a **site-preserving** scenario, and
  2. a **more invasive capacity-adjustment** scenario.
- For each scenario, specify:
  - expected savings,
  - employment impact,
  - investment needs,
  - governance implications,
  - and conditions for acceptance.

The next round should focus on testing whether a **workable compromise** exists, rather than restating absolute positions.
