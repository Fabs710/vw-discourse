I’ll be equally direct.

## My overall position
The company needs a real restructuring, but I **do not** accept a restructuring that starts from plant closures and compulsory redundancies in Germany. As regional anchor shareholder, I will not support an approach that makes the workforce and the state’s industrial base the first casualty of management’s strategy problem.

I do accept that the company has a competitiveness problem and that German operations must become more efficient. But efficiency must be achieved in a way that preserves the company’s long-term role in the region, protects the German footprint where economically possible, and respects co-determination as a real governing principle, not a formal ornament.

---

## Where I agree

### 1) The company needs durable change
Management, the family, investors, and the expert are right on one central point: this cannot be cosmetic. The status quo is not good enough.

### 2) We need hard numbers
The expert’s insistence on site-by-site transparency is exactly right. Before anyone asks for closures, layoffs, or wage sacrifice, we need:
- utilization by site,
- product pipeline through 2030,
- break-even thresholds,
- conversion capex,
- savings by measure,
- and a clear comparison of alternatives.

### 3) Voluntary and flexibility-based tools belong in the package
I support:
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- internal transfers,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary departure programs,
- productivity improvements,
- and carefully designed capacity optimization.

### 4) A prolonged conflict would be harmful
I agree that strikes and open confrontation would damage the company, the workforce, and the region. That is precisely why management should stop using closure pressure as the starting point.

---

## Where I disagree

### 1) I do not accept closure-first logic
Management and investors are treating plant closure as if it were simply another neutral adjustment tool. It is not. In a regionally anchored industrial company, closure is an irreversible political, social, and economic decision.

If a site can be preserved through reallocation, conversion, shift changes, or volume transfer, then closure is not justified.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies as the default fallback
If the company’s strategic misjudgment is corrected through job destruction alone, that is neither fair nor politically sustainable. Forced layoffs should be the absolute last resort, not the first serious option.

### 3) I do not accept a deal that weakens co-determination
The state’s veto role and the existing co-determination structure are part of the company’s legitimacy and stability. Any restructuring that tries to sideline those rights will face resistance from me.

### 4) I do not accept “shared sacrifice” that is really one-sided burden shifting
The union is right to insist that labour cannot be the only adjustment variable. If employees give up wage elements, working-time security, or flexibility, then management, owners, and capital must also contribute materially.

---

## My reaction to the expert assessment
The expert report is useful, and it makes one point very clear: **voluntary measures alone may not be enough if the overcapacity is severe**. That is an important warning.

But the report does **not** prove that closures are necessary. It says closures become relevant only if:
- overcapacity is large,
- alternatives fail,
- and no site can be brought to viable utilization.

That means management still has to prove the case, site by site. Until then, closure is an unproven claim, not a decision.

---

## To management
You keep saying the economics require structural change. I am not disputing that. What I am disputing is your assumption that structural change must mean German site closures and job cuts as the primary solution.

I want the numbers, in detail:
- Which sites are overcapacity?
- What is the minimum viable utilization at each site?
- What volume can be shifted?
- What product or platform allocations are possible?
- What is the conversion capex for repurposing?
- What savings come from shift reduction, working-time measures, and voluntary exits?
- Where exactly is closure unavoidable, if anywhere?

If you cannot answer that, then your plan is not yet serious enough to ask the state to back it.

And I will say this plainly: **I will not support a package that guts employment in the state while management frames it as inevitability.**

---

## To the works council
I understand and largely support your refusal of closure-led restructuring and forced redundancies. Your defense of jobs, sites, and co-determination is legitimate.

That said, I need to be honest: a purely declaratory “no closures, no layoffs” stance is not enough on its own. If the company has real overcapacity, then we need to show a credible alternative that is economically durable.

So I would urge you to help shape a serious alternative around:
- site-preserving volume reallocations,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- voluntary instruments,
- retraining,
- internal mobility,
- and productivity agreements.

I am prepared to back such a route strongly. But I need it to be more than a political slogan; it has to be a workable industrial plan.

---

## To the union
I agree with your insistence on shared sacrifice and on protecting the collective framework. But I also need to be frank: temporary or limited labour concessions alone will not convince me if they are not matched by commitments from management and owners.

A fair settlement must include:
- binding job and site protection where feasible,
- no compulsory redundancies if alternatives exist,
- a real investment and product commitment,
- and a meaningful contribution from capital and management to the restructuring effort.

If workers are asked to give, then the company leadership and owners must also bind themselves to long-term industrial commitments. Otherwise it is not a partnership.

---

## To the controlling family
You are right that the company needs durability, not cosmetic compromise. On that point we agree.

But durability is not created by allowing management to hollow out the German footprint. Durability comes from a credible industrial future that preserves the company’s regional anchor and keeps the workforce on board.

I expect you to support:
- a negotiated restructuring,
- preservation of sites wherever economically possible,
- serious investment commitments,
- and a fair burden-sharing arrangement.

If the only answer becomes closure and headcount reduction, then the company may look cleaner on paper but weaker in its legitimacy and long-term stability.

---

## To the institutional investors
I understand your focus on margin restoration and deal certainty. But certainty built on social rupture is brittle, not durable.

You want a package that holds. Then it has to respect:
- the company’s social license,
- the state’s anchor role,
- co-determination,
- and the regional economic function of the German plants.

I am not defending inefficiency for its own sake. I am saying that a company this important cannot be treated like a disposable cost structure.

---

## What I can support
I can support a package that includes:
- site-by-site transparency,
- capacity optimization,
- product and volume reallocations,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary departures,
- retraining and redeployment,
- targeted labour contributions,
- and investment commitments that secure the German footprint.

I can also support selective consolidation **only if** management proves, with numbers, that a site truly cannot be made viable otherwise and that all alternatives have been exhausted.

---

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- plant closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as the default fallback,
- erosion of co-determination,
- or a restructuring that makes the regional economy absorb the main shock.

---

## Bottom line
My threshold is clear:

1. **Protect German jobs and sites wherever economically possible**
2. **Preserve co-determination and the state’s veto role**
3. **Require transparency before irreversible decisions**
4. **Accept only negotiated, socially responsible restructuring**
5. **Reject closure-led adjustment unless it is proven unavoidable**

So I am open to a hard restructuring, but only one that keeps the company an industrial anchor in the region. If the proposal does not do that, then I will not support it.
