We are entering this discussion with one clear principle: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of transformation. What management is now putting on the table goes far beyond a normal restructuring: it breaks with the historic job-security framework, threatens whole sites, and shifts the risks of strategic missteps onto employees. That is unacceptable.

Our priorities are straightforward:

1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **Prevent forced layoffs.**
3. **Secure binding job guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Protect co-determination and the workforce’s real influence over the restructuring path.**
5. **Pursue savings through alternatives** — better capacity utilisation, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, voluntary measures, training, and a fair contribution from capital and management.

If the company needs to save costs, then the burden cannot fall primarily on jobs and sites. We are prepared to discuss a serious restructuring, but only if it is **socially responsible, site-secure, and negotiated**, not imposed.

So my position is clear:  
**No closures. No forced redundancies. No fait accompli.**  
If management wants a solution, it must come with **binding guarantees, transparent numbers, and alternatives that preserve the future of the German plants and the livelihoods built there.**
