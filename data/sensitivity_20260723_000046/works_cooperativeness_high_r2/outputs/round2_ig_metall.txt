I will be direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even parts of management and the owners on one central point: **this cannot be solved with a cosmetic restructuring**. The company needs a durable answer to overcapacity, cost pressure, and investment needs.

I also agree that:
- voluntary tools, redeployment, retraining, and working-time solutions belong on the table,
- a prolonged conflict would be damaging,
- and the restructuring must be economically credible.

### Where I disagree — and this is the core issue
I do **not** accept that the necessary adjustment must be paid for mainly by employees through:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures,
- or a permanent dismantling of the collective bargaining framework.

That is not a “mechanism” I can treat neutrally. It is a red line.

If management and the owners want labour peace, then they must stop framing job destruction as the default solution. If the burden falls overwhelmingly on the workforce, then this is not a shared-sacrifice package — it is a transfer of the crisis onto employees.

### To management
You say you need permanent savings. Fine. But you have not yet shown that **closures and forced layoffs are the only way** to get there.

So I want specifics, not general pressure:
- What exact overcapacity exists by site and function?
- What savings come from working-time, product shifts, internal redeployment, and capacity management?
- What management-side and owner-side contributions are on the table?
- What investment commitments will secure the future business?

If the answer is just “we need more pain from labour,” then you do not have a restructuring plan; you have a wage-cut plan.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- protection of co-determination,
- and serious alternatives.

But I want to sharpen one thing: **job protection must be matched with a serious, balanced contribution package**. That means we should be ready to discuss:
- temporary or targeted labour-cost contributions,
- working-time measures,
- flexibility in capacity,
- and voluntary instruments,

**if and only if** they are tied to binding site and job security and are shared fairly across the company.

I will not support a deal where workers give and management merely promises to “consider” its part later.

### To the regional state
I appreciate your insistence on social legitimacy and governance stability. That matters. But legitimacy is not preserved by ambiguity. If the state wants to prevent escalation, it must help force a real compromise from the shareholder side and management side as well — not just encourage the workforce to show flexibility.

### To the controlling family and investors
You both speak about durability and competitiveness. Good. Then show willingness to contribute to a durable settlement:
- management restraint,
- owner commitment,
- investment guarantees,
- and a serious willingness to preserve the collective framework.

If you insist on structural change, then the change must be balanced. Workers cannot be the only adjustable cost center in the company.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a package with:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **A negotiated reduction in labour costs or working time**, but temporary, limited, and fairly designed
4. **Voluntary departures only**
5. **Redeployment and retraining**
6. **Binding site and investment guarantees**
7. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
8. **A fair contribution from management and owners**

### What I am not prepared to discuss
I am **not** prepared to discuss:
- closures as a starting point,
- forced redundancies as an acceptable fallback,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or a deal where workers finance the restructuring while capital preserves its position untouched.

### My bottom line
If the company wants a settlement, it must be a **shared-sacrifice package** with:
- real contributions from all sides,
- secure jobs and sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- and a durable collective agreement.

If management cannot move beyond closures and layoffs, then this conflict will escalate. And if that happens, it will not be because the workforce refused responsibility — it will be because the burden was made unacceptable and unfair.
