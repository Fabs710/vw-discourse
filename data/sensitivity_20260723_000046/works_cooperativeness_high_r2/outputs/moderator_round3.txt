**Moderator summary — Round 3**

### 1) What has been agreed
- This is **not** a cosmetic issue; all sides accept that **real restructuring** is required.
- There is broad agreement on the need for **site-by-site transparency** and hard numbers.
- Most parties accept that **voluntary measures**, **redeployment**, **retraining**, **working-time flexibility**, and **productivity improvements** should be part of the package.
- There is also shared recognition that a **prolonged conflict** would damage the company, workforce, and region.
- The expert assessments converge on one point: **voluntary and flexibility-based measures can mitigate but may not fully solve structural overcapacity** if it is large.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Labour-side and state representatives largely reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management, family, and investors keep them on the table as a possible structural tool.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour-side actors reject them.  
  - Capital and management view them as possible if economics require it.
- **Scope of labour contribution:**  
  - Labour accepts limited contributions only alongside binding site/job guarantees.  
  - Management and investors argue that concessions must be material and durable enough to restore competitiveness.
- **Co-determination and veto expectations:**  
  - The workforce side insists these rights must be substantive.  
  - Management and investors argue they cannot override economic necessity.
- **Whether all German sites can be preserved:**  
  - Labour and the state want a site-preserving outcome.  
  - Management and investors say this must still be proven feasible by the numbers.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much overcapacity actually exists, by site**, and whether it is mild, material, or severe.
- The trade-off between:
  - **keeping all sites open** with lower near-term disruption, versus
  - **capacity consolidation** with higher one-time social cost but potentially stronger long-run savings.
- **Which sites could be repurposed** through product transfer or conversion, and at what capex.
- Whether **working-time, voluntary exits, and redeployment** can close enough of the gap without closures.
- The balance between:
  - **job/security guarantees**, and
  - **credible margin improvement and investment capacity**.
- The risk that a “site-preserving” deal may **defer rather than solve** the underlying cost problem.
- The risk that a consolidation-led deal may **trigger conflict and implementation delays** that erode savings.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principle to **quantified scenario comparison**. Please focus on:
- a **site-by-site table** for all German plants,
- current and forecast **utilization**,
- **minimum viable utilization**,
- **product/model pipeline through 2030**,
- **savings** from non-destructive measures,
- **conversion capex** where repurposing is proposed,
- and **EBITDA / free-cash-flow impact** under:
  1. a **site-preserving scenario**, and  
  2. a **capacity-consolidation scenario**.

The goal should be to identify where a negotiated, site-preserving solution is genuinely viable, and where it is not.
