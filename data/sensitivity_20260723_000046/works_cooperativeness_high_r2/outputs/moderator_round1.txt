**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that **voluntary measures**, **redeployment**, **training/retraining**, **working-time adjustments**, and **capacity optimization** should be part of the discussion.
- Most parties agree that a solution should be **negotiated**, not imposed unilaterally.
- There is shared recognition that a **prolonged conflict** would be harmful to the company, employees, and value creation.
- There is general support for a package that is **durable** and linked to a credible **industrial plan** and future investment.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council and union reject them categorically; management, investors, and to a lesser extent owners/state see them as possible or necessary under certain conditions.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side rejects them; management and capital-side actors do not rule them out if needed for structural adjustment.
- **Scope of job guarantees:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030; management says this depends on whether sufficient savings and capacity changes are delivered.
- **Extent of workforce contribution:**  
  Everyone expects some labour-side contribution, but the scale, form, and duration are disputed.
- **How much structural change is enough:**  
  Management and investors want substantial, permanent cost and capacity reduction; employee representatives and the state emphasize site preservation and social protection.
- **Role of co-determination and governance:**  
  Employee side and state insist on preserving strong co-determination and the existing governance balance; this is not a point of consensus with management, though it is not rejected outright.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** how much employment/site protection can be maintained while still achieving material margin improvement.
- **Voluntary vs. compulsory adjustment:** whether voluntary instruments can deliver enough savings and capacity reduction.
- **Temporary concessions vs. long-term guarantees:** whether workforce sacrifices should be exchanged for binding protection of jobs/sites and for how long.
- **Site preservation vs. industrial consolidation:** whether underutilized sites can be kept open through new products or shifts, or whether some consolidation is unavoidable.
- **Shared sacrifice vs. burden shifting:** what specific contributions will come from employees, management, owners, and capital holders.
- **Short-term conflict avoidance vs. long-term restructuring depth:** whether a faster deal risks being too shallow, or a deeper deal risks escalation.
- **Social legitimacy vs. financial sufficiency:** how to design a package that is both implementable and economically credible.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package elements**: identify which sites, functions, and workforce measures are under discussion.
- Ask each side to state:
  - the **minimum acceptable restructuring outcome**,
  - the **maximum acceptable social cost**,
  - and the **specific contributions** they are prepared to make.
- Prioritize discussion of a **sequenced package**: immediate savings, medium-term restructuring, and conditional job/site guarantees.
- Request **transparent assumptions and numbers** on overcapacity, savings targets, and required investment so the trade-offs can be assessed concretely.
