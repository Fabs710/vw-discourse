Management, owners, investors: I have listened carefully, and I will be equally clear in response.

We agree on one point only: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. But a “real restructuring” cannot mean throwing away plants, breaking the job-security framework, and using the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort.

### Where I agree
- Yes, the cost base must be addressed.
- Yes, voluntary measures, retraining, redeployment, and smarter capacity use belong on the table.
- Yes, a prolonged conflict would damage everyone.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures are simply one instrument among many. For us, that is not a neutral mechanism — it is a direct attack on jobs, on regional industrial capacity, and on the social contract that has held this company together.

I also do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the “necessary” fallback if management cannot immediately get the numbers it wants. That would mean management’s strategic failure is paid for by employees who did not create it.

### To management
You say the economics must change permanently. Fine. Then show us a package that does that **without closures and without forced layoffs**. If you have not exhausted all non-destructive alternatives, then you have not made your case.

What I need from you is not rhetoric about competitiveness, but **transparent figures and a site-by-site plan**:
- What exactly is the overcapacity?
- Which sites are supposedly affected and why?
- What savings are being targeted from each lever?
- What product and investment commitments will secure the German footprint to 2030?

Until then, talk of closures is premature and unacceptable.

### To the controlling family and the state
You both say you value stability and the long-term franchise. Then use that influence now. Stability is not preserved by threatening the workforce with plant shutdowns and job cuts. If you want labour peace and durability, then the ownership side must back a settlement that protects the industrial base.

I will be frank: co-determination is not a decorative feature. It is a veto and a real voice in the future of this company. Any attempt to sideline that or to force a fait accompli will meet fierce resistance.

### To investors
You are asking for “deal certainty” and margin improvement. I understand that. But certainty built on social destruction is not a sustainable solution. If the package is too harsh to be accepted, then you do not get certainty — you get conflict, strikes, and a damaged company.

So if you want a result that holds, you need to support a solution that:
- keeps **all German plants open**,
- avoids **compulsory redundancies**,
- preserves **co-determination**,
- and still delivers savings through negotiated, socially responsible means.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time reductions,
- internal transfers,
- voluntary departure programs,
- training and qualification,
- temporary and fair contributions from labour **if and only if** jobs and sites are secured,
- and productivity measures that do not destroy the workforce’s future.

### What we are not prepared to discuss
We are **not** prepared to discuss:
- closures as a starting point,
- forced layoffs,
- dismantling the job-security framework,
- or any settlement that treats German sites as disposable.

### Bottom line
If management wants a settlement, it must come with:
1. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No plant closures**
4. **A genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
5. **Transparent numbers and a negotiated alternative plan**

That is our line. It is not a bargaining pose; it is the minimum acceptable basis for discussion. If that cannot be met, then there is no serious agreement to be had from the workforce side.
