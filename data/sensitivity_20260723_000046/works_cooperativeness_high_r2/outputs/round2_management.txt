I appreciate the clarity from the employee side, but I need to be just as clear: **your current position does not solve the problem we face**.

## Where I agree
I agree on three points:
- This must be a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- A prolonged conflict would damage everyone.
- Voluntary measures, redeployment, retraining, working-time flexibility, and smarter capacity use all belong on the table.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** agree that:
- **all German plants must remain open regardless of economics**,
- **compulsory redundancies are never acceptable under any circumstances**,
- or that we can restore competitiveness while preserving the current cost and capacity structure.

That is the core issue. If we keep every site open and rule out hard capacity decisions in advance, then we are not restructuring the company — we are preserving overcapacity and delaying the next crisis.

## To the Works Council
You are asking for:
- all plants open,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- and co-determination shaping the outcome.

I understand the demand, but I must reject the idea that these are available **without a material structural contribution from labour**.

You keep saying management must first prove that every non-destructive alternative is exhausted. Fair enough — and we are doing that. But let me be equally direct: **you have not shown how the required savings will be achieved if closures and compulsory reductions are ruled out from the start**.

So here is what I need:
- site-by-site cost and capacity measures that close the gap,
- a labour-cost contribution that is large enough to matter,
- and a credible path to margin restoration.

If you cannot provide that, then “no closures” is not a solution; it is a refusal to confront the economics.

## To the Union
You say this must be a shared-sacrifice package, not a transfer of pain to employees. On that principle, we are not far apart.

But “shared” has to be real. At the moment, what I hear is:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of the collective framework,
- and only temporary or limited concessions from labour.

That is not enough.

If we want to restore competitiveness, then the workforce contribution must be **durable and material**. I am open on the mechanism, but not on the result. The result must reduce our cost base structurally.

And to be very clear: if management is asked to preserve every site, then the labour side must be prepared to make deeper concessions on:
- pay,
- working time,
- flexibility,
- and workforce size.

Otherwise the package will not work.

## To the Regional State
I share your concern that a destructive process would damage trust and the regional economy. That is precisely why I am pushing for a negotiated restructuring rather than an uncontrolled escalation.

But I do **not** accept the suggestion that closure is somehow merely a political threat that should be pushed aside. If a site is structurally uneconomic, then leaving it untouched is not responsible stewardship. It is deferred damage.

Your call for site-preserving alternatives is reasonable **only if those alternatives are actually sufficient**. If they are not, then we need the courage to make hard decisions.

## To the Controlling Family
I agree with you on one thing: this cannot be a cosmetic compromise. We need durable savings and labour peace that actually holds.

Where I differ is that labour peace is not a substitute for economics. A deal that looks stable but leaves the cost base uncompetitive will fail later, and at a higher cost.

We need your support for a package that is:
- economically sufficient,
- durable,
- and not blocked by a blanket refusal of structural change.

## To the Institutional Investors
I agree with your focus on margin improvement and deal certainty. Those are the right priorities.

But the path to certainty cannot be a settlement that preserves everything and changes nothing. If you want credible returns, you need to support a package with real capacity reduction and meaningful labour-cost savings.

## My position
Let me state this plainly:

### What I am prepared to discuss
- Voluntary departure programs
- Redeployment and retraining
- Working-time flexibility
- Capacity reductions
- Product and shift reallocations
- Temporary or targeted labour-cost measures, **if they are material and durable**
- Site-specific solutions where they are economically credible

### What I am not prepared to accept
- A blanket guarantee that every German plant stays open
- A blanket ban on compulsory redundancies
- A deal that leaves the company structurally overstaffed and uncompetitive
- A cosmetic compromise dressed up as “stability”

## Bottom line
If the employee side wants guarantees through 2030, then the package must create the conditions for those guarantees to be credible:
- lower fixed costs,
- lower overcapacity,
- and a sustainable margin path.

Without that, guarantees are not worth much.

So I am not moving off the core requirement: **this restructuring must deliver real, durable cost and capacity reduction**. If your proposal cannot do that, then it does not meet the company’s needs.
