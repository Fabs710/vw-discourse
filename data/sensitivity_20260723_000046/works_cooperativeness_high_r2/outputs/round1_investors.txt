I’ll be direct: **I support a restructuring only if it credibly restores competitive margins.** If the outcome leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive, then it is not a solution from our perspective — it is value destruction.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the controlling family, and the state on the core diagnosis:
- the company’s German cost base is too high,
- capacity is out of line with demand,
- and the brand needs durable margin improvement, not cosmetic fixes.

I also agree with the workforce side on one point:
- **a destructive, prolonged conflict would destroy value** and should be avoided if possible.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support a settlement that simply protects all sites and jobs while relying on vague promises of “future improvements.” That would leave the company with the same structural problem and a weaker financial position.

I also do **not** support management using closure threats as a negotiating tactic if the real objective is just to force a symbolic concession. We care about results, not drama. If closures are not necessary, then do not use them as leverage. But if the economics genuinely require them, then they cannot be ruled out in advance.

### What matters to us
From a capital-markets perspective, three things matter:

1. **Credible margin improvement**
   - We need to see a clear path back to competitive returns.
   - If the package does not materially improve margins, we will not view it as credible.

2. **Durable cost reduction**
   - Temporary measures are not enough.
   - We need structural savings that survive beyond the next bargaining round.

3. **Deal certainty**
   - Prolonged strikes, political drift, or ambiguous “frameworks” are negative for value.
   - A bad but certain deal is often better than an endless conflict that keeps eroding earnings.

### My view on the current positions
- **Works council / union:** Your refusal of any plant closure or forced redundancy is understandable politically, but it is too rigid for investors. If that line means the business cannot get to competitive economics, then it is not acceptable.
- **Management:** You are right that the company cannot remain structurally uncompetitive. But you need to show that you have exhausted serious alternatives before insisting on irreversible measures.
- **State and family owners:** You are right to demand a real restructuring, but not if it ends in a half-measure that protects governance optics while margins stay weak.

### What would move us
We would support a package that includes:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- substantial and durable labour-cost savings,
- voluntary and negotiated workforce reduction tools where possible,
- internal redeployment and retraining where economically sensible,
- and, if needed, closure or consolidation of genuinely uneconomic capacity.

What we would not support is:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- “no closures, no redundancies” as a blanket rule,
- or a deal that preserves employment at the expense of long-term competitiveness.

### Bottom line
We are not aligned with any side’s rhetoric. We are aligned with the financial outcome:
**restore margins, reduce structural costs, and end uncertainty quickly.**

If the final package does that, we will back it.  
If it does not, we will treat it as inadequate and continue pressing for stronger action through our capital allocation and market voice.
