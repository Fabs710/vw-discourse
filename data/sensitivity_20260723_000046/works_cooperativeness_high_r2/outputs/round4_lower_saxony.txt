I will be equally clear.

## My position
As the regional anchor shareholder, I cannot support a restructuring that begins from **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** in Germany. That would violate the company’s role as a regional economic anchor and would create political and social damage that is not justified unless every alternative has been proven exhausted site by site.

At the same time, I do **not** support a cosmetic “protect everything” posture if the numbers show that parts of the footprint are structurally unviable. The company does need real restructuring. But that restructuring must be designed to **preserve jobs, preserve industrial capacity in the state where possible, and maintain co-determination as a real governing force**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, investors, and the expert on several points:

- This is a **real structural problem**, not a minor adjustment.
- The company needs **durable competitiveness improvements**, not a short-lived patch.
- **Site-by-site transparency** is essential.
- **Voluntary measures, retraining, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and productivity gains** belong in the package.
- A prolonged conflict would hurt everyone.

I also agree that labour must contribute. But “contribute” does not mean being asked to pay for the entire adjustment through closures, layoffs, and erosion of protections.

## Where I disagree
### To management
I do **not** accept your framing that closures and forced layoffs are simply normal tools if overcapacity exists.

Yes, if a site is truly below viable scale and cannot be repurposed, then difficult decisions may follow. But you have **not yet demonstrated that case**. Right now, you are still asking for acceptance of irreversible measures before the evidence is fully on the table.

So I want:
- utilization by site,
- product pipeline through 2030,
- minimum viable utilization by site,
- savings from shift reductions and working-time measures,
- conversion capex for repurposing,
- and a real comparison of the site-preserving and consolidation scenarios.

Without that, closure is a claim, not a justified conclusion.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that workers should not be the only adjustment variable. That is correct.

But I also need to be frank: a blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no site change” position is not yet a restructuring answer. It is a red line. That is legitimate in bargaining, but it does not solve the economic question.

If the company can be rebalanced by:
- product allocation,
- shift consolidation,
- internal transfers,
- temporary working-time changes,
- voluntary departures,
- and targeted investment,

then that is the route I want. If not, then we need to talk about selective consolidation — but only on the basis of hard proof, not as a first resort.

### To the controlling family
I agree that durability matters and that a destructive conflict would damage value. But durability is not created by preserving an uncompetitive structure. If the family wants the company to remain strong, it must back a solution that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable.

That means no bluffing with closures as leverage, but also no pretending that every plant can remain untouched regardless of economics.

### To the investors
You are right that a cosmetic deal is not acceptable. But a deal that imposes the social cost of closure before the evidence is complete is also not acceptable.

Your demand for durable savings is legitimate. My demand is that those savings be pursued first through:
- capacity optimization,
- operational adjustments,
- repurposing where possible,
- and a fair burden-sharing package.

If, after that, some sites still cannot be made viable, then we can discuss consolidation. But not before the numbers are fully exposed.

## My bottom line
I can support a tough restructuring if it:
1. preserves German jobs and sites **where economically possible**,
2. protects the state’s co-determination and veto role,
3. uses closures only as a **last resort**, not a starting point,
4. ensures that any labour contribution is matched by contributions from management, owners, and capital,
5. and is based on transparent site-by-site evidence.

What I cannot support is a package that treats regional plants as disposable and workers as the main shock absorber.

## What I want next
I support the call for a quantified comparison, but I want it framed correctly:

[SUMMON: independent site-by-site industrial and financial analysis, including utilization, break-even thresholds, repurposing capex, employment impact, and EBITDA/free-cash-flow outcomes under:
1. a site-preserving scenario,
2. a consolidation scenario,
with explicit identification of which sites are viable, convertible, or genuinely at risk.]

Until that analysis is on the table, I will not move off my core line:
- no closure-led restructuring,
- no forced layoffs as a default,
- and no hollowing out of the company’s role in the region.

If the numbers later show a truly unavoidable exception, I will assess it carefully. But the burden is on management to prove that case, not on the state to pre-approve destruction.
