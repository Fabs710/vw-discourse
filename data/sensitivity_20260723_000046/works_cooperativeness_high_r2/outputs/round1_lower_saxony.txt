I appreciate the clarity from all sides, but I want to be equally direct from the perspective of the regional state and long-term anchor shareholder.

### First: what I agree with
I agree with management on one point: **the company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. The cost base, capacity situation, and investment needs cannot simply be wished away.

I also agree with the workforce representatives that **the adjustment cannot be dumped primarily on employees and German sites**. This company is not just an abstract balance sheet; it is a major regional employer and an industrial anchor. A restructuring that destroys trust, tears up site commitments, and triggers mass social damage is not a sustainable solution.

### Where I disagree with management
What I cannot accept is a logic that starts from plant closures and mass job cuts as the default answer.

That is not just a “mechanism,” as you put it. It is a profound strategic and social choice, and it has consequences that go far beyond this quarter’s margin target:

- It weakens the company’s industrial base in Germany.
- It risks permanent damage to the regional economy.
- It undermines employee trust and co-determination.
- It creates political and social fallout that can easily make the restructuring harder, not easier.

If the company wants my support, it needs to show that it has exhausted **site-preserving alternatives** before moving to irreversible steps.

### Where I agree with the works council and union
I support the principle that:
- **forced redundancies should be avoided if at all possible,**
- **German sites should be preserved where viable,**
- and **co-determination is not negotiable**.

I also agree that workers have already contributed significantly to the transformation and should not be asked to pay for management’s strategic mistakes alone.

### But I am not endorsing a blank refusal
I do not think a simple “no closures, no reductions, no change” position is enough either. If the company’s economics are deteriorating, then some combination of:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- voluntary departure programs,
- product reassignment,
- and negotiated labour-cost measures

may be necessary.

The difference is this: **those measures must be designed to protect the industrial footprint and avoid social dislocation wherever possible**.

### My position
From the regional state’s perspective, a viable settlement must have these features:

1. **No plant closure as a first resort**
   - Closure must not be treated as the starting point.
   - Every alternative to keep sites open must be seriously tested.

2. **No compulsory redundancies if there is a negotiated alternative**
   - We can discuss workforce reductions through voluntary, phased, or internally managed instruments.
   - Forced layoffs should remain the last resort, not the first tool.

3. **A credible industrial plan**
   - If labour is asked to contribute, management and owners must put a concrete investment and product plan on the table.
   - Savings without a future model are just managed decline.

4. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**
   - I am very clear on this: the governance balance is part of the company’s legitimacy and stability.
   - Any attempt to sideline the state or weaken the existing veto and board role would be unacceptable.

5. **A fair burden-sharing package**
   - If employees are expected to make concessions, then management restraint, owner commitment, and future investment guarantees must be part of the deal.

### What I would support
I could support a negotiated restructuring if it:
- preserves the German industrial core,
- avoids mass layoffs and closures,
- includes significant but fair workforce contributions,
- and comes with binding commitments on investment, site development, and future products.

### What I would not support
I would not support:
- a restructuring built around closure threats,
- a settlement that forces the workforce to absorb most of the pain,
- or any outcome that erodes the state’s co-determination and veto role.

So my message is: **yes to restructuring, no to a destructive downsizing strategy**.  
If management wants the regional state to help carry this through, it will need to bring forward a solution that is economically credible **and** socially responsible. Otherwise, it will not get the broader legitimacy and governance support it needs.
