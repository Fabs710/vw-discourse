I hear the red lines you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic restructuring is not an option**, and neither is a path that leaves our German cost base structurally uncompetitive.

We are facing a hard reality:
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying.
- Our German factory costs are materially above target.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales objective.
- We need to free up funds for product and technology investment now, not in some distant future.

So let me respond directly.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- This cannot be a sham restructuring.
- Any solution must be durable, not temporary.
- We need a negotiated agreement, not unilateral diktat.
- Voluntary instruments, redeployment, retraining, and capacity optimization all belong on the table.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the proposition that:
- every German plant must remain open regardless of economics,
- there can be no compulsory job reductions,
- and the workforce can veto structural change while the cost base stays out of line.

If we simply preserve every site and avoid any hard capacity decisions, then we are **not solving the crisis** — we are postponing it. That would be irresponsible to the company, the brand, and the future investment needs of the business.

### What management needs
We need a package that delivers:
1. **A genuine structural reduction in capacity and fixed cost**
2. **A meaningful labour-cost contribution**
3. **A sustainable path to restore margins**
4. **A framework that allows investment through 2030**

I am open on the **mechanism** — closures, partial consolidation, product-line shifts, working-time changes, or other structural measures — but I am **not flexible on the result**: the economics must change materially, and permanently.

### On your demands
- **No closures at all**: not acceptable as a blanket rule.
- **No forced redundancies ever**: not acceptable if the only alternative is to keep uneconomic capacity in place.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**: only possible if they are tied to a credible and sufficient cost and capacity package. Guarantees without structural savings are meaningless.
- **Protection of co-determination**: yes, but co-determination is not a substitute for competitiveness.

### On shared sacrifice
I hear the call for shared burden. That is fair in principle. But “shared” cannot mean that management and owners make symbolic gestures while labour cost remains untouched. We need **real** contributions from the workforce side — and they must be big enough to matter.

So my position is this:
- We are prepared to discuss a negotiated settlement.
- We are prepared to combine different measures.
- But we will **not** accept a deal that preserves the current overcapacity and cost structure.

If the employee side is willing to engage on a package that delivers **substantial, durable savings**, then we can talk about guarantees, transition measures, and the social design of restructuring. If not, then we are heading into a confrontation that management did not choose lightly, but cannot avoid if the company is to remain competitive.

You want to avoid closures; I understand that.  
I need to avoid a permanently uncompetitive company.  
That is the real issue in front of us.
