I appreciate the clarity from management, the works council, the union, and the regional state. Let me be equally clear from the perspective of the controlling family owners.

We are **not** interested in preserving the status quo for its own sake. We are also **not** interested in a headline-grabbing restructuring that damages the company’s long-term value and then leaves the owners and employees with a weakened industrial base. Our focus is simple: **restore competitiveness, protect the core franchise, and do so in a way that is durable and governable**.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on the central diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high,
- the capacity situation is not sustainable,
- and the company needs a restructuring that actually changes the economics.

I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state on one important point:
- **a destructive, one-sided restructuring would be a mistake**.

If this turns into a prolonged conflict, it will erode value, distract management, weaken customer confidence, and damage the brand. That would be a failure of stewardship.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support a blanket rule that says:
- no plant closures ever,
- no compulsory redundancies ever,
- and no meaningful structural change.

That position is not credible if the business has genuine overcapacity. It may be politically attractive, but it does not answer the competitiveness problem. If the company cannot adapt its footprint and cost structure, then we are simply postponing a bigger problem.

At the same time, I also do **not** support management treating closures as the default or only serious option. That is too blunt, and it risks destroying value through conflict and loss of operating capability.

### What matters to us
What would move us is a **credible, durable restructuring package** with four elements:

1. **Real structural savings**
   - Not symbolic.
   - Not temporary accounting fixes.
   - The economics must improve in a way that lasts.

2. **Industrial and labour peace**
   - A package that reduces the risk of permanent confrontation is more valuable than a pure hard-power approach.
   - We need a settlement the company can actually implement.

3. **Protection of the company’s long-term franchise**
   - We will not support measures that permanently impair the enterprise’s value or undermine its ability to invest in future technologies.

4. **Governance stability**
   - The existing ownership and co-determination architecture is part of this company’s stability.
   - Any solution that attempts to sideline control or provoke a governance crisis is unacceptable.

### On the employee side
I understand the refusal of compulsory redundancies and the concern about closures. But I need to be direct: **if the only answer is “nothing fundamental changes,” then there is no basis for a settlement**.

If the workforce wants us to support a site-preserving solution, then it must come with **material contributions**:
- capacity flexibility,
- working-time arrangements,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary departure mechanisms,
- and, if necessary, temporary labour-cost concessions.

Those contributions must be real enough to restore margins. Otherwise they are not a solution, just delay.

### On management
Management should not mistake support for a restructuring mandate as support for any specific instrument. If plant closures are the only lever it can imagine, then it has not done enough to present alternatives. The burden is on management to show that it has tested:
- site consolidation,
- product reallocation,
- shift and capacity adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- and other non-destructive measures.

But I am equally clear that if those alternatives do not close the gap, then hard decisions cannot be ruled out in principle.

### My bottom line
The controlling owners will support:
- a **serious restructuring**,
- a **durable improvement in margins and competitiveness**,
- and a **negotiated solution that preserves labour peace if it is economically real**.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a permanent cost problem dressed up as stability,
- or a conflict strategy that weakens the company’s long-term value.

So my position is this:
- **No blank cheque for closures.**
- **No veto on all structural change.**
- **Yes to a credible package that restores competitiveness while avoiding unnecessary destruction of value.**

If the parties want our backing, bring forward a solution that is economically sufficient, socially manageable, and stable enough to hold. Without that, there is no reason for the ownership side to endorse the restructuring path.
