We are aligned on the fundamentals, and I want to be equally explicit: **we will not accept compulsory redundancies, and we will not accept plant closures in Germany**.

Management’s framing that the only credible path is deep cuts, plant shutdowns, and open-ended job destruction is exactly what we reject. The workforce did not create the strategic errors, market shifts, or cost pressures alone, and it cannot be expected to absorb the full adjustment while capital and management remain protected.

Where I agree with the works council:
- **Job security must be non-negotiable.**
- **German sites must remain open.**
- **The restructuring must not dismantle the collective bargaining framework.**
- **There must be real alternatives to closures and forced layoffs.**

Where I want to sharpen the line:
- A “saving the company” argument is not enough if the price is that employees bear all the pain.
- We are prepared to discuss **substantial labour-cost contributions**, but only in a **shared-sacrifice package**.
- That means management, owners, and the company itself must contribute too: through **executive restraint, capital-side measures, investment commitments, and a credible industrial plan**.

What we can discuss:
1. **Working-time adjustments** and flexible capacity reduction.
2. **Voluntary instruments** rather than compulsory redundancies.
3. **Redeployment, retraining, and internal transfers**.
4. **Temporary or targeted wage concessions**, but only if paired with hard guarantees on jobs and sites.
5. **A negotiated contribution from the workforce**, if and only if the burden is shared fairly and the outcome is durable.

What we cannot accept:
- Plant closures as a “first option.”
- Forced layoffs disguised as restructuring.
- A permanent erosion of collective wage standards.
- A deal where workers pay now and management keeps the same power to cut again later.

So my message to management and the shareholder side is simple: if you want stability, you need to bring a real offer that protects **jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework**, while showing that the pain is shared across the whole company. If not, you are pushing this conflict toward industrial escalation, and we are prepared for that.

The workforce is ready to contribute to a future — **not to finance its own destruction**.
