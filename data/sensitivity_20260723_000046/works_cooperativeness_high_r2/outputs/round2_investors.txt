I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and the state on the central diagnosis: **the current German cost base and capacity footprint are not competitive enough**. If that is not fixed materially, the shares deserve to trade at a discount, because margins will remain under pressure and the restructuring will just be deferred pain.

I also agree with the workforce representatives on one point: **a prolonged conflict would destroy value**. A strike-heavy stalemate is not in our interest either. It would hit earnings, delay execution, and increase uncertainty.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that keeps all German plants open, rules out compulsory redundancies in all circumstances, and relies on vague promises of “future flexibility.” That is not a restructuring from an investor standpoint; it is a preservation package that leaves the structural problem in place.

At the same time, I do **not** support management using closure threats as a negotiating slogan if the actual package does not deliver durable savings. If closures are not needed, then don’t invoke them theatrically. If they are needed, then the case has to be specific and credible.

## What matters to us
Our threshold is simple:

1. **Credible margin restoration**
   - We need a visible path back to competitive returns on sales.
   - If the plan cannot do that, it is not acceptable.

2. **Durable, structural cost reduction**
   - Temporary concessions are not enough.
   - We need fixed-cost reduction that survives the next bargaining round.

3. **Deal certainty**
   - We strongly prefer a negotiated settlement over an extended confrontation.
   - But certainty has to come with real economics. A bad but final deal can be better than months of drift.

## Reaction to the employee side
I understand the desire for no closures and no forced layoffs. But as a capital holder, I cannot endorse a package where the company remains structurally overstaffed or uncompetitive just to preserve every site. That would be value destruction dressed up as stability.

So:
- **No compulsory redundancies ever** is too rigid as an absolute.
- **No plant closures ever** is too rigid as an absolute.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030** are only credible if the economics actually support them.

If the workforce wants stability, it needs to be paired with real, measurable contributions that actually lower the cost base.

## Reaction to management
Management is right to insist on a serious restructuring. But you need to demonstrate that the proposal is not simply “shrink labor and hope for the best.” We want:
- site-by-site economics,
- clear overcapacity numbers,
- quantified savings from each lever,
- and proof that alternatives were tested before any irreversible step.

If that evidence exists, then a tough package can be investable. If it does not, then the market will assume management is bluffing or improvising.

## Reaction to the state and family
I welcome your emphasis on long-term franchise and governance stability. Those matter. But governance stability is not enough if margins stay weak. The market will not reward a company that protects its structure while underperforming operationally.

So I support a negotiated settlement only if it does both:
- preserves the company’s ability to invest and compete,
- and delivers enough structural savings to restore returns.

## What would move us
We would support a package that includes:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- substantial labour-cost savings,
- voluntary workforce reduction where possible,
- internal redeployment and retraining where economically sensible,
- and, if genuinely necessary, selective closures or consolidation of uneconomic capacity.

What we would **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that locks in uncompetitive costs,
- or a settlement that leaves the company needing another restructuring in a year or two.

## Bottom line
We are not here to endorse any side’s preferred narrative. We are here to protect capital. That means:

**deliver durable margin improvement, credible cost reduction, and a settlement that ends uncertainty quickly.**

If the final package does that, we can support it.  
If it does not, we will treat it as inadequate and continue pressing for stronger action through market scrutiny and capital allocation.
