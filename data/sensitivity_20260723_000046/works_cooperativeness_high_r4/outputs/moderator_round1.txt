### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides acknowledge the company is in a **serious structural crisis** and that **symbolic or cosmetic measures are insufficient**.
- There is broad willingness to discuss a **negotiated restructuring** rather than immediate confrontation.
- Several parties accept that the package should include some mix of:
  - **capacity adjustments**
  - **working-time flexibility**
  - **voluntary exit instruments**
  - **internal redeployment / efficiency measures**
  - **investment commitments for future products and technology in Germany**
- There is also shared recognition that **industrial peace, legitimacy, and long-term competitiveness** matter.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and regional state reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management and capital-side actors insist closures must remain possible if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour-side and state actors reject them.
  - Management and investors will not rule them out categorically.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030**
  - Labour and state want binding guarantees.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say blanket guarantees are not credible if they block restructuring.
- **Scope of co-determination / governance role**
  - Labour and state want a strong role in shaping restructuring.
  - Management wants operational room to execute change.
  - Investors want governance that enables decisive action.
- **How burden is shared**
  - Labour insists sacrifices must be reciprocal and time-limited.
  - Investors focus on measurable cost reduction and reject deals that preserve an uncompetitive structure.
  - Management says the current cost base must come down materially.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Job security vs. economic adjustment**: how much employment and site protection can be guaranteed without undermining required savings.
- **Voluntary measures vs. enforceable restructuring**: whether voluntary exits, working-time changes, and internal moves can produce enough reduction on their own.
- **Short-term labour contribution vs. long-term commitments**: what workforce concessions would be matched by binding investment and employment commitments.
- **Regional legitimacy vs. financial performance**: how to preserve the company’s social and political anchor in Germany while restoring margins.
- **Sequence and credibility**: what measures would be front-loaded, what would be conditional, and how success would be verified.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **concrete package design**:
  - quantify the size of the cost gap,
  - identify which savings can come from voluntary/internal measures,
  - specify what binding guarantees are feasible,
  - and define what remains unresolved if closures or redundancies stay on the table.
- Ask each side to state:
  1. the **minimum acceptable package**,
  2. the **non-negotiable condition** they would need in return,
  3. and the **evidence** they require that a proposed alternative is economically sufficient.
