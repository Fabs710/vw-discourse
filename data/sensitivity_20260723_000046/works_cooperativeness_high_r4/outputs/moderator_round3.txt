## Moderator readout: where the impasse lies

The core impasse is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **whether restructuring can be made credible without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

Specifically, the parties are stuck on three linked questions:

1. **Burden allocation**
   - Labour side: insists workers must not bear the main burden through closures and forced layoffs.
   - Management / owners / capital: insist the company will not reach required competitiveness without stronger footprint reduction.

2. **Evidence threshold**
   - Labour side wants plant-level proof that non-closure measures cannot work.
   - Management / capital side say the burden is on labour to show an economically equivalent alternative.

3. **Scope of guarantees**
   - Labour and the state want binding site and job guarantees.
   - Management and investors want flexibility to adjust if the package underdelivers.

So the deadlock is: **guarantees versus adjustment power**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal A: Two-phase hybrid with protected first phase
A time-bound hybrid package could be structured in two phases:

- **Phase 1, binding and protected**
  - no German plant closures
  - no compulsory redundancies
  - voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, shift reductions, and productivity measures
  - binding investment and product commitments
  - plant-level milestones and transparent reporting

- **Phase 2, only if strict economic triggers are met**
  - any further adjustment would require an independent verification step
  - triggers would need to be objective, pre-agreed, and based on quantified underdelivery

This bridges labour’s demand for immediate protection with management’s demand for a credible backstop.

---

### Proposal B: No-closure pledge with a narrow, formula-based contingency clause
A stronger no-closure commitment could be paired with a tightly limited contingency mechanism:

- default rule: no plant closures and no forced redundancies through 2030
- exception: only if an independent plant-level review shows a defined, material shortfall after all agreed non-closure measures are implemented
- any exception must be:
  - objective
  - formula-based
  - time-limited
  - subject to co-determined review

This is designed to reduce the “backdoor closure” fear while preserving a narrow safeguard for extreme underdelivery.

---

### Proposal C: Plant-by-plant equivalence test as a decision gate
Before any closure decision is activated, an independent modeller would compare, site by site:

- current utilization
- fixed-cost absorption
- run-rate savings from non-closure measures
- one-time costs
- timing to savings
- margin and return-on-sales impact

The practical compromise would be:

- if a site can be restructured to target without closure, closure is off the table for that site
- if not, the parties must then decide whether to deepen non-closure measures or accept a narrowly justified footprint change

This keeps the debate evidence-based rather than positional.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council / Betriebsrat
**Pointed question:**  
If an independent plant-level model shows that one or two sites still leave a material cost gap even after voluntary exits, shift changes, and redeployment, what additional non-closure measures would you accept before concluding that the package has failed?

### 2) Industrial Union
**Pointed question:**  
Would you accept a time-limited employee contribution if it were explicitly tied to a binding no-closure period, measurable investment commitments, and a verified plant-by-plant savings test?

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**Pointed question:**  
Will you commit to publishing a plant-by-plant comparison of closure-free, hybrid, and closure-led options, with assumptions, timing, and savings clearly shown, before insisting that closures remain necessary?

### 4) Regional State Government
**Pointed question:**  
If the numbers show that a hybrid package can materially close the gap without closures, are you prepared to publicly support that route and use your influence to anchor it against future reopening of the closure issue?

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**Pointed question:**  
What specific, quantified cost and capacity targets would persuade you that a closure-avoiding hybrid is economically adequate enough to support, rather than insisting on closure flexibility?

### 6) Institutional Investors
**Pointed question:**  
If a hybrid package produces verified run-rate savings and improved margins, but not the maximum possible footprint reduction, would you support it as long as it meets return-on-sales and execution milestones?

### 7) Independent Expert
**Pointed question:**  
What minimum level of permanent run-rate savings, relative to the current gap, would you regard as necessary for a closure-free or hybrid package to be credible rather than merely deferential?

## Procedural next step suggestion
A workable next round would be to ask the independent modeller to return with:

- a **site-by-site savings table**
- a **three-scenario comparison**
- and a **trigger framework** for any contingency clause

That would allow the dispute to move from principle to verifiable trade-offs.
