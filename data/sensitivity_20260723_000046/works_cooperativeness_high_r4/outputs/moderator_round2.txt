### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between:

1. **Labour/state side**: insists on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding site/job guarantees** as preconditions.
2. **Management/investor/family side**: insists that **closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available** unless a closure-free alternative is proven economically equivalent.

So the dispute is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **which instruments may be used**, and **who must carry the risk if the non-closure package falls short**.

A second layer of impasse is **credibility**:
- Labour says management has not proven that closures are necessary.
- Management says labour has not produced a quantified alternative that closes the gap.
- The state wants a solution that preserves regional industrial substance, but has not yet defined what level of adjustment it would accept.
- Investors want speed and certainty, but not a symbolic deal.
- The family holding wants value protection and governance stability, but not open-ended conflict.

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options for discussion.

#### Option 1: Time-bound hybrid with review gates
A negotiated package that:
- removes selected shifts, consolidates volume, or reallocates models across plants,
- includes voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time measures,
- includes a time-limited employee contribution,
- and contains **hard review gates** at set milestones.

**Structure**
- No immediate plant closures.
- No compulsory redundancies during the first phase.
- If quantified savings and utilisation targets are missed by a defined review date, then a second-phase footprint decision becomes negotiable.

**Purpose**
- Gives labour security in the short term.
- Gives management/investors a credible escalation mechanism if savings do not materialise.

---

#### Option 2: No-closure pledge with bounded contingency clause
A package that includes:
- a formal no-closure commitment for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that same period,
- but a pre-agreed contingency mechanism if the company suffers a defined adverse deviation in market demand, margin, or cost performance.

**Structure**
- The contingency clause is narrow, objective, and formula-based.
- Triggers are financial and operational, not discretionary.
- Any activated fallback would first require renewed negotiation and co-determination review.

**Purpose**
- Preserves the works council’s core demand while giving management and capital markets a protected exit if assumptions prove false.

---

#### Option 3: Plant-by-plant equivalence test
Before any decision on closures, the parties jointly commission an independent plant-level comparison of:
- fixed-cost absorption,
- utilisation,
- conversion cost,
- savings from shift reductions,
- savings from voluntary exits,
- and the one-time cost of closure.

**Structure**
- Each site is assessed against a common threshold.
- Closure is allowed only if the independent model shows that non-closure measures cannot achieve economic equivalence at that site.
- The result is reviewed jointly by management, labour, state, and the family holding.

**Purpose**
- Converts the argument from principle into site-specific evidence.
- May allow some plants to be protected while identifying any outlier site where a harder adjustment is unavoidable.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council / Betriebsrat
**Question:**  
What specific, quantified savings package would you accept as sufficient to replace closures and compulsory redundancies, and by what date would you be prepared to verify whether it is actually delivering those savings?

#### Industrial Union
**Question:**  
If management accepts no closures in the first phase, what binding employee contribution and productivity commitment can the union guarantee so that the package is not simply a delay of the restructuring?

#### Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Will management commit to a quantified comparison of closure-led, hybrid, and closure-free options, including one-time costs, run-rate savings, and timing, before insisting that closures remain necessary?

#### Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What exact level of site rationalisation, if any, would you consider politically and economically acceptable while still preserving the state’s regional industrial role?

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What minimum economic threshold would lead you to support a closure-free or hybrid settlement, and would you accept a time-limited no-closure period if it were backed by enforceable fallback triggers?

#### Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What quantified savings, timing, and margin-improvement milestones would be sufficient for you to support a hybrid or closure-free package instead of insisting on immediate footprint reduction?
