I appreciate the specialist input, but I need to be equally clear: **the scenario tables do not justify making German plant closures the default answer**.

What they do show is this:
- a **purely cosmetic** deal would fail,
- a **closure-led** solution is economically powerful but highly disruptive,
- and a **hybrid** or strong **closure-free** package may well be sufficient if the savings are quantified and binding.

That is exactly why I cannot accept management’s attempt to keep closures as a standing threat while the burden of proof remains on everyone else.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not symbolic politics**. The cost gap must be closed. Overcapacity cannot simply be wished away. The company must remain investable and competitive.

I also agree with the expert that:
- non-closure measures only work if they are **broad, quantified, and enforceable**,
- and a hybrid package is often the most realistic compromise.

That said, “real restructuring” does **not** automatically mean plant closures and forced redundancies.

## Where I disagree strongly
I disagree with management and the capital side on the idea that:
- closures must remain on the table as a matter of principle,
- and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out.

That is too blunt for a company of this importance, with this governance structure, and this regional responsibility.

From my perspective, the test is not whether a closure would save money in isolation. The test is whether the company can achieve a **durable, competitive package without destroying regional industrial substance**.

If the answer is yes, then closures are not necessary and should not be used as leverage.
If the answer is no, then management has not yet done the work of proving alternatives.

## On the works council and union
I support their insistence that workers should not be the sole shock absorber. That is entirely justified.

I also support the demand for:
- no forced redundancies,
- strong site protection,
- investment commitments in Germany,
- and a fair employee contribution that is time-limited and reciprocated.

But I want to be precise: I will not support a package that is just a political slogan. If labour is asking for no closures and no compulsory redundancies, then the counter-package must be **hard-nosed and measurable**. It must actually close the gap.

That means:
- capacity reductions through shifts and line rationalisation,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity gains,
- and a real labour-cost contribution.

## On the expert’s hybrid scenario
The hybrid scenario is the only one in the room that sounds remotely capable of balancing:
- competitiveness,
- industrial peace,
- and preservation of regional capacity.

That does **not** mean I am endorsing any hybrid package automatically. It means I am willing to consider one if it contains the right safeguards.

For the state, the acceptable direction is a package that:
1. **preserves German plants where economically defensible**
2. **avoids forced redundancies**
3. **delivers measurable savings**
4. **protects our co-determination and veto role**
5. **commits to investment and future products in Germany**

Anything less is not acceptable.

## What I would require
A serious alternative to closures must include:
- a quantified savings plan,
- transparent plant-by-plant capacity use,
- binding employment and site guarantees for a defined period,
- voluntary rather than compulsory workforce reduction,
- and a reciprocal contribution from owners and management.

If management cannot show that a non-closure or hybrid package is economically sufficient, then it is not yet entitled to ask for irreversible decisions. The burden of proof lies with those proposing to cut deeply into jobs and sites.

## My bottom line
I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** that protects jobs and plants in Germany while restoring competitiveness.
I am **not** prepared to support:
- a closure-led strategy as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as a normal tool,
- or any deal that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

So my position is:
- **Yes** to a quantified hybrid or closure-free package if it genuinely closes the gap.
- **No** to plant closures as the default path.
- **No** to forced layoffs.
- **No** to sidelining the regional state or hollowing out regional industrial capacity.

If needed, I would welcome a tighter comparison of the hybrid scenario against a strengthened closure-free package, with explicit assumptions on job numbers, site utilisation, and cash effects. But I will not move on the core principle that this company’s German anchor role must be preserved.
