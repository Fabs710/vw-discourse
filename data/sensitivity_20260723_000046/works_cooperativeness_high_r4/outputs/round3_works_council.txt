I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **this is a real structural crisis**, and nobody can solve it with slogans.

I also agree that:
- a **quantified** package is needed,
- **industrial peace** matters,
- and we need **investment in future products and technology in Germany**.

I even accept the expert’s basic conclusion: non-closure measures can contribute meaningfully, but they must be broad and credible. Fine. We are not denying reality.

## Where I disagree sharply
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to normalize **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** as if they were ordinary management tools.

They are not ordinary to us. They are the destruction of jobs, sites, families, and trust.

So let me restate the line very clearly:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No erosion of the historic job-security framework**
- **No unilateral restructuring backed by threats**

If that sounds inflexible, it is because the workforce is being asked to bear the consequences of a strategy failure it did not create.

## To management
You say closures must remain on the table unless an alternative is proven equivalent. But you still have not proven that.

What I have heard is:
- “we need structural change,” yes;
- “we need durable savings,” yes;
- but then immediately: “therefore closures must remain possible.”

That is not proof. That is preference.

If you want us to accept deeper concessions, then show the numbers:
- plant by plant,
- site by site,
- with transparent assumptions,
- and with a real comparison of alternatives.

Until then, your closure threat is just leverage.

## To investors and the family holding
You keep saying the closure-free path may underdeliver. That may be true in the abstract. But you are ignoring the full cost of your preferred path:
- strikes,
- loss of trust,
- political backlash,
- damaged execution,
- and a long-term collapse of cooperation in a co-determined company.

That cost is not external. It belongs in the calculation.

If you insist on treating the workforce as the easiest variable to cut, then do not be surprised if the workforce mobilizes all legal and industrial means to resist.

## To the regional state
I appreciate that you do not want German industrial substance destroyed. But I need more than sympathy.

If you truly want to preserve the regional base, then you should support:
1. **all German plants remaining open**
2. **no forced layoffs**
3. **binding investment and product commitments**
4. **a real co-determination role in the restructuring**
5. **public backing for a negotiated alternative to closures**

Anything less leaves workers carrying the risk while others claim political concern.

## On the expert’s scenarios
The scenario comparison was useful, but I do not accept the conclusion that a hybrid path automatically requires footprint reduction in the form of closures or a “harder adjustment.”

A real hybrid can mean:
- capacity reduction through shifts,
- line rationalisation,
- model allocation changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- and a fair, time-limited labor contribution.

That is the kind of package I am willing to discuss.

What I am **not** willing to discuss is a “hybrid” that is just closure language dressed up more politely.

## What would move us
I will be direct: the only basis on which labour can consider a serious settlement is this:

### 1. Binding site guarantee
All German plants remain open through 2030.

### 2. No compulsory redundancies
Workforce reduction only through voluntary instruments, retirement, redeployment, and negotiated solutions.

### 3. Real co-determination
The works council and employee representatives are involved in shaping the restructuring, not just informed after the fact.

### 4. Reciprocal sacrifice
If employees contribute, then management and owners must also contribute materially.

### 5. Investment and product commitments
German sites must receive concrete future work, not vague promises.

If those five points are not on the table, then there is no settlement from our side.

## On the facilitator’s options
### Option 1: Time-bound hybrid with review gates
This is the only option I see as remotely discussable — **if** the first phase contains:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and binding guarantees.

But I will not accept a setup where “review gates” are just a trapdoor to closures later. If you want review gates, they must review delivery of the agreed package, not reopen our red lines by default.

### Option 2: No-closure pledge with bounded contingency clause
This only works if the contingency is extremely narrow, objective, and does **not** function as a backdoor for closures and layoffs.

My problem is simple: once you insert a trigger that allows closures later, the guarantee is weakened from day one.

### Option 3: Plant-by-plant equivalence test
This is better than abstract threats, and I support transparency. But the burden should be on those proposing closures to prove necessity.

And I want to be absolutely clear: an “equivalence test” does **not** mean every site is up for liquidation if it fails some spreadsheet threshold. It means management must first prove that non-closure measures cannot work.

## My minimum acceptable package
Since the moderator asked for concrete terms, here they are:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **Working-time and capacity adjustments**
- **Voluntary exits only**
- **Internal redeployment**
- **Temporary and negotiated employee contribution**
- **Investment commitments for German future products**
- **Joint design under co-determination**

That is the minimum acceptable package. Anything weaker is not a real solution; it is an attempt to shift the burden onto the workforce.

## What I require as evidence
If management wants to claim the package is insufficient, then I require:
- a transparent cost-gap calculation,
- a plant-level savings comparison,
- a quantified estimate of what voluntary and internal measures achieve,
- and proof that those measures genuinely cannot close the gap.

Not assertions. Not pressure. Proof.

It does **not**.

If management, owners, investors, and the state want a durable solution, they must build it **without closing German plants and without compulsory redundancies**. That is the line.
