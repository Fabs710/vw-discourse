Let me respond very clearly.

I **do** agree with one point from both of you: this crisis cannot be managed with cosmetic measures. We need a restructuring that is **durable**, not a temporary patch. And yes, the company must continue to invest in future products and technology in Germany.

But I have to be equally clear on the central issue: **your red lines, as stated, do not solve the cost problem we are facing.**

### Where I agree
- **A fair negotiated solution is preferable to escalation.**
- **Voluntary instruments should be part of the package where possible.**
- **Investment in future products matters** and is part of any credible agreement.
- I accept that labour should not be asked to carry the entire burden alone.

### Where I disagree
What I cannot accept is the demand for **absolute guarantees of all German plants remaining open through 2030** and **no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances**.

That position ignores the reality we are facing:
- German factory costs are **far above target**
- The European market is shrinking
- Competition is intensifying
- Our return-on-sales target is not being met
- We need funding for the transition to 2030

If we lock ourselves into preserving every site and every structure exactly as it is, then we will simply preserve an **uncompetitive cost base**. That is not a responsible outcome for the company, its owners, or its long-term industrial future.

### My position
I am prepared to discuss a package that achieves **real structural savings**. That can include:
- capacity reductions,
- shifts in production,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- labor-cost contributions,
- and, where necessary, plant-level changes.

But I need to be blunt: **I will not sign up to a deal that rules out closures or deep structural change in advance.**

### On job guarantees
A blanket job guarantee to 2030 is not credible if it prevents the restructuring needed to make the business viable. If there is to be security, it must be earned through a package that actually delivers:
- lower fixed costs,
- lower overcapacity,
- and a realistic path back to competitiveness.

### On industrial action
Prolonged strikes will damage the company, hurt customers, and undermine trust with investors and owners. They will not change the underlying economics. If anything, they make it more obvious why we need a structural solution now, not later.

### What I am prepared to consider
If the employee side is willing to engage on a package that delivers:
1. **material and durable cost reduction**,
2. **meaningful capacity adjustment**,
3. **a real labour-cost contribution**,
4. **and a credible investment commitment for the future**,

then I am prepared to talk about how those measures are sequenced and how workforce impact is managed.

But I will not pretend that the current structure is sustainable just because it is familiar. It is not.

So my message is simple: **we need a restructuring that changes the economics, not one that protects every legacy structure and calls it compromise.**
