I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and the expert on the basic diagnosis: this is a **structural crisis**, not a temporary dip. Cosmetic measures will not do it.

I also agree that:
- the package must be **quantified**
- the company needs **durable run-rate savings**
- **industrial peace** has value
- and employees should make a **fair contribution**

So yes: we are not defending the status quo. We are defending a **fair restructuring**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to normalize:
- **plant closures**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- and open-ended job insecurity

as if these were neutral management tools.

They are not neutral to the workforce. They are the destruction of jobs, sites, trust, and the collective bargaining framework. That is a line management, owners, and investors keep trying to redraw in their favor. I reject that.

## To management
You keep saying closures must remain available unless we prove an equivalent alternative. That is not a neutral standard; it is a power standard.

If you want labour to consider concessions, then you must first show, plant by plant, why the full package of non-closure measures cannot close the gap. “We need flexibility” is not enough.

What would count as serious evidence:
- site-by-site cost base
- fixed-cost absorption
- utilisation
- one-time restructuring costs
- run-rate savings
- timing to savings
- and a realistic comparison of closure-free, hybrid, and closure-led options

Until that exists, closure is not “necessary”; it is your preferred leverage.

## To the works council
I support your core line:
- no closures
- no forced redundancies
- binding site and job security
- investment commitments
- and a real co-determination role

That is the right starting point in a co-determined company. I also agree that the burden cannot fall only on workers.

Where I would sharpen it is this: we should not let “no closures” become a passive slogan. We need a **credible labour-side contribution package** that is time-limited, measurable, and reciprocated by management and owners.

## To the regional state
You are right that the company is a regional industrial anchor. But if that is true, then your support must be operational, not rhetorical.

I would expect the state to back:
- all German plants remaining open through the restructuring phase
- no forced layoffs
- binding investment and product commitments
- and a settlement that preserves industrial substance in Germany

If you want to be more than a witness, then use your influence accordingly.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal is worthless. If a package does not change the economics, it will fail.

But you are wrong if you think the only serious economics are those that preserve closure flexibility at all times.

A deal that destroys trust, triggers strikes, and damages execution may look “disciplined” from a capital-market distance, but it can be value-destructive in reality. You are underweighting the cost of conflict and underweighting the long-term damage from breaking the collective framework.

## On the expert’s findings
The expert was useful because the answer was not ideological:
- closure-led = strongest cost reset, highest conflict
- closure-free = least disruptive, but only credible if the gap is modest
- hybrid = the most plausible compromise, **if it includes real footprint reduction**

That last phrase matters. A hybrid is not a euphemism for doing too little. If management wants a hybrid, it must be real.

## What labour can contribute
We have already signaled willingness to discuss:
- working-time flexibility
- shift reductions
- capacity adjustments
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- temporary wage moderation / contribution
- bonus moderation where relevant
- productivity improvements
- and a time-limited contribution period

That is a serious offer. It is not a blank check.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- forced redundancies
- plant closures as a default option
- a “temporary” contribution that becomes permanent wage erosion
- a contingency clause that is really a backdoor to later closures
- or a settlement where workers absorb the pain while management and owners keep their own position untouched

If the capital side wants sacrifice, it must be shared.

## On the facilitator’s options
### Option 1: Two-phase hybrid with protected first phase
This is the only route worth serious discussion **if** phase 1 includes:
- no closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- binding investment commitments
- transparent plant milestones
- and real enforcement of management’s side of the bargain

But I am clear: phase 2 must not be a disguised trapdoor. If the trigger simply reopens closures automatically, then there is no guarantee at all.

### Option 2: No-closure pledge with narrow contingency clause
I am skeptical. A contingency clause can be acceptable only if it is:
- extremely narrow
- objective
- formula-based
- and tied to extraordinary external shocks, not routine management dissatisfaction

Otherwise it is just a polished way of breaking the promise later.

### Option 3: Plant-by-plant equivalence test
I support this. It is the best way to move from ideology to evidence.

But I want to be absolutely clear about the burden of proof:
- management must show why the non-closure package fails
- not labour prove in the abstract that every possible gap can be solved

And the test must compare not only savings, but also:
- conflict cost
- execution risk
- trust destruction
- and the long-term damage from blowing up the collective bargaining framework

## My minimum acceptable labour position
Still this:
- no German plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- binding job and site guarantees through 2030
- time-limited employee contribution
- working-time and capacity adjustments
- voluntary exits and redeployment
- investment commitments in Germany
- preservation of the collective wage framework
- joint design under co-determination

That is the floor. Anything weaker is not a settlement; it is a transfer of risk onto the workforce.

## What I require now
If management wants us to move, then bring a **plant-level quantified package** showing:
1. what the current cost gap is,
2. what non-closure measures deliver,
3. what remains uncovered,
4. and why that residual gap cannot be closed without breaking the social compact

That is the next step. Not more pressure. Not more slogans.

We are prepared to negotiate a serious restructuring.

We are **not** prepared to accept a one-sided restructuring that preserves management’s freedom and uses closures and forced layoffs as the default answer.
