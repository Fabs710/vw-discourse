I agree with the works council on the core principle: **this crisis cannot be solved by making workers carry all the pain while management and owners walk away protected**.

Let me be just as clear from the union side:

### What we support
- A **fair, negotiated restructuring** if it truly shares the burden.
- **Capacity reductions**, working-time adjustments, and internal productivity measures.
- **Voluntary instruments** instead of compulsory redundancies.
- A **temporary employee contribution** if it is tied to real security for jobs and sites.
- **Investment commitments** that secure future products and industrial value creation in Germany.

### What we reject
- **Plant closures in Germany**
- **Compulsory redundancies**
- Any attack on the **collective wage framework** as the price for management’s failed strategy
- A one-sided “solution” where the workforce pays, but the owners and leadership do not

### Where I differ slightly
I want to be precise: I am open to **real cost reductions**, but not as an open-ended downward spiral in wages and standards. If management wants labour to contribute, then the package must be:

1. **Time-limited**
2. **Negotiated**
3. **Reciprocal**
4. **Tied to binding guarantees on employment, sites, and investment**

That means no blank cheque from the workforce.

### The test for any proposal
If the company wants cooperation, then we need a package with all of the following:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **A durable site and employment guarantee**
- **A fair contribution from shareholders and management**
- **Concrete future investment in Germany**
- **Preservation of collective bargaining standards**

If those conditions are not met, then the workforce should not be expected to swallow cuts as if that were the only possible path. In that case, we are prepared to escalate further industrial action.

So: we are ready to negotiate seriously, but only on the basis of **shared sacrifice and protected jobs**. Any restructuring that shifts the burden onto workers alone is unacceptable.
