### Core impasse
The central blockage is this:

- **Labour side** demands binding **no-closure / no-forced-redundancy** guarantees and treats plant closures as unacceptable by default.
- **Management / owners / investors** insist that **closure and redundancy flexibility must remain available** unless a package can be shown to close the gap economically.
- **The state** wants to protect regional industrial substance and co-determination, but will not yet accept that closures are necessary.
- **The expert** indicates that a **closure-free package may work only if the gap is moderate and the measures are unusually deep**; if the gap is large, a pure no-closure deal is unlikely to be sufficient.

So the impasse is not over diagnosis; it is over **what degree of footprint adjustment must remain available** and **who bears the burden of proving necessity**.

---

### Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

#### Proposal 1: Phased hybrid with hard first-phase protections
A possible bridge is a **two-phase agreement**:

- **Phase 1**: no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies
- immediate measures:
  - shift reductions
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - working-time adjustments
  - temporary employee contribution
  - overhead and productivity actions
- binding investment and product commitments in Germany
- transparent plant-level milestones and independent verification

**Trigger to Phase 2**:
- only if independently verified savings fall materially short after a defined period
- trigger must be objective, formula-based, and narrowly defined

This may allow labour to secure immediate protection while management retains a strictly bounded review mechanism.

---

#### Proposal 2: No-closure pledge with an extraordinary-shock contingency
A second bridge is a **strong no-closure commitment** paired with a **very narrow contingency clause**:

- no closures and no compulsory redundancies as the default rule
- contingency activated only by:
  - extraordinary external shocks, or
  - independently verified severe underdelivery against agreed savings milestones
- contingency must be:
  - objective
  - formula-based
  - time-limited
  - and not usable for routine restructuring drift

This would test whether labour and the state can accept a promise that is strong but not absolute.

---

#### Proposal 3: Plant-by-plant equivalence test with independent modelling
A third bridge is to suspend final positions and run a **plant-level equivalence test**:

- compare closure-free, hybrid, and closure-led options plant by plant
- include:
  - utilization
  - fixed-cost absorption
  - headcount structure
  - one-time restructuring costs
  - run-rate savings
  - timing to savings
  - industrial conflict / execution risk
  - regional and governance consequences

The purpose is not to decide politics, but to establish whether any **non-closure package can credibly match the economics**. If it can, that strengthens the no-closure case. If it cannot, it clarifies where and why not.

---

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
If an independently verified plant-by-plant model shows that one site cannot be kept open **without missing the required savings target**, would you accept any bounded fallback mechanism, and if so, what exact conditions would make it acceptable?

#### To Industrial Union
What is the maximum time-limited employee contribution package you would endorse, in quantified terms, if it were matched by binding investment and no-closure protections in phase 1?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What precise non-closure measures, with quantified savings and timing, would you commit to before asking for any closure or compulsory redundancy option to remain available?

#### To Regional State Government
Are you prepared to support a phased hybrid only if it includes a narrowly defined contingency clause, or do you require an explicit no-closure commitment as the starting point?

#### To Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a settlement that removes immediate closure rights for phase 1, provided there is an objectively triggered review gate later, or do you require closure flexibility from day one?

#### To Institutional Investors
What minimum measurable run-rate savings, and by when, would make you support a closure-free or hybrid agreement instead of immediate footprint reduction?

#### To the Expert
What threshold of savings gap, in your view, is the practical dividing line between a credible closure-free package and one that is likely insufficient?
