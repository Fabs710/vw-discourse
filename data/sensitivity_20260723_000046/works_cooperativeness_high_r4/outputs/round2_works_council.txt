I will respond directly, because the issues are now concrete.

### First: what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one point: **this company is in a real structural crisis, and cosmetic measures are not enough**. If management has a genuine cost and capacity problem, then we are prepared to discuss serious operational measures.

I also agree that **a prolonged conflict is harmful**. It harms workers, the company, customers, and the broader region. That is exactly why management should stop treating the workforce as the adjustment buffer.

### Where I strongly disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must stay on the table as normal options.

That is not an “economic necessity” in the abstract. It is a choice about who pays for management’s failed strategy.

Let me be very clear:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No demolition of the historic job-security framework**
- **No unilateral restructuring by fait accompli**

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are the boundary of what is acceptable.

### On management’s argument
Management says the cost base is too high and the market is weak. That may be true. But your conclusion does not follow automatically from your diagnosis.

You have not shown that:
- every internal alternative has been exhausted,
- capacity reductions, working-time changes, redeployment, and voluntary exits are insufficient,
- or that closures are the only way to restore competitiveness.

So far, what I hear is: “Accept irreversible damage because we want speed and freedom of action.” That is not a convincing basis for a decision with thousands of jobs at stake.

### On the investors’ position
I understand the demand for measurable savings. But I reject the idea that the only credible savings are those achieved through closures and layoffs.

If investors want “hard financial terms,” then here are hard questions for you:
- What is the quantified savings potential from working-time reductions?
- What can be achieved through internal redeployment and plant utilisation changes?
- What is the value of preserving industrial peace instead of detonating it?
- What is the long-term cost of destroying trust in a co-determined company?

If you ignore these, you are not being rigorous; you are just privileging the most brutal instrument.

### On the state and family owners
I welcome the fact that both of you are resisting a closure-led strategy. That is the right instinct. But I need more than general sympathy.

If you want labour to carry a contribution, then you must support **binding guarantees**:
1. **All German plants remain open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies through 2030**
3. **Investment commitments for future products and industrial value creation in Germany**
4. **A real role for co-determination in designing the restructuring**
5. **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

Without those guarantees, any employee contribution is just a one-way concession.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are still ready to talk about:
- temporary working-time reductions
- voluntary instruments
- internal transfers
- capacity adjustments
- productivity improvements
- negotiated employee contributions

But these must be tied to **credible security**. Not vague promises. Not “we’ll see later.” Not a blank cheque.

### Minimum acceptable package from our side
If there is to be a real negotiation, then the minimum acceptable package is:
- no German plant closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding site and job guarantees to 2030,
- and a restructuring plan that is jointly shaped under co-determination.

If that is not on the table, then there is no real deal — only pressure.

### The test for management
If you claim that closures are unavoidable, then prove it with:
- transparent data on the cost gap,
- a quantified comparison of alternatives,
- and a serious assessment of non-closure options.

Until then, we will continue to resist closures and forced redundancies with all means available, including industrial action and our co-determination rights.
