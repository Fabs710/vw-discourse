From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the baseline is simple: **we need a credible, durable path back to competitive margins and a defensible return-on-sales target.** Everything else is secondary.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, and partly with the family holding, that this cannot be solved with cosmetic measures. The current cost base appears uncompetitive, and if the restructuring does not materially improve margins and capacity utilisation, then it will just push the problem into the future.

We also agree with the point made by the workforce, the union, and the state that a destructive, open-ended conflict is value-destructive. Prolonged strikes, brand damage, delivery disruption, and political escalation all destroy enterprise value. We do not want that.

### Where we disagree
Where we differ sharply is on the idea that the only acceptable frame is “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as a hard red line.

That is not a credible financial answer.

If the company truly has structural overcapacity and a cost base above target, then a package that excludes the possibility of closures or meaningful structural workforce reduction up front is too weak unless it delivers **equivalent, verified savings** through other measures. “Fairness” is not the issue for us; **economic adequacy** is.

So:
- **No** to a cosmetic compromise that preserves an uncompetitive structure.
- **No** to a solution that protects every legacy site while margins remain structurally impaired.
- **No** to paying for industrial peace with shareholder value erosion.

### On management
Management is right to insist that this must be durable. But durability means more than confrontation. If the proposal is simply “accept closures or else,” that may satisfy urgency but it does not automatically produce the best value outcome. Management needs to show that the package is not just harsh, but effective.

If there are alternatives that achieve the same or better cost reduction with less disruption, we would prefer those. But they must be:
1. **quantified**
2. **binding**
3. **front-loaded enough**
4. **credible in execution**

Vague promises of working-time flexibility or voluntary exits are not enough if they do not close the gap.

### On the works council and union
We understand the refusal of compulsory redundancies and plant closures, but from an investor standpoint those positions are only relevant if they are paired with a plan that actually restores competitiveness. Otherwise they function as a veto on adjustment.

A “negotiated employee contribution” is acceptable only if it is:
- material,
- time-limited,
- and genuinely improves the cost structure rather than merely deferring pain.

We would not support a settlement that trades permanent margin impairment for temporary labour peace.

### On the regional state
We appreciate the concern for regional industrial capacity and social legitimacy. But the state cannot expect the market to finance sentiment. If political constraints make economically necessary restructuring impossible, then the result will be prolonged underperformance and further value destruction. That is not a sustainable position for any anchor shareholder.

### On the family holding
We agree with the family holding on one central point: a purely symbolic deal would be unacceptable. But we would push further than the family seems willing to say explicitly: if the business requires closure or sharp structural reduction to restore returns, then that cannot be ruled out simply because it is politically uncomfortable.

The relevant question is not whether a site is historic. It is whether it is economically justified.

### Our bottom line
We will support **only** an outcome that credibly restores competitiveness through:
- material and durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- verifiable labour-cost savings,
- and a clear path back to target margins.

What we will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- an open-ended conflict,
- or a deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

If the employee side and the state want to avoid closures, they need to offer a package that matches the scale of the problem in hard financial terms. Otherwise, from our perspective, the company is being asked to preserve employment by sacrificing shareholder value, and that is not acceptable.
