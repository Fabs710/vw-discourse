We are in a crisis, but let me be absolutely clear from the outset: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

The workforce has already shown willingness to carry a fair share of the burden. What we will **not** accept is that management turns a structural challenge into a one-sided attack on jobs, sites, and the historic protections that have helped build this company.

### Our core priorities are:
1. **All German plants must remain open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job guarantees and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A genuine co-determination role for the works council in shaping any restructuring.**
5. **A fair, negotiated solution that distributes sacrifice across all sides, not just labour.**

### What we are prepared to discuss:
- Temporary or negotiated capacity adjustments
- Working-time solutions
- Voluntary instruments instead of compulsory redundancies
- Internal efficiency gains
- A fair employee contribution if, and only if, the company provides binding long-term security for sites and jobs
- Investment commitments for future products and technology in Germany

### What we reject:
- Closing any German plant
- Relocating production out of Germany at the expense of existing sites
- Breaking the historic job-security framework
- Using the crisis as a pretext for unilateral cost-cutting on the backs of employees

Management cannot demand loyalty while discarding trust. If the company wants the workforce to contribute, then it must first offer **credible guarantees** and a restructuring path that protects industrial capacity in Germany. Otherwise, resistance will continue — including industrial action and using all co-determination rights available to us.
