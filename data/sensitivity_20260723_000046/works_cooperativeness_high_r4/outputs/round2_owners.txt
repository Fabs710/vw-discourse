I will be direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the investors, and the expert on one essential point: **this company has a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary one**. A cosmetic package would simply postpone the pain and erode value further. We also need to avoid a destabilising conflict that damages the brand, customers, and long-term industrial capability.

I also agree with the state and labour that **legitimacy matters**. If we break labour peace completely, the company pays for it in execution risk, reputation, and lost time. That is not in the family holding’s interest.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the works council’s and union’s attempt to make:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **all German plants open through 2030**

into hard prior conditions.

That is not a restructuring framework. It is a veto on necessary adjustment.

Nor do I accept the state’s implication that because closures are disruptive, they should therefore be off-limits. Disruption is not the point; **economic adequacy is**. If the cost gap is real and durable, then some footprint and workforce reduction must remain possible unless an alternative package is truly equivalent.

### On the expert’s scenario work
The scenario comparison is useful, and it supports a conclusion I think some here are trying to avoid:

- **Closure-led** restructuring is the strongest financial reset.
- **Closure-free** restructuring is the least disruptive, but also the most likely to underdeliver.
- **Hybrid** is the most plausible compromise, but only if it includes **real capacity removal** and not just softer language around the same problem.

That is the key point. If “hybrid” means preserving all sites while shifting hours and hoping for productivity gains, then it is just closure-free restructuring under another name. That is not enough if the gap is structural.

### What I would support
I am prepared to back a package only if it is **credible on economics** and **stable in governance**. That means:

1. **Material, durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity adjustment**
3. **Voluntary exits and redeployment as first instruments where feasible**
4. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**
5. **Binding investment commitments in Germany**
6. **A clear path to restored margins**
7. **No outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that preserves every legacy structure and calls it compromise,
- a package that relies on permanent margin sacrifice to buy short-term peace,
- or a governance arrangement where the owners are forced into accepting value erosion in the name of political comfort.

I also will not support any outcome that weakens the company’s governance control or turns restructuring into a permanent transfer of veto power away from those responsible for the capital.

### On closures and layoffs
I am not asking for closures for their own sake. But I am also not prepared to rule them out in advance.

If management can show that a hybrid or closure-free package **quantifiably** closes the gap, then I would prefer that. But if the numbers do not work, then insisting on no closures and no compulsory redundancies is not stewardship; it is denial.

### My position in one sentence
I support a negotiated restructuring only if it is **economically sufficient, governance-safe, and durable**; otherwise, I would favour a harder footprint adjustment rather than a false peace that destroys value slowly.

### What I would ask for next
Before anyone asks the owners to endorse a final package, I want:
- a **plant-by-plant savings and utilisation comparison**,
- a clear **headcount and cash impact** under the hybrid and closure-free options,
- and a realistic assessment of what employee contribution is actually achievable without forcing the company into under-restructuring.
