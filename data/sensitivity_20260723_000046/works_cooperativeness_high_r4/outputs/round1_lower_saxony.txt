I appreciate that management is saying the problem cannot be solved by symbolism alone. I also hear the workforce side insisting that a restructuring must not become a unilateral assault on jobs. On those two points, there is at least a common recognition that **a durable solution is needed**.

But I need to be equally direct: **I cannot support a restructuring built around German plant closures and forced redundancies as the default answer.** That would cross a line for the regional government, economically and politically.

### Where I agree
- The company does need a **credible long-term path back to competitiveness**.
- A mere postponement of the problem would be irresponsible.
- **Voluntary measures, working-time adjustments, capacity reductions, and negotiated contributions** are legitimate tools.
- Investment in future products and technology in Germany is essential.

### Where I disagree with management
What I cannot accept is the idea that the only credible path is to start by taking German sites off the table for closure.

You say the cost base is too high and the market is under pressure. I do not deny that. But a strategy that simply closes plants or removes large numbers of jobs in a blunt way risks:
- destroying regional industrial capacity,
- undermining social trust in the company,
- triggering avoidable conflict,
- and weakening the very production base that has made this firm an anchor employer.

That is not just a political concern. It is an industrial one. A company that burns through its social capital and regional legitimacy may “save” cost on paper while damaging its future ability to operate with stability.

### Where I agree with the workforce side
The works council and union are right that the burden cannot fall only on employees. If workers are asked to contribute, then the company, owners, and management must also commit to:
- **binding investment in Germany**,
- **credible employment protection**,
- and a restructuring that preserves industrial substance rather than hollowing it out.

I also take seriously the point that the company’s co-determination and regional anchoring are not decorative features. They are part of how this company has historically worked, and they are part of what gives it legitimacy.

### What I will not accept
I do **not** support:
- a plan that guts regional employment,
- a decision process that sidelines the state as a blocking minority shareholder,
- or any restructuring that weakens the co-determination balance and turns the workforce into the sole shock absorber.

Equally, I do not accept a position that says “no changes, no matter what.” The company does need real savings and a more competitive structure. But those savings must be pursued **without destroying sites and without compulsory redundancies as the first instrument**.

### What I would support
I am prepared to back a negotiated package if it includes:
1. **No plant closures in Germany as a starting principle**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity adjustments and working-time solutions**
4. **Voluntary exits and internal redeployment**
5. **A fair employee contribution, time-limited and reciprocated**
6. **Binding investment and product commitments for German sites**
7. **A real role for co-determination and the state in any final package**

That is not “consensus for its own sake.” It is the minimum basis for a solution that preserves the company’s regional anchor role and still addresses competitiveness.

### My bottom line
If management insists that plant closures must remain on the table as the primary lever, then I would view that as premature and unacceptable. The burden of proof is on management to show that every alternative has been exhausted before it asks for irreversible cuts in Germany.

So my position is clear:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **No** to a closure-led strategy.
- **Yes** to negotiated sacrifice.
- **No** to forcing the regional workforce and the state to pay for a strategy failure they did not create.

If we are to move forward, the next step must be a package that protects sites and employment in Germany while restoring competitiveness through negotiated, reversible, and socially sustainable measures.
