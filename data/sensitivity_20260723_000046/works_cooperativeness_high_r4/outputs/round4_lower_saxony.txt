I will speak clearly.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on the diagnosis: this is a **serious structural crisis**, not a temporary dip. Cosmetic measures will not do.

I also agree that:
- the company needs a **real, quantified restructuring**
- **industrial peace** matters
- **investment in future products and technology in Germany** is essential
- a purely confrontational path would damage the company and the region

And I agree with the expert and moderator that the right question is not abstract principle, but **what package actually closes the gap**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the way management, investors, and the family holding keep normalizing **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** as if they were the obvious starting point.

They are not neutral tools in this company. They are the most destructive instruments available for:
- employment in the state,
- regional industrial stability,
- trust in co-determination,
- and the company’s role as an anchor employer.

From my position, closures are not a routine first resort. They are a last-resort contingency, and even then only if a serious alternative has been fully tested.

I also do **not** accept a package that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role or treats it as an obstacle to be bypassed. That role is part of the governance architecture of this company and part of why long-term legitimacy exists.

## To management
You keep saying the burden is on labour to prove an economically equivalent alternative. I reject that framing as one-sided.

Management has the responsibility to show, transparently and plant by plant:
- the actual cost gap,
- the savings from non-closure measures,
- the timing of those savings,
- and the residual gap after all internal measures are counted.

Without that, “closures remain necessary” is not a conclusion; it is a preference.

I am prepared to support a strong restructuring package if it is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **socially balanced**
- and genuinely protects the German industrial base

But I will not support a reflexive turn to closures just because that is the most forceful option on paper.

## To the works council and union
I understand and share your resistance to blanket closures and forced layoffs. That is legitimate.

But I also need to be frank: a position that simply says “no closures, no redundancies” without fully engaging the numbers will not be enough to carry a long-term settlement. If we want to defend sites and jobs, we need a package that is strong enough to survive economic scrutiny.

What I can support from the labour side is:
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- temporary and negotiated employee contributions
- shift and capacity adjustments
- productivity improvements
- and binding commitments on future work in Germany

That is the kind of burden-sharing that can be defended politically and economically.

What I cannot support is a deal that preserves every structure unchanged and calls that protection. That would just postpone the crisis.

## To the state side
You are right to insist that the company remains a regional industrial anchor. That is exactly why the state has to be more than a passive commentator.

I would expect the state to support:
- protection of German plants as far as economically defensible
- avoidance of forced layoffs where possible
- a real hybrid restructuring path
- binding investment and product commitments in Germany
- and preservation of co-determination

But I also need the state to be honest: if a plant-level analysis shows that one site cannot be kept open under any realistic non-closure package, then we cannot pretend that every site is equally protectable. Regional responsibility requires hard judgment, not slogans.

That said, **we are not there yet**. I have not seen proof that closures are necessary rather than chosen.

## To the family holding
I recognize your focus on value protection and governance stability. That is legitimate. But value is not protected by forcing through a solution that detonates industrial peace and weakens the company’s regional legitimacy.

A settlement that is technically efficient but politically and socially ungovernable will not be durable. You should know that in a co-determined German company, execution risk is value risk.

So yes:
- I want material cost reduction
- I want real capacity adjustment
- I want a credible path to profitability

But no:
- I do not accept closure as the default answer
- I do not accept a “hybrid” that is merely softer language for the same destructive outcome
- I do not accept weakening the state’s seat at the table

## To investors
You are right that symbolism is not enough. I agree with that.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible package is one that keeps closures permanently available from day one. That assumption underestimates the economic damage of industrial conflict and the long-term cost of breaking trust.

A package that protects the workforce, preserves the regional anchor, and still delivers meaningful savings can be better value than a hard reset that triggers a prolonged fight and destabilizes execution.

## On the expert’s analysis
The expert’s analysis is useful and, importantly, it confirms something I have been saying from the beginning:

- **Closure-led** gives the strongest cost reset but carries the highest conflict and execution risk.
- **Closure-free** is least disruptive but may underdeliver if the gap is large.
- **Hybrid** is the most plausible compromise **if it includes real footprint reduction and measurable run-rate savings**.

That is the key point. A hybrid cannot just be a cosmetic middle path. It has to be economically real.

What I take from that is:
- if the gap can be closed with internal measures plus real capacity adjustments, that should be the route
- if the gap cannot, then we need to examine where deeper change is truly unavoidable
- but we are not entitled to assume closures first and ask questions later

## My position on the facilitator’s options
### Option 1: Two-phase hybrid with protected first phase
This is the only route that looks seriously discussable to me.

But the first phase must include:
- no German plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- binding investment commitments
- transparent milestones
- and real co-determination in implementation

I will not accept a “protected first phase” that is simply a delayed trapdoor to the same outcome later. If there is a second phase, it must be triggered only by clearly defined, objective, independently verified failure to deliver the agreed package.

### Option 2: No-closure pledge with a bounded contingency clause
This can only be considered if the contingency is:
- narrow
- formula-based
- objective
- and tied to extraordinary, clearly defined underdelivery or external shock

I am cautious here because such clauses can become backdoors. But I am not ruling out a tightly disciplined version if it truly protects both credibility and employment.

### Option 3: Plant-by-plant equivalence test
I strongly support this.

This is the right next step because it moves the debate from politics to evidence. But the comparison must include:
- utilization
- fixed-cost absorption
- conversion cost
- one-time restructuring costs
- run-rate savings
- timing to savings
- and the impact on margins over 3 years

It must also include the **regional economic consequences** and the **governance consequences**, not just the spreadsheet.

If a site can be restructured without closure, then that should be preferred. If a site cannot, then we need to see that clearly and honestly before any irreversible decision is made.

## My minimum position
I will support a negotiated restructuring only if it protects the company as a regional economic anchor and preserves co-determination.

That means:
- no blanket closure program
- no compulsory redundancies as a routine tool
- serious capacity adjustment
- voluntary measures first
- time-limited employee contribution
- binding investment commitments in Germany
- and a verified, site-by-site economic test

I am not prepared to support a settlement that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s role.

## Bottom line
I am open to a strong, pragmatic restructuring — but not to a reflexive closure-led solution.

If management wants my support, it must bring a **plant-by-plant, numbers-based, closure-avoiding package** that is serious enough to restore competitiveness **without destroying the company’s regional base**.
