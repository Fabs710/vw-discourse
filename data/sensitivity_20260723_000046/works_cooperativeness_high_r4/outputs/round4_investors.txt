We agree with the diagnosis: this is a structural crisis, and cosmetic measures will not restore value. We also agree that prolonged conflict destroys value. Where we differ is on what counts as a credible solution.

## Where we agree
- **Management** is right that the cost base must come down materially.
- **The expert** is right that closure-free measures can contribute, but may not be enough if the gap is large.
- **The union and works council** are right that trust and execution matter.
- **The state** is right that a purely destructive outcome would damage legitimacy and long-term industrial strength.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A blanket no-closure, no-redundancy line is not enough
The workforce side keeps repeating:
- no German plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- all plants open through 2030

That is not a restructuring answer. It is a constraint. If the company’s German footprint is genuinely overcapacity and the brand is still missing its ROS target, then investors will not back a deal that preserves an uncompetitive structure just because it is politically easier.

We will not support an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally impaired.

### 2) A cosmetic “hybrid” is not acceptable
We are open to a hybrid package, but only if it includes:
- real capacity reduction
- measurable run-rate savings
- enforceable milestones
- durable labour-cost improvement

A hybrid that merely delays the hard decision, re-labels voluntary measures, and hopes for productivity miracles is not credible. That would be value destruction by postponement.

### 3) Guarantees must be funded by economics, not wishes
Binding site and job guarantees are only acceptable if the package actually restores competitiveness. If the economics do not work, then a guarantee becomes a promise to preserve underperformance.

We do not support locking in an uncompetitive cost base.

## To management
You are right not to accept a symbolic settlement. But you still need to show more than diagnosis.  
We want a **plant-by-plant comparison** of:
- utilization
- fixed-cost absorption
- conversion cost
- one-time restructuring costs
- run-rate savings
- timing to savings
- impact on margins and ROS over 3 years

If closures remain on the table, show why non-closure measures are insufficient in quantified terms. If a hybrid can do the job, fine — but prove it.

## To the works council and union
You are correct that workers should not be the only ones paying. We agree. But you are not entitled to demand permanent protection while also refusing the instruments that may be necessary to fix the economics.

Voluntary exits, working-time changes, redeployment, and temporary employee contributions can be part of the answer. They are not automatically enough. If the residual gap remains material, then closure flexibility stays necessary.

## To the state
Your concern for regional industrial substance is legitimate. But regional responsibility also means insisting on a financially viable company. Protecting every site regardless of economics is not stewardship; it is deferral.

## To the family holding
You are right that governance stability matters. But governance stability is not preserved by preserving every legacy structure. A deal that fails to restore competitiveness will eventually destroy value and stability alike.

## Our position
We will support **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**, but not:
- a cosmetic compromise
- an under-restructured outcome
- a settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired

So our position remains:
- **No** to a hollow no-closure settlement.
- **No** to a deal that freezes an uncompetitive footprint.
- **Yes** to a quantified hybrid if it truly closes the gap.
- **Yes** to deeper structural measures, including closures, if they are economically necessary.
