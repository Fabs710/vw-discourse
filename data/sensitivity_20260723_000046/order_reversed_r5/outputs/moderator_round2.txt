### 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **real restructuring**, not a symbolic compromise.
- **Prolonged conflict destroys value** and should be avoided.
- **Capacity must be reduced** relative to demand.
- **Labour must contribute** to the adjustment.
- Savings must be **measurable, durable, and implementable**.
- Future investment depends on a **competitive operating model**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures / compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Investors and management see them as potentially necessary if savings are otherwise insufficient.  
  - The union and works council reject them.  
  - The state and family owner resist making them the default instrument.
- **Whether non-closure measures can be enough:**  
  - Labour and the state believe a package can still work through working-time changes, wage restraint, redeployment, overhead cuts, and selective capacity measures.  
  - Investors and management doubt these alone can close the structural cost gap.
- **How to weigh financial credibility vs. social/political feasibility:**  
  - Capital wants maximum fixed-cost reduction.  
  - Other stakeholders insist the package must also be governable, politically viable, and socially stable.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **minimum recurring savings target** has not been quantified in a shared way.
- There is no agreed split between savings from:
  - labour concessions,
  - management/overhead cuts,
  - productivity gains,
  - capacity reduction,
  - and site rationalization.
- The parties have not resolved whether **“equivalent savings”** can be achieved without closures.
- The cost of **implementation risk** versus the benefit of **larger structural savings** remains unresolved.
- There is no common position on how much **job security**, **site protection**, and **investment commitment** can be traded for competitiveness gains.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Move from principle to **scenario-based numbers**:
- Define the required annual savings range.
- Compare at least two packages: **no-closure** vs. **partial closure/site rationalization**.
- Show for each package:
  - recurring savings,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - implementation risk,
  - and impact on margins/free cash flow.
- Then test whether any package can satisfy both:
  - **financial credibility**, and
  - **institutional/social feasibility**.
