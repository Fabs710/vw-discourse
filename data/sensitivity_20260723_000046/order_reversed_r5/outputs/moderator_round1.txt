### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- There is broad agreement that:
  - **cost reduction is necessary**,
  - **capacity must better match demand**,
  - **some labour contribution is required**,
  - and the business needs **room to invest in future products**.
- There is also agreement that **prolonged conflict would destroy value**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures and compulsory redundancies**:
  - Management, investors, and the family holding see them as possible or necessary tools.
  - The state government, union, and works council strongly resist them.
- **Scope of labour’s contribution**:
  - Employers/investors want a **material, durable** contribution.
  - Labour representatives want any sacrifice to be **shared**, not concentrated on employees.
- **How to achieve savings**:
  - Management favors **structural, lasting changes** and says temporary measures are insufficient.
  - Labour side wants **non-destructive alternatives first** and resists permanent site/job losses.
- **Governance and social limits**:
  - The state government and works council stress **regional, political, and co-determination constraints** that limit what can be imposed.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Cost savings vs. job and site preservation**
- **Speed and certainty of restructuring vs. social and political stability**
- **Permanent structural fixes vs. temporary/ reversible measures**
- **Who bears the burden**: employees alone, or a broader shared-sacrifice model involving management and owners
- **Competitiveness gains vs. the risk of triggering conflict and implementation blockage**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **concrete package design**:
  - quantify required savings,
  - identify which measures are structural,
  - specify what can be achieved without closures,
  - and clarify what concessions each side can actually make.
- Ask each side to present:
  - **minimum acceptable conditions**,
  - **non-negotiable red lines**,
  - and **one realistic compromise option**.
- Keep the discussion anchored on one question: **What package can deliver measurable competitiveness gains while remaining implementable under the governance constraints?**
