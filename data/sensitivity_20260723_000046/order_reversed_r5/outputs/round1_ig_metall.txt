We agree on one thing: **the company needs a real turnaround, not a cosmetic one**. Where I sharply disagree is on the assumption that this can be achieved by putting the main burden on employees through plant closures, forced layoffs, and permanent wage erosion.

### What I agree with
- The cost base must be addressed.
- Capacity has to be adjusted to market reality.
- A negotiated contribution from labour is legitimate.
- The company needs room to invest in future products.

So no, we are not defending the status quo. We have already shown that by submitting a counter-proposal and accepting that **labour costs and capacity must be part of the solution**.

### What I reject
What I will not accept is a restructuring in which:
- **workers pay all or most of the bill,**
- **sites are closed as the default answer,**
- **forced redundancies are used as the first instrument,**
- and the company then presents that as the only “credible” path.

That is not a fair turnaround. It is a transfer of risk and pain onto the workforce while management and owners preserve their options.

### On the management argument
Management says temporary measures are not enough. Fine — but that does **not** justify permanent social damage as the starting point.

If you want durable savings, then bring a package that combines:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- and management/owner contributions as well.

That is the only basis on which we will discuss a durable settlement.

### On plant closures
I need to be very clear: **plant closures are not an acceptable first-choice instrument**. They would cross a line for us if used to force a settlement. In this governance structure, with co-determination and blocking positions in place, you should not assume closures can simply be imposed.

If there are alternatives that can deliver savings without destroying sites and jobs, those alternatives must be taken seriously first. That includes:
- redeployment,
- internal reorganization,
- reduced working hours,
- suspension or reduction of bonuses,
- and other negotiated labour-cost measures.

### On the state government and owners
I welcome the fact that you both say you do not want a cosmetic solution. But fairness matters. If you want labour to contribute, then **owners and management must also contribute visibly**. It is not acceptable to demand sacrifices from employees while leaving strategic risk, governance control, and financial exposure untouched above them.

### Our position
We are willing to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package** that:
- protects jobs,
- protects sites,
- preserves the collective wage framework,
- and delivers real savings.

But we are **not** willing to endorse:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures,
- or a restructuring that makes employees bear the full burden.

If management insists that closures are unavoidable, then it will not get a cooperative settlement from us. It will get resistance — and the workforce has already demonstrated that it is prepared to act.

So the question is not whether there will be sacrifice. The question is **who carries it, and whether it is shared fairly**.
