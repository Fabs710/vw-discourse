Let me be equally clear: **the workforce will not accept a “turnaround” built on plant closures and forced redundancies**.

I hear the investors, the family owners, and management all repeating the same word: “credible.” Credible for whom? A package is not credible if it destroys the company’s industrial backbone, breaks trust, and tears up the historic job-security framework. That is not a turnaround. That is a rupture.

### Where I agree
I agree that the company needs a **real** solution, not a cosmetic one.  
I agree that the cost base must be addressed.  
I agree that capacity has to reflect demand.  
I agree that the business needs room to invest in future products.

But those points do **not** automatically lead to closures, compulsory layoffs, or the unilateral dismantling of guarantees.

### Where I strongly disagree
What I reject is the logic that says:
- because the market is difficult,
- because competition is tough,
- therefore German plants must be closed and workers must pay the price first.

That is not an objective necessity. That is a choice.

And I must say this plainly: management has not earned the right to demand sacrifices from the workforce while presenting closure as the default instrument. You speak about durability and competitiveness, but breaking the trust that holds this company together is not durable. It creates a social conflict that will last far beyond any quarterly savings.

### On the “structural savings” argument
If you want structural savings, then bring forward measures that are structural **without being destructive**:
- working-time reductions,
- internal reallocation,
- training and redeployment,
- temporary adjustments where necessary,
- lower management overhead,
- fair contributions from shareholders and executive leadership,
- and a binding commitment to product investment in Germany.

What I will not accept is the idea that the only serious path is to cut sites and jobs to the bone.

### On plant closures
Plant closures in Germany are a red line.  
Forced redundancies are a red line.  
Breaking the historic job-security assurances is a red line.

If management insists on those instruments, then it should expect resistance, not cooperation. The workforce has already shown that it is ready to mobilize, and I will continue to defend every German site.

### On fairness
The burden must not fall on employees alone. If the company needs a contribution, then:
- management must reduce its own cost base,
- owners must support a long-term industrial strategy,
- and labour must be treated as a partner, not as disposable adjustment mass.

### My position
I am prepared to discuss a tough restructuring **only if it is based on**:
- **no plant closures,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **binding site and job guarantees to 2030,**
- **and a real co-determination role in shaping the future plan.**

Without those conditions, there is no basis for a settlement.  
If the others want conflict, they will get conflict.  
If they want a solution, they need to stop treating the workforce as the variable to be sacrificed.
