We share the core objective: **the company must regain competitiveness, restore margins, and protect long-term value**. On that point, I agree with the capital markets side. A cosmetic settlement that preserves the current cost structure would be irresponsible.

That said, I want to be equally clear on two boundaries:

1. **Value destruction through prolonged industrial conflict is not acceptable.**  
   A strike-driven escalation, reputational damage, and management paralysis would destroy value just as surely as inaction.

2. **Any restructuring must be durable and governable.**  
   We are prepared to support meaningful structural change, but not a chaotic process that undermines the company’s ability to execute or the owners’ governance position.

### Where I agree
- **Material cost reduction is necessary.**
- **Capacity must reflect market reality.**
- **Labour contributions have to be real, not symbolic.**
- **Investment in future products only makes sense if the cost base is actually competitive.**

### Where I differ
I do **not** see plant closures or forced layoffs as automatic answers. They may be part of the solution, but they are not the only legitimate route, and they come with heavy execution and political risks in this governance structure. Given the co-determination vetoes, the special-law constraints, and the broader stakeholder environment, a plan that relies solely on confrontation is likely to be slow, costly, and unstable.

So from our perspective, the right path is:

- **a negotiated restructuring with real savings**,  
- **a measurable labour-cost contribution**,  
- **capacity reductions where necessary**,  
- **and a credible commitment to future investment and product competitiveness**.

If management can only show savings by threatening a long conflict, then the plan is not yet good enough. If, however, there is a settlement that materially lowers cost, improves utilization, and preserves the ability to invest, we would support it.

### Bottom line
We are **not** defending legacy structures for their own sake.  
We are **not** open to a settlement that merely postpones the problem.  
We are open to a tough restructuring — but it must be **effective, durable, and stable**.

So I would put the burden on management and the labour side alike: bring forward a package that actually closes the competitiveness gap. If that requires difficult concessions, those concessions must happen.
