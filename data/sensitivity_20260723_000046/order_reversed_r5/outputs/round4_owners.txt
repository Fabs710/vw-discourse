## My position

I support a **real restructuring** and I support **durable cost reduction**.  
I do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base.  
I also do **not** support turning plant closures into the automatic starting point before the alternatives are properly tested.

That remains my position.

## Where I agree with the investors

You are right on the core financial standards:
- the package must be **material**
- it must be **durable**
- and it must be **credible in implementation**

If a no-closure package cannot actually close the structural gap, then it is not enough. I accept that.

What I do not accept is the implication that only closures can prove seriousness. That is too simplistic, and in this governance environment it is dangerous. A technically strong plan that cannot pass the supervisory board, co-determination, and regional realities is not value creation; it is theatre.

## Where I agree with management

Management is right that the company needs a **structural reset**, not temporary relief.  
Working-time changes and wage restraint alone will not solve a deep competitiveness problem.

But I disagree with the tendency to treat closures as the first and cleanest answer. They may be the strongest lever on paper. They are not automatically the best lever for value preservation here.

If management wants ownership support, it should show:
- the size of the actual cost gap,
- what non-closure measures can achieve,
- what remains after that,
- and only then whether site rationalization is truly required.

That sequence matters.

## Where I disagree with the union and works council

I understand the social and political resistance. I understand why you are defending jobs and sites. But I do **not** accept the claim that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” is sufficient simply because it is desirable.

The expert has now confirmed what should be obvious:
- non-closure measures can deliver **material savings**
- but they may still fall short if the overcapacity and fixed-cost burden are large

So the burden is on you to prove that your package is not only socially preferable, but economically sufficient.

At this stage, I have not seen that proof.

And I will also be blunt: if the workforce contribution is real, it cannot be the **only** serious sacrifice. There must also be visible restraint from management and overhead, yes — but if the numbers still do not add up, then insisting on full site preservation becomes a demand to keep an uncompetitive structure intact.

That is not stewardship.

## Where I agree with the state government

You are right to insist that any solution must be **governable** and must not destroy the regional industrial base.  
A settlement that cannot survive co-determination and political reality is not credible.

But I do not accept “governable” as a synonym for “no hard choices.”  
And I do not accept regional protection as a veto on every meaningful structural measure.

If the company cannot restore competitiveness, the footprint erodes anyway. The region is not protected by postponing reality.

## On the expert analysis

The expert has done exactly what was needed:
- no-closure restructuring can be **material**
- partial rationalization is usually **stronger on durability**
- full closure-led restructuring is **strongest on paper but riskiest to execute**

That is the right framework.

What follows from that is not “no closures ever,” and not “closures now.”  
What follows is: **show me the numbers**.

If the no-closure package truly delivers:
- enough recurring savings,
- enough capacity reduction,
- enough fixed-cost removal,
- and enough contractual durability,

then it deserves support.

If it does not, then selective site rationalization stays on the table.

## What I will support

I will support whichever package meets all three conditions:

1. **Material savings**
2. **Durable competitiveness improvement**
3. **Implementation certainty under this governance structure**

That may be a no-closure package.  
It may be partial rationalization.  
But it must be real.

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that only postpones the problem,
- or a plan that preserves every site while leaving the company structurally above peer cost levels.

I also will not support a closure-first posture unless the no-closure route is shown, clearly and quantitatively, to be insufficient.
