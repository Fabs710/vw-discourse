Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable return to competitive margins** and a **material reduction in the cost base**.

What matters to us is not preserving every plant, guarantee, or legacy cost structure. What matters is whether management can show a believable path back to acceptable return on sales and free cash flow in a market that is getting tougher, not easier.

### What we would support
- **Deep structural cost reduction**
- **Capacity adjustment to match demand**
- **A negotiated labour contribution** that is meaningful and durable
- **A clear implementation path** with timing, accountability, and measurable savings
- **Investment in future products** only if it is backed by an actually competitive cost base

### What we would not support
- A **cosmetic settlement** that preserves the current cost base while calling it a restructuring
- Any agreement that leaves the company **structurally uncompetitive**
- Prolonged conflict, strikes, or uncertainty that destroys value without fixing the underlying problem
- Commitments that protect every site or every job at the expense of the business’s long-term viability

### Our priority
We are not here to defend any particular plant or legacy arrangement. We are here to protect capital through a sustainable turnaround. If the company needs closures, workforce reductions, or changes to labour terms to restore competitiveness, then those measures should be on the table.

At the same time, we would prefer a **negotiated solution** over a drawn-out confrontation, because delay and disruption are costly. But the settlement has to be real. If the final package does not materially improve margins, we will regard it as insufficient.
