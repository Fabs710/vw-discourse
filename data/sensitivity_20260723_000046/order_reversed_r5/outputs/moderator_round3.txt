**Moderator Summary — Round 3**

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement across all parties that:
- the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic deal;
- **prolonged conflict destroys value**;
- **capacity must be reduced** in some form;
- **labour must contribute**;
- any settlement must be **durable** and **implementable**;
- the decision should be based on **quantified financial comparisons**, not slogans.

There is also agreement that management, ownership, labour, investors, and the state all have relevant constraints that cannot be ignored.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved disagreement is over **plant closures / compulsory redundancies**:

- **Investors and management**: do not want closures ruled out in advance; they argue non-closure measures may be insufficient if the cost gap is large.
- **Union and works council**: reject closures and compulsory redundancies; they argue a serious restructuring can still be achieved without them.
- **State government and family owner**: are open to restructuring but insist it must remain governable, socially acceptable, and consistent with co-determination and regional commitments; they do not accept closures as a default answer.

A second disagreement concerns **what counts as a credible “no-closure” package**:
- some parties view it as potentially viable if savings are deep and durable;
- others view it as likely to fall short if it does not remove enough fixed cost and capacity.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
The discussion still has not resolved the central trade-offs between:

- **magnitude of savings** vs. **social and political acceptability**;
- **durability of cost reduction** vs. **reversibility of concessions**;
- **implementation certainty** vs. **economic strength of the restructuring**;
- **short-term restructuring costs** vs. **3-year margin and free cash flow improvement**;
- **preserving sites and jobs** vs. **fully resetting the cost base**.

The expert input clarified that:
- no-closure measures can deliver **material savings**;
- partial rationalization is usually stronger on durability and fixed-cost removal;
- closure-led restructuring is strongest economically on paper but carries the highest execution risk.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principle to **scenario comparison**. Parties should work from a common template comparing:
1. **No-closure restructuring**
2. **Partial closure / site rationalization**
3. **More aggressive closure-led restructuring**

For each scenario, the group should focus on:
- recurring annual savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- implementation risk,
- and 3-year impact on margins and free cash flow.

The discussion should also identify:
- which savings are truly **structural**,
- which are **temporary or reversible**,
- and what minimum package would be required to make a no-closure option credible.
