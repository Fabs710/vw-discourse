We agree on one central point: **this company needs a credible turnaround, not a cosmetic one**. If the cost base stays structurally uncompetitive, then investment, margins, and ultimately jobs are all at risk.

But I need to be equally clear: **I cannot support a restructuring that solves the numbers by gutting the company’s regional footprint**.

### Where I agree
- A **real labour-cost contribution** is necessary.
- **Capacity has to match demand**.
- The company needs a **durable path back to competitiveness**.
- A long, unresolved conflict would damage everyone, including the workforce and the state as shareholder.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that plant closures in Germany are the default or first-choice solution. In this governance structure, closures are not just a technical cost measure; they are a decision with major regional, social, and political consequences. If a proposal depends on shutting sites while cutting jobs on a large scale, then it crosses a line for us unless every serious alternative has been exhausted and the impact is genuinely unavoidable.

Likewise, I would not support a package that shifts the burden almost entirely onto employees while shareholders and management preserve strategic flexibility for themselves. That is not a fair restructuring, and it will not be stable.

### What a serious solution must contain
From our perspective, any acceptable package must include:
- **Preservation of key sites and industrial capacity in Germany**
- **No mass layoffs as the primary instrument**
- **A negotiated social-partnership approach**
- **Temporary and reversible measures first**: working-time adjustments, targeted efficiency measures, redeployment, training, and phased capacity changes
- **Clear future investment commitments** tied to keeping production and development anchored in the region
- **A contribution from all sides**, including management discipline and a credible product strategy

### My bottom line
I am prepared to back a tough restructuring **if it protects the company without hollowing out its regional backbone**. But I will not support a plan that treats job cuts and site closures as the easy solution simply because it is faster on paper.

Given our blocking rights and co-determination position, I would urge management and the controlling shareholder to come back with a package that is genuinely competitive **and** socially sustainable. Otherwise, they should not expect support from us.
