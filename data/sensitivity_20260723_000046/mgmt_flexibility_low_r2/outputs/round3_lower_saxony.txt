I will be direct.

I agree with the diagnosis from management, the family owner, investors, the works council, and the union on one essential point: **the company needs a credible restructuring**. Costs must come down, capacity must be adjusted, and the EV transition needs a viable industrial base. I also agree that a cosmetic deal would be worthless.

Where I **do not** agree is with the repeated attempt to make **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** the default logic of the process.

## Where I agree
- The company cannot stand still.
- Non-closure tools are not symbolic; they can deliver real savings.
- Voluntary exits, retirement-based reductions, redeployment, qualification, working-time changes, and productivity improvements all belong in the package.
- Management, owners, and capital must contribute too.
- A settlement must be durable and economically credible.

## Where I disagree
### 1) No German plant closures as the starting point
I do not accept that closures are the inevitable first answer. This company is a regional anchor, not a disposable asset. A closure in the state would mean:
- permanent job losses,
- damage to the supplier base,
- loss of skills and industrial identity,
- and major political and social fallout.

That is not a routine restructuring measure. It is a last-resort decision with structural consequences. I will not support making it the default.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies as a planning assumption
I also do not accept compulsory redundancies as an acceptable baseline. If management wants flexibility “just in case,” that simply shifts all risk onto the workforce. That is not social partnership; that is coercion by another name.

### 3) Co-determination and the state’s veto role are not negotiable
I reject any logic that treats governance rights as an obstacle to be managed away. The special statute and co-determination are part of how this company is structured. They are not ornaments. Any restructuring that sidelines them would be unacceptable.

## On the expert input
The expert did **not** say closures are unavoidable here. What was said is more nuanced:
- non-closure measures can often cover a large share of the gap,
- avoiding compulsory redundancies is feasible in some cases,
- but it requires time, attrition, wage flexibility, and serious management discipline.

That actually supports a negotiated path, not a rush to closures. It means the question is not “closures or nothing.” It is whether management is prepared to pursue a serious hybrid package before reaching for irreversible measures.

## To management
You keep saying the burden is on the other side to produce an equivalent package. I disagree with that framing.

If you want to break with the historic job-security framework, then **you** must show:
- the exact savings requirement,
- the savings already achievable through non-closure measures,
- what has been tested,
- and why the residual gap cannot be closed through further negotiated measures, including management and shareholder contributions.

So far, I hear a lot of “we need flexibility” and not enough hard proof that closures or forced layoffs are necessary rather than merely convenient.

## To the works council and union
I support your core line: **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of co-determination or collective protections**. That said, I also want us to stay concrete and quantified. A purely defensive position is not enough if we want to preserve the company long term.

If we are going to protect sites and jobs, then we need a package that is economically real:
- capacity reduction through negotiated means,
- real labour-cost contributions,
- internal reallocation,
- retirement-based exits,
- and binding investment commitments.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that the company cannot remain uncompetitive. But long-term value is not created by threatening the workforce into submission. If you want returns, you need a viable company. And a viable company in this case depends on:
- industrial peace,
- skilled labour retention,
- continued German production,
- and a stable governance framework.

I do not accept the idea that site guarantees through 2030 are “not credible” by definition. They are credible if the package is credible. That is the point of negotiation.

## On the moderator’s bridge ideas
A bridge period can make sense only if it is a real bridge to a durable settlement, not a delay tactic.

### “No forced layoffs during a defined bridge period”
That is acceptable only if it is paired with a binding framework that seriously pursues non-closure measures and does not pre-program closures at the end of the period.

### “Site continuity with capacity reduction, not full closure”
This is the right direction. Keep plants open, reduce capacity, consolidate production, adjust shifts, and use internal relocation. That is restructuring without destroying the regional base.

### “Conditional guarantees linked to measurable savings”
I can support measurable implementation checkpoints, but not a setup where guarantees are effectively worthless because management can pull them at will. If workers contribute, they need real security in return.

## My position
I am prepared to back a negotiated restructuring if it includes:
1. **Binding site and employment guarantees through 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No German plant closures in the current package**
4. **Material labor-cost and flexibility contributions**
5. **Management and shareholder contributions**
6. **Capacity reduction through reversible, negotiated measures**
7. **Clear investment commitments for future products**
8. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is the minimum basis for a serious settlement.

## Bottom line
I am not here to administer decline or to approve closures because they are cleaner on paper. I am here to protect the company as a regional economic anchor and to ensure it remains viable for the long term. If management and the owners want my support, they must bring a quantified, non-destructive package that keeps German sites open and avoids forced layoffs. If they cannot, then we have not reached a serious solution.
