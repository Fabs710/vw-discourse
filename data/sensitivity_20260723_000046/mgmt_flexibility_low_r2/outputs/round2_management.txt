We have now heard the key point from the restructuring specialist, and it confirms what management has been saying from the start: **non-closure measures can help, but they do not automatically solve a structural cost problem of this scale**.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert on several points:
- **Non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings.**
- **Voluntary exits, retirement pathways, working-time changes, and productivity measures are all useful tools.**
- A **hybrid package** can sometimes avoid the most drastic measures.
- The real test is not rhetoric but **run-rate savings, timing, durability, and certainty**.

That is exactly why I am not dismissing alternatives out of hand.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt by the employee side and the state government to turn “no closures” into a fixed starting point. The specialist has just confirmed the central issue: **non-closure measures usually do not replicate 100% of closure economics**. In other words, if the savings requirement is large and permanent — and here it is — then you do not get to declare plant closures off-limits and still demand full competitiveness.

That is not a plan. That is a veto on reality.

I also do **not** accept the idea that management must somehow prove every alternative in advance before closures can even be discussed. We have already shown:
- the market is shrinking,
- the cost base is too high,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

That is sufficient to establish the need for a deep structural correction. The burden is now on the other side to show a package that actually closes the gap. Not a promise. Not an aspiration. A package that delivers **equivalent savings, on time, with durability**.

## To the Works Council and union
You both repeat the same red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of protections.

I understand why you say that politically. But from a restructuring perspective, those positions are only credible if you can show a concrete alternative that achieves the same economics. So far, I have not heard one.

The expert’s assessment is important here: a non-closure package may cover **40% to 80%** of the savings of a closure package. That means there may still be a **material gap**. If you want to rule out closures, then you must explain how you intend to close that gap without leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

And let me be blunt: **binding site guarantees through 2030 are not credible if they freeze the footprint regardless of market reality**. A guarantee that prevents adaptation is not a guarantee; it is a liability.

## To the state government
I appreciate the political and regional concerns, but I reject the implication that protecting every site is the same as protecting the company. It is not.

If the business case shows overcapacity, then refusing closures categorically may preserve jobs in the short term while destroying the company’s ability to invest, compete, and remain a major employer in the long term. That is not stewardship. That is postponing the bill.

You asked for evidence that non-destructive alternatives have been exhausted. The answer is simple: **we are evaluating them now**, but we will not let that become an open-ended delay tactic. Time is not on our side.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree with you on the economic point: a cosmetic deal is unacceptable. If a compromise merely preserves the old structure in a new form, it will fail.

But I also want to be very clear: if we stop short of the measures needed to restore competitiveness, then there will be no credible path to protect investment capacity to 2030. There is no future product plan without a viable earnings base.

## My position after the expert input
The expert did not move me toward accepting a no-closure settlement. If anything, the expert reinforced my position:

- **Non-closure measures are necessary, but not sufficient by default**
- **A durable savings package must be large enough and fast enough**
- **If the savings gap remains, deeper structural measures remain on the table**
- **We are not bound to preserve every historic guarantee if it blocks competitiveness**

## What I can accept
I am prepared to support a package that includes:
- material labour-cost contributions,
- real capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits and retirement-based reductions,
- working-time measures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and clear investment commitments.

But only if it produces **verifiable, permanent, and substantial savings**. If the numbers do not add up, then we will not pretend they do.

## What I cannot accept
I will not accept:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a deal that preserves uncompetitive overcapacity,
- or a “socially acceptable” compromise that fails the P&L.

The company does not need a symbolic peace. It needs a structural reset.

## Bottom line
So I am putting this plainly: **show me a non-closure package that closes the economic gap at the scale required, and I will engage seriously. If you cannot, then you are not offering an alternative — you are asking management to accept decline.** That is not an option.
