We share the same basic red lines: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear, though: if management is asking the workforce to make painful concessions, then **those concessions must be part of a genuinely shared-sacrifice package**. Workers cannot be expected to carry the entire burden while capital, owners, and management keep all strategic options and all financial cushions for themselves.

Where I agree with the Works Council:
- **Capacity has to be adjusted**, but it must be done through negotiated, reversible instruments, not by shutting plants.
- **Working-time reductions, internal redeployment, qualification, and retirement-based measures** are the right direction.
- There must be **binding site and employment guarantees** if employees are expected to contribute.
- The workforce must not be treated as a passive cost block; it needs a **real role in shaping the transformation**.

Where I draw the line:
- I will not support any plan that includes **forced layoffs disguised as restructuring**.
- I will not accept a “future investment” narrative if it is used to justify **permanent wage dumping or open-ended insecurity**.
- I will not accept a deal where employees give up pay or guarantees while management and owners make no comparable contribution.

Our union is prepared to be constructive, and we have already shown that by putting forward a counter-proposal that includes:
- **labour-cost contribution**
- **capacity reductions**
- **employee financial participation**
- in exchange for **job security and site security**

That remains our basis. But the condition is simple: **shared pain, shared responsibility, shared protection**.

So to management and the owners: if you want peace and a durable settlement, bring something real:
- a **credible guarantee against closures**
- a **moratorium on compulsory redundancies**
- a **fairly distributed contribution from the shareholder and management side**
- and a restructuring that preserves the collective wage framework instead of attacking it

If that does not happen, then you should expect this conflict to escalate. We are not here to manage workers’ fear while others preserve their freedom of action.
