The positions are now clear enough, so I will be equally clear.

Management is still presenting the workforce with a false dilemma: either we accept plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or we accept “realistic” restructuring. That framing is unacceptable. **Realistic restructuring does not have to mean destroying plants, breaking the historic job-security framework, and forcing people out of the company.**

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the company needs a credible restructuring**. I also agree that:
- costs must come down,
- capacity must be aligned with demand,
- qualification, redeployment, working-time tools, and retirement-based exits should be used,
- and the company needs a future investment plan for EVs and new products.

But agreement on the diagnosis does **not** mean agreement on the prescription.

### Where I disagree — sharply
I disagree with management, and also with the investors’ logic, on one central issue: **you are treating plant closures and forced layoffs as acceptable “options” before exhausting the alternatives that protect jobs and sites.** That is not a responsible process; it is a threat.

I also disagree with the idea that the burden of proof lies mainly with the employee side. No — **the burden of proof lies with management to show that it has exhausted every non-destructive alternative** before reaching for closures or compulsory redundancies.

And let me be precise:  
- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or the job-security framework.**

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are the boundary of what is acceptable.

### To management
If you want a serious discussion, stop hiding behind abstract “structural correction” language and put numbers on the table:
- How much savings are needed?
- From which plants and functions?
- By when?
- And why exactly can this not be achieved through internal measures, working-time adjustments, temporary reductions, redeployment, qualification, and retirement-based transitions?

If the answer is “because we want maximum flexibility,” then say so honestly. But do not call that necessity.

### To the investors
You say you want durable competitiveness and no cosmetic peace deal. Fine. So do we. But a durable solution is not one that permanently weakens the company’s social foundation.

If you force through closures and mass job cuts, you may get a short-term accounting improvement, but you will also buy:
- labour conflict,
- loss of trust,
- higher industrial risk,
- weaker identification with the company,
- and a damaged long-term production base in Germany.

That is not “risk-adjusted value creation”; that is value destruction dressed up as discipline.

### To the controlling family and the state government
You both speak about long-term industrial responsibility. Then act like it.

If you mean that seriously, then you should back a restructuring that:
- keeps all German plants open,
- rules out compulsory redundancies,
- preserves co-determination,
- and binds management to a long-term site and employment framework.

Anything less means the old promises were only valuable until management got impatient.

### What we can support
We are prepared to support a serious alternative package, but only if it contains all of the following:
1. **Binding site and employment guarantees through 2030**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **Capacity reduction through reversible, negotiated measures**
4. **A fair contribution from management, owners, and capital**
5. **A real co-determination role in shaping implementation**
6. **Investment commitments that secure the future of the German sites**

That is the minimum basis for a settlement.

### What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- a “guarantee” that can be broken whenever the market gets difficult,
- closures presented as unavoidable after only a half-hearted review of alternatives,
- permanent insecurity for workers while ownership keeps all strategic freedom,
- or any attempt to use the restructuring to weaken collective protections.

### Bottom line
If management and the owners want peace, they need to come back with a plan that **keeps every German plant open and avoids compulsory redundancies**. If they cannot do that, then they should be honest that they are choosing confrontation.

We are not here to administer the downsizing of the workforce. We are here to prevent it. And with the rights we have, we can do exactly that.
