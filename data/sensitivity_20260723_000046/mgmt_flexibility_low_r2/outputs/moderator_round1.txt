### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **credible restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that **costs must come down** and **capacity must be adjusted** to market reality.
- There is general support for **non-closure tools** where possible: redeployment, qualification, working-time reductions, retirement-based exits, and internal reallocation.
- All sides say a **negotiated settlement** is preferable to open conflict, if it produces durable results.
- There is shared recognition that the transformation to electric mobility requires a **viable industrial and investment plan**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works Council and union reject them; management, investors, and to a degree the family holding do not rule them out if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: employee side rejects them; management and investors keep them as an option if voluntary measures do not suffice.
- **Job-security / site guarantees**: employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors question whether such guarantees are feasible if they limit restructuring flexibility.
- **Burden sharing**: workers demand comparable contributions from management, owners, and capital; management and investors want evidence that employee-side measures can deliver sufficient savings first.
- **Governance / co-determination**: the employee side and state government insist these must remain intact; no one has proposed a settlement on this point that satisfies both flexibility and protection concerns.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required**, and which mix of measures can deliver them.
- **Whether non-closure measures can achieve savings equivalent to closures** in scale, timing, and durability.
- **What level of employee contribution is acceptable** in exchange for what level of job/site security.
- **What management, owners, and capital will contribute** beyond asking the workforce to adjust.
- **Which sites, if any, are viable to keep under guaranteed conditions**, and which capacity reductions would still be needed.
- **How to balance short-term restructuring pressure with long-term industrial base preservation**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **quantified proposals**: savings, timing, affected sites/functions, and durability.
- Ask each side to state **its minimum acceptable package** and **its non-negotiables**.
- Require a clear comparison between:
  1. a package based on **non-closure measures**, and  
  2. a package involving **harder structural measures**.
- Focus discussion on **who contributes what**, **in exchange for which guarantees**, and **how implementation risk is managed**.
