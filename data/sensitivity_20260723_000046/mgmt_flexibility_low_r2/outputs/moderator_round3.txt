### Core impasse
The main blockage is not the need for restructuring. All sides accept that. The impasse is over **what counts as an acceptable restructuring tool**:

- **Employee side / state:** wants binding protection against plant closures and compulsory redundancies.
- **Management / investors / family:** want to keep closures and involuntary reductions available if needed to close the cost gap.
- **Expert input:** indicates non-closure measures can cover a large share of the gap, but not always all of it, and timing matters.

So the dispute is fundamentally about **whether a no-closure package can be made economically credible, and if not, what fallback is permitted**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridging positions that could be explored:

#### 1) Time-limited no-closure bridge with measurable milestones
A defined bridge period, e.g. 18–24 months, during which:
- no German plant closures occur,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- and all parties commit to quantified savings measures.

At the end of the bridge period:
- the parties review whether agreed savings have been achieved,
- and whether further structural measures are still necessary.

This could help bridge the gap between the employee side’s demand for security and management’s demand for economic discipline.

#### 2) Site continuity plus capacity reduction package
Instead of closure, the parties could agree to:
- keep all German sites open,
- reduce capacity through shifts, product migration, line consolidation, and internal reallocation,
- combine this with voluntary exits, retirement-based reductions, and working-time measures.

This would preserve site presence while still allowing meaningful restructuring.

#### 3) Conditional protection with a hard fallback mechanism
The parties could agree on:
- binding protection against closures and involuntary redundancies,
- but only if a predefined savings threshold is met through non-closure measures,
- with a jointly verified reporting process.

If the threshold is not met, the parties would re-open negotiations on fallback measures under a time-limited and clearly defined procedure.

This gives both sides something: protection on one side, and an economic safeguard on the other.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
What specific combination of voluntary exits, working-time measures, internal transfers, and labor-cost concessions would you accept as sufficient to close the savings gap without closures or compulsory redundancies?

#### Industrial Union
What measurable concessions on wages, flexibility, or working time are you prepared to put on the table to make a no-closure package economically credible?

#### Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap remains after all non-closure measures are fully quantified, and what evidence shows that this gap cannot be closed without plant closures or involuntary redundancies?

#### Regional State Government
Under what concrete conditions would you be prepared to support a time-limited bridge arrangement that temporarily freezes closures and layoffs?

#### Controlling Family Holding
What level of economic underperformance would still justify keeping all German sites open, and what non-financial criteria matter enough to you to outweigh short-term restructuring efficiency?

#### Institutional Investors
What minimum combination of savings, timing, and durability would make a no-closure restructuring acceptable from your perspective?

#### Expert
What assumptions about workforce age structure, voluntary exit capacity, and timing would most strongly determine whether a no-closure package is likely to succeed?
