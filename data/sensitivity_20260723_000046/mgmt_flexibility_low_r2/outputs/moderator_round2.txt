Core impasse identified: **the dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed, but about the acceptable instrument mix and decision rule for closing the savings gap.**

## 1) Where the impasse lies
The main block is between two different standards of sufficiency:

- **Employee side / state government:** non-closure measures must be exhausted first; closures and compulsory redundancies should be ruled out or treated as last-resort exceptions.
- **Management / investors / controlling family:** alternatives are welcome, but only if they close the gap **quantitatively, durably, and on time**; otherwise closures and involuntary reductions remain available.

So the stalemate is not just “jobs vs. competitiveness.” It is:
- **what counts as a credible alternative,**
- **who bears the burden of proving it,**
- and **whether the footprint can remain fixed while still delivering the required savings.**

## 2) Concrete compromise positions
### Proposal A: “No forced layoffs during a defined bridge period”
A time-limited compromise could be:
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period, e.g. through a bridge phase,
- mandatory use of voluntary exits, retirement-based exits, redeployment, training, working-time tools, and internal transfers,
- with a jointly reviewed trigger if the savings target is not met by a set date.

This gives the employee side protection against immediate involuntary cuts, while giving management a structured fallback if the gap remains open.

### Proposal B: “Site continuity with capacity reduction, not full closure”
Instead of full closure vs. no change:
- keep plants formally open,
- reduce shifts, consolidate product lines, or partially idle underutilized capacity,
- relocate specific volumes or functions inside Germany,
- pair this with investment commitments and productivity targets.

This may bridge the divide between “no closures” and “full structural reset” by reducing fixed cost without immediate plant shutdowns.

### Proposal C: “Conditional guarantees linked to measurable savings”
A package could include:
- binding employment and site guarantees for a defined period,
- in exchange for measurable labor-cost and flexibility contributions,
- plus a governance checkpoint where all sides verify whether savings targets are being met,
- and only then decide whether deeper measures are still necessary.

This would make guarantees conditional on performance rather than purely symbolic or purely open-ended.

## 3) Pointed question to each stakeholder
### To management
**What exact savings target must be achieved by each site/function, and which non-closure measures have you already quantified as insufficient?**

### To the Works Council
**Which specific non-closure measures would you accept as binding and measurable if they allow you to keep plants open and avoid compulsory redundancies for a defined period?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What permanent labor-cost and flexibility concessions would you consider proportionate enough to make a no-closure settlement economically credible?**

### To the Regional State Government
**If a conditional no-closure package meets the savings target but requires strict monitoring and phased adjustments, would you back it even if it limits future footprint flexibility?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Are you willing to support a time-limited protection package that restricts closures now, if it includes a credible fallback mechanism should the savings gap remain after the bridge phase?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a hybrid solution if it delivers verifiable run-rate savings and capacity reduction, even if it does not maximize short-term flexibility for management?**
