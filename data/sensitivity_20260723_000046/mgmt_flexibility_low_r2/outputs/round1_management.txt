I hear the red lines you are setting, but I need to be equally clear: **the company’s current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**. German production is too expensive, capacity is too high for the market we now face, and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target. If we do not make a **real structural correction now**, we will simply postpone the crisis and make the eventual adjustment harsher.

So where I agree:
- Yes, this must be a **durable transformation**, not a one-off cut.
- Yes, **retraining, redeployment, working-time measures, and retirement-based exits** should be used where they genuinely deliver savings.
- Yes, this cannot be a purely top-down exercise; we need a negotiated settlement to make it implementable.

Where I disagree:
- **A blanket “no closures” position is not credible** if the result leaves us with the same uncompetitive cost base.
- **No compulsory redundancies** is not something I can simply accept if the numbers do not add up through voluntary and negotiated measures alone.
- A guarantee that effectively freezes the industrial footprint through 2030, regardless of market reality, would be a **cosmetic solution**. That is exactly what we cannot afford.

The union talks about shared sacrifice, and I agree with that principle — but it has to be real, measurable, and sufficient. That means:
- a **genuine labour-cost contribution**
- **material capacity reduction**
- a structure that brings down the fixed cost base on a lasting basis
- and conditions that allow the company to invest in future products and technology

I am not interested in symbolic gestures or temporary bridges. We need a package that changes the economics of the German operations. If that can be achieved without closures, I am open to the form. But if it cannot, then we cannot rule out deeper measures simply because they are difficult.

To be direct: **the burden of proof is on the employee side to show how you would achieve the necessary savings without structural plant and workforce changes**. If you can produce a package that truly restores competitiveness, I will engage. If not, management will continue to press for the more far-reaching route.
