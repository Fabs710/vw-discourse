We are listening carefully, and from a capital-markets perspective the key issue is not symbolism, it is whether this restructuring can **restore competitive margins on a durable basis**.

### Where we agree
- **The current cost base is too high.**
- **Capacity has to come down.**
- **A purely cosmetic settlement would be unacceptable.**
- **The company needs a credible industrial future with investment in next-generation products.**
- **A negotiated outcome is preferable to prolonged conflict if it delivers real savings and certainty.**

### Where we disagree
What we will not support is a package that:
- preserves the existing cost structure in a new form,
- delivers only temporary relief,
- leaves the German operations structurally uncompetitive,
- or protects every historic guarantee while asking capital to absorb the margin damage.

A “no closures” position may be politically understandable, but from our side it is **not sufficient on its own**. If the economics require deeper capacity rationalization, then that has to be confronted honestly. The same applies to compulsory redundancies: we prefer avoidance, but we will not endorse a settlement that fails to achieve the necessary cost base simply because the hardest tools are off the table.

### Our core test
We will support **whatever package credibly gets the company back to competitive margins** with:
- **durable cost reduction**
- **clear capacity alignment to demand**
- **deal certainty**
- **limited execution risk**
- and **no open-ended erosion of returns**

That means any alternative to closures must be more than a promise. It must show:
1. **How much savings** are delivered,
2. **When** they hit the P&L,
3. **How durable** they are,
4. **What capacity comes out**, and
5. **Why this is better than plant closure on a risk-adjusted basis**.

### To the Works Council and Union
We respect the emphasis on shared sacrifice, but we need to be blunt: worker protections only matter to us if the result is still economically viable. If the compromise leaves the company with:
- structurally impaired margins,
- continued overcapacity,
- or a permanently inflated fixed cost base,

then we would view that as shifting the burden onto shareholders through lower returns and weaker valuation.

We can accept negotiated labor contributions, working-time changes, redeployment, and retirement-based exits **if they are large enough and enforceable enough**. But we will not back a deal that simply buys time.

### To management
Your argument is credible only if it is specific. “Structural correction” is not enough. We need to see:
- quantified savings,
- plant- and function-level measures,
- implementation timing,
- and a clear bridge back to the return-on-sales target.

If plant closures are truly necessary, say so and justify them. If they are not necessary because an alternative package can do the job, then bring that package forward and demonstrate equivalence. We will back the solution that best protects enterprise value, not the one that is easiest to sell politically.

### To the state government and family owner
We understand the governance realities, but our patience is limited. Blocking every hard decision while the margin problem persists would be value-destructive. If the controlling shareholders want support from capital markets, they need to back a settlement that is **credible on economics**, not just balanced on stakeholder optics.

### Bottom line
We are **not** attached to closures as a matter of principle. But we are also **not** willing to accept a restructuring that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.

So our position is:
- **Yes** to durable restructuring.
- **Yes** to meaningful cost reduction and capacity cuts.
- **Yes** to negotiated alternatives if they truly deliver equivalent savings and certainty.
- **No** to cosmetic peace deals.
- **No** to preserving the old model in a slightly adjusted form.

Show us a package that credibly restores competitiveness, and we will support it. Show us a compromise that merely delays the necessary adjustment, and we will not.
