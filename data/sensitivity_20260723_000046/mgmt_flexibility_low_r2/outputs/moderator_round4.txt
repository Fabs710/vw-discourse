## Core impasse

The disagreement is no longer about **whether restructuring is needed**. All sides accept that.

The impasse sits at three linked points:

1. **Whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain available as fallback tools**
   - Employee side, state, and partly the family want them off the table.
   - Management and investors want them retained as leverage and contingency.

2. **Whether non-closure measures can be treated as sufficient**
   - Employee side says yes, at least as the primary route.
   - Management and investors say only if they close the quantified savings gap on a durable, timely basis.

3. **What constitutes a credible guarantee**
   - Employee side wants binding site/job security through 2030.
   - Management and investors want guarantees only if tied to economic performance and a fallback if targets are missed.

So the stalemate is essentially:  
**security first** vs **economic fallback first**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Time-limited bridge with hard triggers
A structured bridge period could be established for a fixed term, during which:
- no German plant closures are initiated,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- and the parties jointly implement the non-closure package.

In return, the bridge would include:
- quarterly savings verification,
- joint monitoring,
- predefined trigger points,
- and a clearly specified review date.

If the agreed savings milestones are met, the bridge extends into a longer site-security settlement.  
If they are not met, the parties reopen the question of deeper structural measures.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
It gives the employee side immediate protection while giving management/investors a performance-based review.

---

### Proposal 2: No-closure first, conditional fallback only after quantified exhaustion
The parties could agree on a sequencing rule:
1. Exhaust non-closure measures first.
2. Measure them against a jointly agreed savings target.
3. Only if a residual gap remains after verified implementation does the group revisit additional structural options.

To make this workable, the parties would need to define in advance:
- what counts as “exhausted,”
- what savings assumptions are bankable,
- and what evidence is required before fallback options can be discussed.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
It addresses management’s demand for economic realism while respecting the employee side’s insistence that closures not be the default starting point.

---

### Proposal 3: Site continuity with capacity reduction plus a protected review clause
The parties could commit to:
- keeping all German sites open for a defined period,
- reducing capacity through shifts, product-line consolidation, working-time adjustments, and redeployment,
- and inserting a review clause tied to site-by-site economics.

The review clause would not automatically authorize closures. It would instead trigger:
- independent verification of the residual gap,
- a fresh bargaining round,
- and escalation only if the gap cannot be closed by non-closure means.

**Why it may bridge the conflict:**  
It preserves the employee-side red line in the near term while giving management a structured opportunity to revisit unresolved economics later.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific non-closure measures are you prepared to quantify, site by site, as sufficient to close the remaining savings gap by 2027/2028 if wage moderation and working-time changes are only partially accepted?**

### Industrial Union
**Which combination of labor-cost, flexibility, and headcount measures would you regard as the maximum feasible package before you would acknowledge a verified residual gap?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings gap remains after your preferred non-closure measures are fully haircut-adjusted, and what evidence would you accept that closures are unnecessary?**

### Expert / Turnaround Finance Specialist
**Can you provide a site-by-site threshold showing at what residual gap level non-closure measures stop being economically comparable to closure economics?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete state-backed conditions would you require to support a bridge arrangement without enabling a later shift into closures or involuntary layoffs?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to support a time-limited no-closure settlement if it includes measurable burden sharing from owners and a binding review mechanism, or do you require an explicit fallback from the outset?**

### Institutional Investors
**What level of verified run-rate savings, by when, would make a no-closure package investable from your perspective?**
