I will be equally clear.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the company needs restructuring**. Costs must come down, capacity must be adjusted, and the EV transition needs a credible industrial plan. I also agree that a cosmetic deal would be worthless.

I also accept the expert’s core point: **non-closure measures can produce substantial savings** if they are designed seriously and backed by real commitments. That is exactly why we submitted a counter-proposal with labour-cost and capacity reductions. We are not denying reality.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the attempt to turn “closures and compulsory redundancies remain available” into the baseline bargaining position.

That is not neutral. That is a pressure tactic to move the burden onto workers while management and owners keep strategic freedom.

Let me state our limit plainly:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No German plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No open-ended insecurity for the workforce**

That is not a slogan. That is the boundary of what we can accept.

## To management
You say the burden is on us to show an “economically equivalent” alternative. I reject that framing.

If you want to break with the existing protections, then **you** must prove, site by site and measure by measure:
- the exact savings gap,
- what non-closure measures have already been tested,
- what they deliver,
- and why closures or involuntary layoffs are truly necessary rather than just more convenient.

So far, I hear a lot of “flexibility” and “durability,” but not a convincing business case that workers must pay through plant closures and forced redundancies.

If management wants serious negotiations, then bring the numbers:
- current and target cost base,
- savings by measure,
- timing,
- and what remains after non-closure measures.

## To the investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic peace deal would be worthless. We do not support that either.

But you are wrong if you believe “durable savings” only mean closures and layoffs. Durable savings also come from:
- fair labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reduction without closure,
- management overhead cuts,
- productivity improvements,
- and product/investment commitments that secure the volume base.

A settlement that blows up trust and industrial peace is not durable. It creates risk, not value.

## To the controlling family
If you truly care about long-term value, then act like long-term owners.

Long-term value is protected by:
- retaining skilled labour,
- preserving industrial peace,
- securing a stable bargaining framework,
- and maintaining production in Germany.

Threatening the workforce with plant closures first and asking for trust later is not long-term stewardship. It is value destruction through confrontation.

## To the regional state government
Your blocking role matters. Use it.

Do not help normalize a process where closures become the fallback after the workforce has already conceded enough. A regional anchor company should not be forced to solve its problems by cutting away its industrial base.

If you support a bridge, it must be a bridge to:
- binding site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and real reallocation of work inside Germany.

Not a bridge to closures later.

## On the moderator’s bridge options
### 1) Time-limited no-closure bridge
This can only be considered if it is not a Trojan horse.

A bridge period is acceptable only if it is explicitly tied to:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and employment guarantees through 2030**
- **measurable management and owner contributions**
- **joint monitoring with real co-determination**

If the bridge is just a pause before the same threat returns, it is not a compromise.

### 2) Site continuity plus capacity reduction
This is the only sensible direction.

We can reduce shifts, consolidate product lines, reallocate volumes, use working-time tools, and adjust internal flows. That is restructuring **without destroying sites**.

So yes to capacity reduction.  
No to closure.  
Yes to internal reallocation.  
No to forced redundancies.

### 3) Conditional protection with a fallback
Absolutely not, if the fallback is closures or involuntary layoffs.

A guarantee that disappears when management says the numbers are inconvenient is not a guarantee. It is insecurity with a deadline.

## What we can support
We can support a serious package only if it includes all of the following:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
4. **Capacity reduction through negotiated, reversible measures**
5. **Real burden sharing from management, owners, and capital**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the collective wage framework**

That is the floor.

## What we reject
We reject:
- closures disguised as “footprint optimization,”
- layoffs dressed up as “flexibility,”
- guarantees that can be revoked at management’s convenience,
- and any settlement that shifts structural risk entirely onto workers.

If management, owners, and investors want peace, they need to come back with a package that **keeps every German plant open and rules out compulsory redundancies**.

If they cannot do that, then they are not seeking compromise. They are asking the workforce to absorb the company’s strategic mistakes.

## Bottom line
We do not need more lectures about realism. We need a real settlement.

And the reality is this: **the company can restructure without plant closures and without forced layoffs if the burden is shared fairly and if management stops treating worker protections as negotiable collateral.**

We will not move off that line because others repeat pressure points. If collaboration is refused, we will use every co-determination right, every supervisory-board vote, and every mobilization tool available to defend it.
