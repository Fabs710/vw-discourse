We are aligned on the goal, but not on the standard of proof.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert that:
- the German cost base is too high,
- capacity has to come down,
- the current margin profile is not acceptable,
- and a cosmetic peace deal would destroy value.

We also agree with the works council, union, and state government on one important point:
- a prolonged strike or open conflict would be value-destructive.

So yes, restructuring is necessary. That is not in dispute.

## Where we disagree
Our disagreement is with the employee side and the state when they try to make **“no closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** absolute starting points.

That is not a financially credible restructuring doctrine. It is a preferred outcome.

The expert was helpful here: non-closure measures can cover a **large share** of the gap, sometimes even most of it. But the same expert also made clear that they do **not always replicate closure economics**, especially when the structural gap is large and timing matters. That is exactly our concern.

We do **not** support a package that leaves the company with:
- structurally impaired margins,
- an uncompetitive cost base,
- and no real fallback if voluntary measures underdeliver.

## To management
We agree with your core concern: the savings must be **durable**, not symbolic. We also agree that voluntary exits, retirement-based reduction, redeployment, working-time tools, overhead cuts, and productivity improvements should be used aggressively.

But we need you to be equally disciplined:
- quantify the gap by site and function,
- show what non-closure measures really deliver,
- and separate bankable savings from optimistic assumptions.

If you want investor backing, we need a model, not a slogan.

[SUMMON: Turnaround finance specialist to present a site-by-site bridge between non-closure measures and closure economics, with run-rate savings, one-time costs, and timing to 2027/2028.]

## To the Works Council
We hear your insistence on no closures and no forced redundancies. We understand the social and governance logic, but we do **not** accept it as an economic guarantee.

If you want to keep every German plant open, then you need to show how the package closes the gap:
- by how much wages/flexibility move,
- how much headcount really comes out,
- what fixed cost is removed,
- and how quickly the savings hit the P&L.

“Keep all sites open” is not enough. The question is whether that also restores competitive margins. If not, then it is not a solution.

## To the Industrial Union
We appreciate that you are willing to discuss labor-cost and flexibility concessions. But your position still seems to assume the company should absorb all strategic risk while the workforce gets full protection.

That does not work for capital, and it does not work for long-term value.

If the business case requires deeper cuts, then voluntary-only solutions may be insufficient on timing and scale. We are not asking you to like that. We are saying you cannot simply exclude it and still expect the savings target to be met.

## To the Regional State Government
We agree with your regional responsibility argument. But a blocking role cannot be used to freeze the company in place.

A bridge period is fine only if it is a bridge to a **quantified** outcome:
- real savings,
- real capacity reduction,
- real time-bound commitments,
- and a clear fallback if the numbers do not work.

A bridge without a hard economic test is just delay.

## To the Controlling Family
We agree that long-term value matters and that a value-destructive conflict would be harmful. But long-term value is not protected by preserving every historic promise regardless of market conditions.

If the residual gap remains after non-closure measures, you need to retain the option of deeper structural action. That is not anti-social. It is basic value protection.

## Our position
We are prepared to support a hybrid settlement only if it satisfies all of the following:
1. **Quantified, durable savings**
2. **A clear path back to competitive margins**
3. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
4. **Real labor and management contributions**
5. **A fallback if non-closure measures underdeliver**

What we will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that protects every site while margins stay impaired,
- or a settlement that rules out the only tools that may ultimately be needed to restore competitiveness.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend closure as a principle. We are here to defend **risk-adjusted capital value**.

If a no-closure package truly closes the gap on a run-rate basis by 2027/2028, with credible durability, we can support it. If it does not, then closures and involuntary reductions must remain available.

That is the standard. Not sentiment, not politics, not optics. Competitive margins, durable savings, and deal certainty.
