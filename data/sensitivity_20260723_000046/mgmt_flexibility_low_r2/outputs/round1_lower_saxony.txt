As the regional state shareholder, I will be very clear: **I agree that the company needs a credible restructuring**. I do **not** agree that the only way to achieve it is to threaten German plants and jobs as if they were disposable.

### Where I agree
- The cost base has to come down.
- Capacity has to be adjusted to market reality.
- The transformation to electric mobility needs a viable industrial plan, not wishful thinking.
- A purely symbolic “restructuring” would be irresponsible.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the logic that plant closures and mass job cuts are the default answer.

That approach would:
- damage the regional economy in a way that goes far beyond the company
- undermine trust in the company’s social partnership model
- create political and industrial conflict that will make implementation harder, not easier
- weaken the long-term industrial anchor role that this group still has in Germany

I also do **not** accept an approach that treats the workforce as the only side that must absorb pain. If management and owners want a durable settlement, then the burden must be shared in a way that is **socially fair and economically credible**.

### What I can support
I am open to a package that includes:
- **capacity reduction without closures where possible**
- **redeployment, qualification, and phased exits**
- **working-time solutions and temporary sacrifices**
- **a negotiated employee contribution**
- **clear investment commitments for future products and technologies**
- **binding job and site guarantees for any sites that remain viable**

### What I cannot support
I will not support:
- **plant closures in Germany as a first resort**
- **forced redundancies**
- **a restructuring that guts regional employment**
- **any attempt to sideline the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **a deal that asks workers to surrender security while capital keeps full flexibility**

### On management’s position
Management keeps saying the burden of proof is on the employee side. I would reverse that in part: **the burden is also on management to demonstrate that it has exhausted all alternatives before reaching for closures**. If the company wants legitimacy for deep cuts, it must show:
- why internal restructuring is insufficient,
- what contribution is being made by shareholders and management,
- and how it intends to preserve the company’s social and regional responsibilities.

### On the union and works council
Your insistence on shared sacrifice is understandable, and I think it is the right framework. But shared sacrifice only works if it is tied to **shared protection**. If employees contribute, they must receive something tangible in return:
- security,
- a long-term industrial commitment,
- and a credible future for the plants that remain.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to engage constructively on a difficult restructuring. But I will **not** back a solution that relies on closures and large-scale job destruction in the state. If there is a serious alternative package that restores competitiveness **without hollowing out the company’s regional base**, I will support it. If not, then you should expect me to use every vote and every board right available to me.

What I need to hear next is not a repetition that “something must give,” but a **specific plan** showing how competitiveness will be restored **without breaking the company’s social foundation**.
