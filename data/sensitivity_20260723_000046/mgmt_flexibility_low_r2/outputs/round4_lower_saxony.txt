I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, investors, the works council, the union, and the expert on the **diagnosis**:
- the company needs restructuring,
- costs must come down,
- capacity must be adjusted,
- and a cosmetic peace deal would be worthless.

I also agree that **non-closure measures can deliver real, material savings**. That is not in dispute. Voluntary exits, retirement-based reductions, redeployment, working-time tools, wage moderation, management overhead cuts, and productivity improvements all belong in the toolbox.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the attempt to turn “closures and compulsory redundancies remain available” into the default logic of the process.

That is the core problem. It is not a neutral technicality. It is a choice to put the burden of adjustment primarily on the workforce and on regional industrial stability.

### To management
You say the employee side must prove an “economically equivalent” alternative. I reject that framing.

If you want to break with the existing job-security framework and keep closures and involuntary reductions on the table, then **you** must show, site by site and measure by measure:
- the exact savings gap,
- what non-closure measures have already been quantified,
- what they deliver,
- and why deeper structural measures are truly unavoidable.

So far, I hear a lot of “flexibility” and “durability,” but not enough hard proof that closures or forced layoffs are necessary rather than simply more convenient.

### To investors
You are right to insist on durability. But durability is not the same as permanent social damage.

A restructuring that destroys trust, triggers strikes, weakens co-determination, and destabilizes the regional supplier base is not durable in any serious sense. It may improve a spreadsheet for a quarter; it increases execution risk for years.

### To the controlling family
If you mean long-term value, then you must act like long-term owners. Long-term value in this company depends on:
- skilled labor retention,
- industrial peace,
- continued German production,
- and stable governance.

That means closures must not become the first resort, and they certainly must not become the assumed baseline.

### To the Works Council and union
I support your core insistence that the workforce must not be treated as the sole adjustment variable. But I also want to keep the discussion economically grounded. A purely defensive stance is not enough if we want to preserve the company over the long term.

## What the expert has actually said
The expert’s input is important, and it does **not** justify the management’s preferred reading.

What I heard is:
- non-closure measures can cover a **substantial share** of the gap,
- in some cases even most of it,
- but the outcome depends on the **size of the gap**, **timing**, and **how deep the labor and management contributions are**.

That supports a serious negotiated restructuring. It does **not** support treating closures as inevitable from the outset.

## My position
I am prepared to back a package that:
- protects German sites,
- preserves jobs as far as realistically possible,
- uses non-closure measures first,
- and includes real contributions from management, owners, and capital.

But I will not support a process that starts from the assumption that German plant closures are a normal or acceptable fallback.

That is where I draw the line:
- **no plant closures as the starting solution,**
- **no compulsory redundancies as the default,**
- **no sidelining of co-determination or the state’s veto role.**

These are not bargaining ornaments. They are core to what makes this company a regional anchor rather than just another balance-sheet object.

## What would move me
What could move me is a **quantified, site-by-site restructuring package** that shows:
1. what savings non-closure measures deliver,
2. what remains,
3. what management and shareholders contribute,
4. and how jobs, sites, and future investment in the state are protected.

That is the right next step.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- closures disguised as “footprint optimization,”
- layoffs dressed up as “flexibility,”
- guarantees that can be withdrawn whenever management gets impatient,
- or a restructuring that treats the workforce as the only shock absorber.

If the other parties want my support, they need to come back with a serious package that keeps the company competitive **without sacrificing the regional industrial base by default**.

## Bottom line
I am not here to ratify an ideology of downsizing. I am here to protect the company as a long-term industrial anchor in the state, while helping it become viable again.

If management and owners cannot show that a no-closure or near-no-closure package is insufficient, then they should not be demanding closures as a matter of principle. And if they can show it, then they still have to explain why they have not exhausted the alternatives.

That is the standard I will apply.
