I will be equally direct.

I agree with management on one point: this cannot be solved by symbolism. The company needs a durable turnaround, and it needs investment capacity for future products. I also agree with the ownership side that implementation certainty matters — a deal that collapses in the supervisory board or in industrial relations is not a solution.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only serious route is plant closures, forced redundancies, or dismantling the German industrial base. That is where I draw the line.

### Where I agree
- The current cost structure is not competitive enough.
- The company needs real, durable savings.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to prolonged open conflict.
- The workforce will have to contribute, and management and owners must also contribute.
- The end state must be economically credible, not a cosmetic compromise.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) No plant closures as a first response in Germany
Management keeps treating closures as a normal instrument. For me, they are not a normal instrument. As an anchor shareholder and regional government, I cannot support a path that starts by sacrificing sites and regional jobs before all other options have been exhausted.

If you want my support, you need to show that comparable savings can be achieved through:
- capacity reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

#### 2) No compulsory redundancies
I do not accept forced layoffs as the fallback solution. Not in a company that is central to the region’s economy, and not when there are still negotiated, socially responsible alternatives available.

#### 3) No bypassing of co-determination or the special shareholder protections
Any path that sidelines the workforce representation or ignores the state’s blocking position is not going to pass. That is not a procedural detail; it is a core governance reality.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that this cannot be a one-sided restructuring. The workforce has already contributed, and it is reasonable to demand that management and ownership contribute visibly as well.

That said, I need to be frank: a blanket “no” to every capacity adjustment is not enough if the company truly has structural overcapacity. I am prepared to back a negotiated solution that protects plants and jobs, but it must still deliver real operational change and measurable savings.

So I would urge you to sharpen the alternative:
- What exact capacity can be reduced without closures?
- What working-time or redeployment measures can close the gap?
- What employee contribution is on the table, and under what guarantees?
- What investment commitments are needed to make this durable?

### To management
Your argument for competitiveness is understood. But you are underestimating the political, legal, and industrial limits of what can actually be implemented here.

If you come with a plan that assumes closures and layoffs will simply be accepted, you will run into a wall: in the supervisory board, in collective bargaining, and in the region. A plan that cannot survive those realities is not credible.

If you want the regional state to support a restructuring, then present a package that:
- preserves core sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- includes real savings,
- and secures the company’s long-term footprint in Germany.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that the company needs financial discipline. But discipline is not the same as forcing through the most destructive option.

A package that destroys trust, triggers industrial conflict, and weakens the company’s regional anchor role may look clean on paper, but it creates long-term risks that do not show up immediately in your models. I will not support a solution that improves short-term optics at the expense of long-term resilience.

### My position, concretely
I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** if it includes:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- capacity adjustments instead of site destruction,
- a fair burden-sharing contribution from employees,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- and preservation of the state’s co-determination and veto role.

I am **not** prepared to support:
- closure-led restructuring,
- mass layoffs,
- or any deal that turns the company into a weaker employer in the region.

### Bottom line
I am open to tough restructuring. I am **not** open to deindustrialization.

If management wants my support, it needs to come back with a serious alternative that delivers durable competitiveness **without** gutting jobs and sites in the state. Otherwise, I will use the leverage that the special statute and our board position give us.
