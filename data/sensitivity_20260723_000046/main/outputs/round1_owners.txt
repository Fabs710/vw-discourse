As the controlling family holding, I need to speak plainly.

I agree with management on the central diagnosis: the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable, and delay only destroys value. If the company cannot restore margins and competitiveness, then no stakeholder wins in the long run. That is the reality.

But I also agree with the political and workforce side on one essential point: a plan that triggers prolonged confrontation, strikes, and governance deadlock would itself be value-destructive. The company cannot be turned around through permanent instability.

So my position is:

1. **A meaningful restructuring is necessary.**  
   Cosmetic measures will not suffice. We need durable savings, not accounting tricks.

2. **The objective is competitiveness, not symbolism.**  
   I will not support a package that looks socially elegant but leaves the business structurally weak.

3. **At the same time, we should avoid unnecessary destruction of industrial value.**  
   If comparable savings can be achieved through capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, site specialization, redeployment, and negotiated labour contributions, that is preferable to abrupt closures.

Where I disagree with the works council and union is on the absolute red lines they are announcing. “Every German plant must remain open” and “no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances” may be understandable as bargaining positions, but they cannot be the basis for a credible turnaround unless the savings are demonstrably sufficient. The company’s future cannot be subordinated to slogans.

Where I disagree with management is any assumption that the ownership side will back a plan that permanently impairs the enterprise by provoking a breakdown in labour peace or by forcing an outcome that cannot be implemented under co-determination. A legally or industrially impossible plan is not a plan.

My test is simple:

- **Does the package restore competitiveness and margins?**
- **Does it do so durably, not temporarily?**
- **Does it preserve the company’s ability to invest in the future?**
- **Does it avoid an uncontrolled escalation that damages value for everyone?**

If the answer is yes, the family holding will support it through governance. If the answer is no, we will not support a deal just because it is politically comfortable.

So I would urge all sides to stop treating the discussion as a choice between total closure and total preservation. That framing is not serious. What is serious is a restructuring that is tough enough to work, but stable enough to survive.

I am prepared to back a negotiated solution that includes real workforce contribution and real operational changes. I am **not** prepared to back a settlement that protects every legacy structure at the expense of the company’s long-term value, nor one that pushes through cuts so aggressively that it destroys industrial peace and ultimately value.

That is the standard the ownership side will apply.
