Core impasse identified:

1. **Red-line conflict on structural options**
   - Labour side: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no hidden closure fallback.
   - Management / owners / investors: want enough durable savings that may require partial footprint reduction or closures if the gap is large.

2. **Disagreement on what counts as a credible restructuring**
   - Labour side treats a no-closure package as viable if sufficiently broad and binding.
   - Management / owners / investors argue that a no-closure package may be too weak unless it reaches the upper end of feasible savings.

3. **Trust and sequencing problem**
   - Labour side rejects “review gates” as deferred closure threats.
   - Management and owners reject open-ended guarantees without an escape route if targets are missed.
   - So the real blockage is not only economics, but **how to make commitments binding without making them feel reversible or fake**.

4. **Burden-sharing dispute**
   - Workers accept contribution, but not disproportionate burden.
   - Management and owners want larger structural relief.
   - State wants jobs protected but also a viable company.
   - This makes the key issue: **who contributes how much, for how long, and with what safeguards**.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Binding no-closure package with a quantified trigger system
A compromise could be structured as:
- **All German plants remain open for a fixed protection period**.
- In that period, the parties agree to a **fully quantified savings package** with:
  - voluntary exits,
  - retirement bridges,
  - working-time and shift reductions,
  - overhead and management cuts,
  - temporary wage moderation,
  - and investment commitments.
- A **joint monitoring body** tracks quarterly delivery.
- If targets are missed, the response is **not automatic closure**, but a pre-agreed second-step menu:
  - deeper temporary concessions,
  - added management cuts,
  - additional line/shift rationalization,
  - external productivity review.
- Only if those steps also fail would the parties reopen a higher-level structural review.

This keeps the first phase closure-free, while giving management a credible escalation path.

### Proposal 2: Partial-footprint adjustment without plant closure
A second bridge could be:
- No outright plant closures in this round.
- Instead, agree to **capacity consolidation** through:
  - shift eliminations,
  - line transfers,
  - internal redeployment,
  - product allocation changes,
  - and support-function consolidation.
- This would be paired with:
  - no compulsory redundancies during the first phase,
  - a larger voluntary departure programme,
  - and a defined management and owner contribution.

This may offer more savings than a pure no-closure deal while avoiding the symbolic and social shock of closure.

### Proposal 3: Time-bound protection plus review with non-closure priorities
A third compromise:
- German plants receive a **time-bound protection clause**.
- During that period, the company must exhaust a defined hierarchy:
  1. management and overhead cuts
  2. voluntary labour measures
  3. working-time and productivity measures
  4. selective footprint rationalization without closure
- A review then occurs only after those instruments are proven insufficient.
- Any later review would be constrained by a requirement to **explain why non-closure measures failed**, rather than treating closure as the default fallback.

This preserves the labour side’s concern about hidden closure threats while not permanently foreclosing structural change.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
If the company’s savings requirement proves to be at the upper end of the expert’s range, what exact combination of voluntary exits, shift cuts, overhead reductions, and productivity gains would you accept as a quantified no-closure package?

### To Industrial Union
What is the minimum annual recurring savings level you would consider sufficient to support a durable no-closure settlement, and what temporary employee contributions would you be prepared to trade for binding plant guarantees?

### To Management
What is the smallest quantified savings package you would accept as credible without closures, and what specific management-owned cuts would you commit to before asking for plant or job reductions?

### To Regional State Government
What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to turn your no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy position into an enforceable negotiating position rather than a political statement?

### To Controlling Family Holding
Under what exact savings threshold would you still prefer a no-closure or partial-footprint solution over closures, and what evidence would you accept that the non-closure alternative is durable enough?

### To Institutional Investors
What level of recurring savings, implementation certainty, and timetable would make you view a no-closure package as investable rather than merely symbolic?

### To the Expert
What is the highest realistic recurring savings figure for a no-closure package if all non-closure instruments are used aggressively, and at what point does that package become economically indistinguishable from partial-footprint reduction?
