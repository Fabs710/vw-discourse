## Core impasse
The plateau is not over whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **what counts as a credible restructuring path**:

- **Management, investors, and the controlling family** want a package that is **durable, quantified, and large enough**, and they want to preserve the option of **closures and compulsory redundancies** if needed.
- **Works council and union** will only support a package if it **rules out German plant closures and compulsory redundancies** and preserves co-determination and job-security guarantees.
- **The state government** is trying to hold the line against closures and forced redundancies while still avoiding a weak or purely symbolic deal.

So the blockage is:
1. **scope of permissible instruments**  
2. **whether “no closures” can still be made quantitatively sufficient**  
3. **how to create implementation certainty without triggering industrial conflict or governance deadlock**

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Time-bound no-closure package with hard review gates
A compromise could be built around:
- **no German plant closures for a defined period**,
- **no compulsory redundancies during that period**,
- a **quantified savings target** with binding measures,
- and a **hard review gate** after 12–24 months.

At the review gate, the parties would assess:
- whether savings were delivered,
- whether volumes and margins improved,
- and whether additional structural measures are required.

To reduce the fear of a “Trojan horse,” the package could include:
- explicit limits on what can be reviewed,
- mandatory joint consultation before any later footprint change,
- and agreed triggers for renegotiation.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It gives the workforce side immediate protection, while giving management and investors a route to later structural adjustment if the numbers do not work.

---

### Proposal 2: “No closure first” combined with a quantified partial-footprint fallback
The parties could agree on a two-step structure:

**Step 1:** a no-closure savings package using:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time changes,
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- management contributions,
- overhead reduction,
- productivity measures.

**Step 2:** if the agreed savings target is not reached, then a **predefined partial-footprint reduction** is activated as the fallback.

That fallback could be limited to:
- one site,
- one line,
- one function,
- or one product area,
rather than full closures.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It avoids immediate closure politics while giving capital and management a credible path to structural savings if voluntary measures fall short.

---

### Proposal 3: Shared-sacrifice package with binding management and owner contributions
A bridge package could require all sides to contribute in a defined order:
1. **management and owners first**
2. **employees second**
3. **structural measures only if the gap remains**

Possible elements:
- executive pay restraint,
- dividend moderation or deferred capital returns,
- management headcount reduction,
- employee working-time and pay concessions,
- investment commitments in Germany,
- and a jointly monitored savings dashboard.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It responds to the union and works council demand that workers not pay alone, while helping management and investors see a credible, measurable burden-sharing structure.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies remain fixed red lines, **what exact annual savings number can you deliver through voluntary exits, working-time, productivity, and overhead measures, and by when?**

### To the Industrial Union
What specific employee contribution are you prepared to put on the table that is **temporary, measurable, and large enough** to matter in the run-rate, not just symbolically?

### To Group & Brand Management
What is your **minimum acceptable savings package** in quantified terms, and which elements are truly non-negotiable versus bargaining positions?

### To the Regional State Government
If you reject closures and forced redundancies, **what binding support, pressure, or incentives will you use to ensure management and capital still accept a package that is large enough to work?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
What is your threshold for accepting a non-closure compromise, and are you prepared to support **binding owner contributions** if that is what makes the negotiated solution viable?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a package that avoids closures but includes a **credible, audited, multi-year savings path** and a predefined fallback if targets are missed?

### To the Expert
What is the smallest restructuring package, in quantified terms, that you would still regard as likely to close the gap **without relying on immediate plant closures**?
