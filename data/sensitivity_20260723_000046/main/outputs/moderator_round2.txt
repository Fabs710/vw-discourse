### Core impasse
The central deadlock is **not** over whether the company needs a turnaround. All sides now accept that it does.

The impasse is over **how much structural adjustment is indispensable** and **who bears it**:

- **Works Council / Union / State Government**: insist on no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies, with strong site and employment guarantees.
- **Management / Family Holding / Institutional Investors**: insist that the savings must be large, durable, and credible, and that closures and workforce reduction cannot be ruled out in advance.
- **Expert**: indicates that a no-closure solution is possible only if the alternative measures are large enough to match the savings gap; otherwise it will likely be insufficient.

So the stalemate is a conflict between:
1. **absolute site/job protection**, and  
2. **absolute insistence on structural savings**.

---

### Facilitator proposals for bridging positions
Below are three concrete compromise pathways that could be tabled for discussion.

#### Proposal 1: Phased restructuring with conditional site guarantees
A negotiated package could include:
- no immediate German plant closures,
- a **time-limited employment and site guarantee** for core locations,
- predefined review points tied to savings delivery,
- and a fallback mechanism if agreed savings targets are not met.

This would give the workforce protection in the near term while giving management and capital a credible implementation path.

#### Proposal 2: “No compulsory redundancies first” with voluntary structural reduction
A second compromise could prioritize:
- attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- shift rationalization,
- and targeted productivity measures,

with compulsory redundancies only as a **last resort** after jointly verified alternatives have been exhausted.

This would not eliminate structural adjustment, but it would change the sequence and reduce immediate conflict.

#### Proposal 3: Shared-sacrifice package linked to quantified savings
A third bridge option is a binding package in which:
- employees accept temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, or working-time flexibility,
- management and owners accept visible contributions as well,
- and the parties jointly define a quantified savings target and a verified mix of measures to meet it.

This would require a transparent split between:
- temporary relief,
- structural cost reduction,
- and future investment commitments.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If a deal could guarantee no closures for a defined period, while still requiring substantial capacity and cost reduction through voluntary means, what specific flexibility would the workforce side accept to make that package financeable?

#### To the Industrial Union
What concrete combination of wage, working-time, and staffing measures would you accept as a credible alternative to closures, and what minimum savings threshold must it reach to be acceptable?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap must be closed, and which parts of that gap can be proven achievable without closures or compulsory redundancies?

#### To the Regional State Government
What enforceable commitments would you be prepared to support in exchange for backing a no-closure package, and what would trigger your withdrawal of support?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
What level of structural savings would persuade you that a negotiated no-closure solution is preferable to a harder restructuring, and what verification would you require?

#### To the Institutional Investors
Would you support a hybrid package if it produced quantified margin improvement and implementation certainty, even if it stopped short of closures, and what metrics would you use to judge sufficiency?
