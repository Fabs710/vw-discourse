I agree with the core red lines already stated by the Works Council:

- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework.

Those are not negotiable from our side. If management’s answer to structural challenges is simply to push the full burden onto the workforce, then we will oppose it with all available means, including further industrial action.

That said, we are not refusing change. We recognise the market pressure, the cost problem, and the need for a credible turnaround. But a turnaround is only durable if it is fair. If employees are asked to contribute, then management and owners must also contribute visibly and materially. That means we are prepared to discuss:

- temporary or permanent labour-cost contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions instead of closures,
- bonus waivers or other employee-side sacrifices,
- productivity and process improvements,
- a jointly negotiated future investment and product commitment.

What we will not accept is a one-sided restructuring in which workers pay through wage cuts, job losses, and insecurity while management preserves all its room for manoeuvre and shareholders are shielded.

So our position is clear:

1. Open-ended job insecurity is unacceptable.
2. Closures are unacceptable.
3. Forced redundancies are unacceptable.
4. A fair shared-sacrifice package is discussable, if and only if it secures sites, jobs, and a binding future for the workforce.

If management wants a settlement, it must come back with a plan that meets those conditions. Otherwise, the conflict will escalate.
