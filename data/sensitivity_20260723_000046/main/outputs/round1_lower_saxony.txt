As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: I do **not** accept a restructuring path that solves the cost problem by gutting the company’s German industrial base.

I agree with management on one point: the status quo is not sustainable, and a durable turnaround is necessary. I also agree that the company needs room for investment in future products and technology. But I do **not** accept the premise that the only serious route is plant closures or a regime of job destruction. That would be economically shortsighted and politically irresponsible.

My position is this:

1. **No plant closures in Germany as the default solution.**  
   Closing sites in this state or elsewhere in Germany would damage regional economies, destroy skilled jobs, and weaken the company’s long-term resilience. That is not an acceptable first response to structural pressure.

2. **No forced redundancies.**  
   We can discuss workforce reduction only if it is achieved through socially responsible, negotiated, and reversible measures. Compulsory layoffs would cross a line.

3. **A real restructuring is still possible without breaking the social partnership.**  
   I am open to capacity adjustments, working-time arrangements, internal redeployment, productivity measures, and a fair employee contribution — but only as part of a package that preserves sites, protects core employment, and secures the company’s future in Germany.

4. **Management and owners must also contribute.**  
   If employees are expected to make sacrifices, then the leadership must show the same seriousness through operational discipline, investment commitments, and a credible industrial strategy. Workers cannot be the sole shock absorber for strategic missteps.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that this cannot be a one-sided sacrifice. The state will back a negotiated solution that protects sites, jobs, and co-determination. But I also expect you to engage constructively on measures that improve competitiveness, because protecting the company means protecting its future ability to employ people here.

To management: if you come with a plan that effectively bypasses co-determination, undermines the special shareholder protections, or treats closures as the easy answer, you will run into a firm veto. If, however, you present a serious alternative that achieves savings without dismantling the regional industrial footprint, I am prepared to work toward it.

So my line is simple: **restructuring yes, deindustrialization no.**
