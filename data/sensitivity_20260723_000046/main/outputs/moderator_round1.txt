### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the current cost and capacity structure is **not sustainable** and that a **durable turnaround** is needed.
- There is broad support for **continued investment in future products and technology**.
- Most parties prefer a **negotiated solution** over an uncontrolled escalation.
- There is openness, in principle, to a mix of measures such as **capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, redeployment, productivity improvements, and employee-side contributions**.
- No party wants a purely cosmetic settlement that fails to deliver real change.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works Council, union, state government reject them; management, family holding, and investors say they may be necessary if savings require them.
- **Forced redundancies:** Workforce side and state government reject them; management and capital-side actors will not rule them out in principle.
- **Scale of restructuring:** Worker and political side want restructuring without dismantling the German industrial base; management and investors insist on substantial, durable savings and capacity reduction.
- **Binding guarantees:** Workforce side wants site and employment guarantees through 2030; management and capital-side actors say such guarantees may be unrealistic without credible structural change.
- **Interpretation of co-determination and governance limits:** Worker/political side emphasize negotiated, socially responsible change; management and capital-side actors emphasize the need for implementable, economically sufficient measures.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required** versus **how much employment/site protection can be preserved**.
- Whether the turnaround can be achieved mainly through **internal efficiency and flexibility** or whether it requires **structural footprint reductions**.
- How to divide the burden between **employees, management, owners, and possibly public stakeholders**.
- Whether any package can combine **implementation certainty**, **industrial peace**, and **adequate competitiveness**.
- What specific measures could replace closures or layoffs if they prove necessary to close the cost gap.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: ask each side to specify the minimum acceptable package, including target savings, acceptable instruments, and non-negotiables.
- Test **alternative scenarios** side by side: full preservation, partial capacity reduction, redeployment-heavy model, and mixed restructuring.
- Clarify what each side is prepared to **contribute concretely** if others also contribute.
- Focus the next round on a **single question**: what package can deliver enough competitiveness improvement to be credible, while remaining implementable under co-determination and socially manageable?
