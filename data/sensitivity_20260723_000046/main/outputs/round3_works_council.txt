I’ll be equally direct.

The expert has done one useful thing: it confirms what we have said from the start — **there is no need to jump to closures or forced redundancies if management is willing to do the hard work of a negotiated restructuring**. That is the point. The burden is on management and owners to prove that they need to destroy jobs and sites. So far, they have not.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on three points:
- The current situation is **not sustainable**.
- The company needs a **durable turnaround**, not a cosmetic settlement.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.

I also take seriously the expert’s warning that a weak, symbolic deal would fail. We are not offering that. We are offering a **serious restructuring without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Management is still treating layoffs and closures as the default
You say closures and workforce reduction cannot be ruled out in advance. That is exactly the problem. If you start from that premise, then you have already decided who pays: the workforce.

That is not acceptable.

The expert also said that alternative measures can work if the package is large enough. Good. Then let’s have a package large enough — but **without sacrificing German plants or imposing forced layoffs**.

#### 2) The family holding and investors are demanding an “economically clean” solution at the expense of social stability
You keep asking for “credible savings” and “implementation certainty.” Fine. But a settlement that tears up trust, triggers strikes, and violates co-determination is not certain — it is unstable.

A plan is not credible if it cannot survive in industrial relations and governance. That is not a side issue; it is the core implementation risk.

#### 3) The state government is right about no closures, but too cautious in confronting management
You say you will not support closures or forced redundancies. That is good. But it must be more than a warning. If management keeps pushing the line that layoffs are inevitable, then the state shareholder should use its veto position and political weight accordingly. Otherwise the pressure is one-sided.

### On the expert’s scenarios
I want to be clear: **we do not accept “phased restructuring” if it is just a Trojan horse for later closures**. We have seen this game before. First comes a guarantee “for now,” then comes a review point, then the closure is sold as unavoidable.

No. If there is a deal, it must protect:
- every German plant,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- co-determination rights,
- and the job-security framework.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not saying there should be no change. We are saying the change must be made through tools that do not destroy the workforce’s future. We are prepared to discuss:

- capacity reductions through **shift rationalization** and **production rebalancing**
- **internal redeployment** across sites
- **working-time flexibility**
- **temporary and fair wage restraint**
- **bonus moderation or deferral**
- **productivity and process improvements**
- **management and owner contributions**
- **investment commitments tied to Germany**
- **voluntary exit programs**, only if truly voluntary and not used to force people out

What we will not accept is:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or any “conditional guarantee” that is really just a pretext for later site destruction.

### To management
If you want savings, then bring forward a quantified plan that shows:
- how much comes from overhead reduction,
- how much from process and productivity improvements,
- how much from working-time and shift changes,
- how much from management contribution,
- and how much from voluntary measures.

Do that first. Then we can discuss whether it is sufficient.

What you will not get from us is a blank cheque for closures or forced layoffs because you say the numbers are hard.

### To the union
We are aligned on the red lines. But I want to sharpen one point: any employee contribution must be **strictly temporary, strictly fair, and strictly tied to binding guarantees**. We cannot let temporary concessions become a permanent rollback of job-security and collective protections.

### To the state government
If you are serious, then say it plainly in the boardroom: **no closure-led restructuring in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of co-determination**. Then back that up with your vote and your influence.

### To the family holding and investors
You keep saying the burden of proof is on those defending jobs. I reverse that: the burden of proof is on those proposing to dismantle sites and impose forced layoffs. Show me that every alternative has been exhausted, and show me the numbers. Until then, closures are not “necessary”; they are a choice.

### Bottom line
Our position remains unchanged:
- **all German plants stay open**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **co-determination and job-security guarantees preserved**
- **real restructuring through negotiated, socially responsible measures**

If management wants a settlement, it must come back with a plan that meets those conditions. Otherwise, the works council will continue to resist in the supervisory board, in the plants, and in public.
