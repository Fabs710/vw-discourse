We are not moving off the core line.

### To management
You are still treating closures and compulsory redundancies as if they were ordinary bargaining chips. They are not. For us, they are the destruction of trust, livelihoods, and the company’s social foundation. And let me be precise: **we will not accept any package that includes a German plant closure or forced layoffs**.

You say the business needs durable savings. We do not deny the pressure. But you have **not** yet shown that the only route to savings is tearing up job security and threatening sites. If you want our cooperation, you need to present a package that does all of the following:

- **keeps every German plant open**
- **rules out compulsory redundancies**
- **protects the job-security framework through 2030**
- **delivers measurable cost relief through alternatives**
- **includes binding investment and product commitments for Germany**

That is the standard. Anything less is not a solution we can support.

### To the family shareholder and investors
You both talk about “durable savings” and “competitiveness,” but from where workers sit, that language is being used to justify shifting the burden downward. We reject that logic.

If you want savings, then look at the full toolbox before demanding closures:
- working-time adjustments
- temporary wage moderation
- internal redeployment
- qualification and productivity gains
- management restraint and contribution at the top
- site-specific efficiency measures
- capacity reduction **without** plant shutdowns

A deal that preserves shareholder control but sacrifices sites and jobs is not acceptable. And if you think a prolonged conflict is costly, then understand this clearly: **forced restructuring against the workforce will be more costly**. We can mobilise warning strikes, and we have already shown that.

### To the state government
We appreciate your recognition that the industrial base must not be hollowed out. But that means more than “preserving as much as possible.” It means using your influence to stop management from making German jobs the adjustment variable.

Your role is not to bless closures in the name of realism. Your role is to ensure that the company uses its alternative options first, and that any restructuring respects co-determination, regional employment, and social stability.

### On the quantified options
You asked for concrete trade-offs. Here is ours:

- **Zero German plant closures**
- **Zero compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site guarantees to 2030**
- **A negotiated employee contribution**, but only temporary and only if shared fairly
- **Capacity reductions through shifts, working-time measures, and internal redeployment**
- **Investment commitments tied to future models and technologies**
- **No weakening of co-determination or the collective job-security regime**

If management wants to quantify savings, then quantify how much can be saved through:
- short-time or flexible working arrangements
- temporary reductions in premiums or special payments
- internally agreed productivity improvements
- voluntary programs only
- procurement and overhead reductions
- investment in more efficient production processes

What we will not do is pre-authorize closures or dismissals just because management prefers a brutal shortcut.

### On “objective criteria” for closures or layoffs
For us, the criterion is simple: **if a measure destroys a German plant or imposes forced redundancies, it is unacceptable**. We do not need a technical trigger to bless an outcome that violates our mandate. The workforce did not agree to become disposable because management missed its target.

So let me be clear again:  
**No closures. No compulsory layoffs. No dismantling of job guarantees.**

If management comes back with a package that truly keeps all sites open and shares the burden fairly, we are ready to negotiate the details. If not, then the confrontation continues, including industrial action and use of our rights in the supervisory board.
