I will speak plainly.

## My position
The state shareholder accepts that this company needs a **real restructuring**. We are not defending the status quo for its own sake. But we will not support a restructuring that uses the German workforce and regional industrial base as the primary adjustment variable.

That means:

- **no plant closures in the state as a first or default instrument**
- **no compulsory redundancies as a normal restructuring tool**
- **no sidelining of the state’s veto and co-determination role**
- **no deal that leaves the region hollowed out while calling it competitiveness**

If management wants our support, it must bring a package that preserves the company as a regional anchor and restores competitiveness without breaking the social partnership.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family holding on one point:  
the cost base is too high and the company needs durable savings, not cosmetics.

I also agree that:
- management must contribute visibly,
- investment in future products and technology is necessary,
- and a purely symbolic compromise would be a mistake.

I agree with the works council and union on something equally important:
- employees should not be the only ones paying for the adjustment,
- and a one-sided restructuring would be unstable, politically reckless, and economically short-sighted.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You keep saying closures and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table because alternatives are “not proven equivalent.”

That is not enough.

You have not shown:
- the exact residual savings gap after management restraint, overhead cuts, procurement savings, productivity gains, working-time measures, redeployment, and temporary labour contributions;
- why that gap cannot be bridged through a socially managed package;
- or why the region must pay the price before every alternative has been exhausted.

You are presenting a preference for the harshest tools as if it were a proof of necessity. It is not.

I will not endorse a logic that starts from plant shutdowns and then asks workers and regions to justify why they should survive.

### To the works council and union
I understand your insistence on job security and your refusal of compulsory redundancies. That is legitimate.

But I also have to be honest: if you insist on an absolute “no closures ever, no conditionality, no structural tools whatsoever,” you make it harder to reach a durable settlement.

I do **not** say this to pressure you into accepting closures. I say it because a solution must be economically credible. If the alternative package cannot close the gap, then we need a more carefully designed compromise — not denial on either side.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that the deal must be real, durable, and not cosmetic.

But you should not assume that the most aggressive restructuring is automatically the best one. In this governance setting, a prolonged confrontation can destroy value fast:
- strikes,
- reputational damage,
- supplier disruption,
- loss of trust,
- weakened execution.

A socially workable package can preserve more value than a blunt demolition plan.

## What I can support
I can support a package that includes:

- temporary employee contributions
- temporary working-time flexibility
- internal redeployment
- qualification and retraining
- management and top-level restraint
- overhead and procurement cuts
- binding investment commitments for German sites
- a structured review of performance and transformation milestones
- and a time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies

That is a serious package. It would protect jobs and sites while still forcing real adjustment.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:

- unconditional site closures as a routine option
- compulsory redundancies as a default instrument
- unconditional erosion of job-security guarantees
- or a settlement that shifts the structural burden onto the state, workers, and regions while preserving unrestricted managerial freedom

## On the bridge options

### 1) “No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional”
This is only acceptable if “last resort” is genuinely stringent, objectively defined, and jointly reviewed.

I do **not** accept an open-ended escape clause that can be used whenever management prefers it. But I can support a **time-limited moratorium** with hard review criteria.

### 2) “Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions”
This is the most promising route.

I would expect:
- measurable labour-cost contribution,
- management restraint,
- owner-side financial commitment,
- no compulsory redundancies during the agreed period,
- and a commitment that all German sites remain open for that period.

That is the kind of package the state can defend politically and economically.

### 3) “Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation”
This can work only if conditions are transparent, realistic, and not a disguised closure trap.

A guarantee must not be a probation sentence. It must be a real commitment with clear safeguards. If it becomes an instrument for quietly preparing closures later, it will not have our support.

## What would move me
What would move the state shareholder is a **quantified, socially balanced package** that shows:

1. how much savings come from management, overhead, and procurement cuts  
2. how much comes from temporary labour measures  
3. how much comes from working-time flexibility and internal redeployment  
4. how much investment is committed to German production through 2030  
5. what residual gap remains after those measures  
6. why that residual gap still cannot be closed without destroying sites or jobs

If the remaining gap is real, then we need to discuss how to bridge it **without** gutting the regional anchor role.

## What I will not accept
Let me be explicit:

- no German plant closures in the state as the default restructuring answer
- no compulsory redundancies as the normal tool
- no weakening of co-determination
- no outcome that strips the company of its regional responsibility
- no settlement that treats the workforce as the only adjustment variable

If management continues to insist that closures and layoffs must remain ordinary tools, then they are not offering a balanced restructuring. They are demanding the right to break the social partnership. The state shareholder will resist that.

## My challenge back
Put the numbers on the table.

Not rhetoric. Not general claims of necessity. Numbers.

And then show us why a **time-limited, socially managed restructuring** with:
- shared sacrifices,
- strong investment commitments,
- internal adjustment first,
- and no compulsory redundancies

cannot close the gap.

Until then, I will continue to say: **the company must be restructured, but not at the expense of its regional anchor role, its workforce, or the state’s co-determination power.**
