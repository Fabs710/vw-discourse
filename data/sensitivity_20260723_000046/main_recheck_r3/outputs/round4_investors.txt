We are still at the same valuation test: **does this package credibly and durably restore margins, reduce structural cost, and de-risk the earnings path?** If yes, capital can support it. If not, we will not endorse a cosmetic peace.

## Where we agree
### To management
You are right that the company has a real competitiveness problem and that a weak compromise would be value-destructive. We also agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged strike, because conflict would hurt earnings, execution, and the share price.

### To the family holding and state
We agree that a disorderly confrontation would destroy value and weaken the company’s future investment capacity. We also agree that a socially executable deal matters if it preserves operational stability.

### To labour
We agree on one narrow point: a one-sided settlement imposed entirely on employees would likely be unstable. That would not be good for shareholders either.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your position is still too absolute.

You keep saying:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- all German sites open through 2030,
- no conditionality.

That is only acceptable if you can show, with numbers, that alternatives deliver **equivalent permanent savings and capacity reduction**. So far, I have not seen that case.

Temporary wage moderation, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and productivity measures may help, but they are not obviously sufficient to fix a structural overcapacity problem. If they do not close the gap, then a blanket ban on closures and redundancies simply shifts the pain into future margins and valuation. That is not acceptable.

### To management
We also disagree with any approach that relies on vague necessity claims or uses “freedom to act” as a substitute for a quantified plan.

You still owe the market a clear bridge from problem to solution:
- exact savings gap,
- savings already secured through non-closure measures,
- residual gap,
- and why that residual gap cannot be closed through alternatives.

Without that, you have not earned support for harder tools.

### To the state government
Preserving the industrial base is not, by itself, a value solution. If a site is structurally uncompetitive, shielding it indefinitely can lock in margin impairment. Political discomfort cannot become permanent economic underperformance.

### To the family holding
We agree a cosmetic deal is worthless. But a prolonged confrontation can also destroy more value than a disciplined restructuring. The objective is not symbolic toughness; it is durable competitiveness.

## Our position on the bridge options
### 1) No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional
This is the most defensible starting point **if** “last resort” is real and not symbolic.

We could support a time-limited moratorium on forced redundancies, but only if:
- the package includes measurable savings,
- capacity is actually reduced,
- and there is a joint, objective trigger process if targets are missed.

### 2) Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions
This is the most promising route, but only if the numbers are material.

We would want to see:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management and overhead restraint,
- owner-side financial commitment,
- binding investment for Germany,
- and a credible margin path.

If that combination restores competitiveness, then it is a viable alternative to hard closures. If it does not, then it is just delay.

### 3) Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation
This is acceptable in principle, but only if the conditions are objective and enforceable.

We would not support blank-cheque guarantees. We would support conditional guarantees if they create:
- accountability,
- measurable productivity improvements,
- and a genuine route back to target returns.

## What would move us
We need a package that shows:
1. **material and durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity adjustment**
3. **measurable employee contribution**
4. **management and top-level restraint**
5. **binding investment commitments**
6. **a credible timeline to restore margins by 2030**

That is the threshold.

## What would not move us
- A cosmetic compromise
- A “no closures” slogan without quantified savings
- A permanent cost structure that remains uncompetitive
- A prolonged strike that erodes earnings and valuation
- A settlement that postpones the adjustment rather than solving it

## Bottom line
We are prepared to back a negotiated restructuring **if** it is economically serious and durable.

But we will not support an outcome that:
- leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- preserves every current structure regardless of economics,
- or replaces hard restructuring with political wishful thinking.

If management and labour want our support, bring a quantified package that actually closes the gap. Until then, closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out in advance.
