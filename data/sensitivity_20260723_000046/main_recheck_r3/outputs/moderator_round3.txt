### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix.
- There is broad agreement that the business has a **real competitiveness/cost-base problem** and that **investment in future products, technology, and German production capacity** is necessary.
- There is also agreement that **management and top leadership must contribute**; employees should not bear the full burden alone.
- Most parties accept, at least in principle, that a **negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Closures and compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council and Union reject both outright.  
  - Management, Investors, and the Family Hold­ing want them to remain available as structural tools.  
  - The State Government is open to a socially managed compromise but does not want closures treated as the default.
- **Site security:**  
  - Labour wants binding protection for German sites, ideally across all plants.  
  - Management and capital-side actors accept only conditional or performance-linked guarantees.
- **Conditionality vs. certainty:**  
  - Labour sees conditional guarantees as insecure or disguised threats.  
  - Management/investors see unconditional guarantees as economically non-credible.
- **Whether alternatives are sufficient:**  
  - Labour says alternatives to closures and layoffs have not been disproven.  
  - Management/investors/family say alternatives have not yet been shown, with numbers, to be equivalent in scale and permanence.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction can realistically come from non-dismissal measures alone** versus how much still requires structural capacity reduction.
- The balance between **job security / site protection** and **long-term competitiveness / margin restoration**.
- Whether **temporary sacrifices** by workers, management, and owners can close the gap without permanent workforce reductions.
- How to define a **credible “last resort” trigger** without turning it into either an empty threat or an impossible veto.
- What level of **binding investment commitment** to Germany would accompany any employee contribution package.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principles to **quantified scenarios**: each side should specify savings, timing, and residual gap.
- Ask for a **side-by-side package comparison**:
  1. non-dismissal measures only,
  2. conditional site-security package,
  3. conditional restructuring with last-resort structural tools.
- Require clarity on:
  - expected savings from management/overhead cuts,
  - employee contributions,
  - investment commitments,
  - and the exact conditions under which stronger measures would be triggered.
- Keep the discussion focused on a **durable, measurable, and socially managed solution** rather than repeating absolute positions.
