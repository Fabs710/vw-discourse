I appreciate the clarity from both labour and management. From the state shareholder’s perspective, we are not entering this discussion to defend the status quo at any price, but neither will we endorse a restructuring that destroys the regional industrial base.

Let me be equally direct:

- **I agree** that the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness.
- **I agree** that the current cost base and overcapacity cannot simply be ignored.
- **I agree** that the transition must be negotiated and durable, not a short-lived patch.

But I **do not agree** that plant closures and mass job destruction in Germany should be treated as the default or unavoidable answer. That would be economically, socially, and politically reckless. This company is not an abstract balance sheet; it is a major anchor of regional employment, supplier networks, and industrial capability. Once those structures are broken, they are not easily rebuilt.

To management: if you want support for restructuring, you need to show that you have exhausted serious alternatives before reaching for closures. I am open to:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and productivity measures,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and targeted investment commitments tied to specific sites.

What I am **not** open to is a one-sided model where the burden falls mainly on workers and the state is asked to stand by while jobs and industrial capacity are hollowed out.

To the works council and union: your insistence on fair burden-sharing and site protection is legitimate. The state shares that objective. But we also need a solution that the company can actually carry economically. A blanket refusal to discuss any structural adjustment would not help the workforce in the long run if it leads to a worse crisis later. So I would urge you to stay open to real efficiency measures, provided they are paired with binding guarantees and not just vague promises.

My bottom line is this:

- **No solution that guts regional employment**
- **No solution that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that preserves sites and jobs as far as possible while restoring competitiveness**
- **Yes to social partnership, but not to closure-first restructuring**

If management can put forward a package that achieves durable savings without dismantling the German industrial footprint, the state can engage constructively. If not, you should expect us to use every right we have to block an unacceptable outcome.
