From the capital-markets side, our position is still conditional and financially focused: **we support a restructuring only if it is credible, durable, and large enough to restore competitive margins.** We are not loyal to any one instrument. But we are also not prepared to back a settlement that preserves structural uncompetitiveness.

### Where I agree
- **With management:** the current cost base is too high, overcapacity is real, and margins are not acceptable.
- **With the family shareholder:** a cosmetic deal is worthless; value destruction through delay is real.
- **With the state:** prolonged industrial hollowing-out would be a serious long-term risk.
- **With labour:** a one-sided solution imposed only on employees would be unstable and costly.

### Where I disagree
#### To the Works Council and Union
Your repeated “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is not acceptable **unless** you can show an alternative that produces **equivalent permanent savings and capacity reduction**. So far, I have not heard that.

Working-time flexibility, temporary wage moderation, redeployment, and productivity measures may help, but I do not see evidence yet that they can close the gap on their own. If they cannot, then ruling out closures and layoffs in advance is just transferring the burden into future earnings.

That is not a defensible outcome for shareholders.

#### To Management
I also disagree with any approach that relies on open-ended conflict or vague assertions that “freedom to act” will solve the issue. The market needs a **quantified plan**, not a threat.

If you want capital-market support, bring:
- the expected **savings amount**
- the **timing**
- the **capacity reduction**
- the impact on **utilization**
- the expected **margin uplift**
- and the fallback if talks fail

Without that, you have not earned support for hard measures.

#### To the State Government
I understand your reluctance to accept closures as a default. But “preserve the industrial base” is not, by itself, a financial solution. If a site is structurally uncompetitive, then preserving it at all costs can be value destructive. We would not back a policy that turns political protection into permanent margin impairment.

### What would move us
We could support a negotiated package if it contains all of the following:
1. **material, durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity adjustment**
3. **measurable employee contribution**
4. **management and overhead restraint**
5. **binding investment commitments tied to performance**
6. **a clear timeline back to target returns**

### What would not move us
- A cosmetic compromise
- A deal that keeps all sites open without showing how the economics improve
- A settlement that depends on “hope” rather than numbers
- A prolonged strike or standoff that erodes earnings and valuation

### Bottom line
We are open to alternative measures, but only if they are **economically equivalent** to the required restructuring. If not, then the company must keep closures and layoffs on the table as real tools, not pretend they do not exist.

If management and labour want investor support, bring a package that proves the margin problem is actually being solved. Otherwise, we would view the outcome as inadequate and would reassess our capital allocation accordingly.
