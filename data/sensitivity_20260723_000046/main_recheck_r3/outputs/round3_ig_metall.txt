I’ll respond directly.

## To the Works Council
I agree with your core line: **no compulsory redundancies**, no one-sided burden on the workforce, and no pretending that workers should pay for management’s mistakes alone. That remains the union position.

Where I want to be precise is this: we should not let management frame this as a choice between “closures or collapse.” If they want our cooperation, they must prove — with numbers — that alternatives can deliver real savings. Until then, closures and forced layoffs remain unacceptable from our side.

## To management
You are still not answering the fairness issue.

You keep saying the business needs “material, durable” savings. We do not dispute that. But you have **not** shown that those savings must come from plant closures or compulsory redundancies. You are treating the harshest instruments as if they were normal, and that is exactly what we reject.

If the workforce is asked to contribute through wage moderation, working-time flexibility, productivity measures, and redeployment, then management and owners must also contribute visibly. That means:
- management restraint,
- overhead reductions,
- top-level compensation discipline,
- investment commitment to German sites,
- and a serious redesign of capacity without destroying trust.

What we will not accept is a package where employees absorb the pain, while management and owners keep strategic freedom and upside without matching sacrifice.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal is worthless. But your definition of “real” restructuring still seems too willing to sacrifice jobs and sites as the first answer.

A prolonged conflict, strikes, reputational damage, and loss of trust also destroy value. If you want durable savings, then support a package that combines:
- temporary employee contributions,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- procurement and administrative cost cuts,
- management and owner restraint,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is a real restructuring. It is not a free lunch for labour, but it is also not a demolition plan.

## To the state government
Your concern about hollowing out the industrial base is correct. But that means more than saying “preserve as much as possible.” It means using your influence to insist that management exhausts the alternatives first and that any restructuring respects co-determination, regional employment, and social stability.

## On the compromise bridge options
I’ll be clear.

### 1) “No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional”
This is not enough for us if it still normalizes closures in principle. We do not accept plant shutdowns as a pre-approved backstop. Once that door is opened, it never really closes.

### 2) “Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions”
This is the only direction that can be serious — but only if it means **binding security for German sites**, not selective security for the “best” sites while weaker ones are left exposed.

### 3) “Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation”
We reject this as a disguised threat if it means a guarantee can be withdrawn at management’s discretion. A guarantee that can be taken back later is not a guarantee.

## What I can actually support
I can support a package with:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures**
- **temporary employee contributions**
- **capacity reduction through working-time flexibility and voluntary instruments**
- **internal redeployment and qualification**
- **investment guarantees for German plants**
- **preservation of the collective wage framework**
- **shared sacrifice from management and owners**

I cannot support:
- open-ended insecurity,
- a permanent wage reset imposed only on employees,
- or any package that uses “competitiveness” as a cover for shifting the crisis onto the workforce.

## My direct challenge back
If management insists that closures and forced layoffs are still necessary, then put the numbers on the table:
- What savings come from management and overhead cuts?
- What savings come from top-level compensation restraint?
- What savings come from temporary labour-cost relief?
- What savings come from working-time flexibility?
- What savings come from internal redeployment and qualification?
- What investment volume is committed to Germany through 2030?

If you cannot demonstrate that the alternatives fail, then you have not proven that closures or compulsory redundancies are necessary. You have only shown that you prefer them.

## Bottom line
Let me restate the union position so there is no ambiguity:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Preserve the collective wage framework**
- **Temporary, fairly shared contributions are possible**
- **Binding investment commitments are necessary**
- **Management and owners must contribute visibly**
- **Workers will not be the sole adjustment variable**

If management and the capital side continue to insist that closures and forced layoffs remain ordinary tools, then they should be honest: they are not negotiating a shared solution. They are demanding submission. That will not happen.
