As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and management cannot now solve its strategic problems by tearing up historic job-security commitments and threatening entire sites. A restructuring that destroys livelihoods and breaks trust is not a credible path to long-term competitiveness.

What we will argue for is:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants
- no forced layoffs
- protection of co-determination and the existing job-security framework
- a negotiated restructuring built on alternatives: capacity adjustments, working-time measures, internally agreed efficiency gains, qualification, and fair employee contributions
- real investment commitments for the German sites, so there is a future here beyond 2030

If management wants support for a transition, it must come with credible guarantees, not threats. We are prepared to discuss savings and modernization, but only on terms that protect the workforce and keep every German plant open.

If this is presented as a fait accompli, we will resist it with all means at our disposal, including escalation at the workplace and on the supervisory board.
