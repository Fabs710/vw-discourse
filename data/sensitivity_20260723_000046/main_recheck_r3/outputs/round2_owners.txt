The controlling family holding’s position is unchanged in substance: **we need a durable competitiveness reset, not a cosmetic settlement**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the state on one essential point: **the company’s current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**. If we fail to restore margins, we will erode the long-term value of the core asset, weaken the investment base, and ultimately harm both the workforce and the owners.

I also agree that:
- a negotiated solution is preferable to unmanaged conflict,
- investment commitments matter,
- and a one-sided burden on employees would be unstable and value-destructive.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** agree that the only serious path is to keep plant closures and compulsory redundancies permanently on the table as the default answer. That would be too blunt, and in this governance environment it risks triggering the exact outcome we must avoid: prolonged conflict, reputational damage, operational disruption, and further value erosion.

Equally, I do **not** accept the labour side’s attempt to pre-emptively rule out every structural adjustment. A blanket “no closures, no layoffs, no revisiting guarantees” does not solve the economic problem. If the company’s industrial footprint remains structurally uncompetitive, then the burden is merely postponed.

## To management
You are right to insist that the gap has to close materially. But you still have not shown that closure and forced redundancy are the only way to do that.

What I need from management is not rhetoric about necessity, but a **quantified, credible package** showing:
- how much savings come from labour-cost measures,
- how much from working-time and flexibility,
- how much from productivity and overhead reduction,
- how much capacity reduction is actually achieved,
- and what the margin path looks like by 2030.

If the answer is simply “we need freedom to act,” that is not enough. Freedom without a credible implementation plan is just unresolved conflict.

## To the Works Council and Union
I respect the refusal to accept a one-sided burden on the workforce. That is legitimate.

But I will be equally direct: **a package that rules out all site change, all layoffs, and all structural adjustment in advance is not supportable**. It would lock in inefficiency and shift the real problem onto future balance sheets.

If you want owner support, bring forward an alternative that is economically serious:
- temporary employee contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transfer,
- productivity gains,
- and selective, conditional site adaptation where economics demand it.

What is not acceptable is a “preserve everything” posture dressed up as social partnership.

## To the State Government
I share your concern about hollowing out the industrial base. The family holding has no interest in destroying the regional ecosystem. But the state should also recognize that blocking necessary restructuring simply because it is politically uncomfortable would be a mistake.

The relevant test is not whether every site survives unchanged. The test is whether the company can become competitive **without irreversible damage** to its long-term value and governance stability. That is the standard I will apply.

## To investors
You are right to insist that the package must be real, large enough, and durable. A cosmetic compromise would not be acceptable. But investors should also understand that a protracted confrontation is itself a value risk. The solution has to be credible not only financially, but institutionally.

## My current line
The family holding will support a **negotiated restructuring** only if it delivers:
1. **material, durable cost reduction**
2. **credible capacity adjustment**
3. **labor peace and operational stability**
4. **a viable investment path for German production**
5. **no permanent impairment of governance control or enterprise value**

What I cannot support is:
- a symbolic compromise,
- an open-ended standoff,
- or a settlement that preserves all current structures while leaving the business uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
I am **open to alternative measures**, including temporary employee contributions, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, and targeted investment commitments.

But I am **not prepared** to accept a deal that simply says “no closures, no redundancies, no structural change” and calls that a solution.

Bring me a package that actually restores margins and competitiveness. If it does not do that, then it is not stewardship — it is delay.
