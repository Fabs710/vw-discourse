### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company has a **real competitiveness problem** and that **structural change is necessary**.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated solution**, rather than a purely unilateral management decision.
- Most parties say they are open to some mix of:
  - capacity reduction
  - working-time flexibility
  - internal redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - productivity improvements
  - temporary or partial labour-cost relief
  - targeted investment commitments
- There is shared recognition that **long-term viability in Germany matters**, even if the parties define the means differently.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council and union reject them outright; management, investors, and owner side say they cannot be excluded in principle.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour sides reject them; management and capital-side actors want this option left open.
- **Site guarantees:** Labour wants binding guarantees for all German plants; management/owners/investors will not commit to unconditional site protection.
- **Scope of concessions:** Labour is prepared to contribute, but only in a shared-sacrifice framework; management and capital want savings that are clearly large, durable, and measurable.
- **Role of governance/state:** The state and works council emphasize co-determination, social protection, and industrial base preservation; management and owners emphasize economic freedom to restructure.
- **Threshold for acceptability:** Labour sees closures/layoffs as a last resort or unacceptable; capital-side actors see them as legitimate if necessary to restore competitiveness.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost savings are needed** versus **how much employment/site protection can realistically be preserved**.
- Whether there is a package of **non-closure measures** that can produce savings comparable to closures and layoffs.
- The balance between **short-term labour concessions** and **long-term investment commitments**.
- How to structure **binding guarantees** without removing management’s ability to act if targets are missed.
- How to avoid a deal that is either:
  - too weak to fix competitiveness, or
  - too harsh to sustain labour peace and political support.
- What happens if negotiations fail: whether there is a credible fallback path or escalation plan.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **quantified options**:
- What exact savings, capacity changes, and timelines are being proposed?
- Which sites, if any, are under discussion and under what conditions?
- What concrete employee contributions are acceptable, and for how long?
- What investment commitments can be made, and what are they contingent on?
- What objective criteria would trigger or rule out closures, layoffs, or alternative measures?

The next round should aim to identify one or two **workable package scenarios** with clearly stated trade-offs.
