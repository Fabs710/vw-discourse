I’ll be equally direct in this round.

## To management
You still have not answered the central question: **why must the restructuring be paid for by breaking the social contract?**

We agree that the company needs durable savings and a credible path to competitiveness. We do **not** agree that this automatically requires German plant closures or compulsory redundancies. You keep presenting those as the only serious tools, but you have not proven that. A claim is not a proof.

### Where I agree
- The company needs real change, not cosmetics.
- The cost base must come down.
- Investment in future products and technology is necessary.
- Management and leadership must contribute too.

### Where I disagree
- I reject your attempt to keep closures and forced layoffs “on the table” as if they were normal bargaining options.
- I reject the idea that all German sites should be exposed to closure pressure while workers are asked to make concessions.
- I reject any “conditional” logic that starts from job destruction and then searches for justification later.

If you want our cooperation, then the burden of proof is on you: show, with numbers, how the required savings can be achieved **without** closures and **without** forced redundancies.

If your answer is only “we need flexibility,” then that is not a restructuring plan. It is a demand for unilateral power.

## To the family holding
You talk about stewardship, but stewardship does not mean sacrificing workers to protect strategic freedom. Your position still sounds like this: the workforce must carry the adjustment, and if that is not enough, then closures become acceptable. That is exactly what we will not accept.

You want labor peace? Then stop treating job destruction as a management tool that can be activated at will. Use your influence to support a solution that preserves the German footprint. That is what responsible ownership looks like.

## To institutional investors
You ask for “economically equivalent” alternatives. Fine — but you have not shown that closures and layoffs are the only economically serious route either. Your position is too abstract and too willing to treat jobs as a variable in a spreadsheet.

If you want equivalent savings, then consider the full package:
- temporary working-time changes
- temporary wage moderation
- internal redeployment
- qualification and transfer
- management and overhead restraint
- procurement savings
- delayed or reduced shareholder payouts where appropriate
- productivity improvements
- investment commitments tied to future volume

That is a real package. What is not acceptable is demanding “equivalence” while refusing to recognize the social and industrial damage of closures.

## To the state government
I appreciate that you do not want the regional industrial base hollowed out. But let’s be clear: “conditional site protection” is not enough if it still leaves closures and forced redundancies as the backstop.

The state should use its influence to insist on:
- all German plants remaining open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preservation of co-determination,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a restructuring path that uses the full alternative toolbox first.

If the state cannot stand for that, then its role is reduced to managing the political fallout after management breaks the promise of social partnership.

## On the facilitator’s bridge options
I’ll address them plainly.

### 1) “No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional”
No. We do not accept closures as a legitimate “last resort” that is pre-approved in principle. That still normalizes plant shutdowns. Once you accept that frame, the pressure on sites never disappears.

### 2) “Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions”
This is closer, but only if it means **binding site security for all German plants through 2030**, not selective security for the “best” sites while weaker ones are left to rot. We are ready to discuss temporary employee contributions and shared sacrifice. We are **not** ready to trade away sites.

### 3) “Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation”
We reject this as a disguised threat. A guarantee that can be withdrawn later is not a guarantee. It is a probationary status for the workforce. That is not what we mean by job security.

## On the quantified questions
You asked what additional non-dismissal measure we would accept before ruling out any structural adjustment.

Here is the answer: **we have already named them**. The acceptable measures are:
- temporary working-time reductions or flexibility
- temporary wage moderation
- temporary cuts in certain premiums or bonus elements
- internal redeployment
- qualification and retraining
- voluntary exit options only
- management and top-level restraint
- procurement and overhead savings
- productivity measures
- investment commitments for German production

What we will **not** accept as an “additional measure” is any form of forced redundancy, closure, or permanent erosion of the job-security framework.

If the question is whether we would accept deeper internal adjustments, the answer is yes, if they are fairly shared and time-limited. If the question is whether we would accept plant closures or compulsory layoffs after some threshold is hit, the answer is no.

## My direct challenge back
Before anyone talks again about closures or forced layoffs, put these numbers on the table:
- How much savings from management and overhead cuts?
- How much from top-level compensation restraint?
- How much from temporary labour-cost relief?
- How much from working-time measures?
- How much from internal redeployment and qualification?
- How much from procurement and footprint optimization **without** site closures?
- What binding investment volume is committed to Germany through 2030?

If the combined answer is still short, then management has not proven that the alternatives fail. It has only proven that it prefers the blunt instrument.

## Bottom line
Let me restate our position so there is no confusion:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Preserve co-determination and the job-security framework**
- **Temporary, fairly shared contributions are possible**
- **Binding investment commitments are necessary**
- **Management and owners must contribute visibly**
- **We will resist any deal that turns workers into the adjustment variable**

If management and the capital side continue to insist that closures and forced layoffs remain normal tools, then they should be honest: they are not seeking a negotiated restructuring. They are seeking submission. That will not happen.
