From the capital-markets side, we are still at the same test: **does the package credibly restore margins, reduce structural cost, and do so durably enough to support valuation?** That is the only lens that matters for us.

## Where we agree
### To management
We agree that:
- the cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and a cosmetic compromise would destroy value.

We also agree that the company needs a negotiated solution, not a drawn-out unilateral confrontation. Open conflict would damage earnings, execution, and the equity story.

### To the family holding and the state
We agree that a prolonged industrial breakdown would be value-destructive and would weaken the company’s future investment capacity. We also agree that stakeholder stability matters if it helps preserve long-term enterprise value.

### To labour
We agree on one point only: a one-sided solution imposed entirely on employees would likely be unstable and would probably backfire.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your position is still too absolute.

You keep saying:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of guarantees,
- all German sites open through 2030.

That is only acceptable if you can show, with numbers, that alternatives produce **equivalent permanent savings and capacity reduction**. So far, I have not heard that case.

Temporary wage moderation, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and productivity measures may help. But they are not obviously sufficient to fix a structural overcapacity problem. If they do not close the gap, then a blanket ban on closures and redundancies simply shifts the pain into future margins and valuation.

That is not acceptable for shareholders.

### To management
We also disagree with any approach that relies on vague claims of necessity or uses “freedom to act” as a substitute for a quantified plan.

You need to do more than say closures remain possible. You need to show:
- the required savings gap,
- what is already achieved by non-closure measures,
- what residual gap remains,
- and why that residual gap cannot be closed through alternatives.

Without that, you have not earned support for harder measures.

### To the state government
We understand your concern about the industrial base and regional employment. But preserving sites is not, by itself, a value solution. If a site is structurally uncompetitive, then shielding it indefinitely can just lock in margin impairment.

Political discomfort cannot be allowed to become permanent economic underperformance.

### To the family holding
We agree that a cosmetic settlement is worthless. But we would caution against assuming that the harshest possible restructuring is automatically the best one. A prolonged confrontation can destroy more value than a disciplined, socially workable package.

## Our position on the bridge options
### 1) No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional
This is the most defensible starting point **if** “last resort” is real and not symbolic. We could support a time-limited moratorium on forced redundancies, but only if the package includes:
- measurable savings,
- clear capacity reduction,
- and a joint trigger process if targets are missed.

### 2) Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions
This is the most promising route, but only if the numbers are material.

We would want to see:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management and overhead restraint,
- owner-side financial commitment,
- binding investment for Germany,
- and a credible margin path.

If that combination restores competitiveness, then it becomes a viable alternative to hard closures. If it does not, then it is just delay.

### 3) Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation
This is acceptable in principle, but only if the conditions are objective and enforceable.

We would not support blank cheque guarantees. We would support conditional guarantees if they create:
- accountability,
- measurable productivity improvements,
- and a genuine route back to target returns.

## What would move us
We need a package that shows:
1. **material and durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity adjustment**
3. **measurable employee contribution**
4. **management and top-level restraint**
5. **binding investment commitments**
6. **a credible timeline to restore margins by 2030**

That is the threshold.

## What would not move us
- A cosmetic compromise
- A “no closures” slogan without quantified savings
- A permanent cost structure that remains uncompetitive
- A prolonged strike that erodes earnings and valuation
- A settlement that postpones the adjustment rather than solving it

## Bottom line
We are prepared to back a negotiated restructuring **if** it is economically serious and durable.

But we will not support an outcome that:
- leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- preserves every current structure regardless of economics,
- or replaces hard restructuring with political wishful thinking.

If management and labour want our support, bring a quantified package that actually closes the gap. Until then, closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out in advance.
