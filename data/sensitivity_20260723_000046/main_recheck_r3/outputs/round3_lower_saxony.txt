Let me be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one basic point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix**. The cost base is too high, the competitiveness problem is real, and investment in future products must be secured.

I also agree that:
- a negotiated solution is better than an unmanaged clash,
- management and owners must contribute,
- and a one-sided burden on employees would be unstable and politically reckless.

So yes, there is a real need for change. I am not disputing that.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated framing that plant closures and forced redundancies must remain normal tools simply because management wants them available.

That is not a neutral technical position. For this state, for this company, and for this region, it would be a major break with social partnership and would put the industrial base at risk. I will not support a restructuring that makes German jobs the default adjustment variable.

### To management
You say alternatives are not proven equivalent. Then prove that with numbers.

What you have **not** shown is:
- the exact savings gap after management restraint, overhead cuts, productivity measures, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and temporary labour contributions,
- what capacity reduction is still needed after those measures,
- and why that residual gap cannot be bridged without plant closures or forced redundancies.

Right now, I see a preference for the blunt instrument, not a demonstrated necessity. That is not enough to justify breaking the social contract.

### To the works council and union
I understand — and largely support — your refusal of compulsory redundancies and your insistence on site protection. That is legitimate.

But I also have to say this plainly: a pure “no closures, no redundancies, no conditions” line is only sustainable if the alternative package is genuinely capable of closing the gap. If it is not, then we need a more carefully structured compromise than simply denying reality.

I am **not** asking you to accept closures. I am asking you to engage with quantified alternatives in a way that protects the regional footprint and gives the company room to survive.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that a cosmetic deal would be worthless. But I do **not** accept the idea that “realism” automatically means closure-first logic.

Realism also includes the political, industrial-relations, and regional costs of conflict:
- strikes,
- loss of trust,
- damaged supplier networks,
- long-term reputation harm,
- and the risk of hollowing out the company’s German anchor.

If you want a durable solution, then you must treat site preservation as a serious economic value, not as a sentimental add-on.

## My position on the bridge options
### 1) No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional
This is acceptable **only if** “last resort” is truly stringent and based on jointly reviewed, objective evidence. I will not endorse a loophole that turns “last resort” into a political pretext for closures. The burden of proof must be very high.

### 2) Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions
This is the most promising route.

I can support:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- overhead reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- and binding investment commitments.

But I would expect in return:
- strong protection of German sites,
- no compulsory redundancies during the agreed period,
- and a clear commitment that closures are not the first instrument.

### 3) Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation
This can work only if the conditions are fair, transparent, and not designed as an escape hatch for management.

I do **not** support open-ended insecurity. But I also do not support unconditional guarantees that ignore operational reality. The state can accept conditionality if it is tied to measurable investment, transformation, and capacity plans — not to arbitrary closure threats.

## What I can support
I can support a package with these elements:
- **no compulsory redundancies during the agreed restructuring period**
- **a moratorium on German plant closures for a defined period**
- **temporary, fairly shared labour-cost contributions**
- **management and top-level restraint**
- **working-time flexibility and internal redeployment**
- **qualification and transfer programs**
- **binding investment commitments for German sites**
- **preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is a serious compromise. It protects jobs and sites while still demanding real contributions from labour, management, and owners.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a settlement that starts from closures as the default answer,
- forced redundancies as an ordinary restructuring tool,
- unconditional erosion of job-security guarantees,
- or any outcome that sidelines the state’s co-determination role.

If management comes back saying that even a time-limited moratorium is impossible, then I will conclude that they are not seeking a negotiated restructuring but maximum unilateral freedom. In that case, they should expect resistance at board level and politically.

## My challenge to management
Put your case in quantified terms:
- How much savings do you need?
- How much do you get from temporary labour measures?
- How much from management and overhead cuts?
- How much from productivity and redeployment?
- What exact residual gap remains?
- Why does that residual gap require closures rather than a time-limited, socially managed adjustment?

Until you answer that, I will not accept closures or forced layoffs as “inevitable.”

## Bottom line
The state shareholder is prepared to back a **socially managed restructuring** that:
- preserves the German industrial base as far as possible,
- protects jobs,
- keeps sites open over a defined period,
- and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice.

But I will not sign off on a plan that guts regional employment or reduces our co-determination role. If that is where the discussion is headed, I will say no.
