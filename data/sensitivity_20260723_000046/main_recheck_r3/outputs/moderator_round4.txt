## Core impasse
There are **two different threshold tests** in the room:

1. **Labour / state threshold:**  
   No closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no weakening of site/job security unless every non-dismissal option has been exhausted and quantified.

2. **Management / investors / family threshold:**  
   A restructuring is only credible if it can still use structural tools when the residual gap remains material; they will not pre-commit to ruling out closures or redundancies.

The conflict is therefore not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **whether structural tools remain available before the residual gap has been fully closed and independently verified**.

The expert input sharpened this further: non-dismissal measures may close a large share of the gap, but **not necessarily all of it**. That means the dispute is now about **sequencing, proof, and governance of the fallback**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-dismissal first, with a jointly verified gap test
- Agree a **defined restructuring window** in which:
  - management restraint,
  - overhead cuts,
  - procurement savings,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary wage moderation,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - and voluntary exits
  are implemented first.
- At the end of that period, an **independent audit** verifies:
  - achieved savings,
  - remaining residual gap,
  - and whether the company is on a viable path.

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It gives labour/state the “non-dismissal first” logic they want, while giving management/investors a documented basis for any later structural discussion.

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional site-security pact with a hard no-closure moratorium during the test phase
- All German sites remain open, and there are **no compulsory redundancies during the test phase**.
- In exchange, labour accepts a **material shared-sacrifice package** and measurable productivity commitments.
- Owners and management commit to **quantified investment** and governance transparency.
- If targets are missed, the parties do **not** jump directly to closures; instead, they enter a second-stage joint review with pre-agreed triggers and options.

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It converts “conditionality” from a hidden threat into a structured process, while preserving immediate job/site security.

---

### Proposal 3: Two-tier agreement — guaranteed sites plus a separate fallback mechanism for the residual gap
- Identify a set of **core German plants** that receive a binding protection commitment.
- For the remainder of the footprint, agree a **fallback mechanism** that is not automatic closure, but a jointly governed restructuring review.
- The fallback can include:
  - product allocation changes,
  - capacity consolidation,
  - transfers,
  - further voluntary exits,
  - and only then, if still unavoidable, structural measures.

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It may allow the most protected stakeholders to secure core sites while preserving management’s claim that it still has a path to solve a genuinely residual problem.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact residual savings gap remains after non-dismissal measures, and what independently verified trigger would you accept before any structural tool can be considered?**

### To the works council
**If an independent audit shows that non-dismissal measures close only part of the gap, what concrete additional internal measures would you accept before you would engage on any fallback mechanism?**

### To the union
**Would you support a time-limited no-dismissal pact if it included binding productivity commitments, measurable owner contributions, and an independent verification of the residual gap?**

### To the family holding
**Are you prepared to support a binding no-closure period during the restructuring test phase if it is paired with measurable performance milestones and a credible second-stage review?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you consider backing a package that excludes closures and compulsory redundancies during the first phase, provided the package includes quantified savings, capacity adjustment, and an independently verified residual-gap review?**

### To the state government
**What specific site-security and investment commitments would you require before you would publicly endorse a time-limited restructuring pact with no dismissals?**

### To the expert
**What minimum combination of labour-cost relief, overhead reduction, and capacity/utilization improvement would you regard as sufficient to make a no-closure package economically credible?**
