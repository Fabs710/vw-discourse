The family holding shares the assessment that the company needs a **durable competitiveness reset**. I am not here to defend a status quo that keeps margins weak and leaves the core brand underpowered for the next investment cycle.

That said, I need to be equally clear on the boundary conditions from the owner side:

- **We will not support a cosmetic deal** that postpones the problem.
- **We will not support a restructuring that destroys long-term enterprise value** through unmanaged conflict, reputational damage, or an operational breakdown.
- **We will not accept that governance control is effectively neutralized** by an outcome that leaves the owners with the downside while removing the ability to steer the business.

So my reaction to the positions so far is:

### To management
You are right that the company needs **structural savings** and that capacity, labor cost, and productivity all have to be addressed. I support the principle that the business must become fit for 2030.

But I am not yet convinced that plant closures and compulsory redundancies should be treated as the only credible path. If you want the ownership side to back a hard restructuring, you need to show me a package that is:

1. **measurable**
2. **durable**
3. **operationally realistic**
4. **less destructive to labor peace and the company’s reputation**

If the plan depends on triggering a prolonged confrontation, it risks eroding value faster than it creates it.

### To the Works Council and Union
I understand the refusal of a one-sided burden on labor. That is a legitimate position. But a blanket “no closures, no redundancies, no revisiting guarantees” is not an answer to the economic problem. If the company’s cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, then everyone loses in the end.

So I do **not** accept the idea that all German sites can be protected regardless of economics. That would simply shift the burden into the future and weaken the company’s ability to invest.

What I would regard as constructive is a negotiated package with real substance, for example:
- capacity reduction where needed,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- temporary or permanent labor-cost adjustments,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and binding investment commitments tied to performance.

But those measures must produce **real savings**, not symbolic ones.

### To the State Government
I share the concern that this cannot become a politically driven hollowing-out of the industrial base. The regional and national implications matter. But blocking necessary restructuring also has a cost: it can freeze the company into weakness.

So I would not support a posture that simply prevents change. The question is whether the company can achieve competitiveness without irreversible damage. That is the test.

### My current position
The controlling shareholder side is **open to a negotiated restructuring**, but only if it delivers:
- durable cost improvement,
- credible capacity adjustment,
- labor peace,
- and a viable investment path for the German operations.

What I cannot support is:
- a purely symbolic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the structural problem untouched,
- or an approach that turns this into a prolonged, value-destructive standoff.

If the parties want owner support, bring forward a package that actually closes the competitiveness gap. If not, management should not assume that the family holding will endorse an expensive compromise that preserves inefficiency.
