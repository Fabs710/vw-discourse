I agree with the core message from the Works Council: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable**. If management’s answer to a cost problem is to destroy jobs and abandon sites, that is not a restructuring plan — it is a transfer of the burden onto the workforce alone.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side:

- We are prepared to negotiate a **fair contribution from employees**, including wage moderation, temporary reductions in labor costs, and possibly foregoing certain bonuses or special payments.
- We are also prepared to discuss **capacity reductions, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, qualification measures, and productivity improvements**.
- But any solution must be **shared-sacrifice**, not one-sided austerity.

What we will **not** accept:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No open-ended insecurity for the workforce**

If management wants a durable agreement, then it must put real guarantees on the table:
1. **Secure every German site**
2. **Rule out forced layoffs**
3. **Share the burden across management, owners, and workforce**
4. **Provide binding investment commitments for future products and technology**
5. **Respect collective bargaining as the basis for any settlement**

We are not here to block change for its own sake. We understand the market pressure. But the response must be proportionate and fair. If management insists that only workers should pay for the crisis, then industrial action remains on the table, and members are ready for it.

So my position is: **yes to restructuring, no to closures and forced layoffs, and yes only to a negotiated package where everyone contributes and the future of the sites is secured.**
