I will engage directly.

### To the Works Council
I agree with your core line: **no compulsory redundancies** and **no one-sided burden on the workforce**. That remains non-negotiable from the union side as well.

Where I want to sharpen the position is this: we should not let management turn this into a fake choice between “closures or collapse.” If there are alternatives that preserve jobs and sites, we should force them onto the table. But I will not support any package that weakens the collective wage framework just to buy a short pause. A temporary contribution is only acceptable if it is:
- limited in time,
- fairly shared,
- linked to binding site security,
- and tied to real investment and future volume commitments.

### To management
You are still not answering the key fairness issue.

You keep saying “durable savings,” but you are not yet showing that the savings must come from plant closures and forced layoffs. That is precisely the point of contention. If the workforce is asked to contribute through wage moderation, flexibility, and productivity measures, then management and owners must also contribute in a visible way. That means:
- management restraint,
- overhead reductions at the top,
- real capital commitment to the German sites,
- and a serious effort to redesign capacity without breaking the social contract.

What we will not accept is a structure where employees absorb the pain, while management retains full strategic freedom and the owners retain upside without sharing the burden.

### To the family shareholder and investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal is worthless. But your definition of “real” cannot simply mean job destruction.

If your investment thesis depends on forcing through closures and compulsory redundancies, then you are underestimating the industrial and industrial-relations costs. A prolonged conflict, strikes, reputational damage, and loss of trust will also destroy value. We are not bluffing about that.

If you want durable savings, then support a package that combines:
- temporary employee contributions,
- capacity reduction through working-time and voluntary instruments,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- procurement and administrative cost cuts,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is a real restructuring. It is not a free lunch for labour, but it is also not a demolition plan.

### To the state government
Your concern about hollowing out the industrial base is correct. I would add: the way to avoid that is not by accepting management’s framing that closures are inevitable. It is by insisting on alternatives first and by making any restructuring conditional on site security and future production commitments.

### What I can actually support
I want to be very concrete.

I can support a package with:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **temporary employee contributions** to labour costs
- **capacity reductions** through working-time flexibility and voluntary measures
- **redeployment and qualification**
- **investment guarantees for German plants**
- **preservation of the collective wage framework**
- **shared sacrifice from management and owners**

I cannot support:
- open-ended insecurity,
- a permanent wage reset imposed only on employees,
- or any package that uses “competitiveness” as a cover for shifting the entire crisis onto the workforce.

### My challenge back to management
If you want us to move, put numbers on the alternatives:
- How much savings from working-time flexibility?
- How much from voluntary programs?
- How much from overhead cuts and management contribution?
- How much from deferred bonuses or other owner/manager sacrifices?
- What investment volume is committed to Germany, and for how long?

Until you answer that, “closures are unavoidable” is not an argument. It is a threat.

### Bottom line
The union is prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice restructuring** that protects jobs and sites. But we will not trade away the workforce’s security for a vague promise of competitiveness.

**No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures. No one-sided restructuring.**

If that is not acceptable to management and the capital side, then they should be honest that they are choosing conflict.
