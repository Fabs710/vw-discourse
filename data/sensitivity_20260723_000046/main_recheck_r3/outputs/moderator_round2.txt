### Core impasse
The central blockage is **not agreement on the need for restructuring**. All sides accept that the company needs durable savings, investment, and a credible path back to competitiveness.

The impasse is over **the means of achieving it**:

- **Labour + state**: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, preserve job security and the German footprint.
- **Management + investors + family holding**: they will not preclude closures or compulsory redundancies in advance, because they see them as potentially necessary for permanent capacity reduction and margin repair.

So the dispute is really about **whether restructuring can be made economically equivalent without closures/forced layoffs**, and if so, **what binding commitments and sacrifices each side must accept**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could narrow the gap:

#### 1) “No forced redundancies first, closures only as last-resort and conditional”
A staged model:
- compulsory redundancies are suspended for a defined period;
- plant closures are not ruled out forever, but become possible only if a jointly agreed trigger framework is met;
- triggers would require objective proof that alternatives cannot deliver the required savings.

This could give labour a strong immediate protection while preserving management’s claim that structural tools remain available if needed.

#### 2) “Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions”
A package could combine:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management/top-level restraint,
- owner-side financial contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- procurement/overhead cuts,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

In return:
- all German sites remain open for a defined period;
- no compulsory redundancies during that period;
- any capacity reduction must occur through voluntary and internal measures first.

This would shift the negotiation from “closures versus no closures” to “what quantified savings can be delivered without breaking the footprint?”

#### 3) “Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation”
Instead of absolute guarantees or unconditional closure rights:
- sites receive protection if they meet agreed productivity, transformation, and investment milestones;
- underperforming sites enter a structured remediation process;
- only if remediation fails after jointly agreed review steps can more severe measures be considered.

This creates a middle ground between blanket security and unrestricted restructuring authority.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If management and owners can demonstrate, with numbers, that temporary contributions, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and investment commitments still do not close the savings gap, **what additional non-dismissal measure would you accept before ruling out any structural adjustment?**

#### To the Industrial Union
What specific combination of temporary labour measures, productivity improvements, and site-level flexibility would you regard as economically credible enough to substitute for closures or compulsory redundancies?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings target remains unmet after labor flexibility, management restraint, overhead cuts, and investment-linked productivity gains, and what evidence shows those alternatives are insufficient before you invoke closures or forced layoffs?

#### To the Regional State Government
What state-backed conditions or incentives would you be prepared to attach to preserve sites while still allowing a restructuring that management and investors would regard as financially credible?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies if it were paired with measurable cost reductions, conditional site review triggers, and binding investment commitments?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What level of quantified savings and capacity reduction would you require to accept a no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy restructuring as financially equivalent to the currently discussed hard-reset options?
