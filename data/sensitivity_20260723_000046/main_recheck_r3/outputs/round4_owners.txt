I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the state on the diagnosis: the company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**. Overcapacity, a high cost base, and weak margins cannot simply be wished away. A cosmetic settlement would destroy value.

I also agree with labour and the state on one important point: **a one-sided solution imposed on employees would be unstable**. A prolonged conflict would damage execution, reputation, and ultimately enterprise value. That is not in the family holding’s interest.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the labour side’s attempt to make German sites effectively untouchable in all circumstances.

A blanket:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no conditionality**
- **all sites open through 2030**

is not a serious answer if the economics do not work. It may be understandable politically, but it would hard-code inefficiency and postpone the problem. As a controlling owner, I cannot support permanent impairment of the asset.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept management’s implied shortcut that closures and forced redundancies should remain routine bargaining weapons from the outset. That is too blunt, and in this governance structure it risks destroying value faster than it creates it.

## My position
The family holding will support a restructuring only if it is:
1. **durable and measurable**
2. **competitiveness-restoring**
3. **socially executable without prolonged conflict**
4. **protective of long-term ownership value and governance control**

That means: I want a package that closes the savings gap **first through non-dismissal measures**, but I will not pre-commit to never using structural tools if those measures fail.

## What I need to see
Before anyone asks me to back a hard reset or a blanket protection scheme, I want the numbers laid out clearly:

- savings from management and top-level restraint
- savings from overhead and procurement cuts
- savings from working-time flexibility
- savings from temporary labour-cost relief
- savings from redeployment and qualification
- savings from voluntary exit and early retirement
- savings from productivity measures
- binding investment commitments for German sites through 2030
- the **residual gap** after all that

If that residual gap is material, then we need an honest discussion about how to close it. But I will not support a settlement that refuses to acknowledge the gap and simply converts it into deferred value destruction.

## To the Works Council
I understand your red lines. I also understand why workers do not trust promises of “conditional” protection.

But your position is too absolute if it assumes the current structure can be preserved regardless of economics. That is not stewardship either. It is a veto over adaptation.

So no, I do not accept unconditional guarantees for all plants through 2030. I also do not accept the idea that any discussion of structural adjustment is a power grab. Sometimes it is just the cost of keeping the enterprise viable.

## To the Union
Your insistence on shared sacrifice is right. Your insistence that management and owners contribute visibly is right.

Where I disagree is your refusal to engage with the possibility that even a fair internal package may still leave a residual gap. If alternatives genuinely close it, I am open. If they do not, then pretending otherwise is not protecting labour — it is protecting a temporary comfort zone.

## To Management
I agree with you that the business cannot stay as it is. But you still need to do more than say closures remain “on the table.”

Bring me a quantified case:
- What is the exact gap after non-dismissal measures?
- What is the timeline?
- Which measures are permanent, which are temporary?
- What is the performance trigger?
- Why can’t the gap be bridged without hitting sites or jobs?

If you cannot answer that, then you have not justified the strongest tools. And if you pursue them without a tight case, you risk a conflict that destroys more value than it saves.

## To the State Government
I respect your concern for the regional base and the social contract. That concern is legitimate.

But I cannot support a framework that treats the current footprint as untouchable. The company must remain viable first; otherwise there is no industrial base to protect later.

A time-limited moratorium, if paired with measurable savings and serious investment, is worth discussing. A permanent prohibition on structural adjustment is not.

## On the bridge options
### 1) No forced redundancies first, closures only as last resort and conditional
This is the minimum acceptable governance logic, **if** the trigger is objective and the review is real. I could support a temporary moratorium on compulsory redundancies while alternatives are tested.

But I will not accept a disguised veto that makes restructuring impossible forever.

### 2) Site-security package with quantified employee and owner contributions
This is the most promising route.

I could support:
- temporary employee contributions
- working-time flexibility
- redeployment and qualification
- management restraint
- overhead and procurement cuts
- targeted owner support
- binding investment commitments

If that package closes the gap, excellent. If not, then it has to be acknowledged honestly that further structural action may be necessary.

### 3) Conditional site guarantees tied to performance and transformation
Only if the conditions are objective, time-bound, and genuinely enforceable. Not a bluff. Not a hidden closure mandate. A real review mechanism with measurable milestones.

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- unconditional site guarantees across all German plants
- a permanent ban on any structural adjustment
- a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base
- or an outcome that destroys labor peace and brand value through prolonged conflict

## Bottom line
My position is simple:

- The company needs a real restructuring.
- Employees should not carry the whole burden.
- Management and owners must contribute visibly.
- German sites are valuable, but not immune from economic reality.
- Closures and compulsory redundancies are **not** my preferred route, but I will not rule out all structural tools in advance if the numbers truly leave no alternative.

If the other parties want my support, bring a **quantified, durable package** that closes the gap with non-dismissal measures first. If you cannot do that, then I will not endorse a weak compromise that merely postpones the reckoning.
