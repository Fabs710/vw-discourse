I will be very clear as well: **the state will not support a restructuring that destroys jobs, empties sites, or sidelines our co-determination rights**.

We understand the competitive pressure. We are not denying the need for a credible turnaround. But management’s framing still starts from the most damaging assumption: that the only serious path forward is plant closure and forced structural cuts. That is not acceptable to us, and it is not the only way to restore competitiveness.

Where I agree:
- The company does need a **durable solution**, not a temporary patch.
- German operations must become **more competitive**.
- The transition to future products and technologies requires **investment, not just protection of the status quo**.
- A settlement must be **negotiated**, not imposed.

Where I disagree sharply:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable starting point.**
- **Forced redundancies are not acceptable.**
- Any approach that treats jobs and sites as disposable ignores the company’s role as a regional economic anchor and a long-term industrial system.
- A restructuring that weakens the state’s and workforce’s veto rights would undermine the very governance model that has helped keep this company stable for decades.

To management: if you want material savings, then put concrete alternatives on the table that do **not** rely on closures. That means:
1. **Capacity reduction through reorganization, not shutdowns.**
2. **Working-time flexibility and temporary adjustments.**
3. **Voluntary exit schemes only, with strong safeguards.**
4. **Internal redeployment and qualification at scale.**
5. **Management and owner contributions that are visible and substantial.**
6. **A credible investment commitment for the German sites.**

That is the burden-sharing logic that can be defended politically and socially. What cannot be defended is asking the workforce and the region to absorb the full pain while ownership and management preserve all strategic options for themselves.

To the union and works council: I support your insistence on **binding site and job guarantees**, and I share the view that social peace cannot be bought by threatening closures. But I also need to be honest: if we want a settlement, it must show a path to competitiveness. That may require:
- temporary labor-cost moderation,
- more flexible working-time arrangements,
- and a serious contribution from the workforce, **but only in exchange for binding guarantees and investment commitments**.

So my position is this:
- **No plant closures as a negotiating premise.**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that preserves the industrial footprint and restores competitiveness through alternatives.**
- **Yes to a real social partnership.**
- **No to a model where the solution is simply to cut out the region and call it reform.**

If management cannot move beyond closure threats, then it is not yet presenting a restructuring plan that deserves support.
