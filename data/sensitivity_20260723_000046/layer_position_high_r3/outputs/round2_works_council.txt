Management keeps asking for “durable” cuts, but still refuses to answer the only question that matters: **durable for whom?** For us, durability means secure sites, secure jobs, and a credible future for the workforce. It does **not** mean closing plants and discarding thousands of families in the name of an abstract margin target.

I will be direct.

### Where I agree
- Yes, the company needs a real turnaround.
- Yes, the cost base must be addressed.
- Yes, investment in future products is necessary.
- Yes, pure confrontation destroys value.

### Where I disagree sharply
- **Plant closures are not an acceptable restructuring tool.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **A 2030 job guarantee is not a “freeze”; it is the minimum basis for trust after management has broken trust.**
- **Capacity reduction cannot be code for shutting down German sites.**

Management, owners, and investors keep presenting an false choice: either accept closures now, or accept permanent uncompetitiveness. That is simply not true. There are other instruments available, and we are prepared to discuss them seriously — but only on the basis of **all German plants remaining open** and **no forced layoffs**.

### What a serious package must include
If you want the workforce to even consider contribution, then the package must contain:

1. **Binding closure ban for German plants through 2030**
2. **Binding no-compulsory-redundancy commitment**
3. **Real co-determination in the restructuring design and implementation**
4. **Internal redeployment across the group at scale**
5. **Working-time measures instead of dismissals**
6. **Voluntary exit options only**
7. **Qualification and transformation programs tied to future products**
8. **Clear management and owner contributions**
9. **Investment commitments for the German footprint**

### On the “numbers” question
If management wants numbers, then put forward a package that quantifies savings from:
- reduced overtime and flexible working time,
- temporary labor-cost moderation,
- voluntary offers,
- internal transfer,
- process efficiency,
- supply-chain and procurement savings,
- management compensation restraint,
- and ownership-side support.

Do **not** present plant closure as the only lever and then call that a “package.”

### On employee contributions
We are not refusing contribution. We are refusing **blackmail**.

A negotiated employee contribution can be discussed if it is:
- limited in time,
- fair,
- shared by management and owners,
- and exchanged for binding security.

What we will not accept is permanent wage sacrifice in exchange for temporary promises while closures remain on the table. That is not burden-sharing. That is one-sided transfer.

### To the controlling family and investors
You talk about long-term value, but value is not only financial ratios in the next reporting cycle. Destroying industrial capacity, breaking trust, and provoking strikes also destroys value. If you want long-term stability, then stop insisting that “competitive” means smaller at any social cost.

And let me be very clear: if the governance majority tries to force through a plant closure, the employee side will use every co-determination and industrial means available to stop it. We are not bluffing.

### To the state
I welcome your support for site and job guarantees. But support must mean more than rhetorical opposition to closures. The state should use its influence to ensure:
- no plant closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- and a restructuring that preserves industrial capacity in the region.

### To management
If you really want a negotiable path, then stop anchoring on closures and forced exits. Put a **concrete alternative package** on the table:
- plant-by-plant capacity planning,
- internal allocation of volumes,
- working-time and shift adjustments,
- temporary contribution mechanisms,
- investment commitments,
- and a binding 2030 employment framework.

Until then, there is no basis for us to move.

### Bottom line
Our position remains unchanged:
- **All German plants stay open.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
- **No restructuring by social destruction.**

If management wants a real solution, it must respect those red lines and work within them.
