I will be equally clear.

We are **not** moving because management repeats “competitiveness” and the owners repeat “structural adjustment.” We are moving only if there is a **fair, quantified, reciprocal package** that protects jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework.

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **durable turnaround**, not a short-lived patch.
- The current cost base and capacity are not sustainable.
- Employees can and should contribute **if** the deal is fair.
- A temporary labor-cost contribution in the expert’s **5%–10%** range can be a legitimate bargaining basis **if** it is time-limited and matched by real counterconcessions.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time tools, and management restraint are all valid instruments.

## Where I disagree — strongly
### 1) I do **not** accept plant closures as the starting point
Management keeps saying closures are “on the table” as if that were a neutral technical fact. It is not. It is a choice with massive social consequences.

For us, **German plant closures are not an acceptable restructuring instrument**.  
Not as a first resort, not as a hidden reserve option, not as “equivalent capacity reduction” in disguise.

If the company wants labor peace, then it must stop treating closure as the default answer.

### 2) I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies
Forced layoffs are unacceptable. Full stop.  
If management cannot restructure without dismissals, then it has not exhausted the alternatives.

The acceptable path is:
- voluntary exits,
- internal transfers,
- working-time adjustments,
- qualification,
- and a negotiated labor-cost contribution.

### 3) I do **not** accept a wage sacrifice without binding security
The works council is right on this point: employees will not finance the turnaround while management preserves every strategic option and leaves closures hanging over everyone’s heads.

A temporary contribution is only defensible if it is exchanged for:
- **binding no-closure commitments**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **site and employment security**
- **real co-determination in implementation**
- **visible management and owner contributions**

Without that, it is not burden-sharing. It is one-sided transfer.

## On the expert’s 5%–10% range
This range can be a workable bargaining band — **but only if the package is genuinely reciprocal**.

For us, that means:
- temporary, not permanent,
- limited in duration,
- reversible if commitments are broken,
- and exchanged for enforceable security.

If management insists on keeping closures on the table, then the workforce will not accept a generous contribution. That is not stubbornness; it is rational bargaining.
