I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring**. A temporary patch would only postpone the next crisis. I also accept that **employees must contribute** if we want a settlement that lasts. But I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible path runs through **German plant closures or forced redundancies**.

That is where I draw the line.

## To management
You are right that the current cost base is too high and that the company needs more competitiveness. But you have still not shown that closures are the only way to get there.

What I need to see is a **quantified non-closure package** that proves the gap can be closed through:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation,
- procurement and overhead savings,
- management compensation restraint,
- and owner-side support.

If you cannot show that transparently, then you are asking us to accept plant closures as an assumption, not as a necessity. I will not support that.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your core red lines:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding security for jobs and sites**
- **real co-determination in the turnaround**

That is not “freezing” the company. That is the basis for trust after management has broken trust.

I also agree that a **temporary labor-cost contribution** can be discussed. The expert’s range of roughly **5% to 10% of direct labor cost** is a plausible bargaining basis — but only if it is:
- time-limited,
- reversible if commitments are broken,
- and exchanged for binding guarantees and visible burden-sharing from management and owners.

Without that reciprocity, it becomes one-sided wage extraction. I will not support that.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that deadlock destroys value. But so does provoking a labor conflict and undermining the company’s social legitimacy.

Long-term value is not protected by treating the workforce and the state as residual variables. If you want a durable turnaround, then accept that the company’s regional industrial footprint is part of its strength, not a dispensable cost item.

## To the state leadership
I support your position that the company must remain a regional economic anchor. But that requires more than rhetoric. It means:
- defending sites,
- defending employment,
- defending co-determination,
- and pushing for a restructuring that does **not** start from closures.

I will use the state’s blocking position accordingly. Any outcome that guts regional employment or sidelines our role will not get our support.

## What would move me
A settlement can work if it combines:
1. a **temporary employee contribution** in a fair range,
2. **management restraint** on compensation and overhead,
3. **owner-side financial support**,
4. **working-time and redeployment instruments**,
5. **binding site and employment guarantees**,
6. **investment commitments for the German footprint**,
7. and a **quantified non-closure restructuring plan**.

That is the level of seriousness I expect.

## What I will not accept
- closures presented as the default answer
- forced layoffs
- a permanent wage reset
- guarantees that are merely political theater
- a settlement that preserves investor comfort by sacrificing regional industrial capacity

So my position is unchanged: **I am open to a negotiated restructuring, but not one that destroys plants and jobs in the state.**

Bring the numbers for a genuine non-closure package. Until then, I will not move toward closure-based restructuring.
