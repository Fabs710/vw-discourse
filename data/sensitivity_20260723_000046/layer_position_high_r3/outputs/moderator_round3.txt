Here is a procedural facilitator summary of the plateau point:

## Core impasse
The negotiation is stalled on one central issue:

**Whether German plant closures and compulsory redundancies may remain available as restructuring tools, or whether the deal must instead be built on a binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff framework.**

That split then blocks agreement on everything else:
- employee contribution level,
- job and site guarantees,
- owner-side burden-sharing,
- and whether a “non-closure package” can be economically equivalent to closures.

A second, related impasse is:
**No side trusts the others to move first.**
- The workforce wants binding security before sacrifice.
- Management/owners want structural flexibility before ruling things out.
- The state wants regional anchoring but needs evidence.
- Investors want numbers, not symbolism.

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled for further discussion.

### 1) Phased non-closure standstill with review trigger
A compromise could be:
- **No German plant closures for a defined initial period**, e.g. 24–36 months,
- **No compulsory redundancies during that period**,
- employee contribution at the lower-to-middle end of the expert range,
- and a **jointly agreed review mechanism** at fixed milestones.

If the company misses agreed transformation milestones, the parties would not jump directly to closures, but instead move into a predefined escalation sequence:
1. intensified voluntary exits,
2. additional working-time tools,
3. deeper management/owner contributions,
4. mediated restructuring review.

This preserves security first, while keeping a governed fallback process.

### 2) “Equivalence test” package: non-closure route versus structural route
A second compromise is to stop arguing in abstractions and adopt a **formal equivalence test**:
- management tables a quantified closure-based scenario,
- employee-side representatives table a quantified non-closure scenario,
- an independent expert compares them against the same criteria:
  - savings,
  - execution certainty,
  - social impact,
  - investment needs,
  - and timeline.

The parties then negotiate from the verified gap, not from labels.
This could bridge the main disagreement by converting “closures vs. no closures” into a numbers-based comparison with a common yardstick.

### 3) Reciprocal security package with conditional flexibility
A third compromise would be:
- **binding job and site guarantees through 2030** for all German plants,
- **temporary employee contribution** in the expert range,
- **management compensation restraint** and overhead cuts,
- **owner-side financial support**,
- **clear investment commitments**,
- and a **limited conditionality clause** allowing footprint changes only if:
  - the company suffers a severe defined downturn,
  - all voluntary and internal measures are demonstrably exhausted,
  - and any move is subject to renewed co-determined review.

This offers management a contingency path without making closures the default assumption.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council / Betriebsrat
**What specific package of quantified non-closure measures would you accept as economically equivalent to closures, and what objective trigger would you accept for concluding that equivalence has failed?**

### Industrial Union
**What exact reciprocal concessions would make a temporary 5%–10% labor-cost contribution acceptable to your members, and what enforcement mechanism would you require if management breaches the deal?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What verified cost gap remains after voluntary exits, working-time tools, management restraint, and owner support are applied, and what number would make you conclude that closures are no longer necessary?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete state-side support are you prepared to offer beyond political backing, and what binding conditions would you attach to any support package?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What level of quantified non-closure savings would satisfy you that the footprint can be preserved, and what governance commitments are you prepared to give in exchange for labor concessions?**

### Institutional Investors
**What measurable margin-improvement path would make a non-closure settlement investable for you, and what implementation milestones would you require before you would regard the deal as credible?**

### Expert
**What minimum combination of labor contribution, management restraint, and owner support would you regard as sufficient for a non-closure package to be considered bargaining-plausible?**
