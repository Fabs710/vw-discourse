As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of this transformation. Management cannot now solve strategic mistakes by tearing up the historic job-security framework and threatening entire sites. That is not restructuring — that is shifting the crisis onto employees and communities.

Our priorities are:

1. **All German plants must remain open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A real co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping any turnaround plan.**
5. **A fair, negotiated solution that preserves industrial capacity and long-term viability, not a short-term cost-cutting exercise.**

We are prepared to discuss alternatives to closures and dismissals:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time instruments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- and, if necessary, a negotiated employee contribution that is limited, fair, and tied to binding guarantees.

But let me be equally clear: any proposal that includes plant closures, outsourcing, or forced layoffs is unacceptable to us. We will resist that with all the means at our disposal, including industrial action and public mobilisation.

If management wants a solution, it must come with credible commitments to the workforce, not demands for sacrifice without security.
