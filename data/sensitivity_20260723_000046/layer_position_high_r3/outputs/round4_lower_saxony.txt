I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, owners, investors, the works council, and the union on one central point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix**.

I also agree that:
- open conflict would destroy value,
- employees should contribute if the deal is fair,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and working-time tools must be part of the answer,
- and a **temporary labor-cost contribution in the 5%–10% range** can be a legitimate bargaining basis **if** it is reciprocal and time-limited.

So let me be clear: I am **not** rejecting contributions from management, owners, or employees. I am rejecting a package that is too small, too vague, or too destructive of the company’s regional anchor function to be acceptable.

## Where I disagree — sharply

### 1) I do not accept plant closures in Germany
Not as a starting condition.  
Not as a bargaining threat.  
Not as an “equivalent structural reduction.”

For the regional state, plant closures in the state are not a technical adjustment. They mean jobs lost, supplier networks weakened, municipal tax bases damaged, and industrial credibility destroyed. That is exactly what I cannot support.

If management wants to argue for footprint reduction, then it must prove — with hard numbers — that there is no viable alternative that preserves sites and employment to a meaningful degree. Until then, closure is an assertion, not a necessity.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies as a default restructuring tool
I understand the argument that management wants flexibility. But forced layoffs are not a neutral instrument in a company like this; they are a political and social rupture.

I can support voluntary exits, retirement corridors, internal redeployment, and qualification. I cannot support a deal that treats dismissals as an ordinary lever and asks the state to bless it.

### 3) I do not accept a 2030 guarantee being dismissed as “freezing”
A binding site and employment guarantee is not a sentimental add-on. It is what makes sacrifice credible.

The workforce and the state are being asked to trust management after a crisis. Trust will not be rebuilt if management keeps all strategic exit options open while asking employees to pay. If the company wants flexibility, it must first show restraint and earn it.

## To management
You keep saying “structural reset,” “competitiveness,” and “capacity reduction.” I do not dispute the challenge. I dispute your implicit assumption that the only serious answer is closure risk plus job insecurity.

You have not yet demonstrated that:
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment across the group,
- overhead cuts,
- management compensation restraint,
- procurement and administrative savings,
- and owner-side support

cannot close a large part of the gap.

So bring me the numbers. Not slogans. Not pressure. Numbers.

And if the non-closure route still leaves a gap, then show:
- how big that gap is,
- what additional management/owner contribution you are prepared to make,
- and why you believe the remaining gap genuinely cannot be bridged without harming the regional industrial base.

That is the proper sequence. Closure cannot be the opening move.

## To the works council and union
I support your core red lines:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding security,
- and a temporary, reciprocal contribution only if the package is fair.

I also support your insistence that “capacity reduction” must not become a euphemism for site destruction.

That said, I want to be careful: if we demand absolute guarantees without showing any quantified pathway to competitiveness, we risk leaving the company with no executable plan. That is why I keep asking for the numbers. Not to weaken your position, but to make it durable.

So my ask to labour is: help table a **quantified non-closure package** that is serious enough to protect the region and serious enough to persuade the other side.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that deadlock destroys value. But you are not entitled to solve that by making regional employment the residual variable.

A smaller company is not automatically a stronger company. If German plants are closed, jobs cut, and trust broken, then the group may get short-term savings at the cost of long-term industrial legitimacy and political backlash. That is not a trivial risk; it is a strategic one.

If you want support for a hard restructuring, then show that it is **really necessary**, not merely convenient. And show it in comparison to a serious non-closure scenario.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to support a package built around:

1. A **temporary employee contribution** in the expert’s range, limited in time and reciprocal  
2. **Management compensation restraint** and visible overhead reductions  
3. **Voluntary exits and internal redeployment first**  
4. **Working-time flexibility and shift redesign**  
5. **Qualification and transformation programs**  
6. **Owner-side support for the transition**  
7. **Investment commitments in future products and German plants**  
8. **Capacity reduction through reconfiguration where it genuinely delivers savings**  
9. **A binding no-closure framework for German sites for the agreed term**  
10. **No compulsory redundancies as a negotiated standard during that term**

That is a serious compromise. It protects the regional anchor while still asking everyone to contribute.

## What I will not accept
- Closure-first restructuring
- Forced layoffs as a routine tool
- A “temporary” wage contribution that becomes a permanent reset
- Guarantees that are mere public relations
- A settlement that preserves investor comfort by sacrificing regional industrial capacity

## On the proposed “equivalence” test
I support a comparison of packages — but only if the test is honest and balanced.

The question is not: “How do we justify closures?”  
The question is: “Can the required savings and capacity correction be achieved without closures or forced redundancies, while keeping the company viable?”

That is the test I want.

So yes:
- table the quantified non-closure package,
- table the management/owner contribution,
- and show the savings path transparently.

If the non-closure path is genuinely sufficient, I will support it.  
If management still claims closures are necessary, then it must prove that the alternatives fail — not just say so.

## Bottom line
My position remains unchanged:

- **Protect German sites and employment.**
- **Do not start from closures or forced redundancies.**
- **Use voluntary, flexible, and reciprocal measures first.**
- **Demand visible management and owner burden-sharing.**
- **Keep co-determination and the state’s veto role intact.**

If management wants movement, it should stop treating plant closure as the default and instead bring a credible, quantified non-closure package that protects the regional economic anchor.
