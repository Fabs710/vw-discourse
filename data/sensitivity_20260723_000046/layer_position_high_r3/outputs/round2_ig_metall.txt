I will be equally direct.

I agree with the works council on the core point: **this cannot become a restructuring in which employees carry the full burden while management and owners preserve all options and capital markets get a clean story.** That is not a fair deal, and it will not be accepted by the workforce.

I also agree that the company needs a **durable** solution, not a temporary patch. But durability for us means:
- secure jobs,
- secure sites,
- a preserved collective bargaining framework,
- and a real future for the workforce.

### Where I agree
- The company needs a structural answer, not another postponement.
- Competitiveness must improve.
- We need investment in future products and qualifications.
- Working-time instruments, internal redeployment, and voluntary exits are legitimate tools.
- A limited, negotiated employee contribution can be discussed if it is tied to real security.

### Where I disagree
- **I do not accept plant closures as a “necessary tool” being treated as the default answer.**
- **I do not accept compulsory redundancies.**
- **I do not accept using “durability” as a code word for permanent insecurity.**
- I do not accept that the workforce should give permanent wage concessions while management and owners only speak in generalities about contribution.

### On the works council’s round 2 position
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and binding guarantees,

is consistent with the mandate of the workforce, and I support it.

But I want to sharpen one point: if we are to discuss an employee contribution, it must be **strictly conditioned** and **balanced**. That means:
1. **Time-limited, not permanent**
2. **Reversible if commitments are broken**
3. **Matched by management and ownership contributions**
4. **Linked to binding volume, site, and investment commitments**
5. **Not a substitute for structural responsibility on the company side**

### What would be acceptable to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a package built around:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **Capacity reduction through non-socially destructive means**
- **Working-time reductions / shift flexibility**
- **Voluntary exit programs**
- **Internal transfers across the group**
- **Qualification and redeployment into future technologies**
- **Temporary labor-cost moderation** in exchange for binding guarantees
- **Management compensation restraint**
- **Owner-side financial contribution to the turnaround**

### What is not acceptable
- A closure-first strategy
- Forced layoffs dressed up as “targeted adjustment”
- Permanent wage cuts
- One-sided sacrifice by employees
- A “guarantee” that is merely political theater while closures remain under the table

### To management
You keep asking for “material and durable” savings. Fine. Then show us a package that quantifies savings **without** turning the workforce into the shock absorber of last resort.

If you want our contribution, then put real management-side measures on the table:
- executive pay restraint,
- reduced dividend pressure,
- investment commitments,
- productivity measures not based on job destruction,
- and a clear commitment to preserve the bargaining framework.

If management cannot present a credible non-closure alternative, then the conflict will intensify. We have already shown we can mobilise, and the peace obligation is over.

### To the controlling family and investors
You want long-term value. Then understand this: long-term value is not protected by provoking a labor war and eroding trust in the collective agreement. A settlement that is “financially neat” but socially explosive is not durable; it is merely delayed conflict.

If you want savings, we need **shared sacrifice**, not unilateral extraction.

### To the regional state
Your support for binding site and job guarantees is important. We need you to use your influence to ensure that any package is built around:
- regional industrial continuity,
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and real investment for the sites.

### My bottom line
We are open to a serious restructuring deal, but only one that:
- protects jobs and sites,
- preserves the collective wage framework,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- and distributes the burden fairly.

If the proposal is still “closures first, sacrifice first, security later,” then we reject it.  
If it is “shared sacrifice with binding guarantees,” then we are prepared to negotiate hard.
