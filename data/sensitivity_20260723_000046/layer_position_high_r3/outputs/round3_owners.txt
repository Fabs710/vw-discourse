I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the moderator, management, and the investors on one central point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a delay tactic**.  
I also agree with the works council, union, and state that **open conflict would destroy value** and that any solution must preserve stability and execution capacity.

The expert’s assessment is helpful: a **temporary employee contribution in the 5%–10% range of direct labor cost** can be a legitimate bargaining element **if** it is time-limited, reciprocal, and tied to real security.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** support a package that simply says:
- all German plants stay open,
- no compulsory redundancies under any circumstance,
- and any restructuring beyond that is off-limits.

That is not a turnaround plan. That is a veto on structural change. From an ownership perspective, we cannot accept a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive footprint by definition.

I also do **not** support a closure-first approach if it is used too early or too bluntly. If management wants support for harder measures, it must show that it has seriously tested alternatives first.

So my position is not “closures at any price,” but it is also not “no closures ever.” I need **quantified, credible evidence**.

## To the Works Council
You are right that trust has been damaged and that employees will not contribute to a settlement that feels one-sided.  
You are also right that voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, and qualification should be part of the answer.

Where I disagree is your insistence that:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and binding 2030 guarantees

can be treated as a starting point, before the economics are proven.

That is too rigid. It may protect comfort, but it does not necessarily protect the enterprise.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring a **quantified non-closure package** that delivers:
- comparable durable savings,
- comparable capacity reduction,
- and a credible implementation path.

Without that, your position is a request to preserve the status quo under a different label.

## To the Industrial Union
I agree with you that employees should not carry the full burden alone.  
I also agree that any employee contribution must be:
- temporary,
- reversible if commitments are broken,
- and matched by management and owner contributions.

Where I disagree is your attempt to treat closures as if they were merely a negotiating tactic. They are not. If the company cannot restore competitiveness otherwise, then structural reduction remains on the table.

A 5%–10% temporary labor-cost contribution is meaningful, but it is not enough by itself if the footprint problem remains untouched. I will not support a settlement that buys time while leaving the core economics unresolved.

## To the Regional State Government
I respect the state’s role and its concerns about regional employment. I also agree that a destructive conflict would be bad for the company and the region.

But I do **not** accept that plant closures are simply politically illegitimate and therefore off the table. If a site is structurally uncompetitive and alternatives fail, then governing responsibly also means facing that reality.

That said, I am not demanding closures as the first move. I am asking for a **serious comparison of two packages**:
1. a non-closure restructuring with quantified savings, and  
2. a structural adjustment package that may include closures or equivalent footprint reduction.

That comparison is the only way to determine what is truly necessary.

## To Management
Management is correct that the cost problem is structural.  
But management also has to be careful not to overplay its hand. If you push closures without first presenting a hard, quantified case for why the alternatives are insufficient, you will harden resistance and increase execution risk.

That is not acceptable either.

What I need from management is:
- clear savings targets,
- a breakdown of where the savings come from,
- and an honest explanation of why the non-closure package does or does not close the gap.

## What would move me
I would support a settlement if it includes:
- a **temporary employee contribution** in the plausible bargaining range,
- **management compensation restraint** and overhead reduction,
- **owner-side financial support**,
- **voluntary exits and redeployment first**,
- **working-time flexibility**,
- **investment commitments for future products**,
- and a **quantified restructuring plan** that genuinely restores competitiveness.

If the non-closure route can achieve the same durable economics, I would strongly prefer that.  
If it cannot, then I will not block the company from pursuing structural measures that are necessary to protect long-term value.

## Bottom line
My position remains:
- **No cosmetic settlement.**
- **No prolonged value-destroying conflict.**
- **No permanent impairment of enterprise competitiveness.**
- **No pre-commitment to preserve every site regardless of economics.**

Bring the numbers. Compare the packages. Then we can judge which path protects the company’s future.
