We share one priority above all else: **this restructuring must restore competitive margins in a credible, durable way**. If it does not, the equity story remains broken and capital will keep discounting the business.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current German cost base and capacity look structurally too high.
- **Employees and the state are right** that a pure closure-first fight can destroy value through strikes, delay, and execution risk.
- **The family owners are right** that temporary cosmetic fixes are not enough.
- **The expert’s 5%–10% temporary labor-cost contribution range** is plausible as a bargaining starting point, not a full solution.

## Where we disagree
### To Works Council and Union
We cannot support a package that simply says:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no meaningful footprint change,
- and guarantees through 2030,

if the result is still an uncompetitive cost base and structurally impaired margins.

That would be **value destructive**. It may protect jobs in the short run, but it would erode equity value and likely lead to another crisis later. We are not prepared to endorse a cosmetic settlement.

### To Management
At the same time, we do **not** support using closures as a blunt negotiating weapon if it only hardens resistance and lengthens the conflict. Open-ended confrontation is also value destructive.

If management wants investor support, it must show:
- quantified savings,
- timing,
- implementation certainty,
- and a realistic path to return-on-sales improvement.

“Structural change” is not enough. We need numbers.

### To the State and Family Owners
We understand the governance constraints, but governance cannot become a shield for an uncompetitive structure. If the company cannot earn its cost of capital, ownership stability alone does not protect value.

## What would move us
We would support a package that is **financially convincing and executable**, with:
1. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
2. **Durable labor-cost relief**
3. **Voluntary exits and redeployment as first tools**
4. **Working-time flexibility**
5. **Visible management restraint**
6. **Some owner-side contribution**
7. **A clear investment plan for future products**
8. **No open-ended strike risk**

A temporary employee contribution in the **5%–10%** range can be acceptable **if** it is:
- time-limited,
- reciprocal,
- and tied to binding commitments on jobs, sites, and investment.

But if that contribution is used to avoid any real capacity correction, then it is not enough.

## Our line
We are **not** demanding closures for their own sake.  
We are **not** defending the status quo.  
We **are** insisting on a package that actually closes the competitiveness gap.

So the next step should be a quantified comparison of:
- a **non-closure package** that really delivers savings, versus
- a **structural adjustment package** that may include closures but must still be executed without prolonged deadlock.

If neither package can credibly restore margins, then all sides will continue destroying value — and that is the outcome we cannot support.
