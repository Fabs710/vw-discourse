I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally clear from the ownership side: **we support a real competitiveness drive, but we will not back a solution that merely postpones the problem or destroys value through prolonged conflict.**

### Where I agree
- **The company needs a durable restructuring.** Temporary fixes alone are not enough.
- **Germany’s cost base has to come down.** That is not ideological; it is a business reality.
- **A negotiated settlement is preferable to open-ended confrontation.** Labour peace matters.
- **Investment in future products and technologies is essential.** If we do not secure the next generation, we lose the asset over time.
- **Redeployment, qualification, working-time instruments, and voluntary exits** should all be part of the toolbox.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the answer is to rule out structural change in advance.

To the works council and union:
- “No plant closures” as an absolute principle is not a credible governing position if the footprint is structurally oversized.
- “No compulsory redundancies” may be a legitimate preference, but if it becomes an absolute veto on any meaningful adjustment, then it risks becoming a veto on competitiveness itself.
- Binding guarantees through 2030 sound stabilising, but if they freeze an uncompetitive structure, they would be damaging to the long-term value of the company we all claim to want to protect.

To the regional state:
- I respect the political and regional role, but the company cannot be managed as if preservation of every site is automatically equivalent to preservation of value.
- Governance rights are important, and we will defend them, but they do not remove the need for hard restructuring where the economics require it.

### Our ownership view
The family holding is focused on **long-term enterprise value**, not short-term optics. That means:
1. The company must regain competitiveness.
2. The solution must be durable, not temporary.
3. Labour peace is valuable, but not at any price.
4. We will not support measures that permanently impair governance control or trap the company in a structurally weak position.

### What would move us
We are open to a package that combines:
- **meaningful capacity reduction,**
- **substantial labour-cost relief,**
- **voluntary and targeted workforce adjustments,**
- **site reconfiguration where necessary,**
- **clear investment commitments for the future,**
- and **credible protections against uncontrolled social disruption.**

What we would need to see is not just rejection of closures, but **a concrete alternative that achieves comparable savings and capacity correction**. If the workforce and the state want to avoid closures, they need to help produce a solution that is financially equivalent in effect, not merely politically more comfortable.

### Our bottom line
We will support a restructuring if it:
- restores margins,
- secures competitiveness,
- protects the long-term value of the core business,
- and avoids a prolonged conflict that burns value.

But we will **not** support a settlement that:
- leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged,
- preserves all current capacity by default,
- and calls that a turnaround.

So my question back to the employee side is this: **what materially measurable, durable concessions are you prepared to make that actually close the competitiveness gap?**  
And to management: **show us a restructuring that is economically convincing and socially governable.**

If those two conditions are not met, then there is no credible settlement yet.
