I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the state, the union, and the works council on the basics:
- this company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix;
- the current **cost base is too high**;
- **open conflict would destroy value**;
- a **temporary employee contribution** in the expert’s **5%–10%** range can be legitimate **if it is reciprocal and time-limited**;
- management, owners, and employees all need to contribute in some form.

I also agree that we need to compare **quantified packages**, not just slogans.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept “all plants stay open” as a starting rule
That is not a restructuring principle. It is a veto on structural adjustment.

From an ownership perspective, I cannot support a settlement that pre-commits the company to preserving the current footprint regardless of economics. If the company needs capacity reduction to restore margins, then that has to remain a real option.

### 2) I do not accept a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies
I prefer voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time tools first. That is the right order.

But I will not support a package that removes all execution flexibility in advance. If voluntary tools fail to deliver the required savings and capacity correction, the company must retain the ability to act.

### 3) I do not accept a 2030 guarantee if it freezes an uncompetitive structure
A guarantee has value only if it is compatible with a viable business model. Otherwise it just delays the next crisis.

Trust matters, but trust without competitiveness is not a turnaround.

---

## To the works council and union
You are right that employees should not carry the burden alone. I agree.

But you are wrong if you think “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no questions asked” can be the starting point while asking ownership to absorb the economic risk. That is not burden-sharing; that is a transfer of risk onto capital without giving the company the tools to fix itself.

If you want us to seriously consider preserving every German site, then you need to table a **quantified non-closure package** that is economically equivalent to footprint reduction.

That means hard numbers on:
- labor-cost contribution,
- productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- overhead reductions,
- and investment commitments.

Not assertions. Not moral claims. Numbers.

---

## To management
You are right that the company cannot drift. But you still owe us a transparent case.

I need to see:
- what savings are required,
- what comes from labor,
- what comes from overhead and management restraint,
- what comes from owner support,
- and what gap remains after all non-destructive measures are applied.

If that gap is still large, then you have to show why. If the gap can be closed without closures, then do it. But do not ask ownership to bless a permanent cost structure that cannot compete.

---

## To the regional state
I respect the state’s role and the political reality. But the state’s job is not to preserve every site at any price. It is to help secure a viable industrial base.

That requires honesty about economics. A site that is structurally uncompetitive cannot be protected indefinitely by politics alone.

I am open to a solution that preserves the regional footprint **if** it is economically credible. I am not open to a settlement that simply postpones the necessary adjustment.

---

## What I would support
I would support a package built around:
1. **temporary employee contribution** in the expert’s range,
2. **management compensation restraint** and overhead cuts,
3. **owner-side financial support**,
4. **voluntary exits first**,
5. **internal redeployment and qualification**,
6. **working-time flexibility**,
7. **investment commitments in future products and German plants**,
8. and a **quantified non-closure package** if it genuinely closes the gap.

That is the path to a settlement I could back.

## What I will not support
- a deal that preserves the footprint by default and calls that restructuring;
- a blanket prohibition on compulsory measures;
- a guarantee that freezes the structure through 2030 regardless of market reality;
- a package where employees contribute but the business remains structurally uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
I am open to a negotiated solution. But it must be:
- credible,
- quantified,
- reciprocal,
- and capable of restoring competitiveness.

If the non-closure route can deliver that, I will support it.
If it cannot, then we must not rule out structural measures that protect long-term enterprise value.
