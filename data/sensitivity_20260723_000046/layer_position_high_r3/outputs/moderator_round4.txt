### Core impasse
The central deadlock is **not** over whether the company needs restructuring. All sides accept that it does.  
The impasse is over **what must be traded for labor’s contribution** and **whether structural measures like plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain permissible**.

More specifically, the conflict has crystallized around four incompatible positions:

1. **Labor side red lines**
   - no plant closures in Germany,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - binding security through 2030,
   - labor contribution only if reciprocal and enforceable.

2. **Management / ownership / investor position**
   - cost and capacity reductions must be durable,
   - closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available if needed,
   - no pre-commitment to an unchangeable footprint,
   - labor contribution must materially close the gap.

3. **Shared demand for numbers**
   - all sides ask for a quantified non-closure package,
   - but labor says it is baseline, management says it must prove equivalence.

4. **Trust deficit**
   - labor does not trust “temporary” concessions without binding safeguards,
   - management and owners do not trust a no-closure framework to deliver sufficient flexibility.

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-bound non-closure moratorium with review trigger
A negotiated framework could include:

- a **binding no-closure moratorium for all German plants for a defined period**,
- a **no-compulsory-redundancy rule during that same period**,
- in exchange for a **temporary labor-cost contribution**,
- plus **management compensation restraint** and **owner-side support**.

To address management’s concern, the package would also include:
- a **joint review mechanism** at fixed milestones,
- a **transparent savings dashboard**,
- and a **predefined escalation path** only if agreed targets are missed.

This preserves labor’s security demand while giving management a performance-based review rather than a permanent freeze.

#### Proposal 2: Non-closure package with conditional burden-sharing and automatic rollback
A second bridge option is a package where:

- labor accepts a **temporary 5%–8% contribution**,
- management and owners commit to **quantified counter-contributions**,
- and the deal contains **automatic rollback clauses** if management breaches no-closure or no-layoff commitments.

That means concessions from labor are not permanent unless the reciprocal commitments are honored.  
This directly addresses the trust problem by making the package self-enforcing.

#### Proposal 3: Capacity reduction without site closure
A third compromise would separate **capacity reduction** from **site closure**:

- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- but significant adjustment through:
  - shift redesign,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - voluntary exits,
  - productivity measures,
  - overhead cuts,
  - and product-line reconfiguration.

This allows management to achieve a real footprint/cost correction without triggering the most destructive labor and political consequences.  
It would require the parties to define measurable capacity targets rather than arguing only over whether a site remains open.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If no closures and no forced redundancies are non-negotiable, what **specific quantified labor and operational concessions** are you prepared to accept, and over what duration, to make the package economically credible?

#### To the Industrial Union
What is your **minimum enforceable security package** that would justify a temporary 5%–10% labor-cost contribution, and what verification mechanism would make that acceptable to your members?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings remain after voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, overhead cuts, and owner support are fully counted, and which of those numbers can you commit to publishing for independent review?

#### To the Regional State Government
Are you prepared to support a binding temporary settlement if it preserves German sites for a defined period, even if it requires accepting a time-limited labor contribution and management flexibility conditions?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a **time-limited no-closure commitment** in exchange for a credible turnaround package if it is paired with clear performance milestones and a review clause?

#### To Institutional Investors
What specific combination of labor contribution, management restraint, and capacity correction would you regard as sufficient to restore a credible equity story without requiring plant closures?

#### To the Expert
What package structure would you judge most likely to be bargained successfully in this environment: a fixed moratorium, a conditional review deal, or a phased non-closure restructuring with trigger-based adjustments?
