### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree that the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement that **competitiveness must improve** and that the current situation is financially and strategically unsustainable.
- All sides accept that the solution should include some mix of:
  - **investment in future products/technology**
  - **redeployment and qualification**
  - **working-time instruments**
  - **voluntary exits**
  - **some form of labor-cost contribution**
- There is shared recognition that **open-ended conflict would destroy value** and that a negotiated outcome is preferable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works Council, union, and state reject closures; management, ownership, and investors treat closures or equivalent footprint reductions as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee and state side reject forced layoffs; management and owners say they cannot rule them out absolutely.
- **Scope of site/job guarantees:**  
  Labor and state want binding guarantees through 2030; management, owners, and investors see this as potentially freezing an uncompetitive structure.
- **Degree of cost reduction required:**  
  Management, owners, and investors want **material, durable savings** and capacity reduction; labor side wants any employee contribution to be limited, fair, and exchanged for guarantees.
- **Governance and co-determination:**  
  Labor and state want a strong role in shaping the turnaround; management and ownership want enough flexibility to make structural changes.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are needed, in quantified terms,** and which measures deliver them.
- **Whether comparable savings can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.**
- **What employee concessions are acceptable** if job and site guarantees are to be credible.
- **What management/owner contributions will be visible and substantial enough** to satisfy burden-sharing expectations.
- **How to balance short-term labor peace against long-term competitiveness.**
- **Whether capacity reduction can be achieved through reconfiguration and voluntary measures alone,** or whether some structural shrinkage is unavoidable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **numbers and package design**:
- Define the **required savings target**, the **timeline**, and the **implementation constraints**.
- Put forward **specific alternatives** to closures/forced layoffs, with estimated impact.
- Specify what **binding guarantees** are on offer, and under what conditions.
- Clarify what **concessions from labor, management, owners, and the state** are each prepared to make.

The next round should aim to compare **two or three concrete restructuring packages**, rather than restating red lines.
