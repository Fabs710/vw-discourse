I hear the red lines, but I need to be equally direct: **a package that rules out closures and forced reductions from the outset does not solve the underlying problem**.

We are not dealing with a normal cyclical dip. We are dealing with a **structural competitiveness gap**:
- German factory costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- the market is under intense pressure from lower-cost competitors,
- and this brand is still **not where it needs to be on return on sales**.

If we respond with only temporary instruments, cosmetic savings, or guarantees that freeze the current footprint, we will be back here in a year or two with the same crisis — only worse.

Where I agree with you:
- **A durable solution is needed**, not a one-off.
- **Qualification, redeployment, and investment into future products** are essential.
- A restructuring that simply shifts the burden to the workforce without any binding future perspective would be politically and industrially unsustainable.
- We do need a negotiated settlement.

Where I disagree:
- **“All German plants must remain open” is not a realistic starting point.**
- **“No compulsory redundancies” cannot be a blanket veto if the alternative is a permanently uncompetitive cost base.**
- A **binding site guarantee through 2030** may sound attractive, but if it locks in overcapacity and blocks real adjustment, then it is just postponing the hard decisions.
- A “fair contribution” from employees cannot mean symbolic measures; it has to be **material and durable**.

To be clear about management’s position:
1. **We need a substantial and structural reduction in cost.**
2. **We need a meaningful reduction in capacity.**
3. **We need labour-cost relief that is more than temporary optics.**
4. **We need the freedom to allocate capital to future products and technology.**

I am open on **how** this is achieved:
- capacity reductions without full closures,
- site reconfiguration,
- working-time changes,
- negotiated pay moderation,
- component-level adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exit programs,
- and targeted rather than blanket measures.

But I am **not** open to a deal that leaves all the expensive structures in place and calls that restructuring.

So my question back to you is simple: **what concrete, durable cost and capacity savings are you actually willing to put on the table** that get us to a competitive cost base by 2030? If the answer is only “no closures, no layoffs, and guarantees,” then we do not yet have a viable restructuring — we have a blockade.
