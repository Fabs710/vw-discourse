Here is a concise neutral summary of Round 2.

## 1) What has been agreed
- The company needs a **durable restructuring/turnaround**, not a temporary fix.
- The current **cost base and capacity situation are not sustainable**.
- **Employees are expected to contribute** in some form, but not alone.
- A **temporary labor-cost contribution** is viewed by several parties as potentially legitimate if it is **time-limited, reciprocal, and tied to security**.
- Likely acceptable tools mentioned by multiple sides include:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exit programs,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - management compensation restraint,
  - and some form of owner-side support.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**:  
  - Works Council, Union, and State reject them.  
  - Management, Controlling Family, and Institutional Investors see them as still on the table or necessary if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee side and State reject them.  
  - Management does not rule them out entirely, though it prefers alternatives first.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030**:  
  - Employee side and State want binding guarantees.  
  - Management, owners, and investors do not accept these as a starting constraint.
- **Scope of employee contribution**:  
  - Expert suggested a temporary range of roughly **5%–10% of direct labor cost** as plausible.  
  - Others disagree on whether that is enough, too much, or only acceptable with stronger reciprocal commitments.
- **Whether closures are a negotiating tactic or a necessary structural measure**:  
  - This remains a major fault line.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings can be achieved without closures** versus how much can only be achieved through footprint reduction.
- **What exact mix** of employee, management, owner, and state contributions would be considered balanced.
- **Whether “capacity reduction” can be delivered through reallocation, shift changes, and redeployment** rather than site closures.
- **How to make any employee concession reversible or conditional** if commitments are broken.
- **What quantified savings** each side is actually willing to commit to.
- **How to preserve industrial peace** while still delivering a credible margin improvement.
- **How to compare**:
  - a non-closure package,
  - and a structural adjustment package involving closures or equivalent reductions.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to **quantified options**. Each side should table a concrete package with:
- estimated savings,
- duration,
- required commitments,
- and implementation steps.

A useful structure would be:
1. **Non-closure package** with measurable savings and guarantees.
2. **Structural adjustment package** showing what changes would be required if non-closure savings are insufficient.
3. **Side-by-side comparison** of impact on competitiveness, jobs, sites, and execution risk.
