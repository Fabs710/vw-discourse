I’ll respond plainly, because we are now well past the stage for vague reassurances.

## Where I agree with the others
I agree that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix;
- the current **cost base and capacity situation are unsustainable**;
- employees can and should contribute **if the package is fair and reciprocal**;
- open conflict would damage output, trust, and value;
- a **temporary labor-cost contribution** in the expert’s **5%–10%** range is a plausible bargaining basis **if** it is time-limited and tied to real security.

So no, I am not rejecting change. I am rejecting a restructuring that makes the workforce pay first and pay alone.

## Where I disagree — and why

### 1) I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany
Not as the starting point.
Not as a bargaining threat.
Not as an “equivalent structural reduction.”

A plant closure is not a neutral efficiency measure. It is a one-way destruction of jobs, skills, regional industrial capacity, and trust. If management wants to argue for closures, then it must prove that every serious non-closure alternative fails — with numbers, not rhetoric.

### 2) I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies
Forced layoffs are off the table from our side.

What is acceptable:
- voluntary exit programs,
- early retirement,
- internal transfers,
- redeployment across the group,
- working-time adjustments,
- qualification and retraining.

What is not acceptable:
- dismissals as a restructuring instrument,
- or threatening dismissals to force wage concessions.

### 3) I do **not** accept a labor contribution without binding reciprocity
A temporary contribution is only legitimate if it buys something real:
- binding no-closure commitments,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- site and employment security,
- visible management restraint,
- and owner-side burden-sharing.

If management keeps closures and layoffs “on the table,” then a broad employee contribution becomes one-sided wage extraction. I will not sell that to the members.
