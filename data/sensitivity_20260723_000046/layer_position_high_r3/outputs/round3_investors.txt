We agree with one thing, and it is the only thing that matters from a capital-markets perspective: **this company needs a durable restructuring that restores competitive margins and protects value**. If the outcome does not move the business back toward an acceptable return-on-sales profile, then it is not a solution — it is deferred damage.

## Where we agree
- The status quo is not acceptable.
- A purely cosmetic deal would be value-destructive.
- Open-ended conflict, strikes, and prolonged uncertainty would also destroy value.
- Employees should contribute if that contribution is **limited, reciprocal, and credible**.
- Management, owners, and the state should all share some burden.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and qualification are preferable first tools.

## Where we disagree — sharply
### To the Works Council and Union
We cannot support a settlement whose practical effect is:
- all German plants stay open by default,
- no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances,
- and binding guarantees through 2030,
**if** that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

That would be a **cosmetic settlement**, and we would treat it as such. It might reduce tension for a while, but it would not restore margins. It would simply postpone another crisis and keep the equity story broken.

We understand the desire for security, but investor capital cannot be asked to fund an industrial structure that remains too expensive to compete.

### To Management
We also do not support a blunt closure-first posture if it merely hardens resistance and triggers prolonged labor conflict. That would destroy value in a different way.

What we need from management is not rhetoric about “structural reset” but **numbers**:
- what savings are required,
- by when,
- what comes from labor,
- what comes from overhead and management restraint,
- what comes from ownership support,
- and what footprint changes are actually necessary.

Without that, “durable restructuring” is just a slogan.

### To the State and Family Owners
We respect governance constraints, but they cannot be used to preserve an uncompetitive base indefinitely. If the business cannot earn its cost of capital, then long-term stability is not achieved by blocking every hard choice. It is achieved by making the company viable.

## Our position on the expert’s range
The expert’s suggested **5%–10% temporary labor-cost contribution** is a **plausible bargaining band**, not a final answer.

We could support that range only if it is:
- **time-limited**
- **clearly reciprocal**
- **tied to binding site/job security**
- **matched by management and owner-side contributions**
- **part of a package that materially improves competitiveness**

If it is used to avoid any real capacity or cost correction, then it is not enough.

## What we would support
A package can gain our support if it includes:
1. **Meaningful and durable cost reduction**
2. **A quantified path back to competitive margins**
3. **Voluntary exits and redeployment first**
4. **Working-time flexibility and productivity improvements**
5. **Visible management compensation restraint**
6. **Some owner-side financial support**
7. **A credible investment plan for future products**
8. **A clear implementation timeline with savings milestones**
9. **Industrial peace for a defined period, not endless confrontation**

## What we will not support
- A settlement that preserves the current footprint by default and calls that restructuring.
- A deal that asks employees to give up costs without a real turn in competitiveness.
- A package that is “socially neat” but financially hollow.
- A prolonged strike war that burns cash and destroys market confidence.
- Any outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired.

## The key issue
The dispute is not whether people are protected or costs are cut. It is whether the package is **economically equivalent** to the problem.

So our position is straightforward:
- If a **non-closure package** can genuinely close the competitiveness gap, we support it.
- If it cannot, then the company must not be trapped by a settlement that delays the necessary adjustment.
- But if management cannot quantify the gap and the savings path, it has not yet made the case for either route.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend any particular site. We are here to protect risk-adjusted shareholder value. That means:
- real cost reduction,
- credible savings,
- durable margin recovery,
- and no cosmetic compromise.

Bring a quantified package with a believable path to competitive margins. If it achieves that without closures, fine. If not, then it is not investable — whatever else it may be.
