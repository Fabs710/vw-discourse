Management and the owners keep trying to turn this into a false binary: either we accept closures and forced layoffs, or we accept “uncompetitiveness.” I reject that framing completely.

We have now heard enough to move from generalities to responsibility.

## Where I agree
- Yes, the company needs a durable turnaround.
- Yes, the cost base must improve.
- Yes, employees can contribute if the deal is fair.
- Yes, industrial conflict would damage value.
- Yes, a temporary labor-cost contribution may be discussable if it is limited and reciprocal.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable tool
Let me be absolutely clear: **I do not accept plant closures in Germany.**
Not as a starting point, not as a negotiating threat, not as an “equivalent structural reduction,” and not as a hidden option in the background.

If management says “capacity reduction,” but means closing German sites, then say so plainly. Do not hide the decision behind euphemisms.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
I also do **not** accept forced layoffs.
Voluntary exits, retirement paths, redeployment, and working-time instruments are one thing. Dismissals as a restructuring instrument are another. We will not endorse them.

### 3) A 2030 guarantee is not a freeze
The argument that binding job and site guarantees through 2030 “freeze the structure” is weak.
What actually freezes a company is repeated betrayal, permanent uncertainty, and a workforce that no longer trusts management’s word.

If management wants flexibility, it must first earn trust back with binding commitments. That is how durable restructuring works in Germany.

---

## On the expert’s 5%–10% range
I accept the expert’s assessment as a possible bargaining range for a **temporary** contribution. But let me add the condition management keeps trying to avoid:

That contribution is only defensible if it is exchanged for:
- **binding no-closure commitments**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **real co-determination in implementation**
- **management restraint**
- **owner-side burden-sharing**
- **clear investment commitments for German plants**

Without those, a labor-cost contribution becomes one-sided wage extraction.

And if management is still insisting that closures remain on the table, then the workforce has every reason to refuse any wage sacrifice beyond the absolute minimum.

---

## To management
You say the numbers require structural adjustment. Fine. Then prove it **without** hiding behind plant closures.

Put forward a **quantified non-closure package** with:
- capacity reduction through internal allocation,
- shift and working-time changes,
- process and procurement savings,
- overhead cuts,
- executive compensation restraint,
- voluntary measures,
- internal transfers across the group,
- and investment commitments.

If you believe that package is insufficient, then show the numbers transparently. Do not simply announce that closures are “necessary” and expect the workforce to nod.

What I am not hearing from management is a credible demonstration that all non-socially destructive options have been exhausted before resorting to closures. That matters.

---

## To the controlling family and investors
You keep speaking as if long-term value requires a smaller German footprint. That is not self-evident. Destroying industrial capacity, provoking warning strikes, and breaking the credibility of co-determination also destroy value.

If you want long-term value, then stop treating workers as the residual variable.

A company that is “leaner” because it has gutted its social contract is not automatically stronger. It may simply be more fragile.

---

## To the regional state
You are right to insist that this company remains anchored in the region. But anchoring it means preserving sites and employment, not merely softening the language around cuts.

If the state is serious, then it should stand behind:
- no plant closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and a binding industrial future for all German sites.

Anything less is not a guarantee; it is a hope.

---

## What a serious package must contain
If the discussion is to continue in a serious way, then the package must be built around these points:

1. **Binding closure ban for all German plants through 2030**
2. **Binding no-compulsory-redundancy commitment**
3. **Preservation of co-determination in restructuring design and execution**
4. **Voluntary exits only**
5. **Internal redeployment at scale**
6. **Working-time and shift flexibility**
7. **Temporary, limited employee contribution only if reciprocity is real**
8. **Management compensation restraint**
9. **Owner-side financial and governance contribution**
10. **Investment commitments for future products and German plants**

This is the minimum basis for serious negotiation.

---

## On “equivalent structural reduction”
I need to address this directly because it is being used too loosely.

“Equivalent structural reduction” is often just a coded way to say:  
“We don’t want to say closure yet, but we reserve the right to do it later.”

That is unacceptable.
If a measure destroys a German site, its jobs, and its industrial ecosystem, then it is a closure in substance, whatever label you attach to it.

So no, I will not accept semantic substitutions for closures.

---

## My position on burden-sharing
I am not refusing contribution. I am refusing **blackmail**.

A fair burden-sharing package means:
- employees contribute temporarily,
- management contributes visibly,
- owners contribute financially and politically,
- and the company commits to job and site security.

That is the only basis on which the workforce can even consider moderation.

If management wants the workforce to accept lower labor cost for a time, then it must stop threatening the very jobs and plants that make the company’s German footprint worth preserving.

---

## Bottom line
My line remains unchanged:

- **All German plants stay open.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
- **Real co-determination in the turnaround.**
- **Employee contribution only if it is temporary, fair, and exchanged for binding security.**

If management and owners are serious about a negotiated settlement, they need to stop demanding acceptance of closures as the price of dialogue.

Bring a quantified non-closure package.  
Bring visible management and owner contributions.  
Bring binding guarantees.

Until then, we have no basis to move.
