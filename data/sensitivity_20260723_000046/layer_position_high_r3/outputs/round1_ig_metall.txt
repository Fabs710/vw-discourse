I align with the Works Council on the core red lines: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: the workforce will not accept a restructuring in which employees are expected to pay for management errors and strategic delays while owners, executives, and capital markets are shielded. That is not a fair contribution; it is a transfer of risk downward.

Where I do agree:
- The company does need a durable solution.
- Purely defensive slogans are not enough.
- We are prepared to discuss **capacity reductions, working-time instruments, internal redeployment, qualification, and a negotiated employee contribution**.
- We also accept that the cost base must come down.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures are off the table.**
- **Forced layoffs are off the table.**
- “Structural cuts” cannot mean social destruction.
- Any savings plan that relies mainly on wage restraint from the workforce, without meaningful contributions from management and owners, will be rejected.

What we can discuss seriously:
1. **A negotiated labor-cost contribution** that is temporary, limited, and tied to binding job and site guarantees.
2. **Bonuses, special payments, or component flexibility** rather than permanent wage cuts.
3. **Working-time reduction, short-time-style measures, or capacity adjustments** instead of dismissals.
4. **Redeployment and qualification** so workers can move into future products and technologies.
5. **A credible investment commitment** for the German sites, not just promises about “competitiveness.”

What must be part of any acceptable package:
- **Binding site guarantees**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No closure decisions**
- **Fair burden-sharing**
- **Management and owners contributing visibly**

If management is serious, it should stop presenting closure threats as the starting point and instead put forward a plan based on shared sacrifice. If not, then the union will not hesitate to escalate industrial action. We have already shown we are prepared to do that, and that remains on the table.

So my position is straightforward: **we are open to a restructuring, but not to a restructuring by extinction of jobs and sites.**
