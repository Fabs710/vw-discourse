## Neutral moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement on several points:
- The status quo is not sustainable.
- The company needs a **durable**, not cosmetic, restructuring.
- Any solution must be **implementable** under the company’s governance and co-determination structure.
- A long conflict, strike risk, or political deadlock would damage value.
- An industrial plan through **2030** is needed, including **investment** and **product commitments** for Germany.
- Temporary workforce measures may be discussable by several parties, but only if they are limited and part of a broader package.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The central dispute is still over **which restructuring tools are legitimate and necessary**:
- **Management, family owners, and institutional investors** insist that real structural cost and capacity reductions are required, and that closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out in advance.
- **Works council, union, and regional state government** reject German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, and want job-security and co-determination protections preserved.
- The parties also disagree on whether alternative measures can deliver savings that are truly **equivalent in scale and durability** to the harder options.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several major trade-offs remain unresolved:
- **Cost reduction vs. job and site protection**
- **Structural savings vs. social and political acceptability**
- **Speed and decisiveness vs. implementation risk and industrial peace**
- **Short-term margin improvement vs. long-term trust, capacity, and investment**
- **Temporary workforce contributions vs. permanent erosion of standards**
- **Governance constraints vs. management’s demand for flexibility**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to specifics:
- Ask each side to present a **concrete restructuring package** with quantified savings, timeline, and implementation path.
- Require comparison of **alternative measures** against closures/layoffs in terms of scale, durability, and feasibility.
- Clarify what **binding guarantees** would be sufficient, and for whom.
- Focus discussion on whether a package can both **restore competitiveness by 2030** and remain **governable, socially balanced, and stable**.
