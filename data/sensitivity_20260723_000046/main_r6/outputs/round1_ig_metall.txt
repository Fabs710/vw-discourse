I agree with the works council on the core line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**.

Management cannot treat the workforce as the only adjustment variable while shareholders, owners, and the top side of the company remain untouched. If the company has a cost problem, then the answer must be a **shared-sacrifice restructuring**, not a one-sided job-cutting exercise.

Where I strongly support your position:
- **Every German site must remain open.**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **A binding framework for job security and co-determination** is essential.
- Any restructuring must be tied to **investment, new products, and a credible industrial future**.

Where I will be equally clear:
- I will not support a “competitiveness” package that simply shifts pain onto employees through wage cuts, longer working time, or the erosion of collective standards **without equivalent contributions from management and owners**.
- I will not accept an open-ended contribution from the workforce. If employees are asked to contribute, it must be **temporary, limited, and matched by hard guarantees** on jobs, sites, and future volume.
- I also want to be blunt: if management insists on closures or forced redundancies, we are not in a normal bargaining process anymore. Then we are in a confrontation, and the union will use its leverage.

So the union’s position is:

1. **No closures.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
4. If savings are needed, then only through **negotiated measures** such as:
   - shorter or more flexible working time,
   - voluntary exit and redeployment,
   - qualification and internal transfer,
   - temporary employee contributions,
   - and visible contributions from management, owners, and capital providers.

The burden must be shared fairly. If the company wants peace, it needs to bring a serious package that protects jobs and sites, not a demand that workers finance the restructuring alone.
