As the regional state government and a long-standing shareholder, I need to be equally clear: **I do not accept a restructuring that destroys German jobs, weakens the industrial base in the state, or sidelines our co-determination and veto rights.**

I hear management’s concern about cost pressure, overcapacity, and the need for competitiveness. That is not a fantasy problem; it is real. But the way to address it cannot be a crude logic of closures and job destruction as the first resort. This company is not an abstract balance sheet. It is a core industrial anchor for a region, with deep employment, supplier, and social consequences.

Where I agree with management:
- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a temporary patch.
- The transition to electric mobility and the global competitive environment require real industrial adjustment.
- A negotiated settlement has to contain **credible economic measures**, not just promises.

Where I strongly disagree:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not acceptable as a default instrument.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable basis for a settlement.**
- Any package that treats regional employment and site continuity as disposable would be politically and economically reckless.
- And I will be explicit: an approach that effectively sidelines the state’s role or tries to force through a decision against our blocking position is not viable.

To the works council and union: I support the core principle that **jobs, sites, and co-determination must be protected**. The state will stand behind a solution that preserves the company’s long-term capacity in Germany. But I also want to be frank that a settlement must be economically credible. A pure status-quo defense will not hold if it ignores the competitiveness issue.

So my position is:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Yes to a negotiated restructuring**, if it uses alternatives such as:
   - capacity reductions without closures,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - voluntary exits,
   - redeployment and qualification,
   - temporary employee contributions,
   - and binding investment commitments for future products.
4. Any contribution from the workforce must be **matched by visible commitments from management and ownership**.

I am prepared to work for a compromise that protects the regional economy and restores competitiveness. But I will not support a solution that makes regional deindustrialization the price of adjustment.

So, management: if you want our support, bring a plan that proves you can restore competitiveness **without sacrificing the company’s social and regional anchor role**. If you cannot do that, then you should expect resistance, not consent.
