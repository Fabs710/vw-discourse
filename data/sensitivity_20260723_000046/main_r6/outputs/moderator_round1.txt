### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties say the current situation requires a **durable solution**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated restructuring** rather than unilateral action.
- Most parties accept that any settlement should include some combination of:
  - investment and product commitments,
  - capacity adjustment,
  - working-time instruments,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment/qualification,
  - and some form of workforce contribution.
- There is also shared recognition that a deal must be **governable, implementable, and stable**, taking account of co-determination and the political shareholder structure.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works council, union, and state reject German plant closures as a basis for agreement; management, family holding, and investors say closures or equivalent structural measures may be necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state reject them; management and investors do not rule out compulsory reductions if needed.
- **Scope of adjustment:** Management and investors want a substantial, durable cost and capacity reset; employee side wants changes only if sites and jobs are protected.
- **Labor contribution:** Management and investors want a meaningful workforce contribution; union and works council accept only temporary, limited, and matched contributions.
- **Governance reality:** State and family emphasize that any solution must survive co-determination and political constraints; management stresses urgency and structural necessity.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve sufficient cost reduction without closures or forced layoffs**, or whether that is possible at all.
- **How to balance job and site protection against competitiveness and margin recovery.**
- **Whether temporary employee concessions are enough**, or whether permanent structural changes are required.
- **What concrete investment and future product commitments** will accompany any workforce sacrifices.
- **How implementation risk will be handled** if a deal depends on measures that some parties have already ruled out.
- **Who bears what share of the adjustment burden** among workers, management, owners, and capital providers.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package design**: quantify what savings are needed, what measures are on/off the table, and what guarantees can be offered in return.
- Ask each side to state **its minimum acceptable package** and **its red lines** in operational terms.
- Focus on **credible alternative measures** that could substitute for closures or compulsory redundancies, if possible.
- Require clarity on **investment, product allocation, and timeline through 2030** so any concessions are matched by verifiable commitments.
