As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden, and management cannot now solve a strategic problem by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and hollowing out the company’s industrial base. A restructuring that is built on closure and forced layoffs is not a credible future strategy; it is a short-term cut that creates long-term damage.

What we will argue for is a negotiated solution that secures:

- every German plant remaining open,
- binding job guarantees,
- no forced redundancies,
- protection of co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and a real industrial plan through 2030 that includes investment, product commitments, and fair burden-sharing.

If management wants contributions from the workforce, then the discussion must start with alternatives that preserve sites and employment: capacity adjustments, working-time instruments, voluntary arrangements, redeployment, qualification, and a fair, temporary contribution that is matched by binding guarantees from ownership and management.

But let me be equally clear: if this is presented as a fait accompli with closures and layoffs already decided, we will resist it forcefully. We have the mandate, the legitimacy, and the means to do so.
