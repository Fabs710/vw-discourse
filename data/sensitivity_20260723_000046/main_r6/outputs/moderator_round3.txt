## Core impasse

The central impasse is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **the restructuring method and the burden-sharing rule**.

More specifically, the conflict is between:

- **Management / investors / controlling family**, who want a package that is demonstrably large enough to restore competitiveness, and are unwilling to rule out closures or compulsory redundancies in advance.
- **Works council / union / state**, who want restructuring, but reject closures and forced redundancies as the price of adjustment and insist on preserving the German industrial base and co-determination protections.

So the unresolved question is:

**Can the company achieve equivalent, durable savings without closures or compulsory redundancies, and if yes, what binding trade-offs make that credible?**

The dispute is now over:
1. **scale** — whether non-closure tools are enough,
2. **durability** — whether concessions are temporary or structural,
3. **governance** — whether any package can actually be implemented under the current veto and co-determination constraints.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Hybrid restructuring package with “closure-last” sequencing
A possible bridge is a package that does **not** begin with closures, but also does **not** rule them out forever.

**Structure:**
- First use voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, site specialization, and capacity concentration.
- Only if quantified savings targets are missed after a defined review period, then a limited number of closures or deeper footprint changes become available.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Gives the works council, union, and state a genuine chance to avoid closures.
- Gives management, family, and investors a credible fallback if alternatives do not close the gap.
- Makes “no closure” a sequencing principle rather than an absolute veto.

---

### 2) No-closure package with binding industrial guarantees and measurable triggers
A second compromise is a **strictly no-closure package**, but only if it is paired with hard, written commitments and performance triggers.

**Structure:**
- No German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period.
- In exchange, employees accept temporary and limited contributions.
- Management and owners commit to binding investment, product allocation, and capacity concentration.
- If savings, margins, or volume milestones are missed, the parties reopen the package with pre-agreed escalation rules.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Satisfies the red-line concerns of labor and the state.
- Gives management and investors measurable enforcement points rather than symbolic promises.
- Converts the debate from ideology into monitored execution.

---

### 3) Differential treatment by site: protect core plants, restructure weaker sites harder
A third compromise is to avoid a single company-wide rule and instead use a **site-by-site restructuring map**.

**Structure:**
- Core strategic sites receive protection, investment, and product commitments.
- Weaker or underutilized sites face stronger capacity reductions, specialization, or conversion.
- Adjustment is handled through voluntary exits, transfers, retraining, and staged rationalization, with closures considered only where no viable industrial role remains.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Preserves the German base while acknowledging that not every site can be treated identically.
- Gives management a way to pursue meaningful fixed-cost reduction.
- Lets labor and government focus on protecting strategic employment rather than defending every current footprint unchanged.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the minimum quantified savings gap that only closures or compulsory redundancies can close, and can you publish the plant-by-plant evidence behind that claim?**

### To the works council
**Which concrete capacity-reduction measures, if fully funded and contractually guaranteed, would you accept as sufficient to avoid closures for a defined period?**

### To the industrial union
**What temporary employee contribution terms would you regard as acceptable if they are paired with binding investment, product allocation, and automatic reversal if targets are missed?**

### To the state government
**Will you support a time-bound restructuring framework if it includes measurable performance triggers and a formal review point, even if closures are not fully excluded forever?**

### To the controlling family
**What level of footprint reduction would you consider acceptable if it is achieved without immediate closures but still delivers durable savings and governance stability?**

### To institutional investors
**What specific financial metrics—EBIT margin, fixed-cost absorption, payback period, or cash conversion—would persuade you that a non-closure plan is credible enough to back?**

### To the independent expert
**What minimum combination of capacity concentration, specialization, and workforce reduction would you consider necessary for a no-closure plan to be economically equivalent to a closure-led plan?**
