## Core impasse
The parties are **not primarily disagreeing that restructuring is necessary**. They are disagreeing on **what counts as a legitimate restructuring path**.

The conflict sits on three linked fault lines:

1. **Scope of permissible instruments**
   - Labour and the state want a restructuring path that excludes German plant closures and compulsory redundancies.
   - Management, owners, and investors want the option set to remain open if non-closure measures do not deliver equivalent savings.

2. **Burden-sharing**
   - Employee-side actors require that management, owners, and capital share the burden materially.
   - Management and capital-side actors argue that employee-side measures alone will not close the structural gap.

3. **Proof standard**
   - One side says closures are not proven necessary and demand quantified alternatives first.
   - The other says alternatives are only acceptable if they are shown to be equally durable and large enough, with implementation credibility.

So the plateau is not about whether adjustment is needed, but about **whether there is a jointly acceptable non-closure path that is deep enough, enforceable enough, and economically equivalent enough**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Non-closure first with hard review trigger
A structured package would be agreed now that:
- excludes German closures and compulsory redundancies in the first phase,
- includes capacity concentration, site specialization, voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, and temporary employee contributions,
- and is paired with a **quantified review trigger** at a fixed date.

If agreed savings, utilization, and margin milestones are missed, the parties would reopen the remaining options under a predefined escalation process.

**Purpose:** bridge labour/state red lines with management’s demand for an enforceable fallback.

---

### Proposal 2: Tiered site strategy with protected core sites and intensive adjustment elsewhere
A site-by-site arrangement would distinguish between:
- **strategic core sites** with binding investment and product commitments,
- **consolidation sites** that receive reduced but continued roles,
- and **sites requiring strongest adjustment**, where voluntary exits, specialization, and capacity shifts are concentrated.

This would avoid an across-the-board closure logic while still allowing deeper adjustment where economics are weakest.

**Purpose:** bridge the divide between “no closures anywhere” and “every site must be preserved equally.”

---

### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package tied to verified counter-commitments
Any workforce contribution would be accepted only if matched by:
- binding investment commitments,
- defined product allocation,
- measurable management and owner contributions,
- and automatic reversion of employee concessions if obligations are missed.

This would frame employee contributions as conditional, temporary, and reciprocal rather than open-ended.

**Purpose:** address the union and works council concern that workforce sacrifice must not become one-sided or permanent.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact plant-level savings gap remains after you apply the deepest no-closure package you are willing to accept, and which specific measures account for that gap?**

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**Which concrete operational adjustments would you accept as sufficient to produce the required permanent savings without closures, and what measurable thresholds would define “sufficient”?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What temporary employee contribution, in quantified terms and with what end-date and reversion rule, would you consider compatible with no compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Regional State Government
**If management presents a quantified package that misses agreed targets, will you commit in advance to using your blocking position, or only to political opposition?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Are you prepared to endorse a non-closure package if it achieves verified equivalent savings, even if it requires binding concessions from ownership on investment, governance, or profit expectations?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum metrics on recurring savings, utilization, and 2030 margin improvement would make you support a non-closure settlement instead of insisting on closure-capable flexibility?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, headcount reduction, and complexity reduction would you consider necessary for a non-closure plan to be economically equivalent to a closure-led plan?**
