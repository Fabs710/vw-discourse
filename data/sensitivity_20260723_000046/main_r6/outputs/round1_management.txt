Let me respond very clearly.

I understand the line you are drawing: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, preserve all sites, preserve the existing guarantees. I also understand why you are taking that position. But I have to say just as clearly: **that position, as stated, does not solve the problem we are facing.**

We are in a structural crisis, not a temporary downturn.

- German factory costs are far above target.
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying, including from lower-cost rivals.
- And the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we respond with a package that merely preserves the current footprint and shifts the adjustment into temporary measures, then we will still have the same uncompetitive cost base in 2030. That is not acceptable to management, to the owners, or to investors.

Where I **do** agree:
- A durable settlement must include investment and a credible industrial plan.
- Any workforce contribution must be negotiated, not imposed casually.
- We need solutions that preserve the company’s ability to produce in Germany long term.

Where I **do not** agree:
- I cannot accept a blanket “no closures, no redundancies” line if it prevents the structural correction we need.
- I cannot accept a package that rules out compulsory job reductions in advance while leaving overcapacity untouched.
- I cannot accept the idea that the workforce alone should carry the burden either — but that cuts both ways: it also cannot be that the cost base remains frozen while we pretend competitiveness will somehow return.

To the union: I hear your point that any employee contribution must be temporary and matched by guarantees. But guarantees without a credible cost and capacity reduction are just postponing the problem. A cosmetic deal will fail, and then the consequences will be worse for everyone.

So my position is this:

1. We need a **substantial, durable reduction in costs and capacity**.
2. We are open on the form — closures, production shifts, site-specific changes, working-time measures, voluntary exits, and other structural savings can all be part of the package.
3. But there must be **real scale**, not symbolic savings.
4. There must be a **meaningful labour-cost contribution**.
5. And there must be a settlement that restores margin and frees investment for future products and technology.

If the employee side is prepared to engage on that basis, then there is a path to agreement. If the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no deeper cost change,” then we are not solving the crisis — we are deferring it.

I will be direct: management cannot and will not endorse a solution that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.
