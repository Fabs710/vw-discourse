We share the core point that this cannot be solved by making the workforce carry the whole burden.

Let me be equally clear: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable**. Not as a starting point, and not as the price for management’s failed cost strategy. The workforce has already made significant contributions in the past years, and it is not fair to demand that employees now pay for structural problems alone while management, owners, and capital markets are spared.

That said, we are not pretending there is no crisis. We recognize that the company needs a durable solution, and we are prepared to discuss a **shared-sacrifice package** if it truly secures jobs and sites. That could include:
- negotiated labour-cost relief,
- temporary or structured working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments that do **not** destroy plants,
- foregone bonuses or other employee contributions,
- and productivity measures that are agreed rather than imposed.

But our line is firm: **no forced layoffs, no German plant closures, no demolition of the collective wage framework**. If management wants flexibility, then it must come with **binding guarantees** on employment, sites, and investment, plus a fair contribution from shareholders and top management. We will not accept a restructuring where employees are asked to absorb all the pain in exchange for vague promises.

So to management: bring a credible plan that protects the industrial base in Germany and shares the burden fairly.  
To the works council: we are aligned on the essential red lines.  
To the other stakeholders: if you want industrial peace, you need to show that this is a turnaround for the company, not a transfer of risk onto the workforce.
