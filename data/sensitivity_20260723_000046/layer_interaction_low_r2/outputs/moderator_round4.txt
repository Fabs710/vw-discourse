### Core impasse

The plateau is centered on **burden of proof and sequencing**:

1. **Management / owners / investors** want closures and/or compulsory redundancies to remain available now as credible tools to secure structural savings.
2. **Works council / union / regional state** want **site-preservation and job-security guarantees first**, and closures only if plant-by-plant data prove no alternative can close the gap.
3. The **expert assessments** have clarified that **neither path has yet been proven with actual plant data**.

So the deadlock is not on the need for restructuring — all sides accept that.  
It is on **whether irreversible steps may be pre-committed before the comparative plant-level evidence is produced**, and on **whether a no-closure plan must be tested as a real option before closures are authorized.**

---

### Possible bridging compromise positions

#### 1) Sequenced “proof-first, trigger-later” package
A procedural compromise could be:
- immediate production of the plant-by-plant comparison,
- a defined short timetable,
- and a **conditional** decision rule: closures are not off the table, but cannot be activated until the agreed evidence threshold is met.

This may bridge:
- employee/state demand for evidence before irreversible action,
- management/investor demand that closures remain available if needed.

**Structure:**  
“First model, then decide; if the model shows no-closure cannot meet the target, then pre-agreed triggers activate further steps.”

---

#### 2) Phased restructuring with protected sites and contingency clauses
A second compromise could be:
- preserve all German plants for an initial phase,
- implement a no-closure package with measurable savings,
- set milestones for utilization, product allocation, and capex commitments,
- and include a **review gate** where failure to hit milestones re-opens deeper measures.

This may bridge:
- the works council/union/state insistence on avoiding immediate closures,
- management/owners/investors insistence on enforceable savings.

**Structure:**  
“No plant closes in phase one; if the agreed savings and capacity targets are missed, phase two measures become available.”

---

#### 3) Mixed burden-sharing package with differentiated site treatment
A third compromise could be:
- a common contribution from all parties,
- but **site-by-site differentiation** rather than a blanket closure or blanket guarantee,
- meaning strong sites receive future product and investment commitments,
- while weaker sites get redeployment, gradual volume reduction, or conversion rather than immediate shutdown.

This may bridge:
- the demand for fairness and shared sacrifice,
- the economic requirement to remove structural excess capacity.

**Structure:**  
“Not every plant gets the same solution; each site gets the least destructive option that still achieves the required savings.”

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management / Group & Brand Management
**What specific savings gap remains after a quantified no-closure package, and what is the minimum plant-level evidence threshold that would make you suspend closure plans?**

#### To the Works Council
**If the plant-by-plant model shows one or more German sites cannot be made viable without recurring subsidy, what adjustment to your red lines are you prepared to discuss short of compulsory redundancies?**

#### To the Union
**What measurable employee contribution would you accept in a no-closure package, and what safeguards would you require to ensure it does not become a disguised permanent wage rollback?**

#### To the Regional State
**What concrete use will you make of your blocking position if the evidence shows some footprint reduction is economically unavoidable but a full closure-first approach is not?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a conditional framework that preserves all sites initially but authorizes later restructuring if agreed margin and utilization targets are not met?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**What level of net present value improvement, implementation certainty, and payback period would make you accept a no-closure restructuring over closures?**

#### To the Expert Modellers
**What minimum plant-level inputs are still missing, and what scenario comparison would decisively distinguish “temporary relief” from “durable restructuring”?**

### Facilitator summary proposal

A workable next step, as a procedural matter, would be to ask the parties to agree on:

1. **one shared plant-level model**,  
2. **one common set of assumptions**,  
3. **one deadline for results**, and  
4. **one conditional decision rule** that defines when each side’s fallback position becomes operative.
