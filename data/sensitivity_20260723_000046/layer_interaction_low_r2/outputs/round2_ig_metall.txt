The expert’s contribution is useful, because it confirms what we have said from the beginning: **closure is not a given; it must be proven necessary with plant-level facts**. Right now, management and the owners are still asking the workforce to accept irreversible damage on the basis of a claim, not a demonstrated necessity.

So let me respond directly.

### Where we agree
We agree with the expert, the regional state, and even management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable restructuring, not a delay.

We also agree that the solution must be **quantified, enforceable, and durable**. We are not offering slogans. We have already signaled willingness to discuss:
- labour-cost contributions,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and other negotiated sacrifices.

That remains true.

### Where we disagree
What we do **not** accept is the leap from “there is a cost problem” to “therefore plant closures and forced redundancies are justified.”

The expert was careful: a no-closure path is possible if there is enough future product volume, redeployment, and durable fixed-cost relief. That is exactly why we insist on a serious alternative first. Management has **not** shown that every other route is exhausted.

So the burden remains on management and the owners to prove, plant by plant:
- where the actual overcapacity sits,
- what savings are required,
- what future products are assigned,
- what redeployment is possible,
- and why closures would be unavoidable.

Until that is shown, “closures on the table” is not a conclusion — it is a negotiating threat.

### On the works council’s position
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no German plant closures,**
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

That is not maximalism; it is the minimum condition for an acceptable settlement.

The works council is right to demand:
- plant-by-plant transparency,
- a no-closure scenario,
- and binding site and employment guarantees.

We support that. But I want to be equally clear: a guarantee only has value if it is backed by real economic measures on all sides. That means management restraint, owner contribution, and a fair employee contribution — not one-sided cuts.

### On management’s argument
Management keeps saying that “reversible” measures are not enough. That may be true in the abstract, but it does **not** justify pre-emptively choosing closures. The expert’s analysis shows other levers exist:
- shift reductions,
- workforce reduction via attrition and voluntary exits,
- productivity gains,
- product transfer,
- overhead cuts,
- management restraint.

If management believes these are insufficient, then present the numbers. Don’t ask the workforce to sign away jobs first and promise evidence later.

And let me say this plainly: we will not accept a plan where employees are asked to absorb wage restraint, longer working-time flexibility, and job losses, while management and ownership present themselves as bystanders. That is exactly the kind of unfair burden-sharing we will resist.

### On the controlling family and investors
You both keep talking about “structural savings” as if the only credible path is to shrink the workforce. That is not an industrial law; it is an owner preference unless proven otherwise.

If you want labour peace and a durable turnaround, then your contribution has to be real:
- no symbolic gestures,
- no outsourcing the pain entirely to the plants,
- no hiding behind market discipline while ignoring social consequences.

A durable solution means:
- employees contribute,
- management contributes,
- owners contribute,
- and the company invests in the future.

### On the regional state
Your insistence on preserving Germany’s industrial base is right. But that has to translate into concrete support for a no-closure solution, not just criticism of management. If you believe closures should not be the default, then use your blocking position accordingly.

### Our minimum position remains unchanged
We will not move off these core points:
- **every German plant stays open,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **binding site and employment guarantees through 2030,**
- **a fair, negotiated employee contribution,**
- **and a genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring.**

We are prepared to discuss meaningful contributions from the workforce, including labour-cost relief and operational flexibility, **if and only if** they are tied to site security, job protection, and a balanced burden-sharing package.

### What we want next
If management is serious about an evidence-based process, then bring forward:
1. the **baseline gap** to be closed,
2. a **fully quantified no-closure scenario**,
3. a **closure scenario** only as a reference point,
4. the **employment impact** of both,
5. the **investment and product commitments** tied to each,
6. and the **guarantees** that prevent the workforce from carrying all the risk.

Until then, we do not accept that closures are necessary. And we will not be pressured into trading away jobs and sites for a vague promise of competitiveness.
