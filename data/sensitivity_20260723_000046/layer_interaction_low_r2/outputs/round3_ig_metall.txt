I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable restructuring, not a postponement.

I also agree that the discussion must be **quantified and plant-specific**. If management says there is overcapacity, then it must show that overcapacity in numbers, not in rhetoric.

And I agree with the expert assessments on one important issue: **a no-closure solution is not automatically impossible**. It remains credible **if** it delivers durable fixed-cost reduction, real capacity adjustment, workable redeployment, and future product allocation.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to turn “structural savings” into a synonym for:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and unilateral workforce pain.

That is not a restructuring plan. That is a transfer of risk and cost onto employees.

The works council is right to say that closures are not proven necessary. Management has not yet shown that every alternative has been exhausted. Until that proof exists, closures remain a **choice**, not a necessity.

## To management
You say you need a credible path back to margins. Fine. Show it.

But do not ask the workforce to sign away jobs and wage standards first and promise the evidence later. That reverses the burden of proof.

If you want us to discuss sacrifices, then bring:
1. the baseline gap,
2. a genuinely quantified no-closure scenario,
3. a closure scenario,
4. the employment effect of each,
5. the product allocation behind each,
6. and the binding commitments on investment and site security.

Until then, your closure threat is just that: a threat.

## To the controlling family and investors
You keep talking as if the only serious solution is footprint reduction. That may be your preference, but it is not yet established as the only economically valid route.

If you want durable value, you need more than a short-term margin fix. You need a settlement that preserves the company’s industrial base and social legitimacy. A solution that destroys trust in the workforce will not be stable.

## To the regional state
Your insistence on preserving Germany as an industrial location is correct. But that cannot remain rhetorical. If you are serious, then your blocking position must be used to prevent a settlement where employees carry all the pain and management gets a blank cheque for closures.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework,**
- and **no one-sided burdening of the workforce**.

I support your demand for plant-by-plant transparency and for a serious no-closure scenario. That is exactly the right demand.

At the same time, I want to be equally clear: we are prepared to discuss **real employee contributions** if they are part of a fair package. That can include:
- wage moderation within the collective framework,
- temporary or targeted working-time changes,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- redeployment,
- and other negotiated contributions.

But those contributions are only acceptable if management, owners, and the group also contribute and if the package protects jobs and sites. We will not accept an arrangement where the workforce pays, but management and ownership simply write the plan and walk away.

## What would move us
A fair and durable settlement would need to meet three conditions:
1. **Protect jobs as far as possible and rule out forced layoffs as the first instrument.**
2. **Preserve collective bargaining standards and avoid dismantling the wage framework.**
3. **Share the burden across management, owners, and employees.**

That means real concessions by all sides, not symbolic gestures by workers and hard cuts everywhere else.

## My position for the next round
So I am not moving off the essentials:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures as a starting point.**
- **No weakening of the collective wage framework.**
- **Yes to a fair employee contribution if and only if it is matched by management and owner contribution and tied to job and site security.**

If management cannot produce a no-closure path with hard numbers, then it must at least prove that its closure case is unavoidable. Until then, the workforce has no reason to accept irreversible damage.
