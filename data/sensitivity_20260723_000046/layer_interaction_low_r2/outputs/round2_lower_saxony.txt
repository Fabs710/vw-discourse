I appreciate the expert’s clarification, because it confirms something important: **no one has yet proven that closures are unavoidable**. That matters.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one basic point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable restructuring, not a temporary fix.

I also agree that the solution must be:
- economically credible,
- quantified,
- and enforceable.

And I agree that the burden cannot fall only on employees. Management, owners, and the group as a whole must contribute too.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that “structural savings” automatically means plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

The expert has been careful: a no-closure path may be possible if it delivers:
- durable fixed-cost reduction,
- material capacity reduction,
- credible redeployment,
- and future product allocation.

That means the burden is still on management and the owners to **prove** that closures are necessary, not merely more convenient.

So I am not prepared to endorse closures as the default answer.

### On the works council and union
Your red lines are clear, and I understand them:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- and no unfair burden on workers.

I support the principle that the workforce should not be made to pay alone for strategic mistakes. But I also need to be honest: if we want this to hold, then the alternative package must be real, not symbolic. It must show actual savings and actual capacity adjustment.

That is where the discussion now needs to become concrete.

### On management
Management keeps saying closures remain on the table. That may be true as a bargaining position, but it is **not yet a demonstrated necessity**.

What I need from you is not a general warning, but a serious comparison:
- what is the baseline gap,
- what savings are required,
- what the no-closure scenario delivers,
- what the closure scenario delivers,
- and why the first cannot work.

Until then, I am not convinced that irreversible plant closures should be the first resort.

### On the controlling family and investors
I hear your insistence on structural savings and competitiveness. That is legitimate. But from my standpoint, you are moving too quickly from “the company needs a turnaround” to “therefore sites must close.”

That leap is not justified without the plant-level evidence. If you want durable value, then you should also value stability, social peace, and the company’s role as an industrial anchor. A purely financial logic that ignores that will damage the company’s legitimacy and its long-term capacity to operate here.

### What would move me
What could move the regional state is a restructuring that:
- preserves sites and the industrial base,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and still restores competitiveness.

I am open to:
- working-time adjustments,
- negotiated labor-cost contributions,
- productivity measures,
- internal redeployment,
- management restraint,
- and other socially responsible measures.

But I am **not** open to a plan that guts regional employment or sidelines our co-determination role.

### My position for the next step
So my position is this:

- **No closure should be assumed necessary without proof.**
- **No compulsory redundancies should be treated as inevitable without exhausting alternatives.**
- **A no-closure scenario must be presented seriously and plant by plant.**
- **If that scenario fails, only then do we discuss more intrusive steps.**

### What I expect next
I support the request for a structured comparison, but it must be honest. Management should table:
1. the baseline economic gap,
2. a quantified no-closure scenario,
3. a quantified closure/consolidation scenario,
4. employment and redeployment impacts,
5. investment commitments,
6. and the consequences for co-determination and site stability.

Until that is on the table, I will not endorse plant closures as necessary. And I will not accept any settlement that destroys jobs in the state or weakens the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.
