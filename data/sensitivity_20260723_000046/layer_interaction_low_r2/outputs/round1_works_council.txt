The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. The workforce has already delivered through years of transformation and restraint; it cannot be made to pay for management’s strategic and cost mistakes by losing sites, jobs, and hard-won protections.

If the company needs restructuring, then it must be done through alternatives that preserve every German plant and secure employment through 2030. That means:
- binding site guarantees for all German locations,
- no forced layoffs,
- negotiated, socially responsible capacity adjustments,
- fair contribution from all sides, including management and shareholders,
- and a real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the plan.

We are prepared to discuss measures that make the company fit for the future, but only if they are reversible, socially responsible, and do not destroy the industrial base in Germany. We will not agree to a fait accompli, and we will resist any attempt to break the historic job-security framework.

If management wants a solution, it must come with credible long-term commitments to jobs, sites, and investment — not closures dressed up as necessity.
