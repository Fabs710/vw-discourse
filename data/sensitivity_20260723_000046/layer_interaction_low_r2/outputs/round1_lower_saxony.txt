I will be straightforward.

We recognize the seriousness of the company’s situation and we do not deny that restructuring will require sacrifices. But the way management frames the issue is too narrow and, frankly, too risky for the company’s long-term role in Germany.

I agree with management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable solution, not a temporary patch. It must regain competitiveness and secure investment in future technologies.

Where I disagree is on the implied conclusion that this necessarily requires **plant closures in Germany** and the dismantling of core employment protections. That is not something we can accept as the starting point, and it is not compatible with the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.

From our perspective, there must be a serious effort to find a restructuring path that:
- preserves the German industrial base,
- avoids unnecessary closures,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and keeps co-determination and our blocking rights meaningful in the process.

Management says it needs “hard numbers.” Fair enough. Then those numbers must also prove that closures are truly unavoidable after all alternatives have been exhausted. I have not seen that case made yet. Before anyone reaches for irreversible steps, I expect to see concrete options such as:
- capacity adjustments without site destruction,
- working-time and flexibility arrangements,
- negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- investment commitments tied to site retention,
- and productivity improvements across the group, not only on the backs of workers in Germany.

I also want to be clear that the regional state will not support a solution that simply shifts the burden onto employees while stripping the company of its social balance and regional responsibility. We are prepared to support a negotiated restructuring, but not one that guts employment or sidelines co-determination.

To the works council and union: your insistence on protecting jobs and sites is understandable and legitimate. I share the core concern. But the final solution must be credible economically as well, not only politically. If alternatives can deliver durable savings, they need to be brought forward in a serious, quantified way.

To management: if you want support for deep cuts, you need to show why less destructive options are insufficient. Right now, your position sounds more like a preference for maximum flexibility than a demonstrated necessity.

So my position is this: **we are open to restructuring, but not to closures as a first resort, and not to a plan that destroys jobs and undermines the company’s social partnership model.** If there is a viable path that preserves sites, protects employment, and restores competitiveness, we should pursue it. If not, then you will face resistance from us.
