We agree on the premise that **doing nothing is not an option**. We also agree that the burden cannot fall on employees alone. That is not in dispute.

Where I part company with the works council, the union, and to a lesser extent the state, is on the idea that **every German site must be preserved regardless of economics** and that **no compulsory redundancies** can be treated as a binding premise from the outset.

That is not a restructuring strategy. It is a request to freeze the business model while expecting the owners to absorb the cost.

### On the employee side
I understand the insistence on job security and site protection. It is legitimate to ask for fairness and a real contribution from management and ownership. But I do **not** accept the claim that closures are merely a management preference or that they can be ruled out before the economics are tested.

If the footprint is structurally too large, then preserving every site will simply lock in:
- excess fixed cost,
- weak utilization,
- and continued margin erosion.

That would ultimately threaten more value, more investment, and more jobs over time.

### On the regional state
I respect the state’s role and its concern for the industrial base. But regional responsibility cannot become a veto on necessary adaptation. If there is a credible no-closure solution, I will support it. If there is not, then political discomfort is not a sufficient reason to carry structurally uncompetitive capacity indefinitely.

### On management
Management is right to insist on **hard numbers** and **durable savings**. I support that. What I do not support is a vague negotiating posture that uses “no closures” as a slogan without proving how the same result is achieved.

If management wants the owners to back a difficult restructuring, it must table a clear comparison:
- baseline gap,
- no-closure scenario,
- closure/consolidation scenario,
- headcount impact,
- one-off costs,
- and expected margin improvement.

### My position
I am **open to alternatives to closures** — but only if they are economically equivalent, enforceable, and durable.

I am **not open** to:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- temporary fixes dressed up as restructuring,
- or a package that preserves overcapacity and simply delays the problem.

### What I want next
Bring forward the quantified scenarios. If the no-closure path truly closes the structural gap and protects competitiveness, then it deserves serious consideration. If it does not, then we should not pretend otherwise.
