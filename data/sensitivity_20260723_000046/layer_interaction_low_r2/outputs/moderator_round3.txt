### 1) What has been agreed
- Doing nothing is not an option; a durable restructuring is needed.
- The discussion must be based on hard, plant-by-plant numbers rather than slogans.
- A no-closure solution is not ruled out in principle, but it must demonstrate durable savings and capacity adjustment.
- Burden-sharing matters: no side wants employees to bear the full cost alone.
- Future product allocation, capex, and redeployment are central to any viable path.
- An independent plant-level financial comparison is needed.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Employee representatives and the regional state resist closures as a starting point; management, owners, and investors treat closures/consolidation as a legitimate option if economics require it.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Works council and union reject them; management and capital-side actors will not rule them out in advance.
- **Job and site guarantees:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees and preservation of all sites; capital-side actors view such guarantees as potentially incompatible with restructuring needs.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  All accept some shared contribution, but disagree sharply on how much can be asked of labour versus management/owners.
- **Role of the state/co-determination:**  
  The state emphasizes blocking power and industrial-stability concerns; management and owners caution against governance constraints that could limit restructuring options.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- No plant-by-plant data have yet been supplied to compare:
  - current utilization,
  - fixed-cost burden,
  - future product allocation,
  - capex needs,
  - redeployment capacity,
  - and one-off closure costs.
- The net economic comparison between:
  - a **no-closure package**, and
  - a **closure/consolidation package**
  remains unresolved.
- The time horizon trade-off is still open:
  - short-term social and industrial-relations stability
  vs.
  - longer-term cost base reduction and competitiveness.
- The feasibility of redeploying surplus labor without forced layoffs is still untested.
- It remains unclear whether a no-closure package can produce durable, enforceable savings or would mainly defer the problem.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Keep the discussion strictly on a **plant-by-plant quantified comparison**.
- Require both scenarios to be presented side by side:
  1. no-closure package,
  2. closure/consolidation package.
- For each plant, ask for:
  - utilization,
  - product allocation,
  - fixed costs,
  - capex,
  - redeployment options,
  - recurring savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - and employment impact.
- Use that comparison to test whether a no-closure solution is economically credible or whether footprint reduction is materially stronger.
