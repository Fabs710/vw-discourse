Let me be equally direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable restructuring, not a delay. I also agree that we need **hard numbers**, plant by plant, not slogans.

But I do **not** agree that the current employee-side red lines — **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding guarantees through 2030** — can be accepted as the starting framework. They may be understandable from a workforce perspective, but they do not answer the core economic problem: **our German cost base and footprint are too heavy for the market we face**.

## Where I agree
- The burden cannot fall on employees alone.
- Management, ownership, and the workforce all have to contribute.
- A cosmetic compromise would be worthless.
- Future products and capex matter.
- Plant-level transparency is necessary.

## Where I disagree
### To the works council
You keep saying closures are just a management choice. They are not “just” a choice if the economics require structural capacity reduction. If a site is underutilized, high-cost, and lacks future product allocation, then preserving it indefinitely is not a plan — it is a postponement.

You also say we have not proven alternatives are exhausted. That is exactly why we have called for a quantified comparison. But until a no-closure package shows **durable fixed-cost reduction and real capacity correction**, I cannot treat it as sufficient.

A no-closure solution that only relies on temporary measures, open-ended guarantees, or delayed adjustments is not acceptable. It would leave the company uncompetitive and push the problem into the next cycle.

### To the union
I respect the fact that you are prepared to discuss labour-cost contributions, working-time adjustments, and productivity measures. That is at least a serious starting point.

But I do **not** accept the idea that compulsory redundancies can be ruled out in advance. If the structural gap cannot be closed otherwise, then we need tools that actually remove cost and capacity. I will not pretend that aspiration is a restructuring strategy.

### To the regional state
Your role is important, and I understand your concern for the industrial base. But protecting the industrial base also means ensuring the company remains competitive. A site-preservation strategy that locks in structural losses will eventually damage the base it is trying to defend.

### To the controlling family and investors
I agree with your core point: we need **structural savings**, not symbolic measures. I also agree that investor confidence will not survive a cosmetic deal.

But structural savings must be **implemented**, not merely demanded. That is why I need the plant-level model. The model has to tell us, honestly:
1. where overcapacity sits,
2. what each scenario saves,
3. what each scenario costs,
4. and how fast margin recovery occurs.

## What the discussion still needs
The expert contributions have been helpful, but they also confirm that we still lack the decisive evidence. So I want the next round to be focused on a **quantified comparison**, not continued repetition of positions.

### [SUMMON: Independent plant-level financial modeller to compare no-closure vs closure/consolidation scenarios, by plant, including fixed cost, utilization, product allocation, one-off costs, redeployment feasibility, and time to margin impact.]

That comparison must show, for each German plant:
- current and projected utilization,
- fixed and variable cost burden,
- capex needs to remain competitive,
- future product allocation,
- redeployment options for surplus labor,
- and the savings under:
  - a no-closure package,
  - and a closure/consolidation package.

## My position remains
Until a credible no-closure scenario proves it can deliver the required durable savings, **closures remain a necessary option**. And if the data shows that some plants are structurally unviable, then we must be prepared to take the harder decisions.

I am willing to consider alternatives to closure only if they produce:
- a real and lasting reduction in the cost base,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- and a credible path back to target margins by 2030.

Anything less would be a cosmetic compromise, and that is not acceptable.
