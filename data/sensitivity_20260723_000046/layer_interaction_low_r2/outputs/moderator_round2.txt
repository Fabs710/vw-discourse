## Moderator Summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.** All sides accept that restructuring is necessary.
- **The discussion must be evidence-based.** Several participants, including the expert, stressed the need for plant-level figures rather than general claims.
- **The burden cannot fall only on employees.** There is broad acknowledgment that management and owners must also contribute.
- **Future product allocation and capex matter.** There is agreement that plant viability depends on workload, investment, and assigned future products.
- **A quantified comparison of scenarios is needed.** There is shared support for comparing a no-closure path against a closure/consolidation path on the same financial basis.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  The works council, union, and regional state resist closures as a starting point; management, investors, and the controlling family keep them as a necessary option.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side actors reject them; management has not ruled them out.
- **Guarantees through 2030:**  
  Employee-side actors want binding site and employment guarantees; management and owners say they cannot commit without proof that the economics work.
- **Interpretation of “structural savings”:**  
  One side sees savings as achievable through internal adjustments and burden-sharing; the other argues that durable savings may require footprint reduction.
- **Whether a no-closure solution is credible:**  
  The key dispute is whether alternatives can close the gap without closures or whether they only delay the problem.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Durable fixed-cost reduction vs. site preservation**
- **Capacity reduction vs. employment security**
- **One-time restructuring costs vs. long-term margin recovery**
- **Redeployment/attrition vs. compulsory workforce reduction**
- **Labor flexibility and contributions vs. binding guarantees**
- **Political/social stability vs. industrial competitiveness**
- **Management and owner contribution vs. employee contribution**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to **quantified scenario testing**. Please provide, for each German plant and the group as a whole:
1. the **baseline gap** to be closed,
2. a **no-closure scenario** with quantified savings and employment effects,
3. a **closure/consolidation scenario** with quantified savings, costs, and job impacts,
4. the **future product allocation** under each scenario,
5. the **redeployment feasibility** for surplus labor,
6. and the **implementation timeline and risk** for both options.

The discussion should now focus on which scenario, if either, can deliver **durable competitiveness** with the least overall damage.
