We are listening carefully, and we share several of the premises that have been stated.

First: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable improvement in competitiveness, not a temporary postponement. If margins remain structurally weak, then future investment, product renewal, and ultimately the long-term value of the enterprise are all at risk. On that point, we are aligned with management and with the regional state.

Second: **the burden cannot fall on employees alone**. Any credible turnaround must include contributions from management, labour, and ownership. That is also in the interest of stability and labour peace.

Where we are not prepared to follow the employee side or the state is on the proposition that **all German sites must be preserved regardless of economic reality**. We do not view plant closures as desirable. But neither do we regard them as automatically off limits if they are part of a credible, durable restructuring that protects the larger enterprise.

A few points very clearly:

- **Blanket site guarantees through 2030 are not credible if they foreclose necessary restructuring.**
- **No compulsory redundancies is not a commitment we can make as an owner if no alternative delivers the required savings.**
- **Reversible measures alone are unlikely to solve a structural overcapacity problem.**
- **Symbolic sacrifices are not enough; the numbers must work.**

What would move us is a plan that actually restores competitiveness and protects the company’s long-term value. That means:

1. **Quantified, durable cost reduction**, not just temporary relief.  
2. **A real reduction in excess capacity**, wherever that is operationally necessary.  
3. **A fair employee contribution**, including labour-cost measures, flexibility, and productivity improvements.  
4. **Management discipline**, including visible restraint at the top.  
5. **A clear investment path** for the future product portfolio, so restructuring is not just shrinkage.

To the works council and union: if you want to avoid closures, then bring us a package that is economically equivalent, enforceable, and durable. If you cannot show that it closes the structural gap, then insisting on no closures is simply asking the owners to accept permanent underperformance. That is not acceptable.

To the regional state: we respect the social role of the company, but regional responsibility cannot become a veto on necessary industrial adaptation. The blocking rights and co-determination structures exist for balance, not to freeze the business in place.

So our position is straightforward: **we support a serious restructuring, including plant closures if they are necessary to restore the company’s competitiveness.** We are open to alternative packages only if they deliver the same structural effect. Anything less would be value-destructive and unfair to the long-term owners and the company itself.
