We are not going to support a cosmetic deal that preserves all the current cost problems under a different label.

Our position is simple: **the outcome has to restore competitive margins on a durable basis**. If it does not, then it is not a restructuring; it is a delay. And delay is value-destructive.

So, where we agree:
- We agree with management, and to a degree with the controlling family, that **doing nothing is not an option**.
- We agree that the company needs **structural** cost reduction, not a temporary patch.
- We agree that the burden should not fall only on employees.

Where we do **not** agree:
- We are not interested in blanket assurances that leave the cost base uncompetitive.
- We will not support an outcome that simply protects every site and every legacy arrangement while margins remain structurally weak.
- We are not persuaded by arguments that closures are politically difficult if the numbers show they are needed.

To the works council and union: your red lines may be understandable from a labour perspective, but from a shareholder perspective they are only acceptable if you can show a **credible alternative** that achieves the same economic result. “No closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not enough on its own. We need to see:
- quantified savings,
- durable capacity reduction,
- real labour-cost relief,
- and enforceability.

To management: if closures or material workforce reduction are truly necessary, then say so plainly and back it up with hard numbers. But if you want investor support, you also need to show:
- why this package is sufficient,
- how quickly margins improve,
- and why the savings will not leak away through weak implementation or endless negotiation.

To the regional state: we understand the political constraints, but we cannot price in political discomfort if the business remains structurally uncompetitive. Regional responsibility cannot become a guarantee of permanent overcapacity.

To the controlling family: we note your support for decisive action. That is aligned with our interests, but support for “serious restructuring” has to mean actual delivery, not just rhetoric. If the final package does not materially improve returns, we will regard it as a failure.

Our bottom line is this:

- **Acceptable:** a package that delivers durable savings, credible capacity reduction, and a clear path back to target margins, even if it involves closures or job cuts.
- **Potentially acceptable:** an alternative package without closures, but only if it produces the same financial outcome with strong enforcement.
- **Unacceptable:** a politically convenient compromise that preserves structurally high costs and leaves shareholders with ongoing margin erosion.

If the discussion is going to move, it needs to move on **numbers**, not on promises.
