We agree on the core diagnosis: **doing nothing is not an option**. From an equity-holder perspective, continued margin erosion and a structurally uncompetitive cost base are unacceptable.

Where we agree with others:
- **Management is right** that the discussion must be quantified, not rhetorical.
- **The expert’s point is right** that plant-level economics matter and that a no-closure option cannot be dismissed without testing.
- **The controlling family is right** that cosmetic compromise is value-destructive.
- **The state is right** that social legitimacy and industrial stability matter, but only if they do not lock in permanent underperformance.

Where we disagree:
- We do **not** accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees through 2030” as a default framework. If that package cannot close the structural gap, it is not a solution.
- We do **not** accept preserving every German site as a principle. A company with excess capacity and weak margins cannot be managed for sentiment.
- We do **not** accept temporary concessions that protect headline peace while leaving the cost base uncompetitive. That would be a cosmetic settlement, and we would treat it as such.

Our position is simple:
- **Acceptable:** a restructuring that clearly and durably reduces fixed cost and capacity, with credible margin recovery.
- **Potentially acceptable:** a no-closure package, but only if it is proven to achieve equivalent durable savings, not just delay the pain.
- **Unacceptable:** any deal that preserves overcapacity, protects all sites regardless of economics, and leaves shareholder returns structurally impaired.

What is still missing is the same thing the experts and moderator identified: a **plant-by-plant quantified comparison** of:
1. baseline gap to target margins,
2. no-closure savings and timing,
3. closure/consolidation savings and one-off costs,
4. utilization and future product allocation,
5. redeployment feasibility,
6. and implementation risk.

Until that comparison shows a credible path back to competitive margins, we will not support a settlement built on blanket job/site guarantees. If the numbers support closures or deeper footprint reduction, then those measures should not be ruled out.
