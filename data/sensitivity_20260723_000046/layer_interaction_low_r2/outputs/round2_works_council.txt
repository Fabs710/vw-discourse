Management, shareholders, and investors keep repeating “numbers” as if that alone would justify destroying jobs and sites. It does not.

Let me state our position plainly again:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework.**
- **No decision over the heads of the workforce.**

We are not ignoring the company’s situation. We accept that change is needed. But change does **not** have to mean closure, layoffs, and the breaking of promises that have held this company together through previous transformations.

### Where I agree
I agree with the regional state, the union, and even parts of management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable solution, and the burden cannot sit only on employees.

I also agree that we need **hard numbers**, not slogans. But those numbers must be used to test alternatives, not to pre-decide closures.

### Where I disagree
Management says blanket guarantees are not credible. That is only true if management refuses to search seriously for alternatives. The burden is on management and owners to prove that all options preserving sites and employment have been exhausted.

The controlling family and institutional investors talk about “structural savings” as if that automatically means closure. That is a choice, not a law of nature. There are other levers:
- capacity reduction through agreed internal adjustments,
- working-time arrangements,
- temporary contribution mechanisms,
- retraining and internal redeployment,
- productivity gains negotiated with the workforce,
- management restraint at the top,
- and fair owner participation.

If those measures are not enough, then show us why with transparent figures. Until then, closure is an assumption, not a necessity.

### On the proposal for comparative scenarios
I support the moderator’s call to compare scenarios, but only if the benchmark is realistic and fair. So here is what we expect management to put on the table in the next round:

1. **The exact baseline gap** that must be closed, plant by plant and for the group.
2. **A no-closure scenario** showing how the gap is closed without plant shutdowns or forced layoffs.
3. **A closure scenario** showing why it is supposedly unavoidable — with proof, not assertion.
4. **The employment impact** of each scenario.
5. **The investment commitments** tied to each scenario.
6. **The co-determination safeguards** and binding guarantees through 2030.

If management cannot produce a serious no-closure plan, that tells us more about its willingness than about what is economically possible.

### Our minimum position
Our minimum is not complicated:
- every German plant stays open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site and employment guarantees through 2030,
- and a genuine role for the workforce in shaping the restructuring.

If those conditions are not met, then there is no basis for agreement from the works council. And if management tries to force a fait accompli, it will face resistance from the workforce and from the co-determination side.

### To the regional state
You are right to insist that the company remains a German industrial anchor. Then use your blocking position accordingly. A solution that destroys jobs and undermines social partnership is not in the regional interest.

### To the union
We are aligned on the core red lines. Keep the pressure up.

### To management and owners
Bring a real alternative to closures. Not promises, not “symbolic sacrifices,” but a binding plan that protects every site and every job. If you cannot do that, then your claim that closures are unavoidable is not yet credible.
