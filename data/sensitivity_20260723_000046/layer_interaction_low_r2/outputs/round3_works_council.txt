The discussion is now clear enough to say this directly:

## My position has not changed

I do **not** accept:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the dismantling of the job-security framework.

And I will be equally clear: I do not accept a “restructuring” that shifts the burden onto the workforce while management and owners present closure as the easy answer.

## Where I agree

I agree with everyone on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable solution.

I also agree that we need **hard numbers** and that we should not hide behind slogans. If management claims there is overcapacity, then show it plant by plant. If it claims closures are necessary, then prove why alternatives fail.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You keep saying closures are “on the table” as if that settles the matter. It does not.

You have **not yet shown** that every non-closure route has been exhausted. You have not shown that a serious package of:
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity measures,
- internal redeployment,
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- and product allocation changes

cannot close the gap.

Until you do, closure is a **choice**, not a necessity.

And I reject the idea that the workforce should first give up jobs, sites, and guarantees and only later be told whether that was really needed.

### To the controlling family and investors
You talk about “durable savings,” but you are treating jobs and sites as if they were just variables in a spreadsheet.

They are not. These are livelihoods, regional industrial capacity, and the basis of social peace at the company.

If you want us to accept sacrifices, then the sacrifices must be **shared** and **reversible where possible**. A permanent plant closure is neither reversible nor fair if there is still a credible alternative.

### To the regional state
I welcome your insistence that this company remains an industrial anchor. But that has to mean more than asking for a better tone. It means using your blocking position to prevent a solution that destroys sites and jobs.

If you believe the company’s legitimacy matters, then do not let management turn “restructuring” into a unilateral withdrawal from its responsibility.

### To the union
We are aligned on the core red lines. I support your stance that the workforce cannot be asked to bear the whole burden. Warning strikes and pressure were necessary because management has been acting as if the workforce should simply absorb the shock.

Keep that pressure up. It matters.

## On the expert assessments
The experts have been careful, and that is exactly why management’s position is still not enough.

They did **not** say closures are inevitable. They said:
- the no-closure option has **not yet been proven**,
- but it could still be credible if it delivers durable fixed-cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- redeployment,
- and future product allocation.

That means the correct next step is **not** to accept closures. It is to force management to produce a credible no-closure plan with real figures.

## What I expect next
If management is serious, then table the following for **each German plant**:
1. current utilization,
2. future product allocation,
3. fixed-cost burden,
4. capex required,
5. redeployment options,
6. and the concrete effect of alternative measures.

And for the group as a whole:
1. the baseline gap,
2. the savings from a no-closure plan,
3. the savings from a closure plan,
4. the employment effect of each,
5. and the guarantees attached.

## My minimum remains unchanged
- **Every German plant stays open.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding site and employment guarantees through 2030.**
- **A genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring.**

If those conditions are not met, then there is no basis for agreement from the works council.

## Final point
I will not pretend that a bad compromise becomes acceptable because it is called “credible.” Credibility has to include social responsibility, job security, and respect for the workforce’s rights.

So my answer is simple:
- show us a real alternative that preserves sites and jobs, or
- acknowledge that you are choosing to break promises and force a conflict.

We are prepared for that conflict.
