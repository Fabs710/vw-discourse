## Scenario comparison framework

### A) No-closure scenario
Typical tools:
- working-time reduction
- wage moderation / labor contribution
- voluntary attrition
- internal redeployment
- management overhead cuts
- productivity improvements
- product shifts within existing sites
- delayed or reduced capex at weaker sites, where feasible

#### Economic strengths
- avoids immediate one-off closure costs
- avoids disruption to supply chain and transfer logistics
- preserves optionality if market demand recovers
- reduces political and industrial-relations shock

#### Economic weaknesses
- savings may be too small if plants remain underutilized
- some measures are temporary, not structural
- if product allocation does not change, the company may preserve excess footprint at high cost
- can defer rather than solve the capacity problem

#### Decision test
A no-closure package is credible only if it produces:
1. **durable fixed-cost reduction**
2. **meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **clear redeployment of labor**
4. **future product allocation for retained plants**
5. **enforceable labor-cost contribution**

If it fails any of these, it is likely a delay mechanism rather than a restructuring.

---

### B) Closure / consolidation scenario
Typical tools:
- one or more site closures
- production transfer to retained plants
- workforce reduction
- asset write-downs
- logistics network redesign
- receiving-site capex

#### Economic strengths
- usually delivers the strongest long-run fixed-cost reduction
- removes structural overcapacity directly
- can improve utilization at retained plants
- creates clearer product and footprint concentration

#### Economic weaknesses
- high one-off costs:
  - severance
  - decommissioning
  - asset impairments
  - transfer tooling
  - overlapping ramp-up costs
  - logistics changes
- execution risk if receiving plants cannot absorb volume cleanly
- risk of bottlenecks, quality loss, or delayed launches
- may require additional capex at surviving sites

#### Decision test
A closure case is superior only if:
- the recurring savings are large enough,
- the one-off costs are recoverable in a reasonable period,
- transferred volume can be absorbed without new inefficiencies,
- and the surviving footprint has future product and competitive utilization.
