We hear the red lines you are setting, and I need to be equally clear about ours.

I agree on one point: this cannot be a one-sided burden shift onto employees. A credible restructuring must involve a fair contribution from all sides, including management and, where necessary, owners. But I do **not** agree that “no closures, no forced redundancies, no job reductions” can be the starting point if we want a solution that is actually durable.

The situation is not a normal bargaining round. German factory costs are structurally too high, our European market is shrinking, and our brand is still below its return target. If we only do reversible or temporary measures, we will still have the same overcapacity and the same cost base in 12 or 24 months. That is not a turnaround. It is postponement.

So let me be direct:

- **Plant closures remain on the table.**
- **Material capacity reductions remain on the table.**
- **Labour-cost reductions remain on the table.**
- **A meaningful reduction in the workforce will be necessary.**

I am open on the **mix** of measures, but not on the requirement for **structural savings**. If there are alternatives that achieve the same durable effect — lower fixed costs, less overcapacity, and a cost base aligned with market reality — then we can discuss them. But they must be real, quantified, and enforceable. Vague promises of productivity, goodwill, or “shared sacrifice” will not be enough.

On your specific points:

- **Binding site guarantees through 2030:** I cannot commit to blanket guarantees if they prevent necessary restructuring.
- **No compulsory redundancies:** I am not making that promise today.
- **Fair contribution from all sides:** Yes, but that must include employee-side labour-cost measures, not only management pay or symbolic gestures.
- **Co-determination in shaping the plan:** Yes, within the legal framework. But co-determination cannot become a veto over every change.

To the union and works council: if you want to avoid closures, then come forward with a package that delivers the same economic result — not just protections, but hard numbers. Show how we reduce cost and capacity enough to restore competitiveness and fund future investment to 2030. If that package is credible and durable, I will engage seriously. If it is simply a demand to preserve all sites and all protections unchanged, then we are not solving the crisis.
