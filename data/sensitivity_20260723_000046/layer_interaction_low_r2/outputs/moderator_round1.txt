### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not acceptable.** All sides accept that the company needs a durable restructuring rather than a temporary patch.
- **The burden should not fall on employees alone.** There is broad agreement that management, owners, and workers must all contribute.
- **Any solution must be credible and durable.** Several parties asked for quantified, enforceable measures rather than vague promises.
- **Future investment matters.** There is shared recognition that restructuring should secure the company’s long-term competitiveness and investment capacity.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and regional state reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management, controlling family, and institutional investors keep closures on the table if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee representatives reject them outright.  
  - Management and owners have not committed to ruling them out.
- **Extent of job and site guarantees:**  
  - Labour and state want binding guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management and shareholders say blanket guarantees are not credible if they block restructuring.
- **How much restructuring is enough:**  
  - Labour side seeks alternatives that preserve sites and employment.  
  - Capital side insists on structural capacity and cost reduction at a scale sufficient to restore margins.
- **Role of co-determination and blocking rights:**  
  - Employee and state actors want a meaningful role that constrains unilateral action.  
  - Management and shareholders accept consultation, but not a veto over necessary change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Job security vs. structural savings:** No shared formula yet for how to achieve the required cost reduction without closures or forced layoffs.
- **Temporary flexibility vs. permanent change:** Alternatives such as working-time reductions or labour-cost concessions have been mentioned, but it is unclear whether they would produce durable enough savings.
- **Preserving sites vs. reallocating capacity:** No consensus on which plants, if any, could be adjusted without closure while still delivering meaningful overcapacity reduction.
- **Fair burden-sharing vs. implementation certainty:** There is agreement in principle on shared sacrifice, but not on who contributes what, how much, and under what enforceable conditions.
- **Political/social responsibility vs. investor return requirements:** The tension between maintaining the industrial base and restoring margins remains unresolved.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **comparative scenarios**: one package with closures/reductions, and one without, each with quantified savings, employment impact, and implementation conditions.
- Require each side to state its **minimum acceptable outcome** and its **non-negotiables** in operational terms.
- Focus discussion on **numbers, timing, and enforceability**:
  - required savings,
  - capacity reduction,
  - workforce impact,
  - investment commitments,
  - and safeguards for co-determination.
- Ask management to present the **baseline economic gap** that must be closed, so alternatives can be assessed against the same benchmark.
