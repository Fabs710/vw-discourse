## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a red line
The union, works council, and state keep saying closures are not the default answer. Fine — they need not be the default answer.

But they are still a **legitimate option** if the numbers show that a plant or major line is structurally underutilized and cannot be made competitive through other means.

The expert did **not** rule that out. In fact, the expert made clear that closure-based restructuring is often the strongest way to remove persistent fixed cost. That is not ideology; that is industrial economics.

So I reject any attempt to rule closures out in advance.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not a restructuring strategy
I understand the social and political sensitivity. But management cannot accept a rule that blocks the company from making hard adjustments if voluntary measures fall short.

If the required savings can be achieved via:
- attrition,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time changes,
- and negotiated capacity reduction,

then we should do that. If they cannot, then refusing harder measures in advance is equivalent to refusing restructuring.

### 3) Burden-sharing must be real, not rhetorical
The labor side says workers must not carry the whole burden. I agree. But that must also mean:
- real operational changes,
- real capacity reduction,
- real productivity gains,
- real labour-cost contribution,
- and no hiding behind permanent job-security guarantees that lock in uncompetitive structures.

The state says site protection matters. I accept that. But site protection that prevents economic adjustment is not sustainable. It just delays the same crisis and ultimately makes the adjustment harder.

## What the discussion has clarified

We now have the right framework:

- **Closure-based restructuring**: highest durable savings, highest conflict cost.
- **Non-closure restructuring**: lower conflict, but often insufficient unless it is very deep.
- **Hybrid restructuring**: most realistic in this governance setup, but only if it removes enough fixed cost to be economically real.

That is the key point.

The question is not whether closures are emotionally difficult. The question is whether the alternatives actually close the gap.

And I will be explicit:  
**I will not support a package that preserves an uncompetitive footprint simply because it is easier to defend politically.**
