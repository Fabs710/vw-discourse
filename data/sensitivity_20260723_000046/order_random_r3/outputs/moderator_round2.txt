1) **What has been agreed**
- The restructuring must be **durable** and based on **numbers**, not slogans.
- There is likely a **real cost gap** and some form of **structural change** is needed.
- A package may include several tools: **capacity reduction, working-time changes, productivity improvements, temporary wage moderation, bonus waivers, overhead/procurement cuts, and investment commitments**.
- All sides accept that any viable solution must address **savings durability**, **implementation risk**, and **competitive viability**.
- There is broad interest in comparing **three paths**: closure-based, non-closure, and hybrid restructuring.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures:**  
  Management, investors, and the expert view closures as potentially necessary if fixed overcapacity is large; union, works council, and state reject closures as the default or starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Union and works council reject them outright; management and investors do not rule out harder measures if voluntary options are insufficient.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  Labour insists workers must not carry the main burden; management and investors insist employee concessions must be substantial and structurally meaningful; ownership and state also want visible contributions from management/owners.
- **Burden of proof:**  
  Labour and state say management must prove closures are unavoidable; management and investors say labour must prove non-closure options can close the gap.
- **Scope of acceptable compromise:**  
  Union wants job-security and site-preservation commitments; management and investors want a package that materially restores competitiveness even if that requires deeper footprint reduction.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- The **actual size of the cost gap** at plant-by-plant level.
- How much of the gap comes from:
  - direct labour cost,
  - fixed capacity/underutilization,
  - overhead,
  - procurement/complexity,
  - and restructuring charges.
- Whether a **non-closure or hybrid package** can produce savings that are:
  - large enough,
  - permanent enough,
  - and fast enough.
- The comparative consequences of each option for:
  - employment,
  - regional stability,
  - industrial relations,
  - execution risk,
  - and long-term capability retention.
- What level of **management/owner contribution** would be considered credible and measurable.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Shift from principle statements to a **plant-by-plant quantified comparison**.
- Test three scenarios side by side:
  1. **Closure-based restructuring**
  2. **Non-closure restructuring**
  3. **Hybrid restructuring**
- For each, specify:
  - annual savings,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - employment impact,
  - savings durability,
  - and implementation risk.
- Use that comparison to identify whether a **credible non-closure or hybrid settlement** can close the gap, or whether deeper footprint reduction appears unavoidable.
