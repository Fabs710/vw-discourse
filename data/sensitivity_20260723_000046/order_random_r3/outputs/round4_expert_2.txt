The expert question is squarely within my area. The right answer is: **a credible comparison requires plant-level, cost-driver-level disclosure with consistent scenario assumptions**. Without that, parties can argue about conclusions indefinitely.

## Minimum plant-level data required

For each German site, and ideally for each major line/program within the site, disclosure should include:

### 1) Volume and utilization
- Installed annual capacity
- Actual annual output, by model/program
- Capacity utilization rate
- Planned volume by year for at least 3 years
- Shift pattern and line loading
- Dependence on single-program vs multi-program production

### 2) Workforce and labour cost
- Headcount by site and function
- Direct labour hours per vehicle
- Fully loaded labour cost per hour
- Direct labour cost per vehicle
- Indirect labour cost per vehicle
- Agency/temporary labour share
- Overtime and short-time working levels
- Age structure and retirement eligibility

### 3) Fixed and variable cost structure
- Site fixed costs
- Variable production costs
- Energy, maintenance, logistics, and internal transport costs
- Overhead allocation method
- Corporate overhead assigned to the site
- Depreciation and asset-specific cost burden

### 4) Productivity and complexity
- Units per employee
- Line speed and downtime
- Scrap/rework rates
- Changeover frequency
- Variant complexity
- Internal logistics complexity
- Supplier complexity, if site-specific

### 5) Restructuring option estimates
For each site, management should show the expected:
- annual run-rate savings
- one-time restructuring cost
- cash timing
- P&L timing
- implementation lead time
- durability of savings

And these should be broken out for:
- voluntary exits / attrition
- early retirement
- working-time changes
- wage moderation / bonus restraint
- productivity measures
- overhead reductions
- procurement/complexity simplification
- line consolidation
- redeployment
- plant closure or major footprint reduction

## Scenario set required

Each site should then be modeled under three standard scenarios:

### 1) Closure-based
- Annual savings
- One-time cost
- Jobs removed
- Capacity removed
- Payback period
- Execution risk
- Volume downside sensitivity

### 2) Non-closure
- Annual savings
- One-time cost
- Jobs reduced without closure
- Capacity retained
- Residual fixed cost burden
- Savings durability

### 3) Hybrid
- Annual savings
- One-time cost
- Mix of internal capacity reduction and labour measures
- Remaining underutilization
- Residual gap to target economics

## What makes a hybrid credible
A hybrid is credible only if it:

- removes **material permanent fixed cost**
- reduces **capacity enough to matter**
- remains viable **after temporary concessions expire**
- does **not** rely on optimistic recovery in volumes
- can be executed without excessive legal, labour, or operational disruption

A hybrid is likely cosmetic if:
- most savings come from temporary wage restraint
- the site remains structurally underutilized
- the fixed-cost base barely changes
- the plan depends on future volume recovery rather than current rationalization

## Practical test for the parties
The key question is:

> After all non-closure measures are applied, what is the residual structural gap, and is it small enough to be closed without deeper footprint reduction?

If the residual gap remains large, then the non-closure or hybrid package is incomplete.

## Bottom line
The minimum data set is not just “costs” in the abstract. It is:
- capacity,
- utilization,
- labour intensity,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- restructuring savings by measure,
- one-time cost,
- and durability under a standardized scenario framework.
