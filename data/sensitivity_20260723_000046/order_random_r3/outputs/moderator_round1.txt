### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **structural, durable solution**, not a cosmetic or temporary fix.
- There is broad agreement that **competitiveness must improve** and that the current cost base is too high.
- Several parties accept that a settlement may need to include some combination of:
  - capacity reductions,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - productivity improvements,
  - employee contributions such as wage moderation or bonus waivers,
  - and investment/product commitments for German sites.
- There is also common recognition that **labor peace, site stability, and long-term viability** all matter.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  The union and works council reject closures; management, investors, and the family holding see them as possible if necessary; the state is strongly opposed to closures as a default tool.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  The union, works council, and state reject them; management and investors keep them as an option if no credible alternative emerges.
- **Scope of workforce sacrifice:**  
  Labor representatives insist on fairness and shared burden; management and investors demand savings large enough to materially change the economics.
- **What counts as a credible alternative:**  
  The labor side says closures/layoffs are not the only route; management and investors say any alternative must deliver equivalent, verifiable, durable cost relief.
- **Governance and veto role:**  
  The regional state emphasizes that its co-determination and veto rights are not negotiable.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are needed** versus **how much restructuring pain is acceptable**.
- Whether the company can achieve the required cost reduction **without closures and forced layoffs**.
- How to balance **site protection** against **capacity rationalization**.
- What the **employee contribution** should be, and whether it is temporary, time-limited, or linked to clear concessions from management/owners.
- What **investment and product commitments** can be made in return for labor concessions.
- How to ensure any settlement is both **socially acceptable** and **financially credible**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and mechanisms**: quantify the cost gap, the needed savings, and the savings from each proposed measure.
- Ask each side to specify **which measures are acceptable**, **which are not**, and **under what conditions**.
- Explore a **package deal** linking:
  - workforce contributions,
  - management/owner contributions,
  - investment commitments,
  - and concrete site/job guarantees.
- Clarify whether there is any **mutually acceptable restructuring path** that avoids compulsory redundancies and broad closures while still meeting the required financial targets.
