I will respond plainly, because we are now at the point where evasiveness would be irresponsible.

## My position

I support a **durable restructuring** that restores competitiveness. I do **not** support a solution that simply postpones the problem, nor do I support a settlement where the regional workforce and sites carry the main burden.

But I also do **not** accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the default answer. In this company, with this governance structure, and with the state’s and workforce’s legitimate role, that would be a serious mistake unless absolutely unavoidable and proven by the numbers.

## Where I agree

### With the expert
The expert has helped clarify the real issue: this is a **cost-gap and capacity-utilization problem**, not a debate about sentiment.

I agree that:
- the gap may be material,
- temporary measures alone are not enough,
- and any credible package must remove **permanent** cost.

I also agree that a **hybrid** or **non-closure** package can only work if it is deep enough to take out real fixed cost and excess capacity.

### With the union and works council
I agree that:
- workers must not be the sole shock absorber,
- closures should not be treated as the automatic starting point,
- and trust matters if the company is to remain an industrial anchor.

I also agree that a package based only on wage restraint would be inadequate.

### With the family holding and investors
I agree that the restructuring must be **durable** and **verifiable**. Cosmetic solutions will not do.

I also agree that management must not be allowed to preserve an unaffordable cost structure in the name of social peace.

## Where I disagree

### On management’s framing
I disagree with the way management continues to present closures as the obvious benchmark. The expert did **not** prove that closures are unavoidable. The expert said they are often the strongest way to remove fixed cost — not that they are automatically necessary here.

That distinction matters.

Management has not yet demonstrated that:
- capacity reduction without closure,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- overhead cuts,
- working-time adjustments,
- and targeted productivity measures

cannot close the gap.

Until that proof exists, I will not accept closures as the baseline option.

### On investors’ pressure language
I understand the capital-market concern about margins and speed. But I do not accept the implication that site protection is somehow an indulgence.

A restructuring that triggers industrial conflict, damages regional legitimacy, and weakens the company’s social base is not value-neutral. It may look cleaner in a spreadsheet and be worse in reality.

### On the union’s absolute language
I support the union’s rejection of forced redundancies and closure-first logic. But I will be equally direct: “no closures, no redundancies” cannot become a blank refusal to recognize that some capacity must come out somewhere.

If the package is to work, there must be **real permanent savings**. That means the workforce must engage seriously with:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- voluntary exits,
- and a meaningful temporary contribution.

## My line as anchor shareholder and regional actor

My red lines remain clear:

- **No gutting of regional employment**
- **No sidelining of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **No forced solution that destroys trust in the German sites**
- **No cosmetic compromise dressed up as restructuring**

What I can support is a package that:
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- protects core sites where possible,
- achieves durable savings,
- and shares the burden fairly among labour, management, and ownership.

## What I want to see next

The discussion is finally moving in the right direction, but we still do not have the critical evidence:

1. **Closure-based restructuring**
2. **Non-closure restructuring**

For each, I want:
- annual savings,
- one-time restructuring charges,

- and execution risk.

I also want this broken down by source:
- direct labour cost,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- overhead,

- and capacity utilization.

## Bottom line

So my position is:

- **Yes** to a durable restructuring.
- **Yes** to a serious hybrid or non-closure package if it genuinely closes the gap.
- **No** to closures as the default starting point.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies.
- **No** to a settlement that shifts the main burden onto the workforce while weakening the company’s regional role.

If the numbers eventually prove that a deeper footprint reduction is unavoidable, then we will have to examine that honestly. But that proof has not been made yet, and I will not concede regional sites and jobs on speculation.
