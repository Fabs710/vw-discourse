We agree with the central financial premise now on the table: **this has to be a durable restructuring that closes a real cost gap**. Anything less will be treated by the market as delay, and delay is value-destructive.

That said, we are **not** prepared to endorse a solution that protects every site and every historic arrangement if the result is still an uncompetitive cost base. From our perspective, the only relevant test is whether the package restores **risk-adjusted returns and margins**.

## Where we agree
- The current German cost base is too high.
- Cosmetic concessions are not enough.
- Temporary wage restraint alone will not solve a structural problem.
- A package must be durable, verified, and tied to real savings.
- Overcapacity, not just wages, is part of the problem.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute
The union, works council, and state keep treating plant closures and forced redundancies as essentially off-limits. We understand the social and political reasons, but we do **not** accept them as a financial red line. If the economics require material fixed-cost removal, then the company cannot be forced into preserving an uncompetitive footprint just to avoid difficult decisions.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not automatically value-preserving
We would prefer voluntary solutions and attrition, but only if they deliver enough permanent savings. If not, then refusing compulsory measures up front just shifts the burden into a slower, weaker, and more expensive restructuring. That is not a shareholder-friendly outcome.

### 3) A “hybrid” is only acceptable if it actually works
We are open to a hybrid package. In fact, that is probably the most realistic route in this governance setup. But it must do the arithmetic:
- reduce fixed costs materially,
- remove real excess capacity,
- lower labour cost structurally,
- and do so with enough certainty that margins recover on schedule.

If a hybrid merely postpones the closure decision while leaving the economics broken, then it is a bad deal.

## What we would support
We would support **any package** that credibly delivers:
- a measurable reduction in overcapacity,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- headcount reduction through attrition, redeployment, and where necessary harder measures,
- overhead and procurement cuts,
- and a clear path back to competitive margins.

We are **not** attached to closures as a matter of principle. We are attached to **outcomes**. If a non-closure or hybrid package can close the gap with verified, durable savings, that is fine. If not, then management should not be blocked from using the tools needed to fix the business.

## What would be unacceptable
- A cosmetic compromise dressed up as social partnership
- A settlement that preserves the current cost base
- Long industrial conflict that erodes earnings and execution
- An agreement where workers make visible sacrifices but the economics barely improve
- Any outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired

## To management
You are right that the issue is economics, not sentiment. But you still need to show the numbers more explicitly. Right now, the discussion is moving in the right direction, but not yet with enough precision.

We would want a clear comparison of:
- closure-based savings,
- non-closure savings,
- hybrid savings,
- one-time restructuring charges,
- timing to cash flow and margin recovery,
- and the probability of execution under each scenario.

Without that, we cannot judge whether the proposed compromise is actually value-creating.

## To the union, works council, and state
We respect the need for social stability, but the market will not fund a settlement whose main achievement is that it is politically easier to sell. The company must become competitive enough to earn its capital cost. If that requires real capacity reduction and tougher labour measures, then those cannot be ruled out in advance.

### Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:
- **Yes** to a durable restructuring.
- **Yes** to a hybrid deal if it delivers real, permanent savings.
- **Yes** to non-closure options if they are economically equivalent.
- **No** to any package that leaves the cost base uncompetitive.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
