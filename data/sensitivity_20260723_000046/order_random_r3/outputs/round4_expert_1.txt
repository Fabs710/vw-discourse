The expert question is: **what minimum data set must be disclosed at plant level to distinguish a credible hybrid restructuring from a cosmetic compromise?**

## Minimum plant-level data set

To assess whether a non-closure or hybrid plan is economically credible, I would require, for **each German site and major production line**, at least the following:

### 1) Output and utilization
- Installed annual capacity
- Current annual output
- Capacity utilization rate
- Planned output by model/program for the next 3–5 years
- Utilization by shift and by line, if relevant

### 2) Cost structure
- Direct labour cost per vehicle
- Indirect labour cost per vehicle
- Total labour hours per vehicle
- Average fully loaded labour cost per hour
- Site fixed costs, separated from variable costs
- Overhead allocation methodology used by management
- Energy, maintenance, logistics, and internal transport costs where site-specific

### 3) Workforce composition
- Headcount by site and function
- Share of permanent, temporary, and agency labour
- Age profile and retirement eligibility band
- Skill bottlenecks and redeployment constraints
- Overtime dependence and short-time use, if any

### 4) Productivity and complexity
- Vehicles or units per employee
- Line speed and downtime
- Scrap/rework rates
- Variant complexity and changeover frequency
- Supplier and internal logistics complexity costs
- Degree of commonization across models and platforms

### 5) Restructuring options and savings
For each site, management should disclose estimated savings from:
- voluntary exits and attrition
- early retirement
- working-time reduction or flexibility
- wage moderation / bonus waivers
- overhead cuts
- procurement and complexity simplification
- line consolidation
- internal redeployment
- capex postponement or reallocation
- plant closure or major footprint reduction, if considered

Each estimate should include:
- annual run-rate savings
- one-time restructuring cost
- timing of cash impact
- timing of P&L impact
- assumed implementation rate
- durability after temporary measures expire

### 6) Scenario comparison
For each site, the company should present three standardized scenarios:
1. **Closure-based**
2. **Non-closure**
3. **Hybrid**

And for each:
- annual savings
- one-time costs
- employment impact
- capacity removed
- payback period
- sensitivity to lower volumes
- execution risk

## What makes a hybrid credible vs cosmetic

A hybrid package is credible only if it produces:
- **material permanent fixed-cost reduction**
- **visible capacity reduction**
- **durable post-concession economics**
- **implementation that does not rely on optimistic volume assumptions**

It is likely cosmetic if:
- savings come mainly from temporary wage restraint
- fixed costs remain largely unchanged
- utilization stays structurally weak
- the plan depends on future volume recovery rather than current rationalization
- the one-time cost is low but the run-rate savings are also too small

## Practical threshold for judgment

I would look for the hybrid package to close **most of the structural gap**, not just a minority share of it. If a site remains materially underutilized after the package, then the plan is not yet economically complete.

In practice, the key question is:

> After all non-closure measures are applied, what is the residual gap to target margin or cost position?

If that residual gap remains large, then the hybrid is not a full solution.
