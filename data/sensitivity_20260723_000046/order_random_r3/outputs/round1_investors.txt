We share one important premise with management, the family owners, and the regional state: **this must be a structural solution, not a cosmetic one**. From an investor perspective, that is the non-negotiable point. If the package does not restore competitive margins and close the cost gap durably, it is not a solution.

That said, we do **not** equate “structural” with “plant closures at any price.”

### Where we agree
- **The current cost base is too high.**
- **The German operations must become materially more competitive.**
- **Temporary optics, one-off savings, or vague promises are not enough.**
- **Management cannot simply wait this out while margins erode.**

Those points are clear.

### Where we disagree
The union and, to a lesser extent, the regional state are setting red lines that may sound socially understandable but are financially relevant only if they still allow a credible margin recovery. If “no closures” and “no forced redundancies” become absolute vetoes, then the company may be left with an uncompetitive footprint. That would be unacceptable from our standpoint because it destroys risk-adjusted value and likely just postpones a bigger problem.

We also do not want a settlement that protects every existing structure while asking for only minor employee concessions. That would be a cosmetic compromise, and the market would treat it as such.

### What we would support
We would support **any** package that credibly delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- a clear reduction in overcapacity,
- measurable labour-cost relief,
- improved productivity and utilization,
- and certainty that the business will return to target margins.

That could include:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- targeted pay or bonus concessions,
- site-specific adjustments,
- and investment commitments tied to verified savings.

### What we would not support
We would not support:
- a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- prolonged industrial conflict that damages earnings and execution,
- or a “shared pain” narrative that sounds fair but does not materially improve returns.

### To management
You are right that delay is costly, but the market will also punish an agreement that lacks credibility. You need to show not just urgency, but arithmetic: how the restructuring closes the gap, by when, and with what confidence.

### To the union and works council
If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be economically equivalent, not just politically preferable. We are not interested in slogans about fairness. We are interested in whether the company can sustainably earn its cost of capital.

### Bottom line
Our support goes to the option that **most credibly restores competitiveness**. If that requires hard measures, we will accept them. If it can be achieved through a negotiated alternative with real and durable savings, we would support that as well. But an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired is not acceptable.
