We are aligned on the objective, not on the method.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, and the expert that:
- the current cost base is too high,
- temporary fixes are not enough,
- and the company needs **durable, structural savings** to restore margins and support future investment.

We also agree with labour and the state on one important point:
- a cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive,
- and prolonged industrial conflict would also destroy value.

So the question is not whether restructuring is needed. It is **which restructuring actually gets us back to competitive margins**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a hard red line
We do **not** accept plant closures as taboo.

If the plant-by-plant economics show that a site is structurally underutilized and that the fixed-cost burden cannot be removed otherwise, then closure is a legitimate option. Not pleasant, not preferred — but legitimate.

What we will not support is a deal that preserves uncompetitive capacity simply because closures are politically difficult.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not enough on its own
Voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, early retirement, and working-time changes are preferable. But if those measures do not deliver the necessary permanent savings, then ruling out harder adjustment in advance just weakens the restructuring.

Our standard is not “least painful.”  
Our standard is **risk-adjusted value creation**.

### 3) Labour’s contribution must be durable and meaningful
We are open to:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity measures,
- and capacity reduction.

But those measures must produce savings that are:
- material,
- durable,
- and sufficient to restore competitiveness.

If the package only buys a temporary peace while leaving the cost base structurally impaired, we would not support it.

## On the family and state
We hear the insistence on legitimacy, co-determination, and site protection. Those matter for execution risk.

But governance constraints do not change the economics.  
If the numbers show a deeper footprint reduction is required, then that reality has to be faced.

A veto can block a bad solution. It cannot make an uncompetitive structure viable.

## On management
We agree that management should not be forced into a cosmetic compromise.

But management also should not use closures as the default negotiating posture before the alternatives are properly quantified. Prove the gap. Show the residual after a deep hybrid. Then decide.

## What we need next
We support the requested plant-by-plant analysis, and we want it framed around one question:

> Which package most credibly restores risk-adjusted shareholder value through durable margin recovery?

That means the analysis should show, by site:
- fixed costs,
- utilization,
- direct labour cost per vehicle,
- overhead absorption,
- annual savings under closure, hybrid, and non-closure scenarios,
- one-time restructuring charges,
- execution risk,
- and savings durability after concessions expire.

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant operations finance and valuation analysis comparing closure-based, non-closure, and hybrid restructuring scenarios, with payback, durability, and execution-risk assessment.]

## Our bottom line
We will support:
- any package that **credibly restores competitive margins**,
- any package that delivers **durable, verifiable savings**,
- and any package that avoids a false peace at the expense of long-term value.

We will **not** support:
- cosmetic compromise,
- preservation of an uncompetitive footprint,
- or a settlement that leaves the company structurally impaired.

So:
- **Yes** to serious hybrid restructuring if it is economically real.
- **Yes** to non-closure options only if they are sufficient in substance.
- **Yes** to closures if the numbers show they are necessary.
- **No** to any outcome that fails the competitiveness test.
