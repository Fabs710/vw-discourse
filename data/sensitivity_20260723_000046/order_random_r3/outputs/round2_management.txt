## To the union
You keep repeating “no closures, no redundancies, no dismantling of the framework.” I understand why you say that, but I do **not** accept it as a workable basis for a restructuring.

The expert has now made the key point explicit:

- a **non-closure package can be credible only if it removes enough permanent cost and capacity**;
- temporary wage restraint alone is not enough;
- and if the underlying fixed-cost gap is large, then something more than labour concessions is required.

That is exactly why management cannot accept a package that simply preserves the current footprint and hopes for the best.

So let me be clear:
- **No, I am not asking for symbolic sacrifices.**
- **Yes, I am asking for structural change.**
- **No, I will not sign off on a deal that leaves the company unable to meet its cost target.**

If you want to avoid closures, then bring a package that is economically equivalent in substance:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- verifiable headcount reduction through attrition and redeployment,
- working-time and utilisation changes,
- bonus and wage moderation,
- and a real contribution to closing the gap.

If you cannot do that, then “no closures” is not a solution. It is just a veto.

## To the works council
You say closures and compulsory redundancies are not the only route. That is true in principle. But the expert also confirms that **not all non-closure packages are equal**. A credible alternative must remove real fixed cost and excess capacity, not merely rearrange the pain.

So I reject your framing that management is simply trying to “break protections.” That is not the issue. The issue is whether the German footprint is structurally affordable.

You ask for proof that closure is necessary before it is imposed. Fair enough. But the burden does not sit only with management to prove that every plant must close. The burden also sits with labour to prove that the same savings can be achieved without closures or involuntary measures.

So far, you have not shown that. You have asserted it.

## To the controlling family
I agree with you on one crucial point: **we need hard numbers, not slogans**. The expert has now laid out the relevant comparison:

- closure-based restructuring: highest durable savings, highest conflict cost;
- non-closure restructuring: lower conflict, but often insufficient unless very deep;
- hybrid restructuring: most realistic compromise, but only if it removes enough fixed cost.

That means we are not debating ideology. We are debating whether the package actually closes the gap.

Where I differ from you is this: I do **not** want this discussion to drift into an endless search for a politically comfortable answer. If the hybrid or non-closure route does not permanently fix the economics, then it is not enough. We cannot buy short-term peace at the expense of long-term competitiveness.

The expert has confirmed that:
