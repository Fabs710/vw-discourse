We recognise that the company is under real pressure, and we are prepared to shoulder our share of the burden. But we will not accept a restructuring that dumps the entire crisis onto the workforce.

Our position is clear:

- No compulsory redundancies.
- No plant closures.
- No dismantling of the collective wage framework.
- Any savings must be shared fairly between management, owners, and employees.

We are open to a negotiated package that can secure the sites and jobs while improving competitiveness, for example through:

- capacity reductions and more efficient utilisation,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary or limited wage concessions,
- the waiver of bonuses or other collective financial contributions,
- and binding commitments on future investment and product allocation.

But if the proposal is essentially “workers pay, workers leave, plants close,” then we reject it outright. That is not restructuring; that is one-sided social and industrial destruction.

We are ready for a serious settlement, but it must be durable and fair:
- protect the core German sites,
- protect the workforce from forced layoffs,
- and ensure management and owners also contribute materially.

If that is not on the table, then industrial action remains an available and legitimate means for us.
