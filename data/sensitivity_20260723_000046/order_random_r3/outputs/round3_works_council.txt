I will be just as direct.

We are prepared to engage with the economics. We are not prepared to accept a restructuring logic that treats plant closures and forced redundancies as normal tools and then asks the workforce to “understand” the outcome.

## Where I agree

I agree on four points:

1. **The company needs a durable solution.**  
   No one benefits from a short-lived patch.

2. **There is likely a real cost gap.**  
   I do not deny that competitiveness has to improve.

3. **Temporary wage restraint alone is not enough.**  
   If labour contributes, it must be part of a broader package.

4. **Any viable package must be verified plant by plant.**  
   We need hard numbers, not rhetoric.

So yes: let’s test the economics properly.

## Where I disagree

### 1) Closures are not the default answer
Management, investors, and even parts of the ownership side keep talking as if closures are the natural benchmark and everything else is second-best.

I reject that framing.

The expert did **not** say closures are inevitable. The expert said:
- closure-based restructuring can yield the highest durable savings,
- but non-closure and hybrid packages can still be credible if they are deep enough.

That is a crucial distinction. Credible alternatives exist, and management has not proven they are exhausted.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies remain unacceptable
This is not negotiable for us.

Voluntary exits, early retirement, redeployment, training, working-time changes, and internal flexibility are one thing.  
Forcing people out is another. That would break trust, destroy the social contract, and invite conflict across every site.

So when management and investors say “hard measures may be necessary,” we hear a threat to the job-security framework. That is exactly what we will resist.

### 3) “Shared burden” cannot mean workers carry the main burden
I hear the calls for employee contribution. Fine. But then I want visible contributions from management and ownership too:
- overhead cuts,
- restraint on management spending and payouts,
- procurement and complexity reduction,
- investment commitments for German sites,
- and binding commitments that the workforce is not the only shock absorber.

If only labour pays, then this is not a restructuring. It is social dumping with a corporate label.

The expert’s analysis does not give you a blank cheque. It gives you a choice: prove that non-closure or hybrid restructuring cannot work, or accept a site-preserving solution with real burden-sharing.

We want you to insist on:

Not just “no closures in principle,” but active protection of jobs and industrial capability.
