Core impasse, as it now stands:

1. **Economics vs. red lines**
   - Management, investors, and the family insist that the restructuring must remove enough permanent cost to restore competitiveness.
   - The union and works council insist that closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be the starting point, and ideally not part of the outcome.
   - The state wants site protection and legitimacy, but also a credible solution.
   - Result: the sides disagree on **which tools are legitimate before the numbers are fully settled**.

2. **Burden-sharing**
   - Labour is prepared to contribute, but only if management and ownership visibly share the burden.
   - Management and investors want material, durable savings, but labour sees a risk of one-sided adjustment.
   - Result: no shared answer yet on **who gives how much, and in what form**.

3. **Proof standard**
   - Nearly everyone now agrees on a plant-by-plant comparison.
   - But they disagree on the baseline test:  
     - Is non-closure/hybrid the default unless disproven?  
     - Or are closures a legitimate option unless a non-closure plan proves equivalent?
   - Result: the parties are not yet aligned on **what evidence would settle the question**.

## Concrete compromise positions to bridge the divide

### Compromise 1: “Hybrid-first, closure-last”
- Adopt a formal sequencing rule:
  1. test a deep hybrid package,
  2. then a non-closure package,
  3. only then consider closures for any residual gap.
- In exchange, labour accepts meaningful capacity reduction, voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, and temporary wage restraint.
- Management and investors get a commitment that closure is not excluded forever, only deferred until the alternatives are tested.

### Compromise 2: “No compulsory redundancies, but real capacity removal”
- Agree that forced layoffs are off the table.
- In return, labour accepts:
  - higher voluntary exit targets,
  - redeployment,
  - internal transfers,
  - line consolidation,
  - and binding productivity measures.
- Management accepts that if headcount reductions are needed, they must be achieved through negotiated means, not compulsion.

### Compromise 3: “Burden-sharing pact with triggers”
- Create a package where each side contributes visibly:
  - labour: temporary wage moderation / bonus restraint,
  - management: overhead reduction and governance restraint,
  - ownership: investment commitment and payout restraint.
- Add pre-agreed triggers:
  - if savings fall short by a defined date, the parties reopen the question of deeper footprint changes.
- This gives labour protection against immediate closures, while giving capital a backstop if the package underdelivers.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What specific annual savings target must be met at each site for you to accept a no-closure or hybrid package as sufficient, and what residual gap would still trigger closure review?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What quantified permanent savings would you commit to delivering through wage moderation, voluntary exits, and flexibility, and what measures would you accept if those savings do not fully close the gap?**

### To the Works Council
**Which concrete site-by-site changes would you accept as genuine capacity reduction without closure, and what evidence would convince you that the non-closure route has been exhausted?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you commit publicly to a hybrid-first sequence and define the financial threshold at which you would authorize closure review, rather than leaving closure as an implicit threat?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What level of durable margin improvement would make a hybrid package acceptable to you, even if it preserves some underutilized capacity in the near term?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What enforceable investment, employment, or governance conditions would you require to support a site-preserving restructuring, and what would you do if the numbers show a limited but not complete gap closure?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum data set must be disclosed at plant level for the parties to distinguish a credible hybrid from a cosmetic compromise?**
