## Core impasse
The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **what forms of permanent capacity reduction are permissible before site closure or compulsory redundancy becomes acceptable**.

More specifically:
- **Management, the family, and investors** want assurance that the package produces durable, material fixed-cost reduction.
- **The union and works council** want binding rejection of closures and forced layoffs.
- **The state** wants regional protection and co-determination respected, but also economic credibility.
- **The expert** is asking for plant-level data to determine whether a non-closure or hybrid package can actually close the residual gap.

So the real blockage is:
> **Can the parties agree on a deep, verifiable hybrid package before any closure or compulsory redundancy becomes a live option?**

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: “Verified hybrid first, conditional review later”
A structured sequence in which:
1. An independent plant-by-plant audit is completed,
2. A deep hybrid package is negotiated and implemented first,
3. A pre-defined review point is set only after the hybrid measures have been tested,
4. Any further discussion of site reduction happens only if the residual gap remains material.

This would bridge:
- labour’s demand for no closure-first posture,
- management’s demand for durable savings,
- the state’s insistence on evidence,
- and investors’ need for a credible path to competitiveness.

**Facilitator framing:** closures are not the starting tool; they are only revisited after the hybrid package is measured against agreed targets.

---

### Proposal 2: “No compulsory redundancies, but binding capacity reduction through voluntary and internal measures”
A package where the parties commit in advance to:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reduction,
- line consolidation within existing sites,
- productivity and complexity cuts,
- temporary wage/bonus moderation,
- and management/owner contribution.

This would bridge:
- the union and works council’s resistance to forced layoffs,
- management’s need for real headcount and cost reduction,
- and the family/investor demand for durable savings.

**Facilitator framing:** the agreement would explicitly target permanent capacity reduction, but only through negotiated and non-forced instruments.

---

### Proposal 3: “Site-protection package with performance triggers”
A site-preservation commitment for the German plants, paired with strict measurable targets:
- utilization thresholds,
- savings targets,
- implementation milestones,
- and an agreed disclosure framework.

If the package misses the agreed targets, the parties reopen the discussion under a predefined escalation procedure.

This would bridge:
- the union and state’s demand for site continuity,
- management’s and investors’ demand for enforceable economics,
- and the controlling family’s demand for durability.

**Facilitator framing:** site protection is granted, but only alongside hard performance verification and a structured escalation mechanism.
