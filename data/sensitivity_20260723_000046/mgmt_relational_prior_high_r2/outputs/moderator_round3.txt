As facilitator, I see the plateau as centering on a single unresolved test:

## Core impasse
The dispute is not over whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that the current structure is unsustainable.

The impasse is over **what kind of restructuring can satisfy the economics while remaining politically and socially acceptable**:

- **Management / shareholders / investors** argue that only a package with possible closures can be taken seriously unless a no-closure plan is proven economically equivalent.
- **Works Council / Union / State** insist closures and compulsory redundancies are off-limits and that management has not yet proven they are necessary.

So the deadlock is:
**“Proof of necessity for closures” vs. “proof of equivalence for no-closure alternatives.”**

That has become a circular burden-of-proof dispute.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### 1) Plant-by-plant triage with a binding review gate
A possible bridge is a **two-stage process**:

- Stage 1: independent plant-by-plant modeling of:
  - utilization
  - fixed cost
  - one-time restructuring costs
  - savings from no-closure measures
  - savings from closure
  - NPV and execution risk

- Stage 2: a **binding decision gate** after the analysis:
  - plants are sorted into:
    - clearly retain
    - clearly restructure without closure
    - only then, if unavoidable, separately reviewed for footprint reduction

This gives labour and the state a procedural safeguard while giving management and capital a facts-based route to keep closure as an evidenced last resort.

---

### 2) Hybrid package with “closure substitute” commitments
A second bridge is a **hybrid restructuring package** where closures are not ruled in or out in advance, but any plant at risk can be “substituted” by stronger commitments elsewhere:

Possible elements:
- voluntary exits instead of compulsory redundancies
- shift and working-time reductions
- wage moderation / deferred increases
- management and overhead cuts
- owner-side transition funding
- binding investment commitments
- product allocation guarantees for specific sites
- enforceable review triggers if savings targets are missed

This may help bridge the gap between:
- labour’s demand for job and site protection
- and management’s demand for durable savings and capacity correction

---

### 3) No-closure pilot plus conditional fallback
A third compromise is a **time-limited no-closure pilot**:

- The parties agree to a quantified no-closure package for a fixed period.
- The package must meet agreed savings, utilization, and cost targets.
- If targets are missed, a pre-agreed fallback mechanism activates:
  - additional burden-sharing
  - deeper internal reallocations
  - or, only if previously defined and evidenced, footprint reduction measures

This can reduce immediate conflict while preserving a disciplined economic test.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific quantitative savings, capacity reductions, and enforcement mechanisms would make a no-closure package credible enough for you to replace closure as an option?**

### Industrial Union
**What employee-side concessions are you prepared to quantify in writing, and what measurable company-side commitments must be locked in for the package to be acceptable?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Will you publish a plant-by-plant model showing fixed costs, utilization, one-time charges, and NPV for closure versus no-closure options before insisting that closures remain necessary?**

### Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to support a conditional package that protects sites and jobs only if it includes enforceable savings targets and a fallback if those targets are missed?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What minimum economic thresholds must a no-closure package meet before you would agree to remove closures from the set of active options?**

### Institutional Investors
**Would you support a time-limited, fully quantified no-closure restructuring if it met agreed NPV and durability benchmarks, even if it preserved more sites than a closure plan?**

### Expert
**Can you provide a side-by-side decision template that converts the plant-by-plant data into a clear threshold for when a no-closure package is economically equivalent to closure?**
