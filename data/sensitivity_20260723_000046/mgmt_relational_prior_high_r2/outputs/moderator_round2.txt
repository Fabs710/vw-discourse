### 1) What has been agreed
- The company is **not sustainable in its current form** and needs restructuring.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must include:
  - a **credible industrial plan**
  - **investment in future products/technologies**
  - **durable cost reduction**
  - avoidance of a prolonged conflict if possible
- Most parties accept that **temporary or partial measures alone may not be enough** if the structural gap is large.
- There is shared recognition that **numbers and quantified comparisons** are needed, not just general claims.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Labour, the works council, and the state government reject German plant closures.  
  - Management, the family holding, and investors keep closures on the table as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour and the state oppose them; management and shareholders have not ruled out hard measures if required.
- **Scope of guarantees/co-determination:**  
  - Labour and the state want binding site and job security plus strong co-determination.  
  - Management and investors argue these cannot become a veto on restructuring.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  - Labour wants management, owners, and capital to contribute materially.  
  - Shareholders want a credible employee contribution, but not a package that leaves the company structurally weak.
- **Whether alternatives can match closures:**  
  - The main unresolved dispute is whether non-closure measures can deliver **equivalent, durable savings**.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. footprint preservation:** how much cost reduction is possible without closing sites?
- **Speed vs. social impact:** how quickly savings must be achieved versus how much disruption is acceptable.
- **Durability vs. reversibility:** whether working-time, wage, and voluntary measures create lasting restructuring or only delay it.
- **Investment capacity vs. restructuring charges:** how to fund future products while absorbing one-off restructuring costs.
- **Governance vs. flexibility:** how co-determination and state influence will operate without blocking necessary change.
- **Industrial peace vs. hard restructuring:** whether a tougher compromise can still avoid strikes and value destruction.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **side-by-side quantified options**.
- Ask management to present:
  - plant-by-plant cost and utilization data
  - savings from closure vs. non-closure scenarios
  - one-time charges and timing
  - impact on margins and investment capacity
- Ask labour and the state to present:
  - a fully quantified **no-closure alternative**
  - the exact employee contributions they are prepared to make
  - how the package achieves **durable** savings and capacity reduction
- Keep the next round focused on one question: **Can a no-closure package match the economic effect of closures, with comparable durability and execution certainty?**
