I will be equally direct.

### My position remains clear
The regional state government **will not support plant closures in Germany, mass layoffs, or any restructuring that weakens our co-determination and veto role**. Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are red lines.

We understand the business pressure. We do not deny the company faces a serious competitiveness problem. But there is a difference between a necessary restructuring and a destruction of the domestic industrial base. We will not vote for the latter.

## Where I agree
### With management
You are right that the status quo is not sustainable. The cost base is too high, the market is tough, and investment in future products is essential. I also agree that the company needs a credible industrial plan, not symbolic gestures.

### With labour
You are right that a one-sided solution is unacceptable. Workers did not create this situation and should not be made the sole adjustment variable. I also agree that alternatives to closures must be tested seriously.

### With the family holding and investors
You are right that durable competitiveness matters and that prolonging uncertainty destroys value. A fake compromise that leaves the company structurally weak is not a solution.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You still have not proven that closures are unavoidable. Repeating “the numbers justify it” is not enough. If closures are your preferred instrument, you need to demonstrate why every less destructive option fails on the same savings and durability standard.

Right now, I see a tendency to move too quickly to the harshest tool. That is exactly what we will resist.

### To the family holding
“all options on the table” may sound prudent in a boardroom, but in practice it means keeping closure threats alive while asking the state and workforce to absorb the political and social damage. We do not accept that as balanced stewardship.

### To investors
You are right to care about margins and certainty. But certainty is not created by detonating labour peace and regional legitimacy. A settlement that triggers prolonged strikes, political backlash, and damage to the company’s standing in Germany is not value-preserving.

### To labour
I support your insistence on site protection, but I also want to be precise: vague phrases like “substantial core employment” are not enough if they can be used to hollow out plants by attrition. If we are to defend jobs, then we need enforceable commitments, not soft assurances.

## What I can support
I am prepared to work for a package built on:
- **capacity reductions without plant closures**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **voluntary instruments rather than compulsory redundancies**
- **redeployment and retraining**
- **measured and fair employee contributions**
- **management and owner-side contributions as well**
- **binding investment commitments for future products and technologies**

That is the kind of restructuring that can restore competitiveness without sacrificing the company’s role as a regional anchor.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
1. plant closures in Germany,
2. compulsory redundancies as the default tool,
3. weakening co-determination,
4. or a restructuring that treats the state’s role as an obstacle rather than a legitimate part of governance.

## The real issue
This is not a question of whether the company must change. It must.  
The question is **who pays, how much, and whether the company remains rooted in Germany afterward**.

My answer is: the burden must be shared, but it must not be shifted onto the workforce and the regions through closures and layoffs.

## What would move me
A credible alternative would need to show, in concrete terms:
- how much cost is removed,
- how capacity is reduced without site closures,
- how jobs are protected,
- how investment is secured,
- and how the company reaches competitiveness without breaking the social partnership model.

If management and owners want our support, bring a plan that preserves sites and employment while restoring competitiveness. If the only plan is closure, then we will oppose it.

### Bottom line
We are ready to broker a tough but fair restructuring.  
We are **not** ready to endorse an industrial retreat from Germany.
