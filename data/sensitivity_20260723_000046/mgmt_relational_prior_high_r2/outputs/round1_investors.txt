From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the key test is simple: **does the final package credibly restore competitive margins and protect long-term enterprise value?** Everything else is secondary.

### Where we agree
We agree with management on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is a real issue,
- the brand is missing its return-on-sales target,
- and cosmetic measures will not be enough.

We also agree with the other stakeholders on one important point:
- **a one-sided solution imposed entirely on the workforce is a bad outcome** if it creates prolonged conflict, execution risk, and weakens the company’s future operating model.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that:
- leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- preserves excess capacity simply to avoid hard decisions,
- or replaces real restructuring with a symbolic compromise.

We are not interested in preserving every plant, every legacy guarantee, or every historical arrangement if that means the business continues to underperform. That would be value-destructive.

Likewise, we do **not** support indefinite industrial conflict. Strikes, uncertainty, and governance deadlock all destroy value quickly. If the process drifts into prolonged confrontation without a credible end-state, capital will reprice the business accordingly.

### Our position
We are open to **either** of the following, but only if the numbers are real:

1. **Deep structural cuts**, including closures if they are truly necessary to restore competitiveness; or  
2. **A negotiated alternative** that delivers equivalent, durable savings without closures, and with a clear implementation path.

What we will not back is a “middle way” that sounds balanced but delivers insufficient savings.

### What would move us
To gain our support, the final package must show:
- **quantified, durable cost reductions**,
- **a credible path back to target margins**,
- **execution certainty**,
- and **limited risk of recurring labour conflict**.

If the union and works council want to avoid closures, they need to produce an alternative that is not just politically acceptable but financially equivalent. That means real, measurable, lasting savings — not temporary deferrals.

### On the competing positions
- **Works council / union:** We understand your red lines, but red lines do not change the economics. If the proposal does not solve overcapacity and labour-cost disadvantage, it is not acceptable.
- **State government:** We respect the importance of the regional base, but regional interests cannot override enterprise value indefinitely.
- **Family owners:** We agree that value erosion must stop. But “all options on the table” only helps if the outcome is disciplined, not captured by politics or delay.
- **Management:** You need to show that the restructuring is not just aggressive, but credible and implementable. If you cannot deliver savings without a drawn-out confrontation, that is a management failure too.

### Bottom line
We will support the option that **most credibly restores competitiveness and margins**.  
We will **not** support a cosmetic settlement, and we will **not** support a plan that leaves the company structurally weak.

If management can deliver a firm, durable restructuring with a believable savings profile, we are prepared to back it. If the alternative is a diluted compromise that preserves the current cost problem, we would oppose it through capital allocation and market scrutiny.
