## 3) Core tests

### Test 1: Present value equivalence
A no-closure package is only economically equivalent if:

**NPV(no-closure savings) ≥ NPV(closure savings) – incremental risk penalty**

Where the risk penalty reflects:
- lower certainty
- slower implementation
- durability risk
- higher reinvestment needs

### Test 2: Fixed-cost reduction sufficiency
The package must reduce the **structural fixed-cost base**, not merely defer it.

A good rule is:
- if savings are mostly temporary labor concessions, the package is weak
- if savings permanently remove headcount, overhead, and capacity cost, it is stronger

### Test 3: Capacity correction
If the company has genuine overcapacity, then the solution must show:
- lower installed capacity, or
- materially lower effective capacity through shifts and product allocation

If capacity stays unchanged, the business may still be oversized even if wage costs fall.

### Test 4: Investment capacity
The solution must preserve enough cash for:
- EV / platform investment
- battery / software transition
- tooling and model launches

A “cheap” restructuring that starves investment is not value-creating.

## 4) Practical scoring framework
For each plant, score each scenario 1–5 on:

- **Savings magnitude**
- **Savings durability**
- **Speed**
- **Execution certainty**
- **Strategic importance of site**
- **Supply-chain disruption**
- **Reinvestment burden**
- **Labor conflict risk**

Then weight them, for example:
- 30% economics
- 20% durability
- 20% execution certainty
- 15% strategic / network value
- 15% investment capacity

A plant with:
- low utilization,
- high fixed cost,
- weak strategic role,
- and high replacement savings
is a likely closure candidate unless a no-closure package produces near-equal NPV.

A plant with:
- high strategic importance,
- high utilization potential,
- and lower fixed cost
is more suitable for internal restructuring.

---

## 5) Decision thresholds
A useful operating threshold:

### No-closure package is credible if:
- it captures at least **85–100% of closure NPV** on a risk-adjusted basis,
- most savings are structural,
- implementation can be completed within an acceptable period,
- and the package does not require repeated renegotiation.

### Hybrid package is preferred if:
- no-closure comes close but not fully,
- some footprint correction is needed,
- internal measures cover part of the gap,
- and closures can be limited to the weakest sites or lines.

### Closure is economically hard to avoid if:
- the gap is large,
- savings from internal measures are too small,
- the site has chronically poor utilization,
- and the strategic case for retention is weak.

## 7) Bottom line
The right question is not “closure or no closure?”  
It is:

**Which package delivers the greatest durable reduction in structural cost, at the lowest risk-adjusted NPV cost, while preserving the needed investment capacity?**
