I will be just as direct.

## My position
A restructuring that depends on **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or a weakening of the collective bargaining framework** is still **not acceptable**.

If the company wants a durable solution, then it must be built on:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding job and site security**
- **fair burden-sharing**
- **a real employee contribution**
- **management, owner, and capital-side concessions as well**

That remains our line.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, the state, and the expert on one important point:  
**the status quo is not sustainable.**

I also agree that a vague “everything stays as it is” solution will not work. The company needs:
- lower cost,
- better capacity alignment,
- investment in future products,
- and a plan that can actually be implemented.

So yes: we are not defending inaction. We are defending a restructuring that does not destroy jobs and industrial trust.

I also accept the expert’s point that the right test is not slogans, but **present value of savings, durability, and execution risk**. Fine. Let’s use that standard.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You still have not proven that closures are necessary.

You keep saying the gap is structural, which may be true. But “structural gap” does not automatically mean “close plants.” It means the savings case must be tested honestly across all instruments.

You have not shown that the following cannot deliver the needed effect:
- working-time flexibility,
- wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- internal volume shifts,
- overhead and management cost cuts,
- and binding investment and product allocation decisions.

Until you do, closures are not a fact. They are a choice.

And let me be clear: I do **not** accept the idea that employees should carry the main burden while management preserves its own flexibility and owners preserve theirs.

### To the works council
I support your red lines. No closures, no forced redundancies, no hollowing out by stealth. That is the correct line.

But we also have to be disciplined in the negotiation. If we want to hold that line, we need a **credible no-closure package** that can withstand the numbers test the expert described. That means we should not just say “no” — we should specify what we are prepared to contribute if the other side also contributes.

## What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to negotiate a **quantified, enforceable alternative package** based on shared sacrifice, including:
- **temporary wage moderation or deferred increases**
- **suspension or reduction of bonuses**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **voluntary exit instruments**
- **redeployment and retraining**
- **capacity reduction through shifts and volume changes**
- **management overhead and compensation restraint**
- **owner-side support for transition funding**
- **binding investment commitments for future models and technologies**

That is a real contribution from labour. But it is only acceptable if it is matched by real contributions from management, owners, and capital.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
1. a package that leaves the workforce paying for everything,
2. a “temporary” compromise that just postpones layoffs,
3. vague promises about site security without enforceability,
4. or a restructuring that turns co-determination into a rubber stamp.

The expert said the key question is whether a no-closure package can match closures on savings and durability. Good. Then prove it. But do not pretend closures are justified before that test is done.

## To the state government
I appreciate your clear support for site protection and your insistence on binding safeguards.

But I need to be equally clear: we will not accept “preserve a substantial core” as a substitute for real guarantees. That phrase can be used to hollow out plants over time. We need:
- binding site commitments,
- binding volume commitments where possible,
- and strong oversight of how the restructuring is implemented.

If the state wants to use its influence responsibly, it should insist on a negotiated solution that preserves industrial capacity and avoids forced job destruction.

## To the family holding
You keep saying stewardship means hard choices. That is true. But stewardship also means not shifting the full burden onto workers while keeping capital’s own position protected.

If you want labour peace, then support a settlement that includes:
- employee contributions,
- but also owner and management contributions,
- and a real industrial future for the German sites.

What I do **not** accept is “equivalent savings” as a slogan used to justify closures while refusing to engage on your own side’s contribution.

## To investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is useless. We agree on that.

Where we disagree is that you treat closures as automatically the most credible answer. They are not automatically credible if they provoke strikes, destroy trust, and damage the company’s long-term industrial base.

If you want durable value, then support a package that the workforce can actually stand behind. That means a fair burden-sharing deal, not a value-destroying showdown.

## On the expert analysis
The expert’s assessment does not force one conclusion. It says:
- if the gap is large, footprint reduction becomes more attractive,
- if the gap is moderate, a negotiated no-closure package may work,
- and the real test is whether alternatives are **equivalent in savings and durability**.

That is exactly why we say: show us the numbers. But show them honestly, plant by plant, instrument by instrument.

## What would move us
A serious package would need to include:
1. **binding guarantees that all German plants remain open**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **a real employee contribution**
4. **management and owner-side concessions**
5. **investment commitments**
6. **durable capacity reduction without closures**
7. **a restored collective bargaining framework**

That is the basis for an agreement.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to contribute to saving the company.  
We are **not** prepared to finance restructuring alone.  
And we are **not** prepared to trade away jobs, site security, and collective standards for a “discipline” narrative that leaves management and owners largely untouched.

If the company wants our consent, it must bring a quantified, enforceable no-closure alternative with shared sacrifice.  
If it cannot do that, then it should not expect the union to make concessions that amount to surrender.
