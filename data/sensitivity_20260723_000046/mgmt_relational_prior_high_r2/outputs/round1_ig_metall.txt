I agree with the core line from the works council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**.

Management is treating the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort. That is not acceptable. The crisis did not arise because employees failed to do their part; they have already carried productivity pressure, transformation, and years of uncertainty. Any restructuring that simply shifts the pain onto workers while protecting owners, finance, and management credibility will be resisted.

Where I do agree is that the company needs change. We are not denying the competitive pressure, the weak European market, or the need for cost reductions. But the answer must be **shared sacrifice with binding safeguards**, not closures and forced layoffs. That means, in practical terms:

- capacity reductions or shifts in production volumes, not plant shutdowns;
- working-time and flexibility measures negotiated with the workforce;
- voluntary instruments rather than compulsory redundancies;
- a meaningful employee contribution, but only as part of a balanced package;
- management, owners, and capital also taking a real hit;
- and a credible industrial plan with future products, investment, and site commitments.

What I will not support is a package where workers pay through wage cuts, guarantee removal, and job losses while management calls that “competitiveness.” That is one-sided and destabilising. It would invite escalation, not settlement.

If there is to be a deal, it must include:
1. **binding job and site security**
2. **no forced layoffs**
3. **no plant closures**
4. **fair burden-sharing across all stakeholder groups**
5. **a durable collective agreement that restores peace**

We are prepared to bargain hard and, if necessary, continue industrial action to defend these principles. But we are also ready to negotiate a serious alternative if management comes with a proposal that protects jobs and the German industrial base rather than liquidating it.
