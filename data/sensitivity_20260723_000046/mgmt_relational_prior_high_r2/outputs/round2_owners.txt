I appreciate the clarity from all sides, but I need to be equally clear: **a restructuring that protects every site, rules out compulsory redundancies, and preserves all legacy guarantees regardless of economics is not credible stewardship**. If that is the end state, then the company remains structurally weak, margins stay under pressure, and long-term value continues to erode. That is unacceptable to us.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and even labour on one central point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company needs:
- a durable reduction in cost,
- a credible industrial plan,
- investment in future products and technologies,
- and a solution that avoids prolonged conflict.

I also agree that a one-sided solution would be a mistake. If all the burden lands on the workforce, the package will be unstable, contested, and ultimately value-destructive.

### Where I disagree
#### To the Works Council and Union
Your red lines are understandable politically, but they do not answer the economic problem. Saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no guarantee changes” is not a restructuring answer; it is an attempt to pre-empt the necessary trade-offs.

I do **not** accept that all German plants must remain untouched. I do **not** accept that plant closures are ruled out before alternatives are tested against the same savings and durability standard. And I do **not** accept that the workforce can veto all hard measures while still expecting the company to become competitive.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring a package that is **mathematically equivalent** in savings and durability. Not vaguely “fair,” not politically convenient — equivalent.

#### To the State Government
I respect the regional responsibility, but regional protection cannot become a blanket veto on industrial adaptation. If the outcome preserves every footprint regardless of economics, then we are simply freezing decline. That is not a responsible use of governance rights.

I also agree with you that co-determination matters. But co-determination is there to force negotiation, not to make the enterprise ungovernable.

#### To Management
I support your diagnosis, but support is not a blank cheque. If you want the shareholder side behind a tough restructuring, you need to demonstrate:
- what savings are required,
- what savings each instrument delivers,
- why the proposed mix is the least destructive option,
- and how you avoid a prolonged conflict that destroys value.

You have not yet made the case that closures are the only answer. If that is your position, it still needs proof.

### My position
The family holding will support a **serious structural restructuring**, but not a cosmetic compromise and not a politically frozen outcome.

That means:
1. **All options remain on the table**, including closures if they are genuinely necessary.
2. **Alternatives must be fully tested**, but they must be tested against hard numbers, not hopes.
3. **There must be a real employee contribution**.
4. **Management, owners, and capital also have to bear responsibility**.
5. **The final package must restore competitiveness and margins durably**.

### What would move us
We would be prepared to support a negotiated alternative **if** it can show, concretely:
- comparable and durable savings,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- clear implementation certainty,
- and a credible path back to target profitability.

If such a package exists, then good — it should be preferred over unnecessary disruption. But if it does not, then we should stop pretending that the company can be made competitive without hard industrial change.

### What we cannot accept
We cannot accept:
- an indefinite defence of every plant,
- a settlement that preserves structural overcapacity,
- a promise that no one will be forced to adjust no matter what the economics require,
- or a solution that protects short-term political comfort while weakening the enterprise for years.

### Bottom line
We want a durable, disciplined restructuring that restores the company’s competitiveness **and** preserves labour peace where possible. But labour peace cannot be bought by sacrificing the company’s future.

So my message is simple:
- Bring us a credible, quantified alternative if you want to avoid closures.
- Otherwise, do not expect shareholder support for a diluted compromise that leaves the business uncompetitive.
