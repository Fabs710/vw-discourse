I appreciate that management is being explicit about the scale of the problem, and I do not dispute that the company needs a durable improvement in cost and competitiveness. I also agree with the union and works council on one central point: a one-sided solution that puts the full adjustment burden on the workforce is neither fair nor sustainable.

But I need to be equally clear: **the regional state government will not support a restructuring that closes German plants, destroys regional employment, or sidelines our co-determination and veto role.** Those are not negotiable from our side.

### Where I agree
- The company needs a credible industrial plan.
- The current cost base has to come down.
- Investment in future products and technology is essential.
- A negotiated solution is far better than uncontrolled escalation.
- Some form of workforce contribution may be necessary if it is genuinely part of a balanced package.

### Where I disagree
- I do **not** accept plant closures as the default answer.
- I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the primary adjustment tool.
- I do **not** accept weakening or bypassing the co-determination structure that has long underpinned this company’s stability.
- I do **not** accept a restructuring that treats the German industrial base as disposable.

This company is not only a balance-sheet case; it is a major regional economic anchor. Decisions taken here have long-term consequences for suppliers, local labour markets, municipal finances, and social cohesion. If we hollow out the domestic base now, we may gain short-term relief at the cost of long-term weakness, political backlash, and a damaged ability to innovate and produce in Germany.

### What I can support
I am open to a package built around:
- capacity reductions without plant closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- internal redeployment and retraining,
- measured labour-cost contributions,
- and binding investment commitments in return.

But that requires management to show that the savings are real and durable **without** crossing the red line of closures and mass job destruction. If management says only closures can do the job, then I would question whether every less destructive option has actually been exhausted or whether the burden is being shifted too quickly onto the workforce and the regions.

### My bottom line
If there is a restructuring path that:
1. preserves German sites,
2. avoids compulsory redundancies,
3. protects a substantial core of employment,
4. keeps co-determination intact,
5. and restores competitiveness through a negotiated industrial plan,

then the state government is ready to engage constructively and use its role to help broker that outcome.

If the proposal instead relies on closures and a deep cut in regional employment, then I must say plainly: **that is not acceptable to us, and we will use our rights accordingly.**
