I will be equally direct in round 2:

**Our red lines remain unchanged:**  
- **No German plant closures**  
- **No compulsory redundancies**  
- **No breaking of the existing job-security framework**  
- **No restructuring that sidelines co-determination**

Management, the family holding, and investors keep repeating “all options on the table” as if that were a neutral statement. It is not. In practice it means keeping the threat of closures and layoffs alive while asking the workforce to accept the damage in advance. We reject that logic.

### Where I agree
I do agree on one point with almost everyone here: **the company needs a credible industrial plan**. We are not defending the status quo as a business model. We know the market is under pressure, the cost base is high, and investment in future products is essential. If there is a serious plan for competitiveness, we will engage on it.

But a serious plan does **not** start with plant closures and forced layoffs. It starts with:
- capacity adjustments through internal allocation,
- working-time flexibility,
- retraining and redeployment,
- voluntary instruments,
- real investment in product and technology,
- and a fair contribution from management, owners, and capital.

### Where I disagree
#### To management:
You have still not shown that closures are necessary rather than chosen because they are the easiest blunt instrument. Saying savings are needed is not enough. You must prove that you have exhausted alternatives that preserve sites and jobs. Until then, closure is not a solution; it is a threat.

#### To the family holding:
Saying “all options should remain on the table” sounds balanced, but it ignores the legal and moral reality of co-determination and the workforce’s contribution to this company. We are not an adjustable cost item. If long-term stewardship matters, then long-term employment stability matters too. Do not hide behind abstract value arguments to justify tearing up historic protections.

#### To investors:
You are focused almost exclusively on margin restoration and execution certainty. But a package that destroys trust, triggers prolonged strikes, and tears apart the domestic industrial base is not “certainty.” It is short-term accounting at the expense of long-term capability. If you want durable value, then durable labour peace and stable sites are part of that value.

#### To the state government:
I appreciate your support for site protection, but I need to be blunt: “preserve a substantial core of employment” is too vague if management uses it to justify hollowing out whole plants through gradual attrition. We need binding protections, not soft language.

### On savings and numbers
If the question is whether alternatives can deliver real savings: yes, they can — but only if all parties stop pretending workers alone must pay.

There are several routes to substantial savings without closures:
- **capacity reduction through volume management and internal relocation**
- **temporary working-time reductions or flexible models**
- **voluntary exits only, with no forced redundancies**
- **redeployment across sites and functions**
- **training and qualification to shift production into future platforms**
- **renegotiated internal cost structures, including management and overhead contributions**
- **owner-side support for the transition instead of dividend-first thinking**

If management claims this is not enough, then bring the actual numbers and show us why. Do not ask us to accept plant closures as a reflex.

### What would move us
Let me be precise: the only thing that could change our position is a package that provides:
1. **binding guarantees that all German plants remain open through 2030**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **enforceable site and job security**
4. **real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
5. **investment commitments for future products and technologies**
6. **fair burden-sharing from management, owners, and capital**

Without those, there is no basis for agreement.

### To be clear on leverage
We are not coming here as petitioners. Co-determination gives labour real blocking power, and we will use it. The workforce is already mobilised. If management tries to force a fait accompli, it will face resistance across the German sites.

So my message is simple:

- **Bring a restructuring plan that preserves plants and jobs, or do not expect our consent.**
- **Bring quantified alternatives, not vague assertions that closures are unavoidable.**
- **Bring binding guarantees, not promises that can be withdrawn once the pressure eases.**

If you cannot do that, then the responsibility for escalation will not lie with the workforce.
