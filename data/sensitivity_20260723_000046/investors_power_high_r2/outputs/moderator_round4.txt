As facilitator, I would summarize the plateau as follows:

## Core impasse
The conflict has narrowed to one central issue:

**Whether a credible restructuring can be achieved without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and if so, what level of permanent cost and capacity reduction is required to make that alternative economically acceptable.**

More specifically, the sides are split on three linked points:

1. **Management / investors / family holding**
   - want a restructuring that is economically sufficient and durable;
   - treat closures as a legitimate benchmark or fallback if no alternative matches the savings.

2. **Works council / union / state government**
   - reject closures and forced redundancies as starting instruments;
   - require job-security and co-determination protections to remain intact;
   - insist that management, owners, and investors contribute more visibly.

3. **The unresolved technical question**
   - whether the no-closure route can be quantified as truly equivalent in recurring savings, capacity reduction, and 3–5 year margin impact.

So the deadlock is not over the need for change; it is over **the acceptable instruments and the burden of proof**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure test with independent verification
A structured compromise could be:

- a **binding no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy trial period** for a defined term, e.g. 12–18 months;
- an **independent plant-by-plant audit** with agreed assumptions;
- **mandatory milestones** on savings, utilization, and capacity reduction;
- if milestones are missed, the parties do **not** jump automatically to closures, but reconvene on a predefined escalation path.

This may bridge the gap by giving labour protection up front while giving management and investors a measurable test of economic viability.

---

### Proposal 2: “No closure first” with stronger non-layoff adjustment tools
A second compromise could package the restructuring around stronger non-destructive measures:

- working-time reduction or shift removal;
- specialization of sites;
- redeployment and retraining;
- voluntary exit schemes;
- management/overhead cuts;
- owner or investor support;
- product allocation and investment commitments.

In exchange, management could receive:
- faster implementation timelines,
- stronger flexibility in assignment rules,
- and defined review points tied to audited savings.

This may bridge the split by preserving the no-closure principle while still delivering a serious cost reset.

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional fallback without automatic closure
A third option is to separate the **existence** of a fallback from its **automatic activation**:

- the package would not contain a built-in closure trigger;
- instead, if targets are missed, the parties would first examine:
  - which measures underperformed,
  - what additional non-destructive tools remain,
  - what management or owner commitments must increase;
- only after that would deeper structural measures be revisited.

This could reduce the perceived “loaded gun” effect for labour while still reassuring capital-side stakeholders that underdelivery will not be ignored.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific quantified conditions would make a no-closure package acceptable to you as economically credible through 2030, without relying on closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Union
**Which concrete labour-side measures are you prepared to trade for binding investment, ownership, and management commitments, and what minimum savings target do you consider indispensable?**

### To Management
**What is the exact plant-by-plant savings and capacity-reduction target, and can you show a no-closure path that meets it on equal assumptions before reintroducing closures as a fallback?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What enforceable conditions would you attach to state support so that no-closure, no-forced-redundancy restructuring remains credible rather than merely aspirational?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What binding owner-side contribution are you prepared to make if the company is to pursue a no-closure restructuring with independent verification and no automatic closure trigger?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of recurring fixed-cost reduction, utilization improvement, and execution certainty would you require before you would treat a no-closure package as economically equivalent?**

### To the Expert
**What threshold of recurring savings and capacity reduction should be used as the benchmark for calling a no-closure package “economically comparable” in this specific network?**
