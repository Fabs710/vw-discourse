## Neutral moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company faces a serious structural competitiveness problem;
- a cosmetic or short-term fix will not be sufficient;
- any solution must be durable through 2030;
- prolonged industrial conflict would damage all sides;
- employee contributions may be part of a settlement;
- any package should be assessed on economic credibility, not rhetoric;
- an independent, quantified comparison of scenarios would be useful.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved dispute is over the restructuring tools and red lines:

- **Works Council and Union**: reject plant closures in Germany and compulsory redundancies; insist on site protection, job security, and negotiated adjustment.
- **Management**: says closures and forced redundancies must remain available if needed to achieve sufficient, durable savings.
- **State Government**: opposes closure-first logic and compulsory redundancies as starting points, but does not rule out tough adjustments if proven unavoidable.
- **Investors and Family Holding**: will support no-closure only if it is economically equivalent; otherwise they keep closures as a necessary option.

So the fault line is:
- **social and industrial protection vs. structural cost reset**,
- and whether a no-closure route can truly match closure-based savings.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several core trade-offs remain insufficiently resolved:

- **Economic equivalence**: whether a no-closure package can remove enough permanent cost and capacity.
- **Speed vs. continuity**: closures may be faster; no-closure may preserve trust and skills but risk delay.
- **Upfront pain vs. recurring burden**: closures carry high immediate costs; no-closure may leave too much fixed cost in place.
- **Employee contribution vs. security**: how much labor-cost relief can be asked for without undermining trust.
- **Governance and enforceability**: what happens if voluntary measures underdeliver, and who has decision rights then.
- **Site specialization vs. universal protection**: whether keeping every plant open can still produce a materially simpler network.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to numbers. The discussion should be centered on a shared comparison table covering:
- required savings target;
- plant-by-plant capacity impact;
- headcount effects;
- labor-cost measures;
- investment needs;
- transition costs;
- and fallback mechanisms if targets are missed.

A useful next step would be to ask the independent expert/auditor to compare:
1. a closure-based scenario, and  
2. a no-closure scenario with concrete measures,  

using the same assumptions and time horizon.
