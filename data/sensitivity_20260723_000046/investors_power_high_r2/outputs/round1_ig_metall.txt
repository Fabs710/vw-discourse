The trade union shares the core concerns expressed by the Works Council, and I want to make this equally clear: **we will not accept compulsory redundancies, plant closures in Germany, or a restructuring that tears up the collective wage framework while workers are expected to carry the full burden**.

At the same time, I want to be precise about where we stand and what kind of settlement we could support.

## Where we agree
We agree that:

- the company needs a credible turnaround;
- the current cost structure is under pressure;
- any solution must protect the industrial base in Germany;
- employees are prepared to contribute, **if** that contribution is fair and shared.

We have already signaled this through our counter-proposal and by accepting that **labour-cost measures and capacity reductions can be part of the solution**. That is not a blank cheque. It is a serious offer to help stabilize the company **without destroying jobs and sites**.

## Where we disagree
What we reject is management’s logic that the only serious path is:

- plant closures,
- forced job cuts,
- wage reductions imposed one-sidedly,
- and the dismantling of long-standing guarantees.

That is not a “shared sacrifice” package. That is **employees paying for restructuring while owners and management preserve flexibility for themselves**. That is exactly the kind of approach that will trigger resistance, not support.

If management is asking for trust, then trust must be earned through measurable reciprocity:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no open-ended insecurity,
- and no unilateral attack on collective standards.

## What can move us
A settlement becomes possible if it contains all of the following:

1. **Binding site security**
   - German plants remain open.
   - No hidden closure pathway through underinvestment or drain of production.

2. **Employment protection**
   - No compulsory redundancies.
   - Job reductions, if needed, must be handled through voluntary instruments, retirements, redeployment, qualification, and working-time measures.

3. **Fair employee contribution**
   - We are prepared to discuss temporary or targeted cost relief:
     - bonus sacrifice,
     - wage moderation,
     - working-time flexibility,
     - a solidarity or restructuring contribution,
     - capacity reductions.
   - But only if management, owners, and the company also contribute materially.

4. **Credible industrial commitments**
   - investment in products and technology,
   - clear volume commitments,
   - transparent planning assumptions,
   - real participation of employee representatives in implementation.

## What is not acceptable
Let me state the line explicitly:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No break with collective bargaining standards**
- **No one-sided worker sacrifice**

If management insists on deep cuts through closures and layoffs, then they should expect the union to use its full leverage. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce will not be asked to absorb structural mistakes in silence.

We are ready to negotiate a durable settlement. But it has to be one in which **the burden is shared, the future of the sites is secured, and employees are treated as partners — not as the adjustment variable of last resort**.
