We will be direct.

## Our position

As capital-market shareholders, our test remains the same:

- **the restructuring must materially restore competitiveness**,
- **fixed costs must come down in a durable way**,
- **margins must recover on a credible 3–5 year path**,
- and **the final package must be executable with high certainty**.

We are not interested in a cosmetic settlement, and we are not prepared to support a deal that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.

---

## Where we agree

### With management
Management is right that the company has a **real structural cost and capacity problem**. We agree that:
- a symbolic deal would destroy value;
- a turnaround must be durable through 2030;
- labor cost relief has to be meaningful;
- and the final package must be judged on economics, not sentiment.

We also agree that **no-closure** is not automatically a financial solution. It may be socially preferable, but that is not our metric. Our metric is whether the company’s earnings power improves enough, long enough, and with enough certainty.

### With the expert
The expert’s core point is exactly the right one:  
a no-closure package is only credible if it achieves **comparable permanent savings, capacity reduction, and simplification**.

That is the standard we will apply. Not slogans. Not bargaining posture.

### With the union and works council, in part
We agree that:
- prolonged conflict would destroy value;
- employee contributions are legitimate;
- and any solution should be based on hard numbers.

We also agree that a **plant-by-plant audit** is necessary.

---

## Where we disagree

### With the works council
Your position remains too absolute.

You say:
- every German plant stays open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closure trigger,
- no meaningful change to legacy guarantees.

That is not a restructuring framework; it is a demand to preserve the existing structure while expecting the market to forgive the economics.

We do **not** accept that. If the network is structurally oversized, then the final package must still deliver:
- real capacity reduction,
- real fixed-cost reduction,
- and a materially simpler operating footprint.

If it cannot do that, then it is not investable.

### With the union
You are more pragmatic than the works council, but still too defensive.

We agree with your insistence on transparency and shared burden. What we do **not** accept is the implication that a package built only from:
- working-time tools,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and wage moderation,

is automatically sufficient.

Those tools can help, but they may not remove enough structural cost. The burden is on the alternative to prove that it does.

### With the state government
We respect your role and your caution, but your position is still too comfortable if it implicitly says:
- protect every site,
- oppose closures as the starting answer,
- and still expect the economics to work.

That is an aspiration, not a plan.

If closures are to be avoided, then the no-closure package must be **economically equivalent**, not merely politically easier.

### With the family holding
We agree with you that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value.  
We also agree that the company cannot carry too much cost and duplication.

Where we would push back is any temptation to treat closure as an ideological default. Closure is only justified if the alternative cannot deliver comparable permanent savings and simplification.

So we are aligned on the economic test, not on pre-judging the instrument.

---

## Our bottom line on the choice

We are **not** pro-closure for its own sake.  
We are **pro-competitiveness**.

That means:

- If a no-closure package can prove it achieves **equivalent economic effect**, we will support it.
- If it cannot, then the company must retain the ability to use deeper structural measures, including closures, because we will not back a deal that leaves the business structurally weak.

That is not a threat. It is a capital allocation standard.

## Our final line for this round

We will support a serious restructuring package **only if** it demonstrates:
- durable cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- simplified network structure,
- and a credible return to competitive margins.

We will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- an open-ended conflict,
- or a package that preserves too much cost and complexity in the hope that pressure will fade.

If the no-closure alternative can meet the test, fine.  
If it cannot, then management must not be prevented from using the tools required to restore competitiveness.
