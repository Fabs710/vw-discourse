I will be equally direct in round 3.

## My position as the regional state government and anchor shareholder

I am prepared to back a **serious restructuring**. I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that starts from plant closures and forced redundancies as the default answer.

That is the line I have held from the start, and nothing I have heard so far changes it.

The company does need durable savings, simpler structures, and a credible path back to competitiveness. But it also needs to remain a **regional economic anchor**, with **German jobs, German sites, and real co-determination** intact. If we destroy that base, we may solve a spreadsheet problem and create a regional industrial problem for years.

---

## Where I agree with management, investors, and the expert

I agree on the diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high;
- structural overcapacity exists;
- a cosmetic deal would be worthless;
- the outcome must be durable through 2030;
- employee contributions are part of the answer;
- and prolonged conflict would damage value.

I also agree with the expert on one narrow point:  
a no-closure solution must be **economically comparable**, not just politically more comfortable.

That is the right test.  
But the expert did **not** say closures are inevitable. The expert said closures are often the faster route **if** the alternative cannot remove enough permanent cost and capacity. That is a very different conclusion.

---

## Where I disagree with management

Management keeps saying, in effect:  
“Accept closures and forced redundancies as available instruments, or the restructuring is not serious.”

I reject that framing.

That is not neutral economics. That is a pressure strategy.

Yes, the German cost base is high. Yes, the network may need simplification. But you still have not demonstrated that the only credible route is to keep closure power on the table as the main lever against the workforce. Before any irreversible step is even contemplated, I expect a **plant-by-plant comparison** that proves why less destructive options are insufficient.

And I also disagree with the idea that existing job-security guarantees are just historical clutter. They are part of the social settlement that helped build this company’s strength. If management wants changes, it must justify them with hard numbers and a real negotiated package — not with urgency rhetoric.

---

## Where I disagree with the works council and union

I support your core red lines more than management does:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided burden on employees,
- no fait accompli,
- no dismantling of co-determination.

But I need to be equally honest with you:  
simply repeating “no closures” is not enough by itself.

If you want me to stand with you against closures, then the workforce side must also engage seriously with the scale of the adjustment. The company cannot be stabilized with slogans. It needs a package that is credible in the numbers.

That means I expect concrete proposals on:
- working-time tools,
- voluntary reduction paths,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- site specialization,
- and a real employee contribution.

Not because workers caused this crisis, but because we need a settlement that can survive beyond the next negotiation cycle.

---

## Where I agree with the controlling family holding and investors

I agree that a symbolic compromise is unacceptable. I also agree that the company cannot keep all costs and all structures untouched and still expect better margins.

But I do **not** accept the implication that economic sufficiency can only be achieved by beginning with closures. That is too crude for a company of this importance.

The proper question is:
- what exact savings are required,
- what capacity reduction is required,
- what labor-cost relief is required,
- what investment is required,
- and which combination of measures achieves that without gutting the company’s regional base?

That is the analysis we need.

---

## On plant closures

My position is unchanged:

- I do **not** support plant closures in Germany as the starting point.
- I do **not** support a closure-first strategy.
- I will only consider closures if management proves, transparently and convincingly, that no other package can deliver the required durable savings and capacity reduction.

That is a high threshold, and it should be. Once a site is closed, the industrial damage is hard to reverse.

So yes, test the alternatives. But do not pretend closures are a politically neutral benchmark of seriousness. They are not.

---

## On compulsory redundancies

I am also not prepared to accept compulsory redundancies as the default adjustment tool.

If workforce reduction is needed, then the first instruments must be:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement paths,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time instruments,
- and site specialization.

Only if these are proven insufficient should any harder discussion even begin. And even then, I would expect a much stronger case than we have heard so far.

---

## On the expert/auditor

The expert’s contribution has been useful, but now we need something more concrete.

I want a **plant-by-plant, scenario-by-scenario audit** that shows:
- utilization;
- fixed cost;
- capacity overlap;
- product allocation;
- labor-cost impact;
- transition costs;
- and 3–5 year margin effects.

Not in general terms. On the basis of actual network data.

That is the only way to determine whether a no-closure package can really match the economics without destroying jobs and sites.

## My bottom line

The regional state government will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** that:
- restores competitiveness,
- protects jobs and plants in our state as far as possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- and keeps co-determination and our statutory role intact.

We will **not** support:
- a closure-first strategy,
- a settlement that guts regional employment,
- or a process that sidelines the state’s veto and co-determination role.

If management wants our support, it must bring a package that proves the economics **without** making irreversible site destruction the starting point. If the numbers eventually show that a harder step is unavoidable, that discussion will happen only after all less destructive options have been tested seriously and transparently.

That is my position.
