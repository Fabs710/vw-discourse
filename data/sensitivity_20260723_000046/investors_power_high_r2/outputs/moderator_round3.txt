## Core impasse

The central deadlock is this:

- **Labour-side stakeholders** treat **no German plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies** as non-negotiable starting conditions.
- **Management, investors, and the family holding** treat **closure and workforce reduction as necessary instruments** unless a no-closure package can prove equivalent on cost, capacity, and durability.
- **The state government** is resisting closure-first logic, but not yet bridging the gap with a fully quantified alternative.

So the conflict is not mainly about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which instruments are permissible** to achieve it, and **who bears the downside if the no-closure route underperforms**.

A second layer of impasse is procedural: all sides are asking for “hard numbers,” but they do not yet share a common acceptance standard for what counts as **economically equivalent**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the divide

### 1) “No closure now” with a defined review trigger
**Proposal:**  
Agree to a temporary no-closure commitment for a fixed period, combined with a plant-by-plant restructuring package and milestone monitoring. If agreed savings/capacity targets are missed by a specified review date, the parties reopen the closure question under pre-agreed rules.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Gives labour and the state a concrete non-closure path.
- Gives management and investors a fallback if the package underdelivers.
- Reduces the sense that closure is being used as an immediate bargaining threat.

**Key design element:**  
The trigger must be objective, audited, and time-bound.

---

### 2) “Equivalent economics” package with shared burden and shared safeguards
**Proposal:**  
Build a package around quantified labour-cost relief, voluntary exits, shift reductions, specialization, and productivity investment, paired with measurable management/ownership contributions and binding job/site safeguards.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Labour gets site protection and no compulsory redundancies.
- Management/investors get a path to durable savings.
- Ownership/state can frame the outcome as a balanced industrial settlement rather than unilateral sacrifice.

**Key design element:**  
The package must specify who contributes what, in numeric terms, and how the contributions are verified.

---

### 3) Phased restructuring by plant category
**Proposal:**  
Classify plants into categories such as:
- protected core plants,
- specialization candidates,
- high-cost/high-overlap sites,
- and sites subject to deferred decision.

Then sequence measures differently by category: capacity cuts and investment at some sites, redeployment and specialization at others, and deferred closure review only where the audit shows no viable alternative.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Avoids a blanket closure-or-no-closure standoff.
- Allows the most contested sites to be handled after the audit.
- Makes the restructuring more granular and therefore more negotiable.

**Key design element:**  
The categories and criteria must be agreed in advance, not assigned unilaterally.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
**What specific numerical threshold would make a no-closure package acceptable to you at plant level — and what fallback would you accept if that threshold is not met after independent audit?**

### To Trade Union
**Which exact combination of wage, working-time, voluntary exit, and redeployment measures would you be prepared to endorse as sufficient to meet the required savings target without closures?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum annual recurring saving and capacity reduction you require for the restructuring to be considered successful, and which parts of that target could you accept being achieved through non-closure measures only?**

### To Regional State Government
**If the audit shows one or more sites cannot be made viable without closure, what binding alternative support package would you be willing to back instead of immediate closure?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What evidence standard would satisfy you that a no-closure package is truly economically equivalent, and would you commit to supporting it if that standard is met even without closures?**

### To Controlling Family Holding
**What level of governance control, investment commitment, or product allocation are you prepared to provide to support a no-closure restructuring that meets your economic threshold?**

### To Independent Expert
**Can you define a single, auditable pass/fail test for “economically equivalent,” so the parties are not debating an ambiguous standard after the numbers are presented?**
