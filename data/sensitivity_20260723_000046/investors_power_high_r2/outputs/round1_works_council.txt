As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No breaking of existing job-security and co-determination commitments**

Management cannot expect the workforce to accept a restructuring that shifts the burden one-sidedly onto employees while permanently destroying sites, livelihoods, and trust. We are not against securing the company’s future — we are against a crisis solution that sacrifices the workforce and weakens the industrial base in Germany.

What we will argue for is:

1. **A negotiated restructuring that keeps every German plant open**
2. **Job guarantees through 2030**
3. **Alternative savings measures instead of closures and forced layoffs**
   - capacity adjustments through agreed working-time instruments
   - internal redeployment and qualification
   - voluntary solutions only
   - a fair employee contribution, but only if it is matched by credible commitments from management and owners
4. **A real co-determination role in shaping the turnaround**
   - no fait accompli
   - no unilateral announcement of closures
   - transparent numbers, transparent assumptions, and binding guarantees

If management wants employees to contribute, then the company must also contribute: through investment, product commitments, and a credible long-term industrial strategy for Germany. We are prepared to negotiate seriously, but not on the basis of plant closures, forced layoffs, or the abandonment of historic guarantees.

If that is the direction management insists on, then they should be prepared for fierce resistance — in the boardroom, in collective bargaining, and on the shop floor.
