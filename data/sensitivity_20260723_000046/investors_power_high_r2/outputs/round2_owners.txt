## My position on the core issues

### 1) Plant closures
I do **not** support closures as the default or first-choice instrument.

But I also do **not** support ruling them out in advance if the alternative cannot remove enough permanent cost and capacity. If a no-closure solution is truly equivalent, I prefer it. If it is not, then closures cannot be excluded on principle.

So the real test is:
- does the alternative reduce fixed cost enough,
- does it simplify the network enough,
- and does it hold through 2030?

If not, then we have a problem.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies
I prefer to avoid them. They are socially and industrially damaging.

But I cannot support a framework that makes workforce reduction impossible if the savings target requires more than voluntary measures can deliver. The company must retain the ability to adjust if the economics demand it.

### 3) Labour-cost concessions
Employee contributions are legitimate, but they must be:
- material,
- durable,
- and enforceable.

A symbolic gesture will not restore competitiveness. If labour wants to avoid deeper structural cuts, the contribution has to be substantial enough to matter.

### 4) Guarantees and co-determination
Guarantees cannot be treated as sacred if they block adaptation. At the same time, they cannot simply be torn up in a way that destroys trust and triggers a destructive conflict.

So any change must be:
- negotiated,
- transparent,
- and linked to a real economic package.

Co-determination must remain fully respected. I will not support any attempt to bypass governance or create a fait accompli.

## What would move me

I would support a settlement only if it clearly delivers all of the following:

1. **Durable cost reduction**
   - large enough to restore competitive margins.

2. **Real capacity reduction**

3. **A credible employee contribution**
   - temporary or structural, but financially meaningful.

4. **Network simplification and productivity improvement**
   - site specialization, less duplication, and better utilization.

5. **A credible investment plan**
   - future products and technology must still be funded.

6. **Labour peace**
   - because a long conflict would destroy the very value we are trying to protect.

7. **Governance stability**
   - no erosion of ownership control, no bypassing of board process, no side deals.

## What I will not support

I will not support:

- a one-sided burden on employees;
- or a prolonged conflict that destroys enterprise value.

I will also not support a closure-first approach unless the numbers show that nothing else can deliver the required permanent effect.

## On the expert’s assessment

- What is the plant-by-plant capacity effect?
- What headcount reduction is actually achievable?

Until those questions are answered, nobody should pretend the final solution is obvious.
