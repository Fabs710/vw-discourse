As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will speak plainly.

I agree with all parties on one fundamental point: **this company needs a real turnaround, not a cosmetic one**. The cost problem is serious, and doing nothing would be irresponsible to the company, the workforce, and the region.

But I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible answer is to keep plant closures and forced redundancies as central options. That is too blunt, too risky, and too damaging for a company that is an industrial anchor in our state.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family holding on these points:

- the cost base is too high;
- capacity and complexity need to come down;
- the solution must be durable and financially credible;
- employee contributions can be part of the answer;
- prolonged conflict would damage value and jobs.

I also agree with the expert’s core message: **a no-closure solution is only credible if it delivers comparable permanent savings**. That is the right test.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with management’s attempt to keep closures and forced redundancies as the default pressure point.

The expert did **not** say closures are automatically required. The expert said closures are often the faster route **if** the problem cannot be solved otherwise. That is very different. Before anyone even contemplates closing a German site, we need a serious, quantified comparison of alternatives.

And I also disagree with the investors’ and management’s implication that the workforce must first prove it deserves site protection. That is not negotiation on equal footing. The burden is on management to show why less destructive options are insufficient.

## My position on plant closures
Let me be explicit:

- **I do not support plant closures in Germany as the starting point.**
- I will **not** back a closure-first strategy.
- I will only consider closures if management can demonstrate, transparently and convincingly, that no other package can deliver the required durable savings and capacity reduction.

That is a high bar, and rightly so. Once a site is gone, it is gone. The skills, supplier networks, and regional economic effects are not reversible.

## My position on compulsory redundancies
I am also not prepared to accept compulsory redundancies as the default mechanism.

If workforce reduction is needed, then the first instruments must be:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement paths,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time instruments,
- and site specialization.

If management cannot reach its target with those tools, then it has not exhausted the socially and economically preferable options. I will not endorse a strategy that treats people as the easiest variable to cut.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert’s statement actually strengthens my position in one important respect: it confirms that a **no-closure restructuring is not impossible**. It is conditional. That means we should not accept management’s framing that closures are somehow inevitable from the outset.

The expert also noted that no-closure solutions may require:
- substantial labor-cost relief,
- binding capacity reduction,
- productivity gains,
- and enforcement if targets are missed.

That is precisely where I think the discussion should go next: not to closure announcements, but to a **tested package**.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring if it contains all of the following:

1. **Binding protection for German sites**
   - no closure-first approach;
   - every German plant must remain open unless closures are proven unavoidable.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - adjustment must happen through socially responsible, voluntary means.

3. **A quantified employee contribution**
   - yes, there must be a contribution;
   - but it must be matched by management and owners.

4. **Real industrial commitments**
   - product allocation, investment, training, and future work in Germany.

5. **Transparent governance**
   - no fait accompli;
   - no sidelining of the state’s statutory role;
   - no unilateral decision-making disguised as urgency.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot support:
- a package that destroys jobs and sites in our state,
- a restructuring that weakens the company’s regional anchor function,
- a deal that shifts all pain onto the workforce,
- or a governance process that treats co-determination and the state’s veto rights as obstacles to be bypassed.

Those are not negotiable for me.

## To management
If you want my support, bring a package that is economically serious **without** making closures the starting instrument. Show us the numbers on:
- capacity reduction,
- labor-cost reduction,
- productivity gains,
- investment needs,
- and how the savings hold through 2030.

If that package truly fails, then we can have the harder discussion. But you do not get to begin with irreversible measures and call that realism.

## To the works council and union
I hear your red lines, and I share the concern for jobs and sites. But I also have to be honest: saying “no closures” without engaging the economic target is not enough by itself. If you want a durable settlement, then you need to engage seriously with the scale of the adjustment — while still defending the industrial base.

That means I expect you to come forward with:
- concrete working-time tools,
- voluntary reduction paths,
- qualification and redeployment concepts,
- and a real contribution that does not become a back door to closures later.

## To investors and the family holding
You are right that a symbolic deal is not acceptable. But you are wrong if you think value is best protected by making closures the first and easiest option. A company of this importance destroys value if it destroys trust, skills, and regional embeddedness.

A structurally healthier company is not one that shrinks itself by breaking its social base.

## My bottom line
The regional state government will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** that:
- preserves German sites as far as possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- secures jobs and investment,
- restores competitiveness,
- and keeps our co-determination and veto role intact.

We will **not** support a closure-first strategy.

We will **not** support a settlement that guts regional employment.

And we will **not** accept being sidelined in a decision that affects the industrial future of our state.
