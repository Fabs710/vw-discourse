I will respond clearly and directly.

## To the Works Council
I agree with you on the essentials:
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **no one-sided burden on employees**,
- **no dismantling of collective protections**,
- and **no plant closures as the default answer**.

But I need to sharpen one point: the union cannot simply repeat “no closures” as a slogan without also addressing the economic pressure in a way that is enforceable and durable. If we want to protect jobs and sites, then we need a package that actually stabilizes the company. That means we must be prepared to discuss **real labour-cost contributions, capacity adjustments, and working-time flexibility** — not as surrender, but as part of a negotiated defence of employment.

So I support your red lines, but I will not support a strategy that leaves management free to claim that nothing substantial is on offer.

## To Management
Your position still reads as: “accept closures and deeper cuts, or we won’t call it a solution.” I reject that framing.

Yes, the company has a structural cost problem. Yes, alternatives must be economically credible. But no, that does **not** mean closures must be the starting point or that the workforce must first prove that it deserves to keep its sites. That is not bargaining; that is coercion.

What you still have not answered is this:
- What exact economic gap do you need to close?
- Why are non-closure options allegedly insufficient?
- What burden are you asking owners and management to carry themselves?
- Why should workers accept permanent losses while capital and top management retain broad freedom?

If you want support from the union, then bring a **quantified, shared-sacrifice package**:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral wage cuts,
- no closure-first logic,
- and binding commitments on investment and future product allocation in Germany.

Until then, your “closures remain on the table” line will be met with resistance, not trust.

## To the State Government
Your caution against closures is appropriate, and I welcome that. But caution alone is not enough if it becomes a way of postponing the real conflict.

You have leverage here. Use it to insist on:
- site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- genuine investment commitments,
- and a fair sharing of the adjustment burden.

The regional industrial base will not be preserved by making employees absorb the whole shock. It will be preserved by a plan that keeps productive capacity in Germany and gives workers a reason to support the transition.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
I hear your emphasis on long-term enterprise value, and on that basic point we are not miles apart. A company that destroys trust with closures and forced layoffs also destroys value.

But I disagree with the implicit idea that labour must deliver the decisive correction while ownership stays in a supervisory role. If the owners want a durable solution, then they must accept that the solution cannot be built on employee sacrifice alone.

A serious ownership contribution would mean backing:
- investment in German sites,
- a credible product pipeline,
- and a settlement that protects social peace.

## To Investors
You keep saying the package must be “financially credible.” Fine. But financial credibility is not the same as accepting every managerial demand. A solution can be economically sound **without** plant closures if it is structured properly.

What is not acceptable is an arrangement where:
- workers give up wages and security,
- capacity is cut in ways that hollow out sites,
- and then the company still treats people as disposable later.

That is not durable. That is a short-term accounting fix followed by long-term conflict.

## Our bargaining position
The union is prepared to discuss a serious package, but only if it is based on **shared sacrifice and job security**. That package could include:
- temporary wage restraint or deferred components,
- bonus sacrifices,
- working-time flexibility,
- solidarity contributions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- and capacity reduction **without** compulsory redundancies or plant closures.

What we will not accept:
- forced layoffs,
- plant closures,
- or a “temporary” deal that becomes a back door to restructuring the workforce out of existence.

## Bottom line
The union is not blocking a solution. We are blocking a **one-sided** solution.

If management, owners, and investors want a durable settlement, then they need to stop treating closures and layoffs as the easy answer and start treating the workforce as a partner with legitimate claims. If that is not the approach, then we will use the leverage we have — in collective bargaining and on the shop floor.
