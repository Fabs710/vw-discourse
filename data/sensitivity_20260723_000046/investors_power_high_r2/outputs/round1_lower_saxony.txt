As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I am not here to rubber-stamp either extreme. I do agree on one central point: **the company needs a credible turnaround**. Pretending that the status quo can simply continue would be irresponsible to the company, the workforce, and the region.

But I need to be very clear about where I stand.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the cost base must come down;
- the company needs more competitiveness and more room to invest;
- employee contributions can be part of a settlement;
- the solution must be durable, not cosmetic.

I also agree that a negotiated path is preferable to a destructive open conflict. A social-partnership solution is the right model for a company of this importance.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the framing that **plant closures in Germany are the necessary starting point**.

That position is too blunt, too risky, and too damaging to the long-term industrial base that this company and this region depend on. Once a German site is closed, the damage is not reversible. You lose skills, supplier networks, training capacity, and trust. You also create political and social consequences that do not disappear because a spreadsheet says the measure is “efficient.”

If management wants to argue that every other option has been exhausted, then it must prove that convincingly and transparently. At this point, I do not see that proof.

## To management
You are right that a purely symbolic adjustment will not solve the structural problem. But you are wrong if you think the only credible restructuring is one that includes closures as a first resort.

Before this board even considers such a step, I would expect a serious package that explores:
- capacity reduction without site closure,
- working-time instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit measures,
- temporary and targeted employee contributions,
- stronger product and investment commitments,
- and a fair burden-sharing arrangement.

That is not “ignoring reality.” That is managing a crisis responsibly.

## To the works council and union
I also need to say this directly: **you will not get a settlement simply by declaring every hard measure off-limits**. If the company’s economic problem is real, then some combination of capacity reduction, cost relief, and flexibility will be necessary.

So I support your core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures as the default answer,
- no one-sided sacrifice.

But I will not support a position that rejects all meaningful restructuring. That would put the company at risk in the medium term and eventually threaten even more jobs.

## What I can support
I am prepared to back a negotiated package if it includes:
1. **Binding protection for German sites**
   - closures should not be the preferred instrument;
   - they should be off the table unless absolutely unavoidable and proven.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - adjustment must happen through voluntary and socially responsible measures.

3. **A measurable employee contribution**
   - yes, labour will have to contribute;
   - but it must be matched by management and owners.

4. **Investment and industrial commitment**
   - the company must commit to products, platforms, and future work in Germany;
   - otherwise any savings package is just a bridge to decline.

5. **Transparent governance**
   - no side deals, no fait accompli, no sidelining of co-determination or the state’s statutory role.

## My bottom line
I am **not** prepared to support an approach that makes German plant closures the central instrument of restructuring.

I am **also not** prepared to support a position that blocks every adjustment and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

So the path forward has to be a serious negotiated restructuring:
- protecting sites and employment as far as possible,
- delivering real cost relief,
- preserving the company as a regional economic anchor,
- and keeping the state’s co-determination and veto role intact.

If management comes back with a proposal that respects those principles, we can work with it. If it comes back with a closure-first strategy, I will not treat that as acceptable.
