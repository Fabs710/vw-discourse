## 1) What has been agreed

There is broad agreement that:

- the company faces a **real structural cost and competitiveness problem**;
- a **durable turnaround** is needed, not a symbolic or temporary fix;
- **prolonged conflict would damage value** and should be avoided;
- **employee contributions are part of any solution**, if they are shared fairly;
- **transparency, negotiation, and co-determination** are necessary;
- **future investment in products/technology** is important if the company is to remain viable.

There is also some shared interest in **exploring alternatives to immediate closures**, such as capacity adjustments, redeployment, qualification, working-time measures, and voluntary exits — provided these are economically sufficient.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain

The main unresolved disagreements are:

- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council and union reject them.
  - Management, investors, and the family holding keep them on the table.
  - State government opposes closures as the default but does not fully rule them out if proven unavoidable.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour-side actors reject them.
  - Management and investors say workforce adjustment must remain possible if needed.

- **Scope of employee concessions**
  - Labour is open to contributions only if matched by binding commitments from management/owners.
  - Investors and management want concessions that are substantial, durable, and financially meaningful.

- **Status of existing guarantees**
  - Labour wants existing protections maintained.
  - Management and investors argue that some historic guarantees may need to be changed to enable restructuring.

- **How to achieve sufficient savings**
  - Labour argues a no-closure path may work if structured properly.
  - Management/investors question whether alternatives can deliver equivalent, durable cost reduction.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed

The discussion has not yet resolved:

- **What exact economic target must be met** for the restructuring to be considered sufficient;
- **Whether no-closure solutions can match the financial impact** of closures and forced job cuts;
- **How much employee sacrifice is acceptable**, and for how long;
- **What binding commitments from management/owners** would offset employee concessions;
- **How to preserve industrial capacity and jobs** while still achieving the needed fixed-cost reduction;
- **What governance safeguards** would ensure no side makes a one-sided move.

In short, the parties still need to bridge the gap between **social acceptability** and **financial sufficiency**.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round

For the next round, the discussion should move from positions to **testable packages**:

- define the **required savings and capacity reduction** in numbers;
- compare **closure-based vs. no-closure alternatives** on the same economic basis;
- specify **which employee contributions** are actually on offer;
- specify **which binding commitments** management, owners, and the state can make in return;
- identify a **narrow set of options** that could meet both competitiveness and social protection goals.

A useful next step would be for each side to present its **minimum acceptable package** and the **one or two concessions** it is genuinely prepared to make.
