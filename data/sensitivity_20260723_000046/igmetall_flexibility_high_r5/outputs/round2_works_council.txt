I will answer directly.

To management, the family, and the investors: your diagnosis of competitive pressure may be real, but your conclusion is still wrong if it relies on **plant closures, forced redundancies, or the tearing up of job-security commitments**. We do not accept the idea that German plants must be sacrificed to prove seriousness. That is not restructuring with the workforce; that is restructuring against the workforce.

### Where I agree
I agree that this company needs a durable solution, not cosmetic delay.
I agree that productivity, flexibility, qualification, and capacity management all matter.
I agree that a one-sided burden on employees would be unacceptable.
I also agree that a prolonged confrontation would be damaging.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the premise that “durable” automatically means closing plants or threatening compulsory layoffs.
I disagree that binding job security through 2030 is somehow incompatible with competitiveness.
And I disagree that employees must first surrender protections before management proves it has exhausted every alternative.

If you want cooperation, then you need to stop treating closures as the default instrument.

### Our position remains unchanged
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No unilateral breaking of historic job guarantees**
- **No hollowing out of co-determination**
- **No deal that shifts the adjustment burden mainly onto the workforce**

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not refusing responsibility. We are prepared to discuss measures that protect jobs and sites while delivering real adjustment, including:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary departure instruments,
- temporary productivity and organization measures,
- negotiated burden-sharing from the company side.

But let me be clear: these are acceptable only if they are part of a package that **preserves every German plant** and **avoids forced layoffs**. Anything else crosses our red line.

### On the moderator’s “compromise” ideas
A phased review with closure triggers is not a compromise from our perspective if it leaves the sword of Damocles over the plants. That is simply delayed coercion.

A “no forced layoffs” hierarchy is only acceptable if the endpoint does **not** become forced layoffs through the back door. We will not sign up to a staged process whose hidden purpose is to manufacture later redundancies.

And “site protection conditioned on economically equivalent savings” sounds reasonable in abstract terms, but not if the price is permanent insecurity for workers and the threat of closures if management later decides the numbers are still not to its liking.

### To management
If you want a serious discussion, then bring forward a plan that does **not** include closures, does **not** include compulsory layoffs, and does **not** dismantle guarantees.

If you insist that closures remain on the table, then you are not asking for dialogue; you are asking us to negotiate under threat. That is exactly what we reject.

### To the family and investors
Your long-term value argument is noted. But long-term value is not served by destroying trust, provoking industrial conflict, and breaking the social contract that made this company stable in the first place.

A German industrial base cannot be managed like a short-term portfolio. If you want durable returns, you need durable legitimacy. That means preserving sites, jobs, and co-determination, not eroding them.

### To the state government
We appreciate your support for the workforce, but we need more than statements. We need you to use your blocking position to prevent any closure agenda and to insist on a settlement that protects German sites and employment.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate **how** to secure the future of the company.
We are **not** ready to negotiate **whether** German plants stay open and whether workers keep protection from forced layoffs.

Those are not bargaining chips. They are the baseline.
