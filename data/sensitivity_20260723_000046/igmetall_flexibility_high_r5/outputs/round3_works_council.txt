What you have not shown is why that cannot be large enough **if management is forced to present a real plan instead of a closure threat**.

## On the expert analysis
I do not dispute the technical point that closures remove fixed cost. Of course they do.

But the expert’s own analysis also shows the flaw in management’s position: **the full closure route has the highest execution risk, highest industrial conflict risk, and highest governance blockage risk**. In other words, the “strongest” tool on paper may be the weakest route in reality if it detonates trust and causes implementation failure.

So if the best risk-adjusted outcome is a hybrid, then let us be precise:
- hybrid does **not** mean “some cuts now, more closures later”;
- hybrid means **no immediate closures**, no compulsory redundancies, and a serious package of non-closure measures that actually reduce cost and adjust capacity.

If that cannot be done, then management should say so openly. But until it does, we will not negotiate away site security in advance.
