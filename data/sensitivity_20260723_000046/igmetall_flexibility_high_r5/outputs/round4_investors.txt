We agree on the diagnosis, but not on the burden of proof.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real competitiveness problem**.
- A **cosmetic settlement** would destroy value.
- The workforce should **not carry the whole burden**.
- Any solution must be **durable**, not a temporary peace.
- A **hybrid** is more plausible than a pure slogan-driven solution.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
We do **not** accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as a sufficient answer **unless** you can show, in hard euro terms, that the alternative is economically equivalent.

Right now, what you are offering sounds attractive socially, but financially it is still an assertion:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity gains,
- wage moderation.

Those can help, but you have not shown they close the structural gap. If the company’s domestic cost base remains uncompetitive, then shareholders absorb the downside through lower margins and weaker valuation. That is not acceptable.

### To the State Government
We respect the regional role, but political blocking power cannot be used to freeze an uncompetitive footprint. We would support site protection **only if** it is tied to quantified, durable savings. Otherwise, the state is asking capital to subsidize delay.

### To the Family Holding
We agree that a conflict-heavy closure program can destroy value if execution fails. But “hybrid” is not a substitute for hard numbers. If you want to rule out deeper footprint reduction, then you need to prove that the non-closure package is strong enough, not merely preferable.

### To Management
You still have not given the market the plant-level bridge we need:
- savings by measure,
- cash cost by scenario,
- utilization impact,
- and the margin path to 2030.

Without that, “closures remain on the table” is only a bargaining posture, not a convincing restructuring case.

## Our position
We would back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**. But that means:
- **durable structural cost reduction**,
- **meaningful capacity adjustment**,
- **measurable labor contribution**,
- **implementation certainty**,
- and a **credible return to competitive margins by 2030**.

We will **not** back a deal that preserves overcapacity and calls it compromise.

- We support a negotiated solution.
- We do **not** support a settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.
- We do **not** support cosmetic protection of sites at the expense of equity value and long-term returns.
