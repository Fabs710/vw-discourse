I will respond plainly.

## My position

The state government **does not accept plant closures in Germany as the default answer**. We also do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the first instrument. And we certainly do **not** accept a restructuring that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking rights.

That said, I do **not** deny the economic reality. The company needs a durable competitiveness package. But it must be a **socially responsible restructuring**, not a demolition of the regional industrial base.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that this is not a temporary dip. The cost base is too high, and the company needs real adjustment if it is to remain strong.

I also agree with the point that cosmetic measures are not enough. If the result is only delay, then the company will be weaker later.

### With the union and works council
I agree that **employees cannot be the only adjustment variable**.

I also agree that a destructive conflict would harm the company, the region, and the workforce.

And I agree that internal redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and negotiated burden-sharing are all sensible tools.

### With the family and investors
I agree that the company must recover competitiveness and that a purely symbolic settlement would be unacceptable.

---

## Where I disagree

### To management
I disagree with the conclusion that closures and mass job cuts must remain a central option.

The finance expert’s point is useful, but it does **not** automatically justify closures. It shows only that closures are a strong tool for removing fixed costs. That is not the same as saying they are the only acceptable tool, or that the regional and social costs are tolerable.

A company this important cannot be managed as if plant footprint decisions were purely technical. They are industrial-policy decisions with long-term consequences for employment, suppliers, skills, apprenticeships, and legitimacy.

### To the works council
I also need to be direct with you: a blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no changes, no review” posture is **not enough** if it does not answer the cost problem.

If all German plants stay open and the cost base remains structurally too high, then you are asking the state and the owners to sign off on drift. That is not responsible either.

### To the union
I support fair burden-sharing, but I cannot endorse a package that weakens the company’s future under the banner of social peace. If employee contributions are modest or temporary, while the structural mismatch remains, then we have not solved the problem.

### To the investors
I do not accept the implication that the only credible route is to sacrifice sites in Germany to satisfy return targets. Long-term value also depends on legitimacy, industrial peace, and regional embeddedness. A company that loses its social anchor can destroy value just as surely as one with excess overhead.

---

## On the expert’s analysis

The expert is right on one core point: **if the cost gap is large, a non-closure package must be unusually strong to be equivalent**.

That matters.

But the expert’s analysis also shows something else: the realistic path is likely a **hybrid**. And in my view, “hybrid” must mean:

- no immediate closure,
- real capacity reduction,
- real labor-cost contribution,
- real productivity gains,
- voluntary and internal adjustment first,
- and binding commitments on investment and future model allocation.

What it must **not** mean is:
- a temporary truce,
- vague promises,
- or a staged process that simply prepares future closures after the workforce has already conceded.

That would be unacceptable.

---

## What I can support

The state could support a package only if it meets these conditions:

1. **No plant closures as the first step**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the default instrument**
3. **Binding site and employment safeguards**
4. **A negotiated and measurable employee contribution**
5. **Management and owner contributions as well**
6. **Real productivity and capacity measures**
7. **A credible investment commitment in future products and technology**
8. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**

If those conditions are met, we can support a restructuring that protects jobs and gives the company a future.

---

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- a closure-led restructuring,
- a package that gutts regional employment,
- a deal that treats German sites as disposable,
- or any attempt to bypass the state’s governance rights.

If management wants approval, it must come back with a package that is economically serious **and** regionally responsible.

---

## To the moderator’s compromise ideas

### 1) Phased restructuring with review gates
This is only acceptable if the first phase is genuinely protective:
- no immediate closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- measurable savings from non-closure measures,
- and a review that is **open-ended enough to permit correction**, not a hidden countdown.

### 2) No forced layoffs plus voluntary and internal adjustment first
This is the right sequencing. But it must stay true in practice. No backdoor forced redundancies after people have already conceded.

### 3) Site protection conditioned on equivalent savings
This is the only framework I can take seriously, because it preserves the political and social core while still demanding real economic results.

But “equivalent” must be proven, not asserted. If savings are not real, then the state will not be used to bless the arrangement.

---

## My bottom line

The regional state government is prepared to broker a serious settlement, but not a surrender of the state’s industrial base.

We want:
- competitiveness,
- yes;
- restructuring,
- yes;
- burden-sharing,
- yes;
- but also **jobs, sites, co-determination, and regional stability**.

If management, the family, and the investors want our support, they must bring forward a package that respects all of that. If not, then they should not expect the state to stand aside while German plants are sacrificed.
