## Core impasse
The parties are not mainly disputing whether the company has a competitiveness problem. They are disputing **what counts as an acceptable restructuring instrument**.

The blocking fault line is:

- **Employee side / state**: closure and forced redundancy must not be treated as ordinary tools, and site security must be preserved.
- **Management / investors / controlling owner**: the company needs a structural cost reset large enough to be credible by 2030, and they do not accept an absolute no-closure rule unless the alternative is economically equivalent.

So the dispute is no longer about diagnosis. It is about **equivalence**:
- What package can reduce cost enough?
- What package can do so without closures or compulsory redundancies?
- What level of site/job protection can be made binding?

The expert input sharpened this: a pure no-closure package may be too weak, a closure-led plan is strongest financially but riskiest politically and operationally, and a hybrid is the only plausible bridge if it is substantial enough.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to test

### 1) Binding no-closure package with measurable triggers
A compromise could be a **no-closure agreement for a defined period** paired with:
- multi-year wage moderation,
- reduced bonuses / supplementary pay,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- retraining/redeployment,
- productivity commitments,
- and audited capacity measures.

In return, management would receive a binding cost-reduction path.  
A review trigger would be based on **objective financial metrics**, not a discretionary closure option.

**Bridge function:** addresses employee/state demand for site security while giving management/investors measurable cost relief.

---

### 2) Low-closure hybrid with strict guardrails
A second compromise is a **limited footprint adjustment** only if all non-closure measures fail to close a defined gap:
- closures excluded initially,
- but one or more lines/sites may be adjusted only after agreed milestones,
- compulsory redundancies prohibited except as a last-resort, narrowly defined fallback,
- transfer, redeployment, and voluntary exits prioritized first.

This makes the hybrid explicit:  
**no immediate closures, but not a permanent veto on structural change if the agreed package misses targets**.

**Bridge function:** may be acceptable to management/investors if the economic gap remains large, while still giving workers stronger protection than an open-ended closure threat.

---

### 3) State-brokered social pact with binding investment and employment commitments
A third compromise is a broader pact in which:
- the company commits to German-site investment, product allocation, training, and qualification,
- labor accepts a multi-year contribution package,
- owners and investors accept governance constraints and transparent reporting,
- the state uses its role to bind all sides to an enforceable industrial roadmap.

This would not necessarily rule out all footprint changes forever, but it would replace ad hoc closure pressure with a **structured industrial transformation plan**.

**Bridge function:** aligns public legitimacy, competitiveness, and workforce protection in one framework.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What quantified savings package would you consider sufficient to accept a binding no-closure agreement through 2030, and what objective metrics would prove that the agreement is economically credible?**

### Industrial Union
**Which labor-side concessions are you prepared to make in euro terms and headcount terms, and where is your line between “acceptable capacity adjustment” and an unacceptable backdoor to closures?**

### Management
**What exact cost gap remains after all non-closure measures are modeled, and can you show a plant-by-plant bridge proving that closures are truly necessary rather than only more convenient?**

### Regional State Government
**Will you commit to using your blocking position to reject any plan that includes premature closure logic, and if so, what specific non-closure conditions would you require instead?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to support a legally and politically binding no-closure or low-closure settlement if it meets defined economic thresholds, or do you still require closure optionality regardless of alternative viability?**

### Institutional Investors
**What minimum 2030 margin improvement and implementation certainty would make you accept a no-closure or low-closure hybrid instead of a more aggressive footprint reduction?**

### Expert / analyst
**Which single combination of measures most plausibly closes the gap: labor-cost relief, utilization improvement, voluntary exits, or limited footprint reduction — and what is the smallest closure component, if any, that makes the package credible?**
