As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price of a restructuring that management failed to prepare responsibly.

If the company wants a future, then it must be built with the workforce, not against it. That means:

- Binding site guarantees for all German plants
- Binding job guarantees through 2030
- No forced layoffs
- No unilateral closure announcements
- A genuine role for co-determination in shaping any restructuring
- Fair, negotiated burden-sharing, not one-sided cuts on employees

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect sites and jobs: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, temporary measures, internal redeployment, qualification, and productivity improvements. We are not prepared to trade away the industrial base of this company or the livelihoods of thousands of families.

Management has lost trust by putting historic guarantees into question. If it wants cooperation, it must stop treating the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort. We are ready for serious negotiations, but only on the basis of job security, site security, and respect for co-determination. If those are not on the table, then resistance will remain firm.
