Below is a **concrete comparative model** of the three options, using **directional industrial-restructuring assumptions** rather than company-disclosed numbers.

I’m treating this as a **large German OEM passenger-car operation** with:
- high fixed plant overhead,
- structurally elevated labor cost,
- partial overcapacity,
- and a need to restore margin by 2030.

## 1) Scenario comparison at a glance

| Item | No-closure package | Low-closure hybrid | Closure-led plan |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Annual savings** | Moderate | Medium-high | High |
| **Upfront restructuring cash cost** | Low-moderate | Moderate-high | High |
| **Headcount impact** | Moderate reduction, mostly voluntary | Larger reduction, mix of voluntary + selective structural actions | Largest reduction, including compulsory exits |
| **Utilization improvement** | Limited to moderate | Moderate to strong | Strong |
| **2030 operating margin effect** | Usually insufficient unless concessions are unusually large | Credible if package is large and enforceable | Strongest, assuming implementation succeeds |
| **Implementation risk** | Low industrial conflict, high economic under-delivery risk | Balanced risk | Highest conflict and execution risk |

---

## 2) Directional savings ranges by measure

These are **scenario ranges**, not claims about this company specifically.

### A. No-closure package
Typical measures:
- wage moderation
- bonus/supplement moderation
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits
- redeployment/retraining
- process productivity

**Indicative annual savings:**
- roughly **mid-hundreds of millions of euros** if the package is substantial
- could be less if measures are temporary or narrow

**Problem:**
- if all sites stay open, a large share of fixed cost remains
- so even good labor concessions may not fully repair the unit-cost problem

### B. Low-closure hybrid
Typical measures:
- one or more plant lines reduced, or a site partially reconfigured
- voluntary exits plus some structural capacity reduction
- labor-cost moderation
- shift rationalization
- overhead and indirect labor cuts

**Indicative annual savings:**
- roughly **upper-hundreds of millions to around a billion euros** in a large OEM context, depending on scale

**Why it works better:**
- it removes some fixed cost and not just variable labor cost
- retained plants can run fuller, improving utilization and unit economics

### C. Closure-led plan
Typical measures:
- closure of one or more plants or major production units
- transfer of volume to retained sites
- workforce reduction, including compulsory redundancies if needed
- logistics simplification and overhead elimination

**Indicative annual savings:**
- usually the **largest structural saving**
- can reach the **highest end of the range** because it removes true fixed cost

**Trade-off:**
- high upfront cash charge
- high labor conflict risk
- high governance blockage risk
- savings may arrive later than planned if implementation is delayed

---

## 3) Restructuring cash cost

### No-closure package
Usually lowest upfront cost:
- voluntary exit payments
- retraining
- some working-capital and reorganization costs
- limited severance

**Cash cost:** low to moderate

### Low-closure hybrid
Moderate to high:
- some severance
- line consolidation
- retooling / transfer costs
- site-specific transition costs

**Cash cost:** moderate-high

### Closure-led
Highest upfront cost:
- severance
- decommissioning
- asset impairment
- transfer logistics
- supply-chain transition
- possible mitigation payments or prolonged conflict cost

**Cash cost:** high

This is the main reason closures often look “best” on long-term margin but are hardest to execute.

---

## 4) Utilization effect

Utilization is the operational hinge.

### No-closure
If volumes remain spread across the same footprint, utilization gains are limited.
- better scheduling helps
- but fixed-cost absorption remains weak if plants stay underfilled

### Low-closure hybrid
This is the best compromise for utilization:
- fewer shifts
- some line consolidation
- better load on remaining assets
- improved labor productivity per vehicle

### Closure-led
Highest utilization improvement at retained sites:
- volume is concentrated
- overhead is absorbed more efficiently
- logistics can simplify
- but only if the transfer is executed cleanly

---

## 5) Effect on 2030 operating margin

### No-closure package
Most likely outcome:
- margin improves somewhat
- but often **not enough** if the domestic footprint is materially overbuilt relative to demand

It can work only if labor-side contributions are unusually strong and durable.

### Low-closure hybrid
Most plausible route to a credible 2030 margin recovery:
- enough fixed-cost reduction to matter
- less disruption than full closure
- better chance of board and labor acceptance

### Closure-led
Best pure margin potential:
- strongest fixed-cost reset
- strongest long-run unit-cost improvement

But if implementation fails, the realized margin benefit can be much worse than modeled.

---

## 6) Implementation risk

### No-closure package
Main risk is not industrial conflict; it is **economic insufficiency**.
It may buy peace but fail to fix the business.

### Low-closure hybrid
Main risk is **complexity**:
- trigger disputes
- sequencing problems
- partial delivery
- argument over whether commitments are “equivalent enough”

### Closure-led
Main risk is **execution failure**:
- strikes
- supervisory-board blockage
- political resistance
- supplier disruption
- delayed savings

This is why the financially strongest plan is not automatically the best real-world plan.

---

## 7) What I would say is economically plausible

### If the question is:
**Can a no-closure package fully solve the cost problem?**

My answer:
- **Only if the labor contribution is unusually large, durable, and paired with real capacity reduction**
- otherwise, probably **no**

### If the question is:
**Can a hybrid do enough?**

My answer:
- **Yes, potentially**
- it is the most plausible compromise if it includes:
  - multi-year labor-cost relief
  - real capacity reduction
  - voluntary exits at scale
  - productivity measures with enforcement
  - and some footprint adjustment

### If the question is:
**Is a closure-led plan economically strongest?**

My answer:
- **Yes, on a pure fixed-cost basis**
- but it also carries the highest implementation risk and upfront cash burden

---

## 8) Bottom line

If I reduce it to one sentence:

- **No-closure** is socially easier but often too weak.
- **Closure-led** is financially strongest but operationally and governance-wise the riskiest.
- **Hybrid** is usually the most realistic restructuring architecture in a German co-determined setting, provided it is large enough to move the cost base.
