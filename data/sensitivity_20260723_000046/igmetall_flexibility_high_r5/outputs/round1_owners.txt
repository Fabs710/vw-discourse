I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me respond from the perspective of the controlling family holding, which is ultimately responsible for the long-term value and continuity of this company.

### Where I agree
I agree with management that **the company has a structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary one. Shrinking demand, intense competition, and an uncompetitive cost base cannot be wished away.

I also agree with the works council, the union, and the state government on one important point: **a value-destructive confrontation would be a disaster**. A prolonged strike, reputational damage, and industrial instability would harm the company, the workforce, and the owners alike.

And I agree that **one-sided burden shifting onto employees is not a durable strategy**. If the company is asking for sacrifice, then it must be part of a credible long-term settlement that preserves the enterprise.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that nothing fundamental can change in Germany.

A blanket refusal of closures, a blanket refusal of compulsory redundancies, and the preservation of every existing guarantee regardless of economics would not be stewardship; it would be a path to gradual erosion of the core asset. That is not in the interest of the family, the workforce, or the region.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept a simplistic “close plants first and sort out the damage later” approach. A restructuring that destroys trust, creates permanent industrial conflict, or weakens the company’s social legitimacy would also destroy value.

So I reject both extremes:
- no frozen status quo,
- but also no reckless restructuring that damages the company’s long-term franchise.

### What I would regard as necessary
What the company needs is a **credible, durable competitiveness package** that delivers real cost relief and capacity alignment, while preserving industrial peace as far as possible.

That means:
- measurable reductions in structural cost,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- labor-cost contributions that are real, not symbolic,
- improved productivity,
- redeployment and qualification where feasible,
- and a framework that gives the company room to invest in future products and technology.

### On plant closures and job reductions
I am not ideologically attached to closures. But I am also not prepared to rule them out if they are necessary to restore the company’s viability.

That said, closures are not something to use lightly. If there are alternatives that achieve the same structural effect and are genuinely durable, I would prefer those over a drawn-out fight that weakens the business. But those alternatives must be economically equivalent, not merely politically more comfortable.

### On guarantees and job security
I understand the importance of site and job security. But **binding guarantees through 2030 with no material restructuring would not be credible** if they leave the company overcosted and undercompetitive.

A guarantee only has value if the company remains strong enough to honor it. If guarantees prevent the adjustment needed to secure the future, then they become part of the problem.

### On burden sharing
I agree that burden sharing must be visible across all sides:
- employees,
- management,
- owners,
- and, where relevant, other capital providers.

But I would be very clear: burden sharing must reduce the cost base in a way that is structurally meaningful. It cannot just be deferred costs, accounting devices, or temporary pauses.

### On the state and co-determination
I respect the regional government’s role and the company’s special governance structure. Any workable settlement will have to survive the supervisory board and the realities of co-determination.

But that governance structure does not remove the need for change. It means change has to be negotiated intelligently. It does not mean change can be vetoed forever.

### Bottom line
My position is this:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to negotiated solutions if they are economically real.
- **Yes** to employee and owner contributions.
- **No** to a settlement that preserves all sites and all guarantees while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that destroys value.

If management can bring forward a package that restores competitiveness, I will support it. If the only proposals that survive are cosmetic, then we will have failed to do our duty as owners.
