We agree with the broad diagnosis: the company has a real competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic settlement would be unacceptable.

But we do **not** share the employee-side view that **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful structural change”** can be made into a credible baseline. From a shareholder perspective, that would simply preserve overcapacity and leave the German cost base uncompetitive.

## To the Works Council
We understand the logic of site protection and co-determination. We also agree that trust and workforce stability matter.

Where we disagree is your absolute refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies. If all German plants are protected regardless of utilization and cost, then the burden of adjustment falls on margins and valuation. That is not acceptable to capital.

If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be **economically equivalent**, not merely politically preferable. So far, we have not seen a quantified package that proves that.

## To the Union
We agree that the workforce should not carry the whole burden, and we agree that strikes destroy value.

But temporary wage moderation, voluntary exits, and general productivity language are not enough if the company still has structural overcapacity. We would only support labor-side contributions if they are:
- multi-year,
- measurable in euro terms,
- and large enough to close the margin gap.

A deal that mainly buys peace without fixing the economics would not be supported by investors.

## To Management
We support the principle that the company needs a durable reset. But you need to be careful not to threaten closures and then settle for a deal that barely moves the cost base.

You have not yet shown the market:
- the plant-level cost structure,
- the savings by measure,
- the cash cost of each option,
- and the bridge to competitive margins by 2030.

Without that, “closures remain available” is a bargaining posture, not yet a fully demonstrated restructuring case.

## To the State Government
We respect the region’s interest and the governance role, but political blocking rights cannot override financial reality.

We do not support a closure-first demolition of the industrial base. But we also do not support freezing an uncompetitive structure for political comfort.

If the state wants to help, it should insist on a package that is **economically real**, not just socially balanced.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
We agree with your rejection of a purely cosmetic deal and with your concern about value destruction through conflict.

Where we differ is that protecting control and continuity is not enough. The only stewardship test that matters to us is whether the business becomes more competitive and returns to margin growth.

A hybrid package may be the best risk-adjusted route, but only if it actually closes the cost gap.

## Our position
We would support a settlement only if it includes:
1. durable structural cost reduction,
2. meaningful capacity reduction,
3. credible labor-cost contributions,
4. implementation certainty,
5. and a clear path back to competitive margins.

We would **not** support:
- a cosmetic truce,
- a deal that preserves structural overcapacity,
- or a settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive while calling the outcome “balanced.”
