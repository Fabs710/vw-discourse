### Core impasse

The central deadlock is between:

1. **Employee-side actors** (Works Council and Union), plus partially the **state government**, who insist on:
   - no plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - protection of German sites and guarantees,
   - and only negotiated, shared-sacrifice adjustment.

2. **Management, family holding, and institutional investors**, who insist on:
   - a **durable structural reduction** in cost and capacity,
   - meaningful labor-cost contributions,
   - and the option of closures/job reductions if alternatives do not achieve equivalent savings.

In short:  
**one side demands binding protection of sites and jobs; the other demands binding structural change and does not accept preservation of the current footprint unless it is economically equivalent.**

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to test

#### 1) Phased restructuring with review gates
A compromise package could allow **temporary site and job protections for a defined period**, paired with **measurable restructuring milestones**.

Possible structure:
- no immediate compulsory redundancies,
- no immediate closures,
- but agreed capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures,
- plus a **joint review after 12–18 months**,
- with pre-agreed triggers if cost or volume targets are not met.

This may bridge:
- the employee demand for security,
- and management/investor demand for real structural change.

#### 2) “No forced layoffs” plus voluntary and internal adjustment first
A second compromise is a **sequenced adjustment hierarchy**:
1. internal redeployment,
2. qualification/retraining,
3. working-time reductions,
4. voluntary exit packages,
5. selective site-specific adjustments only if earlier steps fail.

This would preserve the principle of avoiding compulsory redundancies while still creating a path to genuine workforce reduction if needed.

#### 3) Site protection conditioned on economically equivalent savings
A third compromise is to keep sites open **if** the employee side agrees to a package that produces savings comparable to closures, such as:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus moderation,
- longer working-time flexibility,
- reduced overhead,
- and binding productivity commitments.

In this model:
- closures become a fallback, not a first move,
- but employee-side guarantees become conditional on measurable economic output.

---

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If closures are off the table, what specific combination of working-time, redeployment, and productivity measures would you accept as sufficient to deliver real structural savings?

#### Industrial Union
What labor-side contributions are you prepared to put on the table that are large enough to be seen as economically meaningful by management and investors, not merely symbolic?

#### Group & Brand Management
Will you commit to a transparent sequence of alternatives before any closure decision, including concrete thresholds that would prevent closures if equivalent savings are achieved elsewhere?

#### Regional State Government
What enforceable commitments would you require in exchange for supporting any workforce contribution, and are you prepared to condition your approval on no forced redundancies?

#### Controlling Family Holding
What level of economic improvement would persuade you that protecting sites is preferable to closures, and are you willing to define that threshold in advance?

#### Institutional Investors
Would you support a negotiated package that delays closures if it delivers verified, durable savings through labor flexibility and productivity gains, and if so, what minimum financial outcome would you require?
