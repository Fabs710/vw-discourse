## Core impasse
The central blockage is this:

- **Management / investors / family** want a restructuring that delivers **durable, quantified cost and capacity reduction**, and they keep closure optionality on the table.
- **Works Council / union** will not accept any settlement that starts from or contains **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or weakening of site protection**.
- **State government** is willing to support restructuring, but only if it does **not** become a de facto industrial downsizing of German sites.
- The **expert input** suggests a no-closure package may be too weak on its own, while a closure-led plan may be too risky to implement cleanly.

So the deadlock is not about whether the company needs change; it is about **which instruments are allowed** and **how much verified savings they must produce**.

---

## Facilitation proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Time-bound no-closure bridge with hard review gates
**Facilitator framing:**  
A first-phase agreement could prohibit closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period, while requiring a large, verified labor-side and operational contribution.

**Possible elements**
- 18–24 month no-closure period
- voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, working-time flexibility, bonus moderation
- binding productivity and utilization targets
- quarterly joint reporting on savings and headcount
- an agreed review gate that does **not automatically authorize closures**, but triggers renegotiation if targets are missed

**Why it may bridge sides**
- Gives the Works Council / union the immediate protection they want
- Gives management / investors measurable delivery milestones
- Gives the state a chance to support a socially contained transition

---

### Proposal 2: Selective capacity reduction without plant closure
**Facilitator framing:**  
Instead of debating “all sites open vs closures,” the parties could agree on **line-level or shift-level capacity reductions**, site specialization, and product reallocations that reduce cost without full shutdowns.

**Possible elements**
- eliminate a shift or underused line at selected sites
- concentrate models or platforms into fewer production blocks
- preserve legal site status while reducing excess capacity
- prioritize voluntary exits and internal transfers
- link this to investment commitments in the retained network

**Why it may bridge sides**
- Reduces overcapacity in a more limited way
- Avoids the symbolic and political rupture of full closures
- Allows management to claim real structural change
- Allows labor to preserve the plant network

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid package with an independently verified economic threshold
**Facilitator framing:**  
Parties could agree that closures are not permitted unless an independent model shows the non-closure package leaves a clearly defined residual gap above a jointly accepted threshold.

**Possible elements**
- independent expert builds the plant-by-plant model
- parties agree in advance on the metric: EBITDA, ROS, unit cost, utilization, cash cost
- if the non-closure package closes, for example, 80–90% of the gap, no closure is triggered
- if the gap remains above a threshold, the parties return to bargaining with the model as the basis

**Why it may bridge sides**
- Converts the dispute from assertion to measurement
- Gives management a route to prove necessity
- Gives labor a clear opportunity to avoid closures through quantified concessions
- Reduces the “threat logic” concern

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact residual cost gap remains after a quantified no-closure package, by plant and by year through 2030?**

### To the Works Council
**Which concrete internal measures would you accept as sufficient to absorb excess capacity if the model shows they deliver a defined savings target without closures?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-bound no-closure bridge with binding productivity, working-time, and voluntary-exit commitments if it included hard review gates and verified savings?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a negotiated restructuring that includes measurable capacity reduction short of closures, even if it still requires difficult labor-side concessions?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What level of verified non-closure savings would you regard as enough to rule out footprint reduction, and will you commit to that threshold in advance?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a hybrid path if independent verification shows it materially improves margin and utilization by 2030, even if it does not maximize immediate cost removal?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum combination of labor concessions and capacity adjustment would you regard as credibly sufficient to restore competitiveness without immediate plant closures?**
