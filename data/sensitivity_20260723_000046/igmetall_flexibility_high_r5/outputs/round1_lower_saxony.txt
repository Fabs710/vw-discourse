I want to be very clear from the regional government and anchor-shareholder perspective:

We **do** recognize the economic pressure on the company. We are not denying the need for restructuring, and we are not demanding that the status quo be frozen at any price. But we **do** reject the logic that the only credible path forward is plant closures and forced job destruction in Germany.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: this company needs a **durable** solution, not a short postponement. I also agree that competitiveness, productivity, and a lower cost base matter if the company is to invest in future technologies and remain a strong industrial anchor.

I also agree with the union and works council that a restructuring imposed one-sidedly on the workforce is politically and industrially unsustainable. If the company wants a stable future, it needs legitimacy, and that requires social partnership.

### Where I disagree
I strongly disagree with the idea that closures and large-scale job cuts are the necessary starting point.

From our side, that is not an acceptable assumption. This company is not just any asset on a balance sheet. It is a core industrial base for the region, with major employment, supplier networks, skills, and public significance. Plant closures in Germany would have consequences far beyond the factory gate: for families, local economies, apprenticeships, and the company’s social license to operate.

I also do **not** accept an approach that treats the state’s role as merely symbolic while others decide the future unilaterally. Under the special statute and our shareholder position, the regional government has a real say. We will use it if necessary.

### What we can support
We can support a restructuring that:
- **preserves sites wherever possible**
- **avoids forced layoffs**
- **uses negotiated capacity adjustments**
- **includes working-time and productivity measures**
- **includes qualification, redeployment, and internal mobility**
- **shares the burden fairly across capital, management, and labor**
- **protects the company’s long-term industrial base in Germany**

That means we are open to employee-side contributions **if** they are part of a balanced package and tied to binding commitments on jobs, sites, and future investment. But we will not support a package that simply strips guarantees, closes plants, and calls that “competitiveness.”

### What would move us
A credible alternative to closures would have to show:
1. real and measurable cost relief,
2. preserved employment to the greatest extent possible,
3. no compulsory redundancies,
4. no hollowing out of the German industrial footprint,
5. and a binding commitment to future investment and product allocation.

If management can present such a package, we will engage constructively. If it cannot, then it should not expect the state to bless a course that damages the region and undermines co-determination.

So my position is straightforward:
- **No automatic plant closures**
- **No mass layoffs as the first resort**
- **Yes to negotiated restructuring**
- **Yes to shared sacrifice**
- **No to gutting the company’s regional footprint**

We are prepared to broker a serious settlement, but only one that keeps this company strong **and** keeps it anchored in Germany.
