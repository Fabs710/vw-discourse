Let me be equally clear: I hear the rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies, but I do **not** accept a restructuring that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving our German cost base structurally uncompetitive.

We are in a hard industrial reality:
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our brand is missing its return-on-sales target,
- and our German factory costs are materially above where they need to be.

If we respond with only temporary workarounds, we will simply postpone the problem and weaken the company further. That is not acceptable to the business, the owner, or the long-term security of the remaining jobs.

Where I agree:
- This cannot be a one-sided burden on employees.
- A durable settlement needs negotiated solutions, not management theatrics.
- Qualification, redeployment, working-time tools, and productivity improvements must all be part of the package.

Where I disagree:
- A blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no changes to guarantees” position does not solve the structural mismatch.
- Binding guarantees through 2030, with no meaningful capacity adjustment, would lock in the very cost base that is undermining the brand’s future.
- If the only acceptable outcome is to leave the current structure largely intact, then we are not restructuring — we are delaying decline.

So my position is this:

1. We need a **durable reduction in capacity and cost**.
2. That must include a **genuine labour-cost contribution**.
3. We are prepared to consider alternatives to closures **only if they deliver the same structural effect** — not cosmetic savings, not temporary deferrals.
4. If voluntary, negotiated measures cannot achieve that, then closures and deeper job reductions remain on the table.

On burden-sharing: yes, management, owners, and other stakeholders must contribute. But employee contributions cannot be symbolic. They must materially move the cost base.

I am prepared to negotiate seriously on how this is done. But I will not commit to a package that preserves all sites first and asks competitiveness to somehow follow later. The order matters: the company must become viable enough to invest in the future. Without that, there is no future to protect.
