As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and abandoning historic guarantees. A restructuring that is built on closure and forced layoffs is not a solution we can support.

What we are prepared to discuss is a different path:
- binding job security for all German sites through 2030,
- preservation of every German plant,
- no operational dismissals,
- real co-determination in shaping the restructuring,
- and a fair contribution that is negotiated, temporary, and socially balanced.

If management wants savings, then we will insist on alternatives that protect sites and livelihoods:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary programs only,
- internal redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany.

But let me be equally clear: if this is presented as a fait accompli, with closures and layoffs already decided, then you should expect fierce resistance. We have the legal power to block such decisions, and we are prepared to use it. We are also prepared to mobilize the workforce.

The test is simple: protect the plants, protect the jobs, and restore trust with binding guarantees. Without that, there is no acceptable agreement.
