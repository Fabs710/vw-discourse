## 2) Is the claimed savings requirement likely real?

Yes — at least directionally.

Given the facts described:
- shrinking European demand,
- EV transition investment burden,
- intense price competition,
- domestic cost base above target/peers,
- ROS below target,

it is financially plausible that the company needs a **material recurring cost reduction**. In turnaround terms, that usually means:
- structural fixed-cost reduction,
- lower labour cost per vehicle,
- better plant utilization,
- and lower complexity.

So the **savings requirement is likely real**. The exact amount is **unknown**.

## 3) Are plant closures financially justified on the current record?

### Short answer
**Not yet proven.**

A closure case requires a plant-level necessity test, not just a group-level cost complaint.

To justify closure/consolidation financially, management would need to show:
1. the size of the recurring margin gap,
2. how much can be closed by non-closure measures,
3. the residual gap,
4. which plants are structurally loss-making or uneconomic at projected volume,
5. why redeployment/product allocation cannot solve it.

That evidence has not been provided in the conversation.

### Finance standard
A plant closure is financially justified if:
- the site has persistent negative contribution after avoidable cost adjustments,
- utilization is too low to absorb fixed costs,
- future product allocation is insufficient,
- and non-closure measures do not close the gap within a credible horizon.

At present, those conditions are **asserted** but not **quantified**.

## 4) Can non-closure alternatives credibly close the margin gap?

### Yes, potentially — but only if the gap is moderate
Non-closure measures can close a meaningful part of the gap, especially where:
- labour cost is high relative to peers,
- overtime/working-time patterns can be adjusted,
- attrition and voluntary departures are available,
- internal redeployment can reduce headcount without severance shock,
- productivity and footprint optimization are underexploited.

### But they may be insufficient if the gap is large
Non-closure measures usually have limits:
- voluntary exits are finite,
- working-time measures often deliver only temporary or partial savings,
- productivity gains are real but typically slow and incremental,
- temporary labour concessions do not solve permanent overcapacity.

So the question is not whether non-closure measures matter — they do — but whether they are enough.

## 5) Typical financial structure of a non-closure package

A credible non-closure bridge would usually include:

- **Voluntary departures / attrition**  
  Often the largest immediate headcount lever, but only if the company has enough eligible employees and a high natural turnover base.

- **Working-time flexibility / reduced hours**  
  Helps near-term cash but may be reversible and not fully structural.

- **Temporary labour contribution**  
  Useful for immediate margin relief, but usually not a permanent fix.

- **Productivity and complexity reduction**  
  Essential, but often slower and less certain than headcount/capacity actions.

- **Redeployment / qualification**  
  Valuable for social mitigation, but financially effective only if it truly removes cost, not merely relocates it.

In most manufacturing turnarounds, these measures can bridge **part** of the gap. Whether they bridge **all** of it depends on the size of the required savings.

## 6) What threshold would make closure financially justified?

Because no numbers are provided, only a rule of thumb is possible.

### Closure becomes more likely justified when:
- the residual gap after non-closure measures remains **material and recurring**,
- the plant is chronically underutilized,
- fixed-cost absorption cannot be restored through volume allocation,
- and the cost to keep the site open exceeds the cost of consolidation, including severance and transition costs.

### Non-closure package is more likely sufficient when:
- the required savings are modest relative to payroll and overhead,
- the gap is mainly labour-cost driven rather than footprint-driven,
- utilization can be improved by product allocation,
- and the business has enough time to realize attrition, productivity, and working-time savings.

## 8) What numbers are needed to settle this properly?

To decide whether closures are necessary, management should produce a quantified bridge showing:
1. current operating margin / ROS gap versus target,
2. annual recurring savings target,
3. savings by non-closure measure,
4. residual gap,

Without that bridge, the dispute remains unresolved.
