### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option**; all sides accept that a restructuring is needed.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated** solution rather than a purely unilateral one.
- Several parties agree on possible elements of a package:
  - temporary / negotiated labour contribution,
  - voluntary departures,
  - internal redeployment and qualification,
  - working-time and productivity measures,
  - investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany,
  - a solution that avoids prolonged conflict if possible.
- There is shared recognition that any outcome should be **durable, measurable, and credible**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, state, and family holding reject them; management and investors see capacity reduction as necessary, potentially including closures or equivalent permanent volume removal.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and state reject them; management wants no exclusions if needed for structural savings; investors want a credible path to savings, not necessarily a specific method.
- **Scope of job protection:** Labour seeks binding protection for sites/jobs; management and capital insist this cannot be absolute if competitiveness is to be restored.
- **Cost reduction target and method:** Management, investors, and family holding want quantified, durable savings; labour and state want savings without one-sided burdening of workers.
- **Acceptability of conflict:** Labour is prepared to mobilize if needed; management and investors want implementation certainty and warn against deadlock.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **structural savings** without closures or forced redundancies, or whether equivalent savings can realistically be found otherwise.
- What level of **capacity reduction** is necessary, and how it would be distributed across sites.
- How to make any employee contribution both **meaningful enough for competitiveness** and **limited/fair enough to be acceptable**.
- How to balance **site and employment protection** against the need for a lower cost base.
- How to link **investment commitments** to concrete cost and capacity measures so they are credible to all sides.
- How to avoid a settlement that is either:
  - too weak to restore competitiveness, or
  - too harsh to survive co-determination and maintain labour peace.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and design options**:
  1. What exact savings are required?
  2. What specific package of measures could deliver them?
  3. Which measures are acceptable as alternatives to closures and compulsory redundancies?
  4. What binding guarantees or timelines would make a deal credible for each side?
- Ask each side to present:
  - one **minimum acceptable outcome**,
  - one **non-negotiable red line**,
  - and one **concrete fallback proposal** with quantified effects.
