I will be direct as well.

We are not going to get a durable solution by repeating that closures are “structurally necessary” while still failing to show the quantified bridge. Management is still asking everyone else to accept the harshest instrument before proving that the alternatives are insufficient. I do **not** accept that.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept a fake comfort zone where all sites and all guarantees are frozen while the company remains uncompetitive. That would be managed decline. So the issue is not whether restructuring is needed — it is. The issue is **how much**, **where**, and **with what safeguards**.

## To management
You have not yet shown that plant closures or compulsory redundancies are unavoidable.  
Your argument rests on general statements about overcapacity and cost pressure, but the advisors have made clear that the decisive information is still missing:
- current and target return-on-sales,
- annual savings requirement,
- plant-level economics,
- savings from voluntary, working-time, redeployment, and productivity measures,
- residual gap after those measures.

Until that bridge is on the table, I will not treat closures as economically proven. In our governance structure, “we prefer this option” is not enough to justify a measure that would hit regional employment and permanently damage trust.

What I can support is a **serious restructuring package** that delivers real savings without defaulting to closures:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement and attrition,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- measurable productivity improvements,
- and a temporary, negotiated employee contribution.

But this must be tested against the actual gap. If the gap is not closed, then management still has to explain why it believes the alternatives are insufficient. Right now, that explanation is incomplete.

## To the works council and union
I agree with your core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of protections,
- no burden shift onto workers alone,
- and no closure as a first resort.

That position is legitimate, and I support it. It reflects the social partnership that has long made this company stable.

Where I want to be equally clear is this: if you want to block closures, you must also be prepared for a disciplined, quantified alternative that truly lowers cost and capacity. “No closures” cannot remain only a principle; it has to be matched by a credible operational package.

So I support:
- voluntary measures,
- internal redeployment,
- reskilling,
- working-time tools,
- and a time-limited contribution.

But I will not support any package that merely preserves the status quo and hopes the market turns around.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right about one thing: a paper compromise would be worthless.  
But a hard solution is not automatically a good one. A restructuring that destroys trust, weakens co-determination, and damages the company’s regional anchor role would also be value-destructive.

Durability comes from a solution that is economically real **and** socially legitimate. This company is not just a financial asset; it is a major industrial employer and a core part of the regional economy. That means you cannot treat plant closures as a clean technical fix and ignore the political and social cost.

## To the investors
You are correct that a cosmetic settlement would be bad for value. I share that concern.  
But I do **not** accept the leap from “the company needs savings” to “closures are inevitable.” The advisors have explicitly said that this is not proven on the information available.

If the required savings can be achieved through:
- volume consolidation,
- productivity,
- labor-cost contribution,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary departures,
- and investment-backed redeployment,

then that is the better route. If not, then show the numbers. But until that is done, I will not endorse closures as the default answer.

## My position
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- German sites protected as far as economically feasible.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No automatic plant closures.
- A quantified, durable restructuring with real savings.
- A fair, temporary employee contribution.
- Binding investment and product commitments for Germany.
- Co-determination and the state’s special rights preserved.

### Red line
- Any settlement that guts regional employment.
- Any attempt to bypass the state’s veto role or co-determination.
- Any restructuring that shifts the burden primarily onto workers.
- Any closure package that is not demonstrably necessary after alternatives are tested.

I am prepared to support a package built around:
- voluntary departure programs,
- retirement and attrition,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- a temporary and limited employee contribution,
- and binding commitments to investment and future products in Germany.

But I need management to place the quantified bridge on the table. Without that, the discussion on closures remains premature.
