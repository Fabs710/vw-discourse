I’ll be direct.

The works council and union keep repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of protections” as if saying it often enough makes the economics disappear. It doesn’t. I agree with the advisors on one point: we still need hard numbers. But I disagree with the implication that, until those numbers are tabled, management must suspend all hard measures and accept a deal that may be economically meaningless.

## To the works council
You are asking for:
- all plants to stay open,
- no forced redundancies,
- job security through 2030,
- and only voluntary, socially managed adjustments.

That is **not yet a restructuring solution**. It is a wish list unless it closes the cost and capacity gap. I am not prepared to lock in every site and every guarantee while the brand remains off-target and the German cost base stays structurally too high.

I do agree with one thing: we need a quantified bridge. But that cuts both ways. If your alternative can genuinely produce the same durable savings, then show it. If it cannot, then you cannot simply declare closures off-limits and expect competitiveness to return by itself.

## To the union
You are right that workers should not bear the full adjustment alone. I have said repeatedly that a labour-cost contribution must be part of the answer, but it must be **meaningful and durable**, not symbolic and temporary.

Where I disagree with you is your insistence that a temporary contribution plus voluntary measures is enough by default. That is not established. Temporary concessions do not solve permanent overcapacity. Working-time changes and attrition help, but they do not automatically fix a network that may be carrying too much fixed cost for the market reality we face.

If your package closes the gap, fine — but so far that case has not been made.

## To the state shareholder
You keep saying closures should be a last resort. I can accept that as a principle. I do **not** accept it as an excuse to rule them out in advance. If the footprint remains too large, if utilisation is too low, and if the savings bridge does not close otherwise, then you cannot ask management to protect every structure in the name of regional stability.

Stability without competitiveness is not stability. It is managed decline.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right that we need an economically real solution. But “real” means making the hard choices, not just insisting on toughness rhetorically. If we are serious about long-term value, then we cannot allow a settlement that preserves too much cost base and leaves the company underpowered for 2030.

## To the investors
You want durability and no cosmetic deal. I agree. But durability requires structural change. That may include closures or equivalent permanent capacity removal. It definitely includes a meaningful labour-cost reset. What it does **not** include is a package that protects every site, relies on temporary concessions, and hopes volume magically recovers.

## My position, clearly
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- A **durable** reduction in capacity and cost.
- A **meaningful** labour-cost contribution.
- A package that restores the brand’s return-on-sales trajectory.
- Freed funds for future products and technology to 2030.
- A negotiated settlement that avoids prolonged conflict **if** it still delivers the savings.

### Red line
- Any “deal” that leaves the cost base fundamentally uncompetitive.
- Any cosmetic compromise that delays the problem.
- Any package that excludes closures or equivalent permanent capacity reduction **without** proving the savings gap can be closed otherwise.

### Concrete fallback proposal
I am prepared to work from a package built around:
- voluntary departures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and investment commitments.

But this must be tested against the actual savings requirement. If the residual gap remains large, then capacity consolidation, including closures, must stay on the table. I will not pre-empt that before the numbers are shown.

## Bottom line
I am not here to preserve every plant at any cost. I am here to restore competitiveness and protect the company’s future. If the alternatives you advocate can genuinely do that, demonstrate it in numbers. If not, then we need the room to make the structural cuts that reality requires.
