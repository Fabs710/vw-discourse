As the regional state shareholder, I want to be equally clear: **a restructuring that destroys core jobs or closes German plants is not acceptable**. That is not only a political preference; it would damage the company’s role as a regional economic anchor and weaken the long-term industrial base we all depend on.

I do agree with management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The cost base, market pressure, and earnings weakness are real. If the company remains uncompetitive, then jobs and investment are at risk anyway. So yes, there must be a durable restructuring.

But I strongly disagree with the implication that the only credible path is **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and one-sided wage dumping**. That would be the wrong answer economically and politically. It would also provoke exactly the kind of conflict that undermines the company’s stability for years.

To the Works Council and the union: I support your core line that **German sites, employment, and co-determination must be protected**. I also support the idea that any employee contribution has to be **negotiated, temporary, and fair**, not imposed as a permanent loss of standards. But I want to be honest: simply saying “no closures, no layoffs” is not yet a complete restructuring plan. We need a package that shows how savings will be achieved in a way that is credible to the company’s future.

What I would consider a serious basis for agreement is this:
- no plant closures as the starting point,
- no compulsory redundancies as long as alternatives exist,
- capacity adjustments through voluntary and socially managed measures,
- working-time and productivity solutions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and a binding investment commitment for future technologies and production in Germany.

If management needs relief, then it must come with **clear reciprocal commitments**:
- preserve sites,
- protect employment as far as possible,
- maintain co-determination,
- and ensure that any burden-sharing is time-limited and proportional.

So my position is:
- I am **not** prepared to support a restructuring built on closures and mass layoffs.
- I am **prepared** to support a negotiated restructuring that preserves the company’s regional footprint and creates a credible path back to competitiveness.
- I will use the state’s rights to prevent a settlement that sidelines the workforce or strips the company of its social anchor.

Management should not misread this: if the proposal remains a blunt cut at the expense of jobs and sites, it will face resistance not only from labour but also from the state shareholder. If, however, there is a serious plan that balances competitiveness with social responsibility, then we can work with that.
