I agree with the core message from the Works Council: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable basis for a settlement**.

From the union side, I want to be equally clear. The workforce has already borne a lot of the burden of transformation, investment, and uncertainty. We will not sign up to a restructuring where **workers pay the full price for management’s strategic and cost problems** while owners and top management are spared. That would be neither fair nor durable.

That said, we are not rejecting every form of adjustment. We have already shown that by putting a **counter-proposal** on the table. We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary and negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reduction,
- flexible working-time solutions,
- voluntary departures,
- internal redeployment,
- and site-specific productivity measures.

But these only make sense if they are tied to **real guarantees**:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of the collective wage framework,
- and a credible commitment to future investment and production in Germany.

Where I disagree with management’s current logic is this: if the company comes only with “deep cuts” and demands that employees absorb the pain while safeguarding everything else for capital and management, then there is no fair deal. That is not restructuring; that is shifting the burden downward.

If management wants a settlement, it needs to show willingness to share responsibility:
- owners must contribute,
- management must contribute,
- and the burden must be distributed fairly.

If the answer is still closures, forced layoffs, and unilateral wage dumping, then you should expect escalating industrial action. We are not looking for conflict for its own sake, but we are absolutely prepared to defend jobs, sites, and collective standards.
