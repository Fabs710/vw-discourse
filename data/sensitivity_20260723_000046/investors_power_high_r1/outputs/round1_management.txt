Let me be equally direct.

I hear the rejection of plant closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand why you take that line. But I cannot accept a position that starts and ends with “no closures, no layoffs” and offers only vague alternatives. That does not solve the structural problem we face.

The facts are not in dispute:
- German factory costs are too high.
- Our European market is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying, including from lower-cost and Chinese competitors.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.
- We need funds for future products and technology, not just to preserve the status quo.

So I agree with one part of what you both said: any restructuring must be durable, not cosmetic. But I strongly disagree that job guarantees through 2030, site preservation at every German plant, and the exclusion of compulsory redundancies can be the starting point if they prevent a real cost reset. That would lock in an uncompetitive cost base and leave us weaker, not stronger.

Let me be clear on what management needs:
- a structural and sustainable reduction in capacity,
- a genuine labour-cost contribution,
- and savings that are large enough to restore competitiveness and margin.

If we do not achieve that, then we are simply postponing the crisis. And I will not sign up to a deal that burns time, satisfies nobody, and still leaves the company exposed.

At the same time, I am not saying that the only route is immediate, blunt closure of plants tomorrow morning. I am open to alternatives if, and only if, they deliver equivalent structural savings. That means:
- credible capacity reductions,
- meaningful labour-cost reductions,
- durable flexibility measures,
- productivity gains with hard targets,
- and a settlement that frees up resources for investment through 2030.

What I cannot accept is a “socially balanced” package that preserves all sites and all guarantees while expecting management to absorb the full adjustment. That is not balanced; it is a refusal to confront the economics.

So my message is simple:
- If you want no closures, then bring me a package that achieves the same cost and capacity effect without closures.
- If you want no compulsory redundancies, then show how you will still generate the scale of structural savings we need.
- If you want investment commitments, then they must be financed by real cost relief, not wishful thinking.

We are prepared to negotiate. We are not prepared to pretend that the current structure is sustainable.
