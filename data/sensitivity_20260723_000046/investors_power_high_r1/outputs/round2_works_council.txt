Let me respond very clearly to what has been said.

### To management
You are still asking the workforce to pay for strategic failures that were not created by the workforce.

You say the cost base is too high. I do not deny that the company must remain competitive. But your conclusion — closures, compulsory redundancies, and the breaking of existing protections — is exactly what we reject. If you present “competitiveness” as a one-way demand on labour while keeping plant decisions and structural adjustments on your side alone, then you are not negotiating a restructuring; you are demanding capitulation.

What is still missing from your side is not rhetoric, but a **real alternative design**:
- No closure of any German plant.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Meaningful capacity adjustment through voluntary and socially managed measures only.
- Internal redeployment before any external reduction.
- A binding investment and product commitment for Germany.
- A genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring, not just being informed after the fact.

If you cannot deliver savings without crossing those lines, then your plan is not fit for the company’s social compact and will not pass through the governance reality here.

### To the union
I support your line on no closures and no forced layoffs. That remains the correct position.

I also support your insistence that employees should not bear the burden alone. A temporary, negotiated contribution may be discussable only if it is tied to hard guarantees. But let us be honest: “temporary” cannot become a permanent downgrade by another name. We have seen that trick before. Once guarantees are surrendered, they are rarely restored.

So yes:
- negotiated and time-limited contributions can be discussed,
- voluntary departures can be used,
- flexible working-time tools can be used,
- but only if the core framework stays intact.

### To the state shareholder
I welcome your clear statement against closures and compulsory redundancies. That matters.

But I want to be equally direct: support is only meaningful if it is backed by action when management tries to push through a destructive package. The workforce will not be reassured by speeches about regional stability if, in the end, the shareholder side lets management hollow out the German footprint.

### To the family holding
You say you do not support a paper compromise. Fine. Neither do we.

But let us not pretend there is only one form of “hardness.” A hard line against labour is easy. A hard line against destroying trust, sites, and long-term industrial capability is what the company actually needs. If the controlling owners want durability, then they should back a settlement that protects the plants and the workforce, rather than forcing a confrontation that will poison the company for years.

### On the key question: what is acceptable?
I will state it without ambiguity.

#### Minimum acceptable outcome
- All German plants remain open.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Binding job-security framework through 2030.
- No unilateral weakening of co-determination or existing protections.
- Capacity and efficiency improvements only through negotiated, socially responsible measures.
- A real future-investment commitment for German sites.

#### Red line
- Any plant closure in Germany.
- Any compulsory redundancy.
- Any attempt to use restructuring as a vehicle to dismantle the job-security framework.

#### Concrete fallback proposal
If management insists that savings are required, then the only path we will even discuss is:
- voluntary programs only,
- retirement and attrition-based reductions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time reductions or flexibility,
- productivity improvements jointly designed with employee representatives,
- temporary and strictly limited employee contribution,
- and binding commitments on new products, investment, and site utilization in Germany.

That is the alternative path. It is the only credible one.

### What I reject
I reject the idea that “equivalent savings” automatically requires closures. That is management’s claim, not a law of nature. Before anyone talks about closing plants or dismissing workers, show us:
- the exact savings required,
- the exact gap after alternative measures,
- and the exact reason why negotiated alternatives cannot close it.

Until then, closures and forced layoffs are not justified; they are simply the easiest option for management.

### Final point
We are not here to provide cover for a destructive restructuring. We are here to defend the workforce, the plants, and the co-determination framework that makes this company socially stable.

If management wants a settlement, it must come with binding protection for jobs and sites. If not, then you will face resistance — in the boardroom, in the plants, and in public.
