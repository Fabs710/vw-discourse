I will be blunt.

We all agree that doing nothing is not an option, and I welcome the demand for numbers. But I do **not** accept management’s repeated attempt to keep closures and forced redundancies as a standing threat while still failing to prove necessity. In this governance structure, that is not “hard realism”; it is pressure politics.

## To management
You still have not delivered the quantified bridge:
- current margin gap,
- annual recurring savings target,
- savings by each non-closure measure,
- residual gap after those measures,
- plant-by-plant economics.

Until that is on the table, I will **not** treat closures as justified.  
And I will say this clearly: no irreversible closure announcement should be made before the bridge is independently reviewed.

I do agree with you on one point: the company needs durable savings, not a cosmetic peace deal. But “durable” does **not** automatically mean closing plants. That has not been proven.

## To the works council and union
I agree with your core line:
- no plant closures as a default,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of protections,
- no burden shift onto workers alone.

That said, I also need to be equally clear: a non-closure alternative must be **quantified and credible**, not just morally correct. Voluntary departures, attrition, redeployment, working-time flexibility, productivity gains, and a temporary employee contribution may well be part of the answer — but they must be tested against the actual gap.

What I support is:
- a serious restructuring package,
- one that protects sites and jobs where economically feasible,
- and one that keeps co-determination intact.

What I do **not** support is treating “all sites remain open” as a slogan without numbers behind it. We need proof, not posturing.

## To the regional state government
I am aligned with your insistence that regional stability matters and that closures should not be the first resort. But we must avoid drifting into a position where “last resort” becomes “never.”

My line is:
- quantified proof first,
- irreversible measures only if necessary,
- and full consideration of non-closure measures before any closure logic is activated.

That is consistent with our responsibility as an anchor shareholder.

## To the controlling family holding
I understand your point that a paper compromise is worthless. I agree.  
But a hard restructuring is not automatically the right restructuring. If there is a path that preserves German plants, avoids forced layoffs, and still closes the gap through voluntary and negotiated measures, that is the path you should back.

I will not support a package that destroys trust, weakens co-determination, and damages the regional anchor role in the name of speed.

## To the investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. So do I.  
But I do **not** accept the jump from “the business needs savings” to “closures must remain on the table as an assumption.” The advisor has made clear that closures are **not yet financially proven as necessary**.

If the bridge shows a large residual gap after all non-closure measures, then we will have to confront that. But not before.

## My position
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- German plants protected as far as economically feasible
- no compulsory redundancies
- no closure decision before quantified proof
- a durable restructuring with real savings
- a fair, temporary employee contribution
- binding investment and future-product commitments for Germany
- preservation of co-determination and the state’s special rights

### Red line
- any outcome that guts regional employment
- any forced redundancy
- any closure used as leverage before necessity is proven
- any attempt to weaken the state’s veto role or co-determination

### Concrete fallback proposal
I am prepared to support a package built around:
- voluntary departure programs,
- retirement and attrition,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures jointly developed with employee representatives,
- a temporary and limited employee contribution,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

But I want that package measured against the actual savings target. If it closes the gap, then it is the right path. If it does not, then management must **prove** why stronger measures are truly unavoidable.
