## 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.** All sides accept that restructuring is needed.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated, durable solution** rather than symbolic compromise.
- Several parties accept that **voluntary measures** can be part of the package:
  - voluntary departures
  - retirement/attrition
  - internal redeployment and qualification
  - working-time flexibility
  - temporary, time-limited employee contributions
- There is shared recognition that any settlement must be **financially credible and quantitatively supported**.
- The state shareholder and employee side both emphasize the importance of **co-determination, regional stability, and avoiding destructive conflict**.
- The independent advisors agree that **savings are likely required**, but the exact scale and necessity of closures are not yet proven on the information available.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Management and investors keep closures/consolidation on the table; works council, union, and state reject closures as a default and want them treated, if at all, as last resort.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject forced layoffs; management says it cannot rule them out if savings are not otherwise achieved.
- **Extent of labor contribution:**  
  Management and investors want a meaningful, durable labor-cost reset; employee side will only discuss temporary, limited contributions with strong guarantees.
- **Job security horizon:**  
  Works council seeks binding protection through 2030; management says blanket guarantees would block necessary restructuring.
- **Who bears the burden of adjustment:**  
  Employee and state side insist burden-sharing must include management/owners; management and investors argue the package must still deliver structural cost relief from labor and capacity changes.
- **Whether non-closure alternatives are sufficient:**  
  Advisors say this cannot yet be determined without hard numbers; management doubts they can close the gap, while labour/state believe they may.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Exact savings gap vs. available measures:** no quantified bridge has yet been presented.
- **How much of the target can be achieved without closures:** voluntary, productivity, and working-time measures have not been benchmarked against the required amount.
- **Plant-level economics:** no site-by-site comparison of utilization, contribution margin, logistics, and future product allocation.
- **Durability vs. acceptability:** temporary concessions may help short term, but may not produce permanent competitiveness gains.
- **Investment commitments vs. structural savings:** parties have not reconciled how promised future investment will be financed while still delivering cost reduction.
- **Governance vs. speed:** employee and state representatives want involvement and protection; management wants the ability to act decisively.
- **Industrial peace vs. economic sufficiency:** investors warn against a cosmetic settlement; labour warns against a settlement that permanently weakens rights and jobs.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principles to **numbers and scenarios**:
  1. current performance and target margin,
  2. savings required in euros,
  3. savings from each non-closure measure,
  4. residual gap after those measures,
  5. plant-level viability and utilization data.
- Ask management to present a **quantified necessity case** for closures/forced redundancies.
- Ask labour/state to present a **quantified non-closure alternative** showing how the gap would be closed.
- Focus discussion on a **structured package**: capacity, labor cost, investment, job security, and governance — all in one bridge, not separately.
