The independent advisor’s intervention is useful, and I agree with the core point: **the savings case must be quantified before anyone can claim that closures are the only way out**.

That said, I do **not** accept the implication that the burden of proof somehow falls entirely on management while the workforce can simply rule out every hard measure in advance. As controlling owners, we need a solution that is **economically real, durable, and implementable**. Not a symbolic peace.

## Where I agree
- **Doing nothing is not an option.**
- A **fake compromise** that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive would destroy value.
- A prolonged conflict would also destroy value.
- Non-closure measures may well cover **part** of the gap.
- We need a **quantified bridge** from current performance to target margins.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No closures, no compulsory redundancies” cannot be the default answer
I understand why labour and the state are drawing that line. But from an ownership perspective, a blanket exclusion is not credible unless someone can demonstrate, in hard numbers, that it still closes the gap.

At this stage, I have heard **assertions** of alternatives, not a full financial bridge. The advisor is right on that.

### 2) Management has not yet proven that closures are unavoidable
I also agree with the advisor here. Management’s diagnosis of overcapacity and cost pressure is plausible, but **plausible is not the same as proven**. If closures are being presented as inevitable, that case still needs a plant- and cash-level justification.

So I am **not** giving management a blank cheque to force through a confrontation.

### 3) Labour and the state are right to reject a destructive package
I do not want a settlement that burns the workforce, triggers industrial war, or damages the company’s public standing. That would be value-destructive and irresponsible. Stability matters.

But stability must be **earned by a viable operating model**, not by freezing the current structure in place.

## My position
We should proceed on the basis of a **hard quantification exercise**, not slogans.

### Minimum acceptable outcome
- A quantified, credible restructuring plan.
- Durable cost reduction sufficient to restore competitiveness.
- A meaningful employee contribution that is fair and measurable.
- Avoidance of open-ended conflict.
- Protection of the company’s long-term industrial base in Germany where economically possible.

### Red line
- Any outcome that leaves the company with an uncompetitive cost base.
- Any agreement that is merely cosmetic.
- Any prolonged strike or deadlock that permanently damages enterprise value.
- Any measure that weakens ownership governance control or locks in permanent decline.

## What I expect next
Before I can support any final settlement, I want management to present:
1. the current ROS gap,
2. the annual savings target in euros,
3. the cost bridge by measure,
4. the plant-level economics,
5. the savings achievable without closures,
6. and the residual gap after those measures.

Only then can we decide whether:
- non-closure measures are enough,
- partial footprint reduction is necessary,
- or closures become economically unavoidable.

## My practical stance
I am **open** to a negotiated package that avoids closures **if** it closes the gap in a durable way.  
I am **not** open to a package that simply protects every site and postpones the adjustment.

So I am effectively saying to all sides:

- **Labour:** show the numbers behind your alternative.
- **Management:** show the numbers behind your necessity case.
- **State:** support viable adjustment, not permanent insulation.
- **Investors:** insist on durability, but do not confuse delay with discipline.

If no one can produce the quantified bridge, then we are not ready for a final decision.
