The correct test is:

**Required recurring savings**  
minus  
**savings from non-closure measures**  
= **residual gap**

Interpretation:
- **Small to moderate residual gap**: non-closure package may be enough.
- **Large residual gap**: permanent capacity reduction becomes more likely.
- **Very large residual gap**: closures or equivalent shutdown/consolidation become financially justified.

A German plant can remain viable under a non-closure restructuring if it has:
- acceptable utilization after volume consolidation,
- manageable logistics and overhead burden,
- a future product allocation,
- labor flexibility and productivity improvement potential,
- and enough recurring savings from labor/capacity measures to restore economics.

A plant becomes harder to defend if it is:
- structurally underutilized,
- lacking future product volume,
- carrying high fixed overhead,
- and unable to reach competitive cost levels without permanent footprint reduction.
