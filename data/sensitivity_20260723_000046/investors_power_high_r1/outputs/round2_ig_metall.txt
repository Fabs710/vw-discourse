I will respond directly.

### To the Works Council
I agree with your core line on the essentials:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no restructuring by destroying trust and protections**

That is not negotiable from the union side either.

Where I want to sharpen the point is this: if management wants a deal, then “capacity adjustment” cannot become a hidden route to mass job destruction. We are prepared to discuss **voluntary, negotiated, socially managed** reductions, internal redeployment, qualification, working-time tools, and temporary burden-sharing. But any package must keep the **collective wage framework intact** and must not shift all risk onto workers.

Your demand for a binding job-security framework through 2030 is aligned with our position. I support that. But let’s be realistic: if management is unwilling to give real guarantees, then no amount of polite language will make this acceptable.

### To management
You keep arguing that “equivalent savings” require closures and compulsory redundancies. I do **not** accept that as a given.

What you have not done is prove, in concrete terms, that:
- all alternatives have been exhausted,
- voluntary measures are insufficient,
- internal redeployment cannot absorb part of the adjustment,
- capacity reductions cannot be achieved through mix, utilization, shift, and working-time tools,
- and that the cost effect cannot be met by **shared sacrifice** across management, owners, and labour.

Instead, you are presenting a preferred solution and calling it economic necessity. That is not the same thing.

Let me be explicit:  
If your “durable restructuring” means **workers lose jobs, sites disappear, and wage standards are weakened while capital and management remain protected**, then we will oppose it with everything we have — including industrial action. That would be an unacceptable transfer of the crisis onto the workforce.

What could move us:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a **temporary** employee contribution,
- a meaningful contribution from management and owners,
- binding investment for German sites,
- and a genuine commitment to future products and volumes here.

If you want labour peace, bring a package that is fair. Not one that simply asks us to sign off on a permanent downgrade.

### To the state shareholder
I appreciate your clear rejection of closures and forced layoffs. But support has to be more than a political statement. If management comes back with the same one-sided package, the state side must be prepared to use its leverage — not just express concern afterward.

Your point that stability matters is right. Prolonged conflict would harm the company and the region. Exactly. That is why management should stop testing the workforce with maximalist demands and start negotiating a balanced package.

### To the controlling family holding
You say you do not support a paper compromise. Fine — neither do we.

But a “hard” solution is not automatically a good one. A hard line that breaks the collective wage framework, undermines co-determination, and loads all adjustment onto employees is not strength; it is short-termism.

If owners want a durable outcome, then durability comes from:
- fair burden-sharing,
- maintained production capability,
- protected sites,
- and a workforce that can accept the deal because it is not being sacrificed for somebody else’s balance sheet.

### Our minimum acceptable outcome
Let me state ours plainly:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
4. **Temporary and negotiated employee contribution only**
5. **Meaningful contribution from management and owners**
6. **Binding investment and product commitments for German sites**
7. **Capacity adjustment through voluntary and socially managed measures**

### Our red line
- Any closure
- Any forced layoff
- Any attempt to make workers bear the full adjustment
- Any dismantling of collective wage standards through the back door

### Concrete fallback proposal
We are still willing to discuss a serious alternative package, but only on these terms:
- voluntary departure programs
- retirement and attrition
- internal redeployment and qualification
- working-time flexibility and reduced hours where needed
- productivity improvements jointly developed
- temporary wage contribution or waiver of certain payments, time-limited and limited in scope
- investment guarantees for German production and future models

That is the route to a deal.

### What I am not prepared to do
I am not prepared to endorse a “savings first, guarantees maybe later” approach. We know how that story ends: the burden becomes permanent for workers, while promises of future security evaporate.

So my message is simple:
- If management wants cooperation, it must be real cooperation.
- If management wants sacrifice, it must be shared sacrifice.
- If management insists on closures and forced layoffs, then it should expect resistance, not consent.

If needed, I am prepared to escalate industrial action in support of this line.
