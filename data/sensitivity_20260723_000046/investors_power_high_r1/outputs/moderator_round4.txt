## Core impasse
1. **Labour/state side**: insists that closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable unless necessity is proven, and that no irreversible site decision should be made before a verified savings bridge exists.
2. **Management/owners/investors side**: insists that a durable restructuring must remain able to include permanent capacity reduction if the quantified gap cannot otherwise be closed.
3. **Shared blocker**: the parties still lack an agreed, plant-by-plant quantified bridge showing:
   - current margin gap,
   - recurring savings target,
   - savings from each non-closure measure,
   - residual gap,
   - and site-level economics.

So the process is stuck between **“no closure before proof”** and **“no viable settlement if closure is excluded in advance.”**

## Concrete compromise positions to test
### 1) Sequenced decision package
- Immediate agreement on a **verified data package** with plant-by-plant economics.
- A **time-limited standstill** on irreversible closures and compulsory redundancies during verification.
- Non-closure measures begin at once, but closure/consolidation remains only a **contingent last step** if the verified residual gap stays material.

### 2) Conditional non-closure first, with trigger thresholds
- Parties agree on a **non-closure package first**: voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, working-time flexibility, productivity measures, and temporary employee contribution.
- A pre-agreed **numeric trigger** is set: if the residual gap after these measures is above a defined threshold, the parties reopen discussion on capacity reduction.
- If the gap is below the threshold, closures are removed from the table.

### 3) Site-protection plus adjustment model
- German sites remain open **for a defined period**, but with differentiated site-specific measures rather than a uniform approach.
- Workforce protections are preserved, but each site gets an individualized target for cost, volume, and productivity.
- In exchange for no immediate closures, labour accepts a **temporary, tightly bounded contribution** and accelerated operational changes.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder
### To management
**What exact residual savings gap, in euros per year, would remain after all non-closure measures, and what threshold would make you formally rule out closure at a given site?**

### To the works council
**If the verified bridge shows a material residual gap after voluntary, working-time, and productivity measures, what specific additional adjustment would you consider short of closure or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the union
**Would you accept a time-limited employee contribution tied to binding job-security and investment commitments, if and only if the company publishes a plant-by-plant savings bridge and a no-closure standstill during verification?**

### To the regional state government
**What leverage is the state prepared to use to enforce disclosure and a standstill, and what outcome would it actively support if the verified numbers still show a large residual gap?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Will you commit to backing a non-closure package first, provided it is numerically credible, and only support permanent capacity reduction if that package demonstrably fails against the verified bridge?**

### To the institutional investors
**What level of quantified proof would you require before supporting any permanent capacity reduction, and would you back a standstill period to allow that proof to be produced?**

### To the independent finance advisor
**Can you produce a plant-by-plant bridge that separates avoidable savings from structural savings, and identify the point at which non-closure measures are no longer sufficient?**
