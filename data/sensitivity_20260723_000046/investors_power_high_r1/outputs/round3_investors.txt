## Our view
We **agree** with management, the family holding, and the independent advisors on the central financial point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value**. The status quo is not investable. If the final package does not restore competitive margins on a credible path to 2030, we will not support it.

We also **agree** with the state, works council, and union on one important point: **prolonged industrial conflict is value-destructive**. A long strike or governance deadlock would damage margins, cash flow, and confidence quickly.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- all sites preserved,
- and binding job security through 2030

is **not acceptable as a default outcome** unless you can show, in quantified terms, that the same structural savings are achieved elsewhere.

So far, we have not seen that quantified bridge.

We do **not** support a settlement that protects every legacy structure while leaving the cost base uncompetitive. That is not protection of jobs in the long run; it is a slower decline.

### To the state shareholder
We respect the political and regional constraints, but “last resort” cannot become “never.” If the plant network is still carrying too much fixed cost, then some **permanent capacity reduction** may be unavoidable. Regional stability matters, but only if the business remains viable.

### To management
We support your diagnosis more than your tone. You are right that hard structural change is needed. But you still have not presented the market with a **fully quantified bridge** showing:
- current margin gap,
- target margin,
- savings by measure,
- residual gap,
- and plant-level implications.

Until that is on the table, your case for closures is plausible, but not yet proven. And if you push a package that cannot survive co-determination, you will destroy value through delay and uncertainty.

## Our position
We will support **only** a package that credibly delivers all of the following:

1. **Quantified, durable savings**
2. **A real capacity reduction**  
   This may be closures, consolidation, or an equivalent permanent removal of overcapacity.
3. **A meaningful employee contribution**
4. **A credible implementation path** with low risk of endless renegotiation
5. **Investment commitments** tied to concrete performance, not used as a substitute for cost discipline

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- a “peace deal” that merely postpones the problem,
- or a package where the burden is pushed mainly onto capital while the operating structure remains bloated.

## Bottom line
We are not here to choose between “closures at any cost” and “no change at any cost.” We are here to protect risk-adjusted value.

If management and labour can produce a **credible, quantified non-closure package** that still closes the margin gap, we will back it.  
If they cannot, then **some permanent capacity reduction must remain on the table**.
