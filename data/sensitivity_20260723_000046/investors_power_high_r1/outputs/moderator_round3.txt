Core impasse identified:

1. **Quantification gap**
   - Management and investors argue that structural action may be needed.
   - The works council, union, and state shareholder say closures/forced redundancies are not justified until the savings bridge is quantified.
   - The advisor confirms the bridge is still missing.

2. **Instrument gap**
   - Labour-side stakeholders will discuss only non-closure restructuring, voluntary measures, and time-limited contributions.
   - Management, family holding, and investors insist that permanent capacity reduction must remain available if the numbers require it.

3. **Governance/trust gap**
   - Labour and state fear a fait accompli and pressure strategy.
   - Management and investors fear a symbolic deal that preserves costs and delays adjustment.

## Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the conflict

### Proposal A: “Numbers-first, measures-second” sequencing
- Management submits a **single quantified bridge** within a fixed deadline.
- The bridge must show:
  1. current margin gap,
  2. savings target,
  3. savings by non-closure measure,
  4. residual gap,
  5. plant-level economics,
  6. closure/consolidation scenario only as a comparator, not a starting point.
- Until that bridge is verified, **no irreversible closure decision** is taken.
- In parallel, the parties pre-negotiate a **non-closure implementation menu** so no time is lost.

### Proposal B: “Conditional non-closure package” with automatic review trigger
- Parties agree now on a package of:
  - voluntary departures,
  - attrition and retirement,
  - redeployment/reskilling,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - productivity measures,
  - temporary and limited employee contribution,
  - binding investment commitments.
- The package is made **conditional**: if, after independent verification, a defined residual gap remains above an agreed threshold, then the parties reopen discussion of permanent capacity measures.
- This gives labour protection against premature closures and gives management/investors a path if the gap remains too large.

### Proposal C: “Site-by-site protection with performance gates”
- Instead of treating all German sites identically, each plant is assessed against transparent criteria:
  - utilization,
  - product allocation,
  - fixed-cost burden,
  - logistics complexity,
  - contribution to future programs.
- Sites that meet agreed performance gates get **extended protection and investment commitments**.
- Sites that fail the gates enter a **jointly managed review process** before any closure decision.
- This preserves co-determination and avoids blanket closures while keeping structural adjustment available where proven.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
Will you commit to presenting a **fully quantified savings bridge** by a fixed date, and to refraining from any irreversible closure announcement before that bridge has been independently reviewed?

### To the Works Council
If the independent numbers show a residual gap that cannot be closed by voluntary and temporary measures alone, are you prepared to discuss **site-specific adjustments short of closures**, rather than an absolute refusal to consider any capacity change?

### To the Union
Will you agree to a **time-limited, measurable employee contribution** tied to binding job and investment guarantees, if that contribution is part of a verified non-closure package?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you use your shareholder leverage to require **transparent disclosure of the savings bridge** and to support a conditional non-closure package if it is economically credible?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you accept a sequencing rule that prevents closures from being used as leverage before the numbers are disclosed, even if that delays the ability to choose the most aggressive restructuring option?

### To the Institutional Investors
Would you support a package that prioritizes **verified recurring savings and protected German investment**, even if it relies first on non-closure measures and only later tests permanent capacity reduction as a fallback?

### To the Independent Restructuring Advisor
Can you define a **clear threshold residual gap** above which plant consolidation becomes financially justified, and below which a non-closure package should be considered sufficient?
