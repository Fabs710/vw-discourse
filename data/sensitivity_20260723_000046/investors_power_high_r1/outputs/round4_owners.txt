I will be direct.

I agree with the shared premise that **doing nothing is not an option** and that a **cosmetic compromise would destroy value**. I also agree that we need **hard numbers**, not slogans. That much is clear.

Where I **do not** agree with the labour side is the attempt to convert the current lack of a quantified bridge into an argument that closures or compulsory redundancies are off the table. That is too convenient. It protects the existing footprint by default and risks leaving the company structurally uncompetitive. I will not support a settlement that merely preserves comfort and postpones the real adjustment.

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- all sites preserved,
- and binding job security through 2030

is **not acceptable as a default outcome** unless you can show, in numbers, that the gap is closed otherwise.

I agree with you on one point: **temporary contributions must remain temporary**, and they must be tied to real guarantees. I do not want a hidden permanent wage reset. But I disagree that this alone proves closures unnecessary. Temporary concessions and voluntary exits may help, but they do not automatically fix structural overcapacity or a high fixed-cost base.

### To Management
You are right that the company needs a durable restructuring and a meaningful labour-cost contribution. I support that. But you still have not delivered the **quantified savings bridge** that would justify demanding irreversible measures now.

So my position is:
- **show the numbers**
- **show the residual gap**
- **show the plant-level economics**
- **then test whether non-closure measures are sufficient**

Until then, I will not endorse closures as a first move. But I also will not endorse an absolute exclusion of closures or consolidation before the numbers are known.

### To the Regional State Government
I agree with your sequencing principle: **no irreversible closure decision before independent verification of the bridge**. That is sensible and necessary in this governance structure.

But I do not accept any drift toward “last resort” meaning “never.” If a site is structurally underutilized and the numbers show that non-closure measures cannot close the gap, then permanent capacity reduction remains a legitimate option. Regional stability matters, but managed decline is not stability.

### To the Institutional Investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. I share that concern. But I also do not accept the presumption that permanent capacity reduction is the starting point. The advisor has been clear: closures are **not yet proven** because the quantified bridge is missing.

Your position should therefore be:
- demand the numbers,
- demand durability,
- and accept that the instrument mix is still open.

I support a real restructuring. I will not support a fake one. But I will also not bless a closure-led strategy before necessity is proven.

## My position, clearly
### Minimum acceptable outcome
- A **quantified** restructuring plan
- **Durable** cost reduction sufficient to restore competitiveness
- A **meaningful, time-limited** employee contribution
- No prolonged industrial conflict
- Binding investment and future-product commitments for Germany
- Protection of long-term value and governance control

### Red line
- Any outcome that leaves the cost base fundamentally uncompetitive
- Any paper compromise
- Any prolonged strike or deadlock that permanently damages enterprise value
- Any measure that undermines ownership governance control

### Concrete fallback proposal
I am open to a package built around:
- voluntary departures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and investment commitments.

But that package must be tested against the **actual savings requirement**. If the residual gap remains large, then capacity consolidation — including closures — must remain available. I will not pre-empt that before the numbers are on the table.
