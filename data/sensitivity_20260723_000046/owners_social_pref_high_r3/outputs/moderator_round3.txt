### Core impasse

The main deadlock is between two different decision rules:

- **Labor/state side:** irreversible measures such as plant closures or compulsory redundancies should be ruled out unless management proves they are unavoidable.
- **Management/investor side:** closures and forced reductions must remain available if they are economically necessary, and they resist any package that precludes them in advance.

A second, related impasse is about **sequencing and trust**:

- The workforce wants **security first**, then contributions.
- Management wants **contributions first**, with final site decisions left open.
- Each side sees the other’s sequencing as creating unilateral risk.

A third impasse is evidentiary:

- All parties now agree on the need for **site-by-site quantified analysis**.
- But no shared threshold has been accepted for what counts as “enough” savings from alternatives before irreversible measures may be considered.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible bridging positions

#### 1) Time-limited no-closure moratorium with audit trigger
A compromise could be a **defined moratorium on German plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a fixed period** (for example, 12–18 months), during which all parties implement a quantified package of voluntary and reversible measures.

**Features:**
- no closures / no forced redundancies during the moratorium,
- binding employee contribution measures,
- binding management and owner contributions,
- independent site-level audit with agreed metrics,
- automatic review at a fixed date against pre-agreed savings targets.

**Bridge value:**  
This addresses labor’s demand for immediate security while preserving management’s claim that the issue must still be tested against numbers.

---

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring with “capacity reduction first” and closure as last-resort, site-specific option
A second compromise is to agree that **the default restructuring path is partial capacity reduction**, with closures considered only if a specific site fails a jointly defined viability test.

**Features:**
- priority on redeployment, internal consolidation, voluntary exits, working-time measures, and product allocation changes,
- no blanket closure commitment, but no closure decision without site-specific proof,
- each site gets a viability review based on utilization, fixed cost, logistics, and capex needs,
- closure only if a site is shown to be structurally non-viable after the negotiated measures.

**Bridge value:**  
This creates a middle path between “no closures ever” and “closures remain fully open everywhere.”

---

#### 3) Security-for-contribution package with escalating commitments
A third bridge is a **conditional exchange**:

- if employees provide quantified temporary concessions,
- then management, owners, and the state provide binding job-security and investment commitments,
- but if the company misses defined savings milestones, the parties re-open the structure in a controlled way.

**Features:**
- employee contributions are temporary and quantified,
- management commits to no forced redundancies during the agreed period,
- owners commit to investment and governance support,
- if targets are missed, the parties enter a second-stage negotiation with predefined options.

**Bridge value:**  
This links sacrifice to security and gives investors a pathway to stronger action if the first package fails.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**What exact savings target do you need, by year, and what is the minimum set of measures that closes that gap without any plant closure?**

#### To the Works Council
**If an independent audit showed that reversible measures cover, say, 70–80% of the gap but not all of it, would you accept a time-limited no-closure moratorium paired with a second-stage review?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support temporary concessions only if they are tied to binding no-forced-redundancy guarantees and an independent benchmark for when the package is deemed sufficient or insufficient?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**Will you commit to using your shareholder and statutory leverage to require a full site-by-site comparison, and then accept the outcome if it shows one site is structurally non-viable?**

#### To the Family Holding
**Are you prepared to back a no-closure moratorium if it is paired with measurable savings milestones and a pre-agreed trigger for deeper action if those milestones are missed?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a staged restructuring in which the first phase excludes closures, provided the package contains hard savings, hard milestones, and a second-stage option if the target is not met?**
