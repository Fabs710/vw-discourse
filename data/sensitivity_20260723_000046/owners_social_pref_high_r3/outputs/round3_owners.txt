I will be direct.

The family holding supports a **real restructuring**, not a symbolic pause. We also support the principle that the burden cannot fall on employees alone. But we do **not** support the current attempt to make **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no structural change”** the starting line. That would freeze the problem in place and erode value.

## Where I agree

### With management
- The cost and capacity problem is real.
- Labor concessions alone will not solve a structural overcapacity issue.
- The discussion must be quantified, not ideological.
- A durable solution must restore margins, not just defer pain.

### With the works council, union, and state
- A one-sided burden shift would be unacceptable.
- Prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, training, working-time flexibility, and temporary moderation are all legitimate tools.
- Any restructuring must be credible enough to be implemented inside the company.

### With the economist/modeler
- The key issue is site-by-site economics.
- Reversible concessions have a ceiling.
- If overcapacity is large, some form of hard capacity reduction may be economically superior.
- Partial capacity reduction is often the best compromise if it really closes the gap.

## Where I disagree

### 1) I do not accept an upfront blanket “no closures” position
To the works council, union, and state: you are treating plant closure as if it were a forbidden topic rather than an economic outcome that may or may not be justified.

That is not a serious governance posture.  
I am not saying closures should be the default. I am saying they cannot be ruled out **before** the numbers are tested.

### 2) I do not accept blanket job guarantees through 2030 if they freeze the restructuring
A guarantee is only valuable if the company remains viable. If it prevents the company from adjusting its footprint and cost base, it becomes a value-destroying straitjacket.

### 3) I do not accept “temporary” concessions that are too small to matter
If employee contributions are to substitute for closures or forced reductions, they must be **material, durable, and measurable**. Symbolic concessions are not a solution.

### 4) I do not accept a process that delays hard choices indefinitely
A prolonged dispute, with threats but no decisions, would be worse than a difficult settlement.

## What the economist’s analysis tells me
The model is consistent with a simple truth:  
- if the gap is modest, negotiated measures may be enough;  
- if the gap is structural and large, then partial capacity reduction or closure becomes economically necessary.

That means the only responsible next step is a **site-by-site verification**, not a political veto and not a management ultimatum.

## What I expect from each side

### Management
You need to come back with:
- the actual savings target,
- the savings by measure,
- the residual gap,
- and a site-by-site case for any closure or major reduction.

Do not present closures as a slogan. Present them, if at all, as an economic necessity proven against alternatives.

### Works council and union
If you want to avoid closures, then you must be prepared to discuss:
- meaningful voluntary exits,
- working-time changes,
- targeted pay moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- contractor and external spend reduction,
- productivity measures,
- and some form of capacity reduction.

But I need to be clear: **I will not support a package that promises every German site untouched, every job protected, and every legacy protection intact if the economics do not support it.**

### State government
Your veto power and blocking minority are real, but they are not a license to deny the economics. Use your leverage to force a quantified plan, not to pre-decide the outcome.

### Investors
You are right that cosmetic peace is not enough. But you also need to accept that the best outcome may not be “maximum pain”; it may be a **structured, partial rationalization** that is hard but survivable.

## My position as controlling shareholder

The family holding will support a package only if it meets all of the following tests:

1. **It materially restores competitiveness and margins**
2. **It protects the long-term industrial base**
3. **It avoids value-destructive instability**
4. **It includes a real employee contribution**
5. **It includes visible management and owner contribution**
6. **It is backed by a quantified site-by-site business case**
7. **It does not permanently impair governance control or enterprise value**

What I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- a blanket no-closure veto without economic proof,
- a settlement that merely postpones the problem,
- or a restructuring that destroys trust without delivering a stronger company.

## Therefore
I support the following process, and only this process:
- a **site-by-site independent verification** of savings and viability,
- a comparison of **(1) no-closure concessions, (2) partial capacity reduction, (3) closure**,
- and then a negotiated package that is hard enough to work.

[SUMMON: independent site-level restructuring auditor to verify management’s site-by-site savings assumptions, closure necessity claims, and employment impact estimates before any irreversible decision.]

Until that analysis is completed, I will not endorse irreversible measures on general assertions alone. But I also will not endorse a blanket no-closure framework that ignores economic reality.

That is the family holding’s position.
