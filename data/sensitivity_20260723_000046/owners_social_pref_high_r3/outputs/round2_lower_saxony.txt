I will be equally plain.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: this company needs a **real** restructuring, not a cosmetic truce. I am not defending the status quo as such.

But I **do not** accept your framing that closures and forced redundancies are simply the necessary next step unless everyone else gives up first. That is not a serious negotiating stance for a company of this importance; it is an ultimatum.

You still have not shown, in a way that justifies irreversible damage, that:
- every alternative has been exhausted,
- the same savings cannot be reached through negotiated capacity reduction,
- and site closure is the only remaining instrument.

If you want the state to consider difficult concessions, you need to come forward with a **quantified, site-by-site case**:
- what savings are required,
- what can be achieved by voluntary exits, working-time changes, redeployment, and internal consolidation,
- what remains after that,
- and why a particular closure would be unavoidable.

Until then, I cannot support a path that starts with plant closures as a bargaining lever. That would be economically risky, politically reckless, and socially destructive.

## To the Works Council and Union
I agree with your core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided burden shifting,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- and no sacrifice of employees as the first and easiest answer.

I also agree that the workforce has already contributed substantially and cannot be asked for open-ended concessions.

That said, I need to be just as clear: **a blanket refusal of any structural change is not enough either**. If the company truly has overcapacity, then there must be a credible adjustment. The question is **how**, not whether reality exists.

So I can support:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary and limited wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- training and qualification,
- consolidation of functions,
- and negotiated capacity reduction.

What I cannot support is a package that says “no closures, no forced layoffs, no meaningful restructuring” while leaving management with no route to competitiveness. That would only delay the next confrontation.

## To the Family Holding
I share your concern that a destructive conflict would damage value. I also share your view that the burden cannot sit on one side alone.

Where I part company with you is if “value protection” becomes a justification for keeping the restructuring debate too abstract. We need hard numbers and hard choices, but they must be guided by long-term industrial logic, not by a reflex to cut wherever it is fastest.

The regional state will support a serious package if it protects:
- the company’s German industrial base,
- viable sites and jobs in the state,
- and the long-term competitiveness of the core brand.

But I will not endorse a settlement that uses the regional base as a disposable variable.

## To the Institutional Investors
You are right to insist on credibility. I understand the need for durable savings and a believable path back to target margins.

But I need to be equally clear: **an outcome that destroys industrial peace, regional trust, and co-determination will not be durable value creation**. It may look “hard-nosed” on paper; in practice it can become a long, expensive conflict with severe operational and political costs.

So yes:
- the savings must be real,
- the adjustment must be durable,
- and employee contributions cannot be symbolic.

But no:
- we should not treat closures as the default solution,
- and we should not assume that a broad threat of irreversible cuts is the best way to secure implementation.

A settlement only works if it can actually be carried through inside the company and accepted in the region.

## My position, explicitly
The state can support a restructuring **only if** it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A quantified alternative package must be tested first**
4. **Employee contributions must be real but limited and time-bound**
5. **Management and owners must also contribute visibly**
6. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

I am open to:
- targeted capacity reduction,
- internal consolidation,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- and negotiated productivity measures.

I am **not** open to:
- a restructuring that guts regional employment,
- plant closures presented as the first resort,
- or a deal that strips the state and workforce of meaningful influence.

## What I need next
If management wants this to move, bring back:
- the **actual savings target**,
- the **specific measures** and their expected contribution,
- the **gaps** after voluntary and negotiated instruments,
- and a **binding investment and job-security package** that makes concessions politically and industrially defensible.

Without that, I will not move toward closures or forced layoffs. And I will use the rights attached to our stake and statute to prevent a bad outcome.
