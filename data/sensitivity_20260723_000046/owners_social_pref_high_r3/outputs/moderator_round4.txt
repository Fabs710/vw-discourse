Core impasse identified:

1. **Security first vs. structural flexibility first**
   - Labour/state/works council/family want irreversible measures off the table unless a site-by-site case proves otherwise.
   - Management/investors insist that a durable cost and capacity reset may require keeping closures and compulsory redundancies available.

2. **What counts as “enough” adjustment**
   - One side says reversible measures, internal consolidation, and temporary concessions should be tried first and protected by guarantees.
   - The other says those measures may be insufficient if the structural gap remains large.

3. **Sequencing and trust**
   - Labour fears concessions now will be followed by later closures.
   - Management fears a no-closure framework will freeze the footprint and prevent a real reset.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise 1: Time-bound protection period with quantified test
A negotiated **12–18 month protection period** with:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- and a site-by-site audit completed early in the period.

In exchange:
- labour contributions,
- management/owner contributions,
- and binding milestones for savings and investment.

At the end of the period:
- the parties review whether the residual gap is still structurally material,
- based on independent verification.

**Bridge value:** gives labour real security during negotiation, while giving management a defined process to test whether non-closure measures are sufficient.

---

### Compromise 2: “No closure unless last-resort threshold is proven”
A staged framework in which:
1. reversible measures are implemented first,
2. partial capacity reduction is then assessed,
3. closures are considered only if an independent audit shows:
   - persistent underutilization,
   - negative site economics,
   - and that internal measures cannot close the gap.

This would be paired with:
- transparent savings-by-measure reporting,
- and agreed decision criteria.

**Bridge value:** preserves management’s claim that hard measures may be needed, while addressing labour’s demand that closure not be the default bargaining tool.

---

### Compromise 3: Security-for-contribution package with conditional safeguards
A package where employees accept temporary moderation only if the company commits to:
- no forced redundancies during the negotiated period,
- no surprise closures,
- investment commitments in Germany,
- and a binding process for any future structural decisions.

If the package falls short, the parties return to the table rather than move straight to irreversible action.

**Bridge value:** makes concessions reciprocal and tied to enforceable protections, without requiring an immediate blanket no-closure pledge.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
What exact savings target must be achieved, and how much of it is still uncovered after voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, contractor cuts, and productivity actions?

### To the Works Council
If an independent site-by-site audit shows one or more sites remain structurally non-viable even after partial capacity reduction, what decision rule would you accept for addressing that residual gap?

### To the Union
Would you accept a time-limited contribution package if it were paired with binding no-forced-redundancy protections and an independently verified review point before any irreversible step?

### To the State Government
Would you support a formal moratorium plus audit process even if it leaves open the possibility of later structural decisions, provided those decisions are based on transparent site economics?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to back a package that includes binding security commitments for a defined period, even if that limits short-term flexibility, in exchange for a credible restructuring path?

### To the Institutional Investors
Would you support a staged restructuring framework if it produces measurable savings, independent verification, and a defined trigger for any further action, rather than immediate closure rights?
