### Neutral moderator summary — Round 2

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic pause.
- There is broad agreement that **purely one-sided burden shifting** would be unacceptable.
- All sides acknowledge that **voluntary departures, redeployment, training, working-time flexibility, and temporary cost moderation** should be part of the toolbox.
- There is shared concern that **prolonged conflict, strikes, or a breakdown in trust** would damage the company.
- There is also broad agreement that any solution should be **quantified** and **credible** rather than based on general assertions.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** The Works Council, Union, and State reject closures as a starting point or bargaining lever; Management, Investors, and the Family Holding say closures cannot be ruled out if the economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and the State oppose them; Management and Investors will not exclude them unless an alternative package fully closes the gap.
- **Job security framework:** Labour wants binding job security through 2030 and preservation of existing protections; Management and Investors see that as too rigid if structural change is needed.
- **Scale of employee contribution:** Labour is open to limited, temporary concessions only with guarantees; Management and Investors want materially larger, durable savings if closures are to be avoided.
- **Role of ownership/state:** The State and Works Council want stronger blocking action against closures; Management and Investors want governance actors to accept that restructuring may require hard choices.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are actually needed** versus how much can be delivered through negotiated measures.
- **Which sites, if any, are structurally non-viable**, and whether that can be demonstrated site by site.
- **Whether capacity reduction can be achieved without closures** through consolidation, internal shifts, or other hard measures.
- **What management, owners, and employees each contribute** in a way that is visibly balanced.
- **What binding protections and investment commitments** would make employee concessions acceptable and durable.
- **How to reconcile long-term competitiveness with employment protection** without simply postponing the problem.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should be driven by a **quantified comparison of options**:
- Management should present the **savings target**, the **gap**, and a **site-by-site assessment** of alternatives to closures.
- Labour should specify which **temporary, reversible concessions** it would consider under what binding guarantees.
- Ownership, investors, and the State should clarify what **support, constraints, or conditions** they will apply to a negotiated package.

A useful next step would be to compare, side by side:
1. a negotiated no-closure package,
2. a package with hard capacity reduction,
3. and the expected costs, risks, and employment effects of each.
