I’ll be direct.

## To management
Your own economist has confirmed what we have been saying: **you have not yet proved that closures are necessary**. You have proved that the company needs a real restructuring, yes. But “real restructuring” does **not** automatically mean plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

What I accept:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary and limited wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- training,
- contractor and external spend reduction,
- internal consolidation,
- and, if needed, targeted capacity reduction.

What I do **not** accept:
- German plant closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as a bargaining tool,
- or a restructuring that guts regional employment while calling itself competitiveness.

Your own finance modeler made the key point: **the right question is site-by-site economics**. Good. Then bring us that analysis in full, for every threatened site:
1. current utilization,
2. fixed overhead,
3. product overlap,
4. transfer cost,
5. one-time restructuring cost,
6. savings from negotiated measures,
7. residual gap,
8. and why closure is superior to a negotiated alternative.

Until then, closure is not “necessary”; it is **a threat**.

And I will also reject the attempt to turn “durable reset” into a euphemism for permanent job insecurity. A binding job-security framework is not a luxury. It is what makes concessions politically and socially legitimate.

## To the works council and union
I agree with your central line: **no closures, no forced redundancies, no one-sided burden shifting**. That remains the correct starting point.

I also agree that the workforce should not be asked to pay for management failure by surrendering sites first and asking questions later.

Where I will sharpen the line is this: if we are open to temporary concessions, they must be tied to **real protections** and **real investment commitments**:
- no German plant closures during the negotiated period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of co-determination,
- time-limited employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and visible owner support for future products and investment.

I will not support a “temporary” package that leaves the closure weapon permanently loaded.

## To the family holding
You are right that value destruction from a badly executed restructuring would be serious. But value destruction also comes from **breaking trust, industrial peace, and regional legitimacy**.

So I agree with you on one thing: the company needs hard numbers, not slogans. But I disagree if “hard numbers” are used to justify keeping closures as the default fallback before alternatives are genuinely exhausted.

Your role as owner should be to support a settlement that:
- restores competitiveness,
- protects the industrial base,
- and does not convert the region into a mere cost variable.

That means backing a negotiated restructuring **first**, with closures only if a site-by-site analysis proves that no other path works.

## To the investors
You are correct that cosmetic peace is not enough. A weak deal that postpones the problem would be irresponsible.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible restructuring is one that keeps closures and forced layoffs in constant play. Credibility is not the same as severity. It is the ability to produce a package that is:
- economically sufficient,
- operationally executable,
- and socially sustainable.

Your own model shows that a **partial capacity reduction** is often the best compromise. That is where I see the real opening:
- hard capacity adjustment,
- but not automatic site closure,
- and not forced redundancies as a first answer.

That is the path I would consider credible.

## My position, plainly
The state can support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A quantified alternative package must be tested first**
4. **Employee contributions must be real but limited and time-bound**
5. **Management and owners must also contribute visibly**
6. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

I am open to:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labor-cost moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- and targeted productivity measures.

I am **not** open to:
- a restructuring that guts regional employment,
- plant closures presented as the first resort,
- or a deal that strips the state and workforce of meaningful influence.

## What I want next
Management should return with a **site-by-site comparison** of:
- no-closure package,
- partial capacity reduction,
- and closure options,

including:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- employment impact,
- utilization impact,
- and investment requirements.

But let me be clear on the political bottom line:  
**until a site is shown to be truly non-viable, I will not support closure or forced layoffs.**  
And I will use the rights attached to our stake and statute to prevent a bad outcome.
