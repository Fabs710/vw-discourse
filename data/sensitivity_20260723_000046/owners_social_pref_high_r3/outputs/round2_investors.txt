We are aligned on the **diagnosis** and on the need for a **durable** solution. We are **not** aligned on the standards for what counts as durable.

From the capital markets’ perspective, the central issue is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins and fix the cost base, or does it merely postpone the problem?** On that question, we are still not satisfied.

## Where we agree
- The company’s German cost base is too high.
- Capacity is too large for the market environment.
- A cosmetic settlement would destroy value.
- Prolonged conflict and strikes are also value-destructive.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, training, and targeted labour-cost moderation all belong in the mix.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be the starting point
To the Works Council, Union, and State: we understand the social and political red lines. But as shareholders, we cannot endorse an outcome that effectively rules out all hard capacity reduction before the economics are tested.

If some sites are structurally non-viable, then **pretending otherwise is not job protection; it is value destruction**. We do not support closure as a default or a threat for its own sake, but we also do not support an upfront veto on any closure regardless of economics.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is only acceptable if the numbers still work
We are open to avoiding forced layoffs **if** the package delivers real and measurable savings through other means. But that condition has to be proven, not assumed.

So far, we have heard a list of instruments, not a quantified plan. We need to see:
- the savings target,
- the timing,
- the capacity reduction achieved,
- and the residual gap after voluntary and negotiated measures.

If that gap remains material, then simply saying “no forced redundancies” is not a financially credible end-state.

### 3) Blanket job guarantees through 2030 are too rigid
To labour and the state: we understand the need for security, but **blanket guarantees can freeze the very restructuring needed to save the company**.

What we can support is:
- site and job security where economically viable,
- time-limited protections tied to defined concessions,
- and a clear link between employee contributions and future investment.

What we cannot support is a structure that locks in today’s cost and capacity problem until 2030 while margins continue to erode.

### 4) Management still needs to prove the case better
To management: you are right that hard measures may be necessary. But “closures remain on the table” is not yet a sufficient investment case.

You need to present a **quantified comparison**:
- what savings are needed,
- what can be achieved through negotiated instruments,
- what remains uncovered,
- and why any proposed closure is economically superior to the alternative.

Without that, we will not support irreversible measures either.

## What would move us
We would support a package that includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified savings target** with timing and implementation milestones.
2. **Real labour-cost reduction**, not symbolic concessions.
3. **Real capacity reduction**, whether through closures, consolidation, or equivalent hard cuts.
4. **A meaningful employee contribution** that is limited, durable, and negotiated.
5. **Investment commitments** to future products and technology.
6. **Enough deal certainty** that markets can believe the company has actually changed.

## Our bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution, but not to a settlement that preserves the current structure and calls that competitiveness.

- If negotiated instruments can truly deliver the required savings, we can support them.
- If they cannot, then hard capacity reduction, including closures, cannot simply be ruled out.
- What we will not support is a half-measure that leaves the company structurally impaired and margins still below target.

Management should come back with numbers, not generalities.  
Until then, we remain supportive of **real restructuring**, but not of **cosmetic peace**.
