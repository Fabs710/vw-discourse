As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden in this transformation. If management wants a solution, it must be a negotiated one that protects jobs, preserves every German site, and gives binding security for the future. Any restructuring must be based on:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- exclusion of forced layoffs,
- a real role for co-determination in shaping the transformation,
- and a fair distribution of the burden that does not load the crisis onto employees alone.

We are prepared to discuss alternative measures that avoid closures and forced layoffs: capacity adjustments, shifts in working time, internal reallocation, training, voluntary departures, and sensible efficiency measures. But there must be no unilateral fait accompli, no breaking of trust, and no dismantling of the social contract that has supported this company for decades.

Management cannot demand loyalty from the workforce while threatening its livelihood. If the company needs a future, it must be built with the employees, not against them.
