We share the core line just stated by the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral breaking of the collective framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade-union side: if management expects the workforce to contribute, then that contribution must be **limited, negotiated, and matched by contributions from owners and management**. We are not prepared to let employees become the sole shock absorber for years of strategic missteps, market pressure, and investment decisions made elsewhere.

What we are prepared to discuss is a **shared-sacrifice package** that can stabilize the company without destroying jobs and industrial capacity, for example:
- temporary or targeted wage moderation,
- suspension or reduction of bonuses,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal flexibility and redeployment,
- voluntary exits only, not forced layoffs,
- training and qualification measures for the transition,
- capacity reduction through negotiated instruments rather than closures.

What we will **not** accept:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- open-ended insecurity disguised as “flexibility,”
- a cost-cutting program that loads all pain onto employees while ownership and top management stay protected.

If management’s position is simply “accept layoffs and closures or else,” then there is no basis for a settlement. In that case, the workforce will not stand still. The peace obligation has ended, and we have the means to escalate.

If, however, management is serious about an alternative, then it must put something concrete on the table:
- binding job protection,
- binding site security,
- a real commitment to future investment,
- and a fair burden-sharing model that includes all stakeholders.

We are ready to bargain hard, but not to surrender the workforce’s interests.
