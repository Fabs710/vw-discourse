I hear the red lines you have set, but I need to be equally clear about ours.

We are not in a normal bargaining round. We are facing a structural competitiveness crisis: German factory costs are materially above target, capacity is too high for the market we now face, and the brand is missing its return-on-sales goal. If we do not create a durable cost and capacity reset now, we will simply postpone the next crisis and weaken the company further.

So let me respond directly:

- **On plant closures:** I am not ruling them out. If we cannot achieve a sustainable reduction in overcapacity and fixed costs through other measures, then closures remain on the table. A cosmetic arrangement that preserves every site regardless of economics is not an answer.
- **On compulsory redundancies:** I prefer to avoid them, but I will not accept a framework that makes real restructuring impossible. The company needs a path to reduce headcount materially.
- **On site guarantees through 2030:** I cannot commit to blanket guarantees for all sites if the numbers do not work. What I can commit to is that future investment will follow competitiveness, and that viable sites with a sustainable role in the production network should have a future.
- **On employee contributions:** Yes, there must be a real labour-cost contribution. Working-time changes, moderation of pay growth, and removal or adjustment of legacy protections are all part of the necessary package. But they must be meaningful, not symbolic.

Where I do agree with you:
- The solution must be negotiated.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and internal flexibility should be used where they genuinely help.
- We need future investment and a credible industrial plan, not just cuts for their own sake.

But I want to be frank: **a proposal that excludes closures, excludes compulsory reductions, and guarantees all sites to 2030 does not meet the company’s needs.** That would leave the cost base structurally uncompetitive and fail to restore margin.

If the employee side wants to avoid the hardest measures, then it needs to bring forward a package that achieves the same economic effect:
- a sizable, durable labour-cost reduction,
- capacity reduction that is real and permanent,
- and enough flexibility to align production with lower European demand.

I am prepared to discuss the form this takes. I am not prepared to negotiate away the objective.
