We are still nowhere near an acceptable basis for agreement.

I acknowledge one point from management, the family holding, and even the investors: the company needs a credible competitiveness plan, not a cosmetic pause. But I reject the idea that “credible” must mean plant closures or compulsory redundancies. That is a false choice, and it is being presented far too quickly as if workers had already agreed to absorb the cost.

### To management
You have still not shown, with numbers, that closures are unavoidable. You speak of overcapacity and cost pressure, but you have not demonstrated why:
- voluntary departures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- reduced use of contractors and external spend,
- and targeted capacity shifts

cannot achieve the same savings without destroying sites and jobs.

You are asking the workforce to accept irreversible damage on the basis of a general diagnosis. That is not enough. If you want us to even discuss deeper changes, you must put a quantified alternative on the table that excludes:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and the dismantling of the existing job-security framework.

Until then, your “hard-nosed” restructuring is just a demand that employees pay for management’s failure.

### To the family holding and investors
I hear your concern about long-term value, and on one point you are right: a destructive confrontation would harm the company. But you are wrong if you think value is protected by threatening closures first and expecting the workforce to fold.

A strategy that breaks trust, triggers industrial conflict, and tears up co-determination is not prudent ownership. It is short-term pressure with long-term damage. If you want a durable solution, then back a negotiated industrial plan that keeps every German site open and secures jobs. Anything else just shifts risk onto workers while claiming it is “discipline.”

### To the state government
We welcome your clear stance against closures and compulsory redundancies. But that stance must be backed by action, not just concern. As a blocking shareholder and political actor, you have leverage. Use it to prevent any restructuring that sacrifices sites as the price of a quick financial fix.

### On concessions
I will be equally clear: the works council is not refusing all change. We are prepared to discuss:
- internal flexibility,
- working-time arrangements,
- training and redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- and limited, negotiated employee contributions

—but only under binding guarantees:
- **no plant closures in Germany,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **job security through 2030,**
- **real co-determination in shaping the restructuring,**
- and **serious investment commitments** for future products and technologies.

Without those guarantees, there is nothing to bargain about. Employees cannot be asked to make “real contributions” while management keeps the option of closing plants and cutting people out of the company.

### What is still missing
If management wants an actual discussion, bring the following:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the exact gap that alternative measures cannot cover,
3. the exact sites allegedly at risk and why,
4. the exact concessions management and ownership will make,
5. and the binding guarantees that make any employee contribution acceptable.

Until then, I will say this plainly: **no closures, no forced layoffs, no surrender of co-determination.**  
If management tries to force the issue, we will continue to use every available means to resist it.
