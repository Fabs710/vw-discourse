As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking long-standing guarantees, and turning entire sites into sacrifice zones. We are not here to rubber-stamp a fait accompli.

Our priorities are:

1. Every German plant must remain open.
2. No forced layoffs.
3. Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
4. A real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the restructuring.
5. Any contribution from employees must be fair, temporary, and tied to secure investment, future products, and long-term site protection.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that reduce costs and capacity without closures and without dismissals:
- working-time adjustments,
- flexible capacity management,
- negotiated contributions,
- efficiency measures,
- and an industrial plan that secures future production in Germany.

But let me be equally clear: if management insists on closures, unilateral cuts, or tearing up the job-security framework, then it will face full resistance from the workforce and from us in co-determination. We have the means and the mandate to block this.

So the burden is on management and the owners to bring forward a solution that protects jobs, protects sites, and gives workers a credible future. Otherwise, there is no basis for agreement.
