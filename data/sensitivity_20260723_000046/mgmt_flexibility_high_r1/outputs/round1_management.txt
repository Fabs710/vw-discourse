I hear the red lines you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic deal that leaves our German cost base structurally uncompetitive is not an option**.

We are in a real crisis, not a theoretical debate. Demand in Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our German factory costs are materially above target. The brand is missing its return-on-sales objective, and unless we fix this now, we will not have the financial room to invest in future products, software, battery technology, and the 2030 portfolio.

So let me respond directly:

- **I agree** that we need a durable solution, not a short-term patch.
- **I agree** that the workforce cannot be expected to carry the entire burden alone.
- **I disagree** that “no closures” can be an absolute line if the result is that the cost and capacity problem remains unresolved.
- **I disagree** that job guarantees through 2030 are compatible with the level of structural change we need, unless they are tied to a real, material contribution on labour cost and capacity.

### On the substance
Plant closures are not a threat for effect. They are on the table because the current structure is too expensive for the market we are facing. If we do nothing decisive, we will lose more ground, not less. That would be worse for the company, worse for investment, and eventually worse for jobs overall.

That said, I am not insisting that closures are the only path. What I am insisting on is this:

1. **A durable reduction in capacity**
2. **A genuine labour-cost contribution**
3. **A package that restores medium-term margins**
4. **Enough flexibility to invest for 2030**

If you can deliver comparable savings through negotiated alternatives — working-time changes, capacity reductions, site-specific restructuring, pay moderation, and verifiable productivity improvements — then that can be discussed. But it must be **comparable** in scale and durability. Not symbolic, not temporary, not reversible at the first sign of pressure.

### On job security
I will not commit to unconditional site guarantees that prevent restructuring. That would simply freeze the problem in place and postpone the crisis. If there is to be security, it has to be earned through a package that meaningfully changes the economics of the business.

### On employee contributions
A fair contribution means more than small savings around the edges. We need real relief on labour cost. That can come through a combination of measures, but it cannot be so limited that it fails to move the company back toward competitiveness.

### On co-determination
I acknowledge the workforce’s role in governance and the need for a negotiated outcome. But co-determination is not a veto on reality. If we cannot get to a deal that materially fixes the cost base, then we will have to continue pursuing the structural options that are available.

So my position is straightforward:

- **I am prepared to negotiate alternatives to closures**
- **I am not prepared to accept a deal that preserves all sites unchanged and leaves the cost base intact**
- **I am open on instruments, not on the need for deep, durable savings**

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that actually removes the same amount of cost and capacity. Otherwise, we are just delaying the inevitable.
