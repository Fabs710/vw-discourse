## Core impasse
There are **two non-overlapping veto lines**:

1. **Works Council / Union / State**: no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.  
2. **Management / Investors / Family**: no agreement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive or fails to deliver durable savings.

The unresolved question is therefore not whether restructuring is needed, but **whether the required savings can be achieved without formal closures or forced layoffs, while still closing the structural cost gap**.

A second, procedural impasse is also visible:
- Management and capital are asking for proof of equivalence from the no-closure camp.
- The employee side is asking management to prove that closures are truly unavoidable.

So the table is stuck on **burden of proof plus acceptable instruments**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “No closure, but permanent capacity reduction inside open sites”
A possible bridge would be:
- keep all German plants legally open,
- but consolidate models, remove shifts, mothball selected lines, and reduce installed capacity materially,
- pair this with voluntary exits, working-time reductions, and overhead cuts,
- and bind the package to measurable savings and volume commitments.

This would aim to give management/capital **real structural savings** while preserving the employee-side red line on formal closures.

---

### Proposal 2: “Time-limited protection in exchange for quantified burden-sharing”
A second bridge would be:
- a hard job-and-site protection period through a defined date,
- in exchange for a quantified employee contribution now,
- plus quantified management/owner contributions on overhead, investment, and governance discipline,
- with automatic review triggers if volume or margin assumptions deviate materially.

This would aim to make the restructuring **durable but phased**, so the workforce does not carry an open-ended burden and management gets enforceable savings.

---

### Proposal 3: “Hybrid package with conditional fallback”
A third bridge would be:
- launch a hybrid no-closure package first,
- set a clear savings target and deadline,
- establish a joint monitoring panel with worker, management, and shareholder representation,
- and define a conditional fallback if the savings gap is not closed by the review date.

This would not force immediate closures, but it would give management and investors a mechanism to avoid endless delay.

### To Management
What exact residual savings gap remains **after** you include voluntary exits, overhead cuts, productivity gains, and reduced shifts — and what specific figure would make a no-closure package acceptable to you?

### To Works Council / Union
What specific combination of capacity reduction, voluntary exits, working-time measures, and productivity commitments would you accept as **proof of durable restructuring** if every German plant remains formally open?

### To State Government
What binding safeguards would you require to support a no-closure restructuring, and what measurable indicators would trigger you to withdraw support if the plan becomes a disguised closure strategy?

### To Controlling Family Holding

### To Institutional Investors
What minimum level of durable savings and implementation certainty would make you prefer a negotiated no-closure hybrid over a closure-led plan?

### To the Expert
