The regional state government takes this crisis very seriously, because this company is not just another listed asset — it is a major industrial anchor for the state, for suppliers, and for thousands of families who depend on it.

I want to be clear at the outset: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys regional employment or turns German sites into expendable parts.** We also will not accept a process in which the state’s role as a legitimate co-determination and blocking shareholder is sidelined.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: the company cannot simply drift forward with an uncompetitive cost base and no credible path to future investment. The transition to electric vehicles, software, and battery technology requires financial strength, and we cannot ignore market realities.

I also agree with the union and works council that the burden cannot fall on the workforce alone. A lasting solution has to be shared and must preserve the company’s industrial base in Germany.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only serious options.

When management says “closures are on the table,” that may be true in a narrow financial sense, but it is not an acceptable starting point for a company of this importance when there are still alternatives to be exhausted. Closing German plants would have consequences far beyond the balance sheet:
- permanent damage to regional employment,
- weakening of supplier networks,
- political and social instability,
- and a loss of trust in the company’s social partnership model.

Nor do I accept the idea that job guarantees through 2030 are automatically incompatible with restructuring. That depends on the package. If management comes forward with a credible plan that achieves comparable savings through **capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, efficiency gains, temporary employee contributions, and investment commitments**, then site and job guarantees are not a fantasy — they are part of the bargain that makes such a plan legitimate and durable.

### What I will support
The state is open to a negotiated restructuring that:
- avoids plant closures where at all possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- secures future product allocation and investment in Germany,
- includes temporary, fair employee contributions,
- and preserves the company’s long-term competitiveness without hollowing out its domestic base.

But I am equally clear on the limits: **a one-sided package that cuts jobs, breaks guarantees, and treats German sites as the adjustment mass will face our opposition.** That is not a responsible industrial policy, and it is not consistent with our role as a long-standing anchor shareholder.

### On governance
We will insist that the co-determination structures are respected. The workforce has an effective veto for a reason, and the state’s blocking-minority position under the special statute is not ceremonial. Any solution must be negotiated, not imposed.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to support a tough restructuring if — and only if — it protects the industrial core in Germany, preserves meaningful employment, and delivers durable competitiveness through a fair burden-sharing arrangement.

Management should come back with a real alternative to closures, not a demand that everyone else absorb the pain while ownership and strategy remain untouched.
