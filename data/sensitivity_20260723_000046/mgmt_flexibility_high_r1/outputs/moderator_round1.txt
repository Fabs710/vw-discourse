### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides recognize that the company faces a serious competitiveness and profitability problem.
- There is broad agreement that a purely cosmetic deal is unacceptable.
- There is also broad agreement that prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging.
- All parties accept, at least in principle, that a negotiated solution is preferable to unilateral action.
- Several sides expressed openness to alternatives to closures, including:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary labour-cost relief,
  - capacity adjustments,
  - productivity measures,
  - and investment commitments tied to any employee contribution.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state reject them as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state reject them; management does not rule out restructuring outcomes that could imply workforce reduction.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:** Employee side wants binding guarantees; management says unconditional guarantees are incompatible with the scale of restructuring needed.
- **Size and permanence of employee contributions:** Management and capital providers want material, durable savings; employee side says contributions must be fair, temporary, and matched by security and investment.
- **Governance/co-determination:** Employee side and state emphasize strong participation and blocking rights; management insists co-determination cannot amount to a veto on necessary change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- Whether the required savings can be achieved without closures and without forced layoffs.
- How to quantify a “comparable” alternative package if closures are avoided.
- What mix of labour-cost relief, capacity reduction, and investment commitments would be sufficient.
- Whether any site/job protection can be made conditional, partial, or phased rather than absolute.
- How to balance short-term pain, long-term investment capacity, and labour peace.
- What enforcement mechanism would make any negotiated alternative credible and durable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from red lines to **numbers and comparators**:
  - What exact savings are needed?
  - What exact measures can deliver them?
  - Over what time horizon?
- Ask each side to specify its **minimum acceptable package** and its **non-negotiables** in operational terms.
- Test whether a no-closure solution can match the economic effect of closure-based restructuring.
- Clarify what form of job/site security is possible if employees accept measurable contributions.
- Keep the next round centered on a single question: **Which package delivers comparable competitiveness gains with the least destructive social and governance cost?**
