We are moving now from generalities to the decisive question: **can management deliver the needed savings without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies?** From the Works Council’s perspective, the answer must be yes — otherwise this is not a restructuring plan, it is a social demolition plan.

I will respond directly to each side.

### To management
You keep repeating “structural overcapacity” and “durable savings,” but you have not demonstrated that closures are the only way to achieve them. That is the central weakness in your case.

If you want to claim that no-closure alternatives are insufficient, then put the figures on the table:
- how much cost reduction is required,
- how much comes from labour,
- how much from capacity,
- how much from management and overhead,
- and what specific shortfall remains after negotiated measures.

Until then, “closures are on the table” is not a plan; it is pressure.

And let me be very clear: **we do not accept a deal that destroys plant security and then calls that “durable.”** A durable solution must last because it is economically sound *and* socially legitimate. A package imposed through fear, strikes, and broken guarantees is not durable — it is unstable.

### To the family holding and institutional investors
You both talk about value, margins, and competitiveness. Fine. But value is not created by blowing up the company’s industrial base and forcing a confrontation with the workforce.

If you support closures in principle, you are effectively saying that German plants and German jobs are expendable whenever management declares them inconvenient. That is not stewardship. It is short-termism dressed up as discipline.

You say you do not want prolonged conflict. Then stop backing a strategy that predictably causes it. A shutdown threat against German sites is exactly what triggers resistance, distrust, and escalation. If you want stability, back a negotiated alternative with binding guarantees.

### To the state government
I appreciate that you have said closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable. That is the right starting point. But “we are open to restructuring” must not become a back door to eroding the job-security framework.

The state’s responsibility is not just abstract industrial policy. It is also regional employment, supplier stability, and trust in the company’s social compact. If this company breaks that compact, the damage will outlast any quarterly improvement.

### What we can discuss
We are not refusing responsibility. We are willing to discuss:
- temporary working-time adjustments,
- negotiated flexibility across sites,
- phased capacity adjustments through attrition and redeployment,
- fair and temporary wage moderation,
- voluntary offers,
- training and qualification measures,
- and an industrial plan that secures future model allocation in Germany.

But these only make sense if they come with **binding protections**:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
4. **A real role for co-determination in shaping implementation**
5. **Investment and product commitments for German sites**

### What we are not moving on
Let me state the non-negotiables again because there is no ambiguity:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **No tearing up of the historic job-security framework**
- **No restructuring by ultimatum**

If management cannot achieve the required savings within those limits, then the burden is not on workers to accept sacrifice; the burden is on management and the owners to rethink the strategy.

### On the numbers question
I agree with the moderator that numbers matter. So here is the real test for management and capital:

- Show us a package of **comparable savings** that avoids closures.
- Show us that it is durable through 2030.
- Show us that it does not rely on permanent insecurity.
- Show us that German production remains viable, not merely “reduced.”

If you cannot do that, then you are not presenting an alternative — you are just repackaging the same unacceptable plan.

### Bottom line
The Works Council will not endorse any restructuring that includes plant closures or compulsory redundancies. We are ready to negotiate serious alternative measures, but only within a framework that protects jobs, sites, and co-determination.

If the others want agreement, they need to stop treating German plants and workers as the adjustment variable. That is the line.
