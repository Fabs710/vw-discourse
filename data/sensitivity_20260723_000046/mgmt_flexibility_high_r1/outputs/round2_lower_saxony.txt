I will be very clear.

### To management
I agree that the company cannot continue with a structurally uncompetitive cost base. I also agree that this must be a durable solution, not a temporary patch.

But I **do not accept** your framing that closures are the necessary default and that site protection is somehow incompatible with competitiveness. That is too one-sided for a company of this importance in our state. You have not shown that every serious alternative has been exhausted.

If you want my support, then bring a package with:
- **hard numbers** on the savings gap,
- a clear split between savings from labour, management, overhead, and capacity,
- and a credible explanation of why a negotiated alternative cannot close the gap.

Until then, “closures are on the table” is leverage, not a solution.

### To the Works Council and the Union
I agree with your core position that **mass job destruction, compulsory redundancies, and a social dump are unacceptable**.

I also agree that the workforce should not be the only one contributing. There must be a fair burden-sharing arrangement, and it must include management and ownership, not just employees.

Where I am more cautious than you is on absolutes. I support strong protection for jobs and sites, but I will not pretend that every existing structure can remain unchanged if the economic reality truly requires adjustment. What I can support is a **negotiated restructuring that avoids closures and forced layoffs** while still delivering real savings through:
- temporary working-time measures,
- capacity consolidation without site shutdowns where possible,
- voluntary instruments,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a time-limited employee contribution tied to investment and future production commitments.

But this only works if it is tied to **binding guarantees**:
1. no compulsory redundancies,
2. no German plant closures,
3. real investment commitments for our sites,
4. and preservation of the co-determination role.

That is not a cosmetic demand. It is what makes any contribution politically and socially legitimate.

### To the family holding and institutional investors
I understand your insistence on long-term value and margins. That is legitimate. But value is not protected by triggering an avoidable rupture with the workforce and the region.

You are right that a symbolic compromise is worthless. You are **not** right if you assume that closures are the cleanest or best answer regardless of the governance and political consequences. In this company, those consequences matter because they determine whether a restructuring can actually be implemented and sustained.

A package that cannot pass the supervisory board, provokes a strike, and damages the company’s social legitimacy is not a strong value case. It is a weak one.

### My position
The state government will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** if it meets these conditions:

- **protects German employment as far as possible**
- **avoids plant closures**
- **avoids compulsory redundancies**
- **preserves the company’s role as a regional anchor**
- **delivers real, durable competitiveness gains**
- **includes fair contributions from all sides**
- **respects co-determination and our veto rights under the special statute**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- a one-sided closure strategy,
- a package that gutted regional employment,
- or any attempt to sideline the state’s role or the workforce’s co-determination.

That is not an acceptable route for a company this central to our region.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to back a tough deal — but it must be a **socially responsible restructuring**, not a demolition plan.

If management and capital want our support, they need to come back with a credible no-closure alternative that delivers real savings, real investment, and real security for the state’s industrial base. If they cannot, then they should not expect us to lend political or governance cover to plant closures in Germany.
