## Core impasse

The central blockage is this:

- **Management / investors / family holding** are asking for **durable, structural savings** and are not convinced that open-site measures alone close the gap.
- **Works council / union** will not accept **German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or erosion of job-security and co-determination**.
- **State government** wants **no closures and no forced layoffs**, but also needs a solution that is economically credible.
- The result is a stalemate between **economic sufficiency** and **governance/social acceptability**.

In short:  
**the sides disagree not only on the instrument, but on what counts as a sufficient restructuring outcome.**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Open-site restructuring with binding capacity reduction
A compromise could be built around:

- no formal German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- reduced shifts and line consolidation inside open sites,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- overhead cuts and productivity commitments,
- and a binding implementation package with plant-level targets.

**Bridge function:**  
This addresses the employee-side red lines while giving management/investors a real capacity-reduction mechanism.

**Key condition:**  
It must be quantified plant by plant, with a defined residual gap and enforcement milestones through 2030.

---

### 2) Time-limited protection in exchange for quantified burden-sharing
A second compromise could be:

- a fixed-term no-closure / no-forced-layoff commitment,
- employee-side wage moderation or working-time measures for a defined period,
- matching management and owner contributions,
- and a hard review point based on agreed operational metrics.

**Bridge function:**  
This gives management durability and employees time-bound security.

**Key condition:**  
The review mechanism must not function as a disguised closure trigger without new negotiation.

---

### 3) Hybrid package with no-closure first, fallback only by renewed agreement
A third option could be:

- implement the no-closure package first,
- monitor savings and utilization against agreed benchmarks,
- if the gap remains material, trigger a second-stage negotiation rather than an automatic closure fallback.

**Bridge function:**  
This may reduce mistrust by removing immediate closure pressure while preserving a path for later correction if the economics fail.

**Key condition:**  
Any second-stage step would require a fresh negotiated mandate, not a unilateral fallback.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact residual savings gap remains after you fully count voluntary exits, reduced shifts, overhead cuts, and productivity gains — by plant — and what portion of that gap is specifically tied to fixed overcapacity rather than labor cost?**

### To works council / union
**If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are non-negotiable, what specific capacity reductions and labor contributions would you accept that are large enough to make the restructuring economically credible?**

### To the state government
**Will you support a binding open-site restructuring package only if it includes measurable capacity reduction and labor-cost relief, even when it stops short of formal closures?**

### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to prioritize an implementable negotiated solution over a closure-led solution if the negotiated package can be shown to deliver comparable durable savings and lower execution risk?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum quantified improvement in margins and fixed-cost reduction would make a no-closure or hybrid package acceptable to you as an investment outcome?**
