## To management

Where I disagree is the way you keep using “durable” to justify making **German plant closures and forced redundancies** the baseline option. That is not proof. It is pressure.

You still have not answered the central question in a credible, plant-level way:
- What is the residual savings gap **after** voluntary exits, reduced shifts, overhead cuts, productivity gains, and temporary working-time measures?
- How much of the remaining gap is truly fixed-capacity overhang, and how much is a labour-cost problem?
- Why does that residual gap require closures rather than deep but open-site restructuring?

Until you show that clearly, closure is **your preference**, not a necessity.

## To the family holding and investors
I agree with you that implementation matters and that a deadlocked plan destroys value.

- co-determination veto risk,
- strike escalation,
- political resistance,
- prolonged execution delay,
- and value destruction before any savings arrive.

So if you care about value, you should not back the most confrontational tool unless it is truly unavoidable.

What would actually create value is a **negotiated structural downsizing without closures**:
- reduced shifts,
- model consolidation inside sites,
- voluntary exits,
- overhead cuts,
- productivity commitments,
- fair employee contributions,
- and binding investment in German plants.

That is the path with the best chance of being implemented.

## On the moderator’s bridge ideas
### 1) “No closure, but permanent capacity reduction inside open sites”
This is the only bridge worth discussing seriously.

But it must be honest:
- no closure in fact,
- no forced redundancies,
- no plant hollowing-out,
- no stealth transfer of production out of Germany,
- and no undermining of the collective framework.

If management wants capacity reduction, then it must be done through **open-site restructuring**, not by turning plants into shells and calling that compromise.

### 2) “Time-limited protection in exchange for quantified burden-sharing”
Yes — a **time-limited employee contribution** is discussable.

But time-limited must mean exactly that:
- temporary labour-cost relief,
- clear end date,
- measurable management and owner contributions,
- and binding site/job protection in return.

We are **not** trading away security for vague promises.

### 3) “Hybrid package with conditional fallback”
This is where I disagree most strongly with the moderator’s framing.

A “fallback” to closures is not neutral. It is a coercive threat that shifts leverage onto workers and makes the whole process less trustworthy.

If you want a deal, then the deal must stand on its own. We are not signing up to a process where concessions are extracted now and the closure threat is reopened later.
