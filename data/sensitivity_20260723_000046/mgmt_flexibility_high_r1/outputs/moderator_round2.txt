## Moderator Summary

### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company needs **durable cost reduction** and **some form of restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that a **cosmetic or symbolic compromise is not acceptable**.
- Most participants accept that **prolonged conflict, strike risk, and supervisory-board deadlock would destroy value**.
- There is shared openness to **negotiated measures** such as:
  - temporary working-time adjustments,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - wage moderation or bonus restraint,
  - productivity and overhead measures,
  - and investment/product commitments for German sites.
- The expert assessment agrees that a **hybrid negotiated solution** is the most plausible path if it can be made sufficiently substantial and durable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**:  
  - Works Council, Union, and State Government reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management says closures may be necessary if equivalent savings cannot otherwise be achieved.  
  - Investors and the family holding are open to either path if it is economically credible and implementable.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee side and State Government reject them.  
  - Management has not fully committed against them in principle, though it prefers negotiated alternatives.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030**:  
  - Employee side and State Government want binding guarantees.  
  - Management and shareholders argue these cannot be unconditional if restructuring must remain adaptable.
- **Whether a no-closure package can be economically equivalent**:  
  - Employee side says yes, if burden-sharing is fair and binding.  
  - Management and investors say only if it produces comparable, durable savings and capacity reduction.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **exact savings gap** has not been quantified in shared numbers.
- There is still no agreed split between savings from:
  - labour,
  - capacity,
  - management/overhead,
  - and productivity.
- It remains unresolved **how much structural capacity reduction is needed** if closures are excluded.
- The parties have not yet reconciled:
  - **durable cost reduction**
  - with **job and site protection**
  - under the existing **co-determination and veto constraints**.
- There is no agreed answer yet on whether **temporary concessions** can be converted into a **lasting competitiveness improvement**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Please move from positions to a **quantified comparison of options**:
  1. closure-led restructuring,
  2. no-closure negotiated package,
  3. hybrid compromise.
- For each option, specify:
  - expected savings,
  - duration of effect,
  - impact on capacity,
  - implementation risk,
  - and governance feasibility.
- The key question for the next round should be:  
  **Which package is both economically sufficient and realistically implementable under the current governance structure?**
