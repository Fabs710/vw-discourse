Let me respond directly, because the discussion has now become more concrete.

## My position remains clear
The union will **not** accept:
- **plant closures in Germany**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **a dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **a restructuring where workers carry the main burden while management and owners remain protected**

That has not changed, and it will not change just because the word “durable” is repeated often.

## Where I agree
I do agree with three points that have been made:
1. **The company needs real restructuring.**
2. **A cosmetic compromise would be worthless.**
3. **Prolonged conflict would damage everyone.**

So no, we are not denying the problem.

I also agree with the expert’s basic conclusion: a temporary package alone is unlikely to solve a structural issue. But that does **not** mean plant closures are the only serious answer. It means the package has to be **structural, fair, and enforceable**.

## Where I disagree
### 1) To management
You keep trying to make closures the benchmark, and then asking us to prove a no-closure alternative is equivalent. That reverses the burden.

If you claim closures are necessary, then **you** must show:
- the exact savings gap,
- the exact shortfall after labor, management, overhead, and productivity measures,
- and why a negotiated structural downsizing cannot close it.

So far, I have not seen that proof. I have seen pressure, not proof.

And I want to be blunt: a plan that depends on the threat of closures to force workers into concessions is not a strong restructuring plan. It is a bargaining tactic.

### 2) To the family holding and investors
You say you are open to either path if it is economically credible. Fine. Then do not hide behind “implementation certainty” while backing a strategy that is highly likely to trigger deadlock and strike escalation.

The expert’s assessment matters: **closure-led restructuring has high execution risk** in this governance structure. That means it is not automatically the rational choice. It may look clean on paper and fail in reality.

If you care about value, then back the path that can actually be executed:
- negotiated capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time adjustments,
- fair employee contributions,
- and binding investment and product commitments.

### 3) To the state government
You are right to reject closures and compulsory redundancies. But I need you to be more than a polite observer.

If the state really stands for regional stability, then it must not allow “restructuring” to become a code word for:
- hollowing out German sites,
- weakening job security,
- and shifting risk onto the workforce.

Your role is to insist on a real industrial compromise, not to bless a one-sided reset.

## On the expert’s analysis
The expert is correct that:
- temporary concessions alone are not enough,
- and a hybrid package is most plausible.

But I want to emphasize the other half of that analysis: **a no-closure package can work if it includes real structural measures**.

That means:
- durable labour-cost relief,
- substantial voluntary exits,
- reduced shifts or reduced-model operation,
- overhead cuts,
- productivity commitments,
- and genuine capacity reduction **without** plant closures.

That is the route we can discuss. Not symbolic cuts, not temporary window dressing, and not closures by another name.

## What a fair package would look like
We remain willing to negotiate a serious contribution from the workforce, but it must be matched by fairness from the other side.

A credible package would need all of the following:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site protection through 2030**
4. **Temporary and measurable labour-cost moderation**
5. **Voluntary exit and retirement pathways**
6. **Reduced shifts / capacity adjustment where needed**
7. **Management and owner contributions to overhead and restructuring costs**
8. **Investment and product allocation for German sites**
9. **A clear end date for employee sacrifice**
10. **A co-determined implementation process**

That is burden-sharing. Anything less is workers paying the bill for everyone else.

## What I will not accept
I want this recorded plainly:
- Not plant closures.
- Not forced layoffs.
- Not a deal where “capacity reduction” is just a euphemism for site shutdown.
- Not a permanent erosion of wages and conditions.
- Not a settlement that says labor peace is purchased by making employees absorb the whole shock.

## What would move us
We can move if management and capital come back with a package that:
- quantifies the savings gap honestly,
- shows how much can be delivered without closures,
- includes serious management and ownership contributions,
- and protects jobs and sites in binding form.

If that is on the table, we will negotiate hard but constructively.

If instead the message is “accept closures or accept worse,” then you should expect resistance, including industrial action. We have not used that leverage yet for decoration.

## Final point
A durable settlement is not one where workers are weakened so that management can claim victory. A durable settlement is one where the company becomes viable **without destroying its social foundation**.

That is still the line.
