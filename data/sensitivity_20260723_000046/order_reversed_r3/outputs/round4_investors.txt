We agree with the moderator and the expert on one central point: **the company needs a durable, quantified reduction in cost and capacity, not a cosmetic settlement.** That is the only standard we care about as capital-market shareholders.

Where we **agree**
- **With management:** the status quo is uncompetitive and the current cost base must come down.
- **With the expert:** non-closure measures can work only if they remove enough fixed cost and capacity, quickly enough, and with real enforcement.
- **With the family holding:** this must be a real restructuring, not a symbolic compromise.
- **With the state, union, and works council:** a destructive strike or a settlement that poisons implementation would destroy value and is therefore unacceptable.

Where we **disagree**
- **With labor and the state:** you keep speaking as if “no closures” is itself a solution. It is not. It is only acceptable if you can show, in numbers, that the alternative is economically equivalent.
- **With management, if it is still treating closures as the first answer rather than the last resort:** you have not yet proven that closures are necessary. A threat is not a proof.
- **With anyone offering open-ended guarantees:** if the package freezes the cost base or blocks meaningful adjustment, it will fail shareholders and the market.

## Our position on the current compromise paths

### 1) Phased no-closure restructuring with a strict trigger
This is the **best path so far**, but only if the trigger is objective, pre-agreed, and enforceable.

We would support:
- upfront savings milestones,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and an automatic fallback if targets are missed.

What we will **not** support:
- a review mechanism that becomes another round of bargaining,
- or a fallback that is politically too weak to ever happen.

If the backstop cannot actually be activated, it is worthless.

### 2) Site-preservation package with materially larger employee and management contributions
Potentially acceptable, but only if the savings are large enough and recurring.

The burden must not fall only on labor. We would want to see:
- management restraint,
- owner backing,
- and binding productivity / overhead reductions,
alongside employee concessions.

If “site preservation” simply means preserving the full legacy cost structure, we reject it.

### 3) Capacity reduction without formal plant closure
This is credible **only if it is economically equivalent to closure**, not just politically more comfortable.

That means:
- fewer shifts,
- line consolidation,
- site specialization,
- indirect cost cuts,
- and measurable headcount reduction.

If the company keeps every site open while failing to remove enough fixed cost, then the market will correctly view it as delay.

## What would move us
We would support a no-closure deal only if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified savings bridge**
   - by year, recurring, and linked to margin restoration.

2. **Real capacity reduction**
   - not just productivity slogans.

3. **A durable labor-cost reset**
   - wage restraint, bonus waivers, flexibility, voluntary exits, or equivalent.

4. **An enforceable milestone system**
   - with independent verification.

5. **A genuine fallback**
   - automatic enough to be credible, not merely symbolic.

6. **Proof that the package restores competitiveness quickly**
   - near- and medium-term, not someday.

## What will not move us
We will not support:
- permanent job-security language that leaves the cost base structurally impaired,
- a compromise that avoids conflict only by postponing the crisis,
- or a package that asks capital markets to fund an uncompetitive structure indefinitely.

## Direct answer to the parties

### To management
Bring the bridge. If the non-closure package closes the gap, we can support it. If it does not, then you must say plainly why.

### To the union and works council
If you want investor support, stop arguing from principle alone. Show the numbers. What exact savings, by when, and with what enforcement? “No closures” is not enough.

### To the state
We respect your regional concerns, but they do not override shareholder economics. Protecting every site is not acceptable if the business remains structurally impaired.

### To the family holding
We need to know whether you will back a real fallback if milestones are missed. Without that, any compromise looks reversible and weak.

## Our bottom line
We are **not demanding closures for their own sake**.

We are demanding:
- a credible return to competitive margins,
- structural and durable cost reduction,
- and a settlement that the market can believe.

If the no-closure route can deliver that, we are open to it.

If it cannot, then closures or closure-equivalent measures remain necessary, however uncomfortable that is for the other stakeholders.
