We agree with the moderator that the deadlock is now very clear: **non-closure measures may be acceptable only if they are economically equivalent in a way that is measurable, durable, and enforceable.** That remains our test.

## To management
We agree with your diagnosis that the current structure is too expensive and that savings must be structural, not symbolic. We also agree that implementation risk matters.

Where we disagree is that you have not yet shown the **residual gap** after the non-closure measures, nor have you demonstrated that closures are the only way to close it.

What we need from you is not rhetoric about “real restructuring,” but a quantified bridge:
- savings required by year,
- savings already achievable without closures,
- remaining gap,
- and the margin impact.

Until that is clear, we cannot tell whether you are asking for a necessary adjustment or simply using closure threats to increase leverage.

## To the union and works council
We understand your red lines, but we do **not** accept a package that preserves the full cost base and then calls that a compromise.

Your problem is not the rejection of closures in principle; your problem is that you still have not put forward a package that proves the same economics can be achieved **without** them.

If you want capital-market support, then the no-closure alternative must include:
- real capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- measurable headcount reduction through attrition/voluntary exits/redeployment,
- and binding enforcement.

What we will **not** support:
- open-ended job guarantees that freeze the cost structure,
- temporary deferrals dressed up as savings,
- or any arrangement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

## To the regional state government
We agree that a shutdown-driven outcome could be value-destructive if it triggers prolonged conflict and political backlash. That is precisely why we are open to a serious no-closure package.

But we will not support the state’s preferred outcome unless it is backed by hard numbers. “Preserve the sites” is not enough if the business remains uncompetitive.

If you want to keep all German sites open, then the package must still deliver:
- hard cost savings,
- capacity reduction,
- and a credible return to target margins.

Otherwise, you are asking shareholders to subsidize underperformance indefinitely.

## To the controlling family holding
We agree that the company needs long-term competitiveness, not a cosmetic settlement. We also agree that value destruction through prolonged conflict must be avoided.

But we need more clarity from you on one point: **would you support an automatic fallback mechanism if the no-closure package misses its savings targets?**

That matters because a non-closure agreement without an enforcement backstop is just optimism. For capital markets, optimism is not a restructuring plan.

A credible compromise could include:
- no immediate closures,
- measurable milestone reviews,
- and a predefined escalation path if targets are missed.

If the family will not support any backstop, then the market will view the package as weak and reversible.

## On the compromise paths
Among the options the moderator laid out, we would view **Option 1** as the most credible **only if** the off-ramp is real and automatic, not politically negotiable after the fact.

That means:
- initial non-closure measures must be quantified up front,
- targets must be strict,
- and if the company misses them, the fallback must be mechanically available.

Option 2 could work only if the employee contribution is large enough to materially move margins, not just symbolically show “shared sacrifice.”

Option 3 is acceptable only if capacity reduction is real enough to change the economics, not just to preserve appearances.

## Our bottom line
We are **not** demanding closures as an article of faith.

We are demanding:
1. **credible savings**
2. **durable margin repair**
3. **enforceable implementation**
4. **a path that restores competitiveness quickly**

If a no-closure package can deliver that, we can support it.

If it cannot, then we will not endorse a settlement that protects every legacy structure while eroding shareholder value. In that case, deeper structural measures remain necessary, whether people like that or not.

## What would move us now
Bring us:
- the exact savings bridge,
- the remaining gap,
- the timing by year,
- the enforcement mechanism,
- and the fallback if targets are missed.

Without that, “no closures” is simply a constraint, not a solution.
