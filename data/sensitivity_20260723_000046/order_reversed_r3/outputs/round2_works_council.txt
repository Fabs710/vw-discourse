Let me be very clear: we are not moving off our core position.

We agree that the status quo is not sustainable. We agree that the company needs a durable solution and real competitiveness. But we do **not** accept the idea that the only “credible” solution is one built on **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and the destruction of the historic job-security framework**.

## To management
You keep repeating that capacity must come out and costs must come down. That is not new information. What is new is that you are asking the workforce to accept irreversible damage in exchange for promises of future competitiveness.

I reject that logic.

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No dismantling of co-determination as a condition for restructuring.**

If you want a serious agreement, then bring a package that proves you can achieve the required savings **without** breaking the social contract that has sustained this company for decades.

And let me be explicit: “capacity reduction” is not a code word that makes closures acceptable. If you cannot reduce capacity through shift changes, product reallocation, working-time flexibility, voluntary instruments, redeployment, and investment-driven volume shifts, then you have not exhausted the alternatives. You have chosen the easiest path for management, not the fairest or most sustainable one.

## To institutional investors
You say you want durable margins and implementable savings. Fine. But your position keeps ignoring a basic reality: a settlement that triggers permanent labour conflict is not “implementable” in any meaningful sense.

A package that destroys trust, provokes strikes, and undermines cooperation at the plant level will not be durable. It will just create the next crisis faster.

So no, we will not treat “share price discipline” as justification for throwing away jobs and sites. If you want real competitiveness, then support a solution that:
- protects the workforce,
- keeps German plants open,
- and secures industrial peace.

Otherwise you are backing a model that may look tough on paper and fail in practice.

## To the controlling family holding
You speak about long-term value. Then act like stewards of a long-term industrial asset, not enforcers of short-term cost-cutting.

Long-term value is not protected by shutting plants and humiliating the workforce. It is protected by:
- maintaining productive capacity,
- preserving skills,
- keeping co-determination intact,
- and securing social legitimacy for the company’s transition.

If the family side supports closures, it will be backing a confrontation that damages the company far beyond the spreadsheet. That is not responsible ownership.

## To the regional state government
I appreciate that you reject closures as the primary instrument. But I need to be equally direct: “preserve core sites as far as possible” is not enough.

We need a **binding commitment** that German plants remain open. We need an explicit guarantee that this restructuring will **not** be used to force compulsory redundancies through the back door.

If the state is serious about being an anchor shareholder, then use your blocking position to defend the industrial base and the jobs in this country. Do not allow “regional responsibility” to become a polite phrase for managed decline.

## To the union
We are aligned on the essential point: **no compulsory redundancies and no closures**.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: we do not need a “maybe if the economics require it” position. We need a firm line, because once the closure logic enters the process, it becomes the weapon used to pressure everyone else into concessions.

The workforce has already shown it will resist. That is not a threat; it is a fact.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing **social destruction**.

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary measures,
- retirement and attrition-based reductions,
- productivity improvements,
- product and investment commitments,
- and a fair employee contribution.

But all of that must happen **inside a binding framework** with:
- **job security to 2030**,
- **no forced layoffs**,
- **no plant closures**,
- and **real co-determination** over the restructuring path.

## What we are not prepared to accept
Let me state the red line again so there is no confusion:

- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of guarantees,
- no restructuring imposed as a fait accompli.

If management insists that those are the price of doing business, then it should not be surprised when the workforce responds with resistance. We have already shown we can mobilize. We will do so again if necessary.

## The burden of proof is on management
You keep asking us to prove there is an alternative. No — **you** have the burden to prove that your preferred path is the only one. Right now, you have not done that.

Until you put a concrete, quantified, enforceable package on the table that achieves the savings target **without closures and without forced layoffs**, your argument remains a demand, not a solution.

So this is our answer:
- If the plan protects every German plant, preserves co-determination, and avoids compulsory redundancies, we will engage seriously on the adjustment instruments.
- If it does not, then we will oppose it with all the means available to us.

We are not here to endorse industrial self-harm. We are here to defend the workforce, the sites, and the future of this company in Germany.
