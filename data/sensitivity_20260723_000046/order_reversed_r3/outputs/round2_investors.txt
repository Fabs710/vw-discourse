We appreciate the clarity from all sides, but our position remains unchanged in the one place that matters: **we will only support a restructuring that delivers a credible, durable improvement in margins and a structurally competitive cost base.** Anything less is just delay with a new label.

### Where we agree
- The **status quo is not viable**.
- A serious package must be **durable, quantified, and implementable**.
- We do not want a prolonged strike or a drawn-out confrontation that destroys value before savings materialize.
- We are open to **multiple instruments** if they actually close the cost gap.

### Where we disagree

#### To the union and works council
We understand your refusal to accept closures and compulsory redundancies. But from a capital-market perspective, **red lines are not a restructuring plan**. If German operations remain structurally overcapacity and the cost base stays above peers, then protecting every site and every legacy guarantee is not a sustainable answer.

What we will not back:
- a settlement that preserves the full cost structure,
- a package that substitutes symbolic concessions for real savings,
- or an outcome that leaves management unable to hit its return-on-sales target.

If you want our support, the alternative to closures must be **economically equivalent**:
- meaningful and enforceable capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- no temporary deferrals,
- and clear proof that the savings are sufficient.

A “socially fair” solution that does not materially fix margins is not value-preserving.

#### To the regional state government
We respect the political constraints, but we cannot endorse a package that makes closures effectively untouchable if they are required by the economics. The market will not reward sentiment if the company continues to underperform.

If closures are avoided, then we need to see:
- **hard numbers** on equivalent savings,
- binding enforcement mechanisms,
- and a timetable that restores competitiveness quickly.

Otherwise, the result is not compromise; it is a transferred burden onto shareholders through lower returns and continued valuation pressure.

#### To management
We support your basic diagnosis: the company needs **structural cost reduction and capacity adjustment**. But the burden of proof is on you to show that the final package is not just tough in rhetoric but tough in execution.

We would ask for:
- quantified net savings,
- timing of savings by year,
- impact on margin and cash flow,
- and a clear link to future investment capacity.

If the package depends on a long period of industrial conflict, that is a serious negative. Deal certainty matters. Savings later are worth less than savings now.

### Our bottom line
We are **not** demanding plant closures as an ideology. We are demanding **results**. If non-closure options can deliver the same structural economics, we can support them. But if the final outcome leaves the German cost base still uncompetitive, then we will not regard that as a successful restructuring — regardless of how many protections were written into it.

### What would move us
A package could gain our support only if it includes all of the following:
1. **Clearly quantified, durable cost savings**
2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
3. **Durable labour-cost adjustment**
4. **A credible implementation path without open-ended conflict**
5. **Evidence that margin restoration is sufficient to support future investment and valuation**

Without those elements, we would oppose it as an expensive postponement of the problem.
