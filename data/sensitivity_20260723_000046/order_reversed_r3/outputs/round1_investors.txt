Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable return to competitive margins** and a **material reduction in the cost base**. We are not interested in a cosmetic compromise that postpones the problem.

What matters to us:

- **Structural cost reduction**, not temporary deferrals.
- **Margin restoration** to a level that supports the group’s investment needs and valuation.
- **Deal certainty** and a path that can actually be implemented.
- **No prolonged industrial conflict** that destroys value in the meantime.

What we would argue for:
- A package that achieves **clear, quantified savings** quickly.
- Meaningful **capacity reduction** if that is necessary to remove chronic overcapacity.
- Labour cost measures that are **durable**, not just one-off concessions.
- A credible link between restructuring and future investment, so the company can compete in EVs and beyond.

What we would argue against:
- Any outcome that leaves the German cost base **structurally uncompetitive**.
- A settlement that preserves all legacy arrangements while claiming victory.
- A drawn-out stand-off that erodes margins, delays action, and weakens the share price.

We are open to different tools — plant consolidation, workforce reduction, working-time changes, pay measures, or a negotiated contribution from employees — but only if the end result is **real competitiveness improvement**. If the proposal does not materially fix the margin problem, we would not support it.
