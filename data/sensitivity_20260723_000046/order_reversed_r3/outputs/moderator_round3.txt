## Core impasse

The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that **cost reduction, capacity reduction, and durable savings are necessary**.

The core impasse is narrower and sharper:

1. **Instrument choice**
   - Management, investors, and the family treat **closures or closure-equivalent measures** as necessary unless proven otherwise.
   - The union, works council, and regional state reject closures as the default and want **site-preserving, no-forced-layoff solutions**.

2. **Proof standard**
   - Capital-side stakeholders require a **quantified savings bridge, enforcement, and fallback**.
   - Labor and the state argue that the company has **not yet proven** that non-closure measures are insufficient.

3. **Credibility of fallback**
   - The capital side sees an automatic fallback as essential.
   - Labor and the state fear that any fallback becomes a **backdoor closure mechanism**.

So the negotiation is stuck on this question:  
**Can a no-closure package deliver the same durable economics as closures, with enforceable safeguards acceptable to both sides?**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the divide

### 1) Phased no-closure restructuring with a strict, pre-agreed trigger
A first phase would implement:
- wage restraint / bonus waivers,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits / retirement-based reductions,
- shift or line consolidation,
- site specialization,
- measured capacity reduction.

These measures would be reviewed against **predefined savings milestones** at fixed dates.  
If milestones are missed, a **pre-agreed second phase** would become available automatically.

Why this may bridge:
- Gives labor and the state a real chance to avoid immediate closures.
- Gives investors, the family, and management a genuine enforcement backstop.

Key design point:
- The trigger must be **objective and mechanically defined**, not reopened for renegotiation.

---

### 2) Site-preservation package with materially larger employee and management contributions
A second path would keep sites open, but only if the package includes:
- a meaningful, time-bound employee contribution,
- visible management/owner contribution,
- binding investment and product allocation to German sites,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- and a credible margin target.

Why this may bridge:
- Addresses labor and state concerns about site survival.
- Addresses capital concerns that the compromise cannot be merely symbolic.

Key design point:
- Contributions must be **balanced and quantified**, not borne by one side only.

---

### 3) Capacity reduction without formal plant closure, but with quantified fixed-cost removal
A third path would focus on:
- shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- product transfers,
- specialization by site,
- indirect-function cuts,
- voluntary headcount reduction.

The condition would be that the package removes enough **fixed cost and capacity** to be economically comparable to a closure case, even if the legal sites remain open.

Why this may bridge:
- Preserves sites and avoids immediate shutdowns.
- Still allows capital-side stakeholders to get real structural savings.

Key design point:
- The savings bridge must show whether this is **closure-equivalent in economics**, not merely in rhetoric.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the quantified savings bridge by year, and what specific residual gap remains after all non-closure measures are applied?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you accept a no-closure package if it is independently verified to close the full recurring savings gap and includes an automatic, pre-agreed fallback?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Would you support a binding fallback mechanism that activates only if hard milestones are missed, even if that means some future structural measures become unavoidable?**

### To the regional state government
**If site preservation is to remain your priority, what level of capacity reduction and labor-cost relief are you prepared to accept as the minimum credible economic tradeoff?**

### To the industrial union
**What exact mix of wage restraint, working-time changes, voluntary exits, and capacity reduction would you accept as sufficient to make a no-closure package credible to ownership and capital markets?**

### To the works council
**Which concrete operational changes at plant level can you support immediately that would reduce cost and capacity without crossing your red lines on closures and forced redundancies?**
