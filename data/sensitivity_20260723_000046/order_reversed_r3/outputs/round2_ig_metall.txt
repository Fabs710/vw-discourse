Let me answer the question directly: **there is a credible alternative to closures and compulsory redundancies — but only if management and the owners are prepared to share the burden in a binding, measurable way.**

I will not pretend that this is easy, and I will not endorse a package that simply repackages layoffs under softer language. But the claim that “no closures means no real restructuring” is false.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the following:
- The current situation is **not sustainable**.
- The company needs **durable savings**.
- Capacity has to be reduced somehow.
- A cosmetic compromise would be useless.
- A prolonged conflict would hurt the company and employees alike.

I also agree with management on one point in particular: **the solution must be implementable**, not just politically convenient.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You keep insisting that closures and forced layoffs are the only credible way to remove enough cost. That is not true. It is one option, but not the only one.

What I do **not** accept is:
- plant closures as a first resort,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a restructuring where employees carry the main burden while management and owners contribute too little.

You say job security cannot be absolute. Fine. But your answer cannot be to dismantle the collective framework and force the workforce to pay for every structural problem in the business.

### To investors and the family holding
You are right that the outcome must be durable and margin-improving. But you are wrong if you treat closures as the benchmark of seriousness.

A serious package can achieve real savings through:
- negotiated capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- partial use of short-time or flexibility instruments,
- a temporary but meaningful employee contribution,
- foregone bonuses or profit-sharing,
- productivity commitments,
- and investment and product allocation tied to the German sites.

That is not cosmetic. That is restructuring with social stability.

### To the state government
I appreciate your resistance to closures and your concern for regional stability. That is exactly why we need a negotiated solution that preserves sites and jobs. But I will also say this clearly: “no closures” is not enough by itself. We need binding guarantees and real burden-sharing from the company side too.

## Our position
Let me state it plainly:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **Protection of the collective wage framework**
- **A fair employee contribution**
- **Binding job and site security**
- **Management and owners must also contribute materially**

That is the minimum for a durable settlement.

## What a real alternative package would have to include
If management wants to avoid closures and forced layoffs, then it must put forward a package with all of the following:

1. **Quantified savings**
   - A clear number, by year, that closes the cost gap.
   - Not aspirations. Not vague efficiency talk.

2. **Capacity reduction without site shutdown**
   - Shift consolidation, product reallocation, line adjustments, and other measures that lower output where needed.
   - But no closure of plants as a bargaining instrument.

3. **A fair employee contribution**
   - We are prepared to discuss wage restraint, bonus waivers, and other labor-cost relief.
   - But this must be **limited, time-bound, and matched by protections**.

4. **No forced redundancies**
   - Reductions through retirement, attrition, redeployment, and voluntary instruments.
   - If management cannot produce a plan without compulsory layoffs, then it has not done its job.

5. **Binding site and investment commitments**
   - Future product allocation,
   - investment in EV and other programs,
   - and a secure future for the German sites.

6. **Management and owner contribution**
   - If the workforce contributes, so must the top. That means executive restraint, internal restructuring, and a visible commitment from shareholders not to externalize all pain onto labour.

## On credibility
Management keeps asking for “credible, enforceable” measures. Good. Then let’s be serious about what credibility looks like.

Credibility is not:
- threatening closures to force submission,
- using the strike threat as a reason to demand more concessions,
- or asking for permanent wage and job erosion.

Credibility is:
- a binding package,
- shared sacrifice,
- and a settlement the workforce can actually carry through without walking out again in six months.

A deal that is imposed against the workforce will not be durable. It will be unstable, contested, and likely lead to the very delay you claim to want to avoid.

## On the savings gap
If management wants a realistic alternative, then bring the figures:
- How much savings are needed?
- What is the exact contribution expected from labour?
- How much can be achieved through capacity adjustments without closures?
- What is management itself willing to give up?
- What guarantees are you prepared to write into the settlement?

Until those numbers are on the table, “closures are necessary” is just a negotiating position, not a proven fact.

## My bottom line
I am **not** shifting from my core line:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and no dismantling of the collective wage framework.

But I am open to a **serious shared-sacrifice package** that includes:
- labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and binding guarantees for jobs, sites, and future investment.

If management and owners are prepared to negotiate that seriously, we can reach a durable settlement. If not, then they should be honest that they are choosing confrontation — because we will not accept a one-sided restructuring that makes workers bear all the pain.

If management wants a concrete labour-side counterproposal, we are ready to discuss one — but only on the basis of **equal burden-sharing and no forced job destruction**.
