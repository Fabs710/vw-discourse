### Core impasse
The deadlock is centered on **how much capacity and cost reduction is required, and whether that can be achieved without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

More specifically:
- **Management / investors / family holding** want a package that is **quantified, durable, enforceable, and sufficient to restore margins**.
- **Union / works council** reject **closures and forced layoffs** as a matter of principle and want **job security and co-determination preserved**.
- **Regional state government** also resists closures and wants the restructuring to protect the regional industrial base.
- The result is a split between:
  - **economic credibility as defined by capital and management**, and
  - **social/territorial protection as defined by labor and the state**.

The unresolved issue is whether **non-closure measures can be made economically equivalent enough** to satisfy investors and management, while still being politically and socially acceptable to labor and the state.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete compromise paths that could be explored:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a conditional closure off-ramp
A package could be structured so that:
- initial savings come from **working-time adjustments, wage restraint, bonus waivers, attrition, redeployment, and product reallocation**;
- a **pre-agreed review point** is built in after a fixed period;
- if savings and capacity targets are missed, then a **narrow, predefined closure or consolidation mechanism** automatically becomes available.

This may bridge:
- labor/state concerns by avoiding immediate closures,
- investor/management concerns by preserving a credible enforcement backstop.

#### 2) Site-preservation package with stronger employee contribution and binding investment commitments
A second path would keep plants open but require:
- **larger, measurable employee cost contributions**,
- **binding productivity and flexibility measures**,
- **investment commitments and model allocation guarantees** to German sites,
- and **hard savings milestones** with monitoring.

This may bridge:
- labor/state demands for site protection,
- investor/management demands for quantified, durable savings.

#### 3) Capacity reduction without formal plant closure
A third compromise is to reduce output structurally without closing sites by using:
- **line consolidation**,
- **shift elimination or rotation changes**,
- **partial site specialization**,
- **voluntary exits and retirement-based headcount reduction**,
- and **temporary but enforceable labor-cost relief**.

This may bridge:
- management’s need to remove capacity and cost,
- labor/state resistance to plant shutdowns.
