**Moderator summary**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the **status quo is not sustainable**.
- There is shared recognition that the company needs **durable cost reduction** and **restored competitiveness**.
- There is broad agreement that any outcome must be **implementable** and avoid a **prolonged destructive conflict**.
- Several parties accept that a viable package may include some mix of:
  - capacity reduction,
  - working-time changes,
  - employee contributions,
  - productivity measures,
  - and future investment commitments.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures / site shutdowns**: Management, capital markets, and the family holding keep them on the table; the state government, union, and works council reject them, especially for German sites.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Labour-side actors reject them; management and investors see workforce reduction as potentially necessary if needed for competitiveness.
- **How to define “credible” restructuring**:  
  - Investors/management want **quantified, structural savings** and margin restoration.  
  - Labour wants **job and site security**, plus shared sacrifice and no one-sided burden shifting.  
  - The state wants competitiveness **without major regional damage**.
- **Who bears the adjustment burden**:  
  - Investors/management emphasize economic necessity.  
  - Labour demands management/ownership contributions and binding protections for employees.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Savings versus employment protection**: How much cost reduction is required, and what level of job/site protection can still be maintained?
- **Durability versus acceptability**: What measures are structurally lasting without triggering a strike or implementation failure?
- **Capacity reduction versus closures**: Whether equivalent savings can be achieved through non-closure instruments, and if so, how they would be enforced.
- **Investment commitments versus concessions**: What future investment, site security, or governance assurances would accompany workforce sacrifices.
- **Regional impact versus group competitiveness**: How to balance margin repair with political and social stability in the affected region.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **specific package design**:
- quantify the savings gap to be closed,
- identify which measures are on the table and which are excluded,
- specify any job, site, or investment guarantees,
- and clarify what each side is prepared to concede in exchange for which protections.

The next round should aim to test whether there is an **equivalent alternative to closures**, or whether the parties remain fundamentally apart on that question.
