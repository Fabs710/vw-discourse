The discussion has reached a clear **core impasse**:

## Core impasse
There is no shared answer to the threshold question:

**Does a no-closure package remove enough fixed cost, capacity, and implementation risk to be economically equivalent to closures?**

- **Capital side, management, and family holding** are not prepared to accept “no closures” unless it is proven equivalent in numbers and enforceability.
- **Union, works council, and state** reject closures as the default instrument and want binding site protection, but have not yet provided a quantified package that conclusively closes the gap.
- The result is a stalemate between **economic credibility** and **site/job preservation**.

A second, related impasse is about **trust in the fallback**:
- Capital and management want an automatic, credible backstop.
- Labor and state fear the backstop is a disguised closure mechanism.
- So the parties disagree not only on the main package, but also on what happens if it fails.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### 1) Verified phased restructuring with a narrow trigger
A structured no-closure package could proceed in phases, with:
- upfront capacity reduction,
- measurable labor-cost concessions,
- management and owner contributions,
- independent verification,
- and a narrowly defined trigger that activates only if agreed milestones are missed.

**Bridge value:**  
This may satisfy investors’ need for credibility while giving labor and the state a genuine chance to avoid immediate closures.

---

### 2) Site-preservation package with quantified equivalence tests
The parties could agree that sites remain open **if and only if** the package meets pre-agreed economic tests:
- recurring savings target,
- fixed-cost reduction target,
- capacity reduction target,
- and timetable for delivery.

If the package clears the tests, closures are off the table; if not, the parties return to the table with the residual gap clearly defined.

**Bridge value:**  
This reframes the dispute from “closure vs. no closure” to “does the package meet the standard?”

---

### 3) Capacity reduction without formal closure, plus binding investment and workforce adjustments
A third path is to combine:
- shift reduction,
- line consolidation,
- product specialization,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and support measures from management/owners,
with binding commitments to future investment and model allocation at German sites.

**Bridge value:**  
This may be the most acceptable route for state, union, and works council while still aiming to satisfy the capital side if the savings are large enough.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### Institutional Investors
**What specific recurring savings, by year, would you consider economically equivalent to closure, and what form of independent verification would make you trust a no-closure package?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a truly automatic fallback if milestones are missed, even if that fallback includes stronger restructuring than the labor side prefers?**

### Regional State Government
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, savings, and binding investment commitments would lead you to support a no-closure settlement despite significant workforce sacrifices?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What is the quantified savings bridge: current gap, savings from non-closure measures, residual gap, and timing—using the same assumptions for both closure and no-closure scenarios?**

### Industrial Union
**What exact package of wage, flexibility, voluntary exit, and capacity measures would you accept as a serious alternative to closures, and what recurring savings would it deliver?**

### Works Council
**Which specific non-closure measures would you endorse at plant level, and what binding guarantees would you require to prevent “capacity reduction” from becoming stealth closure?**

### Expert
**What minimum data set is still missing to judge equivalence objectively, and which side’s assumptions appear most likely to overstate or understate the savings?**
