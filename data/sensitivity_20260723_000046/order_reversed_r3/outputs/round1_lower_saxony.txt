I agree with one core point raised by both capital-market shareholders and the family holding: **the status quo is not an option**. If the German cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, the company’s future, investment capacity, and valuation all suffer.

But I need to be equally clear on the limits from the state’s perspective:

- **We will not support a restructuring that relies on plant closures in the state as the primary instrument.**
- We will not accept a package that **guts regional employment** or treats local sites as disposable.
- And we will not accept being **sidelined** in a decision that has major consequences for the regional economy and social stability.

Where I do agree:
- There must be **real, durable savings**.
- The solution must be **implementable**, not a symbolic gesture.
- The company needs a path that restores competitiveness and preserves its ability to invest in the transition.

Where I disagree:
- The implication that **closures are the default test of seriousness**. They are not. A serious package can also come from:
  - capacity reductions short of closures,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - negotiated employee contributions,
  - productivity improvements,
  - and a credible investment commitment tied to future products and site security.

From our point of view, the only acceptable restructuring is one that:
1. **Preserves core sites and jobs in the state as far as possible**,
2. **Delivers quantified, durable cost relief**,
3. **Maintains social partnership and avoids a destructive conflict**,
4. **Protects the company as a regional anchor**, not just as a short-term margin story.

So I am open to a tough negotiated package, but not to a “closure first” solution. If management wants support, it needs to bring forward an alternative that shows equivalent savings **without permanently damaging the regional industrial base**. If it cannot do that, then I will not be moving toward approval.
