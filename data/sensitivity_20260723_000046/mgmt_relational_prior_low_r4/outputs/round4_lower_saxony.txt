## To management
I agree with you on one point: the company needs **real restructuring**, not symbolism. The expert’s comparison confirms that labor concessions alone are not enough.

But I **do not accept** plant closures or mass compulsory redundancies as the default answer. In this state, that would be economically and politically reckless. It would damage the regional base, provoke conflict, and risk destroying value through delay, strikes, and legal blockage.

If you want support from the state as anchor shareholder, then you must show a plan that:
- preserves a substantial German industrial footprint,
- avoids closures as the first instrument,
- uses voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and overhead cuts,
- and still delivers the required savings.

I am open to tough measures. I am **not** open to a closure-led solution that guts jobs in the region.

## To the works council
I agree with your central red line: **no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a rhetorical position; it is a legitimate protection of the region’s industrial anchor.

But I also have to say this clearly: your answer must be more than “no” to everything. The expert has made clear that a credible non-closure package must be large enough to matter:
- meaningful headcount reduction,
- real fixed-cost removal,
- and capacity or shift reduction.

So I support your resistance to closures, but I need you to engage constructively on a package that actually delivers the savings. Voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and temporary flexibility are the right tools. A blanket refusal to reduce anything would not be credible.

## To the Industrial Union
I agree with you that the workforce cannot be asked to pay alone, and that open-ended concessions are unacceptable.

What I would support is a **time-limited, fair, and quantified** labor contribution in exchange for:
- binding site protection,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- and concrete investment commitments.

What I will **not** support is a scheme where workers give up pay or conditions and management keeps closures in reserve as a threat. That is not social partnership; that is coercion.

## To the controlling family
I understand your concern for value preservation, and I agree that a weak compromise is no solution.

But I disagree with the implication that only a closure-heavy plan qualifies as serious. In this governance setting, execution risk matters enormously. A closure plan that triggers conflict can lose much of its theoretical value.

What I need from you is not just pressure for “material change,” but support for a package that is:
- economically credible,
- socially executable,
- and regionally sustainable.

If the non-closure route can reach the savings thresholds the expert identified, then it is the better value-preserving option. If it cannot, then we should be honest about the gap — but that is not yet a justification for closures.

## To institutional investors
I agree with your insistence on risk-adjusted value. That is the right standard.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that closures are automatically the financially superior route. The expert explicitly warned that strike risk, political blocking, and legal delay can wipe out nominal savings.

So I support a restructuring only if it is:
- large enough,
- fast enough,
- and enforceable enough.

If a negotiated non-closure package can achieve that, then it should be preferred. If not, then we have a problem. But the bar for proving necessity of closures is high, and it has not been met.
