I’ll be direct.

## My position
As dispersed capital-market shareholders, we care about **risk-adjusted value**, not symbolism and not industrial politics for their own sake.

That means we need:
- **durable cost reduction**
- **credible capacity rationalization**
- **visible margin recovery**
- and a plan that is **actually executable** in this governance environment

I am **not** attached to closures as a principle. But I am also **not** willing to support a package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right on the diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and the company cannot fix margins with cosmetic concessions.

I also agree that a weak agreement that simply kicks the can is unacceptable.

### With the expert
The expert’s comparison is the most useful thing in this round.

It makes one point very clear:
- a non-closure package can work,
- but only if it is **big enough** and **fast enough**.

That is exactly the standard I care about. I do not need closures. I need **outcomes**.

---

## Where I disagree

### With the works council
Your position is still too absolute.

You keep saying:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of the job-security framework,
- every German plant must remain open.

That may be politically understandable, but from our perspective it is only acceptable if you can show, with real numbers, that the alternative still restores competitiveness.

Right now, you are asking us to accept a hard constraint **before** proving that the non-closure route can close the economics gap. I am not persuaded.

I also reject the idea that “closures are never necessary” as a default. If a plant-by-plant audit shows the savings cannot be achieved otherwise, then refusing all closures is not responsible stewardship; it is denial.

### With the union
I agree that labor should not bear the burden alone, and I agree that open-ended sacrifice is unacceptable.

But I do **not** accept a view that treats voluntary exits, hours flexibility, temporary moderation, and consolidation as automatically sufficient. The expert has already said those tools may be too small unless they are accompanied by meaningful capacity and fixed-cost reduction.

So if the union wants to rule out closures and forced layoffs, it must still show a package that is economically comparable. So far, that has not been proven.

### With the state
I understand the political need to protect the regional base. I do not dismiss that.

But I do disagree with any framing that makes “no closures” the primary objective regardless of economics. Protecting jobs today by tolerating an uncompetitive structure can destroy more value later. That is not a good outcome for shareholders, the company, or the region.

### With the family
You are right to focus on value preservation and execution risk.

Where I disagree is if “discipline” becomes shorthand for pushing the hardest instrument without proving it is the best one after conflict risk. A closure-heavy plan can have higher nominal savings and still worse realized value if it triggers strikes, political blockage, and delay.

So yes to seriousness, no to assuming closures are automatically the superior answer.

---

## What would move me

A non-closure settlement can absolutely win our support if it meets a clear threshold. Based on the expert’s analysis, I would want to see something like:

- **annual run-rate savings of at least €700m**, preferably closer to **€1bn** if the footprint is large
- **10–15% headcount reduction** in the affected German footprint, achieved mainly through voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and retirement
- **meaningful capacity or shift rationalization**
- **visible cash improvement within 12–24 months**
- and **low execution risk**

If you can show that, then the absence of closures is not a problem. It would actually be preferable if it avoids industrial conflict and protects output.

---

## What would not move me

I would **not** support:
- a symbolic “shared sacrifice” deal that saves too little
- open-ended concessions with vague promises of future efficiency
- a “protected core” that quietly leaves the rest of the footprint to erode
- or a closure-led solution whose supposed savings are eaten by strikes, delays, and transition costs

In other words: I do not care whether the package is called hard or soft. I care whether it **works**.

---

## My response to the proposed tracks

### 1) Locked-in non-closure package with review
This is potentially acceptable **only if** the review is not a backdoor to reopen closures after workers have already given concessions.

If there is a review mechanism, it must be:
- independent,
- time-bound,
- transparent,
- and focused on financial and operational metrics

not on creating leverage for later conflict.

### 2) Protected core with internal consolidation
This is acceptable only if it produces real savings and does not become a way to quietly starve some sites.

“Internal consolidation” has to mean:
- fewer shifts,
- fewer lines,
- lower overhead,
- better utilization,
- and lower fixed cost

not just moving the problem around.

### 3) Time-limited burden-sharing for investment and employment commitments
This is the most promising route, if it is quantified and enforceable.

I would support temporary workforce contributions if they are matched by:
- management and owner contributions,
- real investment commitments,
- and a package that genuinely restores margins

But I will not support a deal where workers give up security and the company gives up nothing durable in return.

---

## My direct message to each party

### To management
Bring a plant-by-plant package that shows:
- how much cost comes out,
- by when,
- through which measures,
- and what the execution risk is

If you cannot prove a non-closure route works at scale, then your insistence on avoiding stronger alternatives is not credible.

### To the works council and union
If you want the market to back a no-closure solution, then show the numbers. Principles alone are not enough.

### To the state
Protecting the regional base is fine. Protecting an uncompetitive structure is not.

### To the family
I support value-preserving restructuring, not simply the bluntest version of it.

---

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back **either**:
- a non-closure restructuring that is genuinely large, durable, and fast enough, **or**
- a limited structural adjustment if that is the only way to get to the required cost base

But I am **not** prepared to back:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a freeze disguised as a deal,
- or an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If nobody has already commissioned one, then I want a **plant-by-plant independent restructuring audit** with a strict comparison of:
- non-closure savings,
- limited closure savings,
- timing,
- execution risk,
- and NPV impact.

That is the level of evidence needed before capital markets should endorse any final settlement.
