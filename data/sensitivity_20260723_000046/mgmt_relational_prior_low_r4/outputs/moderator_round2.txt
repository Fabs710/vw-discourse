## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether the company needs change; all sides now accept that. The impasse is over **the instrument of change**:

- **Employee side** insists on:
  - no plant closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - binding guarantees,
  - and job/security-first restructuring.

- **Management / family / investors** insist on:
  - real structural savings,
  - measurable capacity reduction,
  - and the option of closures and redundancies if needed.

- **State** is trying to hold a middle line:
  - preserve a substantial German industrial base,
  - but still achieve credible restructuring.

So the deadlock is:  
**Who bears the adjustment cost, and how much irreversible action is allowed before guarantees become meaningless?**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Phased restructuring with hard trigger points
A compromise could be a **two-stage agreement**:

- **Stage 1**:
  - voluntary exits,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - temporary employee contributions,
  - management/owner contributions.

- **Stage 2**:
  - only if predefined savings and utilization targets are missed,
  - then a limited second-round discussion on footprint changes begins.

**Key feature:** no immediate closures, but also no permanent veto on structural action.  
This may bridge the gap between the employee side’s demand for protection and management’s demand for credibility.

---

### Proposal 2: Protected core sites with a quantified adjustable perimeter
A second bridge would be to define:

- a **protected core** of strategic German sites/functions,
- and an **adjustable perimeter** of operations where consolidation, shift reduction, or volume transfer may occur.

This would require:
- explicit identification of protected sites,
- explicit savings targets for the perimeter,
- and binding limits on how much adjustment can occur without reopening the agreement.

**Key feature:** the company gets real flexibility; the workforce gets clearer protection than an open-ended restructuring.

---

### Proposal 3: Guarantees tied to measurable workforce contribution and investment commitments
A third possible bridge is an exchange package:

- employees provide:
  - time-limited labor-cost moderation,
  - flexibility,
  - and voluntary reduction instruments;

- management/family provide:
  - binding investment commitments,
  - no forced redundancies during the agreement period,
  - and site-security guarantees for named plants.

This only works if the concessions are:
- quantified,
- time-limited,
- and enforceable.

**Key feature:** security is exchanged for measurable contribution, rather than promised in abstract terms.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific, quantified workforce contribution would you accept if it were paired with binding site guarantees for named German plants and enforceable investment commitments?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which combination of temporary wage moderation, working-time flexibility, and voluntary exit volumes would you consider large enough to substitute for at least part of the footprint reduction management is seeking?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings, by year, would have to be delivered by non-closure measures before you would rule out plant closures in this round?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete form of “protected industrial footprint” would you be willing to support if it still allows selective consolidation or shift reduction at weaker sites?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a binding agreement that excludes closures for a defined period if it delivers independently verified cost-out, capacity reduction, and margin milestones?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**What measurable threshold of cost reduction, capacity rationalization, and margin recovery would make a non-closure restructuring acceptable to you as financially credible?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum package of headcount reduction, fixed-cost removal, and capacity change would you require before concluding that a non-closure alternative is economically comparable to a closure-led plan?**
