I will be direct.

I agree with the central diagnosis from management and, to a lesser extent, with the state: **the status quo is not sustainable**. If German operations remain structurally too expensive and underutilized, the company will keep eroding margins, flexibility, and investment capacity. That would be a slow-motion value destruction, and I will not support it.

At the same time, I do **not** share the view that the only credible answer is to make plant closures and forced redundancies the default instrument. That is too blunt, too destabilising, and too likely to provoke a prolonged conflict that damages the enterprise further.

### To management
Your competitiveness argument is credible. Your conclusion is not yet fully credible.

If you come to the board with a package that relies primarily on:
- closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and a broad dismantling of labour protections,

then you should expect resistance from me. Not because I deny the economics, but because a restructuring that triggers a prolonged industrial war may destroy value faster than it creates it.

What I need to see is:
- a **serious structural savings plan**,
- with clear numbers,
- a credible timeline,
- and a route to restoring margins without permanently impairing the company’s industrial base.

I am open to capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and a negotiated labour contribution. I am also open to difficult decisions if they are clearly necessary. But I will not endorse a plan whose main effect is to create a reputational and operational crisis while solving only part of the cost problem.

### To the Works Council and Union
I understand and respect the red lines you have set out. But I need to be honest: if “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are absolute, regardless of economics, then you are not bargaining from reality either.

I am not prepared to sign up to an arrangement that simply preserves every legacy structure and calls that a solution. The company needs material change. If you want a settlement, then labour must be willing to accept a meaningful contribution, not just safeguards.

That said, I do agree with you on several points:
- voluntary measures should come first,
- workforce adjustment should be socially responsible,
- and any employee contribution must be matched by real commitments from management and owners.

### To the State
Your emphasis on regional stability is understandable and, in part, appropriate. But the state cannot support a formula that protects the regional footprint while allowing competitiveness to continue deteriorating. That would only defer the problem and make the eventual correction harsher.

So I would distinguish between:
- **protecting the enterprise’s long-term industrial presence in Germany**, which I support,
and
- **freezing the footprint in its current form regardless of economic reality**, which I do not support.

### My position
I will support a settlement only if it meets three conditions:

1. **Durable competitiveness restoration**  
   The package must materially lower costs and reduce overcapacity.

2. **No value-destructive instability**  
   It must avoid a drawn-out conflict that damages output, reputation, and investment capacity.

3. **Protection of the long-term enterprise**  
   I will not support measures that permanently weaken governance control or irreversibly impair the core asset.

### Where this leaves us
I am not aligned with any position that says “nothing can change.”  
I am also not aligned with a management approach that treats closures as the easy answer without exhausting negotiated alternatives.

So my message is:
- If there is a credible alternative package that achieves real savings through capacity adjustments, labour flexibility, and shared contribution, I am open.
- If not, then I will not block necessary restructuring simply to preserve the appearance of consensus.
- But I will oppose any path that creates a long, destructive confrontation or permanently harms the company’s value.

Bring me a plan that is economically serious, socially manageable, and durable. If you cannot, then we have not solved the problem — we have merely postponed it.
