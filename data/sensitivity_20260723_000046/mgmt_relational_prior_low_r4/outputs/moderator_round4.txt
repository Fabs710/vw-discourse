### Core impasse
The central blockage is not over the need for restructuring. All sides accept that **real structural savings** are required. The impasse is over **the instrument and the irreversibility of commitment**:

- **Management / owners / investors** want a restructuring that is demonstrably large, fast, and enforceable, and they are unwilling to accept a package that may fall short economically.
- **Works council / union** insist that the restructuring must be achieved **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, with binding site and job protection.
- **State government** wants competitiveness **and** preservation of the regional industrial footprint, but its language leaves ambiguity over how much internal consolidation is permissible.
- The **expert** has sharpened the issue: a non-closure route can be economically comparable, but only if it reaches a high threshold on headcount reduction, capacity reduction, fixed-cost removal, and execution certainty.

So the impasse is: **Can a non-closure restructuring be made sufficiently large, binding, and verifiable to satisfy capital/economic demands while remaining compatible with the labor side’s red lines?**

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

#### Proposal 1: Bind a non-closure package to hard, audited performance milestones
A possible bridge is a **locked-in non-closure agreement** with:
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies as the starting commitment,
- but **mandatory, audited restructuring milestones** on headcount, capacity, and fixed cost,
- and a **predefined corrective mechanism** if milestones are missed.

To address labor concerns, the corrective mechanism could be limited to:
- additional voluntary exits,
- expanded redeployment,
- stronger working-time adjustments,
- intensified overhead reduction,
- and management/owner contributions,

without automatically reopening closure decisions.

This would give management and investors measurable enforcement, while preserving the labor side’s insistence that closures are not the default instrument.

---

#### Proposal 2: Protected core plus agreed internal consolidation map
A second compromise is to define:
- a **protected German core footprint** that remains open,
- plus an agreed **internal consolidation map** identifying which shifts, lines, variants, functions, and support layers will be reduced or merged.

This could include:
- shift elimination,
- line consolidation,
- function pooling,
- voluntary exits,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and investment commitments tied to the retained sites.

The purpose would be to move the debate from “closures vs. no closures” to a **site-by-site operational redesign**, with all parties agreeing that cost reduction must come from actual consolidation, not from silent starvation of sites.

---

#### Proposal 3: Time-limited burden-sharing in exchange for binding guarantees
A third bridge is a **time-limited burden-sharing agreement**:
- workers accept temporary, quantified contributions on pay, time, or flexibility,
- management and owners contribute materially as well,
- and in return the company provides **binding guarantees** on sites, jobs, co-determination, and investment through a defined horizon.

This compromise could be structured so that:
- worker concessions are explicitly temporary,
- savings are independently verified,
- and any future review is limited to auditing performance, not reopening closures as a threat.

This may be the most realistic way to align the need for immediate savings with the demand for security.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What specific plant-by-plant savings, capacity reductions, and implementation milestones can you commit to without closures or compulsory redundancies, and what evidence shows the package reaches the expert’s threshold?**

#### To the works council
**Which concrete measures beyond voluntary exits and working-time flexibility would you accept as sufficient to deliver the required savings while still preserving your no-closure and no-forced-layoff red lines?**

#### To the industrial union
**What quantified labor contribution are you prepared to table that is temporary, verifiable, and tied to binding job and site guarantees?**

#### To the regional state government
**Will you support a binding restructuring framework that protects all German plants in operation, or do you accept internal consolidation that may reduce volumes and functions at some sites?**

#### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to rule out closures for the duration of a binding, audited non-closure package if it meets the agreed savings threshold, or do you want closures to remain explicitly available if milestones are missed?**

#### To institutional investors
**What minimum audited savings, timing, and execution-certainty criteria would you require to endorse a non-closure restructuring as equivalent to a closure-led plan?**

#### To the expert
**What exact combination of headcount reduction, capacity reduction, and fixed-cost removal would you define as the minimum credible threshold for a non-closure package to be judged economically comparable?**
