From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the central question is not whether restructuring is painful. It is whether it restores **competitive margins, durable cost discipline, and investor confidence**.

Right now, I see a lot of positioning, but the financial test is straightforward:

- If the German cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, the equity story stays broken.
- If the outcome is a cosmetic compromise that avoids hard decisions, margins remain under pressure and the share price should keep discounting the group.
- If the company gets forced into a prolonged industrial conflict with no clear end-state, that is also value destructive.

So let me respond directly.

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis: the status quo is not sustainable, and “protect everything” is not a restructuring plan.

But I need to be equally direct: a proposal that relies too heavily on plant closures and compulsory redundancies can also destroy value if it triggers prolonged strikes, political blockage, and implementation risk. From an investor standpoint, **speed, certainty, and durability matter as much as severity**.

So I support decisive cost reduction, but only if it is:
- credible,
- implementable,
- and clearly sufficient to restore margins.

If you cannot get there with the labour side, then the market will not reward rhetorical toughness. It will punish execution risk.

## To the Works Council and the Union
I respect that you want to protect jobs and sites. But your opening position — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of guarantees — is not automatically compatible with restoring competitiveness.

I disagree with the idea that the burden can be mostly absorbed by management, owners, or “voluntary” measures while the structural problem remains largely intact. If the company’s cost base is genuinely out of line, then labour must contribute materially.

That said, I do **not** need closures as a matter of principle. I need **the economics fixed**.

If you can deliver:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost savings,
- tighter working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits and redeployment at scale,
- and a binding, measurable path to margin improvement,

then investors can support an alternative to closures.

But if “no closures” simply means preserving excess capacity and pushing the bill into the future, that is unacceptable. I would not support a settlement that leaves the company structurally impaired.

## To the State
You are right that a destructive regional shock would be bad policy and bad business. But regional sensitivity cannot become a veto on real restructuring. If the state supports a formula that preserves jobs without fixing competitiveness, it is helping postpone the problem.

I would support state-backed stability only if it is tied to:
- real cost reduction,
- genuine footprint rationalisation,
- and a credible industrial plan.

Not a preservation pact dressed up as restructuring.

## To the Controlling Family
You are closer to my position than some of the others in saying the status quo is not sustainable. That is true.

But I also need you to be more concrete. “We need change” is not enough. The market will want to know:
- how much cost comes out,
- over what period,
- with what implementation certainty,
- and what margin trajectory follows.

If the family wants investor support, it should back a solution that is decisive enough to restore returns, not one that merely avoids public controversy.

## Bottom line
Dispersed shareholders will back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**. That may include:
- site consolidation,
- workforce reduction,
- labour-cost concessions,
- and some difficult structural decisions.

What we will **not** support is:
- a cosmetic deal,
- indefinite guarantees that lock in an uncompetitive structure,
- or a prolonged conflict that destroys value without fixing margins.

So my position is simple:

- **Yes** to a credible, durable restructuring with real savings.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally impaired.
- **No** to endless escalation that keeps destroying value.

Bring forward a package that clearly restores competitive margins, and we will support it. Bring forward a compromise that only delays the hard choices, and we will not.
