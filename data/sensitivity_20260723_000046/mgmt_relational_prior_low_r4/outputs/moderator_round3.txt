## Core impasse
The parties agree on the diagnosis:
- the cost base is too high,
- overcapacity must be reduced,
- and cosmetic compromise is insufficient.

They do **not** agree on the acceptable instruments.

The blocking issue is the gap between:
1. **employee-side red lines**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of job security; and  
2. **management / investor / family preference**: a restructuring large enough to produce durable savings and restore margins, even if that requires harder structural measures.

The state shares the no-closure position, but also insists on real savings. The expert has made clear that both closure-led and non-closure plans can work only if they are large enough and executable, which means the dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed, but about **how much structural reduction can be achieved without crossing the red lines**.

## Facilitator proposals
Below are three compromise tracks that may bridge the most conflicting positions:

### 1) Locked-in non-closure package with quantified fallback review
A first-stage agreement could:
- exclude German plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- commit all parties to a quantified savings target,
- include voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, productivity measures, and management/owner contributions,
- and trigger an independent review only if agreed savings milestones are missed.

To address trust concerns, any review would need:
- a fixed timetable,
- independent verification,
- and explicit procedural rules.

This may bridge:
- works council / union red lines,
- state concerns,
- and management/investor demand for measurable progress.

### 2) Protected core with binding site guarantees and internal consolidation
A second-stage model could:
- keep all German sites open for now,
- but permit internal consolidation of functions, shifts, lines, and volumes,
- with binding guarantees against compulsory redundancies,
- while allowing voluntary exits and redeployment across the network.

This would aim to reduce fixed cost and complexity without formal closures, while still giving management room to rationalize operations.

This may bridge:
- employee demands for site security,
- state political concerns,
- and management’s need for actual footprint adjustment.

### 3) Time-limited burden-sharing in exchange for enforceable investment and employment commitments
A third approach could combine:
- temporary employee contributions,
- temporary management/owner contributions,
- investment commitments for German sites,
- product and volume commitments,
- and legally binding employment protections for a defined period.

This creates a bargain: short-term sacrifice in exchange for durable guarantees, rather than open-ended concessions.

This may bridge:
- the union’s demand for reciprocity,
- the family/investors’ demand for durability,
- and management’s need for immediate savings.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
Can you specify the minimum non-closure savings package you would accept as economically credible, including:
- annual run-rate savings,
- headcount reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- and the time by which those savings must be visible?

### To the works council
Would you accept a time-limited agreement that forbids closures and compulsory redundancies **for the restructuring period**, if it also includes:
- binding savings targets,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and an independent review trigger tied to measurable performance?

### To the industrial union
What is the maximum temporary workforce contribution you would consider acceptable in exchange for enforceable guarantees on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and preservation of the collective wage framework?

### To the regional state government
Would the state support a package that keeps all German plants open **now**, but allows internal consolidation and functional rationalization, provided it is paired with binding investment and employment commitments?

### To the controlling family
Would you support a negotiated non-closure package if an independent model shows it can reach a sufficient portion of the required savings with lower execution risk than closures?

### To institutional investors
What specific savings, timing, and margin-recovery thresholds would make a non-closure restructuring acceptable on a risk-adjusted basis?

### To the expert
Can you translate the current discussion into a simple threshold comparison:
- minimum savings under non-closure,
- minimum savings under limited closure,
- and the point at which execution risk makes one clearly preferable to the other?
