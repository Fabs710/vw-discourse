As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

Management cannot solve a strategic problem by destroying thousands of livelihoods, breaking trust, and tearing up the company’s own historic job-security commitments. If the group has a competitiveness problem, then we are prepared to discuss solutions — but only solutions that protect sites, protect jobs, and secure the future of production in Germany.

Our priorities are:

1. No closure of any German plant.
2. No forced layoffs.
3. Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
4. Preservation of co-determination and meaningful worker influence over the restructuring.
5. A fair contribution from everyone, including management and owners, rather than loading the crisis onto the workforce alone.
6. A credible industrial future: investment, product commitments, and a plan that strengthens the German sites instead of hollowing them out.

We are open to alternative measures if they are negotiated fairly and do not cross these red lines. That can include:
- capacity adjustments through working-time measures,
- voluntary solutions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- internal efficiency improvements,
- negotiated labour contributions if they are time-limited, balanced, and tied to guarantees.

But let me be equally clear: if this is presented as a fait accompli — closures, cuts, and the erosion of job protection — then we will resist it with all the means available to us. We have the mandate of the workforce, and we will use our rights at the supervisory board and in collective action to defend every site and every job.
