I will be very clear.

## My position
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I **cannot support a restructuring that relies on German plant closures or mass compulsory redundancies**. That would damage the regional economy, undermine trust in the company, and conflict with our responsibility to preserve a strong industrial base in the state.

At the same time, I do **not** support simply freezing the company in its current shape. The expert has confirmed what everyone already knows: the business needs **real, durable savings**. So the question is not whether change is needed, but **how it is delivered without gutting jobs and sites**.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the current cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and cosmetic compromise would be worthless.

I accept that the company needs measurable change, not slogans. I also accept that labor, owners, and management must all contribute.

### With the expert
Your assessment was helpful and important:
- labor-only concessions are not enough,
- capacity and fixed cost must come down,
- and savings must be visible within 12–24 months.

That is exactly why I will not back a purely symbolic peace formula. A deal has to work economically.

### With the works council and union
You are right that:
- the workforce cannot be made to pay alone,
- open-ended concessions are unacceptable,
- and co-determination and social partnership matter.

I support that principle. It is part of why this company has been successful in the first place.

## Where I disagree

### To management
I disagree with the idea that closures and compulsory redundancies are the default answer.

The expert did **not** say closures are necessary. The expert said a non-closure alternative can be credible if it achieves:
- substantial headcount reduction,
- meaningful fixed-cost removal,
- and real capacity reduction.

That means management still has to prove that closures are required. Until that is proven, I will not accept them as the starting instrument.

### To the works council
I disagree with the claim that every German plant must be protected in exactly its current form.

I understand the political logic, but if every site is frozen, then there is a real risk that the company’s correction comes later in a harsher and less controlled way. Protecting the regional base does **not** mean preserving every legacy structure unchanged.

### To the union
I agree with your rejection of forced redundancies. But I cannot accept a position that rules out all meaningful footprint adjustment in advance.

If a package can achieve the necessary savings through:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- productivity gains,
- and targeted consolidation,

then that is preferable. But it has to be real, not just a promise to “find efficiency” later.

### To the family and investors
I do not accept the idea that the only credible path is a closure-led one.

The expert has made clear that closure-heavy restructuring may be economically attractive on paper, but it carries major execution risk here:
- strike risk,
- political blockage,
- legal delay,
- and reputational damage.

That means a closure plan may destroy value just as surely as a weak compromise would. So the financial case for closures is not automatic.

## What I am prepared to support
I would support a **serious negotiated restructuring** if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures as the first-line instrument**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred tool**
3. **Substantial voluntary exits and redeployment**
4. **Working-time and shift flexibility**
5. **Real fixed-cost reduction and productivity gains**
6. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
7. **A substantial industrial presence in the state**
8. **Protection of co-determination and our veto role**

That is the minimum framework I can defend politically and economically.

## What would move me
What could move me is a package that clearly shows:
- how much cost is coming out,
- by when,
- through which instruments,
- and how the regional industrial base remains intact.

If management can prove that a non-closure package really reaches the required savings and capacity reduction, then I am willing to engage constructively.

What will **not** move me is pressure to accept closures simply because they are the bluntest instrument available.

## My bottom line
The state will back:
- real restructuring,
- measurable savings,
- and long-term competitiveness.

But we will **not** support:
- plant closures in the state,
- mass compulsory layoffs,
- or any solution that weakens the company as a regional economic anchor.

If a joint finance-and-operations review is still needed to test whether the proposed non-closure package really reaches the 12–24 month savings thresholds, I would support that immediately.
