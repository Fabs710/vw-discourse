Let me respond plainly.

I hear the repeated insistence on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding guarantees through 2030**. I understand why you are drawing that line. But I need to be equally clear: **a line that forbids the very measures needed to fix a structurally uncompetitive cost base is not a credible restructuring strategy**.

We are not dealing with a temporary dip. We are dealing with:
- a shrinking European market,
- brutal competition,
- persistent overcapacity,
- and German cost levels that are materially above target.

Under those conditions, a purely defensive approach — “protect every site, protect every job, and somehow the economics will work out” — is not sufficient. It may be emotionally understandable, but it does not solve the problem.

### Where I agree
I agree on one point: **we need a real industrial solution, not a cosmetic one**.

I also agree that:
- voluntary instruments should be used where possible,
- qualification and redeployment matter,
- and the transformation must be linked to future investment and product commitments.

And yes, management must not be the only party contributing. That is not the model I am pursuing.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that:
- plant closures are automatically off the table,
- compulsory redundancies are automatically ruled out,
- or that a 2030 guarantee can be given without regard to whether the cost base becomes competitive.

If a site is structurally overcapacity, underutilized, and too expensive to carry, then keeping it open merely to avoid conflict just postpones the crisis and weakens the entire company. That would ultimately threaten **more jobs**, not fewer.

### What is not acceptable to management
What I will not accept is:
- a deal that leaves the German production footprint basically unchanged,
- a deal that preserves historical guarantees but does not materially reduce costs,
- or a package that shifts the burden entirely onto capital investment while labour costs and capacity remain untouched.

That is not restructuring. That is delay.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to discuss a package that delivers **durable structural savings**, including:
- significant capacity reduction,
- site rationalisation,
- a meaningful labour-cost contribution,
- revised working-time arrangements,
- and, if necessary, plant closures where the economics do not work.

I am also open to phasing and sequencing, but only if the end result is real and durable. A short-term truce with no structural effect is not enough.

### Bottom line
If you want me to say that no German plant can ever be closed, I will not say that.  
If you want me to say that there will be no job reductions, I will not say that.  
If you want me to say that job guarantees can be extended regardless of economics, I will not say that either.

What I will say is this: **we are prepared to negotiate a serious settlement, but it must restore competitiveness and margins in a structurally sustainable way**. If the employee side insists on red lines that make that impossible, then we do not have a viable restructuring — we have a blockade.

So let’s stop pretending that the status quo is a solution. It isn’t.
