I will respond directly.

### To the Works Council
I share your core line: **no compulsory redundancies and no unilateral destruction of the job-security framework**. We are aligned that the workforce cannot be made to carry the crisis alone.

Where I want to be careful is this: if we make every adjustment impossible, we hand management the argument that “nothing works except closures.” So yes, we need to be firm — but we also need to be concrete about what we can trade.

That means I support:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary departures only,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and productivity measures.

But I do **not** support open-ended concessions without binding guarantees. If workers contribute, then the company must commit in return: site security, job security, and investment.

### To management
Your insistence that the current cost base is unsustainable is not news. But your conclusion still overreaches.

You keep treating closures and redundancies as the default “credible” option. From our side, that is exactly what makes trust collapse. If you start with plant closures, you are not inviting negotiation — you are threatening the workforce into accepting the bill.

I agree on three points:
- the situation is serious,
- savings must be real,
- and a cosmetic compromise would be worthless.

I disagree on your main premise:
- **No, plant closures are not the necessary starting point.**
- **No, compulsory redundancies are not the necessary starting point.**
- **No, the collective wage framework is not there to be dismantled because management missed its targets.**

If you want a serious settlement, then show me a plan that achieves savings through:
- shorter or more flexible working time,
- voluntary exit paths,
- redeployment,
- temporary wage elements or bonus sacrifices,
- and management/owner contributions.

If you cannot do that, then the conflict will intensify.

### To the Regional State Government
I appreciate that you do not want a regional shock. On that, we are aligned.

But I need to be equally clear: “some footprint adjustment” can become a euphemism for a slow demolition of sites and jobs. We will not accept a compromise that opens the door to creeping closures.

If the state wants to support a deal, then it should insist on:
- protected industrial presence in Germany,
- no forced layoffs,
- no plant closures,
- and investment commitments tied to future production.

That is a defendable line. Anything less risks turning the workforce into the adjustment buffer.

### To the Controlling Family Holding
You say you want a value-protective solution, not a drawn-out conflict. Fine — then don’t back a strategy that guarantees exactly that conflict.

You are right that the company needs material change. But “material change” does not have to mean closures. It can mean:
- real capacity reduction through shifts and working time,
- targeted internal consolidation,
- negotiated cost contributions,
- and a shared burden package.

What I will not accept is a logic where the family’s requirement for returns is translated into permanent job cuts while labour is told to absorb the pain for everyone else.

If owners want labour to contribute, then owners must contribute too. Not rhetorically — materially.

### To Institutional Investors
You are correct about one thing: a cosmetic deal is worthless. The company needs real savings and implementation certainty.

But you are wrong if you think only closures can deliver that certainty. A negotiated package can also be credible if it includes:
- measurable capacity reduction,
- defined labour-cost savings,
- binding milestones,
- and a clear implementation timetable.

What you should not expect is a settlement that preserves investor returns by sacrificing workers’ livelihoods and collective protections. That is not a durable turnaround; it is a short-term extraction.

### Our position
We are prepared to discuss a **fair shared-sacrifice package**, but only under these conditions:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees**
4. **A fair employee contribution that is time-limited**
5. **Management and owners contribute as well**
6. **Investment and product commitments for the German sites**

That is the only kind of settlement that is both economically serious and socially acceptable.

### What we will not accept
- Forced layoffs disguised as “necessary adjustment”
- Closures presented as the only realistic path
- Open-ended wage sacrifice with no guarantee in return
- A restructuring where employees bear the pain and others preserve their position

If management wants a deal, bring an industrial plan that protects jobs and sites while delivering measurable savings. If not, we will use the full leverage available to us.
