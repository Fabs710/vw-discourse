I agree with the basic diagnosis that the company cannot simply carry on unchanged. A structurally weaker European market, intense price pressure, and an uncompetitive cost base are real problems. I also agree that a serious restructuring must be more than symbolism and that everyone — including management and owners — must contribute.

But I need to be very clear where I draw the line.

### What I support
I support a negotiated restructuring that:
- preserves the company’s long-term competitiveness,
- protects core sites and a substantial part of the German workforce,
- uses voluntary and socially responsible instruments first,
- includes redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility,
- and links any employee contribution to binding future investment and product commitments.

That is a path I can defend politically and in the supervisory board. It serves the regional economy, preserves industrial capacity, and avoids turning restructuring into a one-sided jobs cut.

### What I do not accept
I do **not** accept a restructuring logic that treats plant closures and mass layoffs as the default answer.

If the proposal is:
- closure of German plants,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a hollowing-out of the company’s regional footprint,

then I will oppose it. That would damage the state’s economic base, undermine social stability, and disregard the special responsibility that comes with this company’s role in the region.

I also do **not** accept being told that guarantees are somehow impossible simply because management wants faster cost relief. A company with this history and this ownership structure cannot ask the workforce to absorb the entire adjustment while owners and management present themselves as bystanders.

### To management
Your point that delay has costs is valid. But so does brutal restructuring. Closing plants may create a short-term accounting improvement while inflicting long-term damage on skills, trust, supply chains, and the company’s standing in Germany. That is a risk I will not support lightly.

If you want my support, you need to show that the savings are real **without** sacrificing the company’s regional anchor role. That means:
- credible alternatives to closure,
- meaningful contributions from capital and management,
- and a plan that keeps industrial capacity in Germany.

### To the works council and union
I respect the clear line on no forced redundancies and no closures. That is consistent with the company’s social partnership model and with the state’s interest. But I will also be honest: if we insist on absolute rigidity on all sides, we may end up with no settlement and escalating conflict.

So I am not here to rubber-stamp management’s approach, but I am also not here to endorse a blockage that leaves the company unable to adapt. The question is whether we can secure competitiveness **without** destroying the German industrial base.

### My position
My position is:
- **No closure-driven restructuring as the starting point**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the first choice**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring with site protection, fair burden-sharing, and strong regional investment commitments**
- **No outcome that strips the state of its co-determination role or guts employment in this region**

If management wants support, bring forward a concrete alternative package that proves savings can be achieved through capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, and negotiated contributions — not by making closures the central instrument. Otherwise, I will use the rights attached to our stake and board position to block a solution that is socially and economically irresponsible.
