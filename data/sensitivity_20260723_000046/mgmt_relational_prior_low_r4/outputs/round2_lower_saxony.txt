I will be blunt.

I agree with management, the family, and the investors on one point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. This company does need a credible restructuring, and it cannot be pure symbolism. But I do **not** accept the conclusion that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or necessary starting point.

From the state’s perspective, and under our responsibility to the regional economy, the line is clear:

- **No hollowing out of the company’s German industrial base**
- **No restructuring that destroys large numbers of jobs in the state**
- **No marginalization of co-determination or the special veto rights attached to our stake**
- **Yes to real savings, but only in a socially responsible form**

### To management
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would be worthless. I share that concern. But you are overstating your case when you imply that only closures can produce the required savings. That is not proven.

What I can support is a **serious negotiated restructuring** built around:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted productivity improvements,
- temporary labor contributions,
- and a protected industrial core in Germany.

What I cannot support is a package that starts from plant closures and compulsory redundancies and then asks the workforce and the state to clean up the political damage afterward. That is not a sustainable turnaround strategy; it is a confrontation strategy.

### To the Works Council and the Union
I support your insistence that the burden cannot be shifted onto the workforce alone. I also support your demand that any employee contribution must be tied to binding guarantees and a credible industrial future.

But I want to be equally honest: if every adjustment is treated as forbidden, then we hand management an excuse to escalate. So I would urge you to stay firm on:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures as the starting instrument**
- **binding site protection for core German sites**
- **fair burden-sharing**

while still being prepared to discuss:
- hours,
- flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and time-limited cost contributions.

That is the space where a defensible settlement can exist.

### To the Controlling Family
I hear your point that the company needs material change and that delay destroys value. That is true. But I reject the idea that preserving sites and jobs in Germany is somehow a sentimental indulgence. It is a strategic asset.

If the family wants a durable solution, it should support a package that:
- protects the company’s regional anchor role,
- maintains industrial presence in Germany,
- and avoids triggering a prolonged labor conflict that could damage the brand and the balance sheet more than a negotiated compromise would.

### To the Institutional Investors
You are right to demand measurable savings and implementation certainty. But you are wrong if you assume that certainty only comes through closures. A well-structured negotiated package can also be credible if it includes:
- quantified cost reductions,
- clear milestones,
- enforceable labor contributions,
- and investment commitments.

What you should not support is a “hard” restructuring that looks decisive on paper but creates years of conflict, litigation, strikes, and political blockage. That would be value-destructive, not value-preserving.

### My position
The state will support only a solution that:
1. **preserves a substantial German industrial footprint**
2. **protects as many jobs as possible**
3. **uses voluntary and socially responsible instruments first**
4. **maintains co-determination and our veto role**
5. **delivers real competitiveness gains**

I am not prepared to back a closure-led restructuring as the primary answer. If management insists on that route, it should not expect our support. We will use the rights we have.

If, however, there is a concrete package that achieves structural savings without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies, then I am willing to engage constructively. That is the path I can defend politically, economically, and in the supervisory board.
