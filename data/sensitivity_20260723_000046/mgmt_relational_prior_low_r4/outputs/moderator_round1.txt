### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between:

1. **Employee-side red lines**  
   Works Council and union insist on:
   - no German plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - binding job/site guarantees,
   - and meaningful co-determination.

2. **Management / investor demand for structural change**  
   Management, family holding, and capital-market shareholders insist that:
   - the current footprint and cost base are not sustainable,
   - real capacity reduction is necessary,
   - and any agreement must produce durable savings and competitiveness gains.

**In short:** one side is demanding **hard protection**, the other side is demanding **hard structural adjustment**. The missing bridge is a package that delivers measurable cost reduction **without** appearing as a closure-and-layoff plan.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three possible compromise directions for further negotiation.

#### 1) Sequenced restructuring with a “no forced layoffs first” rule
A package could be structured in phases:
- immediate use of voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, and working-time measures,
- a fixed negotiation period to test whether these measures deliver enough savings,
- only if agreed targets are still missed would more intrusive measures be revisited.

**Purpose:** gives labor a real chance to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, while giving management a pathway to prove whether the softer tools are sufficient.

#### 2) Conditional site protection in exchange for quantified workforce contributions
Employee-side guarantees could be tied to:
- explicit annual savings targets,
- defined productivity and working-time changes,
- voluntary cost contributions from labor,
- and commitments from management/owners on investment, product allocation, and capital support.

**Purpose:** creates a trade: the workforce gets site and job protection, but only if the package demonstrably closes the competitiveness gap.

#### 3) “Protected core / adjustable perimeter” model
The parties could distinguish between:
- a protected core of strategically important German sites and production lines,
- and a limited perimeter of activities that may be consolidated, outsourced, or restructured.

This could include:
- preserving core plants,
- moving selected volumes, functions, or shifts,
- and using internal transfers rather than direct layoffs wherever possible.

**Purpose:** allows real footprint adjustment without presenting the outcome as wholesale closure.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If management offers binding site protection for core German plants, but in return requires measurable capacity reductions, working-time flexibility, and a time-limited labor contribution, **what specific restructuring measures would you accept without crossing your red lines?**

#### Industrial Union
If no forced redundancies and no closures are maintained, **what quantified workforce contribution — in pay, hours, or flexibility — would you put on the table to make the package credible to the other side?**

#### Group & Brand Management
If employee-side guarantees are linked to measurable savings and productivity gains, **which sites, volumes, or functions are you prepared to treat as protected, and which specific operational changes would you accept instead of immediate closures?**

#### Regional State Government
If the only credible deal requires some footprint adjustment, **what minimum level of employment and industrial presence in the region would you consider politically and economically acceptable?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
If a settlement avoids immediate closures but delivers savings more slowly, **what evidence would you require to accept that the plan is still value-protective rather than merely delaying the problem?**

#### Institutional Investors
If the package includes no closures but does include binding capacity reduction, labor-cost savings, and governance commitments, **what specific financial milestones would make you support it as sufficiently decisive?**
