Let me be equally direct in round 2.

I hear the employee side repeating: **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding guarantees through 2030, and only voluntary or temporary contributions**. I understand the political appeal of that position. I do **not** accept it as a sufficient answer to the company’s situation.

## Where I agree
I agree on three things:

1. **The company needs a real industrial solution, not a cosmetic one.**
2. **Management, owners, and labour all have to contribute.**
3. **A prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.**

So yes: I am not interested in a settlement that simply creates a headline and leaves the cost base unchanged.

## Where I disagree
I disagree very clearly with the core employee demand that **plant closures are off the table in advance**.

That is not a negotiating position; it is an attempt to pre-decide the outcome while refusing to confront the economics. If a plant is structurally overcapacity, underutilized, and too expensive, then insisting on keeping it open regardless of the numbers does not protect the company — it weakens it.

I also disagree that **compulsory redundancies can be ruled out categorically** in a restructuring of this scale. I would prefer to avoid them. I have said that before. But I will not pretend they can be excluded as a matter of principle if the cost base remains far above target.

And I absolutely do not accept that **binding guarantees through 2030** can be offered unconditionally while the company is still carrying structural overcapacity. Guarantees without competitiveness are just deferred losses.

## On the moderator’s compromise options

### 1) Sequenced restructuring
I am open to sequencing, but not as a stalling tactic.

A phase-one package based on:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time measures,
- and temporary labour contributions

can be tested. But it must be tied to **hard savings targets** and a **clear review point**.

If those measures do not deliver enough, then we cannot rule out deeper structural action later. I will not sign up to a sequencing model that predetermines the answer as “no closures ever.”

### 2) Conditional site protection in exchange for quantified contributions
This is the only direction that looks remotely workable.

If the workforce is prepared to make a **real, measurable contribution** — not symbolic, but quantified and durable — then I can discuss conditional protection for selected sites and activities.

But I need more than general phrases like “fair burden-sharing.” I need:
- specific labour-cost savings,
- specific capacity reductions,
- specific working-time changes,
- and a credible implementation path.

If the employee side wants site protection, then it has to help close the competitiveness gap in a way the business can actually feel.

### 3) Protected core / adjustable perimeter
This is more realistic than the employee side wants to admit.

Not every site, function, or volume has the same strategic value. A protected core with a more adjustable perimeter is a rational industrial model. That means:
- core strategic production can be preserved,
- but weaker or excess volumes, shifts, or functions may need to move, consolidate, or be reduced.

I reject the claim that this is just a “slow closure strategy.” It is a way to preserve the enterprise while still adapting the footprint to reality.

## To the Works Council
You say no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no erosion of job security. I understand your mandate. But I need to be honest: **if those are absolute and non-negotiable, then you are not actually engaging with the restructuring challenge**.

What I can discuss with you is:
- strong preference for voluntary measures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- protected core sites,
- and conditional guarantees tied to performance.

What I cannot discuss as a starting point is a blank promise that every German plant stays open no matter what.

## To the Union
You say you are open to temporary contributions. Good. Then let’s make that concrete.

If the workforce wants to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, it must show a **material labour contribution**. That can include:
- temporary wage moderation,
- lower bonus components,
- working-time flexibility,
- and productivity commitments.

But “temporary” cannot mean marginal. If the contribution is too small to move the numbers, it does not solve the problem.

## To the Regional State Government
You are right to care about the regional industrial base. I do too.

But I disagree with the idea that supporting restructuring means protecting the footprint in its current form. If we preserve every legacy structure and refuse footprint adjustment, then the company will continue to underperform and eventually face a harsher correction.

So yes, I am open to preserving a **strong industrial presence in Germany**.  
No, I am not open to freezing the footprint exactly as it is.

## To the Controlling Family
You want value protection and less conflict. I agree on the goal.

But value protection requires more than saying “material change” and then hesitating when that means hard decisions. If we want to avoid a prolonged industrial war, we need a package that is clearly substantial enough to restore margins.

That means I need support for:
- real capacity reduction,
- real labour-cost savings,
- and a structure that investors can believe in.

If you want me to hold back from immediate closures, I need a credible alternative that truly changes the economics. Not a promise that the issue can be postponed indefinitely.

## To Institutional Investors
You are right to demand a package that is decisive, measurable, and implementable.

I agree with you that a cosmetic compromise is worthless. Where I differ is this: you do **not** need me to choose closures as the only credible path. What you need is a restructuring that:
- reduces overcapacity,
- lowers labour costs structurally,
- restores return on sales,
- and frees funds for product and technology investment to 2030.

I am prepared to deliver that. But I will not deliver it by pretending the current footprint can simply be preserved untouched.

## My position, clearly stated
I am prepared to negotiate a serious settlement only if it delivers all of the following:

- **durable structural savings**
- **meaningful labour-cost contribution**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **implementation certainty**
- **a protected core industrial presence in Germany**
- **investment and product commitments**

What I will **not** accept is:
- an absolute ban on closures,
- an absolute ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a guarantee structure that prevents real restructuring.

That is the line.

If the employee side wants to avoid the harder instruments, then bring a package that is large enough to close the gap. If it cannot, then we are not discussing solutions — we are discussing delay. And delay is exactly what this company cannot afford.
