Management, shareholders, and even the state keep repeating the same message: “real change,” “structural savings,” “competitiveness.” We hear that. But let me answer just as clearly: **you do not restore competitiveness by threatening closures and tearing up job security first, then asking the workforce to trust you later.**

### First: what I agree with
I agree that the company faces serious pressure:
- shrinking European demand,
- intense competition,
- and a cost base that must be addressed.

I also agree that a settlement must be real, measurable, and durable. We are not asking for slogans. We are prepared to discuss a genuine industrial plan.

### But here is the line you keep trying to push
I do **not** accept the idea that:
- plant closures are a normal restructuring tool,
- compulsory redundancies are somehow inevitable,
- or that workforce protections can be suspended because management wants faster cost relief.

That is precisely the wrong answer. It destroys trust, provokes conflict, and risks long-term damage to the company’s German base.

### On the moderator’s “compromise options”
#### 1) Sequenced restructuring
A sequence can only be discussed if the starting point is firm:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding guarantees through 2030**
- **no fait accompli**

If management wants to test voluntary instruments, working-time measures, redeployment, and qualification, we are open to that. But not as a pretext for later coming back with closures once everyone is weakened. A “no forced layoffs first” rule is meaningless if the endgame is still closure.

#### 2) Conditional site protection in exchange for contributions
Yes, the workforce can contribute. We have already said that.
But any contribution must be:
- time-limited,
- fair,
- proportionate,
- and tied to **binding** site and job guarantees.

We will not accept open-ended concessions that become permanent while management keeps the right to cut jobs whenever targets are missed.

#### 3) Protected core / adjustable perimeter
I reject any model that quietly creates a category of “less protected” sites or functions and then uses that to justify a creeping dismantling of the German footprint.

That is not protection. That is a slow closure strategy by another name.

### To management
You asked what we would accept without crossing red lines. The answer is simple:
- capacity adjustments through working-time measures,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining and qualification,
- voluntary departures only,
- and a shared contribution from management and owners.

What we will **not** accept is:
- closures of any German plant,
- forced redundancies,
- or a restructuring that breaks the historic job-security framework.

If you want our cooperation, bring a plan that keeps every German plant open and gives the workforce binding security. Anything else will meet resistance.

### To the union
I am with you on the core line. We need to stay united:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of collective protections.

If management wants labor to contribute, the contribution must be negotiated under guarantees, not extracted under threat.

### To the state
I understand your concern about avoiding a regional shock. But “some footprint adjustment” is exactly the kind of language that starts with a limited cut and ends with more closures later. We cannot accept a model that normalizes dismantling under the banner of realism.

### To the family and investors
You keep saying the economics must be fixed. Fine — then fix them without sacrificing sites and jobs.
If the only “credible” plan you recognize is one that leaves plant closures on the table, then you are not solving the problem; you are shifting the burden onto the workforce.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary measures,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- and efficiency improvements.

I am **not** prepared to negotiate away:
- every German plant,
- job security,
- or our co-determination rights.

So let’s stop pretending closures are the only serious option. They are not. They are the easiest way to make workers pay for management’s strategic problems, and we will not accept that.
