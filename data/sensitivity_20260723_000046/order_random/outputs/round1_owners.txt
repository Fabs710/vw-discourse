We share the basic diagnosis that the company has a competitiveness problem and that a durable solution has to be more than a short-term patch. I do agree that a purely unilateral approach would be destabilising and could destroy more value than it creates.

But I need to be equally clear on the other side: **a settlement that rules out plant closures and compulsory redundancies in advance is not credible if the cost base cannot otherwise be brought down to a level that restores margins.** If the company’s German footprint remains structurally overcapacity, then refusing to touch capacity at all simply shifts the burden into continued underperformance, weaker investment capacity, and eventually a worse outcome for everyone.

So my position is:

- **I support a serious restructuring.**
- **I do not support empty slogans about shared sacrifice if the numbers do not add up.**
- **I am open to negotiated alternatives to closures where they genuinely achieve comparable savings and lasting competitiveness.**
- **I will not support any arrangement that preserves every site and every legacy guarantee while leaving the company uncompetitive.**

On your points:

- **No compulsory redundancies:** as an absolute, that may be too rigid. I am open to minimizing forced layoffs, but if the alternative is a structurally broken business, then the workforce cannot be fully insulated from the adjustment.
- **No plant closures:** I cannot accept that as a starting red line. If there is a viable path to achieve the required savings through other means, fine. But if not, closures must remain on the table.
- **No unilateral shifting of the crisis onto the workforce:** agreed in principle. But that cuts both ways. Owners, management, and employees all have to contribute.
- **Collective bargaining framework preserved:** yes, but preserving the framework cannot mean preserving all historical cost levels or all guarantees unchanged.

What would move us toward support is a **credible package** that includes:
1. a measurable and durable cost reduction,
2. real productivity and capacity adjustments,
3. a negotiated labour contribution,
4. a clear commitment to future investment and model allocation,
5. and if possible, a solution that avoids plant closures — **but only if it is financially equivalent**.

What will not move us is a proposal that demands full job and site security while offering only symbolic concessions elsewhere. That would be value-destructive and irresponsible to the long-term owners.

So to be direct: **I am prepared to back a tough settlement, but not a veto on structural change.**
