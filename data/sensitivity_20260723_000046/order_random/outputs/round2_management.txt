We are past the point where slogans can substitute for numbers.

I will respond directly to each of you.

## To the Industrial Union and Works Council
I hear your refusal of plant closures and compulsory redundancies. I also understand why trust is low after the end of the job-security guarantees. But let me be just as clear: **an absolute no to closures and forced reductions is not an answer to the structural problem we face**.

You keep describing closures as coercion. From my side, the real coercion is letting the cost base stay permanently above target and then pretending the company can invest its way out of it. That is not protection of jobs; it is the slow destruction of competitiveness.

What I can accept:
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary compensation measures,
- and a negotiated labour contribution.

What I cannot accept:
- a package that leaves all sites untouched,
- preserves legacy guarantees in full,
- and delivers only temporary relief while capacity remains excessive.

If you want closures off the table, then show me a package that **permanently** removes enough cost and capacity to hit a competitive operating margin. Not “protect jobs first and hope for the best” — real structural savings.

## To the Regional State Government
I agree with you on one point: closures are serious and should not be treated casually. They are not my first preference either. But I reject the idea that they are somehow outside the realm of legitimate restructuring.

You say closures are a last resort. Fine. Then prove that the alternatives are sufficient. Until then, you are asking the company to carry structural overcapacity because the social cost of adjustment is difficult. That is not responsible stewardship if it leaves the brand uncompetitive.

And let me also be direct: a blocking role in governance does not entitle any one stakeholder to freeze industrial adaptation indefinitely. The state’s mandate is not to preserve every existing footprint at any cost. It is to safeguard a viable industrial base. That requires hard choices.

## To the Controlling Family and Investors
I appreciate that you are not asking for a cosmetic fix. That is the right instinct. But I also need support for an outcome that is **operationally executable**, not just theoretically severe.

If we are serious about restoring returns, then the company needs:
- durable capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost reduction,
- and room to fund future products and technology to 2030.

I do not accept a compromise that looks prudent on paper but leaves the business structurally underpowered.

## My position, plainly stated
The company’s German footprint is structurally too expensive for the market reality we are facing. That means:

- **We need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.**
- **We need a genuine labour-cost contribution.**
- **We need measures that restore return on sales, not just industrial peace.**
- **We need room to invest in the future.**

## What I am open to
I am open to a negotiated package that avoids closures **only if** it delivers savings equivalent to what closures and deep restructuring would achieve.

That means:
- plant-by-plant capacity correction,
- workforce reduction through voluntary and negotiated means where possible,
- working-time and pay-related contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- and binding commitments that the result is permanent, not temporary.

## What I am not open to
I will not support:
- a blanket exclusion of closures,
- an unconditional ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a deal that preserves every site and every guarantee while the company remains uncompetitive,
- or a short-term peace arrangement that postpones the real adjustment.

## The numbers still need to be confronted
Before we can move any further, I want the following on the table in a structured way:

1. **What is the exact cost gap versus target?**
2. **What capacity reduction is required to close it?**
3. **What can be achieved through voluntary and negotiated instruments alone?**
4. **What labour-cost contribution is realistic and durable?**
5. **What investment commitments can be tied to the package?**

If the union and works council want closures excluded, then I need a package that closes those gaps fully. If not, then closures and stronger workforce adjustment remain legitimate tools.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate, but not to pretend the problem is smaller than it is.

A viable settlement must restore competitiveness, not just preserve the current footprint. If the package does not achieve that, then it is not acceptable to management, to the owners, or to the long-term future of the company.
