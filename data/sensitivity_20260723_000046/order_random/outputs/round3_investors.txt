We are aligned with the CFO, management, and the family on the central economic point: **this company needs a durable, structural reset, not a symbolic peace deal**.

## Where we agree
- The cost base is too high.
- The current footprint likely has structural overcapacity.
- Temporary concessions alone are unlikely to be enough if the gap is as large as management says.
- Deal certainty matters; prolonged conflict, strikes, and drift destroy value.
- Investment and product allocation must be part of the package.

That said, our support is **not** for any restructuring in name only. We care about **risk-adjusted value** and a **credible return to competitive margins**.

## Where we disagree

### To the union and works council
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” position is understandable politically, but **it is not acceptable as an absolute** if it prevents the company from restoring competitiveness.

A deal that protects every site first and asks the balance sheet to absorb the consequences later is exactly the kind of outcome we cannot support. That would leave:
- margins structurally impaired,
- the cost base uncompetitive,
- and shareholders financing delay rather than restructuring.

We are open to negotiated instruments:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- wage/bonus restraint,
- productivity measures,
- and selective capacity reduction.

But if those do not close the gap, then closures and stronger workforce adjustment must remain available. Otherwise this is not restructuring; it is deferral.

### To the state government
We respect the regional role, but **regional protection cannot become a hard veto on economically necessary adjustment**. If the plant-level evidence shows that a site is structurally uneconomic, then insisting on preserving it anyway just shifts the loss into future value destruction.

The state’s interest is not served by preserving every current footprint if the result is a weaker company with less ability to invest.

### To management
We support your insistence on hard numbers, but you still need to make the case **plant by plant**, not just in aggregate. If you want capital markets to back painful change, the company should present:
- the exact cost gap,
- utilization by site,
- the savings from each non-closure measure,
- the one-time cost of closure/downsizing,
- and the margin impact over 3–5 years.

Without that, the market will assume the process is still too political and too vague.

## Our position on the compromise paths

### 1) “No closure first” with a hard review trigger
This is acceptable only if it is real, quantified, and time-bound. It cannot be a mechanism to postpone the problem indefinitely.

For us to support it, the initial package must be:
- substantial enough to matter,
- measurable in annual savings,
- and linked to consequences if targets are missed.

### 2) Site-specific protection
This is the most plausible route, because not all plants will have the same economics. But it only works if:
- viable sites get investment and product allocation,
- weaker sites get credible transition plans,
- and the overall package still produces a material, durable margin improvement.

### 3) Closure as last resort
That is already our view. Closures are not the preferred option, but they are a legitimate tool if the numbers show the footprint is structurally too large. A “last resort” must mean all meaningful alternatives were tested and found insufficient.

## What we will support
We would back a settlement if it delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- real workforce contributions,
- clarity on implementation,
- and a credible path back to target margins.

That can be non-closure only if it is economically equivalent. If it is not equivalent, then it is not acceptable.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- an unconditional ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a cosmetic compromise that preserves all sites unchanged,
- or a settlement that buys social peace while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
Our standard is simple:
**show us a quantified, durable, plant-level package that closes the competitiveness gap.**

If the negotiated alternative can do that, we will support it. If it cannot, then the tools that materially restore competitiveness must remain on the table.
