We recognise the pressure on the company, but our position is clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures.**
- **No unilateral shifting of the crisis onto the workforce.**
- **The collective bargaining framework must be preserved.**

We are prepared to negotiate a **fair shared-sacrifice package** if it genuinely protects jobs and sites. That means:
- **capacity reductions through negotiated instruments**, not closures;
- **working-time measures**, internal redeployment, and temporary adjustments;
- **employee contributions** only as part of a broader package;
- **management, owners, and the company also taking a real contribution**;
- **binding guarantees** for sites, employment, and future investment.

What we will **not** accept is a restructuring where workers pay the price through layoffs, wage cuts, and uncertainty while management and owners avoid comparable sacrifice. That is neither fair nor durable, and it would provoke serious resistance.

If the company wants a settlement, it must be one that **protects the workforce, secures the sites, and spreads the burden evenly**. Otherwise, we are prepared to escalate industrial action.
