I will respond plainly.

## My position

I **do** accept that the company needs a durable restructuring.  
I **do not** accept that the only credible restructuring is one built around German plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

For me, that is the central line in this discussion:

- **No restructuring that destroys the regional industrial base.**
- **No settlement that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking role.**
- **Yes to negotiated, durable cost reduction if it protects sites, jobs, and future investment.**

## Where I agree

### To management
You are right that the business needs more than symbolism. The cost base, capacity, and margin problem are real. I will not defend a status quo that leaves the company uncompetitive.

### To the union and works council
You are right that this cannot be a one-sided burden shift onto the workforce. A deal that asks employees to give up jobs, guarantees, and security while owners and management simply demand sacrifice is neither fair nor sustainable.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right that a cosmetic fix will not work. The company must become more competitive, and any solution must be durable.

## Where I disagree

### 1) Plant closures are not a normal option
I do **not** accept closures as a starting point, bargaining lever, or default tool.

In a company of this importance, closures in Germany are not a routine efficiency measure. They are a last resort, and only if every alternative has genuinely failed. So far, I do **not** see that all alternatives have been exhausted. I see a management preference for maximum pressure.

That is not acceptable to me.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable as a baseline
I am prepared to discuss voluntary exits, early retirement, redeployment, qualification, and working-time measures.  
I am **not** prepared to accept mass forced layoffs as the “normal” answer.

If forced redundancies are presented as the starting assumption, then the social partnership model has already been abandoned.

### 3) The state cannot be treated as a marginal stakeholder
I need to say this directly: any package that effectively bypasses the state’s veto role, or tries to reduce us to a symbolic voice, will meet resistance.

We hold a blocking-minority stake under special statute for a reason. That is not decoration. It exists to protect regional interests, jobs, and long-term industrial stability.

## What would move me

I could support a tough settlement if it is built on these pillars:

1. **Binding site guarantees for the German plants.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the governing principle.**
3. **Capacity reduction through negotiated instruments, not closures.**
4. **A meaningful contribution from management and owners.**
5. **Clear investment commitments in future products and technologies.**
6. **A social-partnership solution that preserves the company as a regional anchor.**

That is the minimum basis for my support.

## On the packages proposed so far

### To management and investors
You keep saying: “prove an alternative is financially equivalent.” Fair enough. But then put the full numbers on the table.

If you want closures or forced layoffs to remain credible, you need to show:
- the exact cost gap,
- the exact savings from your preferred measures,
- why negotiated alternatives are insufficient,
- and why the regional damage would be justified.

Until then, I regard “closures must remain on the table” as pressure, not proof.

### To the union and works council
I hear your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand it politically and socially. But I also need a package that is economically serious, not simply a defensive declaration.

If you want to keep sites and jobs, then come with a concrete restructuring package that is:
- substantial,
- measurable,
- durable,
- and capable of restoring competitiveness.

That means internal adjustments, working-time instruments, productivity gains, voluntary reductions, and perhaps a temporary employee contribution — but not a deal that leaves the company structurally unchanged.

## My boundary

Let me be explicit:  
I will **not** support any outcome that

- guts regional employment,
- closes plants in the state without exhausting alternatives,
- weakens the company’s role as a regional economic anchor,
- or removes the state’s co-determination and veto role from the process.

That is not a negotiable preference for me; it is the basis of my mandate.

## Practical way forward

If the parties want my support, the next step must be a **serious negotiated restructuring package** with:

- quantified savings targets,
- capacity adjustments that avoid plant closures where possible,
- voluntary and reversible workforce instruments,
- a limited and fair employee contribution,
- management and owner contributions,
- and binding investment and site commitments.

If that cannot be achieved, then I will not back a package that sacrifices the region for a supposedly cleaner balance sheet.

## Bottom line

I am open to a hard compromise.  
I am **not** open to a restructuring that treats German plant closures and forced redundancies as the cost of doing business.

If we are to preserve this company as a regional industrial anchor, then the solution must protect the industrial base, not dismantle it.
