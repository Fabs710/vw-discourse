## Core impasse

The deadlock is concentrated in **one central conflict**:

- **Management, the controlling family, and investors** want **credible structural capacity and cost reduction**, and they insist that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available** if other measures are insufficient.
- **The union and works council**, supported by the **state government**, treat **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as unacceptable starting points or red lines**.
- The **CFO’s analysis** shows why this matters: if the cost gap is large and the savings must be durable, temporary concessions alone may not be enough.
- So the dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed, but about **which instruments are permissible to achieve it** and **what evidence would justify them**.

In short:  
**The parties agree on the need for durable competitiveness, but not on whether closures and forced layoffs are legitimate fallback tools.**

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise positions

### 1) “No closure first” with a hard review trigger
A possible bridge is a **phased restructuring pact** in which:
- closures are **not used initially**,
- the company first implements a **full package of negotiated measures**:
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - temporary wage/bonus restraint,
  - productivity measures,
  - and investment commitments,
- and a **joint review point** is set after a defined period, using agreed financial metrics.

If the agreed savings target is not met by that review point, then stakeholders would pre-negotiate **what additional measures become subject to renewed bargaining**. This does not remove the closure issue, but it delays it behind a test of whether negotiated alternatives truly work.

### 2) Site-specific protection with conditional workforce contribution
A second compromise position is a **site-by-site deal**:
- the parties identify which plants are viable with modernization and product allocation,
- those plants receive **binding site and investment guarantees**,
- in return, the workforce agrees to **time-limited cost and flexibility contributions**,
- while any site deemed structurally weak is handled through **conversion, partial downsizing, or special restructuring instruments** before closure is considered.

This bridges the gap by avoiding a blanket “all sites or none” approach and replacing it with **differentiated treatment by plant economics**.

### 3) “Closure as last-resort only” with stronger non-closure savings
A third option is a **formal last-resort clause**:
- closures and compulsory redundancies remain legally possible,
- but only after the company proves, with audited plant-level data, that:
  1. all negotiated alternatives were exhausted,
  2. the remaining footprint still misses the target,
  3. and no equivalent conversion or transfer solution exists.

In exchange, labor would need to accept a **larger non-closure savings package** than it has currently indicated, including possibly:
- stronger productivity commitments,
- longer work-time flexibility,
- and a defined employee contribution.

This preserves management’s fallback option while giving labor a more robust guarantee that closures are not the first move.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Industrial Union
**What exact savings package, in quantified annual terms, would you accept as sufficient to keep closures off the table, and by when can you deliver it plant by plant?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What specific evidence threshold would make you agree that negotiated alternatives are truly equivalent to closures, so that closures could be removed from the decision path?**

### Regional State Government
**If a plant-by-plant model shows one site remains uneconomic even after all negotiated measures, what intervention are you prepared to support short of closure?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to presenting a transparent plant-level model, including closure and non-closure scenarios, before any irreversible personnel or site decisions are announced?**

### CFO / Industrial Planning Specialist
**What minimum savings, utilization, and timing thresholds would you define as the point at which a negotiated alternative becomes economically credible without closures?**

### Institutional Investors
**What margin and return-on-sales improvement would be sufficient for you to support a non-closure restructuring, even if it requires larger upfront concessions and a longer payback period?**

### Works Council
**Would you accept any differentiated solution by plant if it preserved the majority of German sites, and if so, what conditions would make that acceptable?**
