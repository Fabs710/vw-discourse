## Core impasse

The central deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. All sides broadly accept that it is. The impasse lies in **how much capacity adjustment is unavoidable and who must bear the risk**.

More specifically, the conflict is between two incompatible demands:

1. **Labour / works council / state:** restructuring must begin without closures or compulsory redundancies, using negotiated measures first and preserving jobs and sites as the default.
2. **Management / family / investors:** closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out in advance, because they may be needed if negotiated measures do not close the structural cost gap.

That creates a procedural stalemate:
- one side wants **binding protection first, then numbers**;
- the other wants **numbers first, then no guarantee**.

So the dispute is not over the diagnosis, but over the **sequence, credibility, and enforceability** of the solution.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Sequenced restructuring with a binding review gate
A staged deal could be structured as follows:
- Phase 1: implement a substantial negotiated package immediately
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - working-time adjustments
  - productivity measures
  - temporary compensation moderation
- Phase 2: defer any closure decision until a fixed review date
- Phase 3: at the review date, apply jointly agreed plant-level metrics to determine whether further measures are required

To bridge the conflict, this proposal would need:
- a short, defined review period
- jointly agreed KPI thresholds
- automatic escalation only if the targets are missed
- a commitment that no closure announcements are made before the review

This gives labour a real non-closure trial, while preserving management’s ability to act if the numbers fail.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-tiered settlement with differentiated protection
Instead of a single rule for all plants, the parties could split sites into categories:
- **Category A:** clearly viable plants receive strong job and investment commitments
- **Category B:** borderline plants receive negotiated capacity reduction, reassignment, or product allocation changes
- **Category C:** structurally weak plants enter a protected transition process, with exhaustive alternatives tested before any closure step

This could bridge the main divide by:
- avoiding a blanket closure threat
- recognizing that not all plants have the same economics
- preserving the most viable industrial base
- allowing only tightly governed intervention at the weakest sites

To make this acceptable, the model must be transparent, site-specific, and tied to investment commitments.

---

### Proposal 3: “No forced redundancies first” with conditional fallback
A compromise could establish a hierarchy of measures:
1. voluntary exits
2. natural attrition
3. redeployment and retraining
4. working-time flexibility
5. productivity improvements
6. if, and only if, the quantified gap remains, a limited fallback discussion on capacity reduction

This would not ban all future adjustment, but it would set a clear order of preference and require proof before any stronger measure.

For labour, the key benefit is that forced layoffs are not the default.
For management and investors, the key benefit is that the fallback remains available if the package proves insufficient.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Industrial Union
If plant closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded at the outset, what exact combination of voluntary exits, work-time measures, productivity gains, and investment changes do you believe will close the structural gap at plant level?

### To the Works Council
What concrete metrics and time limits would you accept as a fair test of a non-closure package before you would conclude that additional capacity adjustment must be discussed?

### To Management
Which plant-level savings targets can you commit to as achievable through negotiated measures alone, before reopening any discussion of closures or forced redundancies?

### To the Controlling Family
What binding protections on jobs, sites, and investment would you be willing to accept in exchange for a time-limited non-closure trial?

### To Institutional Investors
What level of quantified, durable margin improvement would you regard as sufficient to support a negotiated restructuring that avoids immediate closures?

### To the Regional State Government
Under what plant-level evidence would you be prepared to allow a site-specific transition process, even if that could ultimately include capacity reduction?

### To the Independent Expert
What minimum utilization and savings thresholds should define the point at which a non-closure solution is no longer economically credible?
