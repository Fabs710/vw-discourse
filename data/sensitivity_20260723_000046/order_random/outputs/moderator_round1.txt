## 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company has a **real competitiveness and cost problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **purely symbolic or short-term fix is insufficient**.
- Most parties accept that some form of **shared sacrifice** will be needed.
- There is general openness to **negotiated measures** such as working-time adjustments, redeployment, qualification, voluntary exits, and investment commitments.
- There is shared recognition that **future investment and product allocation** matter for any durable settlement.

## 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  The union, works council, and state government want closures excluded or treated only as an extreme last resort; management, family, and investors insist closures must remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor representatives oppose them; management and investors say they cannot be ruled out absolutely.
- **Scope of workforce contribution:**  
  Labor is willing to contribute only within a package that protects jobs and sites; capital-side actors want a more substantial and durable labor-cost and capacity reduction.
- **Site and employment guarantees:**  
  Labor wants binding guarantees through 2030; ownership and management say broad guarantees may be incompatible with restoring competitiveness.
- **Decision authority / co-determination:**  
  The state and works council emphasize governance, regional responsibility, and participation; management and investors want enough flexibility to execute restructuring.

## 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **meaningful, durable cost and capacity reduction** without plant closures.
- Whether **voluntary, negotiated instruments** can deliver savings equivalent to closures or forced layoffs.
- What **reciprocal contributions** from management, owners, and investors would accompany workforce concessions.
- What level of **job, site, and investment security** is economically feasible, and for how long.
- How to balance **regional/social consequences** against **long-term competitiveness and return on sales**.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **concrete package design**:
- specify the **required savings/capacity target**,
- list the **exact instruments** proposed to reach it,
- identify the **trade-offs each side is prepared to make**,
- and state which elements are **non-negotiable** versus **conditional**.

The next round should aim to test whether there is a **credible negotiated alternative to closures and compulsory redundancies** that still meets the company’s restructuring needs.
