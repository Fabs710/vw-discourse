## Core impasse
The main blockage is **not agreement on the diagnosis**. Nearly everyone accepts that the company has a structural competitiveness problem and needs durable savings.

The impasse is over **what counts as an acceptable restructuring tool and who carries the risk**:

1. **Union / works council / state** want:
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - no plant closures as the starting point,
   - binding job and site protections,
   - and a genuine chance for non-closure alternatives.

2. **Management / family / investors** want:
   - closures and redundancies to remain available if needed,
   - plant-by-plant flexibility,
   - and a package that demonstrably closes the cost gap.

So the deadlock is between:
- **employment protection and bargaining fairness**, versus
- **economic latitude and restructuring credibility**.

The expert’s threshold framing sharpens this: a compromise is only viable if it produces **durable, plant-level savings and sustainable utilization**. That means the real question is whether a non-closure path can meet the economic test without triggering the social red lines.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with a hard review gate
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a first phase made up entirely of negotiated measures:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- wage/bonus restraint,
- productivity measures,
- and product allocation changes.

Then trigger a **short, fixed review period** with jointly agreed KPIs.

**Key features:**
- no irreversible closure decisions before the review,
- no compulsory redundancies in phase one,
- plant-level targets defined in advance,
- automatic escalation only if targets are missed.

**Purpose:**  
This bridges the gap by giving labor a real non-closure trial while giving capital a measurable fallback if the package does not close the structural gap.

---

### 2) Site-tiered settlement with protection tiers
**Proposal:**  
Classify sites into tiers based on the independent model:
- **Tier A:** clearly viable, receives investment and product allocation,
- **Tier B:** borderline, receives intensive restructuring support and transition measures,
- **Tier C:** structurally weak, subject to enhanced review before any closure or downsizing step.

**Key features:**
- no blanket approach,
- no automatic sacrifice of weaker sites,
- stronger obligations for transparency and transition support,
- different instruments for different plants.

**Purpose:**  
This bridges the conflict between the demand for plant-level economics and the insistence that all sites are not identical, while avoiding an across-the-board closure logic.

---

### 3) “No compulsory redundancies first” with defined exceptions
**Proposal:**  
Agree a hierarchy of measures where compulsory redundancies are excluded **at the outset** and may only be considered after:
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- working-time changes,
- and documented productivity/product-mix measures.

**Key features:**
- “first” is operationally defined, not open-ended,
- exceptions require a documented failure of prior measures,
- the sequence is tied to the plant-level model.

**Purpose:**  
This preserves the workforce’s central concern while avoiding an absolute ban that management and investors reject.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To the Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What minimum plant-level savings, utilization, and time horizon would make you accept that a non-closure package is economically credible without closures or compulsory redundancies?

### To the Works Council
**Question:**  
Which specific site-level KPIs would you accept as proof that a negotiated alternative is working, and what would count as a failure that triggers the next step?

### To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a defined first-phase package with no closure announcements and no compulsory redundancies before the review gate, if the workforce accepts measurable concessions?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to back a time-limited no-closure test phase if it is tied to hard financial metrics and a binding escalation mechanism?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum recurring savings coverage and payback period would you regard as sufficient to support a non-closure settlement?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What concrete governance conditions must be met for you to support a restructuring package that preserves regional employment while still allowing site differentiation?

### To the Expert / CFO
**Question:**  
Can you define a single plant-level threshold set — for savings, utilization, and payback — that all parties can use as the shared decision rule for moving from negotiation to escalation?
