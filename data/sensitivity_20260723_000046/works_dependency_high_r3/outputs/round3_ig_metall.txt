I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even management on one essential point: **this company needs a durable restructuring now**. A cosmetic fix, a postponement, or a PR compromise will not hold.

I also agree that:
- internal flexibility matters,
- qualification and redeployment matter,
- working-time solutions matter,
- and employees must contribute if the package is fair and reciprocal.

That remains the union’s position. We are not refusing adjustment.

## Where I disagree
### 1) German plant closures are not an acceptable “starting point”
Management, the family, and investors keep describing closures as if they are simply a rational instrument. They are not neutral. They are the point at which trust, skills, and industrial relationships are destroyed.

If you want a negotiated solution, you do **not** begin by putting German plant closures on the table as leverage and then ask the workforce to pay for the privilege of avoiding them.

I do not accept that logic.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies remain unacceptable
I will say it again: **no forced layoffs**.

Not “not preferred.” Not “only if necessary.” Not “as a fallback if the numbers do not work.”  
If compulsory redundancies remain a live instrument, then the burden is being shifted onto workers after years of concessions and uncertainty.

That is exactly what we will not accept.

### 3) A wage contribution without binding security is one-sided
If management wants a labour-cost contribution, then it must be tied to real guarantees:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a binding job-security framework,
- protection of the collective wage agreement,
- and concrete investment commitments in Germany.

Without that, there is no fair bargain. There is only pressure.

## To management
You say you need a durable reduction in cost and capacity. Fine. Then show us the package that achieves that **without closures and without forced redundancies**.

If you cannot do that, then your claim is not “we have no choice.” It is “we prefer to put the risk on workers.”

That is not acceptable.

And I want to be very clear: the union is not going to sign off on a wage agreement that becomes the vehicle for plant closures or job destruction.

## To the family holding and investors
You keep saying “long-term value.” Then prove that you understand what creates value in an industrial company:
- stable labour relations,
- retained know-how,
- predictable production,
- and a workforce that still trusts management.

If you break that, you may improve short-term metrics. You weaken the company’s industrial base.

So no, I do not accept the argument that closures are merely a hard but normal tool. They are a profound strategic damage.

## To the regional state
Your role matters. If you mean what you say, then your blocking power should be used to prevent a destructive settlement, not to stand aside while management makes closures the default option.

The state should insist on:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and a genuine industrial plan for Germany.

Anything less is not protection of the industrial base; it is commentary on its decline.

## On the moderator’s “trial period” idea
I am open to a **time-bound, quantified, no-closure / no-forced-layoff package** only if it is real and enforceable.

But I am not interested in a short pause that simply postpones the same threat. If a “trial” means the company keeps the knife on the table and reopens the whole question in a few months, then that is not a settlement.

A settlement must give:
- real job security,
- real site security,
- and a real framework for employee contribution.

## What I would consider a serious deal
A serious package could include:
- capacity reduction through internal measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary transitions,
- early retirement and phased exits,
- qualification and redeployment,
- a fair and limited employee contribution,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a binding no-forced-redundancy and no-closure framework for German sites.

That is a credible settlement. It is not weakness; it is shared sacrifice.

## What I will not accept
- plant closures as bargaining leverage,
- compulsory redundancies,
- wage cuts without reciprocity,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or a restructuring where workers carry the pain while management and owners keep all options.

If that is the direction management insists on, then it should expect resistance. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce will not be asked to surrender its wage framework and jobs in the name of “credibility.”

## Bottom line
The union remains open to a negotiated restructuring. But the conditions are clear:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Protection of the collective wage framework.**
- **Binding job and site security.**
- **Employee contribution only in a fair, reciprocal deal.**

If management can bring a fully quantified alternative that meets those conditions, we can talk seriously. If not, then the conflict is being forced from the other side, not by the workforce.
