We are now at the point where everyone is being asked to choose between **real restructuring** and **social destruction dressed up as strategy**. The works council will not accept that false choice.

## First: what we agree with
I agree with the diagnosis that the company needs a durable solution. I agree that internal flexibility, qualification, redeployment, working-time instruments, and voluntary exits are legitimate tools. I also agree that endless delay is not acceptable.

But agreement on the need for change does **not** mean agreement on closures, forced redundancies, or breaking the job-security framework.

---

## Where I disagree — and why

### 1) “Credibility” cannot mean starting from plant closures
Management, the family, and investors keep using “credible” as if it means only one thing: cuts that are painful enough for capital to believe them.

That is not credibility. That is a demand that the workforce prove loyalty by accepting destruction.

A restructuring is not credible because it is brutal. It is credible if it is:
- economically sound,
- durable,
- socially sustainable,
- and legally and industrially capable of implementation.

If you cannot show that without German plant closures, then the claim is not proven. It is merely asserted.

And I repeat: **German plant closures are not acceptable.**

### 2) Compulsory redundancies remain a red line
The same applies to forced layoffs.

The moderator’s “tiered restructuring” language does not change the basic issue: if compulsory redundancies remain available, then the burden ultimately lands on workers who have already carried this company through previous rounds of restructuring.

That is not a partnership. That is a transfer of risk downward.

We will not accept a deal that says:
- first waive guarantees,
- then accept flexibility,
- then lose jobs anyway when management decides the numbers still do not work.

No. If the company wants contributions, it must first commit to:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- and a binding security framework.

### 3) A time-limited guarantee is only acceptable if it is genuine
The moderator speaks of a “time-limited no-closure / no-forced-redundancy trial period.”

Let me be very clear: a trial period is not enough if it is just a postponement mechanism.

We are not interested in a six- or twelve-month pause that ends with the same knife hanging over the workforce. If management wants a serious contribution from employees, then the guarantee must be real and binding for a meaningful horizon — through 2030 — with no closure threats in the meantime.

Otherwise, you are asking us to surrender now for a promise that expires before the pain is even behind us.

---

## On the call for a quantified comparison
Yes, management should produce the numbers. But numbers do not create legitimacy by themselves.

If management presents a “no-closure” scenario and says it leaves a residual gap, my question is simple:

- What exactly is the gap?
- Why can it not be closed by internal measures, management action, investment allocation, or owner contribution?
- Why is the first answer still always jobs and sites?

If the remaining shortfall is small, then the burden should not be dumped onto the workforce through closures or forced layoffs. That would be an admission that management wants the easiest path, not the fairest one.

So yes: bring the numbers. But do not expect those numbers to override our red lines automatically.

---

## To management
You say you want a durable reduction in cost and capacity. Then show me:
- the quantified savings from non-destructive measures,
- the actual contribution expected from management and owners,
- and the investment commitments for German future products.

Do **not** come back with a package that assumes:
- plant closure as an option,
- forced redundancy as a fallback,
- and wage restraint from workers as the main adjustment tool.

That is exactly the model we are resisting.

If the company truly wants a reset, then management must also reset its own logic:
- stop treating the workforce as the adjustable variable,
- stop using closure threats as leverage,
- and stop pretending that co-determination is an obstacle rather than the mechanism that makes a social settlement possible.

---

## To the family holding and investors
You say long-term value matters. Then act like it.

Long-term value is not preserved by:
- destroying industrial trust,
- hollowing out German sites,
- and forcing a conflict that will poison labour relations for years.

If you insist on “durable competitiveness,” then durability must include:
- secure production sites,
- skilled workforce retention,
- co-determined governance,
- and predictability for the regions that host this company.

A company that breaks faith with its workforce may improve a spreadsheet. It also weakens its own industrial backbone.

---

## To the regional state
Your support matters, but it must be more than moral comfort.

If you say no closures and no forced redundancies, then use the blocking power that comes with your role. Do not wait for management to “discover” alternatives out of goodwill. They will not, unless the governance structure compels them to.

The state should be insisting on:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of co-determination,
- and a real industrial plan for German investment and future production.

That is not radical. That is responsible stewardship of the industrial base.

---

## What the works council is prepared to discuss
We are not refusing adjustment. We are refusing destruction.

We are prepared to discuss:
- internal capacity reduction,
- working-time changes,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- phased retirement,
- and limited employee contributions.

But those discussions only make sense if the company gives binding guarantees first:
- **every German plant stays open,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **co-determination remains intact,**
- **and the job-security framework is preserved.**

Without that, any “contribution” becomes a one-way concession.

---

## Bottom line
The works council’s position remains unchanged:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
- **Preserve co-determination and the job-security framework.**
- **Employee contribution only in a settlement that secures jobs and sites.**

So to management, the family, and investors: bring a real alternative if you have one. Not rhetoric. Not pressure. Not staged inevitability.

If you do not, then you are not negotiating a restructuring. You are trying to force the workforce to pay for management’s failure.

And that is where we will continue to resist — with the full weight of the workforce behind us.
