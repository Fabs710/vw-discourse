The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines.

We recognize that the company faces serious pressure and that a restructuring is necessary. But the burden cannot be placed one-sidedly on the workforce while management breaks historic job-security guarantees and threatens sites that are the backbone of this company and of entire regions.

What we will argue for is a negotiated solution that protects every German plant, preserves employment through 2030 with binding guarantees, and secures the company’s future through:
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- internal flexibility and working-time solutions,
- qualification and redeployment instead of layoffs,
- a fair employee contribution only as part of a broader settlement,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the works council in any restructuring path.

If management wants the workforce to contribute, then it must put something substantial on the table:
- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- protection of co-determination and existing job-security frameworks,
- and a credible industrial plan with investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany.

If that is not available, then we will resist this plan with all the means at our disposal, including industrial action and our rights in co-determination. We will not be handed a fait accompli.
