### Moderator Summary — Round 4

#### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix;
- **numbers and a quantified comparison** are now necessary;
- internal measures should be considered, including:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - voluntary exits,
  - phased retirement,
  - and some form of employee contribution;
- a **scenario-based assessment** is the right next step, comparing:
  1. a no-closure / no-forced-redundancy package,
  2. a mixed compromise,
  3. a closure-based restructuring.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The major unresolved disagreements are:

- **Plant closures**
  - Labour representatives and the state oppose closures as a first-choice solution.
  - Management, the family holding, and institutional investors do not rule them out in principle.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour and the state reject them.
  - Management and capital say they cannot be excluded if the economics do not work.

- **Job and site guarantees**
  - Labour and the state want binding security.
  - Management and capital argue that guarantees are only credible if the underlying economics are viable.

- **How to interpret the expert input**
  - All sides accept that internal measures can likely close part of the gap.
  - They disagree on whether the remaining gap could still justify closures or forced layoffs.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The central trade-offs still not resolved are:

- **Security vs. viability**
  - How much job and site protection can be guaranteed without undermining competitiveness?

- **Internal measures vs. footprint reduction**
  - Can non-closure measures close the full recurring cost gap, or only part of it?

- **Employee contribution vs. reciprocity**
  - What level of worker concession is acceptable, and what binding counter-guarantees are required?

- **Short-term stability vs. long-term value**
  - Whether avoiding closures now preserves value, or simply delays necessary restructuring.

- **Governance limits**
  - The extent to which co-determination and state influence can shape the outcome without preventing economically necessary change.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should stay focused on a **structured, quantified comparison** of the three scenarios. The discussion should specifically identify:
- recurring annual savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- headcount effects,
- capacity effects,
- investment requirements,
- and the resulting margin path.

To keep the debate productive, participants should address one question directly:  
**Which combination of measures can restore competitiveness while preserving as much employment and industrial capacity as possible?**
