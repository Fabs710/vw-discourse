## Facilitator summary: where the impasse now lies

The core impasse is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which instruments are permissible before the company is allowed to move to irreversible footprint reduction**.

In procedural terms, the conflict has narrowed to three linked disputes:

1. **Whether German plant closures are excluded in principle or only as a last resort.**
2. **Whether compulsory redundancies are ruled out absolutely or only after a quantified no-closure test fails.**
3. **Whether labor-side contributions can be accepted before, and only if matched by, binding security and investment commitments.**

The expert input suggests a further practical issue:  
there is agreement that a non-closure package might close **part** of the gap, but disagreement over whether it can close the **full fixed-cost gap** if utilization remains structurally too low.

So the central procedural bottleneck is:
**no shared threshold has been accepted for when non-destructive measures are “enough,” and when footprint reduction becomes permissible.**

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: A quantified two-step restructuring process
A structured compromise could be:

- **Step 1:** A time-bound, quantified no-closure / no-forced-redundancy package is implemented first.
- **Step 2:** A jointly defined review date assesses whether agreed savings, capacity reductions, and utilization targets were met.
- **Step 3:** Only if the targets are missed would parties reopen the question of more intrusive measures.

This would allow:
- labour to obtain an initial protection period,
- management to retain a route to escalation if the numbers fail,
- the state and family to preserve a decision path tied to evidence rather than principle alone.

---

### Proposal 2: Binding security in exchange for measurable employee contribution
A second bridge position could be:

- binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy guarantees for a defined horizon,
- in exchange for measurable employee-side contributions such as:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - temporary wage moderation or deferral,
  - voluntary exit instruments,
  - early retirement,
  - redeployment and qualification measures.

To make this credible, the package would need:
- explicit savings targets,
- a defined investment commitment for German sites,
- and an agreed point at which the guarantees expire or are revisited.

This would convert the dispute from “trust me” to “trade-off for trade-off.”

---

### Proposal 3: Mixed footprint reduction without immediate closure
A third compromise would be to avoid immediate plant closure, while still accepting structural adjustment through:

- site specialization,
- consolidation of functions,
- model or platform reallocation,
- capacity reduction by internal means,
- and targeted workforce reduction through voluntary instruments.

This would attempt to answer management and capital’s concern about fixed costs, while preserving the labour side’s core demand that closures and forced layoffs not be the first resort.

It is procedurally useful because it separates:
- **capacity reduction**, from
- **site destruction**.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific combination of internal measures, volume shifts, and employee contributions would you accept as sufficient to prove that German sites can remain open while still delivering a materially lower cost base?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-bound package with measurable savings and a fixed review date if it included temporary employee contributions but also binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy guarantees?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction target that you would accept as evidence that a non-closure route has genuinely failed before any irreversible measure is considered?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete governance conditions would you attach to your support so that a no-closure compromise is enforceable rather than merely declaratory?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to back a mixed compromise that preserves German sites for a defined period if it delivers measurable cost reductions and a credible investment path?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a restructuring that avoids closures if it demonstrably restores margins through quantified recurring savings, even if the adjustment is slower and more negotiated?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum data set would you require to determine whether the non-closure scenario is truly sufficient, or whether some footprint reduction is mathematically unavoidable?**

## Suggested next procedural step

The most constructive next move would be to ask management and the expert team to produce a **side-by-side scenario table** with three options:

1. **No-closure / no-forced-redundancy package**
2. **Mixed compromise with specialization and targeted reduction**
3. **Closure-based restructuring**

Each should show:
- recurring annual savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- headcount effects,
- plant/site impact,
- investment needs,
- and the expected margin path.

That would move the discourse from repeated red lines to a testable comparison.
