### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties recognize that the company is under serious competitive and financial pressure.
- There is broad agreement that a restructuring is necessary and that a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.
- Several possible instruments are acknowledged across the table: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, redeployment, qualification, and internal flexibility.
- There is general support for a package that is durable, quantified, and economically credible.
- Most parties accept that employees may need to contribute, but only in some form of broader settlement.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and regional state reject them; management, the family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour-side actors and the state reject them; management and capital-side actors do not rule them out if other measures fail.
- **Job guarantees / site guarantees through 2030:**  
  Labour-side actors and the state want binding protection; management and shareholders say this cannot be promised if it blocks restructuring.
- **Co-determination and governance:**  
  Labour and state insist restructuring must remain within co-determination frameworks; management and capital want flexibility and warn against governance deadlock.
- **How much burden workers should carry:**  
  Labour wants contributions only in exchange for binding security and investment commitments; management and shareholders want a stronger, more immediate labour-cost and capacity adjustment.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The exact balance between **job/site protection** and **cost reduction**.
- Whether a sufficiently strong restructuring can be achieved **without closures** or **without forced redundancies**.
- What concrete employee contributions would be acceptable, and what concrete guarantees/investments would be returned.
- How to reconcile the need for **speed and credibility** with the need for **social and political acceptability**.
- What governance mechanism could allow a deal to pass while avoiding a breakdown between shareholder, state, and co-determination interests.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **specific package options** with numbers, timelines, and measurable effects.
- Ask management to present at least one **fully quantified alternative scenario** to closures and forced layoffs.
- Ask labour-side actors to specify which forms of flexibility, contribution, or capacity reduction could be acceptable under which guarantees.
- Test whether there is a possible package combining:  
  **no forced redundancies, no German closures, binding investment commitments, and a defined employee contribution** — and, if not, identify exactly which element each side considers non-negotiable.
