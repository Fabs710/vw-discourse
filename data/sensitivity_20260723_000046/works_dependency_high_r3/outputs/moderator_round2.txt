## Core impasse

The central deadlock is a **definition gap over what counts as a “credible” restructuring**:

- **Labour / state side**: credibility requires **no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding security** in exchange for employee contribution.
- **Management / owners / investors side**: credibility requires **verifiable cost and capacity reduction**, and they will not pre-commit to ruling out closures or headcount reductions without proof that alternatives achieve the same effect.

So the dispute is not just about instruments; it is about **sequencing and proof**:
- labour wants **guarantees first, numbers second**;
- capital wants **numbers first, guarantees conditional**.

That sequencing mismatch is the core impasse.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### 1) Scenario-based “no-closure first” package with trigger points
A compromise could start from a **preferred no-closure / no-forced-layoff scenario** for a defined period, but include:
- quantified savings targets,
- capacity reduction targets,
- and **pre-agreed trigger points**.

If the targets are not met by a set review date, the parties would then move to a second-stage negotiation on additional measures.

**Purpose:** gives labour a real no-closure attempt first, while giving management a structured escape from open-ended delay.

---

### 2) Binding security in exchange for a measurable contribution package
A second compromise could pair:
- **time-limited job/site guarantees** for German plants,
- **no compulsory redundancies during the guarantee period**,
- and **clear employee contributions** such as working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, phased exits, and voluntary severance.

In return, management would provide:
- a quantified investment plan,
- product allocation commitments,
- and a joint review mechanism.

**Purpose:** converts abstract trust demands into a reciprocal package with time limits and deliverables.

---

### 3) Tiered restructuring by site and function, not a blanket approach
A third compromise would distinguish between:
- plants / functions that are structurally viable and should be protected,
- and areas where capacity can be reduced through attrition, redeployment, or voluntary exits.

This would replace a binary “all plants vs. closures” conflict with a **site-by-site and function-by-function restructuring map**, jointly reviewed with the works council, union, and state.

**Purpose:** reduces the chance that one side is forced into an all-or-nothing position.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**Question:** If management presents a fully quantified no-closure plan that meets the required savings target, what specific employee contribution would the works council be prepared to authorize, and for how long?

### Industrial Union
**Question:** What minimum combination of working-time, wage, and redeployment measures would the union consider sufficient to support a binding no-forced-layoff agreement through 2030?

### Group & Brand Management
**Question:** Will management commit to presenting, by a fixed deadline, a side-by-side quantified comparison of: no-closure, closure, and mixed scenarios, including headcount, margin, investment, and timing effects?

### Regional State Government
**Question:** What specific governance or blocking actions is the state prepared to use if management does not bring forward a viable no-closure alternative within the agreed timeframe?

### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:** Is the family holding willing to support a time-limited no-closure / no-forced-redundancy trial period if it is tied to measurable milestones and a review clause?

### Institutional Investors
**Question:** If a no-closure scenario demonstrably closes most of the margin gap but leaves a small residual shortfall, would investors accept a conditional bridging package rather than insist on immediate structural cuts?
