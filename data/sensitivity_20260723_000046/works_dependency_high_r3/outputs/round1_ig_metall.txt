We agree on one point: the company is under real pressure, and a restructuring will be necessary.

But I need to be equally clear: **we will not accept a solution in which workers carry the entire burden while management and owners get a free pass.** And we will not accept **plant closures in Germany** or **compulsory redundancies** as the price of this crisis.

The works council is right to stress that this cannot be done by diktat. The workforce has already shown willingness to engage, and our counter-proposal was not symbolic: it signalled openness to **capacity reduction, labour-cost contributions, and internal adjustments**. That remains on the table — but only if it is part of a **fair shared-sacrifice package**.

What is not acceptable:
- closures of German plants,
- forced layoffs,
- open-ended insecurity,
- and breaking the collective wage framework while asking employees alone to absorb the pain.

If management wants real cooperation, then it must also contribute. That means:
- binding guarantees for jobs and sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- using working-time, redeployment, qualification, and capacity tools before any cuts,
- a fair employee contribution only in exchange for concrete security,
- and clear commitments from owners and management on investment, future products, and the industrial base in Germany.

I also want to be blunt about leverage: the peace obligation has ended, and the workforce will not be asked to wait indefinitely while plant closure remains on the table. If this is pushed toward layoffs and closures, then industrial action will escalate.

So yes, we are prepared to negotiate hard. But the line is clear: **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no one-sided austerity for workers.** If there is a durable solution, it must protect jobs, protect sites, and distribute the burden fairly.
