The minimum data set I would require is a **site-level and product-level cost model** sufficient to compare the three scenarios on a like-for-like basis.

## 1) Core operating data
For each German plant and major function:
- current annual output and capacity
- utilization rate
- product/model allocation by plant
- direct labor hours per vehicle or per unit
- direct labor cost per hour and total direct labor cost
- indirect labor / overhead costs
- energy, maintenance, logistics, and depreciation allocation
- quality, scrap, and rework costs
- fixed versus variable cost split

## 2) Restructuring instrument data
For each scenario:
- headcount reductions by site, function, and timing
- voluntary exit volumes and assumed acceptance rate
- early retirement / phased retirement take-up
- working-time reductions or flexibility changes
- wage moderation, deferrals, or concessions
- redeployment and qualification costs
- severance and social-plan costs
- closure / mothballing / transfer costs where relevant
- one-off capex required to make the non-closure option work

## 3) Commercial and product assumptions
- forecast European and German demand by model
- expected volume shift between ICE and EV lines
- pricing assumptions
- contribution margin by model
- planned product allocation over the next 3–5 years
- launch timing and investment timing
- assumption on import substitution versus local production

## 4) Financial output needed
For each scenario, over at least 5 years:
- recurring annual savings
- one-off restructuring costs
- net present value of the package
- break-even volume
- margin path
- return on sales path
- sensitivity to volume, price, wage, and utilization changes

## 5) Labor-market impact data
- jobs eliminated, relocated, or retained by site
- regional employment multiplier effects
- supplier spillovers
- estimated transition time for displaced workers
- expected share absorbed internally versus external labor market

## What would let me determine sufficiency
A non-closure scenario is economically sufficient only if it:
1. closes most of the recurring cost gap,
2. does so at acceptable one-off cost,
3. restores a credible margin path,
4. and does not rely on unrealistic take-up assumptions for voluntary measures.

If the residual gap after all non-destructive measures remains large, then some footprint reduction may indeed be economically hard to avoid. But that conclusion cannot be made responsibly without the above data.

## The single most important missing item
If I had to pick one:  
**a site-by-site fixed-cost and utilization model, with model allocation and margin contribution by plant.**

Without that, no one can tell whether the company has a labor-cost problem, a capacity problem, a product-allocation problem, or some combination.
