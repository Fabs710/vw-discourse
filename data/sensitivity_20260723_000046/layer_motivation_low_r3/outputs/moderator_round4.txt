### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to a single structural fault line:

**Labor-side stakeholders and the state shareholder want a binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy settlement, while management, the controlling family, and institutional investors will only accept that if the closure-free package is quantitatively equivalent to footprint reduction in recurring savings, utilization, and timing.**

So the impasse is not “whether restructuring is needed,” but:

1. **whether closure-free measures can close the full savings gap**, and  
2. **whether any agreement can contain credible, enforceable safeguards without becoming a disguised closure threat or, conversely, a value-destructive freeze.**

A second impasse sits inside that one:
- labor and the state want **guarantees first**,
- management and capital want **proof of equivalence first**.

---

### Facilitator proposals that could bridge positions

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited closure moratorium with quantified review gate
A compromise structure could be:

- a **time-limited moratorium on closures**,
- paired with a **plant-by-plant savings and utilization test** at fixed milestones,
- and a **predefined review mechanism** if quantified targets are missed.

Safeguards would include:
- no closures during the moratorium,
- no compulsory redundancies during the initial phase,
- mandatory reporting on recurring savings, headcount effects, and utilization,
- independent verification of the numbers.

This may bridge:
- labor/state demand for immediate security,
- management/investors’ demand for measurable accountability.

#### Proposal 2: “No closures now” in exchange for deeper, binding economic concessions
A second compromise could be a **closure-free package with stronger internal economic measures**, including:

- larger management and overhead reductions,
- owner/family capital or earnings contribution,
- binding investment commitments for German sites,
- more aggressive shift reduction and capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits plus redeployment,
- stronger productivity commitments,
- explicit timeline and reporting obligations.

This may bridge:
- labor’s preference for site preservation,
- capital’s demand for real savings,
- by making the no-closure path economically harder and therefore more credible.

#### Proposal 3: Conditional footprint flexibility limited to non-core functions
A third compromise could separate **core production sites** from **support and non-core activities**:

- core German plants receive site guarantees,
- while indirect functions, logistics, administration, or selected production streams may be consolidated,
- with no compulsory redundancies as the first tool,
- and only if redeployment and voluntary exits are insufficient.

This may bridge:
- labor’s strongest red lines on plant closures,
- management’s need for meaningful fixed-cost reduction,
- by shifting the adjustment burden to less sensitive parts of the footprint first.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council / Betriebsrat
**If the independent model shows that closure-free measures leave a material residual savings gap, which additional measures are you prepared to accept to close that gap without moving to closures or compulsory redundancies?**

#### To Industrial Union
**What specific, measurable labor-side contribution would you put on the table if management, owners, and investors commit to binding site-security and investment guarantees?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact recurring savings remain after your best closure-free package, and why are those residual costs not bridgeable through deeper management, owner, or productivity measures?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What objective financial threshold or utilization test would you accept as sufficient proof that a closure-free plan is no longer viable?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**What binding capital or earnings contribution are you willing to make to make a closure-free restructuring economically credible?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a phased restructuring with a hard numeric review gate if it preserves sites initially but allows footprint changes only upon verified failure to meet savings targets?**

#### To the Independent Expert
**Which single metric should be treated as decisive in the next round: recurring savings, utilization recovery, or residual fixed-cost burden after restructuring?**
