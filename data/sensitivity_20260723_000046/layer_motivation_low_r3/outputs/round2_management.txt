Let me respond directly, because the situation is too serious for slogans.

## First: what I agree with
I agree on three points raised by labour, ownership, and the state:

1. **A cosmetic restructuring is not acceptable.**  
   We need something durable and structural, not a temporary patch.

2. **The company must remain investable.**  
   If we do not restore margins, we will not have the funds for future products, technology, and the transition to 2030.

3. **Open conflict destroys value.**  
   A prolonged strike or endless confrontation would hurt output, reputation, and the brand. That is not in anyone’s interest.

## Second: what I do not accept
I need to be equally clear: I do **not** accept the workforce red lines as absolute.

### 1) “No plant closures”
That is not a credible starting point.

We are carrying **structural overcapacity** in Germany. If every site must stay open regardless of economics, then we are not restructuring — we are preserving an uncompetitive cost base and postponing the crisis.

If there are alternatives that achieve the same structural savings, I am open to them. But “no closures” as a blanket rule is not compatible with the scale of the problem.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies”
I understand why this is demanded. But if we are serious about restoring competitiveness, then **headcount reduction must be part of the outcome** in some form. Voluntary measures alone are unlikely to close the gap quickly enough.

I am prepared to discuss the mix — voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time adjustments, transfer measures — but I will not pretend that no workforce reduction is needed.

### 3) “No meaningful labour-cost contribution”
That is also not acceptable.

If the company’s German cost base is materially above target and above competitors, then labour costs must be part of the correction. We cannot ask shareholders and the business to absorb the full burden while leaving legacy cost structures intact.

## Third: the central issue
The core question is not whether there is pain. There will be pain either way. The question is whether we apply it **now, structurally, and in a controlled way**, or whether we wait until the company is weaker and the options are worse.

Management’s responsibility is to stop the erosion before it becomes irreversible. That is why I have raised closures and deep restructuring. Not because I enjoy confrontation, but because the numbers do not allow denial.

## Fourth: what I can work with
I am not demanding closures for their own sake. I am demanding **equivalent structural savings**.

So if the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must come forward with a package that is truly comparable in effect:
- substantial and durable capacity reduction,
- a meaningful labour-cost contribution,
- credible headcount reduction,
- productivity improvements,
- and a settlement that restores the brand’s return-on-sales path.

That is the test.

### What would be constructive
A package could include:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- staged reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and a negotiated employee contribution,

**if and only if** these measures close the cost gap in a durable way.

But I will say this plainly:  
**voluntary measures plus promises are not enough unless they deliver real, lasting savings at the scale required.**

## To the works council
You ask for every plant to stay open, every guarantee preserved, and no compulsory redundancies. I understand the motive, but you are asking management to accept a structure that may remain uncompetitive. That is not responsible stewardship.

You say closures are a choice. Yes — and so is allowing the company to drift into weaker margins, lower investment, and ultimately more jobs at risk. I will not choose that.

## To the union
You say any employee contribution must be matched by management and owners. On that, I agree in principle.

But I reject the idea that burden-sharing means the company abandons structural change. A balanced package is not one where everyone gives a little and the core problem remains. It is one where each side contributes to a result that actually restores competitiveness.

If the union is prepared to negotiate a real labour-cost contribution in exchange for credible investment commitments and a durable future model plan, then there is room to work. If the union’s position is simply “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real cost adjustment,” then there is no solution there.

## To the regional state government
I appreciate your focus on the industrial base and your point that a destabilized company is a public problem as well as a corporate one.

But blocking decisive restructuring does not protect the region in the long run. A protected but uncompetitive structure is not a stable one. If the company cannot restore margins, then investment, jobs, and future technology will all be at risk.

So I need the state to be clear-eyed: preserving every site regardless of economics is not the same as preserving industrial strength.

## To the controlling family holding
I agree with your insistence on long-term value and strategic discipline. We cannot settle for a cosmetic deal. If a package does not materially improve competitiveness, it is not worth endorsing.

## Where this leaves us
We are prepared to negotiate. But let me set the boundary clearly:

- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No deal that leaves structural overcapacity untouched**
- **No settlement that fails to deliver durable labour-cost relief**
- **No future investment plan without the savings to fund it**

If the labour side wants to avoid closures, it must help produce a package that is economically equivalent. If it does not, then management will continue to pursue deeper structural measures, including plant-level rationalization.

I am not here to win a rhetorical argument. I am here to restore competitiveness. That requires hard decisions, and I will not pretend otherwise.

If needed, I suggest we now move to a **specific package**: quantified savings, quantified workforce contribution, and quantified investment commitments. But I will not start from the premise that every site is untouchable. That premise is exactly what the company can no longer afford.
