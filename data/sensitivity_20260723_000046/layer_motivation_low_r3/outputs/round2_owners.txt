We agree on the diagnosis that the status quo is not sustainable. We also agree that a cosmetic response would be value-destructive. On that, I am aligned with management, investors, labour, and the state.

Where I do **not** agree is with any attempt to turn “no closures” or “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute preconditions **without** an economically equivalent alternative on the table.

From the family holding’s perspective, the test is simple: **does the package restore competitiveness, margins, and long-term value without triggering open-ended conflict?** If yes, it can be supported. If no, it will not be supported.

### Where I agree
- A durable restructuring is necessary.
- Open industrial conflict would damage the company, the brand, and the ownership value.
- Labour should not be asked to carry the burden alone.
- Any solution must protect future investment capacity.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to escalation.

### Where I disagree
I cannot support a position that effectively says:
- every German plant must remain open regardless of economics,
- no workforce reduction can occur in any form,
- and meaningful labour-cost adjustment is off limits,

**while also expecting the company to become competitive again**.

That is not stewardship. That is delay.

Likewise, I do **not** support closures as a first reflex. Plant closure is a severe, irreversible step, and I am not interested in using it as a bargaining headline. But I also will not declare it taboo if the other side cannot close the cost gap through credible alternatives.

### What would move us
A package could be supported if it contains all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **A real employee contribution**
3. **Meaningful productivity and working-time changes**
4. **Voluntary exits, redeployment, and other non-forced measures first**
5. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies**
6. **A credible path back to target margins**

That is the standard. Anything less risks preserving the appearance of stability while locking in decline.

### To the works council and union
I hear your red lines, but they cannot be absolute if they prevent the company from restoring competitiveness. If you want to avoid closures and forced redundancies, bring forward a package that is **economically equivalent**, not simply politically more comfortable.

I will support a negotiated solution that preserves labour peace **if it actually solves the problem**. I will not support one that merely defers it.

### To management
You are right that the problem is structural. But I also want discipline from management: no rhetorical overreach, no treating every closure as inevitable, and no attempt to use the restructuring to impose more change than needed. Show the numbers, show the required savings, and be prepared to accept alternatives if they truly close the gap.

### To the state shareholder
Your role matters, and I respect the need to preserve regional industrial strength. But that strength depends on a company that can earn its cost of capital. A protected but uncompetitive structure is not sustainable. We should not confuse regional stability with a refusal to change.

### Bottom line
The family holding will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** that restores competitiveness and protects long-term value. We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that leaves structural overcapacity untouched,
- or a prolonged conflict that erodes value.

If the labour side can deliver a closure-free package that achieves the same structural effect, we are open to it. If not, then difficult measures remain on the table.
