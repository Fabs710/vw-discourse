### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not automatically compatible with restoring competitiveness
I hear the concern about trust and social stability. But if we need to reduce headcount materially, then saying “only voluntary measures” may simply not be enough.

I am open on the **mix**:

- retirement bridges,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- transfers,
- qualification measures.

But I will not accept a package that assumes headcount can stay effectively unchanged while the company magically becomes competitive again. That does not add up.

### 3) Labour-cost contribution must be real, not symbolic
The union and works council are right that burden-sharing must be broader than labour alone. But that does **not** mean labour’s contribution can be marginal.

If our German cost base is materially above target and above competitors, then there must be a meaningful, durable labour-cost adjustment. Otherwise the company cannot fund future products, technology, and the transition to 2030.

I am not asking for pain for its own sake. I am asking for a structure that can actually survive.
