We agree on the diagnosis: the status quo is not acceptable, and cosmetic restructuring will not restore value.

But I want to be very clear about what matters to capital-market shareholders: **competitiveness, margin recovery, and deal certainty**. We do not care whether that comes through plant closures, capacity cuts, or negotiated labour adjustments — we care whether the final package is **credible, durable, and sufficient**.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the cost base is too high and structural action is needed.
- **The union and works council are right** that open-ended conflict is value-destructive.
- **The family holding is right** that a weak compromise which leaves overcapacity in place will destroy long-term value.
- **The state is right** that a purely destructive settlement would be politically and socially unstable.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that keeps every plant open, preserves legacy guarantees unchanged, and still expects shareholders to accept permanently weak margins. That would be a transfer of value from owners to an uncompetitive cost structure. We cannot support deferred decline.

At the same time, I do **not** see plant closures as the goal in themselves. They are only justified if they are the **only** way to achieve the needed structural savings. If an alternative package can deliver the same economics with less disruption and lower execution risk, that is preferable from an investor perspective.

### What we require
For us to back a settlement, it must do all of the following:

1. **Close the cost gap materially**
   - not symbolically, but enough to restore a credible return-on-sales trajectory.

2. **Be durable**
   - temporary concessions are not enough if the problem simply returns in the next planning cycle.

3. **Include real burden-sharing**
   - labour contributions, management discipline, and owner support all need to be visible.

4. **Avoid prolonged industrial conflict**
   - strikes and escalating confrontation will destroy near-term value and undermine confidence.

5. **Give investors a believable path to re-rating**
   - we need to see margins, capacity, and cost structures moving back to competitive levels.

### On the current red lines
The labour-side position of “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no unilateral cuts” is only acceptable if there is a package on the table that **actually closes the gap**. If not, those are not red lines; they are vetoes against competitiveness.

The management-side position is also not automatically sufficient. Saying “closures are possible” is not enough. We need quantified evidence that the proposed measures, whatever their form, deliver the required savings and do so with acceptable execution risk.

### On burden sharing
We support a negotiated contribution from employees **if and only if** it is matched by:
- management cost discipline,
- credible investment commitments,
- and a restructuring that really improves economics.

We do **not** support a situation where shareholders are simply asked to tolerate lower returns while the business retains structurally uncompetitive costs.

### Our bottom line
We will support the outcome that **most credibly restores competitiveness and margins**.

That means:
- **Yes** to a negotiated package if it is economically equivalent to deeper restructuring.
- **Yes** to capacity reduction, voluntary exits, working-time adjustments, and productivity measures if they are sufficient.
- **Yes** to plant closures only if they are genuinely necessary to close the cost gap.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that destroys value.

So our message to all parties is simple: bring a package with **quantified savings, quantified workforce contributions, and a believable path to target margins**. If you cannot do that without closures, then the market will not reward avoidance. If you can do it without closures, that is better. But either way, the economics must be fixed.
