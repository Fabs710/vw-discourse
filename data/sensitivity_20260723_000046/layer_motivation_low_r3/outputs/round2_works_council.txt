We are not moving off the fundamental protections that make this company worth trusting in the first place.

I hear the repeated claim from management, investors, and the family side that “the cost base is too high” and that “something structural must happen.” That may be true as a diagnosis. It does **not** justify the conclusion you want to jump to: plant closures, forced redundancies, and the dismantling of hard-won guarantees.

### Where I agree
I agree on one point: a cosmetic plan is not enough. If the company needs a durable solution, then it needs real industrial and financial measures, not press releases.

I also agree that burden-sharing cannot fall on the workforce alone. If employees are expected to contribute, then management, owners, and shareholders must contribute too — visibly and materially.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that “structural adjustment” requires closing German plants or imposing compulsory redundancies. That is not a technical necessity; it is a choice. And it is a choice I reject.

I also do **not** accept the framing that our red lines are somehow unrealistic just because they constrain management. That is precisely what co-determination is for: to stop short-term cost cutting from destroying long-term industrial capacity, trust, and skilled employment.

To the management side: you have not shown that you need closures to close the gap. You have asserted it. That is not enough.

To the owners and investors: I hear the concern for long-term value. But long-term value does not come from gutting the company’s German industrial base and provoking open conflict with the workforce. A forced restructuring may look “decisive” on a slide deck, but it is not necessarily intelligent or sustainable.

### What we can discuss
We are prepared to discuss a package built around:
- capacity reduction without closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits only,
- productivity improvements,
- and a negotiated employee contribution,

but only if this is matched by **binding guarantees**:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of co-determination,
- preservation of the collective job-security framework,
- and a clear investment and product commitment for the German sites.

### What is non-negotiable
Let me say this plainly:
- **No plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral breaking of guarantees.**
- **No imposed restructuring that strips the workforce of its protections.**

If management’s “credible alternative” still depends on closing plants or pushing people out by force, then it is not a credible alternative from our side. It is a confrontation dressed up as necessity.

### On the trade-offs raised by the moderator
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** We reject the false choice that says competitiveness must be bought by destroying jobs. There are other ways to reduce costs.
- **Sites vs. capacity reduction:** Capacity can be reduced by flexible production, shifts, working-time arrangements, and internal restructuring. Site closure is not the only tool.
- **Employee contribution vs. guarantees:** If you want an employee contribution, then it must buy real guarantees — not vague promises.
- **Speed vs. stability:** A rushed settlement that breaks trust is worse than a careful one that preserves the industrial base.
- **Governance constraints vs. economic urgency:** Yes, governance matters, and yes, urgency matters. That is exactly why no side should act as if it can simply dictate terms.

### My bottom line
Bring us a **specific** alternative that:
1. keeps every German plant open,
2. rules out compulsory redundancies,
3. preserves co-determination and job security,
4. includes real contributions from management and owners,
5. and secures investment for the future.

If you cannot do that, then you are not asking for negotiation — you are asking the workforce to surrender its protections in the name of a plan that may well damage the company more than it helps it. We will not accept that.
