We are aligned on one core point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. The current cost base and margin profile are clearly under pressure, and as shareholders we want a durable return to competitiveness, not a cosmetic fix.

That said, I want to be very explicit about what would and would not be acceptable from an investor perspective.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the problem is structural, not cyclical.
- **The family owners are right** that a weak compromise that leaves the company uncompetitive destroys long-term value.
- **The union, works council, and state are right** that open-ended conflict is value-destructive and that any settlement must be durable.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that simply preserves every plant, every job guarantee, and every legacy cost while asking investors to absorb the margin damage. That is not a solution; it is deferred decline.

At the same time, I do **not** think plant closures or mass layoffs are valuable in themselves. If management can achieve the same structural savings through capacity reduction, voluntary exits, working-time changes, redeployment, and a serious labour-cost contribution, that is preferable because it is more predictable and less disruptive.

So our test is not ideological. It is financial:
- **Does the package restore competitive margins?**
- **Is it durable, not temporary?**
- **Is the cost reduction credible enough to re-rate the equity?**
- **Does it avoid prolonged industrial conflict that would destroy near-term value?**

### On the workforce’s red lines
I understand the concern about closures and forced redundancies, but from where we sit, absolute red lines are only acceptable if there is a **credible alternative** that closes the cost gap. If there is not, then insisting on perfect protection for all sites and all jobs is incompatible with shareholder value.

### On burden sharing
We agree that labour should not carry the burden alone. But shareholders also should not be asked to subsidize a structure that remains uncompetitive. We need:
- real employee contributions,
- management discipline,
- and a restructuring that materially improves returns.

### Our position
We would support:
- a negotiated restructuring,
- a meaningful and durable employee contribution,
- capacity reduction and operational simplification,
- and protections for investment in future products,

**if and only if** the final package convincingly restores margins and removes structural overcapacity.

We would **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that preserves uncompetitive labour costs,
- or a prolonged conflict that damages the brand and share price.

If management and the labour side can produce a settlement that meets those tests without resorting to closures, that is the best outcome. But if they cannot, then the market will not reward delay.
