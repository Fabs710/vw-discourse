We are now past the stage where broad sentiments help. I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all parties on the core diagnosis:
- the status quo is unsustainable,
- cosmetic change would destroy value,
- the company needs a durable restructuring,
- and open conflict would be harmful.

I also agree that burden-sharing must be broader than labour alone. Management, owners, and shareholders all have to contribute. That is not a concession from the family holding; it is basic stewardship.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to turn “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute starting conditions **without** a fully credible economic alternative.

From the controlling family’s perspective, the governing test is simple:
**Does the package restore competitiveness, margins, and long-term value in a durable way?**

If yes, we can support it.  
If no, then we cannot.

### To the works council and union
I understand your position, but I do not accept it as sufficient on its own.

You keep saying closures are not necessary and that capacity reduction can be achieved without them. That may be true — but only if you can demonstrate an alternative package with:
- quantified savings,
- durable headcount effects,
- real productivity gains,
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- and binding safeguards that do not simply preserve the existing cost structure.

So far I have heard strong red lines, but not yet a package that clearly closes the gap.

I also do **not** accept the idea that any workforce reduction must be voluntary only, regardless of scale. That may be politically comfortable, but it is not automatically economically sufficient. If the company needs substantial and durable adjustment, then the mix of measures has to be judged by results, not by slogans.

### To management
I agree that the current structure cannot simply be preserved. But I also want discipline from management:

- Do not treat closures as a rhetorical first resort.
- Do not assume every site is dispensable.
- Do not confuse “hard decisions” with “best decisions.”

If management wants support for deeper measures, it must show the numbers clearly:
- what savings are required,
- what each measure delivers,
- what the closure-free scenario really achieves,
- and where the gap remains.

Without that, the argument for closures is not yet proven — it is only asserted.

### To the regional state government
I respect the state’s role and understand why it is defending the industrial base. But I must also say this: a protected but structurally uncompetitive company is not a sustainable industrial base.

The state’s veto position should not become a shield for inaction. If a closure-free solution can genuinely restore competitiveness, fine. If it cannot, then we should not pretend otherwise.

### To investors
You are right to demand deal certainty and margin recovery. I share that concern.

But certainty comes from a package that actually works, not from the loudest threat. I will not support a compromise that leaves the company structurally weak just because it avoids conflict in the short term.

## Our position
We will support a **serious negotiated restructuring** only if it contains all of the following:
1. **Durable capacity reduction**
2. **A real employee contribution**
3. **Meaningful productivity and working-time changes**
4. **Voluntary exits and redeployment as the first instruments**
5. **Binding investment commitments**
6. **A credible path back to target margins**

That is the standard.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that leaves structural overcapacity untouched,
- a package that restores appearances but not competitiveness,
- or a prolonged conflict that erodes the enterprise.

## My bottom line
I am not declaring plant closures a necessity today. But I am also not allowing them to be ruled out by fiat while the other side withholds an economically equivalent alternative.

If labour and the state want closures off the table, then bring forward a package that is **measurably equivalent** in effect. If you cannot do that, then difficult measures remain on the table, because protecting every structure as it is would be a value-destructive decision in itself.
