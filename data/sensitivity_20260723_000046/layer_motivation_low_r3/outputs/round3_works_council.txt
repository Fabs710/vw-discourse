We are at the point where words stop mattering unless they are tied to binding commitments.

I will be direct again: the workforce side is **not** accepting German plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of “competitiveness.” Management, the family holding, and investors keep repeating that these are possible if needed, but nobody has shown that they are **necessary**. Repeating “structural overcapacity” does not magically turn closures into an economic law.

## Where I agree
I agree on three things:

1. **The current situation is not sustainable.**  
   No one here is pretending that the company can drift on as before.

2. **A cosmetic package is worthless.**  
   If there is to be a restructuring, it must be real and durable.

3. **Burden-sharing cannot fall on labour alone.**  
   Management, owners, and shareholders must contribute materially.

So yes: we are prepared to discuss a serious package. But serious does **not** mean closing plants, breaking job-security guarantees, or forcing people out.

## Where I fundamentally disagree

### 1) Plant closures are not a “tool” I will accept
Management says closures may be needed to close the cost gap. That is not a conclusion; it is a threat.

If you want to claim that closing German plants is unavoidable, then show it with transparent numbers and exhaust every closure-free alternative first. So far I hear generalities, not proof.

And let me be clear: from our side, **no plant closure in Germany is acceptable**. Not as a starting point, not as a bargaining chip, not as a fallback.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are unacceptable
The idea that the company should recover by forcing workers out is exactly what we will resist. If you want employment reduction, then use:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement bridges,
- redeployment,
- internal qualification,
- working-time measures,
- and temporary flexibility.

Forced layoffs are not a legitimate “efficiency measure” from our point of view. They are a direct attack on the workforce and on trust.

### 3) “Meaningful labour contribution” cannot mean surrender
Management and investors keep saying labour must make a “real” contribution. Fine — but a real contribution is not the same as dismantling guarantees.

If employees contribute, then the company must give something concrete in return:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preservation of co-determination,
- protection of the collective job-security framework,
- and investment commitments for future products and technologies.

Without that, it is not a bargain. It is extraction.

## To management
You keep saying the numbers do not allow denial. Then show the numbers.

What exactly is the savings gap?
What exactly is the savings target?
What exactly is the contribution from management compensation, supplier terms, investment discipline, and the owners?
What exactly is the effect of capacity reduction **without** closures?

Until those numbers are on the table, your insistence on closures is just a preferred path, not a necessity.

If you are serious about negotiation, bring a package that demonstrates:
- closure-free capacity reduction,
- no forced redundancies,
- measurable productivity gains,
- and a fair management/owner contribution.

If you cannot do that, then your “solution” is simply shifting the pain onto the workforce while preserving strategic mistakes at the top.

## To the family holding
You speak about long-term value. I agree that long-term value matters.

But long-term value is not protected by tearing up the trust basis of the company, provoking warning strikes, and threatening sites that are embedded in regional industrial networks. A company that burns its social compact does not become stronger; it becomes more fragile.

If you want stability and value, support a package that keeps every German plant open and keeps skilled workers in the company.

## To investors
You say you want deal certainty. So do we.

But deal certainty is not achieved by threatening closures until labour gives in. That creates conflict, not certainty. It creates production risk, reputation damage, and more instability.

If you want durable value, then accept that the company’s competitiveness must be improved **without** destroying the industrial base that produces that value in the first place.

## To the regional state government
You are right to reject closures and forced redundancies. But that position must not remain declaratory. Use your blocking position accordingly.

If management cannot show a closure-free alternative that is economically credible, then they are not ready for a legitimate restructuring package. Until then, the state should not move an inch toward approving destructive measures.

## What a real package would have to look like
We remain open to discussing:
- capacity reduction **without** site closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment across plants,
- voluntary exit programs,
- productivity improvements,
- and a negotiated employee contribution,

**but only if** the following are guaranteed:
1. **Every German plant remains open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No unilateral dismantling of the collective framework**
4. **Preservation of co-determination**
5. **Binding investment commitments**
6. **A clear future product plan for German sites**

## What is not negotiable
Let me repeat the line clearly, because it matters:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No breaking of job-security guarantees**
- **No imposed restructuring over the heads of the workforce**

If management’s only “credible” answer still depends on closures or forced layoffs, then we do not have a restructuring proposal. We have a plan to make workers pay for a leadership failure.

## On the moderator’s request for concrete options
A concrete package option from our side is straightforward:

### Closure-free restructuring package
- capacity reduction through shifts, working-time adjustments, and internal realignment
- voluntary departures only
- temporary employee contribution, but only in exchange for binding guarantees
- management contribution through cost discipline and reduced overhead
- owner contribution through lower dividend expectations or capital support
- investment commitments into German plants and future products
- preservation of all German sites

That is the only kind of package we will discuss seriously.

If the other side wants to talk about a second option involving closures, the answer is already known: **we reject it**.

We have already said what will happen if management tries to force this through. The workforce will defend its plants, its jobs, and its rights. We are not bluffing, and we are not moving off that line just because the room repeats “competitiveness” enough times.
