### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one central unresolved issue:

**Whether a restructuring that avoids German plant closures and compulsory redundancies can still close the quantified savings gap with sufficient durability and credibility.**

Everything else now hangs off that:
- Labour side says closures/forced layoffs are not acceptable and not yet proven necessary.
- Management, family holding, and investors say no-closure options are only acceptable if they are economically equivalent.
- The state shareholder rejects destructive measures unless necessity is proven.
- The expert says the answer depends on the numbers: recurring savings, one-time costs, timing, and execution risk.

So the plateau is no longer about general principles; it is about **proof of equivalence** and **how much structural change is enough**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge positions that could reduce the gap while preserving each side’s core red lines as far as possible.

#### 1) Quantified closure-free restructuring trial, time-limited
A package centered on:
- shift reductions / working-time changes,
- voluntary departures and retirement bridges,
- redeployment and qualification,
- overhead and management cost reductions,
- owner capital support or dividend restraint,
- binding investment commitments.

**Bridge feature:**  
This is implemented for a defined period with hard metrics:
- annual recurring savings target,
- headcount reduction target,
- utilization target,
- and a review trigger if the gap is not met.

**Why it may bridge conflict:**  
It gives labour and the state a closure-free path first, while giving management and investors a measurable test rather than an open-ended veto.

---

#### 2) Conditional footprint rationalization with closure as a last-resort trigger
A package in which:
- every effort is made to achieve capacity reduction without site closure,
- but a pre-agreed, quantified review point determines whether limited footprint rationalization is activated if targets are missed.

**Bridge feature:**  
Any closure decision would be delayed, bounded, and linked to transparent economic criteria.

**Why it may bridge conflict:**  
It avoids immediate closure demands while not removing closure entirely from the economic logic. It converts “closure now vs never” into “closure only if the quantified fallback is reached.”

---

#### 3) Shared-burden package with binding guardrails
A comprehensive deal combining:
- temporary employee contribution,
- management compensation restraint,
- owner/shareholder contribution,
- investment commitment,
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- no plant closure during the agreement term,
- and a jointly monitored productivity program.

**Bridge feature:**  
The labour contribution is matched by binding commitments from management and owners, with transparent oversight.

**Why it may bridge conflict:**  
It addresses the workforce’s demand that sacrifice cannot fall on labour alone, while giving investors and management measurable cost relief and governance discipline.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
**What specific, quantified savings from closure-free measures would you regard as sufficient to accept a temporary employee contribution without reopening the issue of site rationalization?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**What minimum combination of wage, time, productivity, and headcount measures would you consider a genuinely durable labour contribution rather than a symbolic concession?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the exact savings gap, and how much of it can you close credibly through measures short of plant closure and compulsory redundancies?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete enforcement or approval conditions will you apply to ensure that a closure-free restructuring is not merely promised but actually delivered?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What binding investment, governance, or capital-support commitments are you prepared to make if labour accepts a substantial but closure-free restructuring package?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**What level of recurring savings and implementation certainty would make a no-closure deal acceptable to you as value-preserving rather than value-destructive?**
