### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the current situation is **not sustainable** and that a **durable restructuring** is needed.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated solution** rather than open-ended confrontation.
- Several parties accept that any package should include some combination of:
  - capacity reduction,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary departures / redeployment,
  - productivity measures,
  - investment in future products and technology.
- There is shared recognition that **burden-sharing must be broader than labour alone** and that management/owners should also contribute.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, and state government reject them; management and some owners/investors do not rule them out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour side and state reject them; management sees workforce reduction as necessary in some form.
- **Labour-cost adjustment:** Management, owners, and investors want a meaningful and durable cost contribution; labour is only open to this if it is balanced by binding guarantees.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour and state want site, job, and co-determination protections; management/owners/investors say these cannot be absolute if they prevent structural change.
- **Assessment of “credible alternative”:** Labour says alternatives to closures must preserve jobs and sites; management/owners/investors say alternatives are acceptable only if they deliver equivalent savings and restructuring depth.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** How much employment protection can be preserved while still closing the cost gap?
- **Sites vs. capacity reduction:** Whether savings can be achieved through internal reallocation and flexibility rather than closure.
- **Employee contribution vs. guarantees:** What level of labour concession would be matched by what binding commitments on jobs, sites, and investment?
- **Speed vs. stability:** Whether a faster restructuring with harder measures is preferable to a slower negotiated package with stronger protections.
- **Governance constraints vs. economic urgency:** How the special veto/co-determination structure will shape what is actually possible.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package design**: quantify the savings gap, identify the minimum required measures, and test which alternatives can close it without closures or compulsory redundancies.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - what they can give,
  - what they require in return,
  - and which items are non-negotiable versus negotiable.
- Focus the next round on **one concrete draft package** with:
  - target savings,
  - workforce measures,
  - investment commitments,
  - and binding safeguards, so the discussion can shift from principles to comparability of options.
