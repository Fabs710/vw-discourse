As the regional state government and a blocking shareholder, I need to be very clear: **I will not support a restructuring that sacrifices German plants, destroys a large number of regional jobs, or sidelines the co-determination and veto architecture that is part of this company’s stability.**

I do agree with management on one point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. A purely cosmetic response would be irresponsible. The company needs a durable solution that restores competitiveness and protects its future investment capacity.

But I strongly disagree with the idea that the only credible path runs through **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and deep unilateral labour-cost cuts**.

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are broadly in line with what I regard as essential public-interest interests:
- no closures in Germany,
- no forced redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of protections,
- and no imposed restructuring without genuine participation.

I also support your demand that any workforce contribution must be matched by real contributions from management and owners. The workforce cannot be the only side asked to pay for strategic mistakes or market pressure.

### To management
If you want my support, you need to come back with a restructuring that is **credible without being destructive**. That means:
- preserving the company as a regional industrial anchor,
- protecting a meaningful industrial base in Germany,
- avoiding irreversible site decisions,
- and presenting alternatives that achieve savings through:
  - capacity reduction,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - internal redeployment,
  - voluntary measures,
  - productivity improvements,
  - and a negotiated contribution from all sides.

I am not saying “no change.” I am saying **no solution that guts the company’s regional footprint**.

### What I can support
I could support a package if it includes:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a clear investment plan for future products and technologies,
- a fair burden-sharing arrangement,
- and a restructuring path that preserves co-determination and the state’s legitimate veto role.

### What I cannot support
I will not support:
- German plant closures as a first resort,
- mass layoffs as the default instrument,
- or any attempt to force through a settlement by threatening the social and political legitimacy of the company.

If management insists that “credible” means closures, then we are at an impasse. But if there is a serious alternative that closes the cost gap without breaking the industrial base, then I am prepared to engage constructively and use my position to help deliver it.

So my message is straightforward: **present a restructuring that is economically sound and socially responsible, or do not expect support from the state shareholder.**
