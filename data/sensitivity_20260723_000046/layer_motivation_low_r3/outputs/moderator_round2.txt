### 1) What has been agreed
- The current situation is unsustainable and a cosmetic response is not sufficient.
- All sides accept that some form of durable restructuring is needed.
- There is broad agreement that open conflict, prolonged strikes, or unilateral action would be damaging.
- All sides accept that employees may be asked to contribute, provided the burden is shared.
- There is also broad support for a package that is quantified, binding, and tied to future investment and competitiveness.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Labour, works council, and the state reject them as a starting point; management, the family holding, and investors say they remain possible if needed to close the gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour, works council, and the state oppose them; management says headcount reduction is likely unavoidable in some form.
- **Scope of workforce protections:**  
  Labour and the works council want collective and co-determination protections preserved; management and owners want room to change the framework if needed.
- **Whether closure-free alternatives are sufficient:**  
  Labour-side actors say yes, if real guarantees are attached; management, the family holding, and investors say only if the economic effect is truly equivalent.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** No common definition yet of what level of cost reduction is sufficient and how it can be achieved.
- **Sites vs. capacity reduction:** There is no agreed mechanism showing how capacity can be reduced without closures, or whether that can deliver the required savings.
- **Employee contribution vs. binding guarantees:** The size, duration, and conditions of labour contributions are still unclear, as are the exact guarantees in return.
- **Speed vs. social stability:** No shared timeline yet for balancing rapid restructuring with negotiated, trust-preserving implementation.
- **Governance constraints vs. economic urgency:** The roles of the state shareholder, co-determination, and ownership authority remain unresolved in practical terms.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **one or two concrete package options**.
- For each option, specify:
  - expected savings,
  - workforce contribution,
  - treatment of sites,
  - treatment of redundancies,
  - investment commitments,
  - and implementation timeline.
- Clarify what level of savings is actually required before closures become necessary, if at all.
- Test whether a closure-free package can be made economically equivalent and, if not, identify exactly where the gap remains.
