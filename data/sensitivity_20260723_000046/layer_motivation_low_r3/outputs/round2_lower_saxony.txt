I will be equally direct.

The state shareholder shares the diagnosis that the current situation is not sustainable. A cosmetic answer will not do, and delaying the problem would be irresponsible. We need a durable restructuring that restores competitiveness and secures the company’s future investment capacity.

But I do **not** accept the management-side conclusion that the only credible path is German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and a unilateral breaking of the company’s social compact.

## To management
You have made a case for structural adjustment, and I do not dispute that cost and capacity issues exist. Where I disagree is on your apparent implication that **closures are the default test of seriousness**.

They are not.

For us, the key question is whether the gap can be closed through a combination of:
- capacity reduction without site closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity gains,
- and a negotiated employee contribution,
while preserving a viable industrial base in Germany.

If your proposal cannot show that closure-free options have been genuinely exhausted, then you have not yet justified the most destructive measures. As a blocking shareholder under a special statute, I will not support a settlement that sacrifices regional employment and weakens the company’s role as an economic anchor unless there is no credible alternative. So far, I do **not** see that threshold met.

## To the works council
Your insistence on protecting sites, jobs, and co-determination is legitimate. I strongly share the view that:
- no plant closures should be treated as a first resort,
- no compulsory redundancies should be the automatic answer,
- and no restructuring should sideline the workforce’s role in this company.

I also agree that if employees are asked to contribute, that contribution must buy something real:
- binding site assurances,
- protection against forced layoffs,
- investment commitments,
- and a credible future product plan.

That said, I want to be candid: “protect every structure exactly as it is” is not a sufficient answer either. The company does need adjustment. But adjustment must not become a pretext for gutting the German industrial base.

## To the union
I welcome your willingness to discuss a negotiated employee contribution, and I agree that the burden cannot fall on labour alone. Management, owners, and governance bodies must also contribute credibly.

Where I stand with you:
- yes to negotiated and temporary employee contributions if they are matched by binding guarantees,
- yes to capacity reduction if it avoids closures,
- yes to a social-partnership solution,
- no to forced redundancies,
- no to plant closures as the first-line instrument,
- no to dismantling the collective framework as the main adjustment tool.

But I must also say this: an employee contribution cannot be used only as a shield against structural change. If there is a genuine package that protects the German sites while restoring competitiveness, the state will back it. If not, then we remain in conflict.

## To the controlling family holding and investors
I understand the concern for long-term value and margin restoration. You are right that the status quo is not an option. But long-term value is not served by triggering a confrontation that damages labour peace, political legitimacy, and the regional industrial ecosystem.

The state’s position is not anti-competitiveness. It is pro-sustainability, and sustainability includes:
- industrial capability,
- social peace,
- skilled employment,
- and the company’s legitimacy in its home regions.

A package that “wins” on paper but loses the company’s regional base and co-determination stability is not a successful restructuring in our view.

## What I can support
I could support a serious settlement if it includes all of the following:
1. **No plant closures in Germany as the preferred path**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and investment guarantees**
4. **A fair burden-sharing contribution from all sides**
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
6. **A credible industrial plan that keeps a meaningful domestic footprint**

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- a package that guts regional employment,
- a restructuring imposed over the heads of labour and the state,
- or any approach that treats the German sites as disposable.

If management wants a different answer, it must bring a concrete alternative package that closes the cost gap **without** destroying the company’s role as a regional anchor. Until then, I will use the full weight of the state shareholder’s position to block a destructive solution.

If helpful, the next step should be a **specific counterpackage** with quantified savings, quantified employee contributions, and binding guarantees on sites and investment. But let me be clear: the starting point is not “closures are inevitable.” The starting point is that closures are unacceptable unless all other credible paths truly fail.
