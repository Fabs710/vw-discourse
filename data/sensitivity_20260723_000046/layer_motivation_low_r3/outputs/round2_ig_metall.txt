I’ll be direct.

We are in substantial agreement on the diagnosis that the current situation is not sustainable and that a cosmetic answer will not do. But we are **not** moving on the fundamental issue: the workforce will **not** be asked to finance the restructuring alone, and we will **not** accept forced redundancies or plant closures as the default solution.

### Where I agree
- **A durable restructuring is necessary.** I agree with management, owners, and investors that the status quo cannot continue unchanged.
- **Employee contributions can be on the table.** I have said this before and repeat it now: we are prepared to discuss a negotiated labour contribution.
- **Capacity reduction and productivity measures are legitimate topics.** If they are designed to preserve sites and jobs, then yes, we can discuss them.
- **A settlement is better than open conflict.** No one benefits from a prolonged industrial confrontation.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Plant closures are not “just another tool”
To management, I say this plainly: closing German plants is not a neutral economic instrument. It is an irreversible escalation that tears out skilled employment, damages trust, and endangers the entire bargaining relationship. You have not shown that closures are unavoidable; you have asserted it. That is not sufficient.

#### 2) Compulsory redundancies are unacceptable
A workforce that has already invested in this company’s transformation cannot be told that the answer is to push people out by force while management and owners speak of “shared burden.” Shared burden means exactly that: shared.

#### 3) The collective framework is not disposable
We will not accept a restructuring that uses the crisis to dismantle the collective wage agreement or strip away long-standing protections. If labour contributes, then it must be within a negotiated framework, not through a forced degradation of standards.

### To management
If you want our cooperation, then you need to come forward with a **real alternative package** that closes the gap without closures and without compulsory layoffs. That package must include:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preservation of the collective wage framework,
- measurable contributions from management and owners,
- and concrete investment commitments for future products and technology.

If you cannot deliver that, then your “solution” is simply asking the workforce to absorb the pain while strategic and ownership-level decisions go uncorrected.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines. I support your position that the burden cannot be placed on the plants alone. The union stands ready to back warning strikes and further industrial action if management tries to force through closures or mass layoffs. But I also want to keep the door open for a serious negotiated settlement if the conditions are met.

### To the regional state government
Your insistence on preserving the industrial base is correct. But that stance has to translate into pressure on management and owners as well, not just into calls for “balanced solutions.” Balanced means:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- no unilateral cuts to wages and protections,
- and genuine commitment to investment and employment stability.

### To the family holding and investors
I hear your concern for competitiveness and value. But long-term value is not protected by triggering a social conflict that destroys labour peace and undermines the industrial core. If you want a durable solution, then support a package that shares the burden rather than one that shifts it downward.

### Our position in concrete terms
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary or staged employee contributions,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction without closures,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- productivity measures,
- and conditional concessions.

But only if the package includes:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
4. **Binding site and investment guarantees**
5. **Material contributions from management and owners**

### What we will not accept
- closures dressed up as “strategic optimization”
- forced redundancies
- one-sided wage cuts
- the workforce bearing the full restructuring burden
- vague promises instead of binding commitments

If management and the owners want peace, they need to stop testing whether the workforce will blink first. We have already shown openness to a fair deal. The next move must be a proposal that is economically serious **and** socially responsible. If that does not come, then our members will expect us to escalate further.
