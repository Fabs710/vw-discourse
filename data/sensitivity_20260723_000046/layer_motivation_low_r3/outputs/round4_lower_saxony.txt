## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, investors, the works council, and the union on one essential point: **the status quo is not sustainable**.  
A cosmetic package would fail everyone. The company needs durable restructuring, not delay.

I also agree that:

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” is not an emotional position; it is a governance and regional-interest position
Management keeps repeating that closures may be necessary if closure-free measures are not “economically equivalent.” That may be true in theory, but you have not shown it in practice.

A plant closure is not just another cost lever. In our governance setting, and for this region, it is an irreversible decision with major industrial and political consequences.  
So no, we will not treat closure as the default fallback simply because it is the cleanest balance-sheet answer.

If you want to argue for closures, you must prove:
- the exact savings gap,
- the exact failure of closure-free measures,
- and why the regional damage is truly unavoidable.

So far, that proof has not been delivered.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable “efficiency” tool
I reject the idea that forced layoffs are simply a technical necessity if voluntary exits are insufficient.

If headcount must fall, then the priority must be:
- voluntary departures,
- retirement bridges,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- and productivity measures.

That is the path we can support.  
Forced redundancies are the path that destroys trust, escalates conflict, and weakens the company’s social compact.

### 3) A “conditional closure trigger” is not a compromise we can support
The moderator’s bridge idea 2 is, from our perspective, just closure with a delayed fuse. That is not a protected solution; it is a threat structure.

We are not prepared to sign up to a framework that says:
“accept cuts now, and if management later deems the numbers insufficient, plants can still be closed.”

That is not how you build confidence, social peace, or regional stability.

## Where I am flexible
I am not defending the status quo. I am prepared to support a **serious closure-free restructuring package** if it is genuinely capable of closing the gap.

That package would need to contain:
- capacity reduction **without** site closures,
- shift reductions and working-time changes,
- voluntary departures only,
- redeployment across plants,
- productivity gains,
- management overhead reductions,
- owner contribution,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites and future products.

I would also consider a **temporary employee contribution**, but only if it is clearly balanced by binding guarantees:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closures during the agreement term,
- preservation of co-determination,
- and a concrete future product plan for German plants.

That is the only form of burden-sharing that can be defended politically and industrially.
