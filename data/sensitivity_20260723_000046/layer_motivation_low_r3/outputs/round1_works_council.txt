We are not accepting a restructuring that destroys jobs, closes German plants, or breaks the historic guarantees that built this company’s trust with its workforce.

Let me be clear from the outset:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral dismantling of pay, protections, or co-determination rights.
- No fait accompli imposed on the workforce.

If management believes there is a cost problem, then the burden of proof is on management to present an alternative that preserves the industrial base, protects employment, and gives the workforce a genuine say in shaping the future. We are prepared to discuss adjustments, but only on the basis of binding site and job guarantees, investment commitments, and a negotiated solution that avoids forced layoffs.

Our priorities are straightforward:

1. Protect every German plant.
2. Secure jobs through 2030 and beyond.
3. Preserve the collective agreements and job-security framework.
4. Ensure real co-determination in any restructuring plan.
5. Avoid irreversible decisions that weaken the company’s industrial backbone.

If management insists on closures, mass layoffs, or the breaking of existing guarantees, then they should expect fierce resistance from the works council and the workforce. We have the mandate, the legitimacy, and the means to defend these sites.
