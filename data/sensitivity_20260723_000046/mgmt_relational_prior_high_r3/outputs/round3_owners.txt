## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with several stakeholders on the basic economics:

- **This cannot be solved by slogans.**
- A viable package must be judged on **NPV, durability, and implementation risk**.
- **Temporary gestures are not enough** if the company’s problem is structural.
- The workforce should not be the **sole adjustment variable**.
- A prolonged strike or public breakdown would destroy value.

That said, the expert’s analysis does **not** get anyone off the hook. It does **not** prove that closures are inevitable; it also does **not** prove that the labour side’s “no closures, no forced layoffs” line is sufficient. It simply confirms that the test is hard.

## Where I disagree with the works council and union
I understand why you are drawing strong lines, but I cannot treat your current red lines as absolute governance principles:

- “No plant closures in Germany”
- “No compulsory redundancies”
- “Every German plant remains open”

Those are **positions**, not yet a credible restructuring answer.

The expert has said clearly that non-closure measures **can** generate meaningful savings, and in some cases approach closure-level savings. Fine. Then prove it. Show, site by site, that the package you are offering really removes enough fixed cost, capacity burden, and overcapacity risk.

Right now, I do **not** see that proof.

What I do see is a tendency to assume that if closure is painful, it must therefore be avoidable. That is not economics. Pain alone does not determine feasibility.

## Where I disagree with management
I also do not accept management’s posture if it is essentially:

> accept closures and forced reductions, or accept decline.

That is too blunt and too likely to produce a value-destructive conflict.

The expert has confirmed that a **broad non-closure package may be economically viable** if it is genuinely hard and durable. So management cannot hide behind closures as if they were the only serious instrument.

Management still has the burden to show, with numbers:

- the real savings gap,
- what closure would deliver,
- what the alternative package would deliver,
- and why the alternative falls short, if it does.

If management cannot show that, then closure remains a **threat**, not a proven necessity.

## Where I disagree with the state government
I respect the state’s role and the regional importance of this company. But I do not accept the implication that regional protection can substitute for industrial viability.

If the state uses its blocking role to freeze every structural adjustment, that would be value-destructive. We cannot preserve industrial capacity by refusing to correct overcapacity and cost. That would only postpone the problem and make it worse.

At the same time, I do agree with the state on one point: closure-first rhetoric is unhelpful and escalatory. If management wants support, it must stop framing the choice as “close plants or fail.”

## What moves me
What could move the controlling family is a **binding, quantified package** that restores margins and protects long-term enterprise value without creating permanent instability.

That means:

1. **A real savings bridge**
   - by site
   - by measure
   - with annualized impact
   - and one-time costs

2. **Durable capacity correction**
   - not cosmetic
   - not reversible
   - not dependent on endless renegotiation

3. **Material employee contribution**
   - yes, but temporary and meaningful
   - not symbolic
   - not open-ended

4. **Management and owner contribution**
   - overhead discipline
   - investment prioritization
   - credible product and platform commitments

5. **Labour peace and implementation certainty**
   - because unstable value is not value protected

## My position on the non-closure path
I am **open** to a serious non-closure alternative.

- show the numbers,
- show the durability,
- show the NPV,
- show the enforcement,
- and show that the package truly matches the required savings.

If the alternative is merely a collection of reversible labour concessions, then it is **not** equivalent.

If it is genuinely equivalent, then I will support it over a destructive closure fight.
