I’ll respond directly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with the expert on the core economic point: this must be judged on **NPV, durability, and implementation risk**, not slogans.

I also agree with everyone that a **cosmetic deal** is worthless. A temporary patch that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive would not solve the crisis.

And I agree that the burden should not fall on employees alone. A serious restructuring must involve:
- management discipline,
- owner support,
- and employee contribution.

## Where I disagree with labour
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to turn:
- “no closures,”
- “no compulsory redundancies,”
- “every German plant remains open,”

into a starting principle that overrides the economics.

That is not a restructuring strategy. It is a red line list.

The expert has now said clearly:
- non-closure measures can be meaningful,
- they can sometimes approach closure-level savings,
- but only if they are broad, durable, and enforceable.

That means the key question is not whether alternatives exist in theory.  
It is whether they **remove enough fixed cost and capacity** in practice.

Right now, I still do not see a package from labour that credibly does that.

## To the Works Council
You keep saying management has not “proved” closures are necessary. Fair enough — but that is only half the test.

The real test is whether your alternative is **economically equivalent**.

So show me:
1. the exact plant-by-plant cost base,
2. the annualized savings from your alternative package,
3. the one-time costs,
4. the NPV compared with closure,
5. and the implementation risk.

Until then, “keep every plant open” is not a solution. It is a preference.

And let me be equally clear: I will not accept a negotiation built around the assumption that the company can preserve all legacy structures and somehow still become competitive.

## To the Union
You are right that temporary measures are not enough if the problem is structural. I agree.

But you then need to accept the logical consequence: if the problem is structural, then the solution must be structural too.

That means:
- real headcount reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- and a real labour-cost contribution.

A purely voluntary, reversible, or short-lived package may sound socially easier, but it does **not** necessarily solve the economic problem.

So I reject the idea that a package is automatically acceptable just because it avoids closures. Avoiding closures is not the goal. Restoring competitiveness is the goal.

## To the State Government
I respect your role and your concern for regional employment. But I need to be blunt: **regional stability cannot become a veto on economic reality**.

If we freeze every site because it is politically difficult to adjust them, then the company simply loses competitiveness more slowly — and the eventual damage is worse.

So yes, I want a negotiated solution.  
No, I do not accept that the state can define the outcome by insisting that plant closures are off-limits regardless of the economics.

## To the Controlling Family
I agree that a value-destructive conflict is unacceptable. But a settlement that preserves every site, every guarantee, and every structural cost while asking only temporary concessions is also value-destructive.

So the family should support a package that is:
- hard,
- durable,
- and economically real.

That means not shielding legacy structures for sentiment’s sake. It means backing a restructuring that restores margins and frees up investment capacity to 2030.

## To Investors
You are right to insist on quantified equivalence. I support that fully.

But I will not accept the implication that closure is the default benchmark and everything else is second-best by definition. The benchmark is **restored competitiveness**. If a non-closure package can genuinely deliver that, good. If not, then stronger measures remain necessary.

## What I am prepared to consider
I remain open to a negotiated package that avoids some or even all closures **if and only if** it delivers all of the following:

1. **A material, durable labour-cost contribution**
   - not symbolic
   - not one-off
   - not quickly reversible

2. **A binding reduction in capacity**
   - if not through closure, then through hard consolidation, model reassignment, and removal of real excess capacity

3. **A credible headcount reduction**
   - through voluntary exits, retirement, transfers, and other instruments
   - but of sufficient scale to matter materially

4. **Revised site and job guarantees**
   - conditional on the restructuring package
   - not blanket guarantees that freeze the company in place

5. **Clear investment commitments**
   - product allocation and technology investment in Germany
   - but only where the restructuring creates the room for that investment

## What I will not accept
Let me be equally clear:
- I will not accept a deal that rules out closures in advance.
- I will not accept a deal that rules out meaningful workforce reduction.
- I will not accept a deal that leaves overcapacity intact.
- I will not accept a cosmetic compromise dressed up as social responsibility.
- I will not accept permanent guarantees that lock in an uncompetitive structure.

That remains the line.

## On the repeated demand for numbers
Yes — bring numbers. That is exactly what needs to happen now.

But let’s be honest about the burden here: if labour wants to eliminate closures from the table, then it must show a package that is not merely politically comfortable, but economically equivalent.

That means a **site-by-site, plant-by-plant bridge**:
- current cost base
- closure-case savings
- closure-case one-time costs
- retention-case savings
- retention-case one-time costs
- NPV
- payback
- implementation risk
- durability

If a specialist is needed to validate the comparison, then yes, we need that input.

## Bottom line
My position is straightforward:

- I do **not** accept closures as the first reflex.
- I do **not** accept “no closures” as sufficient unless it is proven economically equivalent.
- I do accept a hard, quantified restructuring that restores competitiveness and preserves long-term value.

Bring me a package that is materially real, durable, and enforceable. Otherwise, closures and deeper structural measures remain on the table because competitiveness is not optional.
