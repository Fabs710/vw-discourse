### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company needs a **durable competitiveness solution**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement that any settlement must be **negotiated** and must respect **co-determination / governance rules**.
- Several actors accept that **employee-side contributions** may be part of a package, if they are **temporary, fair, and tied to safeguards**.
- There is shared recognition that **management/owners/state** must also contribute, not just the workforce.
- The expert confirmed that **non-closure measures can generate significant savings**, and in some cases may approach closure-level savings if they are broad, durable, and well designed.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council and Union reject closures as a starting point and want all German plants kept open.  
  - Management, Investors, and Family keep closures on the table as a necessary option.  
  - State is open to restructuring but opposes a closure-first approach.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour side rejects them categorically.  
  - Management does not accept an absolute ban if the economics require deeper adjustment.
- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  - Labour side wants binding site and employment guarantees.  
  - Management and investors say guarantees must be conditional on economic viability and not freeze the footprint.
- **Whether non-closure alternatives are sufficient:**  
  - Labour side believes a no-closure solution may still be viable.  
  - Management/investors/family say this must be proven with numbers and may still fall short.
- **Role of the state and governance constraints:**  
  - State insists its blocking role and regional responsibility matter.  
  - Management/investors view this as a constraint but not a veto on structural change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required** versus **how much can realistically come from non-closure measures**.
- **Speed vs. social impact:** closure is faster and more structural; non-closure solutions are less disruptive but may be slower and less complete.
- **Fixed footprint reduction vs. workforce protections:** the debate remains unresolved on whether competitiveness can be restored without closing sites or reducing capacity directly.
- **Temporary concessions vs. permanent change:** labour contributions may help, but it remains unclear whether they can be large and durable enough.
- **Economic equivalence:** no shared, verified calculation yet shows whether non-closure measures can fully match closure economics.
- **Implementation certainty:** there is no agreed path yet on enforceable commitments, sequencing, and monitoring.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **site-by-site, quantified options**.
- Require a **comparison of closure vs. non-closure packages** with:
  - annual savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - timing,
  - durability,
  - and implementation risk.
- Clarify which measures are **acceptable in combination** and which are **non-negotiable red lines**.
- Test whether a package can be both:
  - **economically equivalent** to closure-level savings, and
  - **socially/gov ernance sustainable** enough to secure agreement.
