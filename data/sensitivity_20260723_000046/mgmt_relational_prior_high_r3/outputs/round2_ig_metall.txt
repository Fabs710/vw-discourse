I’ll respond directly.

### Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council on the essentials:
- **No plant closures as the first resort**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No solution that makes workers absorb the crisis alone**

I also agree that management has **not yet proven** that closures are unavoidable. Saying “the cost base is too high” is not the same as proving that closure is the only economically viable instrument.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the framing that a serious restructuring must necessarily mean:
- plant closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or a one-sided wage rollback.

That is not an economic law; it is a management preference unless and until it is shown otherwise.

At the same time, I want to be clear to the Works Council: a **temporary, negotiated employee contribution** is not the same as breaking the wage agreement. If we are to protect sites and jobs, then yes, we can discuss a limited labour-cost contribution — but only under strict conditions:
- it must be **time-limited**
- it must be **fairly shared**
- it must come with **binding site and job security**
- and it must be paired with **real contributions from management and owners**

### Minimum acceptable outcome from the union side
Our minimum acceptable outcome is:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Preservation of the collective bargaining framework**
4. **A negotiated, temporary employee contribution only if it helps secure jobs and sites**
5. **Binding investment commitments for German plants**
6. **A clear pathway to future product allocation and capacity utilization**
7. **Contributions from management and ownership, not just the workforce**

### What we can consider
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary pay moderation or bonus restraint
- working-time adjustments
- redeployment and qualification
- voluntary exit instruments
- early retirement
- a transformation or competitiveness fund
- productivity measures
- capacity reductions **without closures**

But let me stress: these are **not blank checks**. They only make sense if they are part of a serious package that protects employment and keeps the sites open.

### What we cannot accept
We will not accept:
- open-ended concessions
- permanent wage cuts
- a “shared sacrifice” formula where only employees actually sacrifice
- closure threats used as blackmail
- a deal that leaves the workforce with less pay, less security, and no real future

### To management
Your current position still sounds like this: “Give us material labour-cost reductions, or we keep closures on the table.” That is not a trustworthy basis for settlement.

If you want to avoid conflict, then come forward with something concrete:
- which capacities you truly need,
- what investments you are committing,
- what management and owners will contribute,
- and how jobs can be secured without forcing redundancies.

If you cannot do that, then you should expect the workforce to continue resisting, including through industrial action.

### To the state government and controlling family
You both have real influence. Use it to prevent a restructuring that shifts all risk onto the workforce. If you want social peace and industrial stability, then back a package that preserves employment, not one that turns labour into the sole adjustment variable.

### Bottom line
The union is ready for a **hard but fair settlement**.  
We are **not** ready to sign off on a restructuring where workers pay, workers lose, and others merely observe.

If management wants a durable deal, it must accept three principles:
- **no closures**
- **no forced layoffs**
- **shared sacrifice from all sides**

Anything less will face determined resistance.
