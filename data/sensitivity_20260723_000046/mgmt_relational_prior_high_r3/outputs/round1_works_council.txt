As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will **not accept plant closures in Germany** and we will **not accept compulsory redundancies**. That is our red line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of transformation. We are prepared to discuss solutions, but only if they are **fair, socially responsible, and bindingly secured**. For us, that means:

- **Every German plant must remain open**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **Binding job security and site guarantees through 2030**
- **A real co-determination role** for employee representatives in shaping the restructuring
- **Alternative measures first**: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, redeployment, qualification, and negotiated cost contributions rather than destruction of jobs

We understand the competitive pressure, but management cannot solve a strategy problem by making workers pay the full price. If the company needs savings, then the burden must be shared fairly — not imposed through closures, layoffs, and the dismantling of hard-won guarantees.

If management comes with a genuine plan that protects sites and jobs, we are prepared to negotiate seriously. If it comes with a fait accompli, closures, or the breaking of the historic job-security promise, we will resist it forcefully — including industrial action and using all rights available to us in co-determination.
