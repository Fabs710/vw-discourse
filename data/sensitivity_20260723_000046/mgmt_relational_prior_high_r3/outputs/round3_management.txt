I’ll be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert on one essential point: this is not a debate about slogans, it is a debate about **NPV, durability, and structural savings**.

I also agree that non-closure measures can be meaningful. I have said from the start that I am open on **how** savings are achieved. I am not wedded to closures as ideology. If you can deliver a package that is **binding, durable, and economically equivalent**, I will consider it seriously.

And I agree with all stakeholders on one thing: a cosmetic deal is worthless. A temporary patch that leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not a solution.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the attempt to turn “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” into a universal principle that overrides the economic reality.

That is not how this works.

The company faces:
- structural overcapacity,
- a shrinking European market,
- intense low-cost competition,
- and a German cost base above target.

In that context, I need more than a general promise of flexibility. I need a package that **removes enough fixed cost and enough capacity** to restore competitiveness by 2030. If the alternative measures do not do that, then they are not equivalent — they are delay.

## To the Works Council
You say the expert did not prove closures are necessary. True. But that is not the standard. The standard is whether you can produce a **credible alternative that matches the economics**.

Right now, I have not seen that.

You list:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- attrition,
- early retirement,
- capacity optimization within existing sites.

Those are all useful tools. But you have not shown that they produce the same durable effect as footprint reduction. And frankly, they usually don’t unless they are combined with significant headcount reduction and real capacity removal.

So I will say this plainly: **“every German plant remains open” is not a starting assumption I can accept.** If the network has excess capacity, then capacity must come out somewhere. If not through closure, then through equally hard, enforceable measures. Show me those measures in numbers, not in principle.

## To the Union
You are right that labour should not carry the burden alone. I have never said otherwise.

But you are wrong if you think “temporary, fair, limited” contributions can only be discussed after closures are removed from the table entirely. That is not a credible bargaining stance if the business still needs structural repair.

I am not asking for symbolic gestures. I am asking for:
- a real, material labour-cost contribution,
- durable enough to change the economics,
- and part of a package that also includes capacity reduction and headcount adjustment.

A small, time-limited concession that leaves the rest untouched is not sufficient. If the economics remain broken, then the deal fails the company.

## To the State Government
You are right that social legitimacy matters. I do not dismiss that. A prolonged conflict would damage output, trust, and the brand.

But I must push back on the idea that the state can defend regional interests by insisting that every site remains sacred. That is not industrial policy; that is denial.

The state’s role should be to support a **viable** industrial base in Germany, not to freeze every legacy arrangement. If the burden of adjustment is not shared through real structural change, then the company will lose competitiveness anyway — and that would be worse for the region in the medium term.

## To the Controlling Family
I agree with you on one important point: a value-destructive confrontation is unacceptable.

But that cuts both ways. A settlement that preserves every site, every guarantee, and every legacy cost while asking for only temporary concessions is also value-destructive — just more slowly.

You are right that management must avoid crude brinkmanship. I accept that. But you should also support management in insisting that the final package be **economically real**, not politically comfortable.

## To Investors
You are correct to demand a quantified bridge. I support that fully.

But I will not accept your implication that closure is the benchmark and everything else is second-best by definition. The benchmark is **restored competitiveness**. If non-closure measures can genuinely achieve that, good. If they cannot, then we have to use stronger tools.

## What I am prepared to consider
I remain open to a negotiated package that avoids some or even all closures **if and only if** it delivers all of the following:

1. **A meaningful, durable labour-cost contribution**
   - not symbolic
   - not one-off
   - not reversible after one year

2. **A binding reduction in capacity**
   - if not through closure, then through hard consolidation, model reassignment, and removal of real excess capacity

3. **A credible headcount reduction**
   - through voluntary exits, retirement, transfers, and other instruments
   - but of sufficient scale to matter materially

4. **Revised site and job guarantees**
   - conditional on the restructuring package
   - not blanket guarantees that freeze the company in place

5. **Clear investment commitments**
   - product allocation and technology investment in Germany
   - but only where the restructuring creates the room for that investment

## What I will not accept
Let me be unambiguous:
- I will not accept a deal that rules out closures in advance.
- I will not accept a deal that rules out meaningful workforce reduction.
- I will not accept a deal that leaves overcapacity intact.
- I will not accept a cosmetic compromise dressed up as social responsibility.
- I will not accept permanent guarantees that lock in an uncompetitive structure.

That is the line.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate hard and I am prepared to consider alternatives.

What I am **not** prepared to do is sign up to a deal that preserves the old cost base and calls that a future.

If the workforce side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring a package that is:
- materially cheaper,
- structurally durable,
- and economically equivalent to the closure case.

If it cannot, then closures and deeper restructuring remain on the table because competitiveness is not optional.
