I’ll respond directly.

## Where I agree

I agree with the expert on one central point: **this has to be judged on hard numbers, not slogans**.

I also agree that:
- a non-closure package can deliver real savings,
- those savings must be **durable**,
- and a temporary gesture is **not** the same as a structural solution.

So yes: if management wants to claim that closures are unavoidable, it must prove that with a **site-by-site, NPV-based comparison**. We are not prepared to accept “closure-first” as an article of faith.

I also agree with the Works Council and state that the company must not try to solve this crisis by making workers the sole adjustment variable.

## Where I disagree

### To management
Your position is still too close to: “accept closures or accept uncompetitiveness.”

That is **not yet proven**. The expert made clear that non-closure measures can be substantial if they are broad enough. So you do **not** get to present closures as the default answer just because they are administratively cleaner.

You need to show:
- what the **true savings gap** is,
- what a non-closure package can deliver,
- what portion of savings comes from labour cost, overhead, capacity, and utilisation,
- and why any shortfall cannot be closed by a **shared-sacrifice package**.

Until that is on the table, closures remain an **assertion**, not a necessity.

### To the investors
You are right to demand durability and quantification. But you are still too willing to treat plant closure as the benchmark and everything else as second-best.

That is not acceptable from our side. If a negotiated package can deliver most or all of the economic effect **without destroying sites and forcing people out**, then that is not a weak compromise — it is a better restructuring outcome.

Economic equivalence matters, yes.  
But **social destruction is not a free add-on** that we are obliged to pay just to satisfy capital’s preference for simplicity.

### To the controlling family
You say you do not want a value-destructive confrontation. Good. Then stop backing management rhetoric that keeps closures as a bargaining club while refusing to define the real gap.

If the family wants long-term value, it should support a package that:
- preserves viable sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- and shares the burden fairly across all sides.

What I cannot accept is the implication that our red lines are “too absolute” simply because management is impatient. They are absolute because we are not going to sign up to a restructuring where workers lose their jobs, their guarantees, and their security while others call that “balance.”

### To the state government
I agree that this is a regional industrial anchor and that legitimacy matters. But I need to say clearly: **the state’s role should not be to pressure labour into accepting closures by calling them economically mature**.

Your blocking role exists for exactly this kind of situation. Use it to insist on a real alternative, not to facilitate a managed deindustrialisation with softer language.

### To the Works Council
I agree with your core line:
- no plant closures as a starting point,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no bypassing of co-determination.

I also agree that if management wants contributions, it must first prove the business case.

Where I want to be careful is this: “equivalent savings” must **not** become a back door for dismantling the collective wage framework permanently. A temporary employee contribution is one thing. A permanent wage rollback or open-ended erosion of guarantees is something else entirely.

We can discuss a contribution, but it must be:
- temporary,
- clearly bounded,
- fairly shared,
- and tied to hard commitments on jobs, sites, and investment.

## What we are prepared to discuss

We remain open to a serious negotiated package including:
- temporary, limited wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- productivity measures,
- capacity reductions **within the existing German footprint**,
- and a transformation fund.

That is a real bargaining offer. It is not a blank cheque.

## What we cannot accept

We will not accept:
- plant closures as the default solution,
- compulsory redundancies,
- permanent wage cuts,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or a deal where employees carry the burden while management and owners merely observe.

We also will not accept a “shared sacrifice” that is actually just a disguised wage rollback with no binding future for the sites.

## What must happen next

If management is serious, then the next round must produce a **quantified comparison**:

1. **Site-by-site cost data**
2. **Closure-case savings and one-time costs**
3. **Non-closure alternative savings and one-time costs**
4. **A durable burden-sharing formula**
5. **Binding site and employment guarantees**
6. **Concrete investment and product commitments**
7. **A monitoring and enforcement mechanism**

If management cannot put real numbers on the table, then it is not ready to demand our concessions.

## Our minimum acceptable outcome

Let me be explicit:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures as a precondition**
- **Preservation of the collective bargaining framework**
- **A temporary, fair employee contribution only if it protects jobs and sites**
- **Binding investment commitments**
- **A durable restructuring that does not make workers bear all the pain**

## Bottom line

We are prepared to negotiate hard.  
We are not prepared to be disciplined into accepting a closure-led solution just because management, investors, or even some experts say it may be easier.

If a non-closure package can truly match the economics, then management must choose that route and stop threatening livelihoods.  
If it cannot, then management must prove why — with numbers, not pressure.

Until then, our position remains: **no closures, no forced layoffs, fair burden-sharing, and a real future for the German sites.**
