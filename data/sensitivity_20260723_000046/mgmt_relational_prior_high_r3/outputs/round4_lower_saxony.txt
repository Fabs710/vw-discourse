## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the shareholder sides on one point: this cannot be settled by slogans. We need a **site-by-site, quantified comparison** of the options, including:
- annualized savings,
- one-time costs,
- durability,
- implementation risk,
- and NPV.

I also agree that the company needs a **durable competitiveness solution**, not a temporary patch.

And I agree that employees should not be asked to carry the whole burden alone. Any serious restructuring must involve contributions from management, owners, and capital as well.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the growing tendency to treat plant closures as the default benchmark.

The expert has now confirmed something important: **non-closure measures can be substantial and can sometimes approach closure-level savings** if they are broad, durable, and enforceable. That means closures are **not automatically necessary**. They are only justified if management can prove, plant by plant, that alternatives do not close the gap.

So I reject any attempt to turn “closures remain on the table” into a political bluff that pre-decides the outcome.

## To management
You still have not proven that closing German plants is the least destructive viable option.

If you want to keep closures in the discussion, then bring the real bridge:
- current cost base by site,
- annualized closure savings,
- one-time closure costs,
- non-closure package savings,
- NPV comparison,
- and implementation risk.

Until that is on the table, closure is not proven necessity. It is pressure.

And I want to say clearly: I will not support using closure threats to force through a deal that guts regional employment.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that:
- no closures should be the starting assumption,
- no compulsory redundancies should be the first answer,
- and any employee contribution must be temporary, limited, and tied to hard safeguards.

But I also need to be honest with you: if you want every German site protected, then you must be prepared to show that the non-closure package is not just socially preferable, but economically credible.

That means real measures:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- early retirement,
- productivity gains,
- capacity consolidation within the existing footprint,
- and a temporary contribution that is fair and bounded.

A simple “no closures, no layoffs” formula is not enough unless it is backed by numbers.

## To the controlling family
You are right to warn against a value-destructive conflict. I support that.

But if you want long-term value, then you should not back a closure-first logic before the economic case has been proven. Historic trust matters here. The company’s industrial base in Germany is not a disposable asset.

So I expect the family to support a solution that:
- preserves viable sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- respects co-determination,
- and keeps the company rooted in the region.

## To investors
You are right to demand economic equivalence. I accept that standard.

But you are wrong if you treat closure as the benchmark and everything else as second-best by definition. The benchmark is **restored competitiveness without destroying the company’s regional anchor**.

If a negotiated package can deliver the necessary savings through hard but socially responsible measures, then that is not softness — it is a better restructuring outcome.

## My position on the state’s role
I need to be very clear here: the state’s blocking role exists for exactly this kind of case. It is not decorative.

I will not accept a settlement that:
- guts regional employment,
- sidelines the state’s co-determination role,
- or treats the regional economic anchor as a cost center to be trimmed at will.

That is a line I will defend.

## What I am prepared to consider
I remain open to a hard restructuring package if, and only if, it delivers all of the following:

1. **No plant closures as a precondition**
   - closures cannot be the starting point for negotiation

2. **No compulsory redundancies as the first instrument**
   - workforce reduction must come through voluntary, social, and negotiated measures first

3. **A material, temporary employee contribution**
   - only if it is bounded and paired with guarantees

4. **Binding site and employment safeguards**
   - not open-ended, but real and enforceable

5. **Investment and product commitments in Germany**
   - tied to a viable future for the sites

6. **A governance process that respects co-determination and the state’s veto role**
   - no side-stepping, no faits accomplis

## What I will not accept
I will not support:
- a closure-led restructuring that guts employment,
- a deal that weakens or bypasses co-determination,
- a plan that treats the state’s role as cosmetic,
- or a settlement where workers pay while others merely observe.

I also will not accept a deal that leaves the company’s German industrial base permanently weakened in the name of “competitiveness.”
