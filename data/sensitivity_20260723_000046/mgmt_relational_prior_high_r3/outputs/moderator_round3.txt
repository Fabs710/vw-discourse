## 1) What has been agreed
- **Hard numbers are required.** All sides accepted that the debate must be based on site-by-site financial analysis, not slogans.
- **A non-closure package may be meaningful.** Several experts and stakeholders agreed it can generate substantial savings if it is broad, durable, and enforceable.
- **Temporary measures are not enough by themselves.** There is broad agreement that any solution must be structurally durable.
- **A cosmetic compromise is unacceptable.** No side endorsed a purely symbolic deal.
- **A prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.** Management, the family, investors, and others broadly acknowledged that strike escalation or deadlock would harm the company.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Are plant closures necessary?**
  - Management and investors say closures remain a live option if alternatives do not match the economics.
  - Works council and union say closures are not justified unless management proves that alternatives fail.
- **Can a non-closure package be truly equivalent?**
  - Labour and the state say it may be, and closures should not be assumed.
  - Management, family, and investors say equivalence must be demonstrated in hard financial terms and may not be achievable if overcapacity is structural.
- **What level of workforce contribution is acceptable?**
  - Labour accepts only temporary, limited, and conditional contributions.
  - Management and investors want contributions that are materially large and durable enough to change the economics.
- **Are closures a negotiation baseline or a last resort?**
  - Labour and the state reject closures as a starting premise.
  - Management and shareholders treat closures as a legitimate option if no equivalent alternative is proven.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Closure savings vs. one-time costs:** No agreed comparison yet between annualized net savings and restructuring charges.
- **Durability vs. reversibility:** Unclear whether labour-side concessions can remain effective long enough to match permanent footprint reduction.
- **Capacity reduction vs. site preservation:** The discussion has not resolved how much actual capacity can be removed without closures.
- **Social stability vs. financial efficiency:** The parties have not reconciled the cost of job/site loss with the value of faster, more permanent savings.
- **Governance and co-determination vs. restructuring speed:** The role of the state and employee representatives remains a major constraint on any imposed solution.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principles to **plant-by-plant financial evidence**.
- Request a **single comparison table** for each site:
  1. current cost base  
  2. closure-case savings and one-time costs  
  3. non-closure alternative savings and one-time costs  
  4. NPV, payback, and implementation risk  
- Ask each side to state:
  - which concessions are **temporarily acceptable**,
  - which are **non-negotiable red lines**,
  - and what level of savings would make a non-closure package credible.
- Keep the next round focused on **quantified equivalence**, not on reopening the same positional arguments.
