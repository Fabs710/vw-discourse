### Moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that the company needs a **durable competitiveness solution**, not just a short delay.
- There is broad willingness to **negotiate**, rather than impose a unilateral outcome.
- There is general openness to a mix of measures such as:
  - capacity adjustments
  - working-time solutions
  - redeployment
  - qualification/training
  - negotiated labour-cost contributions
  - investment commitments tied to restructuring
- All non-management parties emphasized the importance of **co-determination / governance safeguards** and a **negotiated** process.

#### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council and Union: reject closures.
  - Management and Investors: closures remain on the table.
  - State and Controlling Family: not closure-first, but not absolute rejection either.
- **Compulsory redundancies / workforce reduction**
  - Labour side rejects forced layoffs.
  - Management and Investors want meaningful headcount reduction if needed.
  - Others prefer non-forced instruments but have not ruled out reductions in principle.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees**
  - Labour side wants binding guarantees through 2030.
  - Management says guarantees must be conditional on a substantial restructuring package.
  - Others are open to protection, but not at the expense of locking in uncompetitive structures.
- **How much burden employees should carry**
  - Labour side insists on shared sacrifice with management, owners, and capital.
  - Management and Investors argue labour cost changes must be material and durable.
  - No agreement yet on scale, duration, or form of contribution.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Jobs/sites vs. competitiveness**
  - No shared definition yet of what level of site preservation is economically viable.
- **Temporary measures vs. structural savings**
  - Labour prefers alternatives to closures; management/investors want savings that are durable and equivalent to closure-related gains.
- **Burden-sharing across stakeholders**
  - There is no agreed formula for contributions from employees, management, owners, and shareholders.
- **Investment commitments vs. restructuring depth**
  - The conditions under which the company would commit to future investment in Germany are still unclear.
- **Governance constraints vs. speed of implementation**
  - The process must respect co-determination and shareholder roles, but the timetable and decision rights remain unresolved.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Please move from principle positions to **concrete package design**:
  1. Define which sites, capacities, or functions are under discussion.
  2. Specify the savings target and what counts as an equivalent alternative to closures.
  3. Identify which labour measures are acceptable, for how long, and under what conditions.
  4. Clarify what binding guarantees or investment commitments could be offered in exchange.
  5. Set out governance and decision-making rules for concluding a deal.

In the next round, it would be useful for each side to state:
- its **minimum acceptable outcome**
- its **non-negotiable red line**
- and **one concrete concession** it could consider if others move as well.
