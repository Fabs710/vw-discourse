Below is the plant-level finance interpretation of the round-4 positions and the expert input.

## Core economic point
The expert’s answer is directionally right:

- **Plant closure** usually gives the largest and cleanest **fixed-cost removal**.
- A **non-closure package** can be economically serious, but only if it removes enough of the same cost base:
  - headcount
  - shifts / capacity
  - site overhead
  - support functions
  - and some labor-cost structure

If the alternative is mostly **temporary concessions**, it will usually **not** match closure economics over time.

## What this means in accounting terms
The debate should not be “closure vs. no closure.”

It should be:

1. **What fixed costs disappear under closure?**
2. **What fixed costs remain under a non-closure restructuring?**
3. **How much one-time cost does each path require?**
4. **How durable are the savings?**
5. **How certain is implementation?**

That is the correct comparison.

## Typical economics
Broadly, the expert’s ranges are reasonable:

- **Light measures**  
  temporary wage moderation, hiring freeze, voluntary exits only  
  → usually too small to replicate closure savings

- **Moderate packages**  
  shift cuts, some headcount reduction, tighter overhead, limited pay concessions  
  → can remove a meaningful share, but often not all

- **Strong non-closure package**  
  real capacity consolidation, material headcount reduction, durable labor-cost relief, overhead cuts  
  → can approach closure economics in some cases

## Where the parties are talking past each other
### Works council / union
They are treating:
- “no closure”
as if it were sufficient by itself.

It is not. From a finance standpoint, a no-closure outcome only works if it produces:
- enough annual recurring savings,
- enough capacity reduction,
- and enough durability.

### Management / investors
They are treating closure as if it were automatically superior.

That is also not proven until you quantify:
- closure savings,
- one-time closure charges,
- transfer costs,
- and the NPV of alternatives.

## What the real analysis would need
For each site, the comparison should include:

### A. Current state
- direct labor cost
- indirect labor / support cost
- plant overhead
- depreciation and asset charges
- utilization and throughput
- allocation of shared costs

### B. Closure case
- annual savings from shutting the site
- one-time severance and closure costs
- asset impairment / demolition / remediation
- logistics and transfer costs
- NPV and payback

### C. Non-closure package
- wage / allowance concessions
- working-time measures
- voluntary exits / retirements
- shift elimination
- overhead reductions
- productivity and throughput gains
- one-time implementation costs
- NPV and payback

### D. Implementation risk
- strike risk
- employee acceptance risk
- legal / co-determination timing
- chance that savings reverse after expiration
- execution complexity

## Finance judgment
Without plant-specific numbers, the best conclusion is:

- **A non-closure package can be credible if it is deep, durable, and enforceable.**
- **A temporary labor concession is usually not enough.**
- **Closure remains stronger when the company needs fast, permanent fixed-cost removal.**
- **But closure is not automatically the economically best option if a deep negotiated package can remove most of the same cost base with lower one-time charges and less disruption.**

## What would change the conclusion
I would change the conclusion if the site data showed either of these:

1. **Closure savings are much larger than the best non-closure package**, after one-time costs are included.
2. **The non-closure package cannot permanently remove enough fixed cost**, even with major concessions.

Conversely, a non-closure package becomes more credible if it can show:
- a material reduction in headcount,
- fewer shifts or lower utilization,
- real overhead cuts,
- and contractually durable labor-cost relief.

## Bottom line
The expert’s key statement can be summarized as:

> Non-closure measures can close a large part of the gap, and sometimes most of it, but only if they are structurally deep. Temporary measures alone usually cannot replicate closure economics.
