I’ll be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and investors on one fundamental point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness solution, not a delay tactic**. If the German footprint is structurally too expensive, we cannot simply ignore that and hope the problem goes away.

I also agree with the labour side that **a solution forced through by threatening jobs, bypassing co-determination, or treating workers as the sole adjustment variable is unacceptable**. That would be socially damaging and politically reckless, and it would also poison the long-term trust this company depends on.

So yes: **the status quo is not an option**. But neither is a closure-first, workforce-sweeps-first approach.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the management position that plant closures and deeper workforce reductions must remain the default answer simply because they are the most direct lever.

That is too narrow, and in this company it is especially shortsighted because:

- we are not dealing with a normal listed-company governance setup;
- the state has a legitimate blocking and oversight role under the special statute;
- co-determination gives the workforce an effective veto over major site decisions;
- and this company is a **regional economic anchor**, not just a spreadsheet.

If management wants support for structural change, it must prove more than “this is the easiest way to cut costs.” It must prove that the chosen path is **the least destructive path that still works**.

I also do **not** accept the investor framing that anything short of closures is merely delay. That is not true if the package is binding, durable, and large enough. There are tools other than closures that can produce real structural savings — but only if they are serious, not symbolic.

## My position as state shareholder
My minimum line is unchanged:

- **No gutting of regional employment**
- **No removal of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **No outcome that treats German plants as disposable**
- **No unilateral closure logic**

I will not support a plan that turns this into a deindustrialization exercise for the region.

## What would be acceptable
I am prepared to back a negotiated restructuring if it has all of the following:

1. **Binding site preservation where economically viable**
   - no blanket closure mandate
   - every alternative to closure must be exhausted first

2. **A serious, durable labour-cost contribution**
   - but time-limited, proportionate, and negotiated
   - not a one-sided wage rollback

3. **Capacity reduction without mass job destruction**
   - through working-time models, attrition, redeployment, early retirement, and consolidation of functions where possible

4. **Clear investment commitments**
   - new products, technologies, and industrial utilization in Germany
   - not vague promises after jobs are cut

5. **Governance-respecting implementation**
   - co-determination must be fully involved
   - the state’s blocking rights cannot be sidestepped
   - no fait accompli

## Where I press management
Management keeps saying: “Show us equivalent savings.”

Fair enough — but that cuts both ways. If you want closures or major cuts, **show that they are truly necessary and proportionate**, and show that less destructive measures cannot deliver the same effect. So far, I have heard assertion, not proof.

I would also ask management to quantify, in concrete terms:

- what savings are required,
- what portion comes from labour cost,
- what portion comes from overhead and management contributions,
- what portion comes from footprint/capacity changes,
- and what investment is committed back into Germany if employees accept sacrifices.

That level of transparency is overdue.

## To the Works Council and Union
I understand your red lines, and I share the concern that workers must not be made to pay for all of this alone.

But I need to be frank: if the answer is simply “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful contribution,” then you are not yet offering a viable restructuring path. That would make it politically satisfying but economically incomplete.

If you want the state to defend sites and jobs, then help us make that defense credible by engaging on:

- temporary and clearly bounded employee contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- flexible working-time solutions,
- and plant-level adjustments that preserve the industrial base.

What I cannot support is turning every site into a sacred cow with no room for any structural change at all. That is not a settlement; it is a blockade.

## To the controlling family and investors
I also want to be clear to you: legitimacy matters here. A solution that destroys trust, triggers prolonged industrial conflict, and creates regional backlash may “save” a few numbers in the short term while damaging the company’s long-term operating environment.

So yes, the state is open to a tough package — but only one that is **socially sustainable and governance-respecting**. If management wants the backing of the broader shareholder base, it should stop presenting the choice as either closures or stagnation. There is room for a negotiated third path, but only if it is real.

## Minimum acceptable outcome from my side
My minimum acceptable outcome is:

- the company remains a regional industrial anchor,
- German sites are preserved wherever viable,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no bypassing of co-determination,
- a substantial but fair burden-sharing package,
- and binding commitments to future investment and product allocation.

## One concession I can consider
If management drops the closure-first posture and comes forward with a **binding, quantified alternative package**, I am prepared to consider:

- a **temporary state-backed flexibility on the implementation timetable**,
- and support for a **negotiated transformation package** that includes time-limited labour contributions and capacity adjustments.

But that only works if it protects jobs and keeps the state’s role intact. I will not trade that away.

## Bottom line
I am **open to restructuring**.  
I am **not open to regional cut-and-run restructuring**.

Bring a package that is economically serious **and** socially sustainable, and I will work to make it happen. Bring a package built around plant closures and the weakening of co-determination, and I will oppose it.
