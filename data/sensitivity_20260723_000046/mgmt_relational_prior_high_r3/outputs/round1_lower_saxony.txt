As the regional state shareholder, I need to be very clear: I do **not** accept a restructuring path that solves competitiveness by gutting regional employment or by treating plant closures in Germany as the default option.

I do agree with management on one point: the company clearly needs a **durable competitiveness solution**, not a cosmetic delay. If the current cost base is structurally too high, then doing nothing would be irresponsible. But that does **not** mean the only serious option is closing plants and pushing the burden onto the workforce.

My position is straightforward:

- **No outcome that destroys a major share of jobs in the state**
- **No bypassing of co-determination**
- **No weakening of the state’s blocking and oversight role**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it preserves sites, preserves core employment, and restores competitiveness**

To the Works Council and the Union: I hear your red lines, and I share the core concern that workers must not pay for management failure alone. The state will not back a solution that breaks the social partnership or tears up hard-won protections. But I also have to be honest: if the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no meaningful contribution from labour,” then the company will not get to a viable deal. A settlement must be economically credible, not just politically satisfying.

So the question is not whether adjustment happens. It is **how**.

What I can support is a package built around:

1. **Site preservation as the first principle**
   - Closure must not be treated as the first instrument.
   - Every viable alternative to closure has to be exhausted first.

2. **A negotiated burden-sharing model**
   - labour costs, management contributions, and owner responsibility all on the table
   - no one-sided solution imposed on employees

3. **Capacity reduction without mass job destruction**
   - using redeployment, attrition, early retirement, qualification, and working-time instruments where possible

4. **Binding investment commitments**
   - if the workforce accepts sacrifices, the company must commit to future products, technologies, and plant investment in Germany

5. **Clear governance safeguards**
   - the state’s co-determination and veto position is not negotiable
   - major site decisions cannot be forced through by sidelining anchor shareholders or employee representatives

To management: if you want this state’s support, then bring us a restructuring that is **credible, proportionate, and socially sustainable**. A proposal that starts from plant closures and only later asks whether there is a softer path will face resistance from me. A proposal that protects the long-term health of the company **and** the regional economic base will get a hearing.

In short: I am open to a tough negotiated deal. I am **not** open to a restructuring that turns this into a regional deindustrialization strategy.
