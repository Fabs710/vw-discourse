Here is a procedural readout of the impasse and possible bridging options.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is not mainly about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which restructuring path can be proven to achieve sufficient, durable savings**.

The conflict sits on three linked issues:

1. **Closure as a legitimate option**
   - Management, investors, and the family keep closure on the table as a potentially necessary lever.
   - Works council, union, and state reject closures as a starting principle and want them effectively ruled out unless proved unavoidable.

2. **Proof standard**
   - Labour is asking management to justify closures with plant-by-plant numbers.
   - Management is asking labour to prove that non-closure measures are economically equivalent.
   - Both sides are asking the other side to carry the burden of proof.

3. **Acceptable burden-sharing**
   - Labour accepts temporary, bounded contributions only.
   - Management/investors want meaningful, durable reductions in fixed cost and capacity.
   - The dispute is over whether non-closure measures can be deep enough without crossing labour’s red lines.

## Possible compromise positions
These are facilitator proposals for discussion, not endorsements.

### Compromise 1: “Numbers-first, no irreversible decisions yet”
**Proposal:**  
Both sides agree to a short, protected evaluation phase in which:
- no plant closure decision is finalized,
- no compulsory redundancies are triggered,
- and the independent expert produces a plant-by-plant NPV comparison.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Gives labour the proof-based process it wants.
- Gives management/investors a route to test whether non-closure measures really close the gap.
- Gives the state and family a chance to avoid a rushed, value-destructive decision.

### Compromise 2: “Conditional non-closure package with built-in review gates”
**Proposal:**  
A temporary restructuring agreement is signed now, built around:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- retraining and redeployment,
- overhead/procurement cuts,
- and a temporary employee contribution,
with **explicit review gates** tied to measurable savings, utilization, and NPV milestones.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Satisfies labour’s preference for avoiding closures at the outset.
- Gives management a mechanism to confirm whether the package is producing the required structural savings.
- Preserves a fallback if targets are missed, without predetermining closure.

### Compromise 3: “Selective footprint adjustment instead of blanket closure or blanket preservation”
**Proposal:**  
The parties distinguish among sites and functions:
- some sites are preserved with investment commitments,
- some capacities are consolidated internally,
- some support functions are merged or relocated,
- and any closure discussion is limited to sites where the expert shows that no non-closure package can close the gap.

**Why it may bridge stakeholders:**  
- Reduces the binary clash of “all plants stay open” versus “closures are on the table everywhere.”
- Allows the hardest economics to be addressed site-specifically.
- May be more acceptable to the state and works council than a broad closure program.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What plant-specific savings threshold would make you accept a non-closure package as sufficient, and would you commit in advance to using the independent expert’s model as the decision basis?**

### To the Works Council
**If the independent expert shows that a deep non-closure package still leaves a material NPV gap versus closure at one or two sites, what additional measures would you consider beyond temporary employee contributions?**

### To the Union
**Which exact elements of burden-sharing are you prepared to treat as negotiable if they are limited in duration, legally secured, and tied to binding job protections?**

### To Investors
**Would you support a non-closure solution if it delivers most of the closure economics with lower one-time costs and lower implementation risk, even if it is slower to realize?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Will you support a process that defers any closure decision until the independent site-level comparison is complete, even if that delays a final resolution?**

### To the State Government
**Would you back a conditional package that preserves sites for now but includes measurable exit criteria if the promised savings do not materialize?**

### To the Expert
**Can you provide a neutral decision matrix that identifies, for each plant, the minimum combination of headcount, capacity, and cost measures needed for a non-closure package to reach a closure-equivalent NPV?**
