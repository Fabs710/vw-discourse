As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: **we do not accept a restructuring that destroys regional jobs, weakens the German industrial base, or sidelines the co-determination architecture that has kept this company stable for decades.**

At the same time, I am not pretending the company can simply freeze the status quo. The competitive pressure is real. If the business model is not adjusted, we all lose — workers, the region, and shareholders alike. So the question is not whether there must be change, but **what kind of change, and who bears the burden**.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness solution, not a cosmetic one**. Short-term patching will not secure investment in future products or protect the company’s long-term role in the region.

I also agree with the union and works council that:
- the workforce did not create this crisis alone,
- burden-sharing must be fair,
- and any restructuring must preserve a credible industrial future in Germany.

### Where I clearly disagree
I **do not accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point**. For a company of this importance, closures are not a routine management tool. They have deep regional, political, and social consequences, and they are not acceptable unless every serious alternative has been exhausted and a truly unavoidable case is made. At this stage, I do **not** see that case being made convincingly.

I also **do not accept a restructuring logic that treats the workforce as the only adjustment variable**. If management wants labour cost moderation, capacity reduction, or flexibility, then capital, management, and the company’s strategic decisions must also contribute.

And I am equally clear that **co-determination is not decorative**. The state’s role, and the workforce’s role, are not to be bypassed because restructuring is difficult. Any attempt to force through a fait accompli will meet resistance from us.

### What a viable path would need to include
If management wants support from us, then it needs to bring a package built on these principles:

1. **No mass layoffs and no rushed plant closures.**  
   Capacity adjustment must be handled primarily through negotiated, reversible measures.

2. **Site security and investment commitments.**  
   If workers are asked to contribute, the company must commit to German product allocation, future EV investment, and utilization of the domestic sites.

3. **A fair and temporary employee contribution only if there is reciprocity.**  
   Wage moderation, working-time flexibility, or the trimming of extras can only be discussed if they are time-limited, proportionate, and tied to binding guarantees.

4. **A serious industrial strategy, not just cost cutting.**  
   The issue is not only expense; it is also product mix, utilization, and long-term positioning.

5. **Respect for governance rights.**  
   The special statute, the supervisory-board veto structure, and co-determination are real. Any proposal that ignores them is not a proposal, it is a confrontation.

### To the works council and union
Your position on no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of protections is understandable and in many respects legitimate. I support the insistence on **fair burden-sharing** and **binding commitments**. But I would also urge realism: if the company requires structural adjustment, then the debate should focus on **how to protect the maximum number of good jobs and sites**, not on slogans alone.

### To management
If you come with a package centered on closures, compulsion, and unilateral wage pressure, you will likely trigger not just social conflict but governance conflict. That is a high-risk path. It may look decisive, but it could also destroy trust, delay implementation, and undermine the company’s strategic future.

If, however, you bring a negotiated plan that:
- preserves sites,
- reduces capacity through voluntary and flexible tools,
- provides credible investment,
- and distributes sacrifice fairly,

then the state can engage constructively.

### Bottom line
The regional state will **not** support a plan that guts employment in Germany or strips away our co-determination role. We are prepared to back a restructuring, but only one that is **socially balanced, economically credible, and regionally responsible**.

Management should not mistake our willingness to negotiate for approval of closures. That is not where we are.
