### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**, not just a temporary downturn.
- There is broad agreement that a solution must be **durable**, not a short-term patch.
- Most sides accept that **voluntary measures** should be part of the toolbox, including:
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - attrition and retirement corridors.
- There is shared recognition that any restructuring must include **future product and technology investment**.
- Most actors agree that the workforce should not bear the adjustment burden alone; **burden-sharing** should include management, owners, and capital.
- There is broad acceptance that **co-determination/governance constraints are real** and must be factored into any workable plan.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and regional state reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, the family holding, and investors say closures or equivalent footprint reduction cannot be ruled out if needed for competitiveness.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour side insists on no forced layoffs.
  - Management, family holding, and investors will not rule out compulsory measures if voluntary tools are insufficient.

- **Scope of job security**
  - Labour side wants extended protections, potentially through 2030.
  - Management and capital-side actors say such guarantees are not credible without substantial structural change.

- **Depth of cost reduction**
  - Management, family holding, and investors want significant, measurable labour-cost and capacity reductions.
  - Labour and state side want changes to be negotiated and proportionate, not driven by unilateral cost-cutting.

- **Role of co-determination / veto realities**
  - Labour and state emphasize substantive participation and no fait accompli.
  - Management and capital-side actors accept co-determination but do not want it to block necessary restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is actually required**, and whether it can be achieved without closures.
- **What counts as “equivalent” savings** if plant closures are politically or socially off the table.
- **How far labour-cost moderation can go**, and whether any concessions would be temporary or structural.
- **What binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment** can realistically be offered in exchange for workforce concessions.
- **Who carries the risk** if the restructuring underdelivers: workforce, management, owners, or capital markets.
- **How to make any agreement executable under the governance structure** while still being economically credible.
- **Whether the solution prioritizes maximum job preservation or maximum competitiveness**, and how those objectives are balanced.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package design**: quantify the savings gap, the required capacity adjustment, and the investment commitments.
- Ask each side to state **minimum acceptable conditions** and **non-negotiables** in operational terms.
- Compare two or three concrete scenarios:
  1. no closures / stronger labour concessions,
  2. limited footprint reduction / stronger job guarantees,
  3. hybrid package with phased measures and binding investment commitments.
- Clarify what **measurable safeguards** would make voluntary measures credible and what **triggers** would activate further steps if targets are missed.
