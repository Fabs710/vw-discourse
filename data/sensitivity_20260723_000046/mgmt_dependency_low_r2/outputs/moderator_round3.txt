Core impasse, as it now stands:

1. **Management / shareholders want closure-equivalent savings and a credible route to competitiveness.**  
2. **Works council / union want binding protection against closures and compulsory redundancies.**  
3. **The state wants to preserve industrial capacity and employment, but cannot ignore the economic gap.**  
4. **The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; it is about which instruments are permitted to achieve it and who carries the risk if the no-closure path underdelivers.**

In other words, the dispute has narrowed to **sequencing, guarantees, and burden-sharing**:
- management sees closures/forced layoffs as leverage or fallback,
- labour sees them as unacceptable starting points,
- investors see anything short of structural savings as value-destroying,
- the state wants a negotiated solution but needs it to be economically real.

## Facilitator compromise options

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure pact with hard triggers
A possible bridge is a **binding no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy period** for a defined term, paired with:
- quantified annual savings targets,
- fixed FTE and capacity reduction milestones,
- management/owner contributions,
- and predefined fallback steps if targets are missed.

This would preserve the labour-side red lines for a set period while giving management and investors a measurable performance test.

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring without immediate closures
A second option is a **hybrid package** that avoids immediate plant closures but still permits:
- shift eliminations,
- partial line or function consolidation,
- targeted site specialization,
- voluntary exits and retirement corridors,
- and a limited overhead reset.

This could be framed as footprint reduction **inside** the German base rather than plant shutdowns, if the savings are demonstrated to be closure-equivalent.

### Proposal 3: Site guarantees tied to investment and productivity commitments
A third bridge is a **site-and-investment bargain**:
- binding German site commitments,
- future product allocation and investment pledges,
- combined with measurable productivity, complexity, and headcount reductions.

This would make preservation of sites contingent on an operational modernization package rather than an unconditional promise or an unconditional closure threat.

### To the works council
**Which quantified reduction measures inside a no-closure framework would you accept as sufficient to make the package closure-equivalent, and what objective triggers would you allow if those targets are missed?**

### To the union
**Are you prepared to support a time-limited labour contribution package if it is paired with binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy guarantees plus verifiable savings milestones?**

### To the regional state government
**What concrete governance or political leverage are you prepared to use to require management to present a serious no-closure alternative within a fixed timetable?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Will you instruct management to test a no-closure package first, on the condition that it is quantified and monitored, rather than keeping closures as the default benchmark of seriousness?**

### To the institutional investors
**What minimum metrics—annual savings, capacity reduction, and timing—would make a no-closure restructuring credible enough for your support?**
