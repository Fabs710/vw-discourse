We agree on the diagnosis: this is a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary setback. We also agree that a cosmetic truce would destroy value. Where we differ is on what counts as a credible restructuring.

## Where we agree
- **With management:** the company needs durable cost reduction, not delay.
- **With the family holding:** the solution must be executable and margin-accretive, not merely politically convenient.
- **With the expert assessment:** a no-closure route is possible **only if it is deep enough** to be economically equivalent.
- **With the state:** conflict and social damage are costly too, so a negotiated path is preferable if it is real.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no real downside for labour,
- and binding job security through 2030,

is **not yet a solution**. It is a boundary. We respect the boundary, but capital markets do not reward boundaries; they reward **verified earnings repair**.

If you want to rule out closures and forced layoffs, then you must show how the company still achieves:
- a materially lower fixed-cost base,
- lower capacity,
- real headcount reduction,
- and durable margin improvement.

So far, we have not seen a quantified package that proves that. Until we do, your position looks like a desire to preserve the existing structure while asking shareholders to absorb the downside. We will not support that.

### To the union
We agree that temporary concessions alone are insufficient. But we do **not** accept a blanket refusal of compulsory redundancies in every circumstance. If voluntary exits and redeployment do not deliver the required savings, then the business does not become competitive by declaration.

A strike-heavy or delay-heavy path is also value-destructive. It burns cash, disrupts output, and damages the very margin recovery we need. We are not willing to back an arrangement that substitutes industrial conflict for operational adjustment.

### To management
We agree that the company needs hard change. But do not overstate the case for closures if you cannot execute them cleanly within the governance structure. A plan that is economically neat but institutionally impossible is not credible. We need a package that is:
- numerically specific,
- implementable under co-determination,
- and fast enough to restore margins.

If closures are necessary, justify them with a clear savings case and execution path. If a no-closure package is viable, show the numbers. Right now, the burden is on management to prove either route.

### To the regional state government
We support your preference for preserving industrial capacity **if** the alternative actually closes the gap. But “no closures by default” is not a financial argument. If the no-closure route leaves the cost base structurally impaired, then it is just a softer way to delay the same problem.

## Our position
We will support a settlement only if it contains:
1. **Quantified annual savings**
2. **A real, measurable capacity reset**
3. **Material net FTE reduction**
4. **Permanent—not merely temporary—cost relief**
5. **Visible management and owner contribution**
6. **A governance-executable implementation plan**
7. **Binding monitoring and trigger points**

We are open to a no-closure settlement. We are **not** open to a symbolic one.

## What would move us
A concrete package with:
- the savings gap in euros,
- the FTE reduction target,
- the shift/capacity reduction plan,
- the overhead cuts,
- the management/owner contribution,
- and the investment commitments tied to the restructuring.

If that package is truly closure-equivalent, then we will judge it on the economics, not on whether a plant is shut.

## What we will not support
- A cosmetic compromise.
- A deal that preserves every site but leaves margins structurally impaired.
- A temporary labour concession without permanent savings.
- A conflict path that destroys value through delay, strikes, and underdelivery.
