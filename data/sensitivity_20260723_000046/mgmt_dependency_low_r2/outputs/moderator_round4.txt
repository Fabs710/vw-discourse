## Core impasse
The discussion is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; all sides accept that it is. The impasse is over **the instrument and the proof standard**:

- **Employee-side representatives** require binding no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy guarantees first, with any workforce contribution conditioned on those guarantees.
- **Management, the family holding, and investors** require hard, quantified evidence that a no-closure route can achieve **closure-equivalent savings, capacity reduction, and margin repair**.
- **The state** is willing to back a no-closure path, but only if it is operationally serious and not merely a delay mechanism.

So the deadlock is:
**Who moves first — binding protection or quantified sacrifice — and what exact package counts as “equivalent”?**

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise positions

### 1) Time-limited no-closure pact with hard triggers
A compromise could be:
- no German plant closures for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that same period,
- in exchange for a quantified restructuring package with specific FTE, shift, and overhead targets,
- plus pre-agreed trigger points that activate additional measures if milestones are missed.

This bridges:
- employee demands for protection,
- management’s demand for enforceable economics,
- the state’s desire for stability.

### 2) Phased package: protection first, then structural review
A second compromise could be:
- an initial phase focused on voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, complexity reduction, and overhead cuts,
- paired with a binding governance review after verified savings are measured,
- with closures not implemented automatically, but only reconsidered if a jointly defined savings gap remains.

This bridges:
- the employee side’s rejection of immediate closure logic,
- management’s insistence that the savings gap be closed,
- investors’ demand for measurable progress.

### 3) Hybrid footprint reduction without full plant closure
A third compromise could be:
- no full plant closures,
- but selective line, shift, or function consolidation within sites,
- combined with voluntary separation corridors and permanent overhead reduction,
- plus binding investment commitments for the retained footprint.

This may bridge:
- the workforce’s red line on site destruction,
- management’s need for real capacity reduction,
- and the state’s interest in preserving industrial presence.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If management were to put a fully quantified no-closure package on the table, what minimum **measurable** capacity and FTE reductions would you consider sufficient to make it credible?

### To the Industrial Union
Would you accept a time-limited no-closure settlement if it included binding savings targets, verified milestones, and pre-agreed fallback measures short of plant closure?

### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap, FTE reduction, and capacity reduction figures would make you say publicly that a no-closure route is economically equivalent and therefore viable?

### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to ensure that a no-closure package is not merely announced but actually implemented and monitored?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you instruct management to prioritize a quantified no-closure model first, even if that means temporarily removing closure pressure from the table during negotiations?

### To the Institutional Investors
What specific economic metrics would convince you that a no-closure restructuring is not cosmetic, but sufficiently close to a closure-led outcome to protect value?

### To the Expert
What minimum combination of shift cuts, overhead reductions, and FTE reductions would you treat as the threshold for calling a no-closure package operationally credible?
