I will respond directly, because the issue is no longer whether restructuring is needed. It is. The real question is whether management and capital are prepared to pursue a **serious no-closure solution** rather than using closures and forced layoffs as leverage.

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis: this company has a structural competitiveness problem, and it needs a durable, not cosmetic, solution. I also agree that voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and complexity reduction all belong in the package.

Where I disagree is your continued insistence that **closures and compulsory redundancies remain the default fallback**.

That is not a neutral management position. It is a way to shift the burden onto the workforce before the alternatives have actually been exhausted.

The expert assessment has already made the key point:
- a **no-closure path is possible**,
- but only if it is **deep enough**,
- and only if it includes **real capacity reduction, real FTE reduction, and real cost relief**.

So if you want our cooperation, put the actual numbers on the table:
- the savings gap,
- the FTE target,
- the shift/capacity reduction target,
- the management and owner contribution,
- and the investment commitments.

Until then, “closures may be necessary” is not a solution. It is pressure.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no plant closures as the starting point,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of job security,
- and no restructuring where workers carry the pain alone.

I support your refusal to let this become a sacrifice plan for employees.

Where I would sharpen the line is this: a no-closure settlement cannot be symbolic. If we are going to hold the line on closures and forced layoffs, then the alternative must be operationally serious.

That means we must be willing to discuss:
- temporary wage moderation or deferred extras,
- longer working-time flexibility,
- substantial voluntary exit corridors,
- internal redeployment on a meaningful scale,
- and simplification of products and processes.

But I am equally clear: those talks only make sense if they are matched by **binding guarantees** on:
- sites,
- jobs,
- investment,
- and no compulsory redundancies.

## To the regional state government
I appreciate your support for a negotiated route and your reluctance to start with closures. That matters.

You are right that the company has a regional responsibility. You are also right that a no-closure solution is possible only if it is **deep, quantified, and credible**.

But let me be equally clear: “no closures” cannot just be a political slogan. If we want to avoid closures, then the package must genuinely close the savings gap through:
- headcount reduction,
- shift reduction,
- overhead cuts,
- management contribution,
- and a credible investment roadmap.

So I support your direction, but not as a soft compromise. It has to be a hard negotiated settlement with real operational content.

## To the controlling family holding
You say you want competitiveness and durability. Fine — so do we. But durability does **not** come from starting a confrontation with the workforce and expecting peace.

You keep saying a no-closure path must be economically equivalent. That is true. The expert said exactly that. But the expert also confirmed it is possible if the package is deep enough.

That means your governance role should be used to force management to bring forward the **deep no-closure package**, not to keep plant closure as the benchmark of seriousness.

If you want a stable solution, then back a package that:
- preserves German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- and still achieves the savings target through negotiated means.

If you instead insist that closures stay on the table as the first resort, then you are not reducing risk — you are escalating it.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to reject cosmetic compromise. I agree with that.

But your benchmark must not be “closures prove seriousness.” That is too simplistic. The operational test is whether the company gets:
- durable savings,
- lower complexity,
- lower capacity,
- and a credible future investment base.

If the savings can be achieved without closures, then that is not weakness. It is a better outcome because it avoids the damage of conflict, know-how loss, and long-term morale collapse.

## My position, plainly
The union will support a serious restructuring only if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Real burden-sharing from management and owners**
4. **Temporary and proportionate workforce contributions**
5. **Binding investment and product commitments**
6. **A durable collective-bargaining framework**
7. **A real role for co-determination in monitoring the deal**

What we will not accept:
- closures as a bargaining starting point,
- forced layoffs as a normal adjustment tool,
- or a restructuring where workers carry the pain while management and owners mainly issue demands.

## On the “bridge” proposals
A phased package is only acceptable if it is genuinely protective, not a trap.

I would consider a time-limited arrangement **only** if it contains:
- a binding no-closure commitment,
- a binding no-compulsory-redundancies commitment,
- measurable savings targets,
- and a shared oversight mechanism.

What I will not accept is a so-called “phase one” that simply postpones closure pressure and leaves workers exposed later.

## What needs to happen now
Management needs to stop speaking in generalities and give us the actual package.

We need:
- the quantified savings gap,
- the required FTE reductions,
- the exact shift and capacity reductions,
- the management and owner contribution,
- and the investment commitments.

If management can show a real no-closure solution that is closure-equivalent and fair, then we can negotiate seriously.

If not, then the workforce will not accept being the sole adjustment variable. We have already shown we can mobilize. We are prepared to use that leverage if necessary.

## Bottom line
We are open to a hard, fair settlement.

We are **not** open to:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a one-sided restructuring that treats workers as the sole source of savings.

Bring a real no-closure package with hard numbers, binding guarantees, and shared sacrifice. Then we can talk. Until then, our line remains unchanged.
