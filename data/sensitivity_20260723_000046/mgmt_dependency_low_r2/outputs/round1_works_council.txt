As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. We are prepared to discuss a restructuring that secures the company’s future, but only if it is based on **fair burden-sharing**, **binding site guarantees**, and a **credible industrial strategy** rather than a short-term cost-cutting exercise.

What we will argue for:
1. **All German plants remain open.**
2. **Job guarantees are extended through 2030.**
3. **Adjustments are handled through natural attrition, internal redeployment, qualification, working-time measures, and voluntary instruments — not forced layoffs.**
4. **Any cost contribution from labour must be matched by real commitments from management and owners on investment, future products, and site security.**
5. **The works council and employee representatives must have a real role in shaping the restructuring, not just being informed after decisions are taken.**

What we oppose:
- Plant closures or relocation of production out of Germany.
- Mass layoffs or operational dismissals.
- Breaking existing protections while asking the workforce for sacrifices.
- A fait accompli presented as “there is no alternative.”

We are ready to negotiate seriously, and we understand the competitive pressure. But if management comes with closures, forced redundancies, or the destruction of hard-won guarantees, then it will meet firm resistance — including industrial action and use of all co-determination rights available to us.
