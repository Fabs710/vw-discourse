We agree with the Works Council on the essentials: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures in Germany, and no dismantling of the collective job-security framework as the price of this crisis**.

Management’s current framing is too one-sided. If the workforce is asked for sacrifices, then the burden must be **shared fairly**:
- management and owners must contribute visibly,
- capacity must be adjusted through negotiated, reversible tools,
- and the company must present a credible industrial future, not just a cost-cutting program.

Where we are prepared to be constructive:
1. **Working-time and capacity measures** rather than forced layoffs.
2. **Voluntary instruments**: retirement corridors, internal transfers, qualification, and attrition.
3. **A negotiated employee contribution** only if it is tied to hard guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment.
4. **Temporary labour-cost moderation** or the waiver of certain extras, but not an open-ended wage sacrifice that becomes permanent.
5. **Binding commitments** on future products, EV investment, and utilization of German sites.

Where we clearly disagree with any restructuring logic:
- **Plant closures are not acceptable.**
- **Mass redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **A unilateral pay-down by workers while management and capital escape contribution is not acceptable.**
- **Any attempt to break the collective bargaining framework under crisis pressure will be met with resistance.**

Let me be explicit: the workforce is willing to help secure the company’s future, but not to finance it alone and not by giving up fundamental protections. If management wants peace, it needs to bring a package that includes **fair sacrifice from all sides and real guarantees for jobs and sites**. Otherwise, we will not treat this as a “necessary adjustment” but as an attack on the workforce, and we are prepared to respond accordingly.
