We agree with the core diagnosis and with the need for a durable restructuring. But we will be blunt: **we will not support a deal that preserves an uncompetitive cost base just because it is politically easier to sell**.

## Where we agree

### With management
- This is a **structural** competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip.
- The company needs **durable cost reduction**, not a cosmetic truce.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, training, working-time flexibility, and complexity reduction all belong in the package.
- Future investment only makes sense if the operating base is leaner.

### With the family holding
- A weak compromise that leaves margins structurally impaired would destroy value.
- Governance reality matters; any solution must be executable, not just rhetorically neat.
- The final package must materially improve cash flow and return-on-sales.

### With the expert assessment
The operational point is the key one:  
**a no-closure solution is possible, but only if it is deep enough to be economically equivalent to closures**.

That means:
- real headcount reduction,
- shift reduction,
- footprint/capacity reduction,
- product simplification,
- and overhead cuts.

That is the right standard.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
Your position is understandable socially, but **it is not acceptable as an unconditional framework**:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- job security through 2030,
- and no real structural downside for labour.

That does **not** automatically produce a competitive cost base. If those are hard red lines, then the burden shifts back onto shareholders to fund an uncompetitive structure. We will not support that.

We also do not accept prolonged industrial conflict as a substitute for restructuring. A strike-heavy path would burn cash, delay savings, and damage output. That is value-destructive for everyone, including shareholders.

### With management
We do **not** want management to confuse “hard” with “credible.”  
If you push closures or forced layoffs without a governance-safe route, you risk delay, conflict, and value destruction.

So: do not bring us a plan that is economically neat on paper but impossible to execute under co-determination and the special statute. That would be a false solution.

### With the regional state
We support the state’s emphasis on regional responsibility and social stability. But “no closures by default” is only acceptable if the alternative **actually closes the gap**.

If it does not, then it is just a softer way to postpone the same problem.

## Our bottom line

We are **not** saying closures are the only solution.  
We **are** saying that if closures are ruled out, the alternative must be **closure-equivalent in substance**.

That means:

1. **A quantified savings target**
   - annual run-rate savings sufficient to restore competitive margins

2. **A measurable capacity reset**
   - fewer shifts, less output, lower fixed-cost footprint

3. **Material FTE reduction**
   - not just attrition slogans

4. **Durable labour-cost relief**
   - not purely temporary symbolism

5. **Management and owner contribution**
   - visible overhead restraint and capital support

6. **A fast, executable implementation path**
   - no open-ended review cycles

## What would move us

We would support a settlement only if it contains all of the following:
- a real savings gap, quantified in euros and FTE,
- a concrete no-closure package that matches closure economics,
- binding monitoring and trigger points,
- visible management/owner sacrifice,
- and a credible route back to competitive margins.

If the no-closure route can really do that, fine — we will back it.  
If it cannot, then pretending otherwise is not compromise; it is value destruction.

## What we will not support
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a settlement that protects every site while leaving the cost base uncompetitive,
- or an outcome that substitutes political messaging for hard economics.

So our line remains unchanged:  
**show us a serious, quantified, closure-equivalent package, or accept that the market will continue to punish the company for underperformance.**
