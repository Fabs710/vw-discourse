We hear the concerns, but I need to be equally clear: **the status quo is not viable**.

The company is facing a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip. German factory costs are materially above target, the European market is shrinking, and we are under pressure from lower-cost competitors and China. If we do not take real action now, we will not have the margins or the cash flow to fund the product and technology investment needed through 2030.

So let me respond directly.

### Where I agree
- I agree that the workforce did not create this situation alone.
- I agree that a purely short-term cut would be the wrong answer.
- I agree that any solution must be durable and must support future investment, not just patch the next quarter.
- I agree that voluntary instruments, redeployment, qualification, and working-time measures can and should be part of the package.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) “No plant closures” as an absolute is not acceptable
If every German site is automatically protected, then we are not restructuring—we are preserving overcapacity. That is exactly what we cannot afford. I am not threatening closures for effect; I am stating a business necessity. We need the flexibility to reduce capacity materially, and that may include plant closures or major site restructuring.

#### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be guaranteed upfront
I would prefer to avoid forced redundancies, but I will not promise that nothing compulsory can happen regardless of the size of the gap. If the savings required cannot be achieved through negotiated instruments alone, then we must retain the ability to act.

#### 3) A job-security guarantee through 2030 without structural cost relief is not realistic
A guarantee can only exist if the economics support it. A blanket extension of protections while leaving the cost base untouched would simply lock in uncompetitiveness.

### What I need to see
If the employee side wants to avoid closures and compulsory measures, then I need a package that delivers all of the following:

- **A substantial, durable reduction in labour costs**
- **A real reduction in capacity and overheads**
- **A meaningful contribution from the workforce**
- **Clear productivity improvements**
- **Credible room for investment in future products and technology**

That means we need to discuss:
- wage moderation or other labour-cost concessions,
- longer working-time flexibility,
- reduction of legacy extras,
- voluntary exit and transfer programs at scale,
- and, where necessary, site rationalization.

### On the role of co-determination
We respect co-determination, but it cannot be used as a veto over necessary transformation. The supervisory board structure means no one side can impose terms unilaterally. That cuts both ways. If labour wants to avoid sharper interventions, it needs to engage with the scale of the problem rather than declare core outcomes off limits.

### Bottom line
I am not prepared to endorse a cosmetic compromise that keeps every plant open, preserves every protection, and leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged. That would fail the company and, in the end, fail the workforce as well.

If the employee side is serious about securing German jobs long term, then it must help deliver a **smaller, leaner, competitive footprint**. I am willing to negotiate on the shape of that adjustment. I am **not** willing to negotiate away the need for deep structural change.
