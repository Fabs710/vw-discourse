## Moderator Summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
All parties agree that:
- the current situation is not sustainable,
- a real restructuring is necessary,
- non-binding assurances are insufficient,
- the workforce should not bear the burden alone,
- and any settlement must be credible, durable, and enforceable.

There is also broad acceptance that a negotiated outcome is preferable to prolonged conflict.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issue is **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available as restructuring tools**.

- **Works Council, Union, and Regional State Government**: reject German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, and insist on binding site and employment guarantees.
- **Management, Controlling Family Holding, and Institutional Investors**: say they are open to alternatives, but do not accept blanket exclusions if the company’s cost and capacity gap cannot otherwise be closed.

A second major disagreement concerns **burden-sharing**:
- labour side wants clear commitments from management, owners, and the company in return for employee concessions;
- capital and management sides want any employee protections to remain compatible with a materially stronger economic result.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several central trade-offs remain unresolved:
- **economic credibility vs. site/job protection**
- **durable savings vs. preservation of the current footprint**
- **voluntary/gradual adjustment vs. deeper structural measures**
- **industrial peace vs. faster cost correction**
- **shared sacrifice vs. unequal burden allocation**

The discussion still lacks:
- a quantified savings gap,
- a quantified breakdown of what each side could contribute,
- and a comparison of whether non-closure alternatives can close the gap at sufficient scale and durability.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Next round should focus on **numbers and testable options**, not general positions:
- What is the exact savings target?
- What capacity reduction is required?
- What can be achieved through working-time, productivity, voluntary exits, and labour-cost measures?
- What cannot be achieved without deeper structural action?
- What binding commitments would each side need to accept a package?

The most useful next step would be a side-by-side comparison of:
1. a **no-closure alternative package**, and  
2. a **deeper restructuring package**,  
showing expected savings, timing, enforceability, and risks.
