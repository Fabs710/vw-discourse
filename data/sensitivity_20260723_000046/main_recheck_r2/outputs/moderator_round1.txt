### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that **the current status quo is not viable** and that **some form of restructuring is necessary**.
- There is broad agreement that **the workforce has already borne significant burden** and that **non-binding promises are insufficient**.
- Most participants agree the solution must include **real, durable cost and capacity changes**, not merely symbolic adjustments.
- There is also general openness to **negotiated alternatives** such as working-time changes, productivity measures, capacity adjustments, and employee contributions, provided the package is credible.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works Council, union, and regional state government reject them.  
  - Management will not rule them out.  
  - Family holding and investors say closures are possible only if truly necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state government reject them.  
  - Management does not exclude them.  
  - Family holding and investors are not endorsing them, but are not ruling out deeper structural measures if needed.
- **Scope of employee concessions:**  
  - Labour side wants concessions only in exchange for binding guarantees.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors want meaningful labour-cost and flexibility contributions.
- **Guarantees versus flexibility:**  
  - Labour and state side want binding site and job security.  
  - Management argues it cannot give blanket guarantees that block restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required**, and what combination of measures would meet that target.
- **What counts as an acceptable alternative to closures** if German sites are to remain open.
- **How to balance job security with competitiveness** over the medium term.
- **What management, owners, and capital will contribute** in parallel with employee concessions.
- **How commitments would be made enforceable**, especially on investment, products, and site futures.
- **Whether capacity reduction can be achieved without site elimination**, and if so, how quickly and at what scale.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should narrow from positions to **concrete package design**:
- define the **required savings and capacity reduction target**;
- specify **which measures can substitute for closures and layoffs**;
- identify **what binding guarantees** could be offered in return for employee contributions;
- and clarify **which trade-offs each side is prepared to accept** if a no-closure, no-forced-redundancy solution is to remain viable.
