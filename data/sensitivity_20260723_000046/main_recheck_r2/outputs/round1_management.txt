I will be equally clear: **the status quo is not an option**.

I recognize two points from both of you that are valid:

- The workforce has already carried a heavy burden through years of transformation.
- Vague promises and non-binding statements will not restore trust or deliver a settlement.

But I must challenge the core of your position.

### On plant closures and compulsory redundancies
You are asking me to rule out the very tools that may be necessary to fix a cost base that is structurally too high. I cannot do that. If the company is to remain competitive through 2030, we need **durable, structural savings**, not cosmetic adjustments that preserve excess capacity and leave our German operations uncompetitive.

A deal that merely shifts costs around, shortens hours temporarily, or postpones hard choices is not enough. It would leave us still too expensive in a shrinking European market, while rivals continue to gain ground.

### On the need for a real employee contribution
I agree that the burden cannot fall on the workforce alone. But there must be a **material labour-cost contribution**. That means we need serious movement on:
- pay and variable compensation,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- and removing legacy protections that no longer fit the economic reality.

If we do not secure that, we will not reach the margin and cash-generation targets needed to fund future products and technology.

### On closures versus alternatives
I am open on the **mechanism**, but not on the **outcome**. If the employee side can bring forward an alternative package that delivers savings and capacity reduction at the scale we need, then I will consider it. But it must be:
- **credible**,
- **durable**,
- **enforceable**,
- and large enough to restore competitiveness.

That means not just “capacity adjustments” in name, but real overcapacity reduction. If that cannot be achieved without closing a site, then we have to face that fact.

### On guarantees
I will not give blanket site guarantees through 2030 if they prevent the company from taking necessary restructuring steps. That would be irresponsible. What I can discuss is a package tied to:
- verifiable savings,
- investment commitments,
- and a path to sustainable profitability.

### Bottom line
We need a settlement that restores the brand’s economics, not one that protects every existing structure regardless of cost. I am prepared to negotiate the form, but **not the necessity of deep structural change**. If the alternative you propose does not close the cost gap in a durable way, then it is not a serious solution.
