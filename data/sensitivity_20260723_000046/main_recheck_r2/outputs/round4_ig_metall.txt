I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state government, and even management on one central point: **the current model is not sustainable**. We need a real restructuring, not slogans or delay. I also agree that **non-binding promises are worthless** and that any package must be durable and enforceable.

I also agree that the workforce cannot be the only side contributing. We have already shown openness to that by putting forward a counter-proposal with **labour-cost and capacity measures**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated jump from “the company needs restructuring” to “therefore German plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available.”

That is still not proven. It is being asserted, not demonstrated.

You keep saying “structural” as if that alone settles the question. It does not. The relevant question is whether the savings gap can be closed through a **negotiated, shared-sacrifice package** that avoids closures and forced layoffs. So far, nobody has shown that this cannot be done.

## To management
You want us to accept that closures and redundancies are on the table because they may be needed. Fine — then show the numbers.

I want a side-by-side comparison of:
1. a **no-closure package**, and  
2. a **closure/redundancy package**,

with:
- exact savings target,
- exact capacity reduction,
- exact burden-sharing from labour, management, and owners,
- timing,
- enforceability,
- and execution risk.

Until that is on the table, closure remains a threat tactic, not an economic conclusion.

And let me be clear: if management keeps using closure and compulsory redundancy as leverage, it will get **industrial conflict**, not cooperation. We still have the strike weapon, and members expect us to use it if necessary.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines:
- **no German plant closures**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

I support that position.

But I want to sharpen the practical point: if we ask the workforce for contributions, they must be matched by **binding guarantees** and real contributions from management and owners. Not just words about “competitiveness.”

That means:
- binding site security,
- no operational layoffs,
- investment commitments,
- and measurable employer-side contributions.

I can support labour restraint if it is **temporary, limited, and fair**. I cannot support employees paying while management and owners protect themselves from any real sacrifice.

## To the regional state government
Your stance is right in principle. But principle alone will not hold this line. If you really want to defend the industrial base, then use your influence to block any restructuring that shifts the adjustment burden entirely onto labour.

I would expect you to condition support on:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- investment commitments for future products,
- and a restructuring model that preserves the company’s long-term industrial capacity.

## To the family holding
You speak the language of stewardship and long-term value. Good. Then act like a steward.

A family owner does not defend long-term value by making closure a default bargaining tool. Long-term value depends on:
- labour peace,
- trust,
- skilled workers,
- and a company that still has an industrial future in Germany.

If you want that future, back a settlement that preserves sites and jobs while demanding shared sacrifice from all sides — including owners.

## To investors
You are right to demand measurable savings and durability. I agree with that. But I do **not** accept the idea that the only way to get execution certainty is to keep closures and redundancies available as a club over the workforce.

That may look neat in a spreadsheet. In reality, it risks:
- strikes,
- legal battles,
- political backlash,
- damaged industrial relations,
- and a poisoned implementation climate.

That is not lower risk. That is different risk.

A negotiated no-closure package with binding commitments can be more durable than a closure-first strategy that tears the company apart.

## What would actually move us
Only a package that meets all of these conditions:

- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job guarantees**
- **temporary, measurable labour contributions**
- **visible management and owner contributions**
- **investment commitments for German future products**
- **enforceable implementation rules**

If the company can close the gap within that framework, we are ready to negotiate the details immediately.

If it cannot, then the problem is not our red lines. The problem is that management’s restructuring plan is not good enough.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking **one-sided restructuring**.

Workers will contribute, but they will not be made to pay alone through job destruction and wage erosion while management and owners hide behind “necessity.”

So my position remains unchanged:
- protect jobs,
- protect the collective wage framework,
- reject forced layoffs and plant closures,
- and demand a durable, shared-sacrifice settlement.

If the other sides want a deal, bring one that respects those conditions. If not, expect resistance.
