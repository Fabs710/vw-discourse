### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between:

1. **Labour / state / works council**: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of co-determination or job security; any restructuring must be proven workable within those limits.

2. **Management / investors**: restructuring must be large, durable, and fast enough to restore competitiveness; they will not accept a compromise that preserves excess capacity or leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

3. **Missing common evidence base**: all sides are demanding arithmetic, but no agreed plant-by-plant model has yet been tabled that compares:
   - no-closure savings,
   - closure/downsizing savings,
   - one-time restructuring costs,
   - timing,
   - and execution risk.

So the impasse is not just about principles; it is about **whether the required savings gap can be closed without capacity removal**, and nobody is yet operating from a shared verified dataset.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure trial with hard triggers
A compromise package could be structured as:
- **No German plant closures for a defined period**,
- **No compulsory redundancies during that period**,
- **Binding labour, management, and owner contributions**,
- **Pre-agreed trigger points**: if savings, utilization, or margin milestones are not met by specific dates, then a jointly reviewed second-phase restructuring is automatically opened.

This would give labour and the state a real no-closure commitment while giving management and investors a performance-based exit ramp if the package fails.

---

#### Proposal 2: Site-protection plus capacity reconfiguration
A second bridge position could be:
- **No immediate plant closures**,
- but **major capacity consolidation** through:
  - shift reductions,
  - model reallocation,
  - downtime,
  - product transfers,
  - and targeted repurposing of weaker sites,
- combined with **voluntary exits and natural attrition only**,
- plus a binding review of site viability after a fixed period.

This keeps the symbolic red line on closures intact initially, while still allowing substantial structural savings if the footprint can be redesigned enough to lower fixed costs.

---

#### Proposal 3: Enforced burden-sharing package with governance guarantees
A third compromise avenue is:
- **temporary labour-cost contributions**,
- **visible management pay restraint**,
- **owner-side capital or investment support**,
- **binding German investment commitments**,
- and **legally enforceable co-determination safeguards**,
all tied to a quantified savings target.

This would make the adjustment appear genuinely shared rather than worker-only, which may be the minimum condition for any labour-side acceptance of deeper concessions.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Management
What exact savings gap remains after all non-closure measures, and what plant-by-plant evidence shows that this gap cannot be closed by working-time, product allocation, productivity, procurement, and overhead actions alone?

#### To the Works Council
If closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded, what quantified combination of measures do you believe can still close the full structural gap, and by when?

#### To the Industrial Union
What level of temporary labour contribution is acceptable to you, and what enforceable management/owner counterpart would make that contribution politically and industrially defensible?

#### To the Regional State Government
Are you prepared to support a binding restructuring package that avoids closures only if it includes measurable performance triggers and a follow-up mechanism if targets are missed?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you commit to specific owner-side contributions or investment guarantees that match the workforce’s concessions, and if so, in what form and at what scale?

#### To Institutional Investors
What minimum level of durable cost and capacity reduction would you regard as sufficient, even if achieved without closures, and what evidence would convince you that the no-closure route is credible?

#### To the Expert
Can you provide a side-by-side scenario table showing the minimum annual savings, one-time costs, and payback periods for:
- a no-closure package,
- a partial-downsizing package,
- and a closure scenario?
