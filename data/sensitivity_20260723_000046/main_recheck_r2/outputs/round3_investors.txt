We agree on the end state: **this company must get back to competitive margins, and the current setup is not investable**. But we are not here to subsidize a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base and calls it a restructuring.

## Where we agree
- **Management** is right that the problem is structural, not just cyclical.
- **The union, works council, and state** are right that prolonged conflict destroys value.
- **The family holding** is right that cosmetic compromises are worthless.
- Everyone is right that **non-binding promises are not enough**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” is not acceptable if it leaves the cost base uncompetitive
The labour side keeps repeating that closures are unnecessary, but we still do not see a quantified alternative that clearly closes the gap. A binding promise to preserve every German site through 2030 is only acceptable if the economics still work. If it locks in excess capacity and structurally high costs, then it is not a solution — it is deferred damage to shareholders.

We will not support an outcome that:
- leaves margins structurally impaired,
- preserves overcapacity,
- or turns the restructuring into a value-destroying status quo with better branding.

### 2) Management has not yet made the arithmetic sufficiently clear
Management is right to insist on real structural change, but “necessary” is not the same as “proven.” If closures and redundancies are being used as leverage, then management needs to show:
- the exact savings gap,
- the capacity reduction required,
- what can be achieved through working-time, productivity, voluntary exits, and labour-cost changes,
- and what cannot.

Right now, we have assertions, not a fully transparent case.

### 3) We do not support prolonged industrial conflict
A strike war is not a shareholder solution. It destroys earnings, market confidence, and execution certainty. So we are not siding with a closure-first approach if it triggers prolonged disruption.

That said, avoiding conflict by accepting a weak deal is also unacceptable.

## Our position
We will support **the option that credibly restores competitiveness, cash generation, and margins**.

That means we can support:
- real and durable cost reduction,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- labour-cost contributions,
- productivity and flexibility gains,
- and a settlement that is enforceable.

We cannot support:
- a cosmetic no-closure compromise,
- a deal that protects all legacy structures while leaving the company uncompetitive,
- or a prolonged stalemate that erodes value.

## What would move us
A package would need to show, in numbers:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the exact capacity reduction,
3. what employee concessions deliver,
4. what management and owners contribute,
5. and how the result gets the brand back to target margins on a durable basis.

If a no-closure package can truly match the economics, that is preferable because it likely reduces execution risk. But if it cannot, then we will not back a politically comfortable arrangement that leaves the company weak.

## Bottom line
We are not defending jobs at any price, and we are not endorsing closures at any price.

We are defending **shareholder value**. That means backing the restructuring path that is **economically sufficient, durable, and enforceable**. If the current proposals do not do that, we will oppose them and reallocate capital elsewhere.
