I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company needs a restructuring that is real, durable, and credible. I also agree with the works council and union that the workforce has already made major sacrifices, and that **non-binding promises are worthless**.

I also agree that the company cannot keep carrying an uncompetitive cost base indefinitely. If we do nothing, we risk a slow erosion of competitiveness, investment, and ultimately jobs.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that the necessary adjustment must be translated into **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies**.

That is not a neutral technical detail. It would damage:
- the regional industrial base,
- supplier networks,
- municipal finances,
- trust in the company’s social partnership model,
- and the company’s legitimacy as a regional anchor.

For us as a state shareholder and political actor, that is not an acceptable starting point. It would also be strategically short-sighted. A carmaker cannot secure its future by undermining the very workforce and regional ecosystem that support long-term production capacity.

## To management
You keep saying closures are only “on the table” if alternatives fail. But you still have not shown that alternatives have actually been exhausted.

So my position is:
- **No closure-driven restructuring as the default.**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred instrument.**
- **Yes to serious alternatives that deliver real savings without gutting the German footprint.**

If management wants our support, it must come with a transparent package showing:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the exact capacity reduction needed,
3. what portion can be achieved through working-time, productivity, product mix, and voluntary instruments,
4. and what management and owners are contributing themselves.

Without that, “necessary” remains an assertion, not a case.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your core red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies.

That is not just a political slogan for us; it is a serious boundary. I also support your insistence that any employee contribution must be matched by real commitments from management and ownership.

At the same time, I want to be honest: protecting every site requires an economically credible alternative, not just a defensive no. If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative package must be strong enough to convince even skeptical parties that the savings are real.

So I would urge you to focus not only on rejection, but on **a structured alternative package**:
- capacity reduction through working-time and production allocation,
- natural attrition and voluntary exits,
- temporary and fair cost contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- investment and product commitments,
- and enforceable site guarantees.

That is the path I can defend politically and economically.

## To the Family Holding
You say you do not support closure for its own sake. Good. Then use your influence to prevent management from turning closures into a threat tactic.

Long-term enterprise value is not served by industrial conflict, reputational damage, or destroying trust with the workforce. If the family wants a stable, valuable company, it should back a negotiated solution that preserves skilled jobs and the company’s regional anchor role.

## To Investors
I hear your demand for measurable and durable savings. That is legitimate. But I reject the idea that the only credible route is a settlement that threatens plant closures as leverage over the workforce.

A deal that preserves industrial peace, protects German sites, and still delivers structural savings can create more long-term value than a “clean” but conflict-ridden closure strategy. Value destruction through strikes, litigation, and political backlash is not an acceptable shortcut.

## My line
Our position is clear:

- **No German plant closures as a first resort.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring with real savings, capacity adjustment, and shared burden.**
- **Yes to employee contributions if management, owners, and the company also contribute.**
- **Yes to binding investment and product commitments for German sites.**
- **No decision that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.**

## What would move us
We could support a restructuring if it is built around:
- verifiable savings,
- socially responsible capacity reduction,
- voluntary and phased adjustment,
- binding site and job security,
- and a credible future industrial plan for Germany.

What we cannot support is a plan that treats German plants and jobs as the default adjustment variable.

So the next step is not more generic pressure. The next step is a concrete alternative package that closes the cost gap **without** destroying the company’s regional base. If management has such a package, put it on the table. If not, then closures remain unacceptable to us.
