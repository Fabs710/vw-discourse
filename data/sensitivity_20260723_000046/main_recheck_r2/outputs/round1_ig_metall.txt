We are aligned on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. Management cannot demand that the workforce pay for strategic misjudgments by sacrificing jobs, sites, and the collective bargaining framework.

That said, I want to be equally clear about what a viable alternative must contain from the union side:

- **A real shared-sacrifice package**, not one-sided cuts.
- **Binding site and job security**, not vague promises.
- **Capacity reduction and flexibility measures** where needed, but negotiated and socially responsible.
- **Employee contributions only if management, owners, and the company also contribute materially.**
- **Commitments on future products and investment** so that any restructuring preserves an industrial future in Germany.

Where I agree with you:
- The workforce has already absorbed major change and cannot be expected to bear the whole burden.
- Trust has been damaged.
- Non-binding assurances are worthless.

Where I would sharpen the point:
- If management is still using closures and forced layoffs as the baseline, then it is not negotiating in good faith.
- “Competitiveness” cannot mean permanently lowering wages and weakening protections while leaving ownership and management untouched.
- Any deal must preserve the collective wage framework and avoid setting a precedent that restructuring automatically means mass job destruction.

So our position is not simply defensive. We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time solutions,
- temporary or partial cost contributions,
- capacity adjustments,
- flexibility in bonus or variable pay elements,
- and other negotiated measures.

But this only works if there is a **credible, enforceable commitment to keep every German site open and avoid compulsory redundancies**. Without that, there is no acceptable basis for a settlement, and the union will use its leverage accordingly.
