I’ll respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the status quo is not viable**. We cannot preserve the current cost base, current capacity, and current labour-cost structure and still expect this brand to be competitive in a shrinking European market.

I also agree that:
- non-binding promises are worthless,
- the burden cannot fall on employees alone,
- and any settlement must be durable and enforceable.

So far, that is the common ground.

## Where I disagree — and strongly
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to convert “we need restructuring” into “therefore closures and compulsory redundancies are unnecessary and must be ruled out.”

That is not an argument. It is a red line being asserted as if it were an economic fact.

You keep demanding proof that closures or redundancies are necessary. Fair. Then let’s be precise: I have not seen a credible alternative package from labour or the state that closes the gap **at the required scale, with the required durability, and with the required timing**.

That is the core issue.

If the answer is a package that preserves every German site, preserves most of the current footprint, and relies mainly on temporary restraint and goodwill, then that does **not** solve the problem. It delays it.

## To the Works Council
You say management has not proven necessity. I say the burden is not on us to prove plant closures are desirable. The burden is on all of us to deliver a package that restores competitiveness.

Right now, your position is:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of the job-security framework,
- binding site guarantees through 2030.

That is a political position. It is not yet a viable restructuring plan.

A site guarantee is only useful if the company remains competitive. If it locks in an uncompetitive footprint, it becomes a guarantee of slow decline.

You ask what portion of the gap can be covered by working time, product allocation, productivity, procurement, and overhead. Good question. But you have not produced numbers that show those measures can close the gap. Until you do, “no closures” is not a solution; it is a constraint.

## To the Industrial Union
You are right about one thing: a negotiated package is preferable to open conflict. But I reject the suggestion that industrial action is somehow a substitute for economic logic. Strike threats do not change the underlying margin problem.

You also say closures are being used as leverage. Yes — because we face a real structural gap. Leverage is part of bargaining. The question is whether we can reach a deal before the economics force harder measures.

If you want to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then bring a package that is **arithmetically credible**:
- exact savings target,
- exact capacity reduction,
- exact labour contribution,
- exact management and owner contribution,
- exact timing,
- and enforceability.

If that package can genuinely restore competitiveness, I will take it seriously. If not, then the union is asking the company to accept a weaker future in order to protect the current footprint.

I will not do that.

## To the Regional State Government
I respect your role and your concern for the regional industrial base. But I need to be blunt: regional protection is not the same as industrial competitiveness.

A state-backed insistence on no closures does not make the business stronger. It may simply make adjustment slower and more painful later.

If you want to defend the industrial base, then support a package that actually restores profitability and funds future investment. That means being open to deeper structural measures if the alternative does not close the gap.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You say you want stewardship and long-term value. Good. Then be clear: long-term value is not preserved by protecting every legacy site if the brand remains structurally too expensive.

I am not asking for closures for their own sake. I am asking for a restructuring that is sufficient.

If your position is that every German site must remain open regardless of the arithmetic, then you are not stewarding value — you are deferring the problem.

If, however, you are prepared to back a package that is economically sufficient, then we have common ground. But sufficiency matters more than symbolism.

## To Institutional Investors
You are right to insist on measurable savings, enforceability, and margins that return to target. That is exactly my position too.

Where I differ from the labour side is this: a “no-closure” package is not automatically lower risk. It may reduce visible conflict in the short term, but if it leaves excess capacity and an uncompetitive cost base, it creates bigger risk later.

So yes, I want durability. But durability means:
- structural savings,
- capacity reduction,
- labour-cost contribution,
- and margin recovery.

Not just industrial peace.

## The real test
Here is the test no one has answered yet:

**Can a no-closure package, with binding guarantees, deliver the same scale of durable cost and capacity reduction as a package that retains the option of closures and compulsory redundancies?**

If the answer is yes, show it in numbers.
If the answer is no, then the refusal to consider closures is not principled discipline — it is an obstacle to restoring competitiveness.

## What I am prepared to accept
I am open on the mechanism. I am **not** open on the outcome.

That means I am prepared to discuss:
- working-time changes,
- shift and roster flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- productivity measures,
- product allocation changes,
- and labour-cost contributions.

But those must be **large enough and durable enough** to restore the economics. Cosmetic burden-sharing is not enough.

## My limit
I will not support:
- a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- a deal that preserves excess capacity,
- a deal that freezes the current footprint and calls that restructuring,
- or a deal that asks management to pretend the gap will disappear by itself.

We are not negotiating whether restructuring is needed. We are negotiating whether this company will have the courage to do what is economically necessary.

I am willing to reach a deal without closures **if** it is truly sufficient.
