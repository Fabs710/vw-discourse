Here is a neutral facilitator summary of the impasse, followed by bridge options and one pointed question to each stakeholder.

## Core impasse
The dispute has converged on one central fault line:

- **Management, family holding, and institutional investors** require a restructuring that is **economically sufficient, durable, and enforceable**, and they are not excluding closures or redundancies if those are needed to close the gap.
- **Works council, union, and regional state government** reject German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as a matter of principle and demand a **quantified no-closure alternative** with binding guarantees.

So the plateau is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about:

1. **Whether closures / compulsory redundancies are genuinely necessary**, or only preferred leverage.
2. **Whether a no-closure package can be made arithmetically credible** at the required scale.
3. **Whether binding guarantees can coexist with enough flexibility to restore competitiveness**.

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: Quantified two-track package with decision gate
A structured compromise could be:

- Management publishes a **fully quantified gap analysis**:
  - exact savings target,
  - exact capacity reduction needed,
  - exact contribution expected from labour,
  - exact contributions from management and owners,
  - and a transparent comparison of closure vs no-closure scenarios.
- The parties agree on a **time-limited negotiation window**.
- During that period, the sides implement an **interim no-layoff, no-closure freeze**.
- If the no-closure package meets agreed numeric thresholds, it becomes the preferred path.
- If it does not, the parties pre-agree to a **fallback menu** of structural measures, still subject to co-determination and social safeguards.

This bridges those demanding proof with those demanding protection.

### Proposal 2: Site-security core plus flexible volume adjustment
A second path could separate **site existence** from **production volume**:

- Binding commitments that German plants remain open through 2030.
- Capacity reduction achieved through:
  - shift changes,
  - temporary working-time reductions,
  - product reallocation,
  - planned downtime,
  - voluntary exits,
  - natural attrition,
  - and productivity measures.
- In exchange, labour accepts a **temporary, measurable cost contribution** and more operational flexibility.
- Management and owners commit to **investment funding** for German products and technology.

This directly addresses the no-closure red lines while giving management room to reduce cost and capacity.

### Proposal 3: Shared-sacrifice package with enforcement and review
A third compromise would be a broader burden-sharing deal:

- Employees contribute via **temporary wage restraint, bonus restraint, and working-time flexibility**.
- Management contributes via **overhead cuts, compensation restraint, and governance changes**.
- Owners/investors contribute via **capital support, lower short-term extraction, or reinvestment commitments**.
- All commitments are made **legally binding** with:
  - milestones,
  - reporting,
  - joint oversight,
  - and a review clause at defined intervals.
- If milestones are missed, the parties trigger an agreed **reopener mechanism** rather than an immediate unilateral escalation.

This gives all sides something tangible while reducing the risk of a trust-based agreement failing.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
Can you present, in a side-by-side format, the exact savings gap and the minimum capacity reduction required under:
1. a no-closure scenario, and
2. a closure / redundancy scenario?

### To Works Council
What specific quantified concessions are you prepared to accept if, and only if, the company provides binding German site guarantees through 2030?

### To Industrial Union
What is the maximum temporary labour-cost and flexibility contribution you would recommend to members if it is paired with enforceable job, site, and investment guarantees?

### To Regional State Government
What concrete leverage will you use to ensure that any public or political support is conditioned on binding employment, investment, and site-security commitments?

### To Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a deal that preserves all German sites if the numbers show competitiveness can be restored, and what measurable owner-side contribution are you prepared to make to reach that outcome?

### To Institutional Investors
What specific financial thresholds would make a no-closure package acceptable to you, and what evidence would you require that execution risk is truly lower than a closure-based plan?
