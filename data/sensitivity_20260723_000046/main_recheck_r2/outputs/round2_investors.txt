We agree on the **objective**: the company must restore competitive margins, and the status quo is not investable. What matters to us is not who “wins” the labour conflict, but whether the final package produces a **credible, durable, and enforceable** return to target economics.

## Where we agree
- Management is right that this is **not a cyclical problem only**; the cost base and capacity issue look structural.
- The works council, union, and state are right that **prolonged industrial conflict destroys value**.
- The family holding is right that a **cosmetic compromise is worthless**.
- Everyone is right that **non-binding promises are not enough**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) No-closure / no-redundancy red lines
I understand the political and social logic, but from a capital markets perspective those red lines are acceptable **only if they still deliver the economics**.

If “no closures” means the company keeps excess capacity and remains above competitors on cost, then that is not a settlement we can support. It would simply push the adjustment burden onto shareholders through weaker margins, lower cash generation, and a damaged valuation.

So:  
- **We do not support a deal that protects every site and job while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.**
- We also do **not** support a closure-first approach if it triggers prolonged disruption and destroys execution certainty.

### 2) Management’s current framing
Management is right to insist on structural change, but it has not yet shown enough arithmetic to justify closures as the default outcome. “Necessary” is not enough; we need:
- the **savings gap**,
- the **capacity reduction required**,
- the **cost per measure**,
- and the **timing** of savings.

If alternatives can close the gap with better execution certainty than closures, that is preferable. If they cannot, then deeper structural measures become unavoidable. But the burden is on management to quantify this clearly.

### 3) Labour-side guarantees
We reject blanket guarantees that make restructuring impossible. A binding 2030 site guarantee across all German plants is only acceptable if the alternative package delivers a **real, durable margin recovery**. Otherwise it is just a promise to preserve underperformance.

## What we need to see in a credible package
To support the outcome, we need all of the following:

1. **Material cost reduction**
   - not marginal,
   - not symbolic,
   - and not reliant on assumptions that may never arrive.

2. **Durability**
   - savings must survive beyond a short concession window.

3. **Execution certainty**
   - a deal that is implementable is better than an endless stand-off.

4. **Clear path back to return-on-sales target**
   - management must be able to explain how margins recover and stay recovered.

## Our stance on the current options

- **A cosmetic no-closure compromise:** unacceptable.
- **A prolonged strike war that erodes earnings and market confidence:** also unacceptable.
- **A negotiated package with real capacity reduction, labour-cost contributions, and enforceable savings:** supportable.
- **Plant closures / redundancies only if genuinely necessary and clearly superior in economic terms:** possible, but not the preferred first answer.

## What would move us
We would be prepared to back a settlement if it includes:
- specific and measurable savings,
- credible capacity reduction,
- labour-cost contributions,
- productivity and flexibility measures,
- and a believable route back to competitive margins.

If the employee side can produce a no-closure package that truly matches the economics, we would prefer that to a closure strategy because it likely preserves execution and reduces value destruction. But if the math does not work, then we will not support a politically comfortable deal that leaves the company structurally weak.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend jobs at any price, and we are not here to endorse closures at any price.

We are here to protect shareholder value. That means backing **the option that credibly restores competitiveness, cash generation, and margins**. If the final package does that, we can support it. If it doesn’t, we will oppose it and let capital move elsewhere.
