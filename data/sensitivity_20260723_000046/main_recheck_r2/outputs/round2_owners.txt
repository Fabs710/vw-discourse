I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the following:
- The status quo is not viable.
- The company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one.
- The workforce has already made real sacrifices.
- Non-binding assurances will not solve this.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive.

I also agree that any settlement must be **credible, enforceable, and economically real**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** agree with the increasingly absolute framing from the labour side and the state side that **all plant closures and all compulsory redundancies must be ruled out in advance**.

That position may be understandable politically, but it is not yet a convincing economic answer. If the company truly has structural overcapacity and an uncompetitive cost base, then a serious restructuring must be able to deliver **durable** savings at scale. If your red lines make that impossible, then you are not solving the problem — you are just relocating it into the future.

## To the Works Council and Union
You both say management has not proven why closures are necessary. Fair enough — but I would turn that around: **you have not yet shown that a no-closure, no-forced-redundancy package can close the gap at the required scale and durability.**

That is the key point.

I am open to:
- working-time changes,
- capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary or partial employee contributions,
- and other negotiated measures.

But I am **not** open to a package that merely softens the blow while leaving the company structurally too expensive. If the alternative can truly match the economics of deeper restructuring, then fine — bring the numbers. If not, then ruling out closures and redundancies beforehand is not responsible stewardship of the enterprise.

## To Management
I support the objective of restoring competitiveness and margins. That is necessary.

But I also want to be clear: you should not treat plant closures as the default answer unless you have genuinely tested alternatives. Closure-driven restructuring creates major risks:
- prolonged conflict,
- reputational damage,
- political backlash,
- execution delay,
- and potentially permanent value loss.

So I expect management to come back with a **quantified, staged package** that shows:
- the exact savings gap,
- what can be achieved without closures,
- what cannot,
- and why.

That is the burden management must meet if it wants governance support for deeper measures.

## To the Regional State Government
I understand your concern about the regional industrial base, and I share the desire to avoid reckless destruction of capacity. But a hard political no to any closure, regardless of economics, is not a sustainable governance answer either.

If the company’s competitiveness erodes, the region loses investment, jobs, and influence over time. So I would urge you to focus less on blanket veto language and more on enabling a package that actually restores the company’s economics while preserving as much of the German footprint as possible.

## My position as controlling shareholder
The family holding’s priority is the **long-term value of the enterprise**. That means:
- restoring margins,
- protecting the brand’s competitiveness,
- preserving labour peace if possible,
- and avoiding destabilising conflict.

So I will support a negotiated restructuring **if** it is economically credible and durable.

I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise that preserves overcapacity,
- a politically comfortable but financially inadequate settlement,
- or a framework that permanently blocks the company from taking necessary structural action.

## What would move me
What could move me is a package that is arithmetically convincing and enforceable:
- specific capacity reduction,
- measurable labour-cost contribution,
- productivity gains,
- voluntary and phased workforce adjustment,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a clear path back to target margins.

If that package can be shown to close the gap without closures, then that is preferable.

If it cannot, then the company cannot be forced to choose between competitiveness and fiction.

## Bottom line
I am not here to endorse closure for its own sake.

But I am also not prepared to endorse a blanket no-closure, no-redundancy stance that may leave the company uncompetitive and erode value for everyone.

Bring me a package that is:
- credible,
- durable,
- enforceable,
- and sufficient to restore competitiveness.

If you cannot, then deeper structural measures remain on the table.
