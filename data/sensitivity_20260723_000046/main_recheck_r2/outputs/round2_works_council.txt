Management keeps talking about “necessity” and “durable savings,” but it still has not answered the central question: **why must the adjustment take the form of German plant closures and compulsory redundancies?** You have repeated the conclusion without proving it.

Let me be equally clear in round 2:

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the current situation is not sustainable,
- the company needs a credible restructuring,
- the solution must be enforceable and not cosmetic,
- and the workforce cannot be the only party contributing.

I also agree that we need measurable savings and a real industrial future. We are not defending stagnation. We are defending **viable restructuring without destroying sites and jobs**.

## Where I disagree
I reject the logic that because the cost base is high, the only serious answer is closure. That is too easy, and it is not acceptable.

Management still owes us:
1. a transparent savings gap,
2. a clear breakdown of what is needed,
3. and proof that alternatives cannot deliver it.

Until that is done, closure talk is not a solution — it is pressure.

## Our non-negotiable line
I will restate it because there is no ambiguity:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the job-security framework.**

That is not a rhetorical position. That is the boundary of any settlement.

## What a serious alternative looks like
If management and owners actually want a deal, then put a real package on the table:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants through 2030,
- no operational layoffs,
- capacity reductions through working-time, shift, and production allocation measures,
- natural attrition, retirement, and voluntary instruments only,
- investment commitments for German products and technologies,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping implementation.

If you want employee contributions, then we need:
- a fair burden-sharing arrangement,
- management and shareholder contributions too,
- and legally enforceable guarantees in return.

## To the family holding
You say you do not want closure for its own sake. Fine. Then do not let management use closures as a threat to force the workforce into surrender. The family holding has influence here. Use it to insist on a negotiated solution that protects the company **and** its industrial base.

## To the regional state government
You are right to oppose closures and forced redundancies. But “socially responsible reduction” cannot become a back door to dismantling sites. If the state is serious, it should support a package that protects every German plant and conditions any public support or political backing on that guarantee.

## To the investors
You want durability and execution certainty. So do we. But value is not created by provoking industrial conflict and breaking the company’s social settlement. A closure-driven approach may look neat on a spreadsheet and still destroy value through strikes, litigation, political backlash, and lasting trust loss.

## Bottom line
Management has not yet shown that a no-closure, no-forced-redundancy solution cannot work. Until it does, that remains the only acceptable path from our side.

If you want a credible restructuring, bring forward:
- the exact savings requirement,
- the exact alternative measures,
- and the binding guarantees that make employee contributions possible.

Otherwise, we will continue to resist this plan with all the tools at our disposal.
