I appreciate the clarity from all sides, and I will be equally direct.

Let me start with what I **agree** on:

- The company needs a credible restructuring.
- The workforce has already made major contributions and cannot be treated as the sole shock absorber.
- Non-binding assurances will not solve this.
- The current cost base and capacity situation cannot simply be ignored.

Where I **disagree sharply** is on the idea that the necessary adjustment must take the form of **plant closures in Germany** and **compulsory redundancies**.

That is not an acceptable starting point for us as a regional shareholder and political actor. It would damage not only jobs, but the industrial base of the region, supplier networks, municipal finances, and the company’s own long-term social legitimacy. A carmaker of this scale cannot strengthen its future by destroying the trust and stability that have made it a regional anchor.

To management: I hear the argument about structural savings. But “structural” does not automatically mean “closure.” Before any plant is put on the block, I expect a serious examination of alternatives that achieve real savings **without** crossing those red lines. That includes:

- capacity reductions through internal redistribution rather than site elimination,
- working-time adjustments,
- phased workforce adaptation through natural attrition and retirement,
- negotiated cost contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- and a genuine investment plan for German sites and products.

If the proposal is simply “accept closures or else,” then from our side that is not a viable negotiation; it is an attempt to impose a unilateral outcome. And given the governance structure here, that is not how this company can or should be governed.

To the works council and union: your red lines on no closures and no compulsory redundancies are understandable, and I share the concern. But a viable settlement also has to be economically real. We cannot defend every structure in place if that means the company continues to lose competitiveness and then comes back in two years with an even harder ultimatum. So I would urge you to be prepared for **socially responsible capacity reduction** and **measurable employee contributions**—but only in a package that includes:
- binding site security,
- no forced layoffs,
- investment commitments,
- and a co-determined restructuring path.

So my position is this:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that reduces costs and capacity in a socially responsible way.**
4. **Yes to employee contributions, but only if management and owners also contribute and if the result preserves the German industrial footprint.**
5. **No decision that sidelines the state’s role or undermines the co-determination balance.**

If management believes there is truly no alternative to closures, then it will need to demonstrate that exhaustively and convincingly. At this stage, I do not see that burden as having been met.

So I am not here to bless deep cuts for their own sake. I am here to protect the company’s future **and** its regional anchor role. That means restructuring, yes — but not a closure-driven solution that gut-punches the workforce and the region.
