As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and management cannot now solve its strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and hollowing out the company’s industrial base. A restructuring that is built on closure threats and forced layoffs is not a viable path forward for us.

What we are prepared to discuss is a credible alternative that protects every German site and secures jobs through 2030. That means:

- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- no operational layoffs,
- a negotiated reduction in costs through socially responsible measures,
- capacity adjustments and working-time solutions instead of closures,
- real commitments on future products, investment, and technology at the German sites,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the restructuring.

If management wants contributions from employees, then it must also make binding commitments in return. We are not interested in non-binding promises, vague “socially responsible” language, or a fait accompli presented under pressure. We have seen where that leads, and trust is already damaged.

So my position is simple: restructure, yes — but without plant closures, without forced layoffs, and without sacrificing the long-term security of the German workforce. If those conditions are not met, we will resist this plan with all available means.
