We appreciate the expert input, and we agree that the real test is arithmetic, not rhetoric.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert on the central point: **this business needs a durable step-down in cost and capacity**. A cosmetic settlement that leaves the German footprint overbuilt or the cost base structurally high is not acceptable to us.

We also agree with the works council, union, and state that **prolonged industrial conflict destroys value**. A strike war is not a shareholder solution. It burns cash, delays execution, and damages confidence.

And we agree that **non-binding promises are worthless**. If there is a deal, it has to be enforceable and measurable.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A blanket no-closure position is not enough
The labour side keeps saying closures are unnecessary, but we still do not see a quantified package that clearly closes the gap at the required scale and durability.

A promise to preserve every German site through 2030 is only valuable if the numbers still work. If it locks in excess capacity and a structurally uncompetitive cost base, then it is not a solution. It is deferred damage to shareholder value.

### 2) Management still has not shown the full arithmetic
Management is right that the problem is structural. But “structural” is not the same as “proven necessity” for closures or compulsory redundancies.

We want a side-by-side comparison with:
- the exact savings gap,
- the capacity reduction required,
- what working-time, productivity, voluntary exits, product allocation, procurement, and overhead measures can realistically deliver,
- and what remains uncovered.

Without that, closures remain a leverage tool, not a demonstrated conclusion.

### 3) We do not support a prolonged conflict to force a weak deal
If management presses too hard without numbers, it risks a destructive labor conflict. If labour refuses any structural adjustment, it risks an outcome that protects sites but erodes value. We reject both extremes.

## What would move us
We will support the path that **credibly restores margins, cash generation, and execution certainty**.

That means we can support:
- real and durable cost reduction,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- labour-cost and flexibility contributions,
- productivity and overhead savings,
- and a legally enforceable package.

But we will not support:
- a cosmetic no-closure compromise,
- a deal that preserves all legacy structures while leaving the company uncompetitive,
- or a prolonged stalemate that destroys earnings.

## Our position, plainly
A no-closure package is acceptable **only if** it is arithmetically credible and durable enough to restore competitiveness.

If it cannot do that, then the company must retain the ability to use deeper structural measures, including closures or major downsizing.

That is not ideological. It is capital discipline.

## Bottom line
We are not defending closures for their own sake. We are defending **shareholder value**.

If a no-closure package truly closes the gap, we can support it.
If it does not, then deeper structural action remains justified, because a protected but uncompetitive footprint is not a future we will fund.
