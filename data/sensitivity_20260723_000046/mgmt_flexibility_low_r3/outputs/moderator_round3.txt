## Core impasse

The discourse is stuck on one central issue:

**Whether the restructuring can be made durable without plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and if so, by what quantified combination of permanent capacity reduction, cost correction, and employee contribution.**

More specifically, the blockage is between two standards:

1. **Labor / state / works council standard:**  
   No closures and no forced redundancies unless management proves there is no viable alternative.

2. **Management / investors / controlling family standard:**  
   No-closure is only acceptable if it delivers a large, permanent correction with quantified savings, capacity reduction, and a credible 2030 margin path.

The expert has narrowed the dispute but not resolved it. The numbers now need to answer not just “is restructuring needed?” but **which restructuring package closes the gap with acceptable industrial and governance constraints**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) “No-closure first, closure trigger only if quantified gap remains”
A possible bridge would be to adopt a **staged sequencing model**:

- First test a fully quantified no-closure package with:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - shift reductions,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - overhead cuts,
  - product reallocation,
  - and a temporary but sizable employee contribution.
- Set explicit numeric thresholds for success:
  - annual savings,
  - fixed-cost reduction,
  - capacity release,
  - and 2030 margin effect.
- Only if the package fails those thresholds would a more intrusive option remain under review.

This does not settle the issue, but it creates a **decision ladder** rather than a binary standoff.

---

### 2) “Partial consolidation as the middle path”
A second compromise would be to treat **partial consolidation** as the default negotiating center:

- keep most German sites open,
- reduce or reassign one major site or sub-operation,
- avoid compulsory redundancies as far as legally and operationally possible,
- pair capacity reduction with guaranteed investment into the remaining network,
- and embed social protections, transfer options, and qualification commitments.

This is the clearest procedural bridge between:
- the labor side’s rejection of full closure logic, and
- the investor/management demand for durable fixed-cost relief.

---

### 3) “Temporary contribution with automatic sunset and reopener”
A third compromise would isolate the contentious employee contribution issue:

- employee concessions would be:
  - time-limited,
  - explicitly reversible,
  - numerically capped,
  - and tied to specific restructuring milestones;
- a review clause would reopen the package if the business case does not materialize;
- management and ownership would commit to matching contributions in governance, investment, or overhead reductions.

This addresses the fear that “temporary” becomes permanent wage rollback, while still giving management immediate relief.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact annual savings, fixed-cost reduction, and capacity removal are required for you to accept a no-closure package as sufficient, and what is the threshold below which you would insist on site-level correction?**

### To the Works Council
**What quantified permanent savings would you accept as proof that a no-closure solution is credible, even if it requires substantial shift cuts, redeployment, and time-limited employee contributions?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What is the maximum duration and depth of employee contribution you would accept, and what concrete management or ownership concessions must be in place for that contribution to be legitimate?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you support a partial-consolidation plan if it keeps every German plant operating in some form but reduces capacity at one site, and what binding investment or employment guarantees would you require?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Will you explicitly support a no-closure or partial-consolidation outcome if the model shows equivalent durability, even if it produces less immediate simplification than a closure scenario?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**What minimum 2030 margin uplift and fixed-cost absorption improvement would make you view a non-closure solution as financially credible, even if it is operationally more complex?**
