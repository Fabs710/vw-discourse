Below is a structured, model-based comparison of the three paths, staying strictly in the restructuring / plant-network domain.

## Key caveat
I do **not** have plant-by-plant internal data. So this is a **scenario model**, not a factual forecast. It uses the expert’s framework and reasonable German OEM cost assumptions.

---

# 1) Model assumptions

I assume a German passenger-car network with:
- several assembly / major component sites
- a structurally high fixed-cost base
- overcapacity relative to demand
- a brand-level EBIT margin gap that must be closed by 2030

### Working assumptions
- Required durable improvement at brand level: **€1.5bn–€2.5bn EBIT**
- Of that, German footprint correction needs to deliver roughly **€0.8bn–€1.4bn**
- Current overcapacity is meaningful enough that **temporary measures alone are insufficient**
- Savings are judged on a **durable annual basis**, not short-term cash relief

---

# 2) Scenario comparison

## A. No-closure / no-forced-redundancy

### Typical measures
- all German plants stay open
- headcount reduction via attrition, voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment
- shift cuts / working-time reductions
- productivity programs
- overhead simplification
- negotiated employee contribution

### Estimated durable annual savings
**€300m–€700m**

Breakdown:
- labor concessions: **€100m–€250m**
- shift / working-time reduction: **€50m–€150m**
- overhead simplification: **€75m–€150m**
- productivity / logistics / quality gains: **€75m–€150m**
- product allocation improvements: **€0m–€100m**

### One-time restructuring cost
**€150m–€500m**

Mostly:
- voluntary severance
- retraining
- redeployment
- process reconfiguration

### Employment impact
- **1,500–4,000** direct positions reduced over time
- mostly through non-forced means

### Capacity impact
- **5%–12%** reduction
- little true structural simplification

### 2030 margin effect
**+0.5 to +1.2 percentage points**

### Assessment
This is the least disruptive option, but usually the **weakest on durable savings**. It works only if the structural gap is modest.

---

## B. Partial consolidation

### Typical measures
- one major site or sub-operation is reduced / consolidated
- product or volume transferred to another German site
- shift removals across the network
- larger permanent capacity cut
- some transfers, some voluntary exits, some redeployment

### Estimated durable annual savings
**€700m–€1.3bn**

Breakdown:
- fixed-cost absorption improvement: **€200m–€400m**
- indirect labor / overhead reduction: **€150m–€300m**
- logistics / complexity simplification: **€50m–€150m**
- productivity / utilization gains: **€100m–€200m**
- labor cost correction: **€150m–€250m**

### One-time restructuring cost
**€500m–€1.2bn**

Mostly:
- severance
- write-downs
- transfer / relocation logistics
- ramp-up cost at receiving sites
- temporary inefficiency during transition

### Employment impact
- **4,000–8,000** direct positions reduced
- some can be absorbed by transfer / redeployment

### Capacity impact
- **12%–20%** reduction

### 2030 margin effect
**+1.2 to +2.0 percentage points**

### Assessment
This is often the **best balance** between savings and feasibility. It gives meaningful permanent relief without the full disruption of a closure.

---

## C. One-site closure / production transfer

### Typical measures
- closure of one German plant or one full production footprint
- transfer of production elsewhere
- strong network simplification
- major fixed-cost removal
- substantial job cuts

### Estimated durable annual savings
**€1.0bn–€1.8bn**

Breakdown:
- site fixed-cost elimination: **€250m–€450m**
- indirect labor and overhead cuts: **€200m–€350m**
- logistics / complexity savings: **€100m–€200m**
- better utilization at remaining sites: **€200m–€350m**
- labor cost improvement: **€150m–€250m**

### One-time restructuring cost
**€900m–€2.0bn**

Mostly:
- severance and closure costs
- asset impairments
- transfer and duplicate-running cost
- ramp-up losses
- supply chain changes
- possible launch / quality disruption

### Employment impact
- **5,000–10,000+** direct positions affected
- some transfers possible, but not all

### Capacity impact
- **15%–25%** reduction

### 2030 margin effect
**+1.8 to +3.0 percentage points**

### Assessment
This gives the **strongest structural reset**, but also the highest execution and social-disruption risk.

# 3) Side-by-side summary

| Metric | No-closure | Partial consolidation | One-site closure / transfer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Durable annual savings | €300m–€700m | €700m–€1.3bn | €1.0bn–€1.8bn |
| One-time cost | €150m–€500m | €500m–€1.2bn | €900m–€2.0bn |
| Direct job reduction | 1,500–4,000 | 4,000–8,000 | 5,000–10,000+ |
| Capacity reduction | 5%–12% | 12%–20% | 15%–25% |
| Margin uplift by 2030 | +0.5 to +1.2 pts | +1.2 to +2.0 pts | +1.8 to +3.0 pts |
| Execution risk | Low–medium | Medium | High |

---

# 4) What the numbers imply

## No-closure path
Mathematically possible, but only if:
- the gap is modest, or
- labor concessions and productivity gains are unusually large and durable

In most realistic German OEM cases, this is **too weak** for a large structural gap.

## Partial consolidation
Usually the **most credible compromise**:
- enough permanent savings to matter
- lower disruption than full closure
- more realistic than trying to preserve everything unchanged

## One-site closure / transfer
Best on paper for savings and fixed-cost relief, but:
- highest one-time cost
- highest execution risk
- greatest transition risk
- strongest employment impact

---

# 5) Practical conclusion

If the company needs roughly **€0.8bn–€1.4bn** of German-footprint correction, then:

- **No-closure** can work only at the lower end of the problem.
- **Partial consolidation** is the most likely route to a credible, durable package.
- **One-site closure** is the strongest financial answer if the gap is too large for partial measures.
