## Moderator summary, Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs **real restructuring**, not delay or symbolism;
- the situation involves a **serious structural cost and competitiveness problem**;
- **temporary measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient** if the gap is as large as described;
- the workforce should **not carry the burden alone**;
- any credible solution must be based on a **quantified savings bridge** and a **site-by-site / scenario-based assessment**;
- prolonged industrial conflict would be **damaging to the company and stakeholders**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved dispute is over **the acceptable form of restructuring**:

- **Works council and union**: insist on no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and preservation of job-security and wage frameworks.
- **Management, investors, and controlling family**: argue that permanent capacity reduction is likely needed and that a no-closure outcome may be financially insufficient if it does not deliver durable savings.
- **Regional state government**: resists closures as a first or default solution, but also does not deny the need for structural correction.

So the central split is:
- whether the needed correction can be achieved **without closures or forced layoffs**, or
- whether some form of **permanent site/capacity reduction** is unavoidable.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several important trade-offs remain unresolved:

- **Permanent savings vs. social protection**: how to achieve durable cost reduction while limiting job loss and site closures.
- **Temporary concessions vs. structural change**: whether employee contributions can be temporary only, or must become partly permanent.
- **Equal burden-sharing vs. competitiveness**: how much management, ownership, and capital must contribute alongside labor.
- **Network simplification vs. regional impact**: how to reduce overcapacity without hollowing out the German industrial base.
- **Execution risk vs. economic completeness**: whether a partial or non-closure solution can be implemented reliably enough to satisfy financial targets.
- **Political/governance constraints vs. financial necessity**: how the special governance structure affects what is realistically executable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to **numbers and options**. The parties should respond to a common scenario framework showing:
- **no closure**,
- **partial consolidation**,
- **one-site closure / production transfer**,

with each scenario assessed for:
- annual savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- employment impact,
- capex / investment needs,
- and expected margin effect by 2030.

A useful next step would be to ask each side to state, in concrete terms:
- what minimum outcome they could support,
- what trade-off they are willing to make,
- and what evidence would change their position.
