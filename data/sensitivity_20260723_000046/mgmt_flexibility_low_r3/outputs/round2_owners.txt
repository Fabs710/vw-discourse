We have now heard enough to separate **wishful thinking from restructuring reality**.

My position remains unchanged on the core issue: **this company needs a durable competitiveness reset, not a symbolic compromise that preserves every legacy claim and leaves the cost problem untouched.** I am prepared to support difficult measures if they genuinely restore margins and protect long-term value. I am **not** prepared to support a package that merely postpones the correction.

## Where I agree

I agree with several points made by the works council, the union, the state government, and the expert:

- The workforce should **not** be made to carry the entire burden.
- A purely short-term solution would be value-destructive.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company, the brand, and the investment case.
- Temporary measures alone are unlikely to be enough if the structural gap is as large as management says.
- A quantified savings bridge is essential.

Those are all fair points.

## Where I disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
I cannot support your insistence that:
- every German plant must remain open,
- compulsory redundancies must be ruled out entirely,
- and binding guarantees through 2030 must be granted before any real restructuring is agreed.

That is not a credible governance position if the business remains structurally uncompetitive.

You keep saying management has not proven that closures or forced reductions are necessary. But the expert has now confirmed the central arithmetic: **if the gap is large, temporary and reversible measures are unlikely to close it.** That means the burden of proof is no longer on management alone; it is on those rejecting closures to show a package that delivers **equivalent permanent savings**.

So far, I do **not** see that package.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect the statutory role and the regional responsibility, but I do **not** accept a blanket “no closures” stance as a governance principle.

A company can only preserve its regional industrial base if it remains economically viable. If the domestic footprint is allowed to remain oversized and expensive, the result is not protection of the region — it is slower erosion of the whole enterprise.

### To the Institutional Investors
I agree with your focus on measurable outcomes, but I would push one nuance: the solution has to be not only financially correct, but also executable within this governance structure. Still, your central point stands: **if the final package does not materially improve return on sales, fixed-cost absorption, and cash generation, then it is not acceptable.**

### To Management
I support your insistence that the current structure is too expensive. But I am not giving you a blank cheque for a closure-first strategy either.

You now need to do two things:
1. **Quantify the savings bridge clearly**
2. **Show why the required permanent correction cannot be achieved by lesser measures alone**

If you cannot demonstrate that, then you do not have the case for the hardest interventions.

## What the expert assessment means to me

The expert did **not** say “closures are mandatory.” That would be too simplistic.

What the expert did say is more important:
- no-closure solutions are only credible if the cost gap is moderate,
- temporary concessions are not enough on their own,
- and if the gap is large, some kind of permanent capacity correction is likely required.

That aligns with my own view.

So the question is not whether change is needed. It is **how much permanent correction is needed, and what form it should take**.

## My bottom line

I am prepared to support:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- labor-cost contribution,
- productivity improvement,
- redeployment and qualification,
- partial consolidation,
- and, if unavoidable, site-specific adjustments.

I am **not** prepared to support:
- a deal that preserves every plant regardless of economics,
- a package built mainly on temporary concessions,
- or a settlement that leaves the company structurally weak while claiming success.

## What I want next

Before I would back any final settlement, I want a **real quantified comparison** of:
- no closure / strong concession scenario,
- partial consolidation scenario,
- and closure / transfer scenario,

showing:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- employment impact,
- investment needs,
- and expected margin effect by 2030.

If the parties want to avoid a false compromise, that analysis is the next necessary step.
