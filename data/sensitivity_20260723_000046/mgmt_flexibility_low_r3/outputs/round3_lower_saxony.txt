We are now at the point where the discussion must stop pretending that “the numbers” automatically justify the harshest social outcome.

## My position

I support a **real restructuring**. I do **not** support a restructuring that turns into:
- plant closures in Germany as the default answer,
- compulsory redundancies as a routine instrument,
- or the hollowing out of the state’s co-determination and blocking role.

That is not acceptable to us.

## Where I agree

### To management
You are right that this company needs a durable competitiveness reset, not cosmetic delay. I accept that:
- temporary measures alone are unlikely to be enough,
- the cost base has to come down,
- and the company needs a credible path back to competitive margins.

But I do **not** accept your implication that this automatically means closures or forced layoffs. The expert did **not** say closures are inevitable. The expert said a no-closure solution is only credible if it achieves a **large permanent correction** elsewhere.

That means your case is still **not complete**.

### To the works council and union
I agree with you that the workforce must not carry the burden alone, and that:
- burden-sharing must be fair,
- management, ownership, and capital must contribute,
- and industrial peace should be preserved if a serious negotiated package is on the table.

I also agree that temporary concessions cannot become a disguised permanent wage rollback.

### To the regional state government
I agree that the regional industrial base must be protected and that reckless closures would damage not only jobs, but the long-term resilience of the company and the region.

## Where I disagree

### To management and investors
What I reject is the attempt to convert “structural correction” into a closure-first logic before all other options are genuinely tested.

You keep saying a no-closure package has not been proven. Fair enough. But neither have you shown that closure is **unavoidable**. The burden is not met by repeating that the footprint is expensive. You need a **site-by-site savings bridge** and a serious comparison of:
- no-closure,
- partial consolidation,
- and closure/transfer.

Until that exists, closures are an **assertion**, not a necessity.

### To the works council and union
At the same time, I need to be equally direct with labor: a flat “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no adjustment” stance is not enough if the company’s permanent savings gap is large.

I will not endorse a package that simply preserves every structure and calls that a turnaround. If you want to keep closures off the table, then you must be prepared to discuss:
- real permanent capacity reduction somewhere in the system,
- productivity gains,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- and a meaningful, but fair, employee contribution.

## What I will support

I am prepared to support a package only if it meets these conditions:

1. **No closure is the starting point, not the afterthought**
   - All non-closure alternatives must be fully tested first.

2. **No compulsory redundancies as the first instrument**
   - Any headcount reduction must be handled through negotiated, socially responsible mechanisms.

3. **A quantified savings bridge**
   - I want the numbers, site by site, with permanent vs. temporary effects separated clearly.

4. **Fair burden-sharing**
   - Labor contributes, but so do management and ownership.

5. **Binding protection for jobs and sites where feasible**
   - Especially in the state, because the company is a regional anchor, not just a balance sheet.

6. **Investment and product commitments**
   - Restructuring must strengthen the future industrial base, not just cut today’s costs.

## What I reject

I reject:
- closure-first bargaining,
- forced redundancies presented as “targeted” adjustments,
- vague assurances that the company will somehow become competitive without showing how,
- and any outcome that weakens the state’s veto and co-determination role.

That role is not decorative. It is part of the company’s stability and legitimacy.

## My boundary

I can be pragmatic. I can accept difficult compromises. I cannot accept a solution that guts regional employment or treats plant closure as the easy management answer.

If the company wants the state’s support, then it must produce a restructuring that:
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- protects jobs and industrial substance in Germany,
- and still delivers durable competitiveness.

## What I want next

Before any final conclusion, we need the **side-by-side scenario comparison**:
- no-closure,
- partial consolidation,
- closure/transfer,

with:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- employment effects,
- required investment,
- and impact on return on sales by 2030.
