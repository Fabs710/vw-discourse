As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. We are prepared to discuss a restructuring that secures the company’s future, but only if it is based on **fair burden-sharing**, **binding site and job guarantees**, and a **credible industrial strategy** for the German plants.

What we will argue for:
- **Keeping every German plant open**
- **Securing jobs through alternatives to layoffs**, such as:
  - capacity adjustments
  - working-time measures
  - natural attrition
  - redeployment and qualification
  - negotiated, temporary employee contributions
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**
- **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**, not a fait accompli

What we will oppose:
- Closing plants to solve management’s strategic failures
- Forced job cuts
- Breaking existing commitments to the workforce
- Any plan that treats German sites as disposable while expecting employees to absorb the costs

We are ready to negotiate seriously, but only on the basis of **site security, job security, and fairness**. If management wants change, it must bring a solution that preserves the industrial substance of the company and respects the people who built it.
