We hear the message from the works council and the union, but we need to be equally clear from management’s side:

**The current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable.**  
If we do nothing decisive, we do not protect jobs — we end up destroying them through declining competitiveness, falling margins, and a weaker industrial base.

Where I agree:
- We also want this company to remain a major industrial employer in Germany.
- We also prefer negotiated solutions over uncontrolled conflict.
- We also understand that a durable settlement must be more than a short-term cost patch.

Where I disagree:
- **A blanket rejection of plant closures is not a credible starting point.**
- **No compulsory redundancies as an absolute principle may be politically attractive, but it cannot be the only constraint if the cost base remains uncompetitive.**
- **Binding guarantees through 2030 are only meaningful if they are backed by a restructuring that genuinely lowers structural costs and capacity.**

Let me be direct: the German footprint is too expensive for the market reality we face. Europe is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and the brand must return to sustainable profitability. That requires **real capacity reduction, lower labour costs, and a leaner footprint**. Anything else risks becoming a cosmetic compromise.

That said, I am not insisting that every adjustment must come through closure alone. I remain open on the **mechanism**, but not on the **outcome**. The outcome must include:
- a **durable reduction in capacity**,
- a **material labour-cost contribution**,
- and a settlement that materially improves return on sales and frees capital for future product and technology investment.

So here is the boundary:
- If the employee side is prepared to present a package that delivers **structural savings at the scale required**, we can discuss how that is achieved.
- If the proposal is simply “keep everything open, preserve everything, and rely on temporary measures,” then that does **not** meet the company’s needs and will not be sufficient.

I also want to be transparent that prolonged strikes would damage everyone — production, reputation, and the company’s ability to invest. But the answer cannot be to freeze the status quo in place. We need a settlement that restores competitiveness, not one that postpones the problem.

So my message is simple:  
**We are ready to negotiate, but only around a package that materially cuts excess capacity and cost.**  
If the workforce wants guarantees, those guarantees have to be earned by a restructuring that actually fixes the business.
