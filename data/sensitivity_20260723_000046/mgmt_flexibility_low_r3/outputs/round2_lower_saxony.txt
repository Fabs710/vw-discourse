As the Regional State Government and anchor shareholder, I want to be equally direct.

## Where I agree

I agree with management and the expert on one fundamental point: **the company needs a real restructuring, not cosmetic delay**.  
I also agree that:
- the cost gap is serious,
- the European market is under pressure,
- and temporary measures alone may not be enough.

So I am not defending the status quo for its own sake.

## Where I disagree

### 1) Plant closures cannot be treated as the default solution
The expert was careful, but management is already using the logic of closure as a pressure tool. That is exactly where I draw a hard line.

A restructuring that begins from “we may need closures” is not neutral in this company. In our governance structure, closures are not just an operational detail; they are a decision with consequences for:
- regional employment,
- supplier networks,
- industrial capability,
- and the company’s legitimacy as a public anchor.

I will not support a path that makes closure the easy answer simply because it is the shortest spreadsheet route to savings.

### 2) The workforce cannot be told to absorb the whole correction
I agree with the works council and union that management, ownership, and capital must contribute too. That is not a rhetorical point; it is a governance reality.

What I do **not** accept is the idea that employees must first concede closures, layoffs, and wage erosion before the rest of the system is prepared to move. That is not burden-sharing. That is one-sided adjustment.

### 3) “Permanent capacity correction” does not automatically mean closing plants
Management and the investor side keep collapsing several different things into one:
- capacity reduction,
- site closure,
- forced redundancies,
- and cost reduction.

Those are not identical. If the company needs less capacity, there are still intermediate options:
- shift removal,
- production reallocation,
- platform consolidation,
- shorter working time,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and negotiated productivity measures.

I am not pretending these are cost-free. I am saying they should be exhausted before we accept closures in Germany.

## My position on the expert’s assessment

The expert’s intervention is useful because it confirms something important: **a negotiated solution without closures is possible in principle, but only if there is a large permanent correction elsewhere in the system.**

That means:
- the burden is not only on the workforce;
- and management cannot simply declare closures inevitable without showing the savings bridge.

So I welcome the call for numbers.  
What I do **not** accept is using “the numbers” as a blunt instrument to force through the politically easiest form of restructuring.

## What I would support

I would support a package only if it meets all of these conditions:

1. **No plant closure in Germany unless every non-closure alternative has been seriously and transparently tested**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the first instrument**
3. **A quantified savings bridge**
4. **A binding industrial plan with investment and product allocation**
5. **A fair employee contribution, but limited and temporary**
6. **A real contribution from management and ownership**
7. **Preservation of the state’s co-determination and blocking role**
8. **Site and job protection through the restructuring horizon, with enforceable guarantees where feasible**

That is the minimum basis for serious support from our side.

## What I reject

I reject:
- using closures as leverage before alternatives are exhausted,
- presenting labor as the only adjustable cost,
- treating regional impact as secondary to immediate financial optimization,
- and any outcome that weakens the state’s statutory role in the company.

I also reject the idea that “competitiveness” and “regional responsibility” are opposites. They are not. A company that destroys its domestic anchor base for short-term margin relief will also weaken its long-term resilience.

## To management

If you want my support, bring a package that:
- shows exactly how much permanent savings are required,
- separates temporary relief from structural correction,
- identifies what can be achieved without closures,
- and proves why any proposed site action is truly unavoidable.

Absent that, I will not endorse closure-first logic.

## To the works council and union

I support your insistence on:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- fair burden-sharing,
- and binding protection for jobs and sites.

But I also need to be candid: if you want closures off the table entirely, you will need to engage seriously with a quantified alternative that delivers permanent savings and productivity gains. “No closures” is a legitimate political and social position, but it becomes durable only if the economics are answered convincingly.

## To the controlling family and investors

You are right that the company must remain viable. But viability is not served by turning German plants into sacrificial sites.  
If you want restructuring support, you will have to accept:
- a stronger employee contribution from a negotiated agreement,
- real investment commitments,
- and a solution that does not hollow out the regional industrial base.

## My bottom line

I am prepared to back a tough restructuring.  
I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that normalizes plant closures in Germany as the first serious option.

The next step must be concrete:
- a quantified savings bridge,
- a site-by-site alternative analysis,
- and a negotiated package that keeps the company competitive **without** gutting its regional core.

If management cannot show that, then I will use every statutory and political means available to block a one-sided solution.
