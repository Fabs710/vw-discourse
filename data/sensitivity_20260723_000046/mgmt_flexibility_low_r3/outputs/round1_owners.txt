We share the basic diagnosis that this company needs a credible competitiveness reset. I do **not** believe anyone here benefits from prolonging a structure that keeps margins under pressure and leaves the brand under-invested. From an ownership perspective, that would be value-destructive.

That said, I am **not** prepared to support a “closures first” approach as the automatic answer.

My position is:

- **I support structural restructuring.**
- **I support material cost reduction.**
- **I support capacity adjustment if it is genuinely required.**
- **I do not support a settlement that permanently weakens the company’s long-term industrial base in Germany.**

So I agree with management on one point: temporary fixes and cosmetic compromises are not enough. But I disagree with the implication that plant closures and forced redundancies are the only credible way to achieve savings. They may be part of the toolset, but they are not a preferred starting point, especially in a company with this governance structure and this strategic importance.

To the works council and union: your insistence on fairness, co-determination, and burden-sharing is legitimate. What is **not** acceptable from my side is a position that treats the existing footprint as untouchable regardless of economics. “No closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real change” would simply defer the crisis and damage the enterprise. That would also harm the workforce in the medium term.

To management: if you want support from the controlling shareholder, you need to show that the package is not just about shrinking the company, but about **restoring its earning power**. That means:
- a credible industrial roadmap,
- verifiable cost improvements,
- targeted rather than blanket cuts,
- and a labor settlement that is durable enough to prevent serial conflict.

To the regional state government: I respect the role and the regional responsibility, but a blocking stance against any serious adjustment is not enough. The company must remain viable. Protecting the regional industrial base requires competitiveness, not only protection.

So I will say this plainly:  
**I am open to a negotiated solution, but only if it delivers real structural improvement.**  
If the final package is mostly guarantees, reversible measures, and promises of future discipline without actual cost and capacity correction, then it will not be sufficient.

What I would regard as acceptable in principle:
- capacity reductions where justified,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- working-time and labor-cost measures,
- a fair employee contribution,
- and a restructuring that is tough enough to restore margins.

What I would not regard as acceptable:
- permanent value erosion,
- a hollowed-out company pretending to have been restructured,
- or a settlement that preserves every legacy claim while leaving the business uncompetitive.

I am prepared to back a serious turnaround. I am **not** prepared to back a compromise that simply postpones hard decisions.
