We are aligned on the central point: **the workforce will not pay for this crisis alone**.

Let me respond directly to each side.

## To management
You keep saying the German footprint is “too expensive” and that the cost base must be cut structurally. We do **not** deny that savings are needed. What we reject is the implication that the only credible path runs through **closures and forced redundancies**.

You have **not demonstrated** that the required savings cannot be achieved through a negotiated mix of:
- working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- bonus waivers or deferred compensation,
- and other reversible measures.

So no: “competitiveness” is not a blank cheque for breaking the collective wage framework or imposing dismissals. If you want our cooperation, bring a **quantified savings bridge** that shows how much you need, over what horizon, and how the burden is shared.

What is not acceptable:
- plant closures as a first resort,
- compulsory redundancies,
- unilateral wage cuts,
- or a restructuring that shifts all adjustment costs onto employees.

## To the works council
We are with you on the essentials:
- **no plant closures,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no dismantling of the job-security framework.**

And let me be just as clear: the union will not endorse any “targeted” cuts if they are simply forced layoffs in disguise.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: a settlement must also protect the **collective wage framework**. If employee contributions are part of the answer, then they must be:
- temporary,
- limited,
- fairly distributed,
- and matched by real contributions from management and owners.

Employees can contribute. They should not be the only ones contributing.

## To the regional state government
Your role matters, and your resistance to reckless closures is welcome. But sympathy is not enough. We need you to use your influence to back a settlement that:
- protects sites,
- protects jobs,
- and secures industrial substance in Germany.

A purely political “no closures” stance is not sufficient unless it is paired with a viable industrial alternative. We are ready to work with you on that.

## To the controlling family
You say you do not want a “closures first” approach. Good — then stand firm on that when the numbers get hard.

But let me be equally clear: support for restructuring is only credible if ownership also contributes. That means:
- not just demanding sacrifice from labor,
- not just shifting risk downward,
- but accepting a package where capital, management, and workforce all bear part of the burden.

We can accept a fair contribution by employees if it is in exchange for:
- binding site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a durable collective agreement that preserves wage standards.

## What we can discuss
We are prepared to negotiate a serious package that includes:
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- capacity reductions through shift and working-time tools,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and a negotiated productivity and flexibility effort.

But this only works if the red lines are respected:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No unilateral attack on the collective wage agreement**
4. **Burden-sharing from all sides**
5. **Binding security for jobs and sites**

## Bottom line
We are not here to block change for its own sake. We are here to block a one-sided solution where employees absorb the pain while management and owners define “necessity” and call it realism.

If management wants a durable settlement, it needs to put a **fair, shared-sacrifice package** on the table — one that protects jobs and sites, preserves the wage framework, and gives the company real competitiveness improvements without sacrificing the workforce.

If that is not on offer, then the union will continue to defend its members with the means at its disposal.
