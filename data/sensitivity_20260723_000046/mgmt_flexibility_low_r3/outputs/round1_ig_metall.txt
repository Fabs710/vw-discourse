We share the core assessment that this crisis cannot be solved by making the workforce alone carry the burden.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **Plant closures in Germany are not acceptable.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **A unilateral attack on the collective wage framework is not acceptable.**

The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to defend jobs, wages, and sites. That is exactly what we will do.

At the same time, we are not refusing restructuring in principle. We have already signaled openness to a **fair, shared-sacrifice package**. That means we can discuss:

- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and other reversible measures.

But this only works if it is tied to **binding guarantees** on:

- **no plant closures,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **site security,**
- and a durable collective agreement that preserves the wage framework.

What we will not accept is management presenting job cuts and closures as the only serious option while the workforce is asked to pay first and pay most. If there are savings to be made, then **owners, management, and capital must also contribute**. Fairness is not optional here; it is the condition for any settlement.

So our position is:

1. **We are ready to negotiate.**
2. **We are ready to contribute.**
3. **We are not ready to sacrifice jobs and sites to cover strategic mistakes.**

If management and the owners want a durable settlement, they need to put a serious alternative on the table that protects the industrial base and shares the burden fairly. Otherwise, they should expect escalating resistance from the workforce.
