## Moderator Summary — Round 1

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company faces a serious structural competitiveness problem;
- simple delay or cosmetic measures will not resolve it;
- a negotiated solution is preferable to an open conflict;
- some form of restructuring is necessary;
- options such as capacity adjustments, working-time measures, redeployment, qualification, and temporary employee contributions are on the table;
- maintaining the company’s long-term industrial base in Germany remains an important objective for several parties.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures in Germany**: employee representatives, the state government, and the family are resistant; management and investors see closures as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: strongly rejected by labor and the state; management and investors will not exclude them if needed for cost correction.
- **Scope of burden-sharing**: labor wants owners and management to contribute more; management and investors emphasize the need for substantial employee-side and structural concessions.
- **Guarantees vs. flexibility**: labor seeks binding site and job guarantees through 2030; management and investors argue that binding guarantees are only acceptable if backed by deep restructuring.
- **Definition of “credible restructuring”**: labor emphasizes preservation of sites and employment; management and investors emphasize measurable cost, capacity, and margin improvement.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion has not yet resolved:
- how much capacity reduction is actually required;
- whether required savings can be achieved without closures or compulsory layoffs;
- what mix of temporary versus permanent measures is acceptable;
- what level of employee contribution is proportionate;
- what concrete investment, product, or industrial commitments will accompany restructuring;
- how to balance short-term social protection with long-term financial viability;
- what binding assurances, if any, can be given in exchange for concessions.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
For Round 2, the parties should move from principles to specifics:
- identify the minimum savings and capacity adjustments needed;
- test which combinations of measures could achieve them;
- clarify which guarantees are realistic and which are not;
- define what each side is prepared to contribute;
- focus on a package that is both socially acceptable and financially credible.

The next round should aim to narrow the gap between **site/job protection** and **structural competitiveness** by comparing concrete options rather than repeating bottom-line positions.
