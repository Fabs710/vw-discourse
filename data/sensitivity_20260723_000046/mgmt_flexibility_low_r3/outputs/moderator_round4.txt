The core impasse is now clear:

## Core impasse
The parties are no longer disputing the need for restructuring. They are disputing **the acceptable instrument**.

- **Labor / works council / state**: insist that restructuring must be achieved **without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**.
- **Management / family / investors**: insist that the package must deliver **durable structural savings**, and that if no-closure cannot close the gap, **site-level correction remains on the table**.
- The expert’s input has sharpened the conflict: **no-closure is not ruled out, but it must be quantitatively strong enough**. That leaves the room split on the threshold for “sufficient.”

So the deadlock is not about whether savings are needed; it is about **how much permanent correction is required, and whether that can be achieved without closure or forced layoffs**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise pathways

### 1) “No-closure first, but conditional review”
A structured package could be tested first that keeps every German site open and avoids compulsory redundancies, but includes:
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- overhead cuts,
- product / volume reallocation,
- capped and time-limited employee contributions,
- internal redeployment and qualification.

**Compromise feature:**  
This gives labor and the state the protection they seek upfront, while giving management and investors a measurable test. If agreed savings are not delivered by a fixed milestone, the parties reopen the question of deeper structural correction.

**Why it might bridge:**  
It offers a serious no-closure attempt without permanently excluding management’s concerns.

---

### 2) “Partial consolidation with a no-forced-redundancy floor”
A middle package could accept **some capacity reduction or site-role reduction**, but only under strict constraints:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- voluntary exits and transfers first,
- binding investment commitments in the remaining sites,
- protection of the collective job-security framework,
- and a clear site-by-site savings bridge.

**Compromise feature:**  
This preserves the strongest labor red lines while recognizing the management/investor demand for more durable savings than a purely internal efficiency package may provide.

**Why it might bridge:**  
It may be the most realistic meeting point if the no-closure option cannot reach the required permanent savings, but full closure is still politically and industrially unacceptable.

---

### 3) “Structured bargain: permanent savings target, instrument open”
The parties could first agree on the **required permanent savings and 2030 margin target**, then leave the instrument mix open for negotiation:
- labor-cost measures,
- capacity corrections,
- overhead simplification,
- product reassignment,
- possible site-level adjustments,
- owner/management contributions.

**Compromise feature:**  
This moves the dispute away from “closure vs. no closure” as a starting point and toward a shared target with an agreed burden-sharing formula.

**Why it might bridge:**  
It allows each side to claim its priority is protected: labor can insist closures are not predetermined, while management can insist the final package must still hit the numbers.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What permanent savings threshold would make a no-closure package credible enough for you to accept, and what specific measures would you consider sufficient to reach it?**

### To the Industrial Union
**If no-closure savings fall short of the full target, what limited forms of capacity correction would you still consider compatible with no compulsory redundancies and no permanent wage rollback?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete investment and employment safeguards would you require before supporting any package that includes partial consolidation but no forced layoffs?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Would you explicitly support a no-closure package if it meets the quantified savings bridge, even if it requires stronger owner-side or management-side contributions than you would otherwise prefer?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**What minimum verified annual permanent savings and 2030 margin uplift would make you willing to support a non-closure solution despite a higher upfront execution risk?**

### To Management
**Can you present a site-by-site bridge showing the smallest possible structural intervention that still reaches the 2030 target, before treating closure as necessary?**

## Next-step facilitator move
A useful next round would be to ask the independent modeler to produce **one neutral comparison table** with:
- no-closure,
- partial consolidation,
- and closure/transfer,

showing for each:
- permanent annual savings,
- one-time cost,
- employment impact,
- investment needs,
- execution risk,
- and 2030 margin effect.

That would give the parties a shared factual basis for deciding whether a compromise is still possible.
