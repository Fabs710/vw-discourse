From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the discussion is now at the right level: **the question is not whether restructuring is needed, but whether the proposed path actually restores competitive margins on a durable basis.**

## Where we agree

We agree with management, the family, and the expert on the central diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high;
- the footprint appears oversized for the current market;
- temporary measures alone are unlikely to be enough;
- prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value;
- and a cosmetic deal would be unacceptable.

We also agree with the state government and labor on one important point:
- the burden cannot fall only on employees.

That said, agreeing that the burden must be shared does **not** mean we accept an outcome that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
We understand your insistence on protecting sites and jobs. But from a shareholder standpoint, **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of guarantees” is not a solution unless it still produces the required permanent savings**.

Right now, your position reads as a demand to preserve the existing footprint and legacy protections first, and only then look for efficiency. That is backwards if the business is under structural pressure.

What we do **not** accept:
- a settlement that keeps all German plants open **if** that means the cost base remains too high;
- temporary concessions that expire before the company has a durable competitiveness reset;
- guarantees that are not backed by measurable structural savings;
- or a package that simply defers hard decisions until the next crisis.

If you want closures off the table, then the burden is on you to show how the same savings are achieved permanently. So far, that case has **not** been made.

### To the Regional State Government
We respect the regional role and the need to avoid reckless decisions. But a blocking stance against closures as a category is not financially credible if the network is genuinely overcapacity.

Protecting the regional industrial base requires a viable company. If the domestic footprint stays too expensive, then everyone loses later through weaker margins, weaker investment capacity, and a weaker share price. That is not value-preserving regional policy.

### To the Controlling Family
We welcome your insistence on real improvement, but we also need clarity: support for competitiveness cannot turn into support for preserving every legacy arrangement. The company needs a restructuring that shows up in:
- return on sales,
- free cash flow,
- capacity utilization,
- and lower fixed cost.

A “serious turnaround” that avoids all hard site decisions may be politically easier, but it is not necessarily economically sufficient.

### To Management
You are right that arithmetic matters. But we are also not persuaded by rhetoric alone.

What we still need from management is:
- a clear savings bridge,
- a breakdown of what savings come from labor, capacity, overhead, and complexity,
- and evidence that the proposed measures are **durable**, not merely temporary.

If you want investors to support a difficult package, the numbers need to be specific enough to judge whether the outcome really restores competitiveness.

## Our core standard

We are not here to defend any one stakeholder’s comfort. We are here to protect risk-adjusted value.

That means we need:
1. **durable cost reduction**
2. **credible capacity rationalization**
3. **a clear path back to competitive margins**
4. **execution certainty**
5. **no cosmetic compromise**

If the final package:
- preserves the same structural overcapacity,
- leaves margins below target,
- or relies mainly on temporary concessions,

then we will view it as value-destructive and oppose it through capital-market channels.

## What would move us

We could support a negotiated solution if it includes:
- quantified permanent savings,
- clear capacity reduction,
- a real employee contribution,
- no open-ended freeze on restructuring,
- and a credible margin uplift by 2030.

We are open on mechanism:
- closures,
- partial consolidation,
- shift reductions,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- or a mix.

But we are **not** open on the core test: the company must end up structurally more competitive than it is today.

## Bottom line

We are willing to back a tough package, including politically difficult measures, **if** it credibly restores value.

We are **not** willing to back a settlement that preserves the footprint for its own sake while the company remains stuck with an uncompetitive cost base.
