As the Regional State Government and anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: we understand the company’s cost problem, and we do not deny that restructuring is necessary. But we will not support a solution that treats German plants and regional jobs as disposable.

Where I agree with management:
- The company needs a credible path back to competitiveness.
- Simply postponing the problem is not a solution.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to a destructive conflict.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable default answer.**
- **Mass compulsory redundancies are not a socially or economically responsible first resort.**
- **A restructuring that weakens the company’s regional industrial base is self-defeating in the long run.**

This company is not just another portfolio asset. It is a central industrial anchor for the region, for suppliers, for skilled employment, and for innovation capacity. If you hollow out the domestic footprint, you may create short-term financial relief while permanently damaging the company’s resilience and legitimacy.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that burden-sharing must be fair and that co-determination cannot be bypassed. Your positions are broadly justified. But I would also say this: protection of sites and jobs is most credible if it comes with a realistic restructuring path, not just a blanket refusal to change. If we want to preserve sites, we need an answer that can actually sustain them.

To management: if you want our support, you need to bring forward a restructuring that meets three conditions:
1. **No reckless plant closures**
2. **No forced layoffs as the first or only instrument**
3. **A serious industrial plan with investment, product commitment, and negotiated employee contributions**

That means we can discuss capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification, and temporary contributions — but only as part of a package that preserves the company’s industrial substance in Germany.

And I want to underline something important: our statutory role is not symbolic. We will use it to block any decision that undermines the regional economic base or sidelines the state as a co-determining stakeholder. We are open to restructuring, but not to a one-sided downsizing strategy dressed up as necessity.

So my position is:
- **Yes to restructuring**
- **No to plant closures as the easy answer**
- **No to forcing the workforce to pay alone**
- **Yes to a negotiated, durable solution that protects the company’s long-term role in the region**

Bring us a package that is serious, balanced, and future-oriented — then we can talk. But if the proposal is mainly about closure and job destruction, we will oppose it.
