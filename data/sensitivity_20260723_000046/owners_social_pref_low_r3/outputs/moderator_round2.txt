### Core impasse
The main deadlock is between **labour’s demand for binding protection** and **management/investor demands for structurally meaningful capacity and cost reduction**.

More specifically:
- **Works Council / Union / State**: insist on **no German plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies**, with binding site/job guarantees.
- **Management / Family / Investors**: insist that the company needs **durable structural change**, including the possibility of closures or equivalent capacity removal if alternatives do not produce sufficient savings.
- The result is a clash between **absolute safeguards** and **irreversible restructuring tools**.

A second, related impasse is **trust**:
- Labour does not trust that temporary concessions won’t lead to later closures.
- Management and capital do not trust that “no closure” commitments will still allow a viable turnaround.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete compromise frames that could bridge the most conflicting stakeholders:

#### 1) Time-limited non-closure settlement with hard trigger clauses
- Agree to a **binding non-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy period through a defined date**.
- In return, labour accepts **temporary, bounded contributions** such as working-time reductions, bonus restraint, redeployment, and qualification measures.
- A **jointly agreed review mechanism** with objective financial and operational indicators would determine whether a second phase is needed.
- If targets are missed, the parties move to a pre-agreed escalation process rather than unilateral action.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives labour real short-term protection while giving management and investors a structured path to verify whether savings are sufficient.

#### 2) Differentiated site package with “protect, transform, or repurpose” categories
- Classify sites into three groups:
  - **Protected sites**: guaranteed no closure during the pact period.
  - **Transformation sites**: receive investment, product allocation, and productivity programs.
  - **Repurpose/adjust sites**: no closure initially, but internal consolidation, volume shifts, or functional changes are negotiated.
- Any site considered for closure would first require proof that **internal alternatives were exhausted and quantified**.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It allows differentiation without treating every plant as equally vulnerable, while still giving management a mechanism to address overcapacity.

#### 3) Shared-burden package with explicit management, owner, and capital contributions
- Employee contributions are paired with:
  - **management overhead cuts**,
  - **owner/capital contributions**,
  - and a **binding investment commitment** for future products and German production capability.
- Concessions are **time-limited** and end automatically unless renewed by agreement.
- The package includes a **joint monitoring body** with works council, union, management, state, and owners.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It addresses the recurring complaint that labour is being asked to bear the full burden, while also meeting investor concerns about credibility and measurable savings.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
**If a binding no-closure guarantee through 2030 is not acceptable to management and owners, what narrower, enforceable package would you accept that still gives them sufficient flexibility to claim the restructuring is credible?**

#### Industrial Union
**What exact combination of temporary concessions would the union support if it were paired with hard, legally enforceable protections and objective performance triggers?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What quantified level of savings do you require before you would accept a non-closure alternative as structurally sufficient, and can you specify which measures would count toward that threshold?**

#### Regional State Government
**Will the state shareholder use its governance leverage to block closure-led restructuring unless management first demonstrates that non-closure alternatives were fully tested and quantified?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Would the family support a time-limited non-closure pact if it included measurable savings targets, management cuts, and a joint trigger mechanism for reassessment?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What concrete evidence would make a non-closure, no-forced-redundancy package investable for you: target margins, capacity reduction, cost savings, or implementation certainty?**
