We agree on the diagnosis, but not on the burden of proof or the acceptable end state.

## Our position
As dispersed capital-market shareholders, we need one thing above all: **credible, durable margin restoration**. That requires **structural cost reduction and capacity alignment**. If the final package leaves German operations structurally overcosted or underpowered, we will not support it — regardless of how politically attractive it may sound.

## Where we agree
### With management
You are right that this is not a temporary fluctuation. The company needs a real reset in cost base, capacity, and productivity.

### With the family owners
You are right that a cosmetic settlement would just postpone the crisis and destroy value later. You are also right that a prolonged strike would be costly and could damage the business.

### With the state
You are right that industrial conflict is value-destructive and that this company matters beyond one quarter’s numbers.

### With labour
You are right that any durable settlement must be negotiated if it is to hold.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your repeated position — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding guarantees through 2030 — is **not sufficient on its own** if it preserves overcapacity and keeps the cost base uncompetitive.

We do **not** accept a settlement that simply protects the current structure while asking capital to fund continued underperformance.

At the same time, we do **not** accept management’s tendency to treat closures as automatically proven. That is not yet shown. If closures are being used as the first and only serious lever, management needs to quantify why less destructive options cannot deliver the required savings.

### To Management
You keep saying structural change is unavoidable. We agree. But “therefore closures” is still not a complete argument.

Before asking shareholders to back irreversible steps, show us the numbers:
- savings from internal reorganization,
- savings from working-time and flexibility measures,
- savings from management overhead cuts,
- savings from owner/capital contributions,
- savings from productivity gains and investment,
- and then the incremental value of closure or equivalent irreversible capacity reduction.

If you cannot quantify that case, then closure is still a preference, not a proven necessity.

## What we need to see
For us to support the package, it must deliver:

1. **Durable cost reduction**
2. **Measurable capacity reduction**
3. **A credible path back to competitive margins**
4. **Deal certainty**
5. **Low execution risk**

That means:
- no cosmetic peace deal,
- no permanent protection of an uncompetitive structure,
- and no prolonged industrial war that destroys value.

## On the compromise ideas
### 1) Time-limited non-closure settlement
This is only acceptable if the first phase produces real, measurable savings and the review point is not just a way to defer hard decisions forever.

A moratorium can work only if it buys time for action, not time for denial.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies in exchange for employee contributions
Potentially workable, but only if the savings are real and the package is enforceable. “No forced redundancies” cannot become a blanket immunity from structural change if the economics still do not add up.

### 3) Site-by-site differentiation
This is the most sensible route. Not every site or activity has the same economic value. But differentiation must be based on economics, not politics — and it must still deliver the required savings.

## Our line
We would support a negotiated settlement only if it genuinely restores competitiveness and margins. That means:
- durable savings,
- meaningful capacity adjustment,
- a credible implementation path,
- and no merely temporary accounting relief.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves structural overcapacity,
- or an outcome that leaves the German cost base uncompetitive in the medium term.

## Bottom line
The question is not whether the workforce deserves protection. It does.  
The question is whether the company can return to competitive returns without further structural action.

If yes, show us a package that truly gets there.  
If no, then no amount of rhetoric about fairness or stability will make the equity case work.

If management wants investor support, bring a quantified plan with clear savings, clear timing, and clear execution logic. Without that, we will remain skeptical.
