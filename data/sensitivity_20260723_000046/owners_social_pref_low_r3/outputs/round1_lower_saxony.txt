I will be equally direct.

The state shareholder does **not** accept a restructuring logic that treats German sites and jobs as the default adjustment variable while asking the regions and workforce to absorb the social cost. We are prepared to support a serious restructuring, but only if it protects the company’s role as a regional industrial anchor and preserves a credible long-term base in Germany.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: the company does need a real competitiveness improvement. We cannot defend an industrial structure that permanently loses money and leaves no room for investment. I also agree that the answer cannot be cosmetic or purely symbolic.

I also agree with the union and works council that any solution must be negotiated and future-oriented. A pure top-down closure plan would be socially destructive, politically reckless, and likely unstable in implementation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point. That is not a neutral technical measure; it is a decision with lasting consequences for employment, regional value creation, suppliers, and the company’s legitimacy. Once a plant is closed, that capacity, trust, and know-how are not easily restored.

I also do **not** accept forced redundancies as the main restructuring tool. A company of this importance must not solve its strategic problems by offloading them onto thousands of families and local economies.

At the same time, I would caution the employee side against treating every structural adjustment as illegitimate. If the company truly has excess capacity, then capacity has to be reduced somewhere. But that must be done through **socially responsible, negotiated measures**, not through an irrevocable break with the company’s regional responsibilities.

### What would be acceptable
A viable path would have to include all of the following:

1. **Binding site and employment commitments**
   - not empty promises, but enforceable guarantees for a defined period;
2. **A negotiated reduction in capacity without closures, where possible**
   - including working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, natural attrition, and voluntary programs;
3. **A fair contribution from management and owners**
   - not just demands on labour;
4. **A credible investment plan**
   - especially for future products and technologies;
5. **Respect for co-determination and the special governance structure**
   - the state will not be sidelined in a decision that affects the industrial fabric of the region.

### What is not acceptable
To be clear, the following are red lines for us:
- German plant closures as the preferred solution,
- mass compulsory redundancies,
- erosion of co-determination rights,
- a restructuring that sacrifices the regional industrial base for short-term accounting relief.

### My view of the current positions
Management is right that the company needs structural improvement. But you are wrong if you think that structural improvement requires closures as the first instrument. That is too narrow and too risky.

The works council and union are right to defend jobs, sites, and collective protections. But if their position is interpreted as “no change at all,” that will not be sustainable either. The company needs a solution that preserves as much employment as possible **and** restores competitiveness.

### The path I would support
I would support a package built around:
- capacity adjustment through negotiated, reversible measures first,
- a temporary employee contribution tied to hard guarantees,
- management and shareholder contributions,
- investment commitments for future platforms and products,
- and only if absolutely unavoidable, the least damaging structural measures after exhausting alternatives.

That is the standard I would defend in the supervisory board: **socially responsible restructuring, not industrial retreat**.

So my message to management is: bring a plan that proves you have exhausted non-closure options before asking for plant closures or mass layoffs.

And my message to labour is: be prepared to negotiate real contributions, but only in exchange for binding protections and a credible future for the company and its workforce.
