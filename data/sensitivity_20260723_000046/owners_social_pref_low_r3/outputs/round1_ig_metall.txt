The union shares the core line already set out by the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of collective protections**. On that we are aligned.

Let me be equally clear: **management cannot expect the workforce to pay for this restructuring alone**. If the company wants a durable solution, it has to be a **shared-sacrifice package**, not a job-cutting programme dressed up as transformation.

Where I agree:
- The company does need a credible industrial future.
- Capacity has to be addressed somehow.
- Labour costs and structures can be part of the discussion.
- We are prepared to discuss temporary or negotiated contributions from employees.

Where I disagree:
- **Closing German plants is not an acceptable starting point.**
- **Forced layoffs are not acceptable.**
- **Open-ended insecurity is not acceptable.**
- **Cutting pay or guarantees without equivalent contributions from management and owners is not acceptable.**

If the company is serious about a solution, then the burden must be shared fairly. That means:
1. **Job and site security through 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No plant closures**
4. **A negotiated employee contribution only in exchange for binding guarantees**
5. **Management and owners also contributing materially** — not just asking the workforce for concessions
6. **A credible investment and product commitment** so that we are not simply managing decline

What could be discussable from our side:
- temporary working-time measures,
- voluntary options,
- postponement or reduction of bonuses,
- a solidarity contribution into a restructuring fund,
- flexible capacity adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification.

What is not discussable:
- closures,
- forced job losses,
- unilateral wage dumping,
- stripping away the collective bargaining framework.

The workforce has already shown it is prepared to act. Warning strikes are not a threat for effect; they are a sign that the peace obligation has ended and members expect us to defend their interests. If management insists on making labour carry the full cost, then yes, this will escalate.

So my message is simple: **bring us a restructuring that preserves jobs and sites, shares the pain fairly, and gives the company a future — or face serious resistance from the union side.**
