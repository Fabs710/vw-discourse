From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the priority is straightforward: **restore competitive margins, reduce structural cost, and do it in a way that is credible enough to support the equity story.**

We are not here to defend every legacy site or every historic guarantee. We are here to protect risk-adjusted value. On that basis, I see both the management case and the labour/state concerns — but only one thing ultimately matters: **does the final package materially fix the cost base, or does it merely postpone the problem?**

### Where I agree
- **Management is right** that the company has a structural competitiveness problem.
- **The family owners are right** that a cosmetic settlement would destroy value later.
- **The state government is right** that prolonged conflict and social disruption would be damaging.
- **Labour is right** that a durable solution has to be negotiated rather than imposed if it is to hold.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that preserves the current German cost base through symbolic concessions and soft language about “transformation.” If margins remain structurally impaired, shareholders continue to bear the downside while everyone else claims victory.

I also do **not** support open-ended industrial conflict. Prolonged strikes, uncertainty, and customer disruption are value destructive. A drawn-out confrontation that damages output and confidence would be worse than a hard but credible restructuring.

At the same time, I am not automatically supportive of closures as a headline move if they are simply a way to force value destruction through execution risk. **Closures are acceptable only if they are part of a package that clearly improves the economics** and can be implemented without triggering a longer, costlier breakdown.

### What we need to see
For us to back this, the package must deliver:
1. **Durable and measurable cost reduction**
2. **A credible path back to competitive margins**
3. **Deal certainty**
4. **No merely temporary accounting relief**
5. **A realistic implementation path that avoids prolonged disruption**

### On the specific positions
- **To management:** if you want support from capital markets, bring numbers, not just pressure. Show us how much the package reduces unit costs, what the margin uplift is, and when it lands. If the answer is vague, we will not call that a restructuring.
- **To the works council and union:** your red lines may be understandable, but they are not sufficient if they block real structural change. “No closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are not credible if they leave overcapacity and uncompetitive labour costs untouched. If you want investor support for a negotiated settlement, it must actually move the economics.
- **To the state government:** regional protection is not a substitute for profitability. If the business remains weak, the region ultimately loses more. Support a solution that restores the company’s economics, not one that merely delays hard choices.
- **To the family owners:** I agree with your emphasis on long-term value, but that value has to be evidenced through disciplined restructuring, not just appeals to strategic flexibility.

### Our bottom line
We are open to a negotiated compromise, but **only if it credibly restores competitiveness and margins**. If the outcome is a cosmetic peace deal, a prolonged strike, or a package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive, we will not support it.

The company needs to prove that it is willing to take the steps necessary to remain globally competitive. That is the only basis on which capital markets will stand behind the restructuring.
