I will be equally clear in round 4:

A package that preserves skills, keeps all plants open, and delivers durable savings through internal adjustment is not “soft.” It is the smarter value strategy.

## On the three compromise frames

### 1) Phased non-closure agreement with conditional review
I reject any version of this that contains a hidden exit ramp back to closures or forced redundancies.

A phase can be used to implement:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary programs,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner contributions,
- and investment commitments.

But the result must be **binding security**, not a probation period before layoffs.

If management needs time to prove a better plan, fine. But that time cannot be bought with a gun to the workforce’s head.

### 2) Site-by-site transformation with explicit economic criteria
This is exactly how closure pressure gets repackaged.

“Protected,” “transformed,” “repurposed” — these are often just softer words for selecting sites to be weakened and eventually closed. We will not accept a linguistic detour that produces the same outcome.

If the proposal means internal reallocation of functions, volumes, and investment **while all German plants remain open**, then we can discuss it. If it means ranking sites for future elimination, then no.

### 3) Shared-burden package with enforceable symmetry
Yes, burden-sharing is necessary. But only if it is real and symmetrical.

That means:
- management cuts,
- owner contributions,
- investment commitments,
- temporary labour flexibility,
- and binding site/job guarantees.

It does **not** mean labour gives first, while management retains the right to reopen closures later. That is asymmetry, not fairness.

Let me stop the euphemisms.

A real compromise would mean:
- management drops closures and forced layoffs,

Anything else is not compromise. It is a demand that workers pay for management’s failure.
