Management, investors, and even the moderator are still trying to frame this as if the only serious choices are either closures or accepting a weakened workforce. I reject that framing.

Let me be absolutely clear again:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of job-security protections.**

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are the minimum condition for any agreement to exist.

## On the moderator’s “compromise” ideas

### 1) Phased restructuring with a closure moratorium
A moratorium is not a solution if it is just a countdown to closures later. We are not interested in a temporary pause that hands management a deferred execution right.

If the company wants time, then that time must be used for **non-destructive restructuring**:
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification,
- voluntary programs,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

But I will not accept a “review mechanism” that quietly reopens the door to plant closures and forced layoffs after the workforce has made concessions. That is not a compromise. That is a trap.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies in exchange for cost measures
This is the only direction in the moderator’s proposals that comes close to something workable. But even here, the condition is decisive: **the company must accept binding protection through 2030** for sites and jobs.

If management wants employee contributions, then the company must give real guarantees in return. Not vague intentions. Not soft language. Binding commitments.

### 3) Site-by-site differentiation
We are not opposed to rational internal adjustments. But I will say this plainly: “reviewing marginal or duplicate activities” is often just a polite label for closures by another name.

If there is genuine duplication, then solve it through:
- reallocating production,
- investing in future products at existing German sites,
- shifting volumes inside the German footprint,
- and securing the workforce with redeployment and qualification.

What we will not do is accept one plant after another being declared “marginal” until the company has hollowed out its German base.

## To management
You say the economics must work. Fine. But you have not demonstrated that closures are the only way to make the economics work.

Your argument is always the same: “We need deep structural reduction, therefore closures.” That is not a proof. It is a preference.

If management wants credibility, then show us:
- how much savings come from internal reorganization,
- how much from working-time and voluntary measures,
- how much from management cuts and owner contributions,
- how much from investment-led productivity gains.

Do that first. Only then can anyone seriously claim closures are unavoidable. Until then, the closure demand remains a choice, not a necessity.

And let me add: you cannot ask the workforce to trust a management team that is already breaking historic commitments and then expect us to sign away site security. Trust has to be rebuilt, not demanded.

## To the union
We are aligned on the essentials, and I welcome that. But I want to sharpen one point: **temporary employee contributions must not become permanent wage erosion**.

If the union is prepared to negotiate flexibility, bonuses, or working time, that is only acceptable if it is:
- time-limited,
- clearly bounded,
- and exchanged for legally enforceable guarantees.

Anything else becomes a one-way concession by labour while management keeps the option to come back later for more cuts.

## To the state government
Your role matters, but let’s not pretend political concern alone is enough. If the state shareholder is serious, it should use its influence to block any closure-led solution and support a restructuring that keeps production in Germany.

That means:
- backing binding site and job guarantees,
- insisting on industrial investment,
- and refusing to normalize plant closures as the first instrument.

You cannot stand there as an anchor shareholder and then allow a process that tears apart the very regional industrial base you say you want to defend.

## To the controlling family
You say closures should be last resort. I agree with that part. But “last resort” has to mean something real, not a backdoor path to the same outcome after a few months of pressure.

If the owners truly want long-term value, then they should support:
- a binding no-closure settlement,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a future plan built on investment and internal transformation.

That would be a durable value strategy. What destroys value is using the threat of closure to force the workforce into concessions and then calling the result “stability.”

## To institutional investors
You keep saying you need credible cost reduction and margin improvement. I understand that. But you are acting as if the only credible route is cutting plants and jobs.

A package is credible if it:
- preserves the company’s productive core,
- secures labour peace,
- protects know-how,
- maintains quality,
- and delivers durable efficiency without social destruction.

A deal that triggers prolonged strikes, destroys trust, and wipes out skilled capacity is not “discipline.” It is self-harm.

You say you want risk-adjusted value. Then factor in the risk of industrial conflict, damaged brand reputation, loss of skilled workers, and long-term weakness in the German base.

## My bottom line
I am prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time reductions,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- and management/owner contributions,

**but only under binding guarantees that every German plant stays open and no one is forced out.**

If management cannot accept that, then there is no settlement from our side. We will resist in the supervisory board, at the bargaining table, and in the plants.

So here is the real question for management and the owners:

**Are you prepared to commit to a non-closure, no-forced-layoff restructuring with binding guarantees through 2030, or are you insisting on confrontation?**

Because if you insist on closures and compulsory redundancies, then you should expect the workforce to fight this with everything we have.
