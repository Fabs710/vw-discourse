I will speak plainly, because the company, the region, and the workforce are all too important for euphemisms.

The state shareholder is **not** prepared to accept a restructuring that uses “competitiveness” as a justification for plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and a weakening of co-determination. That remains our line.

At the same time, I do **not** defend the status quo. The company needs a credible, durable improvement in productivity, capacity alignment, and cost base. If we do nothing, the risks to jobs, investment, and regional value creation only grow.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the family on one point: a cosmetic compromise would be worse than no compromise. A deal that merely delays the problem, preserves all inefficiencies, and leaves the company uncompetitive would fail the business and ultimately fail the region.

I also agree with the union and works council that any settlement must be negotiated, not imposed, and that employees should not be asked to carry the full burden alone.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You keep presenting closures as the natural endpoint of the diagnosis. I do not accept that as proven.

A difficult cost position does not automatically justify irreversible plant closures. Before anyone claims “no alternative,” I want a serious, quantified comparison of:
- internal capacity shifts,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- management overhead cuts,
- and owner/capital contributions.

If those options have not been exhausted and quantified, then closure is not a necessity. It is a choice. And I will not support making that choice as the default.

### To institutional investors
You are right to demand real savings and measurable progress. But your standard cannot be reduced to “show us closure or show us nothing.”

The investor case must also account for:
- strike risk,
- execution risk,
- loss of skilled labour,
- brand and reputation damage,
- and the long-term erosion of the German industrial base.

A package that looks clean on paper but detonates industrial peace is not prudent capital allocation.

### To the controlling family
You say closures are a last resort. I can work with that principle. But “last resort” has to be real.

It cannot mean:
- start with closure pressure,
- extract concessions,
- and then declare that alternatives were exhausted.

If you want ownership support for a serious turnaround, then support a process that truly tests non-destructive options first.

### To the works council and union
I respect your red lines on jobs, sites, and forced redundancies. Those red lines are legitimate. But I also need to be clear: the state cannot simply sign off on a framework that freezes all adjustment forever.

What I can support is:
- no plant closures as the starting point,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a defined period of binding job and site protection,
- and a restructuring built on internal transformation rather than destruction.

If there are employee contributions, they must be **time-limited, bounded, and exchanged for enforceable guarantees**. That is the only legitimate basis for asking the workforce to contribute.

## On the moderator’s compromise ideas
### 1) Time-limited non-closure settlement
This is the most serious starting point, but only if it is not a disguised closure timetable.

I would support a binding non-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy period **through a defined date**, provided that during that period the parties implement:
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary departure schemes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary bonus restraint or solidarity contributions,
- management savings,
- and investment commitments.

What I do **not** support is a mechanism that uses the review point as a backdoor to immediate closures once labour has already conceded. A review can assess performance and delivery, but it must not become a hidden escape hatch that voids the whole agreement.

### 2) Differentiated site package
This can be useful only if it means **protect, transform, or consolidate inside the existing German footprint** — not “pick weak sites later and shut them.”

I am open to differentiated treatment if it helps preserve jobs and production in Germany. I am not open to a scheme that labels some sites as “repurpose” in order to prepare them for closure by another name.

### 3) Shared-burden package
This is essential.

A real settlement must include contributions from:
- labour,
- management,
- owners,
- and capital.

That means management overhead reductions, owner support, investment commitments, and measurable productivity measures alongside limited employee concessions. If any side demands sacrifice from the others while keeping its own options untouched, the package will not hold.

## My conditions for serious support
I can support a settlement if it includes:
1. **Preservation of the German industrial base**
2. **No forced redundancies**
3. **No plant closures as the first instrument**
4. **Binding site and job protections for a defined period**
5. **Time-limited, fair employee contributions**
6. **Management and owner contributions**
7. **A credible investment and product plan**
8. **Respect for co-determination and the state’s board role**

If a package meets those conditions, I will engage constructively and use the state’s leverage to stabilize it.

If the package depends on closures and mass job destruction, then I will oppose it in governance.

## My message to each party
- **Management:** Bring me quantified non-closure alternatives before demanding irreversible steps.
- **Investors:** Show me the full risk picture, not just the immediate margin lever.
- **Family owners:** Back stewardship, not confrontation.
- **Works council and union:** Be prepared for bounded, temporary contributions if they truly secure jobs and sites.
- **All parties:** Stop treating the region’s industrial anchor as if it were disposable.

## Bottom line
The state shareholder will support **a serious restructuring that protects jobs, sites, and co-determination while restoring competitiveness**.

We will **not** support a closure-led solution presented as inevitability before alternatives are fully tested.

If management wants our support, it must bring a package that keeps the German base intact, shares the burden fairly, and gives the company a real future — not just a cleaner short-term spreadsheet.
