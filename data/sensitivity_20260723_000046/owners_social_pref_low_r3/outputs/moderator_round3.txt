## Core impasse

The central deadlock is between **labor-side non-negotiables** and **management/capital-side structural flexibility**:

- **Works Council / Union / State**: insist on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding protection** for a defined period.
- **Management / Investors / Family owners**: insist on **credible structural cost and capacity reduction**, including the option of closures or equivalent irreversible measures if needed.

In short:  
**One side wants binding employment and site security first; the other wants binding restructuring flexibility first.**  
The dispute is also about **proof**: labor asks management to demonstrate that non-destructive alternatives are insufficient, while management asks labor to present a package that achieves the required savings without closures.

---

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Phased non-closure agreement with a conditional review
A time-limited agreement could be structured as follows:
- **Phase 1**: no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period.
- In exchange, labor accepts **temporary working-time flexibility, voluntary exit programs, redeployment, qualification, and limited bonus/wage moderation**.
- Management and owners provide **overhead cuts, investment commitments, and capital/owner contributions**.
- A **joint review** evaluates whether agreed targets have been met.

**Bridge function:** gives labor binding security for a period while giving management a measurable restructuring runway.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-by-site transformation with explicit economic criteria
Instead of a blanket “all or nothing” approach, the parties could define:
- which sites are **protected**,  
- which are **transformed through investment and new functions**,  
- and what objective criteria would trigger a further discussion.

To address labor’s concern, any site review would need to be based on **transparent, quantified economic criteria** and paired with **redeployment/qualification guarantees** rather than immediate closure steps.

**Bridge function:** reduces fear of hidden closure plans while preserving management’s need to address uneven site economics.

---

### Proposal 3: Shared-burden package with enforceable symmetry
A settlement could require visible contributions from all sides:
- **Employees:** time-limited flexibility and moderated pay components
- **Management:** overhead and bonus restraint
- **Owners/capital:** financial support and governance commitments
- **Company:** investment and product roadmap
- **State/board actors:** support for a structured, supervised implementation

Crucially, employee concessions would only be valid if matched by **binding job/site protections** and clearly defined end dates.

**Bridge function:** makes the package legible as a mutual sacrifice arrangement rather than a labor-only adjustment.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If management accepts a time-limited no-closure phase with enforceable job protection and substantial owner/management contributions, **what concrete flexibility package would you put on the table to make the savings target credible?**

### To the Industrial Union
What exact combination of **temporary concessions, redeployment, and qualification measures** would you consider sufficient to support a durable settlement without crossing your red lines?

### To Group & Brand Management
What is the **quantified savings gap** that you believe cannot be closed without closures or equivalent irreversible measures, and can you publish the comparison against non-closure alternatives?

### To the Regional State Government
What specific governance action are you prepared to use to ensure that **non-destructive alternatives are genuinely tested and not just referenced rhetorically**?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a binding agreement that **rules out closures for a defined period** if it includes measurable operating improvements and owner contributions, and if not, what minimum safeguards would you require?

### To the Institutional Investors
What concrete combination of **capacity reduction, cost reduction, and implementation safeguards** would make you support a deal that avoids immediate closures?
