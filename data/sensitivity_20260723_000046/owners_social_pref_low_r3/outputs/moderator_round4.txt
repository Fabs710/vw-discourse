### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **labour’s demand for binding, upfront protection against closures and forced redundancies** and **management/investor insistence on retaining credible structural options, including closures, if targets are missed**.

In practical terms, the parties disagree on four linked questions:
1. **Whether closures must be ruled out in advance or remain available as a fallback**
2. **Whether employee concessions can be given without a closure “trapdoor” in the review mechanism**
3. **Whether the restructuring can be done entirely inside the existing German footprint**
4. **Whether “durable savings” require site consolidation/capacity reduction or can be achieved through internal adjustment plus shared burden**

A secondary but important fault line is **trust**: labour and the state do not trust management to use a review process fairly, while management and investors do not trust a permanent no-closure framework to deliver sufficient competitiveness.

---

### Possible compromise positions
Below are three facilitator-style compromise options that could bridge the most conflicting stakeholders:

#### 1) Time-limited non-closure pact with hard, pre-agreed trigger metrics
- All parties agree to a fixed initial phase with:
  - no plant closures
  - no compulsory redundancies
  - temporary working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment and qualification
  - management overhead cuts
  - owner contributions
- In exchange, management receives a **defined review point** with **objective, pre-agreed financial and operational metrics**.
- Crucially: the review would **not automatically authorize closures**; it would only reopen negotiation if the metrics are not met.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It gives labour short-term security while preserving management/investor credibility through measurable milestones.

---

#### 2) Differentiated site strategy inside a protected footprint
- The parties agree that **all German plants remain open during the negotiated period**, but operations, product lines, and investment levels may be redistributed site by site.
- Sites can be “protected,” “transformed,” or “repositioned,” but **no site is designated for closure during the protection period**.
- Any later change would require a fresh quantified case and renewed bargaining.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It addresses management’s and investors’ demand for differentiation and capacity adjustment without triggering the immediate closure conflict.

---

#### 3) Symmetrical shared-burden package with matched guarantees
- Employee concessions are paired with equal or visible contributions from:
  - management
  - owners
  - capital
- The package includes:
  - temporary labour flexibility
  - voluntary programs
  - management pay/overhead reductions
  - owner capital support
  - investment commitments
  - a legally enforceable job/security framework for a defined period

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It reframes the dispute from “who sacrifices first” to “what each side contributes in proportion,” which may help the union, works council, and state engage without surrendering red lines.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If a package contained **binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy guarantees for a defined period**, plus measurable management and owner contributions, what specific flexibility measures would you be prepared to trade in return?

#### To the Industrial Union
What exact form of **review mechanism** would you consider acceptable if it were tied to objective metrics, time-limited employee concessions, and a prohibition on automatic closure triggers?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What is the **quantified savings bridge** showing how much comes from internal measures, how much from owner/management contributions, and how much additional benefit would only be achieved through closures or equivalent irreversible steps?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete governance conditions would you require before you would consider a restructuring credible **without** immediate closures, and at what point would you regard non-closure alternatives as sufficiently exhausted?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you be willing to commit to **specific owner contributions and investment guarantees** in exchange for a time-limited no-closure framework, even if that reduces short-term strategic flexibility?

#### To the Institutional Investors
Would you accept a restructuring that **does not include closures in phase one** if it is backed by measurable cost reductions, capacity adjustments, and a clearly defined trigger-based review later?
