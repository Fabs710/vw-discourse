I will be direct.

## My position
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will **not** support a restructuring that:
- destroys German jobs on a large scale,
- treats plant closures in the state as the default solution,
- or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.

I am open to a hard restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices the region to solve the problem.

---

## To management
You are right about one thing: **doing nothing is not an option**. I agree the company needs durable savings and a credible path back to competitiveness.

Where I disagree is your continuing refusal to treat a **site-preserving alternative** as the baseline test. You keep saying closure remains on the table because the problem is structural. That may be true in abstract, but it does **not** justify making closures your starting point.

The expert has now confirmed something important:
- a **zero-closure solution is possible in principle**,
- but it must be **deep and durable**,
- and we do **not** yet have proof that it cannot work.

That means you have **not** carried the burden of proving closures are necessary.  
So I reject any attempt to present plant closures as the only serious route.

What I would support:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- overhead reduction,
- and a limited employee contribution,

**if and only if** they are paired with binding commitments on:
- German investment,
- product allocation,
- site security,
- and a real role for co-determination in implementation.

What I will not support:
- blanket closure threats,
- compulsory redundancies as an ordinary instrument,
- or a package that weakens the company’s regional anchor function.

---

## To the Works Council
I agree with your core concern: workers and regions must **not** be the sole shock absorber.

I also agree that:
- voluntary and socially managed measures should come first,
- compulsory redundancies should be avoided as far as possible,
- and any workforce contribution must be matched by real commitments from management and owners.

Where I do **not** agree is if “no closures” is treated as an absolute without any regard to economics. I cannot give a blank cheque for the current footprint.

But the reverse is also true: management cannot use “economics” as a shortcut to close plants before they have proven a site-preserving package cannot work.

So the correct next step is not rhetoric. It is a **site-by-site comparison**. The burden is on management to show why non-closure measures fall short.

[SUMMON: independent site-level financial and capacity model to compare zero-closure, partial rationalisation, and closure-based scenarios by plant, including savings, one-off costs, utilization, employment impact, and durability.]

---

## To the union
I support your insistence that employees should not carry the entire burden.

I agree that temporary concessions alone are not enough if they are merely used to delay a structural problem. But that does **not** mean closures are the answer.

What I would back is a **shared-sacrifice package** that is:
- limited,
- time-bound where appropriate,
- and matched by binding commitments on investment and future workload.

What I will not back is a package that:
- weakens job security,
- opens the door to compulsory redundancies,
- or dismantles the collective framework while calling it modernization.

If the company wants labor’s contribution, then labor must receive real security in return.

---

## To the controlling family holding
I agree that long-term enterprise value matters. I also agree that delay can destroy value.

But I do **not** accept the idea that the only way to protect value is to keep closures and workforce cuts permanently available as leverage. Value also comes from trust, industrial peace, and preserving the company’s legitimacy in its home region.

A company that tears up its social foundation may look more flexible on paper, but it becomes more fragile in reality.

So yes: we need competitiveness.  
No: we do **not** need to destroy the regional base to get there.

---

## To investors
You are right to ask for measurable, durable savings. I support that.

But I will not endorse a settlement that equates “durable” with “closure.” Durable savings can also come from:
- permanent capacity adjustment without plant shutdowns,
- productivity gains,
- portfolio concentration,
- overhead reduction,
- and negotiated labor contributions that are credible and enforceable.

What I need from you is the same discipline you demand from others:
- quantified savings,
- implementation risk,
- timing,
- and proof that the company remains a viable regional anchor after the restructuring.

If a site-preserving solution can close the gap, it deserves support. If it cannot, that has to be demonstrated — not assumed.

---

## What I insist on
My line is straightforward:

- **No outcome that guts regional employment**
- **No outcome that removes the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **No closure-first restructuring**
- **Yes to a negotiated solution that preserves sites and jobs as far as possible while restoring competitiveness**

That is not obstruction. That is responsible ownership and political stewardship.

---

## What would move me
I could support a harder package if it is:
- quantitatively proven,
- socially balanced,
- reversible where possible,
- and tied to binding commitments on German investment, products, and future workload.

I will not be moved by abstract claims that closures are “the only real solution” unless that claim is backed by a site-level model.

---

## Bottom line
I am prepared to support a serious restructuring.

I am **not** prepared to support one that turns German plants and regional employment into expendable variables.

If management wants my support, bring a **quantified, site-by-site alternative comparison** that proves why closures are necessary — or show that a site-preserving package can truly deliver the same durable effect. Until then, I will continue to resist closure-led restructuring and defend the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.
