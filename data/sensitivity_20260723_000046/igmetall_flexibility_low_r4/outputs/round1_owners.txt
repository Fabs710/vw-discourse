I appreciate the positions set out so far. Let me be equally clear from the perspective of the controlling family holding.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on the core diagnosis: **the company needs a durable improvement in competitiveness and margins**. If the cost base remains structurally uncompetitive, then neither investment nor long-term employment can be protected. Pretending otherwise would be irresponsible stewardship.

I also agree with the state government and the workforce representatives on one important point: **a purely confrontational solution would be value-destructive**. A prolonged conflict, public escalation, and damage to the brand or supplier network would hurt everyone, including the owners.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** support an outcome that simply preserves the status quo and calls that a compromise.

- **No plant closures** as an absolute line is not something I can endorse in advance.
- **No compulsory redundancies** may be a desirable goal, but only if it is part of a package that truly restores competitiveness.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030** are not credible if they lock in an uncompetitive structure.
- And I will be blunt: **an agreement that protects every site and every existing pattern of employment while only asking for temporary contributions is not sufficient**.

From an ownership perspective, that would amount to postponing the problem and eroding the enterprise.

### What I need to see
I am prepared to support a settlement if it delivers three things:

1. **A durable cost reduction**
2. **A credible capacity adjustment**
3. **A stable industrial peace**

That means I can support alternatives to closures **only if they are economically equivalent in result**, not merely politically more comfortable. If working-time measures, voluntary exits, redeployment, and labor-cost adjustments can actually produce the necessary savings and structural flexibility, then those deserve serious consideration. But they must be real, not symbolic.

### On the employee side
I recognize that employees have already carried a large share of the transformation burden. That matters. But it does not change the underlying economics. If the company’s structure is misaligned with the market, then all stakeholders will have to contribute.

What I cannot accept is a model where:
- the workforce contributes,
- the owners remain passive,
- and the company still lacks the structural room to compete.

That would not be stewardship.

### On plant closures
I do not say plant closures are the preferred route. They are not. They are a serious last-resort instrument. But I also will not rule them out in advance if no credible alternative is brought forward. That would be irresponsible to the company and to the family’s long-term ownership interest.

### My position
So my line is this:

- I support a **negotiated restructuring**.
- I support **socially responsible solutions where they are economically durable**.
- I do **not** support a deal that merely defers hard choices.
- I do **not** support preserving all current structures at the expense of competitiveness.

If the works council, union, management, and state can bring forward a package that achieves real savings, protects as much employment as possible, and restores the company’s future, I will back it. If not, then the board must be prepared to consider more difficult structural measures.

The family holding will judge this on long-term enterprise value, not on short-term optics.
