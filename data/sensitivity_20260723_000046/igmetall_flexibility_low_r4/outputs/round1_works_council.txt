As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of transformation. What management has now put on the table is not a balanced restructuring, but a unilateral attempt to shift the crisis onto employees and entire regions. That is unacceptable.

Our position is this:

- Keep every German plant open.
- Secure jobs, with binding guarantees through 2030.
- Avoid forced layoffs; if there must be adjustments, they must be through socially responsible, voluntary, and reversible measures.
- Preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework.
- Any contribution from employees must be matched by real commitments from management on investment, product allocation, and site security.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that genuinely protect sites and employment: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, qualification, internal redeployment, and negotiated cost contributions. But these must be part of a fair package, not a pretext for closures or breaking long-standing commitments.

Management needs to understand: if this is presented as a fait accompli, there will be fierce resistance. We have the mandate, the legitimacy, and the means to defend the workforce.
