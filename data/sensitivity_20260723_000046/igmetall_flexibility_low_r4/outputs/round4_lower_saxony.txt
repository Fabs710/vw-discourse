I will speak plainly.

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis that **doing nothing is not an option**. I also agree that the company needs **durable savings**, not cosmetic measures. Where I do **not** agree is your repeated move to make closures and compulsory redundancies the default answer before the site-by-site proof exists.

You keep saying the footprint is uncompetitive. That may well be true. But you have **not yet demonstrated** that a site-preserving restructuring cannot close the gap. The expert has said a **zero-closure solution is possible in principle** and that the real question is durability and scale. That means the burden is on you to prove necessity, not on labour or the state to surrender pre-emptively.

I support:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- overhead reductions,
- and binding investment/product commitments.

I do **not** support:
- a blanket closure option presented as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as a routine tool,
- or guarantees being dismissed as “unrealistic” before the negotiation has even matured.

If you want my support, bring the **plant-by-plant numbers** and show me why non-closure measures are insufficient. Until then, closures remain a last resort, not a mandate.

## To the Works Council and union
I agree with your core concern: workers must not be the sole shock absorber. I also agree that **temporary concessions alone are not enough** if the structural problem is real. Where I am firmer is this: if the company needs adjustment, we cannot hide behind absolute language forever.

What I can support is a **negotiated, site-preserving restructuring** with:
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- qualification,
- limited and time-bound employee contributions,
- and binding commitments on investment and future product allocation.

What I cannot support is any deal where the state’s regional role or co-determination is hollowed out, or where German plants are sacrificed without a proven necessity.

But I also have to be honest with you: if the numbers ultimately show that some rationalization is unavoidable, then we should be at the table shaping it — not pretending the business model can be frozen in place.

## To the controlling family holding
I understand your focus on enterprise value and durable competitiveness. That is legitimate. But I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible route to value preservation is to keep closure pressure alive from day one.

A company like this does not only live on margin math. It also depends on:
- trust,
- industrial peace,
- regional legitimacy,
- and a stable social partnership.

Destroying that foundation is not value-preserving; it can be value-destructive.

So yes, I support a hard restructuring. But no, I will not support a restructuring that treats the company’s German base as disposable before the alternatives are genuinely tested.

## To investors
You are right to insist on quantified savings and a path back to target returns. I support that discipline. But I do not share your assumption that the **most aggressive footprint cut** is automatically the best value solution.

A site-preserving package can sometimes outperform a closure-led approach if it reduces conflict, preserves capacity where needed, and avoids legal, political, and industrial-relations delay. That must be tested properly. Not assumed.

## What I want
I want a solution that:
- protects jobs and plants in the state as far as economically possible,
- preserves the company as a regional anchor,
- restores competitiveness,
- and keeps the state’s co-determination and veto role intact.

That means:
1. **no blanket closure mandate**
2. **no compulsory redundancies as a first-line instrument**
3. **a quantified site-by-site comparison**
4. **a serious negotiated package of internal adjustment first**
5. **a true backstop only if the numbers later prove it unavoidable**

## [SUMMON: independent site-level restructuring model]
We need an independent site-level model that compares, by plant:
- annual savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- payback period,
- capacity removed,
- utilization effects,
- workforce impact,
- and durability of savings,

for:
1. zero-closure restructuring,
2. partial rationalization,
3. closure-based restructuring.

That model must answer one question:
**Can a site-preserving package deliver enough durable savings, fast enough, without gutting regional employment?**

## Bottom line
I am not moving to a closure-first position.

I am open to a serious restructuring, including difficult employee contributions, **if and only if** it preserves sites and jobs as far as possible and keeps the company anchored in the region. If management wants more than that, it must prove why the alternatives fail.

Until then, my position is clear:
- protect the regional economic anchor,
- protect co-determination,
- and reject closures and forced layoffs as the starting point.
