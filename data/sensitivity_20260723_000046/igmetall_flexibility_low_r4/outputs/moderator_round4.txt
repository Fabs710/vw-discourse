### Core impasse
The deadlock is not over **whether restructuring is needed**. All sides now accept that a structural problem exists.

The impasse is over **the sequence and proof standard**:

1. **Employee side / state side:**  
   No closures or compulsory redundancies unless management proves, plant by plant, that a site-preserving package fails.

2. **Management / investors:**  
   A site-preserving package is only acceptable if it delivers **durable, sufficient, and fast** fixed-cost relief; otherwise closure-based rationalization must remain available.

3. **Underlying conflict:**  
   Whether closure is a **premature bargaining threat** or a **legitimate last-resort instrument** before the site-level model is completed.

So the core blocker is:
**No shared agreement on what evidence is required before closures can be considered, and no shared fallback if the proof is inconclusive.**

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited, proof-gated no-closure phase
A structured first phase could be established in which all parties commit to a **defined non-closure package** for a fixed period, while an independent site-level model is completed.

**Elements:**
- voluntary exits only
- redeployment and qualification
- working-time flexibility
- temporary employee contribution
- overhead and productivity measures
- binding investment/product review

**Safeguard:**
- closures and compulsory redundancies are suspended during the proof phase
- the independent model must report on each plant by a fixed deadline
- if targets are missed, the parties reopen negotiations on structural options

This is a bridge between “no closure now” and “closure remains on the table later.”

---

#### Proposal 2: Site-preservation first, with narrowly defined structural fallback
A compromise could preserve the employee demand for no blanket closure while allowing management a limited fallback if specific, objective conditions are not met.

**Elements:**
- each plant is assigned a target for savings, utilization, and transformation contribution
- non-closure measures are implemented first
- only sites that fail the agreed metrics enter a second-round review
- any fallback is limited to **partial rationalization**, not immediate full closure

This narrows the conflict by replacing a broad closure debate with a **plant-specific performance test**.

---

#### Proposal 3: Guaranteed employment/security package in exchange for quantified contributions
A deal could combine stronger job security with measurable employee and employer commitments.

**Elements:**
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
- no closures during that period
- employee contribution through working time, pay components, or bonus restraint
- management commitment to product allocation, investment, and capacity utilization measures
- joint monitoring body with worker, management, shareholder, and state participation

**Review condition:**
- if cost targets are not met, the parties do not automatically move to closure; they first exhaust a defined menu of further internal adjustments

This creates a structured bargaining path without treating closure as the immediate default.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact plant-level threshold would you accept as proof that a non-closure package is insufficient, and are you prepared to define that threshold before the model is run?**

#### To the works council
**What specific internal-adjustment measures, if quantified and independently verified as insufficient, would you accept as the point where the discussion must move beyond zero-closure?**

#### To the union
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure phase if it included binding employee contributions and an independent trigger-based review, and if not, what minimum protections would make that acceptable?**

#### To the state government
**Are you prepared to support a structured no-closure phase only if it is tied to measurable site performance, even if that means accepting limited fallback options later?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**Will you commit to withholding any closure-backed pressure until the independent site-level model has been completed and jointly reviewed?**

#### To institutional investors
**Would you support a site-preserving restructuring if it demonstrably delivers the same net present value as closure-based rationalization, even if the payback is slightly slower?**

#### To the expert
**What single metric should determine whether the no-closure scenario is “good enough”: annual recurring savings, payback period, utilization improvement, or net present value after transition costs?**
