As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: **we share the need for a competitive restructuring, but we do not accept a solution that destroys regional employment or treats plant closures in Germany as the default answer.**

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The cost base, market pressure, and investment needs are real. If the company is to remain a strong industrial anchor, it needs a credible long-term path back to competitiveness.

I also agree with the Works Council and the union that **workers must not be made the sole adjustment variable**. The workforce has already contributed heavily to transformation. A fair settlement must involve management, owners, and the company’s strategic choices as well.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that closures and deep job cuts are simply the inevitable starting point.

- **Plant closures in Germany would be a last resort, not a premise.**
- **Forced redundancies are not a socially acceptable restructuring tool here.**
- **A settlement that strips out jobs without a durable alternative for the affected regions is not acceptable.**
- And I will say plainly: **anything that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking role is off the table.**

This company is not just another asset on a spreadsheet. It is a major part of the regional economy, with long-term responsibility to employees, suppliers, and the industrial fabric around the plants.

### What I would support
I am open to a **negotiated restructuring that protects sites and preserves as many jobs as possible**, if it is genuinely credible and durable. That means:

1. **Capacity reductions where necessary, but not automatic plant closures**
2. **Working-time and flexibility solutions**
3. **Voluntary measures before involuntary ones**
4. **Temporary employee contributions only if matched by binding commitments**
5. **Clear investment and future-product guarantees for German sites**
6. **A social-partnership framework with real enforcement and monitoring**

### My message to management
If management wants support from the state as a shareholder and political actor, then it must bring forward a package that is **economically credible and socially sustainable**. A proposal that simply shifts costs onto employees and local communities will not get my support.

If there are alternative measures that achieve comparable savings without shutting plants, then that is the path we should pursue. If not, then management must explain why its own strategic and investment decisions have not produced the flexibility it now demands from the workforce.

### My message to the employee side
I also ask the workforce representatives to stay open to a serious restructuring discussion. Protecting every detail of the status quo may not be feasible. But any contribution from employees must be part of a **balanced package**, not a one-sided surrender.

### Bottom line
I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring built around plant closures and mass layoffs in Germany.

I **am** prepared to back a negotiated solution that:
- preserves sites where possible,
- secures employment as far as feasible,
- restores competitiveness,
- and keeps the company anchored in the region for the long term.

That is the line I will defend in the supervisory board.
