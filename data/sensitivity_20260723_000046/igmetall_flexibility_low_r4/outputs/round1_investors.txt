From the perspective of dispersed institutional investors, the key test is simple: **does the outcome credibly restore competitive margins and protect enterprise value?** Everything else is secondary.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the state on one essential point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The current cost base, especially in Germany, appears too high relative to market realities. If margins remain structurally weak, the equity story deteriorates further and so does access to capital.

We also agree with the workforce side on one point: **a prolonged, destructive conflict would be value-destructive**. A drawn-out strike, public escalation, and uncertainty over plant operations would damage earnings, the brand, and investor confidence. That is not a good outcome for shareholders.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that preserves the existing cost structure and simply labels it “socially balanced.”

- **No closures, no compulsory redundancies, no deep labour-cost contribution** is not credible if the company’s cost base is truly uncompetitive.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030** are only acceptable if they do not lock in structural underperformance.
- **Temporary or cosmetic savings** will not be enough if they leave margins impaired.

In short: we are not interested in optics. We are interested in **durable financial repair**.

### Our position
We will support **whatever package can be shown to deliver real, measurable, durable cost reduction** and a clear path back to target margins. That could include:
- plant closures,
- capacity reduction,
- workforce reductions,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- employee contributions,
- or a combination of these.

We are **not** attached to any particular instrument. We are attached to the result.

### On the employee side
If the union and works council can produce an alternative that genuinely matches the savings and durability of closures, then it deserves consideration. But it must be more than a promise:
- it must be quantified,
- it must be credible,
- and it must be enforceable.

If the alternative only delays the pain while leaving the structure uncompetitive, we would regard that as unacceptable. That would just defer the repricing of the company.

### On management and owners
Management must be careful not to ask for broad shareholder support while offering only vague assurances. Investors need clarity on:
- the size of the savings,
- the timing,
- the implementation risk,
- and the impact on return on sales and free cash flow.

The controlling family and the state may want to limit visible disruption, but they also need to recognise that **value destruction through delay is still destruction**. A deal that protects every local interest but leaves the business weak is not a win for shareholders.

### Bottom line
We are **open to a negotiated solution**, but only if it is:
1. **credible**
2. **durable**
3. **quantified**
4. **sufficient to restore competitiveness**

If not, then we would rather see a harder restructuring than a cosmetic compromise that leaves margins structurally impaired.
