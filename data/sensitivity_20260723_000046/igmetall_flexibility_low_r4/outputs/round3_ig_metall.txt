I’ll be equally direct.

## To management
You still have **not** shown that closures or compulsory redundancies are necessary. You keep repeating “structural problem” as if that settles the question. It does not.

What the expert has said is more precise:
- **zero-closure is possible in principle**,
- but it must be **deep and durable** to work,
- and we do **not** yet have the site-level proof that it cannot close the gap.

So if you want to argue for closures, bring the numbers:
- savings by plant,
- capacity removed,
- utilization effects,
- timing,
- and durability.

Until then, your closure talk is just leverage. We do not accept that as good-faith bargaining.

## To the works council
I support your core line: **no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of the collective framework, and no fake “voluntary” layoffs in disguise**.

Where I want to sharpen the union position is this:
- The workforce is prepared to contribute,
- but only in a package that is **shared, limited, and matched**.

That means:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint,
- foregone bonuses,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a transformation contribution.

But not at any price. Not if the outcome is hidden layoffs, open-ended insecurity, or one-sided pain on workers alone.

## To the state government
Your resistance to closures matters, but “closures as a last resort” is not enough if management keeps using them as a threat every time the talks get difficult.

If you want a serious alternative, use your influence to push for:
- **binding site commitments**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **real investment commitments**,
- and a package that keeps German plants open unless there is independently demonstrated necessity otherwise.

You cannot ask workers to trust a process that starts by threatening them with closure.

## To the controlling family and investors
I understand your concern for durability and value. We share the need for a competitive company. But value is not protected by forcing the workforce to absorb all the pain while management keeps the strategic upside.

A durable settlement must be:
- economically real,
- socially balanced,
- and enforceable.

If you want our cooperation, then support a package that protects jobs and sites while still delivering real savings. Do not confuse “hard” with “fair.”

## What we will support
We are prepared to negotiate a serious package built on:
1. **no German plant closures**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **capacity reduction through internal adjustment**
4. **working-time changes**
5. **temporary employee contribution**
6. **voluntary exits only**
7. **redeployment and qualification**
8. **binding investment and product commitments**
9. **protection of the collective bargaining framework**

That is the framework for a settlement. Not closures. Not forced layoffs. Not a one-sided rewrite of labour standards.

## What we reject
Let me be crystal clear:

- **No closures as a bargaining weapon**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No “temporary” concessions that become permanent worker losses**
- **No deal where employees pay, but management and owners do not**
- **No settlement that leaves the company with a broken collective wage framework**

If management presents a package that violates these limits, then we will oppose it.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert has confirmed what we have been saying all along:
- non-closure measures can materially contribute,
- the real issue is whether management wants to do the hard work of designing a credible alternative,
- and closures are not proven unavoidable.

That means the burden is on management and owners to demonstrate why a site-preserving package cannot work. So far, they have not done that.

- zero-closure restructuring,

including:
- annual savings,

- capacity removed,

## Bottom line
The union will not sign up to a restructuring in which workers bear the pain, sites are threatened, and the collective wage framework is weakened.

If management wants a deal, bring forward a **shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs and plants, with management and owners contributing alongside employees.

If not, then expect escalation.
