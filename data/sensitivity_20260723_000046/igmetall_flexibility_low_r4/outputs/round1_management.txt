I hear the clear line from both the Works Council and the union: **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral cost shifting onto employees**.

Let me be equally clear from management’s side: **the current cost base is not competitive, and that is not sustainable**. We are facing a shrinking European market, severe competition, and German factory costs that are too high relative to the targets we must hit. If we do nothing structural, we will not protect the future of the brand, the investment program, or ultimately the jobs that remain.

So I agree with you on one point: this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. But I strongly disagree with the idea that every plant must be protected regardless of economics.

### Where I disagree
- **Keeping every German plant open is not a credible starting point.**
- **Binding guarantees through 2030 without a structural cost contribution are not possible.**
- **Voluntary-only measures will not be enough** if they do not deliver durable savings at the scale required.
- **A temporary contribution alone is insufficient** unless it materially and sustainably lowers the cost base.

### What management requires
We need a package that delivers:
1. **A substantial and durable reduction in capacity**
2. **A meaningful labour-cost contribution**
3. **Structural cost relief, not just deferrals**
4. **Freedom to reallocate investment to future products and technology**

That can be achieved through different mechanisms, and I am open on the exact form. But the result must be real. If plant closures are the only way to achieve it, then we cannot rule them out in advance. If there are alternative measures that achieve comparable savings with real durability, then we will examine them seriously.

### On your proposed alternatives
I am prepared to discuss:
- capacity reduction
- working-time flexibility
- internal redeployment
- voluntary separation tools
- targeted qualification and transformation measures

But I will be direct: **these alternatives are only acceptable if they produce a cost and capacity effect comparable to closures**. Anything that preserves the current structure while adding temporary concessions is not enough.

### My line
Management is not asking the workforce to “carry the whole burden” alone. But we are saying that the workforce contribution must be part of a **structural settlement**, not a symbolic one.

So the question is not whether there will be change. The question is whether the employee side is prepared to engage on a package that actually restores competitiveness.

If you want us to rule out closures, then bring forward a credible alternative that matches the savings, timing, and durability of a structural site adjustment. Otherwise, we will have to proceed on the basis that all options remain open.
