I agree with the Works Council on the core line: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is non-negotiable from the union side as well.

Management’s framing so far is one-sided. It treats the workforce as the adjustment variable while leaving open whether owners, management, and the company’s capital allocation strategy contribute enough. That is not a fair restructuring. Employees have already taken transformation risk for years; they should not now be asked to absorb the full burden of a cost crisis created by structural market pressures, investment decisions, and strategic missteps.

Where we can engage:
- **Capacity reductions** can be discussed, but only if they are designed to protect sites and jobs.
- **Working-time measures**, flexible shifts, voluntary reductions, qualification, and internal transfers are all on the table.
- **A labour-cost contribution** is possible, but it must be temporary, negotiated, and tied to concrete guarantees.
- Any sacrifice by employees must be matched by **management and owners** through investment commitments, product allocation, and a credible long-term site strategy.

Where I disagree firmly:
- We will not accept a restructuring logic that starts with plant closures.
- We will not accept forced layoffs disguised as “socially responsible adjustment.”
- We will not accept the dismantling of the collective wage framework as a unilateral cost-cutting tool.

If management wants a durable settlement, then it needs to come with a package that shares the pain fairly:
1. **Job security and site guarantees**
2. **No closures**
3. **No compulsory redundancies**
4. **Employee contribution only in exchange for binding commitments**
5. **Clear investment and future-product commitments for the German sites**

If that is not on the table, then the conflict will escalate. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce will not wait indefinitely while management tries to push through a restructuring that externalizes all risk onto employees.
