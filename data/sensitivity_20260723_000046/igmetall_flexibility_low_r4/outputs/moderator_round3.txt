### Core impasse

The deadlock is now concentrated on **proof versus red lines**:

- **Employee side** insists that **no closures and no compulsory redundancies** are non-negotiable, unless management proves site-preserving options cannot work.
- **Management and capital providers** insist that **closures and workforce reductions must remain available** if they are needed to achieve durable savings.
- **The state government and controlling family** are trying to avoid both a value-destructive confrontation and a politically unacceptable closure-led outcome.

So the central impasse is not simply “closure vs no closure.” It is:

> **Can the parties agree on a restructuring package that is economically sufficient without making closures the default, while still keeping closure as a backstop if quantified evidence later shows it is unavoidable?**

A second layer of impasse is **trust**:
- workers do not trust closures will stay a last resort;
- owners/investors do not trust that temporary labor concessions will be durable enough;
- the state does not trust either side to absorb the wider political cost of failure.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the most conflicting stakeholders

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a proof-gated backstop
A possible bridge is a **two-step package**:

- **Phase 1:** implement a site-preserving restructuring package first  
  - voluntary exits  
  - working-time changes  
  - redeployment  
  - qualification  
  - temporary labor contributions  
  - overhead cuts  
  - investment and product commitments

- **Phase 2:** if the independent site-level model shows a remaining gap after Phase 1, then a **limited backstop mechanism** activates for further structural measures.

This could allow:
- works council and union to secure a genuine first attempt at no closures,
- management and investors to retain a defined fallback if the numbers do not close.

#### 2) No compulsory redundancies, but conditional structural rationalization
A second compromise is to separate the two most contested issues:

- agree **no compulsory redundancies for a defined period**
- but do **not** preclude all structural site measures permanently
- instead, allow only **agreed, quantified, and jointly reviewed rationalization measures** if the independent model shows they are necessary

This could preserve:
- worker protection in the near term,
- management’s ability to pursue structural correction if required,
- and the state’s preference for avoiding immediate closures.

#### 3) Site guarantees tied to measurable contributions and review clauses
A third possible bridge is to offer:
- **binding site and job-security guarantees for a limited horizon**
- in exchange for:
  - defined employee contributions,
  - productivity commitments,
  - and scheduled review points tied to agreed financial targets

This would make guarantees **conditional and time-bound**, rather than absolute, which may be more acceptable to owners and investors while still giving labor and the state a protected planning horizon.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What specific site-by-site savings threshold, if reached by non-closure measures, would cause you to rule out closures as unnecessary?**

#### To the works council
**Would you accept a time-bound no-closure commitment if it included a jointly audited trigger mechanism for reviewing further measures should the agreed savings not materialize?**

#### To the union
**Which employee concessions are you prepared to make permanently, and which are strictly temporary, so that management can assess whether the labor contribution is structurally sufficient?**

#### To the state government
**Are you prepared to support a conditional restructuring deal that protects sites in the first phase but allows limited structural adjustment later if an independent model proves it is unavoidable?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**Would you endorse a package that avoids closures initially, even if it requires a scheduled review and a pre-agreed escalation path should the financial targets not be met?**

#### To institutional investors
**What minimum level of permanent cost reduction would make a non-closure package acceptable to you, even if it includes time-bound labor protections and delayed structural decisions?**

#### To the expert
**What would be the clearest quantitative trigger in the site-level model that would distinguish “credible zero-closure restructuring” from “temporary postponement of an unavoidable closure or rationalization”?**
