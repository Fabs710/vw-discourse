I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state government, and even management on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable solution, not a symbolic one.

I also agree that any settlement must be:
- economically real,
- measurable,
- and stable over time.

And I agree that a prolonged conflict would hurt everyone — workers, plants, the brand, and the company’s future.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that “realism” means **plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

That is the line I will not cross.

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework as a one-sided cost-cutting instrument.**

If management keeps closure threats on the table as leverage, then it is not presenting a balanced restructuring; it is trying to force the workforce to pay for adjustment while keeping owners and management insulated.

That is not fair, and it will not produce durable industrial peace.

## On management’s argument
Management says the cost base is uncompetitive and must be brought down structurally. I am not denying that savings are needed.

But the conclusion cannot be: “therefore close plants.”

If the company wants a contribution from employees, then it must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**. That means:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary and socially managed exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- and binding commitments from management and owners.

What I reject is a package where:
- workers lose jobs,
- sites are shut,
- and the collective agreement is weakened,
while management calls that a fair restructuring.

## What I would consider
I remain open to a serious negotiated package if it includes:

1. **No closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity reduction through reversible and socially managed means**
4. **Temporary employee contribution**
5. **Meaningful management/owner contribution**
6. **Binding investment and product commitments for German sites**
7. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**

That contribution from employees can include:
- foregone bonuses,
- temporary wage restraint,
- flexibility on working time,
- a transformation fund,
- and voluntary reduction tools.

But it must be **matched**. Employees cannot be the only ones taking pain.

## To the Works Council
I support your core demand for job and site protection. I also support your insistence that any adjustment be voluntary and socially responsible.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: if management wants labour concessions, then we need **hard guarantees** in return. Not vague promises, not political statements — binding commitments.

## To the state government
Your resistance to closures and forced redundancies is important. Use your influence to push management toward a genuine alternative package, not a threat-based process that simply moves the burden onto workers.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right about one thing: the company needs durable competitiveness. But durable competitiveness cannot be built on destroying trust, breaking wage standards, and provoking open-ended conflict.

If you want stability, then support a settlement that protects the industrial base and shares sacrifice fairly. A deal that cuts only on labour while preserving strategic flexibility for everyone else is not sustainable.

## Bottom line
The union will engage seriously on:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary wage and bonus sacrifices,
- and a negotiated contribution package.

But we will **not** agree to:
- closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the erosion of the collective wage framework.

If management wants a settlement, it must come forward with a **quantified alternative** that achieves savings without forcing workers and regions to absorb all the damage.

If that is not possible, then we are prepared to escalate further. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce expects us to defend its interests decisively.
