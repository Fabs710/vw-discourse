1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties agree that **doing nothing is not an option**.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **durable, credible, and economically real**.
- Most parties accept that **some form of capacity adjustment, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and voluntary measures** should be explored.
- There is shared recognition that **a prolonged conflict would damage the company, employees, and shareholders**.
- Several parties stated openness to **employee contributions**, but only if paired with **binding commitments** on investment, product allocation, and site strategy.

2) **Core disagreements that remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works Council and union reject them outright.  
  - State government resists them and treats them as last resort.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors will not rule them out.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side rejects them.  
  - Others are less explicit, but none has accepted a full no-redundancy guarantee unless competitiveness is secured another way.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees:**  
  - Employee side seeks binding guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management, owners, and investors say that is not credible unless structural competitiveness is restored.
- **Degree of structural change required:**  
  - Employee and state sides want restructuring without closures if possible.  
  - Management, owners, and investors insist on savings at a scale comparable to closure-based restructuring.

3) **Key trade-offs still unaddressed**
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** how much employment protection is possible without leaving the cost base uncompetitive.
- **Site security vs. structural savings:** which plants, if any, can be preserved while still achieving the required savings.
- **Temporary vs. permanent measures:** whether working-time cuts, voluntary exits, and labor contributions can deliver durable impact.
- **Employee contributions vs. management/owner commitments:** what concrete commitments on investment, products, and future allocation would be offered in exchange.
- **Speed vs. social acceptability:** how quickly savings must be realized versus how much time is needed for socially managed transitions.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: savings targets, capacity numbers, timing, and implementation risk.
- Ask each side to specify **what it could accept conditionally** rather than only what it rejects.
- Focus discussion on **package design**: which combination of measures could meet competitiveness goals while minimizing closures and forced redundancies.
- Request **binding elements on both sides**: employee contributions on one side, and investment/product/site commitments on the other.
