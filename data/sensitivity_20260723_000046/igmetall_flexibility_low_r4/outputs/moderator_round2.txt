## Moderator summary, Round 2

### 1) Points of agreement
There is broad agreement that:
- **doing nothing is not an option**;
- the company needs a **durable, credible, economically real** restructuring;
- a prolonged conflict would harm **workers, plants, the brand, and enterprise value**;
- any acceptable solution must be **quantified** rather than rhetorical;
- **non-closure measures** can contribute materially and should be considered seriously;
- further analysis is needed from an **independent site-level financial/restructuring expert**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
The main unresolved divide is over **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies may remain on the table**:

- **Employee-side representatives (Works Council and union)**: reject German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and unilateral dismantling of job guarantees; insist on site preservation and voluntary, socially managed adjustment only.
- **Management**: rejects “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as fixed starting points; argues that structural savings may require site rationalisation and possibly workforce reduction.
- **Owners / investors / state government**: vary in tone, but none have accepted a blanket no-closure commitment; all want a solution that is durable and economically credible.

A second unresolved issue is **the duration and binding nature of any guarantees**:
- employee-side actors want **binding site/job security and investment commitments**;
- management and some owners say such guarantees cannot be granted upfront if they lock in an uncompetitive structure.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
The discussion still has not resolved several central trade-offs:

- **Durability vs. social protection**: whether temporary labor concessions and voluntary measures can produce savings durable enough to match structural cost needs.
- **Capacity reduction vs. footprint preservation**: whether the needed savings can be achieved while keeping all German sites open.
- **Fixed-cost relief vs. implementation risk**: whether avoiding closures leaves too much fixed cost in place, versus the one-off cost and disruption of closures.
- **Employment protection vs. competitiveness**: how to balance job security and regional stability against the need for margin improvement and long-term viability.
- **Political legitimacy vs. governance flexibility**: the extent to which the state’s regional role should constrain restructuring options.
- **Quantified savings gap**: parties still lack a shared, site-level model showing which scenario closes the gap fastest and most reliably.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to **numbers**:

- Require the independent model to compare, **by plant**, at least three scenarios:
  1. **zero-closure restructuring**,
  2. **partial site rationalisation**,
  3. **closure-based restructuring**.
- For each scenario, identify:
  - annual savings,
  - one-off restructuring costs,
  - payback period,
  - capacity removed,
  - utilization impact,
  - workforce impact,
  - and durability of savings.
- Ask each stakeholder to state the **minimum acceptable conditions** under which they would support a site-preserving alternative.

In short: the round clarified the positions, but not the numbers needed to resolve them.
