I will respond plainly.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable competitive reset, and short-term cosmetics will not solve a structural cost problem.

But I **do not** accept your framing that the only credible route is to keep all closures and workforce reductions on the table as leverage. That is not a negotiating starting point I can support as an anchor shareholder with responsibility for the region.

What I **do** accept:
- capacity reduction where it is genuinely needed,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary separation tools,
- and a **limited, temporary employee contribution** if it is tied to real commitments.

What I **do not** accept:
- German plant closures as the default path,
- compulsory redundancies as an ordinary instrument,
- or a “durable” solution that destroys regional employment and weakens the company’s social legitimacy.

If management wants my support, then bring a package that is **quantified**, **durable**, and **site-preserving as far as possible**. I will not back a restructuring that simply shifts the burden onto the workforce and the regions.

## To the Works Council
I agree with your insistence that the workforce is **not** the sole adjustment variable. That is right.

I also agree that:
- no compulsory redundancies should be the objective,
- voluntary and socially responsible measures should come first,
- and any employee contribution must be matched by **binding investment and product commitments**.

Where I cannot follow you is if “no closures” is treated as an absolute without regard to the economics. I am not prepared to endorse a blank cheque for the status quo. If a plant is genuinely unsustainable, we need to examine every alternative seriously.

That said, the burden is on management to prove that no site-preserving solution exists before anyone talks about closures. Until then, I will continue to resist closures in Germany.

## To the union
Your position is politically and socially understandable, and I share much of the substance:
- no forced redundancies,
- social partnership,
- temporary employee contributions only in exchange for guarantees,
- protection of the collective framework.

I support that direction.

But I want to be equally direct: if the union insists on “no closures” while also rejecting any deeper structural adjustment, it will eventually run into the economic reality management is describing. That reality cannot be ignored.

So I am prepared to support a **serious negotiated package** that:
1. keeps German sites open where possible,
2. avoids compulsory redundancies,
3. uses voluntary and reversible measures first,
4. includes a real employee contribution,
5. and secures binding commitments on investment, products, and future workload.

I am **not** prepared to support a package that simply freezes the current structure and calls that fairness.

## To the controlling family holding
I agree that long-term enterprise value matters. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would be destructive.

But I want to be very clear: long-term value is **not** served by casually sacrificing regional employment or turning plant closures into the easy solution. The company’s legitimacy, its industrial base, and its labor relations are assets too.

So yes, we need competitiveness. But we need it in a way that preserves the company as a regional anchor. Value destruction can come from closure-driven rupture just as much as from delay.

## To investors
I hear your point about measurable, durable savings. That is fair.

But I reject the implication that the only credible savings are those achieved by closures or deep job cuts. A negotiated restructuring with working-time changes, capacity reductions, voluntary exits, and targeted cost contributions can also produce durable effects — if management is serious and if the package is properly designed.

What I would require from you is the same discipline you demand from others:
- quantified savings,
- implementation risk,
- timing,
- and proof that the solution does not undermine the company’s social and political foundation.

## My position
My line is this:

- **No outcome that guts regional employment**
- **No sidelining of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **No settlement that treats German plant closures as the easy answer**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that preserves sites and jobs as far as possible while restoring competitiveness**

I am prepared to support a tougher package if it is:
- economically credible,
- socially balanced,
- and genuinely protective of the company’s role in the region.

If management cannot bring that forward, then I will not help push through closures under the guise of realism.

I am open to a hard, negotiated restructuring.

I am **not** open to a restructuring that destroys the regional industrial base or strips the state of its legitimate role in protecting it.
