I will respond plainly from the ownership side.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert on the central point: **savings are needed, and they need to be durable**. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would be value-destructive. A strike-heavy, reputationally damaging confrontation would hurt the brand, the supplier base, and ultimately the long-term interests of all owners.

I also agree that **non-closure measures can contribute materially**. That is important. I am not here to insist on closures for their own sake.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with the employee side’s attempt to make **“no closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** absolute starting points.

From an ownership and governance perspective, that is not a solution; it is a constraint that may or may not be compatible with the economics. If the required savings are large and structural, then the burden of proof is on those rejecting closures to show that an alternative package is genuinely equivalent in durability and scale.

I also disagree with the idea that **binding guarantees through 2030** can simply be granted upfront regardless of the restructuring design. If the company remains overstaffed or structurally uncompetitive, then such guarantees would lock in value destruction.

## What the expert clarified
The expert’s assessment is useful because it cuts through slogans:

- Non-closure measures can help.
- They may even be sufficient in theory.
- But whether they are sufficient in practice depends on the **quantified savings gap** and the **durability** of the measures.

That is exactly the point. We should not argue in abstractions. We need a quantified comparison.

## What I need before ruling out closures
I am **not** prepared to rule out plant closures in advance. That would be irresponsible to the long-term enterprise value I am bound to protect.

But I am also **not** declaring closures the preferred outcome. They are a serious instrument, not a goal.

So the next step must be this:

[SUMMON: Independent restructuring team / site-level financial model to quantify by plant the savings, capacity reduction, utilization, and employment impact of three scenarios: zero-closure restructuring, partial site rationalization, and closure-based restructuring.]

We need:
- site-level cost per vehicle,
- fixed and variable cost breakdown,
- utilization rates,
- product allocation through 2030,
- capex needs,
- timing of savings,
- and implementation risk.

Without that, nobody can credibly claim that closures are unavoidable or that a zero-closure package is sufficient.

## My position on the current lines
### To the Works Council
I hear your no-closure demand, but I do **not** accept it as a governing principle. If the numbers show that a preserved footprint leaves the company structurally weak, then your position becomes a way of postponing the adjustment, not solving it.

### To the union
You are right that employees should not bear the whole burden. But I do **not** accept the idea that employee contributions must remain temporary if the underlying cost problem is permanent. Temporary pain does not fix a permanent gap.

### To management
You are right that the business needs structural improvement. But I will not support a management approach that hides behind closure threats without proving the economics. If you want the owners behind you, bring a quantified case, not just a strategic narrative.

### To the state government
Your insistence on regional responsibility is legitimate, and I take it seriously. But regional legitimacy cannot become a veto on every difficult adjustment. If a site is genuinely non-viable, then preserving it indefinitely is not stewardship; it is deferred loss.

## What I would support
I am open to a negotiated package if it achieves all three:
1. **Durable cost reduction**
2. **Meaningful capacity adjustment**
3. **Stable industrial peace**

That package may include:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary separation,
- redeployment,
- wage restraint,
- bonus sacrifice,
- and product/investment commitments.

But I will only support a non-closure solution if it is **quantified and credible** at the same level as a closure-based package.

## Bottom line
I am not moving to a blanket no-closure position.

I am also not endorsing closures as the first choice.

What I am endorsing is a hard, numbers-based comparison. Until we have that, the honest position is: **all options remain open, and none should be ruled out on political preference alone**.

If the quantified analysis shows that a site-preserving package truly closes the gap, I will support it. If it does not, then I will not block structural measures merely to avoid difficult decisions.
