### Core impasse
The central blockage is **not** agreement on the need for restructuring; all parties accept that a durable reset is needed.

The impasse is over **means and guarantees**:

1. **Employee side / state side**
   - demands **no closures**
   - **no compulsory redundancies**
   - binding security through 2030
   - co-determination and investment commitments

2. **Management / family / investors**
   - require **structural and durable cost reduction**
   - insist that **closures and headcount reductions remain available**
   - want a package that is **economically equivalent**, not just symbolically reassuring

3. **Expert assessment**
   - indicates the deciding issue is whether a **non-closure package can permanently remove enough fixed cost and capacity**
   - temporary labour concessions alone are not sufficient if overcapacity is real

So the deadlock is:  
**security-first guarantees vs. economics-first structural adjustment**, with no agreed proof yet that a non-closure path can meet the required savings.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the gap

#### Proposal 1: “Non-closure first, closure backstop”
- All parties agree to a **non-closure restructuring package as the first choice**.
- The package would include:
  - capacity reduction through shift reductions, line consolidation, internal redeployment, voluntary exits
  - temporary workforce contributions
  - management/owner contributions
  - investment commitments
- A **pre-defined review gate** would be set after a fixed period.
- If agreed savings, utilization, or margin targets are missed, then **specific closure options become eligible only as a backstop**, subject to renewed negotiation and evidence.

**Purpose:** gives the workforce a real non-closure attempt, while giving management/investors an enforcement mechanism.

---

#### Proposal 2: “Time-limited guarantees in exchange for measured structural concessions”
- The employee side would consider **time-limited wage and flexibility contributions**.
- In return, management and owners would provide:
  - **binding no-forced-redundancy commitments**
  - **site-security commitments for a defined period**
  - **investment allocation for German future products**
- The package would include a **measurable structural cost target** and a **joint verification process**.
- If the target is met, guarantees continue; if not, parties reopen talks on deeper measures.

**Purpose:** aligns temporary employee sacrifice with enforceable employer obligations.

---

#### Proposal 3: “Hybrid footprint adjustment with protected core sites”
- Rather than “all sites or closures,” parties identify:
  - a **protected core of German plants**
  - and one or more sites where **capacity is reduced without immediate closure**
- Measures could include:
  - reduced shifts
  - product concentration
  - partial line idling
  - transfer of volumes
  - voluntary exit corridors
- This would avoid immediate shutdowns while still producing **real permanent capacity reduction**.

**Purpose:** acknowledges management’s need for structural change while preserving the employee/state red line against abrupt closures.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
**What specific permanent capacity and cost reductions would you accept as sufficient to make a no-closure package economically credible?**

#### To Industrial Union
**What enforceable employee contribution can you support that is durable enough to matter structurally, while still preserving the no-compulsory-redundancy line?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact non-closure savings model, by site and by year, can you put on the table that shows how competitiveness is restored without relying on immediate closures?**

#### To Regional State Government
**What concrete support or conditionality are you prepared to provide to make a non-closure restructuring politically and economically credible?**

#### To Controlling Family Holding
**Under what verified economic thresholds would you stop treating closures as an open tool and commit to a non-closure path?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What margin and cash-flow outcome would you require to regard a non-closure settlement as economically equivalent to a closure-based restructuring?**

#### To Independent Expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, fixed-cost removal, and labour-cost durability would you define as the threshold for a viable non-closure package?**
