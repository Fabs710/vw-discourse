## Expert assessment: what would make a non-closure package economically credible?

From an automotive restructuring perspective, the central issue is **not** whether the package is politically acceptable, but whether it produces a **durable reduction in fixed cost and excess capacity** over a 3–5 year horizon.

### Bottom line
A **non-closure-only** package can work **only if** it removes enough:
- **permanent fixed cost**
- **permanent capacity**
- **structural labour cost**
- **overhead and complexity**

Temporary concessions alone are usually **not sufficient** if the company truly has structural overcapacity.

---

## 1) What the economics usually require

To be comparable to a closure-based restructuring, a non-closure package must typically deliver:

1. **Permanent capacity reduction**
   - fewer shifts
   - fewer lines
   - lower output capacity
   - product concentration at fewer sites

2. **Permanent fixed-cost removal**
   - indirect headcount reduction
   - plant overhead reduction
   - maintenance/utilities simplification
   - local administrative cost reduction

3. **Durable labour-cost reset**
   - not just one-year wage moderation
   - but a lasting reduction in cost per vehicle or per labor hour

4. **Utilization improvement**
   - retained plants must run materially fuller
   - otherwise the network remains too expensive

5. **One-off restructuring costs that do not swamp savings**
   - severance
   - transfer costs
   - reconfiguration
   - tooling/logistics changes

If these do not add up, the package is only a **delay mechanism**, not a solution.

---

## 2) Closure vs non-closure vs hybrid

### A. Closure-based restructuring
**Most effective for permanent fixed-cost removal.**

**Strengths**
- strongest and most certain reduction in structural cost
- easiest way to remove excess capacity
- improves utilization at surviving plants

**Weaknesses**
- high one-off costs
- industrial conflict risk
- logistics and launch disruption
- political/governance friction

**Economic implication**
If the network is clearly oversized, closure often gives the **best operating-cost reset**, though not always the best net present value once one-offs and disruption are included.

---

### B. Non-closure restructuring
**Can work, but only if it is deep and structural.**

Usually requires:
- shift elimination
- line consolidation
- product concentration
- internal redeployment
- voluntary exits/retirement corridors
- permanent overhead simplification

**Typical risk**
If it relies mainly on:
- temporary pay restraint
- bonus cuts
- short-term working-time changes
- voluntary exits without real capacity reduction

then savings often fade, and the underlying overcapacity remains.

**Economic implication**
A non-closure package is only credible if it achieves **structural savings comparable to the closure option**, not merely lower labor cost for a few years.

---

### C. Hybrid restructuring
**Usually the most realistic compromise.**

This means:
- protected core sites
- real reduction at some sites without immediate full closure
- shift reductions and product concentration
- one or more partial site adjustments
- voluntary exits plus real overhead cuts

**Economic implication**
A hybrid often offers the best balance between:
- durability of savings
- lower execution risk than full closures
- greater realism in a constrained governance system

In practice, if closures are politically blocked, a well-designed hybrid is often the **least value-destructive credible path**.

---

## 3) Minimum threshold for a viable non-closure package

I would want to see, **by site and by year**, at minimum:

- **exact capacity reduction**
- **permanent fixed-cost savings**
- **durable unit labour-cost reduction**
- **utilization improvement at retained sites**
- **one-off costs of implementation**
- **3–5 year margin path**
- **assumptions on conflict/disruption**
- **proof that savings remain after temporary concessions expire**

Without that, no one can claim equivalence to closure-based restructuring.

---

## 4) Practical judgement

### Most likely outcomes
- **Closure-based**: strongest pure cost reset
- **Hybrid**: often best overall compromise
- **Non-closure-only**: viable only if it is effectively a disguised structural downsizing, not a temporary labour deal

### Key rule
If the package does **not** permanently reduce excess capacity and fixed cost, then it will likely fail within a few years.

To compare options quantitatively, I would need:

## Final assessment
A **credible non-closure solution** is possible, but only if it delivers a **durable structural reset** roughly equivalent to closure in economic effect. In most automotive cases, that requires a **hybrid footprint adjustment** rather than a pure no-closure / no-layoff arrangement.
