I appreciate the clarity from each side. Let me be equally clear from the ownership perspective.

We agree on one essential point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness reset, not a symbolic exercise**. The current cost base and capacity situation cannot simply be carried forward unchanged. If margins remain structurally weak, we all lose over time — employees, the region, management, and the owners.

Where I disagree is on any assumption that the right answer is either:
- a **closure-first approach without a negotiated alternative**, or
- a **protect-everything approach that leaves the economics unchanged**.

Both are unacceptable if they produce prolonged conflict or permanent value erosion.

### To the Works Council
I understand the red lines you have set out, and I do not dismiss the importance of stability and trust. But I cannot support a position that says “no closures, no forced layoffs, no framework changes” **without simultaneously offering a credible economic replacement**.

If you want site and employment guarantees through 2030, then they must be matched by something real:
- measurable cost reductions,
- capacity rationalisation,
- productivity improvements,
- and a contribution that restores competitiveness rather than postponing the problem.

A guarantee is only as credible as the underlying business case. A promise that prevents restructuring while the company continues to underperform is not stewardship; it is drift.

### To the Union
You are right that the burden cannot fall only on employees. I agree that a negotiated contribution must be part of any solution, and that management and ownership must also shoulder responsibility.

But I disagree with the implication that “fair sharing” can mean mostly reversible or temporary measures. The company needs **structural** improvement. If the employee contribution is limited to short-term waivers, we are likely to be back in the same place in a few years — only weaker and more fragile.

So yes to:
- working-time flexibility,
- negotiated cost contributions,
- internal reallocations,
- and productivity gains.

But no to a package that looks fair on paper while leaving the structural gap untouched.

### To the State Government
I agree with your emphasis on social stability and the regional role of the company. A disorderly breakdown would be damaging. But I also need to be candid: **regional employment cannot be protected by denying economic reality**.

If a site is structurally uneconomic, then preserving it indefinitely may simply defer a harsher adjustment later. That is not a responsible way to protect the region or the company.

I am prepared to support an approach that avoids unnecessary closures. But that requires a convincing alternative package that materially changes the cost and capacity profile. Without that, “avoid closures” becomes a slogan rather than a solution.

### Our ownership position
The family holding’s priority is long-term enterprise value and governance stability. That means:
- we do **not** want a prolonged industrial confrontation,
- we do **not** want reputational damage,
- and we do **not** want a restructuring that permanently weakens the company’s competitive position.

At the same time, we will not endorse a settlement that preserves control structures and labour peace on the surface while the business continues to erode underneath. That would be a false peace.

### What would move us
A credible package would need to show:
1. a **durable reduction in structural costs**,
2. a **real capacity adjustment**,
3. a **negotiated labour contribution**,
4. **labour peace for a meaningful period**,
5. and a path that preserves the company’s ability to invest in future products.

If that can be achieved without closures, that is preferable. But if the alternative is merely to rule out hard decisions and rely on guarantees alone, then that is not something we can support.

### Bottom line
We are **not** committed to closures for their own sake. But we are also **not** committed to preserving every site and every guarantee regardless of cost.

Our position is:
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No permanent impairment of competitiveness**
- **No prolonged conflict**
- **Yes to a serious negotiated restructuring that actually fixes the economics**

If the other stakeholders want our support, they need to come back with a package that is not just socially acceptable, but economically convincing.
