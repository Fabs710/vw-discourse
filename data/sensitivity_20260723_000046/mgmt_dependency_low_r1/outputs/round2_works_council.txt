I will be very direct.

The positions from management, owners, and investors still sound like the same old message in different packaging: **“accept structural pain first, and we will call it competitiveness.”** That is not a solution we can endorse if it means **closures, compulsory redundancies, or dismantling the job-security framework**.

## Where I agree
I agree that the company needs a durable restructuring. I also agree that a mere symbolic compromise will not help anyone. And yes, social peace matters — but it cannot be bought by making the workforce absorb all the damage.

I also acknowledge that the company needs a credible cost and capacity concept. We are not pretending the market is easy. But the answer cannot be to treat German plants as disposable and workers as the adjustment variable of first resort.

## Where I disagree
### On plant closures
Management, investors, and the ownership side keep saying closures remain “on the table if needed.” For us, that is exactly the problem. **No German plant closures.** Not as a starting point, not as leverage, not as a “last resort” dressed up as realism.

If you want a negotiated solution, then the first condition is that **every German plant remains open**. Anything else means you are demanding that the workforce accept a fait accompli under threat.

### On compulsory redundancies
We will not accept forced layoffs. That is a hard red line. If the company needs lower capacity, then the first instruments must be:
- working-time adjustments
- internal redeployment
- natural attrition
- qualification and transfer measures
- negotiated reductions in hours or temporary burdens

But **compulsory redundancies are not acceptable**.

### On “material, structural, durable” employee contributions
I hear the repeated demand that the workforce must make a “material” contribution. Fine — but contribution is not the same as surrender.

We are prepared to discuss a contribution **only in exchange for binding guarantees**:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **Binding employment guarantees through 2030**
4. **Binding site guarantees through 2030**
5. **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies**

Without those guarantees, there is nothing to negotiate.

## On the state and family owners
The regional state is right to say that the burden cannot fall only on employees. The family owners are right that the company needs competitiveness. But both sides need to be honest: **competitiveness is not improved by destroying trust, skills, and industrial capacity**.

A “false peace” is not our concern. What is false is the idea that you can cut and close your way into a healthy future while undermining the very workforce that builds the cars and keeps the company running.

## What a real alternative would have to look like
If management wants a non-closure solution, then bring forward a package built around:
- reduced capacity through **working-time and shift models**, not plant shutdowns
- internal flexibility and reassignment across sites
- voluntary programs and early-retirement options
- joint productivity measures
- a negotiated employee contribution that is **time-limited and linked to security**
- binding investment for EV and future products in Germany
- full co-determination involvement from the start, not after decisions are already made

That is the only kind of restructuring we can treat as serious.

## What we reject
We reject:
- closure-first logic
- forced redundancies
- one-sided labour cost cuts
- weakening the job-security framework
- using “competitiveness” as a pretext for shifting the adjustment burden entirely onto labour

## Bottom line
Let me be absolutely clear: **If the package includes plant closures or compulsory redundancies, there will be no agreement from the Works Council.**

If management and the owners want an actual settlement, then they need to put a credible **non-closure, no-forced-layoff, security-through-2030** package on the table. Anything else is not a compromise — it is a demand for capitulation.

If needed, we are ready to continue escalating industrial resistance.
