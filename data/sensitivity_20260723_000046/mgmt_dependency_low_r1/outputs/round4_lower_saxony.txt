As the regional state and anchor shareholder, I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, investors, and the expert on one central point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. The cost and capacity problem is real. If we only buy time, we will be back in crisis with a weaker company.

I also agree that:
- **the burden cannot fall only on employees**
- **numbers matter**
- **prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging**
- **temporary concessions alone are not enough if overcapacity is real**

So I am not defending the status quo. I am defending a **real** restructuring that preserves the company’s role as a regional anchor and does not gut the state’s industrial base.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept closure-first logic
Management, the family holding, and investors keep saying closures remain on the table. I understand the economic argument, but I do **not** accept closures as the starting point or as routine leverage.

A German plant closure is not just a cost item. It means:
- lasting damage to regional employment,
- loss of industrial capability,
- weakened regional stability,
- and harm to the company’s legitimacy as an anchor employer.

So my position remains: **closures are not the starting line**. If they are to be considered at all, management must first show that serious alternatives have genuinely been exhausted.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies as a normal restructuring tool
I align with the workforce and union on this: **forced layoffs are not the right basis for a durable settlement**.

If capacity has to come down, then the first tools must be:
- working-time adjustments
- internal redeployment
- qualification and transfer
- voluntary exits
- retirement and bridge models
- productivity and organizational measures

Compulsory redundancies would deepen conflict, damage morale, and destroy trust. That is bad economics and bad governance.

### 3) I do not accept “shared sacrifice” that is one-sided
Management and investors want a “material and structural” workforce contribution. I understand that. But that contribution is only legitimate if it is matched by:
- real security,
- credible investment,
- management restraint,
- and a future the workforce can believe in.

I will not support a model where employees give up pay and guarantees while sites are left uncertain and capital keeps all the strategic freedom.

## My response to management
You are right that a symbolic compromise will not solve the problem. But you are wrong if you think the only serious solution is to keep closures and forced layoffs permanently available until the workforce gives in.

What you have not yet done is prove, with convincing site-by-site numbers, that the restructuring **must** involve closures or compulsory redundancies. I want a real comparison:
- non-closure restructuring,
- hybrid footprint adjustment,
- closure-based restructuring.

Show me which one is least value-destructive over 3–5 years, including one-off costs, conflict risk, launch disruption, and the loss of know-how. Until then, closures are not “necessary”; they are only one possible instrument.

## My response to the Works Council and union
I support your refusal of forced redundancies and your rejection of closure-first logic. But I also need you to engage seriously with the economics.

A sustainable non-closure solution must show:
- how the cost base falls durably,
- how capacity is reduced without shutting plants,
- what permanent productivity gains are achieved,
- and what time-limited workforce contribution is possible in exchange for binding guarantees.

That is not the same as accepting management’s agenda. It is insisting on a credible alternative. If that alternative exists, I will defend it.

## My response to the family holding
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. I agree. But I also reject the idea that “economic realism” automatically means closures.

Realism also includes:
- the value of industrial stability,
- the value of retained skills,
- the cost of labour conflict,
- the importance of regional legitimacy,
- and the long-term damage of destroying trust.

A solution that technically cuts costs but tears apart the social settlement that made this company strong is not good stewardship.

## My response to investors
You are right to demand a durable improvement in margins. But do not narrow the definition of value to immediate cost cutting.

A proper economic comparison must include:
- one-off closure costs,
- execution risk,
- supplier and logistics disruption,
- strike risk,
- launch risk,
- reputational damage,
- and the long-term cost of lost skills.

If you ignore those, your “economic equivalence” is incomplete.

## On the expert assessment
The expert clarified the real issue: **temporary concessions are not enough if overcapacity is real**. I accept that.

What I do **not** accept is the conclusion that the only credible answer is closure or forced layoffs. The test is not whether the workforce likes the package. The test is whether the package:
- permanently reduces fixed cost,
- genuinely lowers capacity,
- preserves industrial substance,
- and restores competitiveness without sacrificing the company’s regional anchor role.

That can be done only if management stops treating closures as the default answer and starts proving alternatives properly.

## What would actually move me
I remain prepared to support a package if it contains all of the following:

1. **No plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Real capacity reduction through non-closure instruments**
4. **A fair, negotiated workforce contribution**
5. **Management and owner contributions**
6. **Binding investment commitments for German future products and technologies**
7. **A credible employment and site framework through 2030**
8. **Real co-determination in the restructuring**
9. **A fully costed comparison of the options**

That is the minimum basis for a serious settlement.

## What I reject
I reject:
- closure-first thinking
- forced layoffs
- using regional dependence as pressure
- permanent erosion of the job-security framework
- one-sided labour sacrifice
- “guarantees” that are only temporary pauses

## Bottom line
My position has not changed:
- **no closure-first approach**
- **no forced redundancies**
- **no agreement that guts regional employment**
- **yes to a negotiated restructuring if it preserves sites, protects jobs, and restores competitiveness**
- **yes to a solution that respects co-determination and the company’s role as a regional anchor**

If management wants my support, it must bring a genuine non-closure package with hard numbers and real investment commitments. Until then, I will not move from my core position.
