Let me be equally direct in round 3.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one point: **a symbolic deal is useless**. The company does need a durable restructuring, and nobody benefits from endless drift.

I also agree that the burden cannot fall only on employees. If management, owners, and investors want a real reset, then they must contribute materially as well. And yes, prolonged conflict would be damaging — but that is not a reason to demand concessions from the workforce first; it is a reason to stop threatening the workforce with closures and forced layoffs.

## Where I disagree — and why
### 1) Closures are not an acceptable “option”
Management, the family holding, and investors keep saying closures remain on the table if needed. From our side, that is exactly the wrong starting point.

A closure-first logic is not a neutral economic tool. It is a threat against jobs, skills, regional stability, and trust. Once you normalize it, you destroy the basis for any credible social partnership.

So I will say it again clearly:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No plant closures as leverage**
- **No plant closures as a bargaining chip**
- **No plant closures as a “least bad” default**

If you want a deal, then the deal must be built on keeping every German plant open.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are a red line
The same applies to forced layoffs. We do not accept compulsory redundancies — not as a first step, not as a fallback, not as a hidden reserve.

If capacity must come down, then use the instruments that do not violate the job-security framework:
- working-time adjustment
- internal redeployment
- qualification and transfer
- voluntary exits
- retirement and bridge models
- productivity improvements

That is the only responsible path. Forced layoffs would mean the company is choosing confrontation over co-determination.

### 3) “Material and durable contribution” cannot mean permanent employee sacrifice
Management and investors keep repeating that workforce contributions must be “structural” and “durable.” I hear that. But I will not accept the idea that only labour must make durable sacrifices while capital and management preserve their freedom of action.

If employees contribute, then that contribution must be:
- **limited in time**
- **clearly defined**
- **tied to binding guarantees**
- **reversible if the company does not deliver**

We are not prepared to hand over permanent concessions for a promise that may evaporate once the pressure is off.

## What would actually move us
I will be precise. A negotiated contribution from the workforce is only possible if it is matched by binding commitments on all of the following:

1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding employment security through 2030**
4. **Binding site security through 2030**
5. **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
6. **Investment commitments for German future products and technologies**
7. **A credible management and owner contribution**
   - overhead cuts at the top
   - restraint in capital allocation
   - a fair sharing of sacrifice
   - no one-sided labour cost extraction

Without those guarantees, there is nothing to negotiate. With them, we can discuss time-limited contributions. Without them, the workforce will not pay for a crisis it did not create.

## On the “fully costed” request
I support the moderator’s call for numbers. But let me be clear about what those numbers must demonstrate:

- not just how much cost can be cut,
- but **how the company can cut cost without closures and forced layoffs**,
- and how it can do so **while preserving industrial substance in Germany**.

If management cannot produce a fully costed non-closure scenario, then that is not proof that closures are necessary — it is proof that management has not yet done its homework properly, or is unwilling to consider alternatives seriously.

## What I reject outright
I reject:
- closure-based restructuring as the default
- threats against individual plants
- forced redundancies
- permanent erosion of the job-security framework
- one-sided “shared sacrifice” from labour only
- using competitiveness as a pretext for breaking trust

The state should not sit quietly if management tries to use regional dependence as leverage. If the company wants social legitimacy, it must respect the workforce and the co-determination structure that has made this company strong.

## To the controlling family
You say you do not want a cosmetic compromise. Fine — neither do we. But cosmetic compromise is not the alternative to closures. A real alternative is a non-closure restructuring that preserves skills, plants, and trust. That is what you should be pushing management toward.

## To investors

- Is it only about short-term margin optics?
- Or does it include the long-term value of skills, labour peace, and industrial capacity?

Because if you ignore those, then you are not measuring value — you are measuring liquidation by another name.

- **No German plant closures**

- **No dismantling of the job-security framework**
