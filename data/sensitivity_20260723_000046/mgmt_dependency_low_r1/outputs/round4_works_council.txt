## Where I strongly disagree
### 1) The “closures as backstop” idea is not acceptable
The moderator’s Proposal 1 — “non-closure first, closure backstop” — is not a bridge from our perspective. It is a closure plan in disguise.

A backstop is not neutral. It means:
- the closure option stays politically alive,
- the workforce is kept under threat,
- management can wait for a trigger and then force the issue.

That destroys trust from the start. If you want a genuine negotiation, then closures cannot remain an open threat. Once that weapon is on the table, every promise becomes conditional, and every commitment from management looks reversible.

So let me say it plainly:

- **No non-closure package with closure backstops**
- **No “review gate” that turns into a later plant shutdown**
- **No built-in exit ramp to closures**

If a site is to be kept open, then it must be kept open.

### 2) “Economically equivalent” cannot mean “same savings at any social cost”
Management, investors, and the family holding keep repeating that the workforce must present an “economically equivalent” alternative. Fine. But equivalence must include the full picture:
- industrial capability,
- skills retention,
- labour peace,
- regional stability,
- and the value of not destroying trust.

If your definition of equivalence ignores those and counts only immediate margin effects, then you are not comparing options honestly. You are just dressing up closure pressure as economic neutrality.

### 3) Forced redundancies remain a hard red line
I need to be explicit because this keeps getting softened in the discussion: **forced layoffs are not acceptable**.

Not as a first step.
Not as a fallback.
Not as a “technical necessity” once enough pressure is applied.

If management wants lower headcount, then it must use:
- retirement corridors
- voluntary exits
- internal redeployment
- working-time changes
- qualification and transfers
- productivity improvements

Those are the tools of a responsible restructuring. Compulsory redundancies are the tool of a management team that has given up on social partnership.
