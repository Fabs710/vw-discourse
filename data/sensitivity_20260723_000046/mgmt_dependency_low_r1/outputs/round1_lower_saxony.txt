As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I am taking a clear but pragmatic position.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **the company does need a durable competitiveness solution**. Simply preserving every structure unchanged while costs remain uncompetitive is not a serious answer.

I also agree with the workforce side that **the burden cannot fall on employees alone**. A restructuring that destroys trust, jobs, and sites in Germany would be economically and politically irresponsible, and it would weaken the company’s long-term industrial base.

### Where I disagree with management
What I cannot accept is a strategy that starts from **plant closures and mass job cuts as the first and preferred instrument**.

That is not a neutral technical adjustment. It would mean:
- lasting damage to regional employment,
- loss of skills and industrial capacity,
- social and political fallout,
- and a serious weakening of the company’s role as a regional anchor.

If management wants support for difficult measures, it must show that it has exhausted serious alternatives. At this stage, I do **not** see a convincing case for treating German sites as disposable.

### Where I disagree with the union only in part
I support the red lines on **forced redundancies** and **blanket closures**. But I also need to be candid: if the company is to remain strong, then **there must be real structural change**. That means the workforce cannot simply ask for protection without any contribution at all.

However, a contribution is acceptable only if it is:
- negotiated,
- proportionate,
- time-limited where possible,
- and clearly tied to **site security, employment security, and investment commitments**.

### My position
The state will support a solution that:
1. **preserves German sites wherever economically viable**
2. **avoids compulsory redundancies**
3. **uses negotiated capacity adjustment, internal flexibility, and working-time instruments first**
4. **includes a fair employee contribution**
5. **includes management and owner responsibility as well**
6. **protects the company’s long-term investment capacity**

What I will not support is a package that:
- guts regional employment,
- removes our co-determination role in practice,
- or uses the restructuring to force through closures that could be avoided through negotiation.

### On the ownership side
Because of the governance structure, this cannot be pushed through as a simple management decree. The company needs a solution that can actually command a broad majority and survive industrial conflict. A confrontational path may be legally and politically possible to threaten, but it is not necessarily governable.

### Bottom line
My message is straightforward:

- **No to a closure-first strategy**
- **No to mass layoffs as the default answer**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it protects jobs, sites, and future investment**
- **Yes to real cost improvements, but not at the expense of the company’s role as a regional economic anchor**

If management and the other shareholders want our support, they need to come forward with a package that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable. If they cannot do that, then they should not expect the state to enable a solution that tears up the industrial and political settlement that has made this company viable for so long.
