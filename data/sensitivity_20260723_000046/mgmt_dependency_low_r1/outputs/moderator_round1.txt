### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring / competitiveness reset**.
- There is broad agreement that **social peace matters** and that a disorderly conflict would be damaging.
- Most sides accept that **the burden cannot fall only on employees**; management, owners, and other stakeholders must also contribute.
- There is openness, in principle, to a **negotiated package** using instruments such as:
  - working-time adjustments
  - internal flexibility
  - capacity reductions
  - productivity measures
  - some form of employee contribution
  - investment commitments for future products/technologies

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management, owners, and investors say closures remain on the table if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs:**  
  - Employee-side and state actors oppose them.  
  - Management, owners, and investors are unwilling to rule them out if structural adjustment requires them.
- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  - Employee side seeks binding site and employment guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management, owners, and investors say such guarantees are not credible unless tied to real cost and capacity changes.
- **Nature of the workforce contribution:**  
  - Union and works council favor negotiated, shared-sacrifice measures linked to security.  
  - Management, owners, and investors want a contribution that is **material, structural, and durable**, not mainly temporary or reversible.
- **Economic logic vs. social protection:**  
  - Employee and state sides emphasize preserving industrial sites and jobs where possible.  
  - Management, owners, and investors emphasize restoring margins and reducing structural overcapacity.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to reconcile **job and site security** with a **credible structural cost reset**.
- What level of **capacity reduction** is necessary, and whether it can be achieved without closures.
- Whether employee contributions can be **time-limited/reversible** or must be **permanent/structural**.
- What management, owners, and public stakeholders will contribute in return for workforce concessions.
- Which measures would be enough to justify **binding guarantees** without merely postponing later conflict.
- How to define a package that is both **economically convincing** and **socially sustainable**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package elements**:
  1. required cost reduction target
  2. required capacity reduction target
  3. possible alternatives to closures
  4. possible forms of employee contribution
  5. corresponding guarantees on employment, sites, and investment
- Ask each side to state:
  - what they can **accept**
  - what they can **trade**
  - what minimum conditions would make a deal possible
- Aim for a **side-by-side comparison** of:
  - closure-based restructuring
  - negotiated non-closure alternative
  - hybrid compromise with partial site adjustments
