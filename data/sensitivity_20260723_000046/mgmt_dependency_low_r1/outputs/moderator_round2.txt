### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring** and that a merely symbolic compromise is not enough.
- There is broad agreement that **the burden cannot fall only on employees**.
- All sides also agree that **prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging**.
- There is general openness to **negotiated measures** such as:
  - working-time adjustments
  - internal redeployment
  - voluntary exit/retirement schemes
  - qualification and transfer measures
  - productivity and flexibility improvements
  - investment commitments for future products/technologies

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works Council, union, and state oppose a closure-first approach and want no German plant closures.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors will not rule closures out in advance if they are needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side, union, and state reject forced layoffs.  
  - Management and capital-side stakeholders say headcount reductions must remain possible if required by the economics.
- **Scope of employee contribution:**  
  - All sides expect some workforce contribution, but employees insist it must be tied to binding guarantees.  
  - Management and investors say contributions must be materially effective and not merely temporary.
- **Guarantees vs. flexibility:**  
  - Employee and state side want binding job/site security through 2030.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say such guarantees are not acceptable unless the structural cost problem is truly solved.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- Whether the company can achieve a **sufficient cost and capacity reset without closures or forced redundancies**.
- How to reconcile **job and site security** with the need for **durable competitiveness and margin improvement**.
- What would count as an **economically equivalent non-closure package**.
- Which stakeholder groups will make **concrete, measurable, and durable sacrifices**, and in what sequence.
- How to prevent a deal that buys short-term peace but leaves the underlying structural problem unresolved.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **quantified options**.
- Ask management to present at least one **fully costed non-closure scenario** and one closure-based scenario for comparison.
- Ask employee representatives to specify what level of **time-limited contribution** they could accept in exchange for which exact guarantees.
- Clarify which measures are **temporary**, which are **structural**, and which are **non-negotiable**.
- Focus the next round on identifying the **least value-destructive credible package** that can still secure industrial and social stability.
