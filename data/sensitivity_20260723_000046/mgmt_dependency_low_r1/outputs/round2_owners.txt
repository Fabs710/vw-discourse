I will be direct.

## To management
I agree with the diagnosis that the company needs a **durable competitiveness reset**. I do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves structural overcapacity and leaves margins below target. That would be value-destructive, and the family holding will not back it.

But I also need to say clearly: **a closure-first strategy that triggers prolonged industrial conflict is not acceptable either**. A plan that technically cuts costs but permanently damages the enterprise through strikes, reputational harm, and governance paralysis is not a solution. It destroys value in a different way.

So I support the objective, but not an inflexible instrument set. If there is a credible alternative that achieves the same structural effect without closures, it deserves serious attention. If there is not, then management must explain precisely why — with numbers, not broad claims.

## To the Works Council
I understand your red lines on trust, job security, and co-determination. I also agree that the workforce should not bear the burden alone.

Where I disagree is your absolute “no closures, no forced redundancies, no framework changes” position. From an ownership perspective, that is only defensible if you can show a package that truly delivers equivalent structural savings and capacity reduction. So far, I hear strong principles, but not yet a fully credible economic replacement.

That said, I am **not** asking for a symbolic sacrifice. If employees contribute, the contribution must be meaningful and durable enough to improve competitiveness, not merely delay adjustment.

## To the union
I agree with you that forced layoffs are highly undesirable and should be avoided if at all possible. I also agree that management and ownership must contribute — not just employees.

But I do **not** accept a model where employee contributions are mainly temporary or reversible while the company still remains structurally uncompetitive. A short waiver, by itself, is not enough. If there is to be a negotiated solution, it must produce a real, measurable reset in labor cost and capacity.

## To the state government
I share your concern about regional stability and the role of the company as an anchor employer. A disorderly shutdown or a permanent weakening of German industrial capability would be a serious mistake.

But I also need to be frank: preserving every site because it is politically difficult to change is not stewardship. If a location is structurally uneconomic, the question is not whether we like that fact, but whether there is a viable alternative. If there is, we should pursue it. If there is not, we cannot simply postpone the issue indefinitely.

## To institutional investors
I agree with your focus on margin restoration and structural cost reduction. You are right that a deal which merely defers the problem is value-destructive.

Where I part company with you is if “the solution” is reduced to closures as the default answer. We should not confuse decisiveness with good governance. The right outcome is the one that restores competitiveness **and** can actually be sustained politically and operationally.

## What would move the family holding
We would support a package if it contains:
1. a **durable structural reduction in costs**
2. a **real capacity adjustment**
3. a **credible employee contribution**
4. **labor peace for a meaningful period**
5. **clear investment commitments for future products and technology**
6. a solution that does **not** permanently impair governance control or the enterprise’s long-term value

That last point matters: we will not accept arrangements that weaken the owners’ ability to steward the company for the long term.

## My position, plainly
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No permanent erosion of competitiveness**
- **No prolonged conflict that destroys value**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it genuinely fixes the economics**
- **No blank-cheque guarantees if the structural problem remains unsolved**

If the other stakeholders want our support, they need to move from slogans to a package that shows, concretely, how the company gets back to target margins without creating a lasting industrial and governance crisis.

If that package exists without closures, I am open to it. If not, then the burden is on management and the workforce side to prove that their alternative is truly equivalent.
