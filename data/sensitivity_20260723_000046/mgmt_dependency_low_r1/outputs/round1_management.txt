We hear the concerns from the Works Council and the union. But I need to be equally direct: **a solution that simply preserves every site, every structure, and every guarantee without a hard cost reset is not credible**.

### Where we agree
- We agree that the company needs a **durable, negotiated solution**, not chaos.
- We agree that **social peace is valuable**.
- We agree that any restructuring must protect the company’s ability to invest in future products and technology.

### Where we disagree
What we cannot accept is a package that:
- leaves **German factory costs structurally above target**,
- keeps **excess capacity in place**,
- and relies on **temporary or reversible measures** that do not fix the underlying competitiveness gap.

That would not restore the brand to target margins, and it would not give us the financial room to invest toward 2030.

### On plant closures and job security
You are asking for binding guarantees through 2030 across the board. I need to be clear: **I cannot commit to blanket guarantees if the cost base remains uncompetitive**. If a site is structurally uneconomic, we cannot pretend otherwise.

The core issue is not symbolism. It is whether the German footprint is sized and costed for the market we actually face. If the answer is no, then **capacity reduction has to be real**, not cosmetic.

### On layoffs and labour contribution
I also reject the idea that management is asking for “pain only for workers.” The company is under severe pressure because the current structure is too expensive relative to demand and competition. That means everyone has to contribute to restoring competitiveness — but the employee contribution must be **material**, not marginal.

That can mean:
- lower labour costs,
- more flexible working-time arrangements,
- productivity gains,
- and a credible reduction in overcapacity.

But I need to stress: **a deal without a meaningful structural labour-cost and capacity contribution will not be enough**.

### What I am prepared to consider
I remain open on the exact route:
- closures,
- partial site adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- shifts in allocation,
- negotiated labour-cost measures,
- and investment commitments for future products.

So yes, I am open to negotiated alternatives **if they deliver the same economic effect**. But I will not sign up to an arrangement that preserves the current cost structure and simply postpones the problem.

### Bottom line
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a **credible, durable package** that materially reduces costs and capacity. Otherwise, management will continue to argue for deeper intervention. We are not here to manage decline by agreement; we are here to restore competitiveness.

So my position is simple:
**No cosmetic compromise, no preserving the status quo, and no solution that fails to deliver a structural reset.**
