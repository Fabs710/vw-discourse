## Core impasse
The central deadlock is not over the need for restructuring; all sides accept that a durable reset is required. The impasse is over **how much structural change must be accepted up front, and whether closures and compulsory redundancies can remain available as tools**.

More specifically, the conflict sits at three points:

1. **Workforce/state-side red line vs management/ownership/investor flexibility**
   - Workforce and state: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding security through 2030.
   - Management/owners/investors: they want the option of closures and headcount reduction unless a non-closure package proves economically equivalent.

2. **Definition of “economic equivalence”**
   - Labour and state want equivalence to include trust, skills retention, labour peace, regional stability, and investment.
   - Capital and management want a narrower test centered on permanent fixed-cost and capacity reduction, margin recovery, and execution certainty.

3. **Sequence problem**
   - Employees want guarantees first, then concessions.
   - Management and ownership want structural concessions first, then durable guarantees.

That sequence issue is what is freezing progress.

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options for discussion. These are framed as procedural proposals, not endorsements.

### Proposal A: Time-boxed non-closure test with objective trigger review
A negotiated package would:
- keep all German plants open during a defined test period,
- include measurable capacity, cost, and productivity targets,
- include employee contributions and management/owner contributions,
- and establish an independent review at a fixed date.

If targets are met, the non-closure path becomes the binding long-term restructuring model.  
If targets are not met, the parties return to the table with the expert’s plant-by-plant data and a predefined fallback process.

This bridges:
- workforce/state demand for no immediate closures,
- management/owner demand for a real structural test,
- investor demand for measurable enforcement.

### Proposal B: Hybrid footprint adjustment with protected core and protected jobs
A hybrid settlement could:
- protect named core sites and core technologies,
- reduce footprint through shifts, product concentration, transfers, redeployment, and voluntary exit corridors,
- avoid compulsory redundancies,
- but allow limited structural adjustments at selected sites if clearly justified and balanced by investment commitments.

This bridges:
- labour’s rejection of forced layoffs,
- management’s need for actual capacity reduction,
- investors’ demand for permanent savings.

### Proposal C: Security-for-concession ladder
A staged agreement could link each level of workforce contribution to a corresponding level of binding security:
- stage 1: wage/working-time/productivity contributions
- stage 2: binding job-security extension
- stage 3: binding site-security extension
- stage 4: investment commitments and co-determination guarantees

This bridges:
- the workforce’s demand that security not be surrendered upfront,
- management’s demand that concessions not be unilateral,
- the state’s demand for credible regional continuity.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded in principle, what specific, site-by-site capacity reduction measures would you accept that produce permanent cost savings of the same magnitude?

### To the Industrial Union
What enforceable combination of voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and productivity measures would you consider sufficient to replace compulsory redundancies while still making the restructuring durable?

### To Group & Brand Management
What minimum non-closure savings threshold, by site and over 3–5 years, would you accept as evidence that closures are not needed?

### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to ensure management provides full numbers and does not use closure threats as an indefinite negotiating instrument?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a time-bound non-closure test if it included measurable milestones, an independent review, and a genuine fallback only after the test fails?

### To Institutional Investors
What exact combination of permanent savings, utilization improvement, and execution safeguards would be enough for you to treat a non-closure or hybrid plan as economically equivalent?

### To the Expert Modeller
What are the minimum plant-by-plant indicators that would let the parties distinguish a durable structural solution from a temporary delay tactic?
