From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the key test is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins and reduce structural cost, or does it merely defer the problem?**

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family owners, and the state on one central point: **the current cost base and capacity are not acceptable**. The company cannot continue with German operations that remain structurally above target while margins are under pressure and competition is intensifying. A durable competitiveness reset is necessary.

We also agree with the union, the works council, and the state that **a disorderly conflict would destroy value**. Prolonged strikes, uncertainty, and a breakdown in industrial relations would be very costly. Capital markets do not reward drawn-out confrontation.

### Where we disagree
We disagree with any position that makes **“no closures, no forced redundancies, no framework changes”** a fixed starting point **without** an equally credible economic substitute.

That is not a solution. It is a constraint. If those red lines are maintained, then the alternative must still deliver:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost improvement,
- and a path back to target returns.

If it does not, then it simply preserves an uncompetitive structure and shifts the damage into the future. That would be unacceptable to us.

We also disagree with any suggestion that the restructuring can rely mainly on **temporary waivers, reversible flexibility, or symbolic concessions**. Those may help politically, but they do not solve a structural margin problem.

### What matters to us
We would support **either**:
1. a deep restructuring with closures and job reductions, **if necessary** to restore competitiveness; **or**
2. a negotiated alternative that avoids closures **only if** it achieves broadly the same economic outcome in a durable way.

But we will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a delay tactic,
- or a deal that protects every site while leaving the cost base materially uncompetitive.

### On labour
We accept that the workforce should not bear the burden alone. But from an investor standpoint, what matters is not whether sacrifice is “fair” in an abstract sense; what matters is whether the package **works**.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then the alternative must be concrete and measurable:
- substantial wage and cost moderation,
- productivity gains,
- capacity rationalisation,
- and enforceable commitments, not just aspirations.

### On management and owners
Management should be judged on whether it secures a credible path to restored margins. The family owners and state can support that, but they should not use governance to preserve a politically convenient but financially weak status quo.

### Our bottom line
We are prepared to back a negotiated solution **if and only if** it delivers:
- a durable reduction in structural cost,
- real capacity adjustment,
- visible progress toward target margins,
- and enough certainty to stabilize the company’s investment case.

If the outcome is a soft compromise that avoids the hard decisions and leaves the business still uncompetitive, we will regard that as value-destructive.

So our position is not “closures at any price,” but it is also not “no closures no matter what.”  
**We will support the solution that credibly restores competitiveness. Anything less will not get our support.**
