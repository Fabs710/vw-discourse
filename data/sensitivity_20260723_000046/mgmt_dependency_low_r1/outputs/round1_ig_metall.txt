The union side shares the core message from the Works Council: **we will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of restructuring**.

Let me be equally clear from the collective-bargaining side:

- **A durable solution must protect jobs and sites**
- **The burden cannot fall solely on the workforce**
- **There must be a fair sharing of sacrifice**
- **The collective wage framework cannot simply be torn up unilaterally**

We understand that the company is under real pressure: weak market conditions, high cost levels, and the need to finance the EV transition. We are not ignoring that reality. But management’s current logic — closures, job cuts, and lower pay first, with workers asked to absorb the shock — is **not acceptable**.

### Where we agree
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary or negotiated capacity reductions
- working-time solutions
- waiver of bonuses or employee-side financial contributions
- flexibility instruments that avoid dismissals
- productivity and cost measures if they are **mutual** and **reversible where possible**

That is a credible path if the goal is to stabilize the company without destroying its industrial base.

### Where we disagree
We reject the idea that German plants are simply disposable cost centers. Closing sites or forcing out large numbers of employees would:
- destroy trust
- intensify industrial conflict
- damage skills and production capability
- undermine the company’s long-term recovery

Likewise, any attempt to push through labour cost reductions **only on the workforce** while owners, management, and other stakeholders shield themselves is fundamentally unfair. If sacrifices are necessary, then:
- management must contribute,
- owners must contribute,
- and the workforce contribution must be tied to **job security and site security**.

### Our position
We are open to a serious negotiated package, but only if it includes:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Binding guarantees for employment and sites**
4. **A fair employee contribution in exchange for security**
5. **A negotiated, collective agreement rather than unilateral cuts**

If management refuses those conditions, then it should not expect peace. The peace obligation has ended, and the workforce is already showing that it is prepared to defend its interests.

So our message is simple:  
**We are ready to bargain hard for a shared-sacrifice solution. We are not ready to accept a restructuring built on layoffs, closures, and one-sided pain for employees.**
