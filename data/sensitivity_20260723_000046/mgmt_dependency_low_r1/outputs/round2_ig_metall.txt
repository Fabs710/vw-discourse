I’ll respond clearly and directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council and the state on the essentials:

- **No forced layoffs**
- **No one-sided burden on employees**
- **A restructuring must be durable, not cosmetic**
- **Industrial peace can only be restored through a fair settlement**

I also agree that simply preserving the current situation unchanged is not realistic. The company does need a credible path to lower costs and better capacity use.

## Where I disagree
### 1) On plant closures
I do **not** accept plant closures as a starting point, and I am not persuaded by the argument that they are simply unavoidable.

If management’s first move is closure pressure, then that is not a serious negotiation — it is coercion. And once closures are normalized, the next step is always the same: more insecurity, more pressure, and employees paying for strategic mistakes they did not make.

So I align with the no-closure line as long as there is a credible alternative. But I also want to be precise: **the alternative must be economically meaningful**. A slogan is not enough.

### 2) On compulsory redundancies
This is a hard red line for us as well: **no compulsory redundancies**.

If the company needs lower headcount or capacity, then the instruments must be:
- voluntary exits
- retirement and bridge models
- internal redeployment
- reduced working time
- qualification and transfer measures
- negotiated temporary burdens

Forced layoffs are destructive, and they would trigger conflict that the company cannot control.

### 3) On “material and structural” contributions
Management, owners, and investors keep repeating that the workforce contribution must be “material” and “structural.” I accept that **employees cannot be the only ones contributing**, but I reject any notion that the workforce should absorb permanent sacrifice while others keep their position unchanged.

A fair package must include contributions from:
- management
- owners
- employees
- and, if needed, public stakeholders

If workers give up parts of pay, bonuses, or flexibility, then there must be **binding security in return**. Otherwise this is not a contribution — it is a transfer of risk downward.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a negotiated package if it includes all of the following:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Binding site and employment security for a defined period**
4. **A negotiated employee contribution**
   - e.g. bonus waivers
   - temporary wage moderation
   - working-time reductions
   - flexibility instruments
5. **A credible management and owner contribution**
   - management restraint
   - investment commitments
   - internal savings on overheads and capital allocation
6. **Real capacity reduction without closure**
   - shift changes
   - working-time models
   - redeployment
   - voluntary programs
7. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
   - no unilateral dismantling of wage standards
   - no move toward permanent individualization of pay

That is the only route that looks like shared sacrifice rather than one-sided damage.

## What I reject
I reject:
- closure-first strategies
- layoffs by pressure
- dismantling the collective wage framework
- temporary concessions with permanent employee losses
- “competitiveness” arguments that mean only labour must pay

If management wants labour peace, it needs to stop treating the workforce as the adjustment reserve of last resort.

## On the round’s central trade-off
Here is the core question:  
**Can the company reduce cost and capacity enough without closures and forced redundancies?**

My answer is: **yes, if management is serious about alternatives and if owners share the burden**.  
If the answer from management is “no, because only closures and layoffs count,” then we are not negotiating a solution — we are heading into conflict.

## My bottom line
We are ready for a fair, durable settlement. But it must be built on:

- **job security**
- **site security**
- **collective bargaining protection**
- **shared sacrifice**
- **no forced redundancies**
- **no plant closures**

If those conditions are respected, we can discuss a genuine contribution from the workforce. If not, then the union will not soften its position — and the industrial conflict will escalate.
