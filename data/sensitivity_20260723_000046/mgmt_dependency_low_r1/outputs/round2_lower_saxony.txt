As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: I am not here to defend a status quo that ignores competitiveness. But I am equally not prepared to endorse a restructuring that treats German sites and regional employment as expendable.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family owner, and the investors on one point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness reset**. If the German footprint is structurally too costly, that has to be addressed. A merely symbolic compromise would only postpone the crisis.

I also agree with the Works Council and the union that **the burden cannot fall on employees alone**. The state will not support a settlement that destroys trust, destroys skills, and pushes the full adjustment cost onto the workforce.

## Where I disagree
### On plant closures
I do **not** accept a closure-first strategy. And I do not accept the idea that closures should remain casually “on the table” as a routine bargaining lever.

A plant closure in Germany is not just an internal efficiency measure. It means:
- lasting damage to regional employment,
- loss of industrial capability,
- political and social instability,
- and a serious weakening of the company’s role as a regional anchor.

If management believes a closure is unavoidable, it must prove that **all serious alternatives have been exhausted**. So far, I do not see that case made convincingly.

### On compulsory redundancies
I align with the workforce side here: **forced layoffs are not the way to secure a durable settlement**. They would deepen conflict, weaken morale, and likely damage productivity and know-how in ways that undermine the very competitiveness management says it wants to restore.

If capacity has to come down, then the first instruments should be:
- working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- voluntary exits and bridge models.

### On the demand for “material” sacrifice
Management, owners, and investors keep repeating that the workforce contribution must be “structural.” I understand the point. But I need to say this clearly: **a contribution is only legitimate if it is balanced by security and a credible future for the sites**.

I do not accept a model where employees give up pay, guarantees, and stability while management and capital preserve all strategic flexibility for themselves.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a package if it contains all of the following:
1. **No plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Negotiated capacity reduction through working-time and internal flexibility**
4. **A fair employee contribution**
5. **Management and owner contributions as well**
6. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies**
7. **A credible employment and site framework through 2030**

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a package that guts regional employment,
- a path that sidelines co-determination in practice,
- or a settlement that uses crisis language to force through irreversible site losses.

The company’s long-term health depends on social partnership, not on confrontation dressed up as necessity.

## My message to management
If you want state support, then bring forward a package that is **economically credible and socially sustainable**. That means real savings, yes — but not by making Germany the sacrifice zone.

## My message to the Works Council and union
I support your refusal of forced redundancies and blanket closures. But I also need you to be prepared for real contribution if the company is to remain strong. That contribution must be negotiated, proportionate, and tied to concrete guarantees — not open-ended pain.

## Bottom line
My position remains:
- **No closure-first logic**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **Yes to negotiated restructuring**
- **Yes to real cost improvement**
- **Yes to protecting the company as a regional economic anchor**

If there is to be a deal, it must preserve industrial substance in Germany, not just financial arithmetic on paper.
