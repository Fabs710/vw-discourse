Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

I hear the rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand the concern about trust. But I will not pretend that the current cost base is compatible with the future of this business. It is not.

We are facing a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip.

- European demand is shrinking.
- Chinese and other low-cost competitors are intensifying pressure.
- Our German factory costs are far above target.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales goal.
- And if we do not fix the cost and capacity structure now, we will not have the freedom to invest in future products, battery technology, software, and the 2030 portfolio.

So I agree with one point: this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. It must be durable. But that is exactly why the current employee-side position is not sufficient.

### On your core demands

**1. “Keep every German plant open.”**  
That is not a credible starting point. I am not committing to preserving every site regardless of economics. We must have the option of plant closures, capacity consolidation, and deeper structural changes if that is what the numbers require.

**2. “No compulsory redundancies.”**  
As a blanket principle, that may sound humane, but it is not a viable guarantee if we need material workforce reduction. We need meaningful headcount reduction, not just language that hides the problem.

**3. “Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.”**  
I am not prepared to give a blanket guarantee that freezes the structure through 2030 while our competitors move faster and cheaper. That would lock in today’s inefficiencies and postpone the problem.

### Where I do agree

I do agree that this must be a negotiated settlement, not unilateral disruption.  
I do agree that we need investment in future products and technology.  
And I do agree that labour can contribute through working-time measures, redeployment, and productivity changes.

But let me be explicit: **those measures alone will not close the gap.** We need a genuine labour-cost contribution and a structural reduction in capacity.

### What management needs

For any agreement to be credible, it must include:

- a **durable reduction in German production capacity**;
- a **substantial reduction in labour costs**;
- a **material reduction in headcount**;
- and enough flexibility to restore the brand’s margin trajectory.

That may be achieved through closures, or through an equally effective package of consolidation, job reductions, working-time changes, and cost concessions. I am open on the means. I am not open on the outcome being too small.

### On strikes and confrontation

I would prefer a negotiated solution. But prolonged strikes that damage output, customers, and investor confidence will not change the underlying economics. They would only waste time and deepen the pressure on all sides. If we are forced into confrontation, management is prepared for that — but I would rather use this moment to reach a settlement that is hard, durable, and fit for the company’s future.

### Bottom line

We are not looking for a symbolic compromise. We need a **structural realignment** that restores competitiveness.

So my position is this:

- no agreement without a **real reduction in cost and capacity**,
- no agreement without a **meaningful employee contribution**,
- no agreement without a package that can actually lift margins and fund investment.

If the workforce side wants to avoid closures, then it must come forward with an alternative that delivers the same structural savings. If it cannot, then closures remain on the table.
