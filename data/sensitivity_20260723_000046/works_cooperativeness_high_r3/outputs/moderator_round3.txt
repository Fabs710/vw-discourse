Core impasse, procedurally:

## Where the stalemate sits
The discourse is blocked by three linked disagreements:

1. **Necessity of closures**
   - Management, investors, and the controlling family treat plant closures or closure-equivalent capacity withdrawal as still potentially necessary.
   - Works council, union, and state government treat closures as unacceptable unless strictly proven unavoidable.

2. **Definition of “credible” restructuring**
   - One side defines credibility as a hard, enforceable savings package that may include closures.
   - The other defines credibility as a binding no-closure package with job and site guarantees.

3. **Lack of a shared quantified bridge**
   - Everyone refers to the expert ranges, but there is still no agreed plant-level savings bridge showing:
     - the actual savings target,
     - what non-destructive measures deliver,
     - and what residual gap remains.

So the deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **which instruments are admissible before the numbers are fully proven**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to test

### 1) Sequenced “no-closure first, closure review last” package
**Proposal:**  
Agree on a first-phase package that uses only non-closure measures for a fixed period:
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- redeployment,
- working-time measures,
- productivity commitments,
- temporary employee contributions.

Then add a **pre-agreed review gate**:
- if independent plant-level analysis shows a remaining gap above an agreed threshold after implementation,
- the parties reopen negotiations on closure-equivalent measures.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It gives the worker side immediate protection from closures, while giving management and investors a defined path to revisit structural measures if the savings prove insufficient.

---

### 2) Site-specific protection with conditional flexibility
**Proposal:**  
Rather than a blanket “all plants” or “closures allowed” rule, classify sites into three groups after independent analysis:
- **Protected sites**: no closure or headcount reduction through 2030.
- **Adjustment sites**: capacity reduction, shift changes, or product reassignment without closure.
- **Review sites**: only these can be reconsidered if the savings gap remains.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It preserves the industrial base where viability is strongest, while acknowledging that not all sites may have the same overcapacity profile.

---

### 3) Guaranteed burden-sharing with enforceable safeguards
**Proposal:**  
Tie any employee-side concessions to hard reciprocal commitments from management and owners:
- no compulsory redundancies for the initial term,
- binding investment commitments,
- co-determination monitoring,
- and a transparent savings dashboard.

If savings milestones are met, the company gets flexibility on future measures; if not, the burden-sharing terms reset.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It converts the argument from “trust us” into a monitored exchange: employee flexibility in return for measurable employer restraint and investment.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the minimum quantified annual savings target you require, and how much of that target can you commit to closing through non-closure measures before reopening the question of closures?**

### To the works council
**If an independent plant-level analysis shows a residual gap after voluntary exits and productivity measures, what additional non-closure instrument would you accept that still protects the job-security framework?**

### To the union
**What recurring labour-cost relief, in quantified annual terms, are you prepared to deliver in exchange for binding no-closure and no-compulsory-redundancy commitments?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you support a time-limited no-closure package if it includes measurable savings, enforceable monitoring, and a predefined review point for unresolved gaps?**

### To the state government
**Are you prepared to endorse a conditional framework that protects sites initially but allows a narrowly defined review of specific sites if independent analysis proves non-closure measures insufficient?**

### To institutional investors
**What precise savings threshold and implementation standard would make a closure-free settlement acceptable to you?**

### To the expert
**Can you provide a site-by-site savings bridge with a clear residual-gap threshold that distinguishes “no-closure plausible,” “closure-equivalent likely,” and “closure necessary”?**
