### Moderator Summary — Round 2

#### 1) What has been agreed
- A **cosmetic deal is not acceptable**; all sides accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that the discussion must be **quantified**, not purely rhetorical.
- Most parties accept that **employee-side contributions are likely necessary** in some form.
- There is general recognition that **industrial conflict would destroy value**, so implementation risk matters.
- The expert’s framing was accepted: the key question is whether a **no-closure package** can close the gap at sufficient scale and durability.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures and compulsory redundancies**:
  - Works council, union, and state government reject them as a starting point.
  - Management says they must remain on the table.
  - Investors are open to avoiding them only if savings are truly sufficient and enforceable.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**:
  - Employee representatives and the state want site/job protection.
  - Management says such guarantees may conflict with the needed restructuring scale.
- **What counts as sufficient restructuring**:
  - Management and investors emphasize deeper capacity and cost reduction.
  - Labour-side actors emphasize burden-sharing without dismantling sites or employment protections.
- **Governance and bargaining leverage**:
  - The state and employee side want protection mechanisms.
  - Management wants flexibility to implement structural change.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **actual size of the savings gap** has not been established.
- No side has yet shown a fully credible comparison between:
  - a **no-closure package** relying on voluntary exits, working-time changes, productivity, and redeployment, and
  - a **closure-led restructuring** with higher certainty but greater conflict.
- The discussion has not yet resolved:
  - the **one-off costs** of each option,
  - the **timing** of savings,
  - the **risk of underdelivery** in voluntary measures,
  - the **impact on production continuity**, and
  - the **net value effect** under different scenarios.
- There is still no shared answer on whether non-destructive measures can achieve the required savings at the necessary scale.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle to **scenario comparison**:
  1. define the annual savings target,
  2. model savings from non-closure measures,
  3. estimate the residual gap,
  4. compare this with closure-led alternatives,
  5. assess timing, execution risk, and net value.
- Ask each party to state:
  - the **minimum package** it would regard as credible,
  - the **quantitative threshold** it requires,
  - and which concessions it considers non-negotiable.
- Keep the next round focused on **numbers, implementation, and trade-offs**, rather than repeated restatement of red lines.
