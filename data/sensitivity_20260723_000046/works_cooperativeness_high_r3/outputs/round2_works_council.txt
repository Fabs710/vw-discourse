Management keeps repeating “structural competitiveness” as if that alone justifies dismantling jobs and sites. It does not.

Let me respond point by point.

### To management
We agree on one thing: a cosmetic deal is not enough. This company needs a durable solution. But your conclusion is wrong.

You say:
- closures must remain on the table,
- compulsory redundancies are unavoidable,
- binding protections through 2030 are too restrictive.

We reject that. Hard.

A restructuring that starts with plant closures and forced job cuts is not a plan for the future; it is a plan to shift the burden onto the workforce and permanently weaken the company’s German industrial base. That is exactly what we will not accept.

If you want labour to contribute, then show us a restructuring that does **not** destroy trust:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no breaking of the job-security framework,
- binding site and job guarantees through 2030,
- and a real role for co-determination in implementation.

If those conditions are not met, then there is no basis for a deal.

### To institutional investors
You are right that a fake restructuring is worthless. But you are wrong if you think “meaningful headcount reduction” must mean forced layoffs or closures. That is a managerial choice, not an economic law.

There are other instruments:
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exit packages,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- temporary capacity adjustments,
- qualification and transition programs,
- productivity improvements negotiated with the workforce.

If you want credibility, then quantify the savings needed and prove that these alternatives cannot deliver them before demanding closures or layoffs. Right now, you are treating the workforce as the easiest lever. That is not acceptable.

### To the controlling family
You say you do not want a value-destructive confrontation. On that, we agree. But value is also destroyed when management breaks trust, tears up guarantees, and triggers mass resistance. You cannot ask for social peace while threatening the workforce’s existence.

If you are serious about long-term value, then back a settlement that preserves:
- domestic sites,
- qualified industrial capacity,
- co-determination,
- and a stable workforce through 2030.

That is not “freezing inefficiency.” That is protecting the foundations of productive capability.

### To the state government
Your position is much closer to ours. We appreciate that you reject closures as a starting point and insist on no compulsory redundancies. But we need more than general political support. We need binding protection.

The state should use its blocking position to ensure:
- no closure decisions,
- no forced layoffs,
- no hollowing out of German production,
- and no bypassing of co-determination.

If management wants your support, then it must present an industrial strategy that keeps every German plant open and uses voluntary, reversible measures first.

### On the numbers
The moderator is right that we need specifics. So here is our benchmark:

1. **First use non-destructive measures**  
   Voluntary attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, training, internal flexibility, and negotiated productivity steps.

2. **Then test capacity reductions without closures**  
   Shift volumes, smooth utilization, and reallocate production within the existing plant network.

3. **Only if there is still a verified gap** would we discuss additional labour-side contributions — but still not closures or compulsory redundancies.

The burden of proof is on management and investors to show that these alternatives cannot close the gap. Until then, closures and forced layoffs are off the table.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate a hard restructuring. We are **not** prepared to negotiate away German plants, compulsory redundancies, or the historic job-security framework.

If management insists that those are the only credible tools, then it is not negotiating — it is demanding surrender. And that will not happen.
