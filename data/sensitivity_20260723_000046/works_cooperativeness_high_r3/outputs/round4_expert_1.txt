Yes. On the restructuring and labour-cost side, the key question is whether a negotiated, closure-free package can close the cost and capacity gap at sufficient scale and durability.

## What is established
- In large German automotive groups, **labour-side measures can produce substantial recurring savings** if they are broad and sustained:
  - voluntary exits / severance,
  - natural attrition,
  - working-time reductions,
  - shift and capacity adjustments,
  - redeployment,
  - training and qualification,
  - productivity commitments,
  - temporary wage or bonus moderation,
  - and selective site-level consolidation without full closure.
- A package built around these instruments can often generate a **material annual savings stream**, especially if the company has:
  - high fixed labour overhead,
  - excess capacity,
  - underutilised plants,
  - and generous legacy guarantees.
- However, **non-closure measures have execution risk**:
  - voluntary exits may undershoot,
  - productivity gains may arrive slowly,
  - working-time cuts can be temporary,
  - and redeployment may shift, not eliminate, cost.
- In German co-determined companies, the feasibility of closures is not just an economic issue; it is also a **governance and bargaining issue** because supervisory-board majorities, works councils, and political shareholders can create effective veto points.

## Likely implications
- A **deep no-closure package** is most plausible when:
  - the required savings gap is moderate rather than extreme,
  - the company can combine multiple instruments,
  - and management is willing to accept a longer adjustment path.
- A **closure-led restructuring** tends to deliver:
  - faster and more certain fixed-cost reduction,
  - but higher industrial-relations conflict,
  - greater one-off restructuring costs,
  - higher legal and operational risk,
  - and potentially larger production-disruption losses.
- From a financial perspective, the right comparison is not “closures vs no closures” in the abstract, but:
  1. annual recurring savings,
  2. one-off restructuring cost,
  3. timing of cash impact,
  4. delivery risk,
  5. and the effect on output continuity and future investment capacity.

## What is not established from the scenario alone
- The **actual annual savings target** is not specified.
- The **plant-by-plant cost base** is not provided.
- The **residual gap** after non-closure measures is not quantified.
- So it is **not possible to say from the information given that closures are necessary**.
- It is also **not possible to say that a no-closure deal is sufficient** without the plant-level bridge.

## Practical restructuring logic
The standard industrial-relations sequence in Germany would usually be:
1. quantify overcapacity and fixed-cost burden,
2. test voluntary and negotiated measures first,
3. benchmark the savings against the target,
4. identify any residual structural gap,
5. and only then assess whether closure-equivalent measures are needed.

That is exactly why the requested **independent plant-by-plant savings bridge** is the correct next step.

## What that bridge should contain
For each plant, the analysis should show:
- capacity and utilisation,
- product mix and volume outlook,
- fixed labour and overhead cost,
- variable cost impact of output changes,
- savings from:
  - voluntary exits,
  - attrition,
  - working-time changes,
  - redeployment,
  - training,
  - productivity actions,
  - shift/line rationalisation,
  - and partial capacity withdrawal,
- one-off restructuring cost,
- implementation timing,
- and the remaining gap versus the company’s required savings.

## Threshold interpretation
A useful interpretation is:
- **“No-closure plausible”**: non-closure measures cover most or all of the required gap with acceptable durability.
- **“Closure-equivalent likely”**: non-closure measures cover a large share, but not enough to fully restore competitiveness.
- **“Closure necessary”**: the residual gap remains too large even after all credible negotiated measures.

That threshold cannot be set responsibly without the actual numbers.
