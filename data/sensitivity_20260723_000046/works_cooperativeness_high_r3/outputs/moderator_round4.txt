## Core impasse

The discussion has stalled on one central issue:

**Whether the company can reach a sufficiently durable savings target through a deep no-closure package, or whether management and investors require closure-equivalent capacity withdrawal to close the residual gap.**

More specifically, the parties are split on three linked questions:

1. **Target ambiguity**  
   No one has yet fixed the actual annual savings target in a way all parties accept.

2. **Evidence gap**  
   The parties agree in principle that a plant-by-plant savings bridge is needed, but it has not yet been produced.

3. **Threshold disagreement**  
   Stakeholders disagree on what level of residual gap would justify closures, compulsory redundancies, or a permanent no-closure commitment.

So the impasse is not only ideological; it is also **procedural and quantitative**: the parties are debating instruments before agreeing on the required scale of savings and the plant-level facts.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Quantified no-closure test phase
A time-limited agreement could be built around a **first-phase no-closure test**:

- no plant closures during the test period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- immediate commissioning of the independent plant-by-plant capacity and savings-bridge model,
- agreed savings milestones,
- and a predefined review point after the model is delivered.

**Purpose:**  
This gives the workforce and state side a meaningful non-closure test, while giving management and investors a disciplined way to test whether the gap can actually be closed without footprint reduction.

---

### 2) Conditional structural option
A second compromise could be a **conditional fallback structure**:

- the parties first exhaust voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, working-time measures, productivity actions, and temporary employee contributions;
- if the independent model still shows a material residual gap above an agreed threshold, then only **predefined closure-equivalent options** may be considered;
- any such option would require a fresh consultation step and co-determination process.

**Purpose:**  
This avoids an open-ended closure threat while not locking management into an economically insufficient outcome.

---

### 3) Balanced burden-sharing package with protection floor
A third compromise could combine **worker contributions with hard protections**:

- temporary working-time or compensation moderation,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- training and productivity commitments,
- investment commitments and future-product allocation,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a binding site/job protection floor through a specified date.

**Purpose:**  
This attempts to bridge the divide between those seeking material cost relief and those seeking social stability.

---

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What specific annual savings target are you working toward, and what residual gap would remain after every non-closure measure has been fully modeled plant by plant?**

---

### To the Works Council
**If the independent model shows a residual gap that cannot be closed without some form of capacity reduction, what exact threshold would you still consider compatible with a no-closure settlement?**

---

### To the Industrial Union
**What quantified, recurring labour-side contribution are you prepared to put on the table if management accepts no compulsory redundancies and a binding implementation role for co-determination?**

---

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum annual recurring savings and what maximum one-off restructuring cost would make a closure-free package acceptable to you?**

---

### To the Controlling Family
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure test phase if it included hard milestones, independent verification, and a pre-agreed fallback only if the quantified gap remains material?**

---

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a conditional arrangement in which closures remain off the table initially, but the independent model determines whether additional measures are justified later?**

---

## Procedural next step

The most useful next step would be to circulate a **single shared mandate** for the independent analysis with agreed assumptions on:

- annual savings target,
- plant-level capacity and utilization,
- fixed and variable cost by site,
- timing of savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- and a residual-gap threshold that triggers review.

That would convert the debate from competing claims into a common fact base.
