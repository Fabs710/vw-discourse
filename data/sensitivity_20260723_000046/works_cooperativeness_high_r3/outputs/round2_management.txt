## First: the shared diagnosis is not enough

But the employee-side response still does **not** answer the core question: **how do we cut the cost base and capacity fast enough, durably enough, and at sufficient scale to restore competitiveness?**

Saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no breaking guarantees” is a red line, not a solution.

## To the Works Council
You keep saying we should start with non-destructive measures only. We already know those measures:
- attrition,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- training,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary exits.

Those are useful, but they are **not enough** on their own.

If you want to rule out closures and forced reductions, then you need to show — with credible numbers — how those instruments will still deliver:
- a **material capacity reduction**,
- a **real labour-cost reduction**,
- and a **structural improvement in returns**.

Right now, you are asking management to accept a package that may preserve the appearance of stability while leaving the cost base too high. That is exactly the kind of cosmetic outcome we cannot accept.

And let me be explicit:  
**binding site and job guarantees through 2030 are not compatible with the degree of restructuring we need, unless you can show equivalent savings elsewhere.**

## To the Union
You are closer to the mark when you say the burden must be shared and that labour-side contributions are possible. I acknowledge that.

So I need to ask directly:
- What is the quantified savings contribution from labour?
- What is the duration?
- What is enforceable?

If the answer is “a lot of flexibility, but still no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies,” then we are back to slogans.

- shrinking European demand,
- aggressive low-cost competition,
- structural overcapacity,
