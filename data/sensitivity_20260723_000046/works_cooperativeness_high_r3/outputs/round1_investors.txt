We agree with the central diagnosis: **the current cost and capacity structure is not competitive**, and a cosmetic deal would be unacceptable. If this company cannot restore its margin trajectory, it will continue to destroy value, and capital will be reallocated elsewhere.

That said, our support is **conditional on a credible, durable path back to competitive margins**. We are not interested in preserving the status quo, but we are equally not prepared to back a restructuring that looks tough on paper while leaving the cost base structurally too high.

### Where we agree
- Management is right that the business needs **real structural improvement**, not delay.
- The union and works council are right that **prolonged conflict is value-destructive**.
- The state and controlling family are right that a **one-sided, purely disruptive closure strategy** could be damaging if it triggers long industrial conflict and weakens the brand.

### Where we disagree

#### To works council and union
Your rejection of plant closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable, but in its current form it sounds like a **constraint, not a solution**.

We cannot support a package that simply protects every site and every legacy guarantee if that leaves the company with an uncompetitive fixed-cost base. If the outcome is:
- no closures,
- no meaningful capacity reduction,
- no durable labour-cost reduction,
- and no measurable improvement in margins,

then that is not restructuring. It is postponement.

We are open to alternatives to closures, but only if they deliver **comparable savings with certainty**. That means:
- credible capacity consolidation,
- genuine headcount reduction,
- durable labour-cost concessions,
- and enforceable implementation, not vague intentions.

#### To management
We agree that closures cannot be ruled out as a matter of principle. But we would also caution against framing closures as the default answer if other paths can achieve the same economics with less disruption.

What matters to us is not whether savings come from closures or from another instrument. What matters is whether the package is:
- **credible**
- **durable**
- **quantified**
- **and sufficient to restore competitive margins**

If management comes back with a deal that claims major savings but lacks enforceability, timing, or scale, we will not consider that acceptable.

#### To the regional state government
We respect the political and regional concerns. But “no closures as the starting point” cannot become a veto on necessary change if it prevents the company from regaining competitiveness.

A protected domestic footprint is only valuable if the business behind it remains viable. If the current structure keeps returns below cost of capital, then value destruction continues. The state should be focused on the company’s long-term viability, not on preserving every site regardless of economics.

#### To the controlling family
We agree that governance stability matters. We also agree that a destructive conflict would damage enterprise value.

But stability without competitiveness is not a solution. If the family wants the market to believe in this turnaround, then the outcome must show that the company can make hard decisions and reduce the cost base materially. Capital markets will not reward symbolic compromise.

### Our bottom line
We will support **only** a settlement that clearly and credibly restores competitiveness. That means:

- a **durable reduction in labour and structural costs**
- a **meaningful capacity adjustment**
- a **measurable headcount reduction**
- and a **credible margin path** that can be defended to investors

We do **not** need closures as an ideological outcome. But we do need the economics to work. If the final package leaves the company with an uncompetitive cost base, we will regard that as a failure and will not support it.

So our message is simple:  
**No cosmetic deal. No prolonged conflict. No outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired.**  
Bring back a package with real, enforceable savings and a clear route to competitive returns.
