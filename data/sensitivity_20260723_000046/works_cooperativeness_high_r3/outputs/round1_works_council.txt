We are entering this discussion with one clear principle: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of transformation. What management is now proposing goes beyond restructuring and into a unilateral dismantling of proven industrial sites, jobs, and trust. That is not an acceptable basis for a deal.

Our priorities are clear:

1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **Rule out operational layoffs.**
3. **Secure binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework.**
5. **Use alternative restructuring tools instead** — temporary capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, training, and a negotiated contribution from labour, but only under fair and binding conditions.

We are not refusing change. We are refusing a restructuring that makes workers pay for management’s strategic mistakes through closures and job destruction. If the company needs savings, then management must first present a plan that protects the industrial footprint, preserves livelihoods, and shares the burden fairly across all stakeholders.

If the proposal includes closures, forced layoffs, or the breaking of the historic employment protections, then we will oppose it clearly and decisively.
