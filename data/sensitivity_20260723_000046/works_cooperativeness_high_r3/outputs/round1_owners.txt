I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **the company has a real competitiveness problem and cannot simply preserve the status quo**. If margins stay where they are, the long-term value of this enterprise will erode, and that is not acceptable for the owners, for employees, or for the regional economy.

But I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible response must be plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That is too blunt, too destabilising, and too likely to destroy value rather than create it.

### To management
You are right that a cosmetic exercise will not work. We need durable cost and capacity improvement, not slogans.  
Where I disagree is on your framing that closures remain the default if the numbers warrant them. A closure-led approach is not something I can support lightly, because it risks:
- prolonged industrial conflict,
- reputational damage,
- loss of trust in governance,
- and permanent impairment of the brand and workforce capability.

That would be value-destructive.

If you want support from the controlling shareholder, you need to show that the restructuring is **credible, durable, and executable without blowing up labour relations**. A plan that depends on forcing through plant closures against the clear veto positions in this structure is not a serious governance path.

### To the works council
I understand and respect the insistence on protecting sites and rejecting compulsory redundancies. Those concerns are legitimate.  
Where I cannot fully align is on the implication that every German plant must be preserved unchanged and that the existing employment framework can remain intact regardless of the economics. That would simply push the problem into the future and weaken the company further.

The workforce should be prepared to consider **meaningful** contributions:
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity adjustments,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and wage-related concessions where necessary.

But those contributions must be tied to a restructuring that actually restores competitiveness. A purely defensive stance will not be enough.

### To the union
I agree that labour can contribute and that a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.  
I also agree that trust matters and that a one-sided burden shift would be counterproductive.

But I disagree with any suggestion that management can be held to binding guarantees while the savings side remains vague. If labour is asked to make concessions, those concessions must translate into measurable improvement in cost, capacity, and margins. Otherwise we are just buying time.

### To the state government
Your emphasis on regional stability and industrial capability is understandable and, in part, I share it. But I would caution against treating “no closures as the starting point” as an absolute principle. If the business case truly requires structural change, then governance must not become a mechanism for freezing all adjustment indefinitely.

That said, your point about avoiding unnecessary damage is important. A solution that preserves the domestic industrial base where possible, uses voluntary instruments first, and maintains co-determination is preferable to a confrontational closure strategy.

### My position as controlling shareholder
I am **not** prepared to support:
- a weak restructuring that leaves the company uncompetitive,
- a plan that permanently impairs enterprise value,
- or any outcome that undermines ownership control or governance stability.

I am **also not** willing to back a value-destructive confrontation if there is a credible alternative.

So the standard is this:
- real structural improvement,
- durable savings,
- a fair but meaningful labour contribution,
- and stability in governance and industrial relations.

If management can deliver that without relying on closures, that is the preferable path. If not, then I will not endorse symbolic half-measures that postpone the crisis. But I will also not rush to support a confrontational closure strategy that could do long-term damage to the asset we are all supposed to steward.

So: **no to cosmetic compromise, no to permanent erosion of value, and no to destabilising escalation.**  
What I will support is a hard, credible restructuring that restores competitiveness while preserving the long-term strength of the company.
