1. **What has been agreed**
- All parties accept that the company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem** and that a **purely cosmetic solution is insufficient**.
- There is broad agreement that any settlement should be **durable**, **negotiated**, and aimed at restoring long-term viability.
- Several parties accept that **labour-side contributions** may be part of a package, including:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - redeployment,
  - training,
  - temporary capacity reductions,
  - productivity measures,
  - and possibly financial concessions.
- There is also shared recognition that a **prolonged industrial conflict would be value-destructive**.

2. **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works council, union, state government, and controlling family oppose them as a preferred path; management and institutional investors keep them available, at least as an option.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Workforce-side actors reject them; management and investors want meaningful headcount reduction and do not accept a blanket exclusion.
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**: Workforce-side actors and the state want strong protections; management and investors see this as too restrictive.
- **Scope of labour contribution**: Labour representatives are prepared to contribute only within a framework that protects sites and jobs; management and investors say the proposed employee-side measures may not be sufficient on their own.
- **Governance balance**: The state and controlling family stress co-determination, stability, and regional footprint; management and investors prioritize flexibility and enforceable restructuring.

3. **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- **How much savings are needed vs. how they are achieved**: No common baseline yet on the size of the required cost/capacity reduction.
- **Savings vs. protections**: No settlement on whether savings come from closures, headcount cuts, capacity consolidation, wage/concession measures, or a combination.
- **Short-term pain vs. long-term viability**: The parties have not reconciled the immediate burden of restructuring with the demand for future investment in products and technology.
- **Flexibility vs. certainty**: Management/investors want implementation flexibility; labour/state want enforceable guarantees.
- **Conflict avoidance vs. structural change**: Everyone prefers avoiding escalation, but there is no agreed mechanism that both delivers material change and preserves industrial peace.

4. **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to **numbers and scenarios**: what savings, headcount change, and capacity adjustment are actually required?
- Ask each side to propose **one concrete package** that meets its red lines and quantify the trade-offs.
- Clarify which measures are **temporary**, which are **structural**, and which would be **legally or politically binding**.
- Focus discussion on a **ranked sequence of options**: voluntary measures first, then redeployment/training, then capacity changes, with closures/redundancies explicitly tested against alternatives.
