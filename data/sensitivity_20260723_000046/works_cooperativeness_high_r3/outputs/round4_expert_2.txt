## 1) Comparative scenario assessment

### A. Deep no-closure package
Typical instruments:
- voluntary exits / severance
- natural attrition
- redeployment
- working-time reductions
- shift / line / capacity consolidation without plant closure
- productivity commitments
- temporary wage/bonus moderation
- training and qualification changes

### Likely economics
**Recurring savings:**  
- Best estimate: **€600m–€900m/year**
- Upside if implementation is strong: perhaps **€1.0bn/year**
- Downside if voluntary exits undershoot or wage concessions are shallow: **€400m–€600m/year**

**One-off cost:**  
- Severance, early retirement, training, redeployment, temporary compensation: likely **material**, but generally lower than closure-led restructuring
- Best estimate range: **€300m–€800m** one-off, depending on headcount reduction depth and pension/severance treatment

**Timing:**
- Faster measures: working-time changes, bonus moderation, short-cycle productivity actions
- Slower measures: voluntary exits, redeployment, training
- Full annual run-rate savings often realized over **12–36 months**

**Execution risk:**
- Moderate to high
- Main risks:
  - under-subscription to voluntary exits
  - slow productivity delivery
  - concessions expiring after a few years
  - “saved” jobs remaining but cost base not falling enough

**Industrial-relations risk:**
- Low to moderate if negotiated well
- Higher if worker-side contributions are large but site/job guarantees are weak

### B. Closure-led restructuring
Typical instruments:
- closure of one or more plants
- headcount reduction through site shutdown
- transfer of some volumes to other sites or external partners
- stronger capacity withdrawal

### Likely economics
**Recurring savings:**  
- Often higher and more certain on fixed overhead
- Best estimate: **€800m–€1.3bn/year** if a genuinely material footprint reduction is executed

**One-off cost:**  
- Usually higher than no-closure
- Severance, transfer cost, write-downs, logistics change, cleanup, duplication during ramp-down, supply-chain disruption
- Best estimate range: **€1.0bn–€2.5bn+**, depending on number and size of sites

**Timing:**
- Can be faster once decided, but actual realized savings may be delayed by ramp-down, labor proceedings, and production migration
- Run-rate savings often more back-end loaded than management hopes

**Execution risk:**
- Lower on the cost model if implemented cleanly
- But operational risk can be high if product transfer, quality, or supplier continuity is disrupted

**Industrial-relations risk:**
- High to very high
- Likely strike risk, legal challenge risk, co-determination friction, political interference, and reputational damage

## 2) Net value logic

The correct comparison is **not** just “who saves more annually.” It is:

**Net value = present value of recurring savings − present value of one-off costs − expected disruption losses**

### Closure-led restructuring
Pros:
- stronger certainty on permanent cost removal
- easier to hit a large savings target if the target is extreme

Cons:
- large one-off cost
- likely output disruption from conflict
- risk of delayed implementation
- possible erosion of future productivity through loss of trust and skilled labor

### No-closure package
Pros:
- lower conflict risk
- faster stabilization of operations
- lower one-off cost
- better chance of preserving know-how and production continuity

Cons:
- savings may be insufficient
- some measures are temporary
- harder to force deep structural change
- risk of underdelivery if governance is weak

### Indicative net-value view
If the company’s required savings need is:
- **≤ €600m/year**: no-closure package is often economically plausible
- **€600m–€900m/year**: borderline; depends on execution quality and durability
- **≥ €900m–€1.0bn/year**: closure-equivalent measures become increasingly likely
- **well above €1bn/year**: plant closure or closure-equivalent footprint reduction likely needed

These are not legal thresholds, just restructuring-economics thresholds.

## 3) What matters most: residual gap

The core question is:

> After all negotiated non-closure measures, what annual gap remains?

A practical threshold framework:

- **Residual gap < €100m–€200m/year**  
  → no-closure still plausible; could be bridged by minor additional concessions or productivity actions

- **Residual gap €200m–€400m/year**  
  → no-closure is strained; likely requires stronger labor concessions or selective capacity withdrawal

- **Residual gap > €400m–€500m/year**  
  → closure-equivalent measures become much more likely to be necessary

- **Residual gap > €600m/year**  
  → closures or very deep footprint rationalization are hard to avoid if competitiveness targets are firm

## 4) Why plant-by-plant analysis is essential
You cannot answer this credibly without site-level data on:
- capacity utilization
- product mix
- fixed vs variable cost
- overtime and shift structure
- local labor agreements
- logistics cost
- transferability of production
- capital intensity
- future model allocation

A plant that is underutilized but highly specialized may be expensive to close but also expensive to keep.  
A different plant may be “protected” politically but not economically.  
So a blunt group-wide average is not enough.

## 5) Recommended savings-bridge structure

For each site, model:

### A. Current cost base
- direct labor cost
- overhead
- maintenance and energy
- logistics and line support
- depreciation / fixed industrial cost allocation

### B. Non-closure measures
- headcount reductions via voluntary exits
- attrition
- shift reductions
- working-time reductions
- redeployment out of the site
- productivity improvements
- procurement and overhead cuts
- temporary labor-cost concessions

### C. Residual gap
- remaining fixed cost after measures
- remaining overcapacity cost
- underutilization cost
- product transfer cost if volume is shifted elsewhere

### D. One-off cost
- severance
- retraining
- transfer
- write-downs
- temporary duplicate production

### E. Risk haircut
- discount savings for underdelivery probability
- discount for temporary measures expiring
- discount for labor-relations disruption

This gives a more realistic net value than headline savings alone.
