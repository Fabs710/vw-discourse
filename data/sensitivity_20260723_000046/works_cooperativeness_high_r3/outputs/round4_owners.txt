## To the works council
I agree with you on one point: a cosmetic deal would be worthless.  
I also agree that trust is not rebuilt by slogans.

Where I disagree is your conclusion that closures and deeper headcount reduction are therefore unnecessary. That is **not proven**.

You keep pointing to the expert’s range for a no-closure package, and yes, it may deliver **€600m–€900m annually**. But “may” is not enough for me. I need to know:

- what the **actual savings target** is,
- what the **residual gap** is after voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, productivity gains, and selective capacity consolidation,
- and whether those measures are durable enough to restore margins, not just delay the problem.

Until that is shown, “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, 2030 guarantees” is not a restructuring answer. It is a boundary. I understand the boundary, but I do not accept it as sufficient.

## To the union
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss labour-side contributions. That is more serious than a pure refusal.

But your package still risks being too soft if it is mainly temporary concessions. Temporary working-time reductions, bonus moderation, and voluntary exits help, but they do not automatically solve a structural overcapacity and cost problem.

So I ask you plainly:

- What recurring annual relief can labour actually deliver?
- For how long?
- And how do you ensure it is enforceable and not later diluted?

If the answer is only modest relief, then it is not enough. If the answer is substantial, I am open to it — but it must be quantified, not aspirational.

## To management
You are right about the core issue: the company needs durable restructuring, not a cosmetic peace.

Where I am not yet aligned with you is the implication that closures are already justified. They may be necessary, but that is **not yet proven**.

You need to show:
1. the annual savings target,
2. the contribution of each non-closure instrument,
3. the residual gap,
4. the time required to realize savings,
5. and the risk of underdelivery.

If you can demonstrate that a deep no-closure package cannot close the gap, then your position strengthens. Until then, closure remains an option, not a conclusion.

## To the state government
I understand your political role and your concern for the regional base. That matters.

I also agree that closures should not be the starting point. But “not the starting point” cannot become “never, regardless of the numbers.” If the business remains structurally uncompetitive, a permanent veto on capacity withdrawal would damage the company and ultimately the region as well.

So I can support a time-limited, no-closure first phase.  
I cannot support an absolute ban that freezes structural inefficiency.

## To institutional investors
You are right to insist that the package must be economically credible. I agree with that.

Where I push back is the assumption that credibility automatically means closures or harsh site rationalisation. That is not yet demonstrated.

The threshold question remains:
- What annual savings level is enough?
- What recurring labour-cost relief is enough?
- What headcount reduction is enough without closures?

If the no-closure path can truly close the gap, I support it. If it cannot, then I will not pretend otherwise.

## My position
I remain open on the mechanism, but not on the standard.

I want:
- a real and durable cost reset,
- credible capacity reduction,
- measurable labour-side contribution,
- and no outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise or ownership control.

I do **not** want:
- a fake peace,
- a delay disguised as restructuring,
- or a settlement that leaves the company structurally weak.

I am not backing a closure-first strategy without proof.  
But I am equally not backing a no-closure promise unless it is shown to be **large enough, durable enough, and credible enough** to restore competitiveness.

If the alternative package meets that standard, I am prepared to support it. If it does not, then closures and stronger structural measures remain legitimate options.
