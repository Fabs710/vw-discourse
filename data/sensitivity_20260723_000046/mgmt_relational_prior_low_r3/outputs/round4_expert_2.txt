The key dataset is the **plant-by-plant cost-gap bridge**.  
That is the most decision-useful document for determining whether a **no-closure, no-forced-layoff** settlement can credibly work.

### Why this is the decisive dataset
It answers the only question that really matters here:

- **What savings can be achieved without closures?**
- **What residual gap remains after all realistic non-closure measures?**

That residual gap determines whether the current footprint can be stabilized through:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- product allocation,
- and management/owner contributions,

or whether **some structural footprint reduction** becomes difficult to avoid.

### Why utilization alone is not enough
Utilization is important, but incomplete. A plant with mediocre utilization may still be viable if:
- it has strategic product allocation,
- logistics are efficient,
- the site can be repurposed,
- and cost per vehicle can be brought down materially.

Conversely, a site with reasonable utilization can still be structurally weak if:
- labor cost is high,
- indirect overhead is heavy,
- complexity is excessive,
- or future volume is not secure.

So utilization is one input, not the answer.

### What the bridge must include
For each German site, the bridge should show:

1. Current and forecast output  
2. Utilization rate  
3. Break-even volume  
4. Direct labor cost per vehicle  
5. Indirect / allocated overhead cost  
6. Logistics and complexity cost  
7. Savings from working-time changes  
8. Savings from voluntary exits / natural attrition  
9. Savings from redeployment / role consolidation  
10. Productivity improvement potential  
11. Management and owner contributions  
12. Product and investment commitments  
13. Residual gap after all non-closure measures  

### How to interpret the result
A no-closure scenario is more credible when the **residual gap** is:
- small relative to total operating earnings,
- bridgeable by durable measures rather than one-offs,
- and not dependent on unrealistic volume assumptions.

It becomes less credible when the residual gap is:
- large,
- recurring,
- and only solvable through permanent cost base reduction or volume consolidation.

### Bottom line
If you want a single dataset to determine whether the company can be stabilized without closures, it is the **site-by-site cost-gap bridge**, because it shows the **residual gap after non-closure measures**.
