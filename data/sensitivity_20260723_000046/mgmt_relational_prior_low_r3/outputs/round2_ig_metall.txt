We are aligned with the Works Council on the essentials: **no compulsory redundancies, no German plant closures, and no erosion of co-determination or the collective wage framework**.

I’ll be direct: management is still trying to make the workforce carry the largest share of the adjustment while keeping closure and layoff threats on the table. That is not a fair restructuring; it is leverage. We will not consent to that.

### Where I agree
I agree that the current structure is not sustainable as-is. I also agree that the company needs:
- a credible industrial future in Germany,
- real productivity improvements,
- investment and product commitments,
- and a package that is durable rather than cosmetic.

I also accept that employees may need to contribute. We have already signaled that. But that contribution must be **temporary, negotiated, and matched by binding guarantees**.

### Where I disagree
#### To management
You keep saying closures “cannot be ruled out.” For us, that is not a neutral statement of economics; it is a demand that workers accept job destruction as the starting point. We reject that.

Before any talk of closures or forced redundancies, you must show that you have fully exhausted:
- working-time solutions,
- internal flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- redeployment and retraining,
- phased capacity reduction,
- and a fair employee contribution.

If those instruments have not been used to the limit, then closures are not necessity — they are a choice.

And let me be equally clear: **a voluntary-only approach is not a fig leaf for hidden compulsory layoffs**. If the “voluntary” numbers do not come together, then we will not accept that the shortfall is automatically filled by forced redundancies.

#### To the family holding and investors
You are right about one thing: we need a durable solution, not a symbolic one. But your definition of durability cannot be “a leaner footprint at any social cost.” If the deal destroys trust, triggers prolonged conflict, and undermines skills and morale, then it is not durable value creation.

A competitive company needs a stable workforce and industrial peace. That is not sentiment; it is operational reality. So yes, there must be real savings — but they must come from a **shared-sacrifice package**, not from one-sided workforce cuts.

#### To the state government
We appreciate that you are not backing closure-led restructuring. But that position must be operational, not just political. If management pushes a package that shifts all pain onto labour, we expect you to use the blocking rights and governance tools you have. That is what anchor ownership means in practice.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We remain open to a negotiated package that includes:
- **temporary employee concessions** under a binding agreement,
- **working-time reductions or flexibility tools**,
- **capacity reductions without closures**,
- **voluntary departures only, not forced layoffs**,
- **investment and product allocation commitments**,
- and a **binding site and job guarantee through 2030**.

We can discuss contributions from employees, but only if they are paired with:
1. no German plant closures,
2. no compulsory redundancies,
3. substantial management and owner contribution,
4. and a real industrial plan for the future.

### On the “numbers”
Management wants numbers. Fine — then put forward a **full no-closure scenario** with:
- the exact savings required,
- what can be achieved through working-time and flexibility,
- what voluntary instruments are expected to deliver,
- what management and owners are contributing,
- and what investment and product commitments secure utilization.

If the answer is that the company “prefers” closures because they are simpler, that is not a sufficient basis to break our red lines.

### Our bottom line
We are ready to bargain hard and responsibly. We are **not** ready to sign away jobs, sites, and collective protections to solve a crisis we did not create.

If management wants a serious settlement, it must move away from closure pressure and bring a fair package with shared sacrifice, binding guarantees, and future work for German sites. Otherwise, the conflict will escalate.
