As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys jobs, closes plants in Germany without exhausting alternatives, or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.**

I recognize the pressure management is under. The cost base has to come down, the market is tough, and the company must remain competitive. But the answer cannot be a one-sided solution that shifts all risk onto the workforce and the region while treating German sites as disposable.

### To management
I agree on one point: **the current structure cannot simply remain unchanged**. But I do **not** accept your conclusion that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the necessary starting point.

A restructuring that is politically and socially unacceptable in the region is not a sustainable restructuring. If you push through a model that gutting domestic employment is the price of competitiveness, you will face serious resistance — including from me in my shareholder and supervisory-board role.

What I would regard as a viable path is:
- **capacity reductions through negotiated, reversible measures first**
- **working-time solutions and internal flexibility**
- **voluntary instruments and retraining**
- **clear investment commitments for German sites**
- **product allocation that gives the plants a future**
- **a fair contribution from all sides, including management and owners**

If there is to be an employee contribution, it must be tied to **binding safeguards**: site protection, job protection, and a credible industrial plan. That is the only basis on which the region can justify support.

### To the works council and union
I support your core concerns: **no blanket closures, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of co-determination**. Those are not negotiable principles for me either.

At the same time, I would urge you not to reduce the discussion to absolute preservation of every current structure. The company needs room to adapt. But adaptation must happen through **social partnership**, not by breaking the plant network and forcing the state to absorb the fallout.

So yes, I am open to a negotiated restructuring — but only if it:
- **protects regional employment**
- **keeps the German industrial base intact**
- **preserves co-determination**
- **shares the burden fairly**
- **provides a durable future for the sites**

### Where I stand
I am **not** prepared to back a closure-led strategy.  
I am **also not** prepared to back a cosmetic compromise that avoids all change.

But I **am** prepared to support a tough negotiated package if it is built around:
1. no rushed closures,
2. no forced layoffs,
3. substantial management and owner contribution,
4. investment and model commitments,
5. and a restructuring path that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor.

If management believes it can force through deep cuts without regard for these conditions, then it will find the state government using all its rights under the special statute and in the supervisory board. That is not a threat for its own sake — it is a statement of responsibility to the region, the workforce, and the long-term health of the company.
