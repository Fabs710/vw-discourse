### Core impasse
The discourse has plateaued because the parties disagree on **sequence and burden of proof**:

- **Employee side / state**: first prove, with site-by-site numbers, that no-closure options fail; only then discuss any footprint reduction.  
- **Management / investors / controlling family**: closures and compulsory redundancy must remain available as credible restructuring tools; they should not be ruled out in advance.

So the real deadlock is not whether the company needs change; it is whether the restructuring begins with a **binding no-closure framework** or with a **kept-open structural reduction option**.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

#### 1) Time-limited no-closure test phase with hard review trigger
A possible bridge would be a **defined testing phase** during which:
- no German plant closures occur,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- employee flexibility/contribution measures are implemented,
- management and owners provide quantified savings and investment commitments,
- and a **mandatory review date** is set to assess whether the gap is closed.

This could address the employee side’s demand for a genuine test, while preserving a later evidence-based decision if the gap remains open.

#### 2) Conditional structural option, but only after quantified exhaustion of alternatives
Another possible compromise is:
- keep footprint reduction **legally and procedurally available**,
- but require a documented sequence showing that:
  - working-time measures,
  - voluntary departures,
  - redeployment,
  - productivity gains,
  - product allocation,
  - and management/owner contributions
  have all been exhausted first.

This would not satisfy a total ban on closures, but it would remove the sense that closures are being used as the default first lever.

#### 3) Hybrid package with asymmetrical protections
A third bridge could combine:
- **binding job-site guarantees for core German sites**,
- voluntary-only measures and retraining at weaker sites,
- targeted operational consolidation,
- and a strict prohibition on compulsory redundancies during the agreement period,
while allowing limited footprint adjustment only if the audit shows it is indispensable and unavoidable.

This could be the most realistic middle path if the plant-by-plant audit shows uneven viability across sites.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact quantified savings gap remains after you apply voluntary measures, working-time changes, redeployment, productivity gains, and owner/management concessions—site by site?**

#### To the works council
**Under what measurable conditions would you accept that a no-closure scenario has genuinely failed and that any further discussion must move to structural adjustment?**

#### To the industrial union
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure test phase if it included binding workforce contributions, full transparency, and a hard deadline for management to publish the savings bridge?**

#### To the state government
**How far are you prepared to use your blocking rights if the final package contains indirect closure logic rather than explicit plant shutdowns?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to commit to concrete capital, investment, or product-allocation concessions before insisting that footprint reduction remains on the table?**

#### To institutional investors
**What level of residual cost gap would still be acceptable to you if it were paired with strong execution certainty, industrial peace, and no compulsory redundancies?**

#### To the expert
**Can you specify the threshold at which the residual gap becomes too large for a no-closure settlement to be credible, and what minimum data would be needed to justify that threshold?**
