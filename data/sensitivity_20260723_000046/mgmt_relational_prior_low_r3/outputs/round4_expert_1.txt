From an operational restructuring standpoint, the **single most decisive data set is the plant-by-plant cost-gap bridge** — not utilization alone, and not a headline cost figure.

### Why the cost-gap bridge is decisive
It answers the core question the parties are actually fighting over:

- **What exactly is the current cost problem?**
- **How much of it can be removed without closures?**
- **What residual gap remains after all realistic non-closure measures?**

That residual gap is what determines whether a **no-closure / no-forced-layoff** settlement is commercially credible or whether some structural footprint reduction becomes difficult to avoid.

### What must be in that bridge
For each German site, you need:

1. **Current output and forecast output**
2. **Utilization rate**
3. **Break-even volume**
4. **Direct plant cost**
5. **Allocated indirect / overhead cost**
6. **Labor cost per vehicle**
7. **Logistics and complexity cost**
8. **Savings from working-time measures**
9. **Savings from voluntary exits / natural attrition**
10. **Savings from redeployment / role consolidation**
11. **Productivity improvement potential**
12. **Management and owner contribution**
13. **Investment and product allocation commitments**
14. **Residual gap after all of the above**

### Why utilization alone is insufficient
A plant can have weak utilization but still be defensible if:
- it has a strategic product,
- cost can be restructured materially,
- logistics are favorable,
- and future volumes are committed.

Conversely, a site with decent utilization may still be structurally uncompetitive if:
- labor cost is too high,
- complexity is excessive,
- or required capex is too large.

### Why a site-by-site view matters
This is a multi-plant network question. The key issue is not “is Germany expensive?” but:
- **which plants are underperforming,**
- **which are strategic,**
- **which can be repurposed,**
- and **what savings can be extracted without closing sites.**

### Practical threshold logic
In turnaround practice, a no-closure solution becomes less credible when the **post-measure residual gap** remains:
- **large relative to EBITDA**, or
- **large enough to keep the brand below target for several years**, or
- **too dependent on one-off concessions that later reverse**.

I would not set a universal percentage without the actual numbers. The right threshold depends on:
- volume outlook,
- EV transition timing,
- capex needs,
- labor peace risk,
- and the company’s required return on sales.

### What would be needed to justify a decision
To decide this properly, you would need:
- audited plant P&Ls or equivalent cost allocations,
- product-by-site volume forecasts,
- labor and non-labor cost breakdown,
- benchmark cost per vehicle versus peers,
- and a quantified savings proposal from each side.

### Bottom line
If you want to know whether the company can be stabilized **without closures**, the **cost-gap bridge by plant** is the key document. Utilization is part of it, but the bridge is the decision-maker because it shows the **residual gap after non-closure measures** — and that is the real test.
