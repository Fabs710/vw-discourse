I will respond directly.

The employee side keeps repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as if those phrases by themselves solve the economics. They do not. The company’s German footprint is too expensive, capacity is too high for the market we actually have, and the current structure does not support the return-on-sales path we need. If we pretend otherwise, we will all lose time while the situation gets worse.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs a viable industrial future in Germany,
- this must be more than a symbolic or cosmetic exercise,
- investment, product allocation, and 2030 planning matter,
- and employee contributions cannot be one-sided if they are to be part of a deal.

I also agree that a prolonged conflict would damage output and confidence. That is precisely why I want a settlement that is real.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) “No closures” is not a serious starting position
The works council says closures are a choice, not a necessity. I disagree. We have not arrived at this point because management likes conflict; we have arrived here because the cost base is structurally uncompetitive and European demand is shrinking. That is not rhetoric. It is the business reality.

I am not saying closures are the preferred outcome. I am saying they cannot be ruled out in advance if we are to achieve a durable reset.

#### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not compatible with an honest restructuring
You can call for voluntary measures, redeployment, and flexibility, and I am prepared to use them. But I will not promise that every reduction can be achieved voluntarily if the required adjustment is substantial. If the numbers do not come through, then a purely voluntary approach becomes a delay tactic.

#### 3) Co-determination is not a veto on competitiveness
To the works council and union: co-determination matters, but it cannot be used to lock in an uncompetitive structure indefinitely. The supervisory board process exists to ensure proper governance, not to freeze the company in place. If we need a leaner footprint to protect the business, that has to be part of the conversation.

### To the union
I hear your willingness to discuss temporary employee contributions, working-time changes, and voluntary tools. That is a constructive statement. But it is not enough on its own.

If you want management to move away from closures and forceful measures, then you need to bring something material:
- verifiable cost relief,
- productivity improvements,
- and a package that actually reduces the structural burden.

A time-limited concession without real capacity correction would not meet the business need.

### To the state government
I respect your role and your statutory rights. But I have to say clearly: using the blocking position to prevent any meaningful restructuring would be irresponsible. The state’s role is to safeguard the long-term industrial base, not to preserve every site regardless of economics.

If you want to protect the region, you should support a package that makes the German operations viable for the next decade, even if that means painful decisions now.

### To the controlling family
I agree that a reckless shock strategy would destroy value. That is not what I am pursuing. What I am pursuing is a disciplined restructuring with quantified outcomes. I also agree that we need a proper plan, not slogans.

But I do not accept the idea that the only acceptable path is a soft, socially comfortable compromise. If the savings are not material, the business remains weak. That is also value destruction.

### What we need
Let me be specific. We need a package that delivers:
1. **meaningful capacity reduction**
2. **substantial labour-cost relief**
3. **productivity gains**
4. **credible investment into future products and technology**
5. **a footprint that is actually affordable**

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that closes the cost gap another way. I am open to that. I am not open to a package that leaves the current structure basically intact and calls that a solution.

### My position
Management is prepared to negotiate a deep restructuring. We are not demanding closures for their own sake, but we are refusing to rule them out. We are not insisting on forced redundancies as the first instrument, but we are refusing to ban them if they become necessary.

That is the honest position.

So here is the choice:
- either we build a package that genuinely lowers costs and capacity, or
- we keep the current structure and accept a further erosion of competitiveness.

I do not accept the second option, and I will not pretend it is acceptable just because it is more comfortable.
