The core impasse is now clear:

## Core impasse
The parties agree on the problem statement — the company needs a durable industrial restructuring — but they do **not** agree on the **sequence** and **red lines**:

- **Employee side / state:** no closures and no compulsory redundancies should be the starting framework, with any restructuring tested first through a plant-by-plant no-closure bridge.
- **Management / capital / family:** closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available if the residual gap cannot otherwise be closed.
- **Expert:** the deciding factor is the **residual gap after realistic non-closure measures**; that gap has not yet been quantified in a way that can settle the dispute.

So the deadlock is not over whether numbers matter — everyone says they do. The deadlock is over **what happens before the numbers are proven** and **what is still permissible if the numbers remain unfavorable**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three concrete bridge options that could narrow the gap between the most conflicting positions.

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure test phase
A fixed, short test phase during which:
- no German plant closures occur,
- no compulsory redundancies are used,
- employee contributions are limited to temporary, proportionate measures,
- management and owners make quantified contributions,
- and a plant-by-plant audit is completed.

At the end of the phase, all parties commit in advance to review the residual gap against agreed criteria.

**Purpose:** gives labour and the state a genuine no-closure test, while giving management and capital a defined decision point rather than an indefinite veto.

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional restructuring ladder
A staged framework in which the company first exhausts:
1. working-time flexibility,
2. voluntary exits,
3. redeployment and retraining,
4. productivity and overhead reductions,
5. management/owner contributions,
6. product and investment commitments.

Only if the independent audit shows a **material residual gap** after those steps would structural footprint changes become discussable — and only under agreed procedural safeguards.

**Purpose:** preserves management’s insistence on economic reality while avoiding immediate closure-led bargaining.

---

### Proposal 3: No forced redundancies, but enhanced voluntary and transitional measures
A package that keeps compulsory redundancies off the table initially, while expanding:
- severance / early retirement / voluntary departure instruments,
- internal job matching,
- retraining budgets,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- and binding product allocation for Germany.

If the residual gap is still large after implementation, the parties would reconvene on whether a second-stage package is needed.

**Purpose:** offers the strongest possible no-forced-layoff route without requiring an immediate final commitment that all sides can accept today.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact residual gap remains after all non-closure measures, and which line item proves that closures are necessary rather than merely preferable?**

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**If the audit shows a substantial residual gap after all temporary and voluntary measures, what additional concessions would you consider before the framework becomes impossible for management to accept?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Are you prepared to accept a time-limited test phase with a defined review date if it temporarily rules out closures and forced layoffs, even if the long-term outcome is not pre-decided?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you commit to using your blocking rights only after the audit is complete and the residual gap is transparently assessed, rather than before the evidence is in?**

### To the Controlling Family
**What level of ownership contribution — capital, guarantees, or governance restraint — are you prepared to place on the table before asking labour to accept any further burden?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a no-closure package if it demonstrably closes the gap and improves execution certainty, even if it preserves more sites than management initially prefers?**

### To the Expert
**What threshold or evidence standard would you use to distinguish a “modest” residual gap from one that makes structural footprint reduction difficult to avoid?**
