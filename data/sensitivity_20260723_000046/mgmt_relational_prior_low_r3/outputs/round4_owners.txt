I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the investor side on the core economic point: **the current structure is not sustainable**. The German cost base is too high, the footprint appears overextended, and if we leave the problem unresolved, value will keep eroding. A cosmetic compromise would be a mistake.

I also agree with the state and the employee side on one important point: **a destructive conflict would damage the company further**. A prolonged strike, a governance deadlock, or a deal that cannot be implemented would hurt the business, the workforce, and the family asset.

So yes: we need a real, durable solution.

## Where I disagree
### To the works council and union
I do **not** accept your attempt to make “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” absolute starting premises.

That may be the preferred outcome, but it is not yet justified by the evidence in this room. You are effectively asking owners and management to pre-commit to preserving every current structure regardless of economics. That is not stewardship. That is freezing a likely overcapacity problem and hoping the market will absorb it later.

I also do not accept the framing that closure language is automatically just pressure and not reality. It may be reality. That is precisely why the audit matters.

### To the state government
I respect the state’s role and the blocking rights under the special statute. But I do **not** accept using governance rights to immobilize the company or to rule out necessary structural change in advance.

Protecting the regional industrial base does not mean preserving every site unchanged. If the footprint remains structurally too large, then the company bleeds margin, loses investment capacity, and weakens the very base everyone wants to protect.

### To management
I support the need for restructuring, but I also think management must stop speaking as if closure rhetoric alone is enough.

“Closures cannot be ruled out” is not a plan. It is a negotiating position. I need the numbers before I can support any structural move. If you want backing for deep change, you must show the site-by-site bridge:
- utilization,
- break-even volume,
- direct and indirect cost,
- savings from working-time and flexibility,
- savings from voluntary departures and redeployment,
- management/owner contribution,
- product and investment commitments,
- and the residual gap.

Without that, you do not yet have a defensible case for footprint reduction.

## What I support
I am prepared to support a **serious restructuring** if it meets three conditions:

1. **It materially restores competitiveness and margins**
2. **It is executable and durable**
3. **It preserves the long-term industrial strength of the company**

That could include a no-closure outcome **if** it genuinely closes the gap.  
But I will not pre-commit to that outcome.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- a governance fight that turns into industrial paralysis,
- or a shock strategy that destroys trust and labour peace.

I also will not support a permanent impairment of the enterprise’s value through a refusal to adjust capacity if the audit shows that adjustment is necessary.

## On the expert assessment
The expert’s assessment was useful and, frankly, the most honest intervention so far.

The key point is this: **the residual gap after non-closure measures determines the answer**.

That does **not** mean closures are automatically justified.  
It does mean the no-closure case has to prove itself against hard numbers. If the residual gap remains large and recurring after all realistic non-closure measures, then some structural footprint reduction may be the minimum durable remedy.

That is exactly why I am not prepared to sign off on an absolute no-closure pledge today.

## To the employee side
You keep saying the burden of proof is on management. I agree that management must present the data. But the burden is not one-sided.

If the workforce wants a no-closure framework, it must also show how the company reaches competitive economics without structural footprint change. Temporary flexibility and voluntary tools may help, but if the plant network is genuinely overbuilt, those measures may not be enough. Saying “not closures” is not the same as providing a viable alternative.

## To the state and labour together
I am open to a **time-limited test phase** and a **hybrid package**, but only if it is genuinely aimed at proving whether the gap can be closed without footprint reduction. That means:
- no cosmetic “test” that simply delays the decision,
- no hidden closure logic disguised as specialization,
- no attempt to force permanent guarantees before the economics are tested.

If the numbers support a no-closure solution, I will support it. If they do not, then we must be honest that structural change is required.

## What I want next
We need the audit completed in a form that can actually decide this issue:

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant cost, utilization, and savings-bridge audit showing the residual gap after voluntary, flexibility, productivity, and product-allocation measures]

That audit must answer:
- current and forecast utilization by site,
- break-even volume,
- direct and indirect cost burden,
- what working-time changes can save,
- what voluntary departures and redeployment can save,
- what management and owner contributions are on the table,
- what product and investment commitments are secured,
- and the residual gap.

## My bottom line
I will not support a cosmetic compromise.  
I will not support a permanent impairment of competitiveness.  
I will not support a governance posture that freezes an uncompetitive structure.  
I will not support a reckless shock strategy that destroys labour peace and value.

I **will** support the smallest effective restructuring that actually restores competitiveness, if the numbers show that is what is required.

If the no-closure scenario can close the gap, prove it.  
If it cannot, then we need the courage to accept the structural changes necessary to protect the company’s long-term value.
