### Core impasse

The central deadlock is between **labour’s non-negotiable red lines** and **management/investor insistence on structural cost reduction**:

- **Works council / union / state**: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, preserve co-determination and job-security guarantees.
- **Management / investors / controlling family**: the current footprint is structurally too expensive; some form of footprint reduction may be needed if savings cannot otherwise be achieved.

So the impasse is **not** whether change is needed. It is **which instruments are admissible** and **who absorbs the adjustment**:
- labour wants a full no-closure framework first;
- management wants closure or layoff options kept available;
- investors want credible, quantified savings;
- the state and family want stability but not immobilism.

---

### Facilitator proposal: possible compromise positions

#### 1) Time-limited no-closure test phase
A structured agreement could establish a **defined trial period** for a no-closure scenario:
- no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies during the phase,
- binding employee contributions,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, voluntary departures, and productivity measures,
- management commits to full transparency on savings achieved.

**Trigger:** if the agreed savings gap is not closed by a fixed date, the parties automatically review additional structural options.  
This could bridge labour’s demand for immediate protection with management’s demand for verified economic results.

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring with protected core sites
A second option is a **hybrid package**:
- all core German sites remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- limited footprint adjustment only in specific non-core functions or underutilized lines,
- strong redeployment, retraining, and investment commitments,
- site/job guarantees for the remaining network through 2030.

This gives management some structural relief while preserving the strongest labour red lines as far as possible.

#### 3) Tradeable concessions package
A third compromise could link concessions and guarantees in a **single balanced exchange**:
- employee contributions are temporary and quantified,
- management and owners commit to matching financial and operational contributions,
- binding product allocation and investment commitments for German plants,
- strict governance oversight and co-determination in implementation.

This would make any sacrifice conditional on tangible future work, rather than on promises alone.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
If a fully quantified no-closure scenario shows a remaining savings gap, **which additional measures would you consider before rejecting any structural footprint change outright**?

#### To Industrial Union
What specific, measurable employee contributions are you prepared to place on the table, and **under what exact guarantees would those concessions be acceptable to your members**?

#### To Group & Brand Management
What is the **minimum cost gap** that must be closed, and can you publish a site-by-site bridge showing how much can be achieved **without** closures before asking for footprint reduction?

#### To Regional State Government
If management fails to prove that no-closure options are sufficient, **what concrete blocking or conditional support measures are you prepared to use, and at what threshold**?

#### To Controlling Family Holding
What level of structural change would you consider the **smallest effective restructuring**, and how much risk of labour conflict are you willing to tolerate before rejecting a harsher plan?

#### To Institutional Investors
If a hybrid package delivers credible savings but stops short of broad closures, **what specific performance metrics would make that solution acceptable to capital markets**?

#### To the Expert Advisor
Which single data set would most decisively determine whether the company can be stabilized without closures: **unit economics by plant, utilization rates, or the cost-gap bridge**?
