I will speak plainly from the state’s perspective as a blocking-minority shareholder and regional political actor.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert, and I agree in part with management and the family holding on one central point: **the current structure is not sustainable as-is**. We cannot pretend that the company can simply carry today’s cost base, utilization profile, and market pressure without any change.

I also agree that:
- a cosmetic compromise would be worthless,
- employee contributions may be necessary,
- investment and product commitments matter,
- and a prolonged strike or legal deadlock would hurt everyone.

So yes: there must be a real restructuring path.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do **not** accept closures or forced redundancies as the starting point
Management keeps saying closures “cannot be ruled out.” That is not the same as proving necessity. From where I sit, that language is still being used as leverage, not as a conclusion reached after exhausting alternatives.

The burden is on management and capital to show, site by site, that they have fully used:
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary departure instruments,
- retraining and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- product allocation changes,
- and management-side restraint.

If those options have not been genuinely exhausted, then closure talk is **not necessity**. It is a choice.

And I want to be absolutely clear: **I will not support a package that guts regional employment or turns our German plants into the first casualty of adjustment.**

### 2) Co-determination is not a hindrance to be bypassed
To management and investors: you keep framing co-determination and the state’s blocking role as friction. From a governance standpoint, that is exactly the wrong reading.

These rights exist to prevent short-term shock decisions that damage the enterprise’s long-term base. They are not decorative. They are there to force a serious, balanced solution.

If a restructuring requires tearing up the industrial peace arrangement or sidelining the workforce, then it is not a durable solution. It is a conflict strategy.

### 3) I reject the idea that every alternative must be ruled out in abstract before we can defend jobs
The expert is right that a plant-by-plant audit is needed. But I do **not** accept the implication that, until a closure case is proven, labour must accept that closures remain on the table as a bargaining default.

The sequence matters:
1. quantify the no-closure bridge,
2. test what burden-sharing can do,
3. only then assess whether any footprint adjustment is still required.

Not the other way around.

## What I support
I am prepared to back a **negotiated, quantified no-closure restructuring test phase** with strict conditions:

- **No German plant closures during the test phase**
- **No compulsory redundancies during the test phase**
- binding employee contributions that are **temporary and proportionate**
- real working-time and flexibility measures
- internal redeployment and retraining
- clear product and investment commitments for German sites
- transparent management and owner contributions
- a hard deadline for the savings bridge to be demonstrated

That is not surrender. That is disciplined restructuring.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a closure-led package from the outset,
- compulsory redundancy as a first resort,
- a “smallest effective restructuring” that is really a coded path to shutting sites,
- or any settlement that weakens co-determination or removes the state’s effective veto role.

## My response to the other parties
### To management
If you want my support, bring the numbers. Not slogans. I want a **site-by-site savings bridge** showing:
- current utilization,
- break-even volume,
- what working-time and flexibility can deliver,
- what voluntary exits can deliver,
- what management will contribute,
- what investment is locked in,
- and what remains after all of that.

If that residual gap is still large, we can discuss what the least damaging response is. But you do not get to start with closures and ask us to treat that as realism.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that jobs, sites, and co-determination matter. But I also need you to meet the company halfway in a way that is credible:
- temporary contribution,
- flexibility,
- redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- and willingness to examine plant-by-plant economics honestly.

What I cannot support is a position that refuses every structural adjustment before the data are even tested. That would be rigid, and it would not protect the company in the long run.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right that a cosmetic deal will not work. But durability does not mean turning closures into the default instrument. A deal that destroys trust and industrial peace can also destroy value.

I am willing to consider a **hybrid outcome** if, and only if, it preserves the regional anchor:
- no broad closures,
- no mass layoffs,
- strong job safeguards,
- and only the minimum structural changes proven necessary by the audit.

But I will not accept a settlement that sacrifices the state’s industrial base to satisfy a short-term footprint target.

## What I want next
[SUMMON: plant-by-plant independent audit of utilization, break-even volume, and savings bridge under a no-closure restructuring scenario, including the residual gap after voluntary, flexibility, productivity, and product-allocation measures]

That audit must answer one question clearly:
**Can the German operations be stabilized without closures or compulsory redundancies, while preserving competitiveness?**

If yes, that is the path I will support.

If no, then the parties must show, transparently and numerically, what the smallest possible structural adjustment would be — and why there is no less damaging alternative.

## Bottom line
My position remains firm:

- **Protect German jobs and plants**
- **Preserve co-determination**
- **Reject closures and forced redundancies as the starting point**
- **Test a real no-closure restructuring first**
- **Only consider any footprint adjustment if the data prove it is unavoidable and if it is truly the minimum necessary**

I am not here to ratify a dismantling of the company’s regional base. I am here to ensure the company has a future **in Germany**, not at the expense of Germany.
