I will be equally direct.

The state government understands the competitive pressure on the company. We are not asking for stagnation, and we are not defending the status quo as if it were costless. But I **do not accept** the way management is framing this as if German closures and compulsory redundancies are the natural or unavoidable starting point. They are not.

## To management
You are right about one thing: the company needs a durable restructuring and a credible path back to profitability. Where I disagree sharply is your implicit assumption that this requires treating domestic sites and jobs as the primary adjustment variable.

That is **not** an acceptable premise from a regional anchor shareholder’s perspective.

What you still have not shown is that you have seriously exhausted the alternatives before reaching for closure and forced-layoff rhetoric:
- working-time solutions,
- internal flexibility,
- redeployment and retraining,
- voluntary departures,
- phased capacity reduction,
- product and investment reallocation,
- and management-side restraint.

If those have not been pursued to their limit, then closures are not “necessity” — they are a choice. And I will not endorse that choice.

I also want to be clear: a restructuring that may satisfy a short-term cost target but tears apart the regional industrial base is **not** a successful restructuring. It creates political, social, and operational damage that will come back as a cost later. That is bad governance, not realism.

## To the works council and union
I agree with your central demand: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no erosion of co-determination**. Those are not symbolic points for me; they are core conditions.

I also agree that the burden must be shared fairly. Employees cannot be the only side that makes concessions while management and owners preserve their position. If a contribution is expected from labour, then it must be:
- temporary,
- proportionate,
- matched by binding guarantees,
- and tied to concrete investment and product commitments.

Where I would caution you is this: the company does need room to adapt, and adaptation cannot mean simply freezing every structure. But that is very different from accepting closure-led restructuring. I am prepared to support difficult adjustments **if and only if** they preserve sites, jobs, and the industrial future in this state.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. I reject that too. But I do not accept the conclusion that “credible” must mean “closures first.” That is a financial logic detached from the regional responsibility of this company and from the governance realities under which it operates.

You speak about durability. A deal that destroys trust and provokes open industrial conflict is not durable. A deal that preserves the company as a regional anchor while restoring competitiveness through negotiated measures is more durable than a shock strategy.

And let me be blunt: if the response to underperformance is always to cut jobs in Germany first, then the company is failing to use its full strategic toolkit. That is not the only way to improve margins.

## My position on the numbers
If management wants a serious discussion, then bring forward a **full no-closure scenario** with:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. the contribution achievable through working-time and flexibility,
3. the management and owner contribution,
4. the investment and product commitments,
5. and the utilization plan for all German sites.

That is the discussion I am prepared to have.

If the answer is simply “we prefer closures because they are simpler,” then I will say plainly: that is **not sufficient** to justify plant closures in Germany or a forced breakdown of the existing job-security framework.

## Bottom line
The state government will support a restructuring that:
- protects German employment,
- preserves sites in this state,
- keeps co-determination intact,
- and makes the company viable for the long term.

We will **not** support a package that:
- guts regional employment,
- removes the state’s effective veto role,
- or uses the crisis as cover for a closure-led dismantling of the industrial base.

I am open to tough negotiation. I am **not** open to surrendering the company’s role as a regional economic anchor.
