The single most decision-useful dataset is the **site-by-site cost and utilization bridge**.

If I had to choose only one, I would pick **plant-level unit economics combined with utilization**, because that is what determines whether a site is:
- structurally viable,
- temporarily underloaded,
- or persistently uneconomic even after labor concessions.

### Why this is the decisive dataset
- **Utilization rates alone** tell you whether capacity is underused, but not whether the plant can still cover its fixed burden.
- **Unit economics by plant alone** tell you cost competitiveness, but not whether volume can be restored through product allocation or shifts.
- The **cost-gap bridge** is ultimately the decision document, but it is only credible if it is built from plant-level unit economics and utilization.

### What it must include
For each German site:
- installed capacity and current output
- utilization by line / shift
- direct labor cost
- indirect labor and overhead
- energy, logistics, maintenance
- depreciation / fixed burden
- break-even volume
- savings achievable without closures:
  - working-time changes
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - productivity gains
  - product-mix changes
- residual gap after those measures

### What it would tell you
- If the residual gap is **modest**, a no-closure settlement may be viable.
- If the residual gap is **large and recurring**, then some footprint reduction is probably the minimum durable remedy.

### Bottom line
If you want the clearest answer to “can the company stabilize without closures?”, the best single dataset is:

**a plant-by-plant cost gap bridge built on utilization and break-even economics.**
