I will be direct.

## To management
I agree that the company needs a real industrial future and that the cost base must come down. I also agree that a cosmetic settlement would be useless.

I do **not** agree that this justifies keeping **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** on the table as bargaining instruments from the outset.

You still have not demonstrated that the company has genuinely exhausted the alternatives. Repeating “structural overcapacity” is not enough. If you want the workforce to consider sacrifices, then show the **site-by-site savings bridge**:

- utilization by plant,
- break-even volumes,
- direct and indirect cost by site,
- what working-time changes can save,
- what voluntary departures can save,
- what redeployment and retraining can save,
- what management and owners will contribute,
- and what product and investment commitments secure future work.

Until that is on the table, closure talk is pressure, not proof.

And let me be explicit: I reject the idea that compulsory redundancies are a normal bargaining lever. They are not a fair starting point for restructuring.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of co-determination,
- no one-sided workforce sacrifice.

I support your demand for a quantified no-closure test phase. That is the right sequence.

But I also need to say this clearly: the workforce can make temporary contributions, but only if they are matched by binding guarantees. If the company asks for concessions while keeping shutdowns and forced layoffs as fallback options, that is not burden-sharing. That is shifting risk onto employees.

## To the regional state government
Your role matters, and I agree that the region must not be sacrificed.

But that has to become concrete. If management tries to smuggle in closures through “optimization,” “specialization,” or “capacity adjustment,” then your blocking rights must be used. Otherwise co-determination and state influence become symbolic while the industrial base is dismantled in practice.

## To the controlling family and investors
I understand your concern for competitiveness and execution certainty. I agree that a purely cosmetic deal would destroy value.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that durability requires keeping closures and forced layoffs available from day one. A durable settlement is one that:
- protects industrial capability,
- preserves skilled jobs,
- avoids destructive conflict,
- and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice.

If your answer to every gap is footprint reduction, then you are not talking about stewardship. You are asking labour to carry the adjustment alone.

## On the expert assessment
The expert has said something useful: the decisive question is the **residual gap after a genuine no-closure package**.

That supports our position, not management’s rush. The proper sequence is:
1. quantify the no-closure bridge,
2. test what burden-sharing can do,
3. only then discuss whether any structural change is truly unavoidable.

The expert has **not** said closures are necessary. The expert has said they may become relevant only if the gap remains large after real alternatives have been tested.

That is a crucial difference.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary departures,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and product/investment commitments.

But only inside a framework that **rules out closures and forced layoffs** as the basis of the agreement.

## What we are not prepared to discuss
We are not prepared to discuss:
- closure-led restructuring,
- compulsory redundancies,
- “conditional” no-closure that is really just delay,
- or any package that weakens the collective wage framework before management and owners have shown their own contribution.

## [SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant savings bridge and utilization audit]
We need an independent audit that shows, for each German site:
- current utilization,
- break-even volume,
- total cost burden,
- savings achievable without closures,
- management and owner contributions,
- product allocation,
- investment commitments,
- and the residual gap.

That is the only basis on which a serious no-closure settlement can be assessed.

## Bottom line
Let me be very clear:

- I support a **quantified no-closure test phase**.
- I support **temporary and proportionate employee contributions**.
- I support **shared burden**, not one-sided pain.
- I do **not** support closures or compulsory redundancies being treated as acceptable bargaining leverage.

If management wants an agreement, bring the numbers and commit to job and site security.  
If not, then this conflict will escalate, because the workforce will not be asked to pay for a restructuring that starts by sacrificing its own people.
