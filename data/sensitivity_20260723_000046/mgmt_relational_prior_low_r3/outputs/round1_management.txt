We hear the message from the works council and the union, but we need to be equally clear from management’s side:

**The current structure is not competitive, and it is not sustainable.**  
German factory costs are materially above target, our European volume base is shrinking, and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales objective. If we do nothing structural, we will not protect jobs — we will simply delay a much harder reckoning.

So let me address your points directly.

### Where we agree
- **We agree that the company needs a viable industrial future in Germany.**
- **We agree that transformation cannot be financed by employees alone.**
- **We agree that investment, product allocation, and a credible plan to 2030 are essential.**

### Where we do not agree
- **No plant closures in Germany** is not a realistic starting point for this crisis.
- **No compulsory redundancies** as an absolute line is also not compatible with the scale of correction required.
- A deal that preserves every site, every legacy structure, and every entitlement while asking only for temporary flexibility will **not** restore competitiveness.

This is not about punishing the workforce. It is about restoring the cost base to a level that allows us to invest in future products, battery-related capability, software, and model renewal. If we fail to do that, the long-term outcome is worse for everyone, including the employees you represent.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not insisting on closures for their own sake. We are insisting on **durable structural savings**. That can include a combination of:
- **capacity reductions**
- **site consolidation**
- **plant closures where necessary**
- **working-time adjustments**
- **labour-cost contributions**
- **voluntary departure programmes**
- **targeted redeployment and reskilling**

But the bottom line is this: **the package must materially reduce both capacity and cost.** Cosmetic measures, temporary savings, or a deal that simply delays the problem will not be acceptable.

### On guarantees
A long site and job guarantee through 2030, as you propose, is only possible if it is matched by a structure that actually makes the business affordable. We are open to binding commitments on future investment and product allocation — but not to a blank cheque that locks in today’s overcapacity.

### On industrial peace
Let me be frank: prolonged strikes will damage output, earnings, and confidence, but they will not change the underlying economics. They would only increase the pressure to take harder decisions. I would much rather reach a negotiated settlement, but that settlement has to be real. It cannot be a protective shell around an uncompetitive footprint.

### Our position
Management is prepared to negotiate a **deep, durable restructuring package**.  
But we will not sign up to a cosmetic compromise that avoids closures, avoids genuine labour-cost contribution, and leaves the company structurally unfit for the next decade.

If the employee side is serious about safeguarding German production in the long term, then we need to talk about:
1. **meaningful capacity reduction**
2. **substantial labour-cost contributions**
3. **binding productivity improvements**
4. **investment commitments tied to a leaner footprint**

That is the only route to a sustainable agreement.
