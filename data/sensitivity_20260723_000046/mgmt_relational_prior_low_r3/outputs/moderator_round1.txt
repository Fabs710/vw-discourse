### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the **current cost base and structure are not sustainable**.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs a **credible industrial future in Germany**.
- Most parties accept that any solution should involve **burden-sharing**, not employee sacrifice alone.
- There is general openness to:
  - **investment and product commitments**
  - **working-time / flexibility solutions**
  - **voluntary instruments**
  - **some form of negotiated restructuring**
- There is also shared recognition that a **prolonged, destructive conflict would be harmful**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:
  - Works council, union, and state reject closures.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:
  - Employee side and state reject them.
  - Management and investors insist they remain possible.
- **Scope of restructuring**:
  - Employee side favors protecting all German sites with adjustments.
  - Management and capital providers want **material capacity reduction** and a leaner footprint.
- **Nature of guarantees**:
  - Employee side seeks binding site/job guarantees through 2030.
  - Management and investors say such guarantees are only possible if the business is structurally reset.
- **Role of co-determination and state influence**:
  - Employee side and state insist these must remain intact and influential.
  - Management and investors warn against using governance rights to block necessary change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is actually needed**, and at which sites.
- **How to quantify employee contributions** versus management/owner contributions.
- **What specific investment and product commitments** would be made in return for labour concessions.
- **Whether voluntary measures can deliver sufficient savings**, or whether deeper structural measures are unavoidable.
- **How to balance job protection with competitiveness** in a way that is credible to both labour and capital.
- **What level of certainty can be offered** without locking in an uncompetitive structure.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **concrete scenarios**:
  1. a **no-closure / no-forced-layoff** scenario and its cost implications,
  2. a **limited closure/consolidation** scenario and its social safeguards,
  3. a **hybrid package** with quantified savings, investment, and job protections.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - **non-negotiables**
  - **what they can contribute**
  - **what minimum outcome they need to support an agreement**
- Keep the discussion centered on **numbers, timing, and implementation**, not general statements of intent.
