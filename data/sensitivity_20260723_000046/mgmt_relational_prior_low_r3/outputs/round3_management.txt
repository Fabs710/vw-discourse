Let me respond plainly, because the company does not have the luxury of euphemisms.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one point: **this situation cannot continue as it is**. The current German cost base is too high, the footprint is too large for the market, and the brand will not get back to its return-on-sales target through aspiration or slogans.

I also agree that:
- a cosmetic deal would be worthless,
- investment and product commitments matter,
- employee contributions can be part of a settlement,
- and a prolonged strike would damage everyone.

So yes: we need a real industrial solution.

## Where I disagree — and strongly

### 1) “No closures” cannot be the starting line
The works council and union keep saying the burden of proof is on management. Fine — we have already provided the core proof: the structure is uncompetitive, demand is shrinking, and the cost gap is real. That is why we are here.

What I do **not** accept is the claim that plant closures are somehow illegitimate before the numbers are tested. They are not illegitimate. They are one of the few structural tools that can actually address overcapacity and fixed cost. If the employee side wants to rule them out in advance, then it is not proposing a solution — it is trying to freeze the current structure and hope the crisis goes away.

It will not.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not compatible with an honest restructuring if the gap is large
I hear the insistence on voluntary-only measures. I am prepared to use every voluntary instrument available:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- early retirement where feasible,
- working-time changes,
- internal transfers,
- and negotiated contributions.

But I will not pretend those tools are guaranteed to deliver the scale of reduction needed. If they do not, then compulsory measures remain part of reality. Refusing to acknowledge that does not make the savings appear.

### 3) Co-determination is not a veto on competitiveness
To the works council and union: co-determination is important, yes. But it is not a shield for an uncompetitive structure. The supervisory board is there to govern the enterprise, not to preserve every current site indefinitely regardless of economics.

To the state: your blocking rights are not a license to immobilize the company. Protecting the regional industrial base means making it viable, not preserving every existing configuration.

### 4) Investors are right about one thing: we need material savings
I agree with the investors on this: a deal that leaves the cost base basically intact would be a bad deal. A temporary concession without structural change is just deferral.

That is why I am not interested in a “peace at any price” settlement. I am interested in a package that actually changes the economics.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert has made the key point very clearly:
- a no-closure scenario is only credible if the savings gap is moderate,
- if the gap is structural and large, limited footprint reduction is more likely needed,
- and the decision turns on hard data: utilization, unit economics, and the cost bridge.

That supports my position, not the employee side’s. The expert did **not** say closures are unnecessary. The expert said no-closure must be tested seriously, but may well prove insufficient.

That is exactly where we are.

## What I will and will not accept

### I will accept:
- a genuine no-closure test phase **if it is time-limited and quantified**
- binding employee contributions
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary departures
- productivity and process improvements
- investment and product commitments for remaining German production
- strong redeployment and training measures

### I will not accept:
- an absolute ban on closures
- an absolute ban on compulsory redundancies
- job guarantees that preserve an uncompetitive cost base through 2030 regardless of business reality
- a deal that gives up structural change in exchange for short-term labour peace
- a settlement that uses governance rights to block necessary adjustment

## To the works council and union
You keep asking why German plants and jobs should be the first sacrifice.

Because the German footprint is where the structural cost problem is most acute. That is the answer. If you have a better way to close the cost gap durably, bring it — with numbers, not slogans. But so far I have not heard a credible alternative that matches the scale of the challenge.

And let me be blunt: if the “solution” is to preserve all sites, preserve all guarantees, and ask management and owners to absorb the entire burden, that is not burden-sharing. That is simply refusing the restructuring.

## To the state government
I appreciate your concern for the region. But the best way to protect the region is to ensure the company survives competitively through 2030 and beyond. That means accepting that some structural adjustment may be unavoidable.

If you use your influence to block any footprint change at all, you may preserve headlines for a while — but you will damage the company’s ability to invest, and that will hurt the region later.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree that we need an orderly, executable package and not shock therapy for its own sake. But I will not let caution become paralysis. We need a restructuring that materially reduces cost and capacity.

If a hybrid package can do that, good. If it cannot, then we must be honest that limited footprint reduction is required.

## The way forward
Here is the only constructive next step:

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant restructuring and utilization audit to quantify the no-closure savings bridge versus the minimum structural footprint reduction required]

That audit must answer, site by site:
- current utilization,
- break-even point,
- direct and indirect labour cost,
- achievable savings from working-time and voluntary tools,
- investment needs,
- and the minimum footprint change needed if the no-closure route falls short.

Until those numbers are on the table, I will not support any attempt to rule out closures or compulsory redundancies in advance.

## Bottom line
Let me be absolutely clear:

- I support a negotiated solution.
- I support burden-sharing.
- I support investment and future products.
- I support voluntary measures and flexibility.

But I do **not** support a settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.

If the no-closure scenario truly closes the gap, show it.  
If it does not, then we need the courage to make the structural changes required.

That is the reality.
