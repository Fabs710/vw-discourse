Management says “the structure is not competitive,” but it is still refusing to answer the decisive question: **why should German plants and German jobs be the first and easiest sacrifice?**

Let me be absolutely clear again:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or the historic job-security framework**

Those are not bargaining chips. They are the foundation of any serious agreement.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: the current situation cannot simply continue unchanged. We do need a credible industrial future, investment, and a durable path to competitiveness. I also agree that a solution must be real, not cosmetic.

But “real” does **not** mean closure-led. It does **not** mean forcing workers to pay for management failures with their jobs and livelihoods. And it does **not** mean treating co-determination as an obstacle instead of the very mechanism that makes a stable solution possible.

### Where I disagree
#### To management
You keep saying closures “cannot be ruled out.” That is exactly the problem. A restructuring built on threatening plant closures and forced layoffs is not a basis for trust — especially after the historic job-security pledge has already been undermined.

You talk about competitiveness, but you have not shown that you have exhausted the non-destructive options:
- capacity adjustments through internal redistribution,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary departure instruments,
- retraining and redeployment,
- phased productivity improvements,
- and a negotiated employee contribution tied to binding guarantees.

If those measures have not been fully used, then closure talk is not necessity — it is leverage. And we will not accept that.

#### To the family holding and investors
You say you do not want a “cosmetic compromise.” Neither do we. But your definition of “credible” seems to begin only where job losses begin. That is not a neutral economic view; it is a choice to prioritize short-term financial engineering over the social and industrial base of the company.

A durable reset is possible without destroying sites. What is not durable is a strategy that breaks labour peace, destroys trust, and turns a skilled workforce into a variable to be cut whenever margins weaken.

#### To the state government
I appreciate your support for no blanket closures and no compulsory redundancies. But I must be equally frank: that position must not remain rhetorical. If management brings a closure-led package, the state cannot simply “urge balance” while allowing jobs to be dismantled by the back door. Use your blocking rights if needed. This is precisely what they are for.

### What we will support
We are ready to engage on a **no-closure, no-forced-layoff restructuring package** that includes:
- binding **site and job guarantees through 2030**
- temporary, proportionate **employee contributions**
- working-time flexibility
- internal capacity adjustments
- redeployment and qualification
- investment and product commitments for all German sites
- a genuine role for co-determination in implementation

That is the minimum basis for serious talks.

### What we will not support
We will not support:
- “site-by-site justification” that is really a pretext for closures
- voluntary-only rhetoric that hides compulsory redundancy plans
- a deal that removes guarantees now and promises investment later
- a settlement that weakens co-determination in the name of speed
- any package where labour alone is asked to absorb the shock

### On numbers and realism
Management and investors keep asking for “material structural savings.” Fine. Then put the numbers on the table for a **full no-closure scenario**:
- What exact savings are needed?
- What volume can be achieved through working-time, internal flexibility, and voluntary tools?
- What management and owner contribution is being made?
- What investment is locked in for German production?
- What concrete product allocations secure future utilization?

If you cannot show that the company can be stabilized without closures or forced layoffs, then you have not demonstrated inevitability — only preference.

### Our line
We are not here to help management impose a fait accompli. We are here to defend the workforce and the industrial base of this company. If the restructuring is serious, it must start from this premise:

**Keep every German plant open. Protect every job from compulsory redundancy. Preserve co-determination.**

Anything else is not a negotiated solution. It is a demand for surrender.
