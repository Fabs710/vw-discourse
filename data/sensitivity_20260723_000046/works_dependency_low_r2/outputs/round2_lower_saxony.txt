As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to respond very clearly.

I appreciate that management is not denying the need for restructuring, and I also appreciate that the employee side is willing to discuss capacity adjustments, productivity measures, and temporary contributions. That is the right starting point. But I must be equally clear: **I do not accept a restructuring logic that begins with plant closures or compulsory redundancies as if those were the normal tools of adjustment.**

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the company needs a **durable industrial solution**, not a cosmetic one. You are right that the German cost base is under pressure, that competitiveness must improve, and that investment in future products and technology has to be secured.

I also accept that **capacity reduction may be necessary**. I am not denying the economic reality.

### With the works council and union
You are right that the burden cannot simply be dumped on the workforce. A restructuring that destroys trust, ignores co-determination, and treats employees as the only adjustment variable would be strategically short-sighted and politically unacceptable.

You are also right that a real solution must include:
- site and employment security,
- qualification and redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a fair contribution from all parties.

## Where I disagree

### On management’s insistence on closures and forced redundancies
This is where I draw a line. Management keeps saying closures cannot be ruled out and compulsory redundancies cannot be excluded in advance. I do **not** accept that framing as the default basis for this decision.

Why? Because it is not just an industrial question; it is a question of **regional stability, social legitimacy, and long-term enterprise value**. If you trigger a confrontation that tears up the company’s social settlement, you may create short-term accounting relief but long-term damage in operations, labour relations, reputation, and governance.

If management wants to argue that specific sites are structurally unviable, then that case must be proved in a transparent, quantified way, and only after all alternatives have been exhausted:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time measures,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- management and overhead discipline,
- and a fair, negotiated employee contribution.

Until that is shown, I will not treat closures as necessary.

### On the union and works council’s absolute language
I understand your red lines, and I share much of the substance. But I also need to be honest: if the position becomes “nothing can move,” then we will not have a workable settlement either. The company does need adjustment.

So my position is not “preserve everything unchanged.” It is: **adjust the footprint and cost base through negotiated, socially responsible means, not through closures and forced layoffs.**

That distinction matters.

### On investors and the family holding
I hear the argument that only the economics matter. But economics cannot be separated from governance and social stability in a company like this. A deal that is technically strong on paper but detonates labour peace and regional trust is not a durable value solution.

At the same time, I do not support an arrangement that keeps every legacy protection intact while expecting the company to absorb the cost forever. That would indeed be postponing the problem.

So yes: **the cost base must improve.**
But no: **that does not automatically justify closures or compulsory redundancies.**

## What I can support

I can support a negotiated package if it includes all of the following:

- **No German plant closures as the restructuring instrument of first resort**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site and employment protection**
- **Capacity reduction where needed**
- **Working-time and flexibility measures**
- **Redeployment and qualification**
- **A fair, temporary employee contribution**
- **Comparable burden-sharing from management and owners**
- **Credible investment and future-product commitments for German sites**
- **Co-determination involved early and materially, not after the fact**

## What I cannot support

I cannot support:
- a solution that guts regional employment,
- a decision that sidelines the state’s co-determination role,
- a plan that uses closure threats as leverage against the workforce,
- or a one-sided restructuring that treats the region as disposable.

## Bottom line

I am prepared to back a serious restructuring that protects the company’s future **and** its role as a regional anchor. But I will not endorse a route that destroys the industrial base in this state.

If management wants my support, it needs to bring a quantified alternative that shows how the required savings can be achieved **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**. If such an alternative does not exist, then the burden of proof remains with management — and I will use the rights attached to our stake and board position accordingly.
