Here is a concise neutral moderator summary of Round 2:

## 1) What has been agreed
Across the stakeholder group, there is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **durable industrial restructuring**, not a cosmetic fix;
- **investment in future products and technology** is necessary;
- **capacity reduction** is likely needed;
- **internal redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures** should be used;
- a **fair contribution from employees, management, and owners** should be part of any package;
- a **quantified comparison of options** is needed rather than rhetoric alone.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved conflict is over the **acceptable restructuring instruments**:

- **Management / investors / controlling family**:  
  insist that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out** if needed for durable competitiveness and fixed-cost reduction.

- **Works council / union / state government**:  
  reject **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** as the basis for restructuring, and argue for solutions that protect sites and jobs.

- **Core dispute**:  
  whether the needed savings can be achieved through a **non-closure or hybrid package**, or whether **closure-led restructuring** is economically necessary.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several trade-offs remain unresolved:

- **Durable savings vs. social/industrial peace**  
  Closure-led restructuring appears strongest on permanent cost reduction, but carries the highest labor-relations and governance risk.

- **Speed/certainty vs. preservation of sites and jobs**  
  More aggressive restructuring may be faster and more certain economically, but more disruptive socially.

- **Temporary concessions vs. structural reset**  
  Employee-side proposals emphasize temporary and negotiated measures; management argues these may be insufficient for a lasting reset.

- **Quantification gap**  
  There is still no shared, scenario-based accounting showing:
  - required savings,
  - site-by-site economics,
  - employment impact,
  - execution risk,
  - and value impact under different options.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principle statements to a **side-by-side scenario comparison** with the same metrics for all parties, ideally covering:

1. **Closure-led scenario**  
2. **No-closure negotiated scenario**  
3. **Hybrid compromise scenario**

For each, the group should assess:
- savings by year,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- employment effects,
- operational feasibility,
- strike/governance risk,
- and time to durable value.

The discussion should then focus on **which package closes the savings gap with the least total risk and the most durable legitimacy**.
