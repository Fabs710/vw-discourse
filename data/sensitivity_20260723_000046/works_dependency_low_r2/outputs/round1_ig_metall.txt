We agree on the core point: **this crisis cannot be solved by simply dumping the burden on the workforce**.

The works council is right to reject **plant closures in Germany** and **compulsory redundancies**. Those are not acceptable starting points for us either. We will not accept a restructuring that destroys jobs, breaks the collective framework, and treats employees as the only adjustment variable.

At the same time, we are not ignoring the economic reality. The company does need a credible cost and capacity solution. We have already shown willingness to engage constructively, including:
- capacity reductions where they are unavoidable,
- flexibility and working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and a fair employee contribution.

But let me be equally clear: **employee contributions are only legitimate if they are shared sacrifices**, not if workers pay while management, owners, and capital remain untouched. If the proposal is lower wages, fewer guarantees, and job cuts for the workforce, while there is no comparable contribution from leadership and shareholders, then that is not a restructuring plan — it is a one-sided transfer of pain.

So our position is:

1. **No compulsory redundancies.**
2. **No German plant closures.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective bargaining framework.**
4. **Yes to negotiated, temporary employee contributions only in exchange for binding site and job security.**
5. **Yes to productivity and capacity adjustments, but in a socially responsible way.**
6. **Yes to a durable industrial commitment, including investment and future products for the German sites.**

If management wants a settlement, it must put a real package on the table:
- security for sites and employment,
- fair burden-sharing from ownership and management,
- and a credible long-term plan for the German footprint.

If not, then the workforce will not carry this alone. We are prepared to escalate further if necessary.
