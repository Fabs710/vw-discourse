I will respond directly and clearly.

### Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state government, and even with management on one essential point: **the company needs a durable industrial solution, not a temporary patch**.  
I also agree that:
- investment in future products and technology is necessary,
- some capacity adjustment is likely unavoidable,
- internal redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility should be used,
- and a one-sided transfer of pain onto employees would be unacceptable.

### Where I disagree
I **do not accept** management’s framing that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain “on the table” as if that were the starting point of a serious solution.

That is not a neutral technical detail. It is a threat to the workforce, and it shifts bargaining power in a way that makes any “partnership” meaningless.

I also do **not** accept the investors’ position that the only thing that matters is the economic result, regardless of the instrument. In practice, that approach often means: cut jobs, weaken guarantees, and call it efficiency. If the social settlement is broken, you do not get stable performance — you get conflict, strikes, and long-term damage.

### On the works council’s line
I support the clear rejection of **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies**. That remains our position too.

At the same time, I want to be precise: if the company needs structural cost relief, then **we are prepared to discuss real sacrifices by employees** — but only under conditions of fairness and reciprocity.

That means:
- temporary wage concessions or deferred elements only,
- capacity reductions instead of closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and binding security for jobs and sites in return.

### What is unacceptable
Let me state the red lines plainly:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the collective bargaining framework.**
- **No deal where employees pay, while management and owners contribute nothing comparable.**

That last point is crucial. If the workforce is asked for wage restraint, flexibility, or lost guarantees, then management must also show restraint, and ownership must also contribute. Otherwise, this is not burden-sharing; it is burden-shifting.

### To management
You say the cost base must change “for good.” Fine. But “for good” cannot mean “for employees only.”

If you want a structurally durable solution, then put a real package on the table:
- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- no closures,
- future-product commitments,
- transparent capacity planning,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

If that is in place, then the workforce can discuss temporary concessions and operational flexibility. But if you insist on closure threats and headcount pressure, you will get resistance — not cooperation.

### To the family owners and state government
I hear your concern about long-term value and social legitimacy. That concern is correct.

But then use your influence to insist on a solution that protects the company’s industrial base instead of validating a management strategy that relies on fear. If you want stability, then you need to support a settlement that is socially sustainable, not just financially aggressive.

### To the institutional investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal that leaves the economics untouched would be a mistake.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible way to improve economics is through plant closures and forced layoffs. There are other levers:
- productivity,
- working-time,
- capacity utilisation,
- overhead discipline,
- qualification,
- and negotiated, temporary employee contributions.

If management cannot demonstrate why those are insufficient, then closures are not “necessary”; they are a choice.

### Our position for the next step
We are prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package**, but only if it includes:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job security**
4. **A temporary, fair employee contribution**
5. **Comparable contributions from management and ownership**
6. **A credible investment and product commitment**

If those conditions are not met, then we will not help manufacture consent for a one-sided restructuring. The workforce will not bear the pain alone.

If management wants to avoid escalation, it should stop using closures as leverage and start presenting a genuinely shared solution.
