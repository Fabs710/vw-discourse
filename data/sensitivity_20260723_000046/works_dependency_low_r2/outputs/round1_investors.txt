As the institutional capital markets, our position is straightforward: we care about **risk-adjusted value**, and right now that means one thing above all else — **a credible, durable return to competitive margins**.

### Where we agree
We agree with management on the core diagnosis: this company’s German cost base is too high, capacity is too heavy, and the current margin path is not acceptable. If the restructuring does not materially improve the return-on-sales trajectory, then it is not value-preserving.

We also agree with the family owner and state government on one important point: **prolonged industrial conflict destroys value**. A drawn-out strike, governance stalemate, or cosmetic settlement would be a negative outcome for shareholders. We would view that as margin erosion by another route.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a “preserve everything” outcome if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

So to the works council and union: your insistence on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no meaningful labour adjustment** is not acceptable if it blocks a real reset of the cost base. A settlement that protects every site and every guarantee while leaving margins impaired is not a solution — it is delay. We would not back that.

That said, we also do **not** automatically endorse plant closures as the only answer. What matters to us is the **economic result**, not the instrument. If management can achieve the same durable savings through alternative measures — capacity reduction, shifts in working time, redeployment, productivity changes, labour-cost contributions — then that is preferable to a value-destructive confrontation.

### To management
We support decisive action, but only if it is **credible and durable**. We will not reward rhetoric about restructuring if the outcome is half-measures, temporary concessions, or a deal that simply kicks the problem into the future.

If you want capital markets to stay supportive, you need to show:
- a quantified path to competitive margins,
- durable fixed-cost reduction,
- clear capacity adjustment,
- and deal certainty.

You also need to be honest about execution risk. A plan that triggers prolonged strikes or board deadlock may look strong on paper but destroy value in practice. We will judge you on the actual path to restored profitability.

### To the state and family owners
We understand the importance of social legitimacy and long-term ownership. But neither political comfort nor legacy governance can be allowed to protect an uncompetitive cost structure indefinitely.

If you block necessary adjustment, investors will treat that as a governance discount. We will price in the risk of weak execution, lower margins, and continued overcapacity. That is not a threat; it is simply how capital allocates.

### Our bottom line
We want — and will support — an outcome that achieves all of the following:
- credible structural cost reduction,
- durable margin improvement,
- no cosmetic settlement,
- and enough certainty to restore investor confidence.

We are **not** committed to any specific instrument, including closures, if the same savings can be delivered otherwise. But we are equally clear that **a settlement leaving the cost base uncompetitive is unacceptable**.

So our standard is simple: show us a real plan that restores competitiveness, not just industrial peace. If that means tough capacity and workforce measures, so be it. If it means negotiated alternatives that genuinely deliver the same economics, we can support that too. What we will not support is stagnation dressed up as compromise.
