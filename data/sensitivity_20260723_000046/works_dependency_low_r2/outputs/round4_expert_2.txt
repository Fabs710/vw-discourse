Below is a **decision-useful restructuring comparison** focused on **industrial economics**, not legal or labor strategy.

## Executive view

Given the information provided, the most likely ranking is:

1. **Hybrid scenario** — best risk-adjusted path in a German co-determined setting  
2. **Closure-led scenario** — strongest permanent savings, but highest execution/governance risk  
3. **No-closure scenario** — lowest disruption, but most likely to under-deliver if the structural gap is large

# 1) Side-by-side scenario comparison

## A. Closure-led scenario
**What it means**
- Close one or more German plants
- Cut capacity materially
- Reduce headcount structurally
- Use redundancies and asset rationalization to reset the footprint

**Indicative steady-state annual savings**
- **€500m to €900m**

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€600m to €1.5bn+**
  - severance
  - decommissioning
  - relocation / transfer
  - environmental and site costs
  - disruption costs

**Margin impact**
- **Strongest medium-term ROS improvement**
- Best if one or more sites are structurally uncompetitive even after productivity actions

**Employment impact**
- Highest direct job losses
- Potentially larger indirect supplier/logistics effects

**Execution risk**
- **High**
- Main risks:
  - strike action
  - governance deadlock
  - delay in implementation
  - production disruption
  - reputational damage
  - loss of talent

**When it is economically justified**
- Persistent low utilization
- High fixed-cost burden
- Significant capex required to keep the site competitive
- Weak or declining product allocation
- Closure NPV clearly superior to alternatives

---

## B. Hybrid scenario
**What it means**
- Avoid blanket closures initially
- Combine:
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - shift/model concentration
  - working-time changes
  - productivity measures
  - overhead cuts
  - targeted site reduction
- Keep closure as a conditional fallback only if agreed triggers are missed

**Indicative steady-state annual savings**
- **€300m to €600m**

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€300m to €900m**

**Margin impact**
- Usually enough to produce a **material improvement**
- Often the **best risk-adjusted value outcome** if the gap is meaningful but not extreme

**Employment impact**
- Material headcount reduction, but more often through:
  - attrition
  - retirement
  - voluntary exits
  - internal redeployment

**Execution risk**
- **Medium**
- Lower disruption than closure-led, but success depends on:
  - discipline in implementation
  - credible targets
  - sufficient labor-cost contribution
  - real capacity reduction, not just deferral

**When it is economically justified**
- There is a real structural gap, but not so large that only closure can close it
- The company needs savings, but also needs to preserve operating stability
- The governance environment makes closure execution unusually costly

---

## C. No-closure scenario
**What it means**
- No German plant closures
- No compulsory redundancies
- Restructuring through:
  - productivity improvements
  - overhead reduction
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - temporary employee contribution

**Indicative steady-state annual savings**
- **€150m to €350m**

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€100m to €400m**

**Margin impact**
- Can help, but may be **insufficient** if the structural gap is large
- Risk of becoming cosmetic if savings are mostly temporary

**Employment impact**
- Lowest direct workforce impact
- Preserves sites and most jobs, but may preserve excess capacity too

**Execution risk**
- **Low to medium economically**
- Can become **high later** if the package underperforms and the issue returns unresolved

**When it is economically justified**
- The structural cost gap is moderate
- There is meaningful room for labor productivity, mix, and overhead correction
- Capacity can be reduced enough without closing sites

# 2) Savings bridge by site logic

Because no plant-level figures are available, the correct way to build the savings bridge is by site category rather than naming a specific plant.

## Site category 1: structurally weak site
Typical profile:
- low utilization
- high fixed costs
- high capex needs
- weak future product allocation

**Best economic treatment**
- closure-led, or at minimum a severe footprint reduction

**Expected economics**
- highest savings per site
- but also highest one-time cost and disruption

---

## Site category 2: borderline site
Typical profile:
- moderate utilization
- some product flexibility
- some scope for productivity and shift rationalization
- capex still manageable

**Best economic treatment**
- hybrid

**Expected economics**
- meaningful savings without immediate closure
- often the best candidate for staged restructuring

---

## Site category 3: strategically viable site
Typical profile:
- good utilization
- future product allocation
- manageable fixed costs
- capex justified by expected volume

**Best economic treatment**
- no-closure with targeted efficiency measures

**Expected economics**
- smaller but useful savings
- preservation of industrial capability

# 3) Practical trigger metrics for escalation

If the goal is “hybrid first, closure only if needed,” the triggers must be hard and measurable.

## Financial triggers
- Annual savings fall **15% to 20% below plan**
- Return-on-sales remains below target for **2 consecutive planning cycles**
- Payback period exceeds the agreed threshold

## Operational triggers
- Plant utilization remains below **70% to 75%**
- Secured model volumes fail to materialize
- Productivity gains miss target by a material margin

## Investment triggers
- Required capex to keep a site competitive exceeds the economic value of keeping it open
- Future platform adaptation cannot be done at acceptable cost

## Implementation triggers
- Voluntary exit volumes fall materially short
- Working-time or labor-cost measures are not ratified
- Strike disruption causes sustained output loss beyond the risk budget

---

# 4) Bottom-line judgment

## If the structural gap is large:
- **Closure-led** becomes economically more compelling
- but the execution risk rises sharply

## If the structural gap is moderate:
- **Hybrid** is usually the best trade-off
- it can capture most of the savings while limiting destructive conflict

## If the gap is modest:
- **No-closure** can work
- but only if the package is deep enough and durable enough to avoid a later relapse
