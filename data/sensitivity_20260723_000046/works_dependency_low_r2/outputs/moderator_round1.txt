### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that **the company needs a credible long-term industrial plan**.
- There is broad agreement that **investment in future products and technology** is necessary.
- Most parties accept that **some capacity reduction and cost adjustment may be required**.
- There is shared opposition, from employee-side and several governance stakeholders, to a **purely confrontational or cosmetic solution**.
- There is also shared concern that **prolonged conflict would damage value and stability**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Employee-side and state/shareholder actors reject closures as a starting point; management says closures cannot be ruled out.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side and several governance actors reject them; management says they cannot be excluded in advance.
- **Scope of workforce contribution:**  
  Management and investors want a durable structural labour-cost effect; employee-side insists any contribution must be temporary, negotiated, and matched by binding guarantees.
- **Meaning of “restructuring”:**  
  Management/investors define success as a permanent cost-base reset; employee-side and state/shareholder actors define success as preserving sites, jobs, and co-determination while adjusting capacity.
- **Decision process and governance:**  
  Employee-side and state actor want co-determination to shape the plan early and materially; management wants latitude to deliver necessary restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is needed**, and whether it can be achieved without closures.
- **Which instruments** can deliver the required savings: working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, productivity measures, overhead cuts, employee contributions, or workforce reduction.
- **What binding guarantees** can realistically be offered in exchange for employee concessions:
  - site security,
  - job security,
  - future investment,
  - product commitments,
  - duration of protections.
- **How burden-sharing is distributed** between employees, management, owners, and capital.
- **What the fallback is** if a negotiated no-closure solution does not produce sufficient economic improvement.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: identify the savings, capacity changes, and employment effects under each scenario.
- Ask each side to specify **non-negotiables vs. negotiable instruments**.
- Require management to present **a comparison of alternatives** that shows why specific measures are needed.
- Ask employee-side and public/shareholder actors to define **what level of economic improvement they would consider sufficient** in exchange for site/job guarantees.
- Keep the discussion centered on **a tradeable package**: cost reduction, employment protection, investment commitments, and governance participation.
