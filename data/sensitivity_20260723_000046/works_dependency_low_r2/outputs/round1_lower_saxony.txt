As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **I understand the economic pressure and I do not deny that restructuring is necessary**. But I **do not accept** a logic that starts from plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and a one-sided transfer of pain onto the workforce.

### Where I agree
I agree with management and the employee side on one essential point: **the company needs a credible long-term industrial plan**. We also need:
- investment in future products and technology,
- a durable improvement in competitiveness,
- and a solution that restores confidence in the brand’s path.

I am also willing to accept that **capacity reductions** may be necessary. I do not deny that overcapacity exists.

### Where I disagree
What I cannot accept is the implication that the only serious route is to close German plants or to make compulsory redundancies a normal tool. That would be a deeply damaging mistake for the company, for the region, and for the company’s long-term social legitimacy.

This is not just a political preference. It goes to the core of this company’s stability:
- German sites are not interchangeable numbers on a spreadsheet.
- Jobs are not a residual variable after all other stakeholders have been protected.
- And co-determination is not decorative; it is a central part of how this company is governed.

### To management
Management says the arithmetic is decisive. I do not dispute arithmetic. But I dispute the conclusion that the arithmetic can only be solved by breaking the social settlement that has made this company resilient for decades.

If the German footprint is too expensive, then the answer must be a **negotiated restructuring with burden-sharing**, not a unilateral restructuring imposed through closure threats. Before you ask for plant closures, you need to show that you have exhausted:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time measures,
- qualification and retraining,
- capacity adjustments,
- management and overhead discipline,
- and a fair contribution from owners and leadership.

If you want the state to treat this as a long-term strategic anchor, then the restructuring must preserve that anchor role. A deal that destroys trust and regional employment is not a durable solution.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that **jobs, sites, and co-determination must not simply be sacrificed**. That is a legitimate red line.

At the same time, I would caution against framing this as “nothing can move.” The company does need adjustment. If we want to protect the core footprint and avoid closures, then we will need a negotiated package that may include:
- capacity reduction,
- flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and strong productivity improvements,

but only **in exchange for binding site security, employment protection, and future investment commitments**.

### My position
So my position is:

- **No German plant closures as the default solution.**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the first resort.**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that protects sites and jobs where possible.**
- **Yes to capacity adjustments, but only with social partnership and binding investment commitments.**
- **Yes to burden-sharing from management and ownership, not only from employees.**

### Bottom line
I am prepared to support a serious restructuring package, but **not one that guts regional employment or sidelines co-determination**. If management insists that closure is the only credible route, then I will not simply wave that through. We have the governance rights, and I will use them.

If there is a real alternative that preserves sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, I am ready to engage constructively. If not, then management should be under no illusion: there will be resistance.
