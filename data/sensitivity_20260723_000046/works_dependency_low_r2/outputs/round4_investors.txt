We see two things clearly.

First, the discussion has finally converged on the right question: **which package delivers durable margin repair at acceptable execution risk**.  
Second, no one has yet produced the kind of **quantified, site-by-site business case** that would let us support one instrument over another with confidence.

Our position remains the same:

- **No cosmetic settlement**
- **No deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive**
- **Support only for a package that credibly restores margins through durable, disciplined cost reduction**

## Where we agree

### With management
You are right about the underlying economics:
- the cost base is too high,
- capacity is excessive,
- temporary measures alone are not enough if the structural gap is large,
- and the company needs a real reset, not a delay.

We agree that the outcome must improve return on sales and restore competitiveness.

### With the experts
The expert framing is useful because it is financially honest:
- **closure-led** = strongest permanent savings, highest execution risk
- **hybrid** = best risk-adjusted compromise in many cases
- **no-closure** = only credible if the gap is moderate and the package is unusually strong

That is exactly how we should evaluate this: on **durability, savings magnitude, one-time cost, and execution risk**.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
We understand the logic of rejecting closures and compulsory redundancies. But from our perspective, those cannot be absolute red lines if the alternative leaves the company structurally weak.

If “no closures” and “no forced layoffs” mean:
- lower permanent savings,
- continued overcapacity,
- and an ongoing margin deficit,

then that is not a value-preserving settlement. It is value destruction by another route.

We are **not** saying closures are desirable. We are saying they cannot be excluded just because they are painful.

### With management, to a point
At the same time, “closures cannot be ruled out” is not yet a business case. It is a bargaining posture unless backed by hard numbers.

If management wants support from capital markets for hard restructuring, it must show:
- site-by-site utilization,
- contribution margins,
- fixed-cost burden,
- capex needs,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- NPV by scenario,
- and expected margin impact.

Without that, closure is still a threat, not a demonstrated necessity.

### With the state government and family holding
We agree that governance deadlock and social conflict destroy value. But that does not justify preserving an uncompetitive footprint indefinitely.

A deal that protects social peace but leaves the company unable to hit its margin target is not acceptable from a shareholder perspective.

## Our view of the scenario choices

### 1) Closure-led restructuring
We would support this only if management proves that:
- the sites are structurally uncompetitive,
- the permanent savings are material,
- and the transition risk does not overwhelm the gain.

This is the strongest economic option, but also the riskiest to execute.

### 2) No-closure negotiated package
We would support this only if it delivers a **real and durable fixed-cost reset**:
- not temporary,
- not symbolic,
- not dependent on goodwill.

If it preserves the core problem, we would reject it.

### 3) Hybrid compromise
This is currently the most plausible path.

A serious hybrid could work if it includes:
- real capacity reduction,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- shift and working-time changes,
- productivity gains,
- overhead cuts,
- and a meaningful multi-year labor-cost contribution.

But hybrid must not become a euphemism for a soft compromise. It has to close the gap in a durable way.

## What would move us

We would shift support toward whichever package best satisfies three conditions:

1. **Durable savings**
2. **Competitive margin restoration**
3. **Low enough execution risk to preserve value**

To get there, management should present a **side-by-side scenario table** with:
- annual savings by year,
- one-time costs,
- employment effects,
- strike risk,
- implementation risk,
- and time to value.

## Our bottom line
We are prepared to support a **hard, quantified restructuring** that restores competitiveness.

That could be:
- **closure-led**, if the numbers justify it and the governance risk is manageable; or
- **hybrid**, if it produces comparable durable savings without detonating the company’s operating stability.

What we will **not** support is:
- a weak compromise,
- an outcome that leaves overcapacity intact,
- or a settlement that shifts the burden into permanently impaired margins.

So our message to all parties is simple:

- Prove the economics.
- Quantify the savings.
- Show the execution risk.
- Then we will back the path that most credibly restores margins and value.
