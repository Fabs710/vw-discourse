I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me respond as the controlling shareholder, through governance, with the long-term owner’s perspective.

### First: where I agree
I agree that this company needs a **credible long-term industrial plan**, not a symbolic gesture.
I also agree that:
- the German cost base is too high,
- capacity has to come down,
- investment in future products and technology is essential,
- and any solution must preserve labour peace as far as possible.

So I am not disputing the diagnosis that change is necessary.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only serious restructuring is one built around **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** as the starting point.

That may be management’s preferred instrument set, but from an ownership perspective it is not automatically the best value solution if it creates:
- prolonged conflict,
- reputational damage,
- governance deadlock,
- or a lasting weakening of the company’s social and industrial base.

A destructive confrontation that burns value for years is not acceptable to us. We are long-term owners; we do not benefit from a “solution” that solves this quarter and breaks the enterprise.

### To management
I hear your point that the economics must change durably. That is correct.

But I am not prepared to endorse a path that treats closures and forced redundancies as the default answer before every alternative has been seriously tested. The burden is on management to demonstrate:
- exactly why internal redeployment,
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- overhead discipline,
- productivity measures,
- and negotiated employee contributions

are insufficient.

If the case for closures is truly compelling, it must be shown in a way that is credible to the board and defensible for the long term. At present, I do not see enough evidence that the only route to competitiveness is a rupture with the workforce.

### To the works council and union
I also want to be direct with you: **a blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to guarantees” is not a complete answer either**.

If the company’s competitiveness is materially below target, then everyone has to contribute. I will not support an arrangement that preserves every legacy protection unchanged while expecting the company to absorb the cost. That would merely postpone the problem and worsen it.

What I can support is a negotiated package with:
- capacity reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- flexibility in working time,
- and a fair employee contribution,

but it must produce a real and durable improvement in the cost base and productivity.

### To the state government
I share your concern about regional legitimacy and social stability. Those are not secondary issues for this company. But I would caution against turning governance into a veto against necessary adjustment.

The company cannot be managed as if the domestic footprint were untouchable regardless of economics. The state’s role is important, but it cannot become a guarantee that structural problems will be left unresolved.

### My bottom line
The family holding’s position is this:

- **We support a serious restructuring.**
- **We support durable cost and capacity adjustment.**
- **We do not support value-destructive conflict.**
- **We are not giving a blank cheque for closures or forced redundancies.**
- **But we also will not endorse a settlement that preserves the status quo under a different label.**

So my test is simple:
Show us a plan that restores margins and competitiveness **without permanent damage to the enterprise**, and we can back it.
Show us only confrontation, slogans, or a refusal to change, and we will not move.
