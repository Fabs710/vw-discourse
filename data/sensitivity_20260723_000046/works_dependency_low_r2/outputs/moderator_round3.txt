## Core impasse

The plateau is now centered on a **single unresolved question**:

**Is a restructuring without German plant closures and without compulsory redundancies economically sufficient to close the company’s structural cost gap, or are those instruments necessary?**

That breaks into three linked disputes:

1. **Instrument dispute**
   - Employee side, state, and family holding reject closures and forced layoffs as default tools.
   - Management, investors, and partly the experts keep them on the table as economically credible instruments.

2. **Evidence dispute**
   - Management is asking for permission to use hard instruments.
   - Others are asking management to first prove, site by site, that softer instruments cannot deliver equivalent savings.

3. **Trust / governance dispute**
   - Employee side and state insist on co-determination, binding guarantees, and social legitimacy.
   - Management and investors fear that precluding hard instruments will produce an underpowered, non-durable deal.

So the impasse is not just about numbers; it is about **which risks the group is willing to bear**:
- the risk of under-delivering savings, or
- the risk of conflict, delay, and value destruction from closures and layoffs.

---

## Possible compromise positions

### Compromise 1: “Hybrid first, closure only by trigger”
A staged agreement in which the parties commit now to:
- a strong hybrid package,
- measurable site and cost targets,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, shift concentration, working-time changes, and overhead cuts,
- and a jointly defined review point.

If the agreed savings or utilization thresholds are not met by the review date, then the parties reopen the question of plant-specific measures.

**Why it might bridge positions:**  
- Gives management and investors a credible path to structural savings.
- Gives works council, union, state, and family holding a non-closure-first framework.
- Preserves leverage without immediately detonating trust.

---

### Compromise 2: “No closures, but binding structural depth”
A no-closure package that is only acceptable if it includes:
- substantial and durable capacity reduction,
- multi-year employee contribution,
- management and owner contributions,
- binding productivity and overhead commitments,
- and strict implementation monitoring.

This would need to be stronger than a symbolic deal and explicitly quantified against the closure scenario.

**Why it might bridge positions:**  
- Addresses employee, state, and family concerns about closures.
- Gives investors and management a chance to achieve durable savings without a closure fight.
- Requires hard numbers, not promises.

---

### Compromise 3: “Site-by-site differentiation”
A differentiated settlement that does **not** decide closure in the abstract, but classifies sites into categories:
- **protected sites** with investment and productivity commitments,
- **restructured sites** with deep shift/model/capacity changes,
- and **review sites** where closure remains a last-resort option only if predefined economic thresholds are breached.

**Why it might bridge positions:**  
- Prevents an all-or-nothing fight.
- Lets each stakeholder accept different outcomes for different sites.
- Reduces the chance that one contested plant blocks the entire deal.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the quantified savings gap, by site, that cannot be closed through voluntary exits, redeployment, shift changes, productivity improvements, overhead cuts, and temporary employee contributions?**

---

### To Industrial Union
**What specific durable measures, with quantified annual savings, would you accept as sufficient to match the economic effect of a closure-led plan without closures or forced redundancies?**

---

### To Works Council
**Which concrete non-closure instruments are you prepared to trade for binding site and job guarantees, and what minimum savings threshold would make that package acceptable?**

---

### To Regional State Government
**Would you support a differentiated settlement that protects some sites but allows a review-based restructuring path for others if agreed savings targets are missed?**

---

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Would you back a staged hybrid agreement now if it included hard triggers, measurable performance gates, and the option to escalate later if the cost reset fails?**

---

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of durable annual savings and time-to-value would make you support a no-closure or hybrid package over an immediate closure-led approach?**

---

### To the Independent Expert
**What specific trigger metrics should be used to decide whether the hybrid package has succeeded or whether escalation to closures becomes economically justified?**
