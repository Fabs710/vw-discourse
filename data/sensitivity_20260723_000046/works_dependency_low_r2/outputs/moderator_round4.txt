## Core impasse
The discussion is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is:

**Management, investors, and the family holding want a restructuring package that can include closures and compulsory redundancies if the economics justify it.**  
**Works council, union, and state government want a restructuring package that excludes closures and forced layoffs as a matter of principle, or at least as the default path.**

So the unresolved issue is not the need for savings, but **which instruments are permitted to achieve them, and under what proof standard**.

A second layer of impasse is procedural:

**The employee side wants management to prove site-by-site necessity before hard instruments are kept on the table.**  
**Management wants the employee side to accept that hard instruments remain available until the quantified alternative is shown to close the gap.**

In short:  
- one side seeks **pre-commitment against closures/forced redundancies**,  
- the other seeks **pre-commitment against ruling them out before the numbers are complete**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified hybrid-first framework with hard triggers
A first compromise could be:

- immediate agreement on a **hybrid restructuring package**
- no closures or compulsory redundancies in the opening phase
- a **jointly defined scorecard** for each site: utilization, contribution margin, fixed costs, capex needs, and expected NPV
- a **fixed review date**
- if agreed targets are missed, escalation to a second-stage discussion where harder instruments may be considered

**Bridge value:**  
This gives employee representatives a real non-closure starting point, while giving management and investors a conditional route to stronger measures if the package underperforms.

---

### Proposal 2: No closure in Germany, but deeper structural obligations
A second compromise could be:

- a commitment to **no German plant closures** for a defined period
- **no compulsory redundancies** during that period
- in exchange, the workforce accepts a **deeper structural contribution**:
  - shift changes
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - overhead cuts
  - temporary wage or benefit moderation
- management and owners commit to **binding investment and product allocation**

**Bridge value:**  
This addresses the works council and union’s strongest red line while still requiring durable savings, not just delay.

---

### Proposal 3: Site differentiation and partial-footprint adjustment
A third compromise could be:

- each site is assessed separately
- rather than deciding “closure vs. no closure” company-wide, the parties agree to:
  - full preservation for strategically viable plants
  - staged reduction for borderline sites
  - intensified restructuring at the weakest sites
- where a site cannot meet agreed thresholds, the parties revisit the option of footprint reduction, but only after a documented joint review

**Bridge value:**  
This avoids a blanket closure logic and also avoids a blanket no-closure rule. It creates room for differentiated treatment, which is the most plausible bridge among the current positions.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management / Group & Brand Management
**If you believe closures or compulsory redundancies may be necessary, will you commit to presenting a site-by-site quantified comparison within a fixed deadline, and suspend any closure decision until that comparison has been jointly reviewed?**

### To the Works Council
**If a hybrid package delivers the same verified savings trajectory without closures, will you support it even if it requires deep working-time, shift, and redeployment changes?**

### To the Union
**What specific temporary employee contribution are you prepared to recommend, and what management or owner counter-contribution would you require for it to be acceptable?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you use your blocking and convening role to insist on a joint timetable and data package, even if that means delaying any final decision until the numbers are complete?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you publicly back a no-forced-layoffs path if, and only if, the hybrid package meets a defined savings threshold and investment commitment, even if that constrains management’s tactical flexibility?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a hybrid package if it is shown to achieve a durable margin repair with lower execution risk than closure, even if the annual savings are somewhat smaller than the most aggressive scenario?**

### To the Independent Expert
**Can you convert your scenario logic into a single agreed threshold framework that defines when hybrid is sufficient and when escalation to harder instruments is economically justified?**
