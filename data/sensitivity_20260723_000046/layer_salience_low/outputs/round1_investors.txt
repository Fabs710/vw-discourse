I agree with the shared diagnosis that the company has a **real competitiveness problem** and that a merely cosmetic solution would be value-destructive. If the German cost base stays structurally above peers, then margins, valuation, and future investment capacity will continue to erode. From an investor standpoint, that is not acceptable.

Where I **do not** agree is with any framing that treats **plant closures as the only credible route** before a serious alternative has been tested. Closed plants, strikes, political escalation, and reputational damage can easily destroy more value than they create if they are used as the first instrument rather than the last resort. But equally, I will not support a package that protects sites while leaving the cost base fundamentally uncompetitive.

My position is simple:

- **No cosmetic settlement**
- **No outcome that preserves structurally uncompetitive margins**
- **No open-ended conflict that destroys value**
- **No guarantee package unless it is matched by real, durable savings**

To the **Works Council** and **Union**: I understand the insistence on avoiding forced redundancies and preserving German industrial capacity. But “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not enough on its own. If that red line means the company cannot reach a sustainable cost base, then it merely shifts the burden onto shareholders through lower returns and eventually a weaker business. That is not a defensible outcome. Any employee contribution must be **material, credible, and durable**, not symbolic.

To **Management**: I support the need for decisive action, but you have to show that the restructuring is not just a blunt exercise in cutting. If you want investors to back painful measures, you need a **clear bridge to competitive margins**, with measurable milestones, timing, and proof that the savings are structurally locked in. If the plan relies too heavily on conflict, uncertainty, or assumptions that the workforce will simply concede, then the market will discount it.

To the **Regional State Government** and **Controlling Family**: I agree that value destruction through prolonged confrontation must be avoided. But “politically difficult” does not mean “economically impossible.” If the governance structure makes all meaningful restructuring impossible, then the company will continue to underperform, and that is ultimately worse for all owners and stakeholders.

What would move us is a package with these features:

1. **Quantified, durable cost reduction**
2. **A credible capacity adjustment plan**
3. **No permanent weakening of competitiveness through half-measures**
4. **A fair but substantial employee contribution**
5. **A clear investment and product commitment**
6. **Deal certainty with minimal strike exposure**

If the proposal can genuinely restore competitive margins without turning into a destructive standoff, we can support it. If not, we will judge it as insufficient and value-eroding.
