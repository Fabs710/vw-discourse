I will be equally direct.

I **agree** with the broad diagnosis that the company cannot simply preserve the status quo and hope the market improves. A structurally uncompetitive cost base in Germany would eventually damage the whole group, including the workforce, suppliers, and owners. From a long-term ownership perspective, doing nothing would be irresponsible.

But I **do not** agree that the answer must begin with plant closures or an approach that creates prolonged conflict and reputational damage. That would be a very expensive way to achieve restructuring, and it risks destroying value before any savings are realized.

### Where I agree with management
- The company needs a **durable** improvement in cost and capacity.
- A cosmetic or temporary deal would be inadequate.
- Future investment and product allocation must be part of the settlement.
- The workforce will likely need to contribute in some form if the company is to regain competitiveness.

### Where I disagree with management
I am not convinced that closing German plants is either the first or the best instrument here.

A plan that treats closures as the default answer may be financially neat in theory, but in practice it can trigger:
- extended industrial conflict,
- political intervention,
- damage to the brand,
- supplier disruption,
- and a loss of trust that makes future change harder.

That is not an abstract concern. For a company with this governance structure, a solution that cannot survive co-determination and political reality is not a real solution.

### To the works council and union
I understand and respect the insistence on:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closure for its own sake,
- and no one-sided burden-sharing.

That said, I have to be clear: **a blanket refusal of any meaningful capacity adjustment would also be unacceptable**. If the structure remains too large for demand, the company will continue to bleed margin and eventually weaken the very jobs you are trying to protect.

So I support your red lines against forced layoffs and disorderly closures, but I cannot support a position that makes all structural adjustment impossible.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate the political and regional responsibility you are pointing to. A major employer cannot be treated like a short-term financial asset.

But I would caution against turning that into an implicit veto on all hard choices. If every site is protected regardless of economics, then the company simply accumulates delayed problems. That is not stewardship; it is postponement.

### What I would consider credible
I could support a settlement if it is built around **durable competitiveness**, not symbolic sacrifice. That means:

- meaningful capacity reduction,
- lower labour costs,
- improved flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification where possible,
- voluntary mechanisms before involuntary ones,
- and a serious investment commitment to German plants and future products.

A time-limited employee contribution may be part of that, but only if it is substantial enough to matter and paired with real structural change. A token contribution in exchange for permanent guarantees would not be acceptable.

### What I will not support
- A deal that preserves the full current cost structure.
- A deal that promises security without competitiveness.
- A deal that produces short-term peace but leaves the company weaker in 2030.
- Any outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or weakens the owner’s governance position.

### My bottom line
I am not here to defend management at any price, and I am not here to side with labour at any price. I am here to protect the long-term value of the company.

So my position is this:

- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to investment and a future product roadmap.
- **No** to pretending that all plants and all structures can remain unchanged.
- **No** to a conflict strategy that destroys value through escalation.

If there is a package that restores margins and competitiveness without unnecessary instability, I will support it. If the only alternatives offered are either cosmetic compromise or destructive confrontation, then I will not endorse either.
