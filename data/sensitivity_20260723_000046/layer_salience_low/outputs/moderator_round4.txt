## Core impasse
The deadlock is now concentrated on **one question**:

**Is a credible 2030 restructuring possible without formal German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or do the economics require at least some irreversible footprint reduction?**

Everything else flows from that.

### Why it is stuck
- **Works Council / Union / State** are treating **no closures** as a core red line, with flexibility only inside that boundary.
- **Management / Investors / Experts** are treating **real recurring savings and capacity reduction** as the non-negotiable test, and regard “no closures” as insufficient unless it produces closure-like economics.
- **Family holding** is in the middle: no closure-first script, but also no blanket protection of every site.
- The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **what counts as enough restructuring** and **who bears the irreversible burden**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions for discussion

### 1) Plant-by-plant gatekeeping with objective triggers
A structured sequence:
- immediate independent plant-by-plant economic review,
- agreed criteria for viability,
- a first phase of non-irreversible measures,
- and pre-agreed escalation if savings/capacity targets are missed.

**Purpose:**  
Lets labour avoid immediate closure decisions, while giving management/investors a route to site-specific action if the numbers warrant it.

---

### 2) No-closure compact with explicit “closure-equivalent” savings test
A time-bound package that keeps sites formally open, but only if it includes:
- shift and line eliminations,
- major volume transfers,
- voluntary exits / retirement bridges,
- working-time flexibility,
- and measurable fixed-cost removal.

**Purpose:**  
Creates a middle path: no formal closure now, but only if the package produces **closure-like economics** and can be verified against milestones.

---

### 3) Conditional guarantees tied to burden-sharing from all sides
A package linking:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closure for the duration of the compact,
- limited and temporary employee contributions,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- and management/owner commitments on cost, governance, and capital support.

**Purpose:**  
Converts “workers must contribute” into a reciprocal structure where labor concessions are matched by durable commitments from management, owners, and possibly the state.

---

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific economic threshold would you accept as proof that a site can remain open without becoming a permanent drag on the company by 2030?**

### Union
**What is the maximum temporary employee contribution you could defend, and what binding guarantees must be attached so it does not become permanent wage erosion?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Which exact sites, if any, are already demonstrably non-viable on a plant-by-plant basis, and what hard savings would each closure deliver compared with deep consolidation?**

### Regional State Government
**Under what objective conditions would you permit site rationalisation, and what evidence must be shown before your blocking position is lifted?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Will you support a restructuring that preserves every site only if the numbers are proven, or will you back irreversible site action where the independent review shows it is necessary?**

### Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a no-formal-closure package if an independent review shows it delivers equivalent recurring savings, capacity reduction, and lower fixed cost through 2030?**

### Expert: Financial Restructuring / Industrial Turnaround
**What measurable milestones would you set for judging whether a no-closure package has become insufficient and should trigger escalation?**

### Expert: Independent Plant-Level Industrial Economist
**Which site-level indicators should be decisive in separating “consolidatable” plants from “structurally non-viable” ones?**
