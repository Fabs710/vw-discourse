### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company faces a **real competitiveness and cost-base problem**;
- a **purely cosmetic or temporary solution is not sufficient**;
- any settlement should be **negotiated** rather than imposed;
- **investment and future product allocation** must be part of any durable deal;
- **employee contributions** may be discussed, but only if they are **fair, limited, and meaningful**;
- **forced redundancies** are strongly resisted by labour-side and public stakeholders;
- **prolonged conflict and uncertainty** would be damaging for the company and all stakeholders.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures:** Management and some capital-side actors keep closures on the table; labour-side actors and the regional state reject them as a starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour-side actors reject them; management wants headcount reduction and does not rule out involuntary measures.
- **Scope of restructuring:** Management and investors argue for **durable capacity and cost reduction**; the works council and union insist this must not come at the expense of sites and jobs.
- **Guarantees:** Labour-side actors seek a **binding site/employment guarantee through 2030**; management says that would be unrealistic if it prevents restructuring.
- **Burden-sharing:** There is no shared agreement yet on **how much employees should contribute**, what management/shareholders contribute, and what conditions must accompany any sacrifice.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
Several central trade-offs remain open:
- **How much capacity reduction is necessary** versus how much site preservation is possible.
- Whether the company can achieve competitiveness through **flexibility, redeployment, and voluntary measures** rather than closures and forced layoffs.
- What level of **employee contribution** would be considered material enough by management and investors, while still acceptable to labour.
- What concrete **investment, product, and employment commitments** could credibly justify worker concessions.
- How to balance **short-term savings** against **long-term industrial presence and social stability**.
- Whether there is a viable **deal structure with enforceable milestones** that can satisfy governance, political, and market expectations.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Next round should focus on **specific package design** rather than repeating red lines. The parties should try to define:
- a **quantified restructuring target**;
- which measures are **voluntary, temporary, or permanent**;
- what **binding guarantees** can realistically be offered;
- what **employee contributions** are on the table;
- and what **investment/product commitments** would accompany them.

A useful next step would be to test whether there is a package that achieves **durable savings and capacity adjustment** without starting from plant closures or compulsory redundancies.
