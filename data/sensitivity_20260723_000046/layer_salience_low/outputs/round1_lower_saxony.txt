I will be direct.

I **agree** with management on one point: the company cannot ignore the structural pressure in the German cost base. If the current model is not competitive, then simply postponing adjustment would hurt the company, the workforce, and the region in the end.

But I **strongly disagree** with the conclusion that this justifies plant closures as the default or that the burden should fall mainly on the workforce.

### To the Works Council and Union
I support your red lines on **forced redundancies** and I understand the insistence on preserving German industrial sites. From the state’s perspective, closing plants in this region is not a normal restructuring measure — it is a major regional-economic decision with long-term consequences for jobs, suppliers, training, and public finances.

That said, I would caution against framing the discussion as “nothing may change.” If we want to protect sites and employment in a durable way, we need a restructuring that is serious enough to restore competitiveness. A purely defensive stance risks making eventual damage worse.

### To Management
If your proposal effectively means:
- closures first,
- then job cuts,
- then wage reductions,
- with no equal commitment on investment and future product allocation,

then I cannot support it.

I would view that as **too one-sided, too risky, and too damaging to the company’s role as a regional anchor**. It also ignores the governance reality: there are effective blocking positions here, including the state’s role and the co-determination structure. A plan that treats those as obstacles to be bypassed will fail politically and institutionally.

### What I could support
I am open to a **negotiated restructuring package** if it includes all of the following:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A real, measurable employee contribution**
   - time-limited
   - fair
   - negotiated
   - not permanent wage dumping
4. **Capacity reductions through socially responsible means**
   - flexibility
   - internal redeployment
   - natural attrition
   - early retirement
   - qualification
5. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
6. **A credible product and technology roadmap**
7. **A site and employment perspective beyond the next bargaining round**

### My concern
If “competitiveness” is defined only as a short-term margin improvement by shrinking the German footprint, then you may win a spreadsheet and lose the company’s industrial base. That would be unacceptable from a regional and long-term perspective.

### My position
So I am **not** endorsing closures. I am **not** endorsing compulsory redundancies. And I am **not** prepared to accept a restructuring that breaks the social partnership model while leaving the workforce to absorb the main cost.

But I **am** prepared to back a difficult negotiated solution if it protects:
- jobs,
- plants,
- co-determination,
- and the company’s long-term competitiveness.

Management should therefore come forward with a package that shows genuine burden-sharing and a real commitment to the German sites. If it does not, then I will side against closures and against any attempt to force through a destructive restructuring.
