I’ll be equally direct.

## To the Works Council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures**
- **no dismantling of collective bargaining**
- **no settlement that makes workers carry the full burden**

I also agree with you that a “contribution” from labour is only legitimate if it is **temporary, capped, and matched by binding guarantees**. If management wants flexibility from employees, then it must give something durable in return: job security, investment, and a credible industrial future.

Where I need to sharpen our common line is this: we should not let “no closures” become a slogan that substitutes for a serious package. A settlement has to be **economically real** and **socially fair**. That means:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- productivity improvements,
- and a limited employee contribution.

But it also means management and owners must contribute materially. If they expect labour to finance the restructuring while preserving their own freedom of action, that is not shared sacrifice. That is one-sided burden shifting.

## To Management
You keep saying the economics require tough measures. Fine — we do not deny that the company has a cost and overcapacity problem.

But you still have **not** proven that closures or compulsory redundancies are necessary rather than merely the easiest route for management. Saying “closures are the cleanest lever” is not the same as saying “closures are unavoidable.”

What you still owe us is not rhetoric, but a **plant-by-plant comparison**:
1. current utilization,
2. fixed cost per unit,
3. break-even volume,
4. savings from deep consolidation,
5. savings from closure,
6. and the execution risk of each path.

Until then, “we need closures” is an assertion, not a demonstrated necessity.

And let me be blunt: if your preferred solution is to weaken the collective framework, cut wages, and keep closures as a threat in the background, then you are not offering restructuring — you are attempting to transfer the crisis onto labour.

That will be resisted.

## To Investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic compromise is worthless. I agree.

Where I disagree is your repeated tendency to treat **no closures** as if it automatically means **no real restructuring**. That is simply wrong.

A non-closure package can be real if it includes:
- binding volume shifts,
- real line and shift reductions,
- headcount reduction through voluntary means,
- labour-cost moderation,
- and hard commitments on products and investment.

What it cannot be is a paper exercise. If it is serious, durable, and enforceable, then it deserves consideration.

But I will not accept the logic that the workforce must prove in advance that every alternative will work perfectly, while management’s closure option gets treated as the default “realism.” That is not neutral analysis. That is a preference.

## To the Regional State Government
You have a blocking position for a reason. Use it.

I agree that the company needs a real restructuring, but I do **not** agree that closures should be the starting point. The state should insist on:
- a binding comparison of alternatives,
- a plant-by-plant economic review,
- and a negotiated solution that preserves German industrial employment.

Do not let the process become a polite path to site liquidation. Once a closure logic takes hold, the damage is irreversible. The state should be the force that prevents an easy slide into job destruction, not the facilitator of it.

## To the Controlling Family
You talk about stewardship and long-term value. I take that seriously. But stewardship means protecting the industrial backbone, not only the margin line.

If you truly want value preservation, then support a deal that:
- keeps German plants open,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- preserves co-determination,
- and shares the burden fairly.

If you back a closure-led approach too early, you may get short-term accounting relief but long-term damage to labour relations, skills, and trust. That is not stewardship. That is value erosion with a cleaner spreadsheet.

## On the expert view
The experts have been useful, but their conclusions are being overstretched.

What they actually show is:
- the company has a structural problem,
- temporary fixes are not enough,
- and a serious package must remove real cost and capacity.

What they do **not** show is that closures must be the first move, or that compulsory redundancies are the inevitable answer.

That distinction matters.

A serious non-closure package must be judged on whether it removes **real** fixed cost and capacity, not on whether it is politically convenient. We are open to that test. What we will not accept is a closure-first mindset dressed up as objectivity.

## Where I stand now
My position remains unchanged:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No German plant closures**
- **Protection of the collective wage framework**
- **Fair, limited, temporary employee contributions only in a genuine shared-sacrifice package**
- **Binding investment, product, and job guarantees**
- **Real co-determination in implementation**

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- selective productivity measures,
- and capped, temporary labour contributions.

But only if the company and owners also commit to their side of the bargain. Workers will not be the sole shock absorber.

## What we are not prepared to accept
We will not accept:
- plant closures as the default answer,
- forced layoffs in any form,
- permanent weakening of collective bargaining,
- or a restructuring that simply shifts the burden onto the workforce.

## My direct challenge back to management and investors
If you insist that a no-closure solution cannot work, then put the comparison on the table — fully and transparently:
- what a deep consolidation package delivers,
- what a closure package delivers,
- what the conflict and execution risks are,
- and why the labour-side measures are allegedly insufficient only after all real options have been included.

Until that is done, you have not proven that closures are necessary.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking a restructuring that makes **workers pay first and most**.

If management wants a durable agreement, it must stop treating closures as the benchmark and start proving that a serious alternative fails. If it can prove that, we will engage honestly. If it cannot, then it is asking for concessions without justification — and that will not be accepted.
