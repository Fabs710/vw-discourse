I agree with the Works Council on the core principle: **no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures in Germany**. Those are not acceptable starting points for us either.

What is unacceptable is a restructuring plan where **the workforce is expected to carry the entire burden** through wage cuts, job cuts, and loss of guarantees, while management and owners present closures as the only serious option. That is not fair, and it will not create a durable settlement.

That said, I want to be equally clear: we are **not denying the economic pressure** on the company. We know the cost base is too high, and we know the market is difficult. But the answer cannot be to destroy jobs and weaken collective standards first. If savings are needed, then they must come from a **shared-sacrifice package**.

Our position is:

- **No plant closures**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **Preserve the collective wage framework**
- **Any contribution from employees must be limited, negotiated, and matched by binding commitments from management and owners**
- **Investment and future-product guarantees must accompany any labour-cost contribution**
- **Capacity reduction may be discussed, but only through socially responsible and reversible measures, not through blunt cuts**

We are prepared to discuss elements such as:

- temporary wage restraint or a time-limited contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- phased capacity adjustments,
- use of internal redeployment and qualification,
- a solidarity-based fund or bonus waiver,
- and productivity measures that do not undermine the collective agreement.

But I will say plainly: if management insists that “competitiveness” means closures, forced redundancies, and open-ended insecurity, then we will oppose it. If needed, we will use the bargaining tools available to us.

So the test for any proposal is simple:
**Does management also contribute, or are workers meant to absorb all the pain?**
If it is the latter, we will not support it.
