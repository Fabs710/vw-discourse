## Core impasse
The main deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is about **the instrument of adjustment**:

- **Labour side / regional state**: rejects plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the starting point, and wants restructuring through voluntary, negotiated, socially protected measures.
- **Management / investors**: believe those measures are unlikely to produce enough durable savings and capacity reduction unless closures or equivalent shutdowns are included.
- **Controlling family**: is open to tough restructuring but wants a socially and politically survivable path, not a closure-first script.

So the conflict is:
**how to achieve durable capacity and cost reduction by 2030 without triggering unacceptable social, political, or governance damage.**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to explore

### 1) Plant-by-plant “proof first” process with a no-closure presumption unless thresholds are missed
A mediated sequence where management must present plant-level economics, and closures are not approved unless specific financial and utilisation thresholds cannot be met through alternatives.

**Elements**
- plant-by-plant cost, utilisation, and product-line analysis
- agreed savings target for 2030
- benchmark comparison against no-closure / consolidation / closure scenarios
- a decision rule: closure only if alternative packages fail to meet a defined savings threshold

**Why this could bridge sides**
- gives management a route to hard restructuring if needed
- gives labour and the state a formal test before any closure
- creates a shared factual basis instead of a principle clash

---

### 2) “Consolidation without formal closure” package, but only if it removes real capacity
A negotiated compromise in which one or more sites are not formally closed, but production is materially reduced, lines are stopped, shifts are removed, and fixed costs are cut enough to approximate closure-level savings.

**Elements**
- line rationalisation / shift elimination
- product concentration into fewer core lines
- voluntary exits and early retirement
- working-time flexibility and temporary labour contributions
- binding investment and product allocation to retained sites
- review clause if savings are not materializing

**Why this could bridge sides**
- preserves the symbolic commitment against closures
- still gives management real capacity reduction
- may be politically more acceptable to the state and workforce
- only works if the savings are genuinely durable and quantified

---

### 3) Time-bound “restructuring compact” with deferred closure option
A phased deal: immediate labour and productivity measures now, with a fixed review point later. If agreed targets are missed, specific sites enter a closure or deep downsizing phase automatically.

**Elements**
- 12–24 month restructuring window
- employee contributions, flexibility, redeployment, productivity measures upfront
- binding investment commitments from company/owners
- pre-agreed trigger criteria for escalation to closure or shutdown
- independent monitoring of performance against targets

**Why this could bridge sides**
- gives labour and state time to prove alternatives
- gives management a credible fallback if targets fail
- reduces immediate confrontation while keeping pressure for real change

## Pointed questions for each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact recurring annual savings and utilisation level do you require by 2030, and what is the minimum footprint reduction that would satisfy those numbers?**

### To the Works Council
**Which specific capacity-reduction measures short of full plant closure would you accept as genuinely durable, and what quantified savings do you believe they can produce?**

### To the Union
**What time-limited employee contribution package would you consider acceptable if it were paired with binding employment, investment, and product guarantees?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to support a plant-by-plant review that could still result in closure if independent evidence shows alternatives cannot meet the required savings?**

### To the Controlling Family
**If management proves that one site is structurally non-viable, will you back a closure decision, or do you require a non-closure alternative regardless of the numbers?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a deep consolidation package with no formal closures if an independent model shows it matches closure-based savings within a tolerable risk range?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction and labour-cost relief would you regard as sufficient to restore competitiveness by 2030 without relying on closures as the primary lever?**
