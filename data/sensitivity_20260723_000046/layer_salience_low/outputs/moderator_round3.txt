### Core impasse

The dispute has narrowed to one central fault line:

**Whether restoring competitiveness by 2030 requires formal German plant closures and possible compulsory redundancies, or whether an equally durable result can be achieved through deep consolidation without closures while preserving site and job guarantees.**

At this point, the parties are not disagreeing that restructuring is needed. They are disagreeing on:

- **the minimum economically sufficient level of capacity reduction**
- **whether “no closures” can still mean real fixed-cost removal**
- **how much burden can legitimately be placed on labour without weakening the collective framework**
- **whether governance actors will accept evidence-based site rationalisation if it is proven necessary**

So the impasse is not diagnosis. It is **the acceptable mechanism of adjustment**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions

#### 1) Plant-by-plant proof process with decision thresholds
A structured review could be established for each German site, using agreed criteria such as:

- utilization
- fixed cost per unit
- break-even volume
- logistics/tooling constraints
- product allocation
- savings achievable under no-closure and consolidation scenarios

**Compromise logic:**  
This does not commit anyone to closures upfront, but it also prevents a blanket “no closures” veto. It forces the debate onto quantified thresholds.

**Possible outcome:**  
- retain sites that meet viability criteria
- consolidate or downsize sites that fail agreed benchmarks
- defer irreversible decisions until evidence is complete

---

#### 2) Deep consolidation without formal closures, but only with hard capacity reduction
A non-closure package could be accepted if it includes binding measures such as:

- line shutdowns or shift removals
- real volume transfer to fewer plants
- voluntary exits and retirement bridges at scale
- product concentration
- measurable fixed-cost elimination
- time-bound labour contributions

**Compromise logic:**  
This preserves the “no formal closure” principle while still requiring actual economic adjustment. It is only valid if the savings are demonstrably durable.

**Guardrail:**  
If capacity reduction is only cosmetic, the package would not count as a true compromise.

---

#### 3) Time-bound restructuring compact with an escalation mechanism
A staged agreement could be used:

- immediate temporary employee contributions and flexibility
- binding investment commitments
- agreed milestones for cost reduction and utilization
- independent review at set dates
- automatic escalation to deeper restructuring measures if targets are missed

**Compromise logic:**  
This allows parties to test whether a less disruptive route can work, while preserving a fallback if it cannot.

**Guardrail:**  
The escalation criteria must be objective and enforceable, or this becomes a delay tactic.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**Which specific German sites, if any, can you show—using plant-level economics—to be non-viable under a no-closure or deep-consolidation scenario, and what recurring savings would each alternative deliver by 2030?**

#### To the Works Council
**If management proves that a particular site is structurally uncompetitive, what exact restructuring outcome would you accept that still preserves your red lines on no forced redundancies and no erosion of co-determination?**

#### To the Union
**What temporary employee contribution package do you consider legitimate, and what binding guarantees must accompany it so that workers are not financing a later closure or a permanent weakening of collective standards?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**How will you use your blocking position: as a veto against closures in principle, or as leverage to require a quantified comparison of all restructuring scenarios before any irreversible decision?**

#### To the Controlling Family
**Will you support closure or site rationalisation if the plant-by-plant evidence shows it is necessary for durable competitiveness, or will you require a non-closure alternative even if it delivers weaker economics?**

#### To Investors
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, cost savings, and execution certainty would you regard as sufficient to support the deal even if it does not involve formal closures?**
