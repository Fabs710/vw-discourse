As the Works Council, my position is clear:

We will **not accept plant closures in Germany** and we will **not accept compulsory redundancies**. Those are red lines.

What we are prepared to discuss are **alternative restructuring measures** that secure the company’s future **without sacrificing sites and jobs**:

- capacity adjustments through flexible production planning,
- temporary working-time measures,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- internal efficiency improvements,
- investment commitments for the German plants,
- and a **binding employment and site guarantee through 2030**.

If management wants the workforce to contribute, then that contribution must be **fair, time-limited, and matched by binding guarantees**. We will not accept that employees pay the full price while management breaks the historic job-security framework and shifts the burden onto the plants.

Our priorities are:

1. **Keep every German plant open**
2. **Avoid forced layoffs**
3. **Protect co-determination and the works council’s role**
4. **Secure long-term industrial viability for 2030 and beyond**
5. **Ensure that any burden-sharing is fair and negotiated**

We are willing to talk, but only on the basis of a solution that preserves jobs, sites, and the company’s social responsibility. If the proposal means closures or forced cuts, then we are firmly against it.
