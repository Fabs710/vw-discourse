I hear the message from the works council and the union, but I need to be equally direct: **we do not have a viable future if we preserve the current cost and capacity structure in Germany.**

Let me be clear on the core issue.

### Where I agree
- I agree that **this must be a negotiated solution**, not a unilateral diktat.
- I agree that **investment, product allocation, and future industrial commitment** matter.
- I agree that **purely short-term cuts would be the wrong answer** if they do not change the structural cost base.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that we can protect every plant, avoid major capacity changes, and still restore competitiveness.

The facts are brutal:
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our German cost base is too high,
- and the brand is not hitting its return target.

If we do not reduce **structural overcapacity** and **labour costs**, then we will simply postpone the crisis and weaken the company further. That would be worse for everyone, including the workforce.

### On closures
You say no plant closures. I am not prepared to rule that out at the outset.

I am not threatening closures for drama. I am saying plainly that **all options must be on the table**, including closures, if that is what it takes to create a cost base that is actually competitive by 2030.

If there is an alternative package that achieves the same durable savings, I will look at it. But it must be **credible, measurable, and lasting**. A cosmetic package or a temporary bridge is not enough.

### On redundancies
I am not looking for random layoffs. What I need is a **substantial reduction in headcount and capacity**, and I need it to be durable.

That can happen through a combination of:
- site adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- voluntary severance,
- productivity measures,
- and, yes, where necessary, further workforce reduction.

But I will not accept a proposal that keeps the same fixed cost base and merely shifts the pain into a short-term wage restraint.

### On employee contributions
A time-limited contribution can be part of the solution, but only if it is meaningful enough to matter. I am not interested in symbolic gestures.

If employees ask for site guarantees and job security, then the company needs a real cost contribution in return. That means:
- lower labour costs,
- more flexibility,
- and a reduction in structural excess.

### On guarantees
A binding guarantee through 2030 for all sites is not realistic if it prevents the restructuring needed to secure the company’s future. I will not sign up to a deal that locks in today’s inefficiencies until the end of the decade.

### My bottom line
I am open to a negotiated package, but only if it delivers:
1. **sustainable capacity reduction**
2. **material labour-cost reduction**
3. **durable improvement in margins**
4. **freed-up funds for future products and technology**

If your position is “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real structural change,” then that is not a solution. It is a delay.

So my question back to you is simple:
**What concrete package do you propose that cuts enough cost and capacity to restore competitiveness without preserving overcapacity?**
