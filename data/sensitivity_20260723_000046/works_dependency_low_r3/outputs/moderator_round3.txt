## Core impasse
The plateau is centered on **one unresolved sequencing dispute**:

- **Management / investors** want to treat **closures and compulsory redundancies as legitimate default tools** if they maximize durable savings.
- **Works council / union** want **no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies** as non-negotiable preconditions.
- **State / controlling family** are positioned in the middle but have not yet forced convergence on a shared test for “equivalence.”
- **Experts** keep saying a non-closure path is possible only if it delivers **comparable permanent savings**, but the group still lacks a **plant-by-plant quantified model** to prove or disprove that.

So the deadlock is not mainly about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **which restructuring instruments are admissible before the numbers are verified**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled without endorsing any side.

### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional closure trigger
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a two-step process:
- Phase 1: implement a plant-by-plant non-closure package using shift reduction, line consolidation, redeployment, voluntary exits, retirement pathways, and time-limited labor contributions.
- Phase 2: only if an independently verified savings gap remains after Phase 1, reopen discussion on limited footprint changes, with closures treated as a **last-resort conditional trigger**, not a starting point.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It gives labor the non-closure test it demands, while giving management/investors a defined pathway if the gap remains.

---

### 2) “No forced redundancies first” with bounded workforce adjustment
**Proposal:**  
Agree that for a defined period, the company will use:
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- retraining,

before any compulsory redundancies are considered. In exchange, labor would accept a time-limited contribution and productivity measures, tied to binding investment and product commitments.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It does not ban compulsory redundancies forever, but it delays them behind a negotiated hierarchy of measures and makes labor contribution conditional on reciprocal commitments.

---

### 3) Site-preservation package with differentiated plant roles
**Proposal:**  
Keep all German sites formally open, but redesign their roles:
- some sites become specialized,
- some absorb line consolidation,
- some shift to reduced schedules,
- some receive new product allocation,
- and one or more plants may be converted to alternative functions rather than closed.

This would be paired with a jointly reviewed savings target and an implementation review date.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It preserves the symbolic and social red line of no closure while allowing substantial structural change and capacity reduction.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Which specific savings gap, plant by plant, remains after all non-closure measures are applied, and what evidence shows that this gap cannot be closed without site closures?**

### To the works council
**If an independently verified plant-by-plant model shows that a no-closure package still leaves a material savings gap, what additional non-closure measures would you be willing to consider to keep the restructuring viable?**

### To the industrial union
**Would you accept a time-limited labor contribution if it is tied to binding no-closure guarantees, no compulsory redundancies, and enforceable investment commitments through 2030?**

### To the regional state government
**Are you prepared to condition your support on a verified plant-by-plant comparison that rules out closure only after non-closure alternatives have been fully tested?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you require management to present a decision-grade non-closure scenario before any closure mandate is allowed to advance through governance channels?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum quantified permanent savings and implementation certainty would make you accept a negotiated non-closure restructuring as equivalent to a closure-led plan?**

### To the restructuring experts
**What specific threshold of permanent cost and capacity reduction would you regard as the minimum for a non-closure package to be economically credible?**
