Core impasse identified:

1. **Management / investors / experts** are requiring a restructuring that removes enough **permanent cost and overcapacity** to restore competitiveness by 2030.
2. **Works council / union** are treating **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies** as unacceptable, and will only discuss adjustment if every German plant remains open and no one is forced out.
3. **State government / controlling family** are aligned with the need for restructuring but want to avoid a closure-led default and preserve the regional and social footprint.
4. The discussion is stuck because there is **no agreed, independent equivalence test** showing whether a non-closure package can close the savings gap with comparable certainty.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Verified no-closure test period”
A time-limited agreement to model and attempt a non-closure package first, under independent verification, before any irreversible footprint decisions.

Elements:
- independent plant-by-plant model,
- binding data access,
- clear savings target by site,
- fixed deadline,
- pre-agreed decision trigger if the gap remains unclosed.

This could bridge:
- works council / union’s demand for no premature closures,
- management / investor demand for measurable permanent savings,
- family / state demand for stewardship and procedural legitimacy.

### Proposal 2: “Site preservation with conditional capacity adjustment”
Sites remain formally open, but capacity is reduced through:
- shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- product reassignment,
- internal redeployment,
- retirement pathways,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification measures.

A limited number of sites could be designated as:
- **core production sites**,
- **specialized support sites**, or
- **conversion sites**,
with binding investment and product commitments through 2030.

This could bridge:
- the workforce’s site-preservation demand,
- management’s need for structural downsizing,
- government/family concerns about maintaining a German anchor.

### Proposal 3: “Burden-sharing package with hard guardrails”
Any labor contribution would be paired with equally binding commitments from management/owners/investors.

Possible structure:
- workforce contribution: temporary, time-limited concessions;
- management contribution: overhead cuts, capex prioritization, executive restraint;
- owner/investor contribution: patient capital, governance support, no short-term pressure for cosmetic savings;
- site protection: no compulsory redundancies for a defined period unless the independent model shows unavoidable residual gap after all non-closure measures.

This could bridge:
- union’s insistence on shared sacrifice,
- investors’ demand for durability,
- state/family demands for legitimacy.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
What is the **minimum permanent savings gap** that must be closed for you to accept a non-closure solution, and will you commit to defining that gap **site by site** before any closure decision is tabled?

### To the works council
If an independent model shows that a non-closure package still leaves a material structural gap, would you accept **conditional site specialization or capacity reduction** short of closure, provided no compulsory redundancies occur?

### To the industrial union
Will you agree to a **time-limited labor contribution** if it is part of a binding package that includes measurable permanent savings, investment commitments, and a no-forced-redundancy period?

### To the regional state government
What concrete support are you prepared to offer to make a non-closure restructuring executable — for example, investment guarantees, training support, or political facilitation — if that helps avoid closures?

### To the controlling family
Will you instruct management to treat the independent non-closure scenario as a **genuine decision path**, with no precommitment to closures before the model is complete?

### To institutional investors
If the independent model shows that a negotiated consolidation package reaches the same 2030 margin outcome with lower conflict risk, will you support it even if it is less “clean” than a closure-led solution?

### To the restructuring experts
What exact threshold would you use to judge a non-closure package “equivalent” to closures: permanent savings, utilization, fixed-cost reduction, or implementation certainty — and which one is decisive?
