### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge the company is under structural pressure and that change is necessary.
- There is broad openness to a negotiated solution rather than an imposed one.
- Several stakeholders accept that any viable plan must include:
  - durable cost reduction,
  - capacity adjustment,
  - investment in future products/technology,
  - training/qualification,
  - and some form of shared sacrifice.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, state government, and controlling family are opposed or highly skeptical; management and institutional investors keep closures on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour representatives, state government, and the family reject them; management and investors will not rule them out.
- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  Labour side wants binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff, and investment commitments through 2030; management and investors say such commitments must be conditional on credible cost savings.
- **How to define “credible” restructuring:**  
  Labour side says alternatives must be negotiated and matched by commitments; management and investors say alternatives must be quantified and must deliver comparable structural savings.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness:** how much workforce reduction or capacity change is necessary, and by what means.
- **Short-term pain vs. long-term certainty:** whether labor concessions can be exchanged for enforceable site/job/investment guarantees.
- **Voluntary/indirect measures vs. compulsory measures:** whether reallocation, working-time changes, retirement paths, and qualification can substitute for closures or forced layoffs.
- **Governance and trust:** how co-determination, shareholder influence, and management authority will be balanced in any package.
- **Durability of savings:** whether proposed alternatives are sufficient to restore margins permanently, not just temporarily.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **side-by-side options** with numbers:
  - expected savings,
  - capacity effects,
  - employment impacts,
  - investment commitments,
  - and timeline to 2030.
- Ask management to specify what exactly is required for a non-closure, no-forced-redundancy alternative to be considered equivalent.
- Ask labour representatives to specify which measures they can accept if they are paired with binding guarantees.
- Aim to test whether a **quantified, enforceable compromise package** is possible before any party escalates to irreversible positions.
