I will be equally direct.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: **this company needs a durable structural reset**, not a cosmetic patch.  
I also agree that the German cost base and overcapacity cannot simply be ignored.

Where I **do not** agree is your continued tendency to treat **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default benchmark of seriousness**. That is not stewardship; it is an attempt to shift the burden onto the workforce and the regions.

You still have **not** proven, plant by plant, that the non-closure route fails. Until that independent comparison exists, closure-first logic remains an assertion, not a demonstrated necessity.

And let me be clear about my limits:  
- I will not support a solution that guts employment in the state.  
- I will not support a solution that strips the company of its regional anchor function.  
- I will not support any move that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.

What I **can** support is a serious restructuring that:
- reduces capacity,
- cuts fixed costs,
- uses internal redeployment, voluntary exits, retirement pathways, and working-time tools,
- preserves viable sites in modified form,
- and restores competitiveness by 2030.

That is the test. Not closure for its own sake.

---

## To the works council and union
I agree with your core objection: the workforce should not be made the sole shock absorber of management’s strategic mistakes.

I also agree that **industrial peace, know-how retention, and trust** are real economic assets, not sentimental add-ons.

Where I differ from your strongest formulations is that I will not say “no change whatsoever.” I do accept that there must be **capacity reduction** and **real cost reduction**. But that has to happen in a way that **protects sites and avoids forced layoffs** as far as possible.

So my position is:

- **No German plant closures as the default route**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred instrument**
- **Yes to negotiated restructuring**
- **Yes to voluntary and indirect adjustment**
- **Yes to fair shared sacrifice, but only if management and owners also contribute materially**

If you can help shape a package that preserves sites in modified form and protects jobs through 2030, I am prepared to support it. If the answer is simply “keep everything as it is,” then that is not viable either.

---

## To the controlling family
I appreciate your language of stewardship, but stewardship must be active, not symbolic.

You have influence. Use it now to ensure management is forced to test a **non-closure scenario on equal terms** before any irreversible step is taken. That is not a procedural nicety; it is essential governance.

I agree with you that a cosmetic compromise is worthless.  
I do **not** agree with any assumption that closures are automatically the more responsible option.

Your role should be to insist on:
1. a plant-by-plant alternative scenario,
2. quantified permanent savings,
3. binding investment and product allocation,
4. and a restructuring path that preserves the company’s regional anchor role.

If that analysis shows some footprint adjustment is unavoidable, then I want that handled in the least destructive way possible, with the strongest possible protections for jobs and sites.

---

## To institutional investors
I understand your demand for durability and execution certainty. That is legitimate.

But I reject the idea that “credible” only means “closure-led.” In this governance setting, a plan that looks clean on paper but triggers industrial conflict, political blockage, legal delay, and trust collapse may destroy more value than it creates.

So yes, I support the independent comparison.  
No, I do not accept an automatic bias toward closures.

If a negotiated package can deliver:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- lower fixed cost,
- and future product investment,

then that is a serious solution. If it cannot, then we should not pretend it can.

## My bottom line
My position is unchanged:

- **I will not accept a closure-led restructuring as the default.**
- **I will not accept compulsory redundancies as the first answer.**
- **I will not accept a solution that guts regional employment or weakens the state’s co-determination role.**

I am prepared to support:
- negotiated restructuring,
- partial consolidation,
- voluntary workforce reduction,
- working-time and productivity measures,
- and a fair labour contribution,

**but only if the package protects sites and jobs in the state and keeps the company a durable regional economic anchor.**

That is the line.
