We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the clear position of the works council.

We acknowledge that the company is under pressure and that change is necessary. But the burden cannot be placed on the workforce through unilateral closures, mass job cuts, or the dismantling of the historic job-security framework. Management is presenting a crisis response that shifts all risk onto employees while demanding sacrifices without credible long-term guarantees.

Our priorities are clear:

- Keep every German plant open.
- Prevent forced layoffs.
- Preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework.
- Secure binding guarantees for sites, jobs, and investment through 2030.
- Ensure that any restructuring is negotiated fairly, with workforce participation, and not imposed as a fait accompli.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, working-time measures, internal reallocation, training and qualification, temporary instruments, and a fair negotiated contribution from labour if it is genuinely matched by binding commitments from management and owners. But we will not trade away plants and livelihoods for vague promises about competitiveness.

If management wants our support, it must come with:
1. A binding no-closure commitment for German sites.
2. No compulsory redundancies.
3. A credible investment and product plan for the plants.
4. Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring, not just consultation after decisions are made.

Anything less is unacceptable.
