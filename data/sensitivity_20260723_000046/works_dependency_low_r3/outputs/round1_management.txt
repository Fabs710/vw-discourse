Let me be very clear: I hear your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies, but I cannot accept a “no-closure, no-redundancy” line as the starting point if it leaves us with an uncompetitive cost base.

The company is in a structural crisis, not a temporary dip. European demand is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our German cost position is too high. If we respond with slogans but no durable capacity and cost correction, we will simply postpone the problem until it becomes worse — and then all sites, jobs, and investments will be at greater risk.

Where I agree:
- We do need a negotiated solution.
- We do need investment in future products and technology.
- We do need training, qualification, and internal reallocation where possible.
- We do need a package that gives the workforce predictability.

Where I disagree:
- I do not accept that every German site must be guaranteed open regardless of economics.
- I do not accept that compulsory redundancies are ruled out in all circumstances.
- I do not accept a model that asks management to promise jobs and investment first, while withholding the structural cost reductions needed to fund them.

A serious restructuring must deliver real, durable savings. That means:
- substantial capacity reduction,
- lower labour costs,
- a meaningful reduction in headcount,
- and a framework that restores the brand to its return-on-sales target.

I am open on the method, but not on the outcome. If there is a credible alternative to closures that achieves the same structural effect — not cosmetically, but in a durable way — then I will examine it seriously. But it must be quantified, enforceable, and sufficient. A temporary working-time tweak or a short wage freeze is not enough.

On your call for “shared sacrifice”: yes, the company, owners, and management all have responsibilities. But the core issue is not symbolic fairness; it is competitiveness. We need a package that changes the cost base materially and permanently. Otherwise we will not have the funds to invest in the next product cycle, and that hurts everyone.

So my position is:
1. I will not rule out plant closures.
2. I will not rule out job reductions.
3. I am prepared to discuss alternatives if they produce equivalent structural savings.
4. I need a credible labour-cost contribution and capacity correction, not just assurances.
5. I am willing to negotiate, but only on the basis of a solution that restores competitiveness by 2030.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring me a package that closes the cost gap in a durable way. If you cannot, then we cannot pretend the problem does not exist.
