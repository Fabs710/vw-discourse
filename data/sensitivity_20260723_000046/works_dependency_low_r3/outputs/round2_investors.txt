We agree on the destination, but not yet on the route.

## To management
You are right about the core economic problem: the German cost base is too high, the footprint appears overcapacity, and a durable margin reset is necessary. We will not support a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

Where we disagree is on posture. Repeating “closures may be necessary” is not enough. If you want capital markets to back a disruptive restructuring, you need to show that it is **financially superior and executable**, not just tougher in tone.

What we need from management is:
- quantified savings by lever,
- plant-by-plant economics,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- implementation risk,
- and a credible path to return-on-sales by 2030.

A plan that is aggressive but not executable destroys value just as surely as a weak compromise. We are not here to bless symbolic toughness.

## To the works council and union
We understand your refusal of plant closures and compulsory redundancies. We also accept that prolonged industrial conflict is value-destructive.

But your current line is only defensible if you can prove that the alternatives produce **comparable durable savings**. So far, you have said what you will not accept, but not yet shown how the company gets to a competitive cost base without material footprint and headcount change.

That matters. Investors cannot back a structure that preserves the current cost base and calls it protection. If the result is a no-closure settlement that merely defers the problem, we will not support it.

So: no cosmetic settlement, no indefinite delay, no package that protects every existing structure while margins remain impaired.

## To the regional state government
You are right to stress industrial presence and execution reality. We agree that value destruction from conflict or a botched closure strategy must be counted.

But “protecting sites” is not the same as protecting enterprise value. If the final package keeps all sites open but leaves the footprint inefficient and expensive, the market will continue to punish the equity.

We can support site preservation only if it is paired with:
- real capacity reduction,
- real fixed-cost removal,
- and a credible margin recovery path.

## To the controlling family
Your stewardship argument is credible only if it allows hard decisions when necessary. We agree that open conflict and permanent franchise damage must be avoided.

But stewardship also means not allowing a political compromise to dilute the restructuring into irrelevance. If the business needs a hard reset on cost and capacity, then the owners must support a package that actually changes the economics.

## Our position
We are open to either of these:
1. **A closure-led restructuring**, if that is genuinely the only route to durable competitiveness; or
2. **A negotiated alternative**, but only if it achieves equivalent structural savings and a credible 2030 margin recovery.

What we will not back:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- temporary labor concessions without structural change,
- or a deal that leaves the company with an uncompetitive German base.
