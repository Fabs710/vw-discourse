We share the core line: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

That is not a rhetorical position; it is the necessary starting point. A restructuring that destroys sites and throws workers out while asking the workforce alone to pay for past miscalculations is not a fair turnaround plan. It would also undermine trust for years and make any “new beginning” far harder to sustain.

At the same time, I want to be precise: we are **not** denying that the company needs adjustment. The market is under pressure, costs are high, and the transition is real. We have already signalled that we are willing to talk about:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal reallocation,
- qualification and retraining,
- temporary wage-related contributions or the suspension of bonuses,
- and other negotiated measures that help restore competitiveness.

But those contributions must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**, not a one-sided demand that workers absorb the pain while management and owners remain untouched.

So my position is:

1. **No closures.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective bargaining framework.**
4. **Any savings must be matched by binding commitments** on jobs, sites, investment, and future products.
5. **Management and owners must contribute visibly**—through guarantees, capital commitments, and real structural responsibility.

If management insists that the only path is closure and forced cuts, then it is choosing confrontation over a negotiated solution. We are prepared for pressure, but we will not accept that the workforce becomes the shock absorber for every strategic failure. If there is a serious proposal that protects sites and employment while securing competitiveness, we are ready to negotiate it. If not, we will defend our members accordingly.
