The regional state government takes this discussion very seriously, and I want to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys German sites, tears up co-determination, or turns the workforce into the sole absorber of management’s strategic failures.**

I do agree with one central point from management: the company does need a credible path back to competitiveness. Pretending otherwise would be irresponsible. The cost gap, market pressure, and transition to electric mobility are real. But the conclusion cannot simply be “therefore close plants and cut jobs.” That would be a short-term accounting fix with long-term damage to the company’s industrial base and to the region.

Where I stand:

- **I agree** that structural change is necessary.
- **I agree** that there must be a durable improvement in cost and capacity.
- **I agree** that any solution has to be enforceable and not merely symbolic.

But I **disagree fundamentally** with the idea that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or acceptable route. As a shareholder and a public actor, I cannot accept a plan that hollows out regional employment and undermines the company’s role as an industrial anchor. We also cannot accept a process in which board-level safeguards and the state’s co-determination role are sidelined.

What I can support is a serious negotiated restructuring that:
1. **Keeps German plants open**, even if capacity is reduced.
2. **Avoids compulsory redundancies** through internal reallocation, qualification, retirement pathways, working-time instruments, and agreed voluntary measures.
3. **Includes a fair contribution from labour**, but only as part of a balanced package.
4. **Includes visible contributions from management and owners** — not just demands on employees.
5. **Secures binding investment commitments** for future products and technologies in Germany.

If management says that only closures can deliver the necessary savings, I would ask: has every alternative been exhausted, and has it been quantified honestly? Because “alternative measures” are only dismissible if they are actually shown to be insufficient. I am not prepared to sign off on irreversible damage before that test is met.

So my message is: bring us a restructuring that protects sites, jobs, and the company’s future. If there is a serious package with durable savings, I am ready to work on it. If the plan remains closure-led and one-sided, then I will oppose it.
