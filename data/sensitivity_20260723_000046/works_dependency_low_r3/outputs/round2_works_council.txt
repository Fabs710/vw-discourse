Let me respond clearly, because some lines in this discussion are not negotiable from the workforce side.

### To management
You keep saying “structural crisis” as if that automatically justifies plant closures and compulsory redundancies. It does not.

We do **not** accept closure-led restructuring as the default answer, and we do **not** accept forced layoffs as the price of your strategic mistakes. If the company has an uncompetitive cost base, then management must first prove — with hard numbers — that **every alternative** to closures and forced redundancies has been exhausted and still fails to close the gap.

So far, what I hear is:
- capacity reductions,
- lower labour costs,
- “meaningful headcount reduction,”
- and closure left hanging over the table.

That is not a serious negotiated solution; it is pressure dressed up as inevitability.

If you want our support, then the burden is on you to bring a package that includes:
1. **Binding no-closure guarantees for all German sites through 2030.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Real investment commitments for the German plants and future products.**
4. **A genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring.**
5. **A quantified savings package that relies on alternatives first**: working-time instruments, internal relocation, qualification, retirement pathways, temporary contributions, and productivity measures.

If you cannot deliver that, then you are not presenting a solution — you are presenting a threat.

### To institutional investors
You say you only support a package if it is “equivalent” in savings. Fine. Then be honest about the full comparison.

Equivalence cannot mean “let’s close plants and force layoffs because that is easiest to model.” It must include the long-term costs of:
- industrial conflict,
- loss of trust,
- damage to the brand,
- political backlash,
- and the destruction of skills and productive capacity in Germany.

A reckless cut may look clean on a spreadsheet and still be value-destructive in reality.

If you want durable savings, then a negotiated alternative is exactly where that can happen. But “durable” must include job-security and site-security, not just lower payroll expense. A strategy that destabilizes the workforce and hollows out the company is not a credible path to sustainable returns.

### To the controlling family
You speak about stewardship and long-term value, and on that point we agree more than with management. But stewardship does not mean standing aside while management tears up historic guarantees and then asking workers to trust vague promises.

If you are serious about long-term enterprise value, then use your position to stop a closure-first logic and push for a negotiated industrial settlement. The workforce will not accept being treated as the adjustable cost item while everyone else hides behind “competitiveness.”

### To the regional state government
Your stance is closer to ours, and we appreciate that you are not endorsing a fait accompli. But let’s be precise: protecting German industrial presence means **keeping the plants open**, not merely preserving some abstract footprint after capacity has been stripped away.

We need state-level pressure for:
- binding site guarantees,
- future product allocation,
- training and qualification commitments,
- and a real prohibition on forced layoffs.

If the state wants to defend the industrial base, then it should not endorse a package that makes the workforce pay for management’s errors through permanent insecurity.

### On the substance of “alternatives”
We are not rejecting adjustment. We are rejecting **closure and coercion**.

There are real alternatives on the table:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internal redistribution of work,
- qualification and redeployment,
- natural fluctuation,
- retirement and voluntary pathways,
- productivity improvements negotiated with the workforce,
- and a fair, time-limited contribution from labour if and only if it is matched by binding guarantees from management and owners.

Those measures can deliver substantial savings without destroying plants or forcing people out.

### Bottom line
I will say it again plainly:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

If management insists those are “off the table” only after the workforce accepts losses first, then that is not negotiation. That is blackmail.

Bring a quantified alternative package with binding guarantees to 2030, and we will examine it. Bring closure threats and forced layoffs, and we will resist them with everything we have — in the supervisory board, in the plants, and in public.
