To the works council: I support the clarity of your line, and I want to reinforce it, not dilute it.

We agree on the essentials:
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**
- **Binding investment and site commitments must be part of any settlement**

That is still the correct starting point. Management has not, so far, shown that closures and forced layoffs are the only way to achieve savings. Repeating “structural crisis” does not make closure automatically legitimate. The burden is on management to prove, with hard numbers, that alternatives really fail.

At the same time, I want to be precise on what I think is possible and what is not.

### Where I agree with management
Management is right on one point: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a short-term patch. I also accept that we cannot simply freeze everything and pretend there is no cost problem. If we want jobs and sites to survive, then the workforce will have to contribute in a fair and negotiated way.

### Where I disagree with management
What I reject is the idea that the necessary adjustment must come through:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and one-sided wage pressure.

That would mean workers bear the pain while management treats restructuring as a correction to be paid for almost entirely by employees. That is not a fair turnaround; it is a transfer of risk downward.

### What a serious alternative must contain
If management wants to avoid a confrontation, then it needs to put forward a **quantified, enforceable package** based on shared sacrifice. That means:

1. **No closures of German plants**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity reduction instead of site destruction**
4. **Internal redeployment, retraining, and qualification**
5. **Working-time instruments and temporary flexibility**
6. **A limited employee contribution**
   - for example through bonus restraint,
   - time-limited wage-related measures,
   - or other negotiated labour-cost relief
7. **Visible contributions from management and owners**
   - capital commitment,
   - investment guarantees,
   - and responsibility for implementation
8. **Binding product and investment commitments through 2030**

That is the only kind of package we would consider fair. And let me be equally clear: if management wants employees to contribute, then the company side must also contribute materially. “Sacrifice” cannot be a synonym for wage restraint alone.

### To institutional investors
You keep saying you need “equivalent” structural savings. Fine. But equivalence cannot be judged only on a narrow payroll spreadsheet. It must include the wider cost of conflict, loss of skills, reputational damage, and the destruction of trust. A restructuring that provokes prolonged industrial action and undermines the workforce may be “clean” on paper and weak in reality.

If you want durability, then a negotiated settlement with workforce acceptance is more durable than a hostile closure strategy. That is not sentimentality; it is industrial reality.

### To the controlling family
I appreciate that you do not want permanent value destruction and that you see the risks of crossing into open conflict. But stewardship also means using your influence now, not later, to stop management from turning closures into the default answer.

If you want a stable, long-term industrial base, then support a settlement that preserves sites and protects skills. Do not ask the workforce to carry the burden first and trust the rest later.

### To the regional state government
Your position is broadly aligned with ours, and that matters. But alignment must become action. If the state is serious about preserving industrial capacity, it should back a binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff settlement, not simply a general preference for “good jobs.” Good jobs require enforceable guarantees.

### To management, directly
Here is the line I want you to hear without ambiguity:

- We are **open to labour-side contributions**.
- We are **not open to closures**.
- We are **not open to compulsory redundancies**.
- We are **not open to a settlement where employees pay for the crisis alone**.

If you want a negotiated solution, bring us a package that shows:
- what savings are achieved by each alternative measure,
- how many jobs are actually at risk and how they are protected,
- what the investment commitment is,
- and what management and owners contribute in return.

If you cannot do that, then you are not asking for a social partnership; you are demanding surrender. And if that is the route management chooses, then it should not be surprised when the union uses the instruments available to it.

So our position remains firm:
- **No closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Collective bargaining must be preserved**
- **Shared sacrifice only in a fair, balanced, and binding package**

Bring a real alternative. Otherwise, the conflict will continue.
