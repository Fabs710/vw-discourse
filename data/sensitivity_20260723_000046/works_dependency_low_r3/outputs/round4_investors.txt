We are aligned on the **goal** but not yet on the **means**.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **durable structural reset**.
- A **cosmetic compromise** that leaves the German cost base uncompetitive would destroy value.
- The decision must be based on a **plant-by-plant, decision-grade comparison**, not slogans.
- Industrial conflict has a real cost and must be priced in.

## Where we disagree
### To the works council and union
I understand your red lines, but from a shareholder perspective I cannot support a package that effectively says:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and no meaningful footprint change,

**if that leaves the cost base structurally impaired**.

That is not a solution; it is a delay. Delays matter because they keep eroding margins and equity value. If the alternative measures truly deliver the same permanent savings, then fine — but that has not been demonstrated yet.

### To the regional state government and controlling family
I appreciate the stewardship language, but stewardship also means not preserving an uncompetitive structure out of political caution. A “balanced” deal that is too weak to restore margins is not balanced for shareholders; it is value leakage.

### To management
I support your insistence on a durable reset, but I also think you need to be more disciplined in how you make the case. Repeating that closures are possible is not enough. You need to show, clearly and plant by plant:
- current utilization,
- unit cost,
- fixed-cost base,
- savings by lever,
- one-time costs,
- execution risk,
- and 2030 margin impact.

Without that, investors cannot judge whether the non-closure route is genuinely inferior or simply less preferred.

## Our position
We are open to either:
1. **A closure-led restructuring**, if that is truly necessary to restore competitiveness; or
2. **A negotiated consolidation package**, but only if it is demonstrably equivalent in permanent savings, capacity reduction, and margin recovery.

What we will **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- temporary labor concessions without structural change,
- or a deal that leaves the German base uncompetitive.

## What needs to happen next
Before anyone locks in red lines, we need the independent comparison that answers whether the non-closure route can actually close the gap.

Until then, our vote with capital remains conditional: **we support restructuring, but only if it is real enough to restore competitive margins.**
