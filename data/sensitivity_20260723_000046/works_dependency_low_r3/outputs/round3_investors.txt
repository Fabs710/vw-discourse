We agree on the destination: **the company must return to competitive margins, and the German cost base must come down in a durable way**. On that point, management is right and the latest expert input is directionally right too.

Where we disagree is on the route and the rhetoric.

## To management
Your insistence on a **structural reset** is correct. We are not interested in a cosmetic compromise that preserves an uncompetitive footprint and merely postpones the problem. If the German operations remain structurally too expensive, equity value will keep eroding.

But you still have not done enough to justify the strongest instruments as the default. Repeating that closures or compulsory redundancies are “not ruled out” is not the same as proving necessity. What capital markets need is:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- quantified savings by lever,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- implementation risk,
- and a credible path to return-on-sales by 2030.

If the non-closure route truly fails to close the gap, say so with numbers. If it does not fail, then do not burn value through unnecessary escalation.

## To the works council and union
We understand your red lines, but let’s be clear: **“No closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not, by itself, a financial solution**. We will not support a package that preserves the current cost base and calls that protection.

That said, you are right about one important thing: a closure-led plan that triggers prolonged strike action, political blockage, and loss of know-how can destroy value too. We are not blind to execution risk. For investors, the best plan is not the harshest plan on paper; it is the one that actually delivers durable savings.

So if you can show a **quantified, enforceable non-closure package** that really removes capacity and fixed cost, we would take it seriously. But it must be more than voluntary exits and soft promises.

## To the regional state government and controlling family
Your emphasis on industrial stability and stewardship is sensible, but it cannot become a veto on real restructuring. Protecting every site at all costs is not stewardship if it leaves the business structurally uncompetitive.

We would support a negotiated outcome only if it includes:
- real capacity reduction,
- permanent cost removal,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- and a credible margin recovery path.

## Our position
We are open to either:
1. **A closure-led restructuring**, if that is genuinely the only way to restore competitiveness; or
2. **A negotiated consolidation package**, but only if it is demonstrably equivalent in structural savings and durability.

What we will not back:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- temporary labor concessions without structural change,
- or a deal that leaves capital trapped in an uncompetitive German base.
