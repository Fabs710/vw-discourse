### Neutral moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that the status quo is not acceptable and that a **real restructuring/turnaround** is needed.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **durable, measurable, and economically credible**.
- There is also agreement that **open conflict would be value-destructive** and that a negotiated solution is preferable.
- Several parties accept, in principle, that a package may include:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary measures,
  - redeployment and training,
  - natural attrition,
  - temporary employee contributions,
  - management restraint,
  - owner/capital contribution,
  - and investment commitments tied to German operations.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works council, union, and regional government reject closures as a starting point; management, family owners, and investors keep closures on the table if needed for durable savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and regional government reject them; management does not rule them out if required.
- **Job and site guarantees to 2030:**  
  Employee side wants binding guarantees; management and owners say this is only possible if the economics are sufficiently improved.
- **Evidence standard:**  
  Employee side and state demand proof that less destructive options were fully exhausted; management/owners/investors argue that closure-free alternatives must be shown to achieve equivalent, permanent savings.
- **Burden sharing:**  
  All sides say contributions should be shared, but they disagree on whether workers are being asked to carry too much and whether capital/management contributions are sufficiently defined.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings each measure contributes** has not been quantified in a way all sides accept.
- There is no shared answer yet on the trade-off between:
  - preserving all sites/jobs, and
  - achieving the level of permanent cost reduction management says is needed.
- The parties have not settled:
  - what counts as a “closure-free” package,
  - how permanent any employee contribution may be,
  - what management/owner contributions are concrete and binding,
  - and what level of headcount or capacity reduction can occur without closures.
- Governance trade-offs remain unresolved:
  - how co-determination, state influence, and shareholder control should operate during implementation,
  - and what binding role, if any, the works council and regional government should have in the turnaround plan.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **side-by-side numbers**:
  1. closure / deep-cut scenario,
  2. closure-free alternative,
  3. cost, timing, execution risk, and headcount impact of each.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - the **minimum acceptable savings target**,
  - which measures are **temporary vs. permanent**,
  - which contributions are **binding**,
  - and what guarantees they are prepared to offer if closures are avoided.
- Keep the discussion focused on one central question:
  **Can a closure-free, no-compulsory-redundancy package credibly deliver the required durable savings?**
