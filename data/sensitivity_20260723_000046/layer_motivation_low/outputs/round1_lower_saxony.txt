I appreciate the clarity from both the works council and the union, and I also note management’s insistence that the company needs a real structural turnaround.

Let me state the regional government’s position just as clearly:

**We will not support a restructuring that destroys German sites, triggers mass compulsory redundancies, or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.** That is not a negotiable starting point for us. This company is a regional economic anchor, and our responsibility is not only to the balance sheet but to jobs, industrial capability, and long-term stability in the state.

That said, I do **not** dismiss the seriousness of the cost problem. I accept that the company needs durable competitiveness, not slogans. If management can demonstrate a credible restructuring that preserves the German footprint, protects a large share of jobs, and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice, then we are prepared to engage constructively.

Where I agree with management:
- The status quo is not sustainable.
- The company needs measurable, durable savings.
- A pure delay strategy would be irresponsible.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures are not the first or preferred answer.**
- **Forced layoffs are not acceptable as a default tool.**
- **Any plan that treats the regional state as a bystander is unacceptable.**

Management is right that numbers matter. So let us talk about numbers, but also about the form of adjustment. Before any closure is put forward as necessary, I would expect the company to exhaust alternatives that are less destructive and more reversible:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- training and qualification,
- natural attrition,
- and a serious investment commitment tied to German production.

To the works council and the union: I support your core insistence that the burden must be shared and that German sites cannot be treated as disposable. But I also want to be candid: **blanket “no change” guarantees are unlikely to be enough if they do not come with a credible economic package.** If we want to defend sites and jobs, we have to help shape a solution that management cannot simply dismiss as insufficient.

What I could support, in principle, is a negotiated package with these elements:
1. **No plant closures during a defined restructuring period**, unless there is truly no alternative and the governance bodies agree.
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the first resort**, with attrition and redeployment used first.
3. **A temporary, proportionate employee contribution** if and only if it is matched by management restraint and owner contribution.
4. **Binding investment and product commitments for German sites.**
5. **A joint review mechanism** with the supervisory board and co-determination stakeholders to monitor implementation.

But I am equally clear on one point: any deal that tries to push through closures or mass layoffs while cutting the state out of the decision will face our opposition. The special rights attached to our stake are there for a reason, and we will use them to protect the regional interest.

So my question back to management is straightforward:
**What is the smallest credible restructuring package that achieves the required savings without closing sites and without breaking the social partnership?**
If you have such a package, put it on the table. If not, then you are asking the workforce and the region to absorb disproportionate damage for a plan that has not yet earned support.
