We agree on the diagnosis that the status quo is not acceptable. The company needs a durable turnaround, not symbolism.

But I need to be equally direct: **I do not support a restructuring that destroys value through open conflict, reputational damage, or a governance breakdown.** Nor do I support a “no change” posture that simply preserves every current structure and postpones the problem. Both extremes are harmful to the family’s long-term ownership interests.

Where I stand:

- **I support restoring competitiveness and margins.**
- **I support a serious, structural solution, not a cosmetic one.**
- **I do not support permanent impairment of the enterprise through uncontrolled industrial conflict.**
- **I do not support any outcome that weakens ownership control or makes the company ungovernable.**

To management: your core point is valid — the cost base must come down, and the company needs measurable, durable savings. But you will not get a stable outcome by framing plant closures as the default answer and expecting the other stakeholders to absorb it. If you push too hard into confrontation, you risk destroying value faster than you create it. I need a plan that is economically credible **and** governable.

To the works council and the union: I hear the red lines on forced redundancies and broad site destruction. I also hear the demand for shared sacrifice. That is the right starting instinct. But I will not endorse a package that preserves all present structures and delivers only temporary relief. If the company truly needs durable savings, then the employee contribution cannot be purely symbolic.

To the regional government: I understand your concern for jobs and the state’s industrial base. That concern is legitimate. But a solution that simply blocks adjustment is not enough. The company needs enough flexibility to remain competitive, or the jobs you want to protect will be at even greater risk later.

So my position is this:

1. **A negotiated restructuring is preferable to a forced showdown.**
2. **Any solution must produce real, durable cost and capacity relief.**
3. **We should avoid permanent, value-destructive plant closure conflict if a credible alternative exists.**
4. **But if alternatives do not deliver sufficient economics, then the burden cannot be shifted entirely onto owners or management.**

I would therefore support exploring an alternative package that meets all of these tests:
- credible and quantified savings,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- substantial but socially managed workforce adjustment,
- temporary employee contribution,
- investment commitments for future products,
- and a clear governance structure that preserves stability and board support.

What I will **not** support is an outcome where:
- the company keeps the same cost base,
- the turnaround is deferred,
- and owners are expected to underwrite permanent underperformance.

So the practical question is not whether adjustment happens. It is **whether the proposed alternative is strong enough to restore competitiveness without triggering avoidable closure conflict**.

If management can show that, I am prepared to back it through governance. If not, then the discussion remains incomplete.
