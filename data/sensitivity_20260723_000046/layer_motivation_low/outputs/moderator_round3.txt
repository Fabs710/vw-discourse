Core impasse:

1. **Economic sufficiency vs. site preservation**
   - Management, the controlling family, and investors are asking whether a closure-free package can deliver **permanent, measurable competitiveness gains**.
   - Works council, union, and regional government are insisting that **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are unacceptable**.
   - The CFO has sharpened the issue: the disagreement is not about whether restructuring is needed, but whether **no-closure measures can reduce fixed costs, capacity, and labor cost enough at steady state**.

2. **Burden of proof**
   - Management says closure-free ideas are insufficient unless proven in numbers.
   - The employee side says closures are only a preference unless management proves they are unavoidable.
   - So the dispute is now less about principles and more about **who must substantiate which scenario and with what data**.

3. **Trust and governance**
   - The workforce wants binding guarantees and co-determination.
   - Management and capital want flexibility and execution certainty.
   - The state and family are positioned between preserving industrial capacity and ensuring economic viability.

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the conflict

### Proposal 1: Two-track settlement with a built-in review gate
A package could be structured as:
- **Track A:** immediate temporary employee contributions, management restraint, overhead cuts, and voluntary measures.
- **Track B:** a pre-agreed conditional review after a fixed period, with quantified triggers for escalation if savings targets are missed.

This could bridge:
- workers’ demand for avoiding immediate closures,
- management’s demand for credible savings,
- and investors’ demand for enforceable performance tests.

### Proposal 2: Closure-free package with hard structural equivalents
A no-closure settlement could be made more credible if it contains:
- real capacity reduction through line or shift removals,
- meaningful headcount reduction via attrition and voluntary exits,
- permanent overhead simplification,
- and binding investment commitments tied to productivity gains.

This would not remove the conflict, but it could align the employee side’s “no closure” demand with management’s “durable savings” requirement.

### Proposal 3: Deferred site decision with binding anti-layoff safeguards
The parties could agree to:
- suspend closure decisions for a defined period,
- prohibit compulsory redundancies during that period,
- and require a jointly audited financial test to determine whether the no-closure path is sufficient.

This may create time for trust-building, while preserving the option to revisit plant decisions if the economics fail.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Will you commit to presenting, within a fixed deadline, a fully quantified side-by-side comparison of the closure and closure-free options, including permanent savings, one-time costs, and headcount impact?**

### To the works council
**If a closure-free package produces verified permanent savings, capacity reduction, and headcount reduction without compulsory redundancies, would you be prepared to negotiate it as a serious alternative?**

### To the union
**What specific temporary employee contributions would you accept, and under what measurable employer-side commitments, so that the burden is demonstrably shared?**

### To the regional government
**Would you use your governance position to block any proposal involving closures or compulsory redundancies before the no-closure alternative has been fully tested and audited?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you back a restructuring that preserves sites if, and only if, the numbers show it is economically sufficient, even if that means supporting a slower adjustment path?**

### To institutional investors
**If the closure-free scenario meets the steady-state EBIT target with credible execution safeguards, will you support it even if it preserves more sites and takes longer to realize?**
