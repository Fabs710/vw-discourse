I hear the clear red lines from the works council and the union, and I will respond just as clearly.

First: **I agree that this company needs a real turnaround, not a cosmetic one.** We are not debating whether the status quo is acceptable — it is not. German factory costs are structurally too high, our European market is shrinking, and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target. If we do not restore competitiveness now, we will weaken the company’s future ability to invest in product, technology, and jobs.

Where I **disagree** is on the idea that we can simply preserve all current structures and still solve the crisis. **That is not credible.** If we keep every site, every capacity level, and the current cost base intact, then we do not solve the problem — we postpone it and make it worse. A deal that leaves us uncompetitive is not a real deal.

So let me be explicit:

- **I am not prepared to accept a purely symbolic package.**
- **I am not prepared to maintain overcapacity at current cost levels.**
- **I am not prepared to rule out all structural measures, including closures, if that is what it takes to achieve durable savings.**

That said, I am not here to dictate terms in a vacuum. I know the governance constraints. I know that any plant-related decision needs the necessary board majority, and I know we need a negotiated settlement to make the restructuring workable. That is exactly why I am saying this openly now.

On the substance, there is room for negotiation **only if it produces real, permanent cost relief**. That means a package with all of the following elements:

1. **A measurable and durable reduction in capacity**
   - Not just temporary underutilisation.
   - Not just internal reshuffling.
   - Real structural adjustment to the production footprint.

2. **A genuine labour-cost contribution**
   - Wage moderation, reductions in allowances, or other permanent cost measures.
   - I am open on the instrument, but not on the principle.

3. **A credible path to job reduction**
   - Preferably through attrition, redeployment, and socially managed measures where possible.
   - But I will not rule out compulsory measures if the numbers do not add up.

4. **A clear investment and product commitment**
   - The savings must fund future platforms, EV competitiveness, and technology through 2030.
   - Cutting costs without reinvestment is also unacceptable.

To the works council: I understand your demand for site and job guarantees, but **blanket guarantees through 2030 are not compatible with a structural turnaround unless the cost burden is shifted enough to restore competitiveness**. If you want stronger employment security, then the package must deliver stronger and more durable cost relief. Those two things are linked.

To the union: I hear your point that the burden must be shared and collective standards should not be casually dismantled. But I will be equally direct: **the company cannot carry above-target German cost structures and still compete against lower-cost rivals.** If the collective framework prevents meaningful adjustment, then the framework itself becomes part of the crisis.

So my position is this:

- **Yes** to a negotiated solution.
- **Yes** to alternatives where they deliver the same structural effect.
- **No** to a deal that preserves the current cost and capacity problem.
- **No** to a package that asks management and owners to carry the burden alone.
- **No** to an outcome that delays hard choices until the business is forced into a worse crisis.

If the employee side wants to avoid plant closures, then it needs to bring forward an alternative that is **at least as strong economically** as the closure path. I am willing to examine it seriously — but it must be real, durable, and measurable.

So I would ask both of you to move from principles to numbers:  
**How much cost reduction, in what form, by when?**  
Because that is the test this company has to pass.
