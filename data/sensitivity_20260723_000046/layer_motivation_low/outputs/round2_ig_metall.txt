I’ll be direct.

I agree with the works council on the core red lines: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of job security as the price of management’s turnaround failure**. And I agree with the regional government that this cannot be treated as a pure management diktat. But I do **not** agree that the answer is simply to freeze the company in place and hope the numbers improve. They won’t.

### To management
You keep saying closures and compulsory redundancies may be necessary, but you still have not demonstrated that you have exhausted the less destructive options. That matters. A credible restructuring has to prove:
- how much cost reduction is needed,
- which portion comes from productivity and capacity adjustment,
- which portion comes from management restraint and owner contribution,
- and only then what remains to be solved.

Right now, your argument sounds like: “we prefer the hardest cut, so accept it.” That is not a case for closures; it is a preference for shifting the burden onto workers. We will not accept that.

What I **can** discuss is a package with real structural effect, provided it protects jobs and the wage framework:
- shorter working time or flexible working-time corridors,
- temporary suspension or reduction of bonuses,
- limited and negotiated wage restraint,
- voluntary instruments and internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- natural attrition,
- temporary capacity reductions,
- and a binding investment commitment for German sites.

But I need to be very clear: **a permanent wage-cutting regime is not on the table**. We will not let this crisis become a precedent for eroding collective bargaining across the group.

### To the works council
Your insistence on site security is correct. But if you want a durable deal, it has to be one management can actually sign and the board can actually carry. That means we need a quantified alternative, not just a principle statement.

If the closure-free package can genuinely deliver the required savings, then I will back it hard. But it has to be credible on the numbers:
- how much savings from labour-cost moderation,
- how much from capacity reduction without closures,
- how much from operational changes,
- how much from owner and management contributions.

I’m willing to defend a serious shared-sacrifice package. I am **not** willing to defend vague assurances that leave the company exposed and the workforce vulnerable later.

### To the regional government
Your veto power is real, and you’re right to use it to prevent a socially destructive outcome. But that also means you should not let management hide behind the threat of closures to extract one-sided concessions. If the state wants a stable industrial base, then it should support a settlement that keeps production and jobs in Germany while forcing everyone — management, owners, and workforce — to contribute.

### To the family and investors
You are right about one thing: open conflict destroys value. That is exactly why the burden cannot be loaded onto workers alone. If you want stability, then you need to support a package that protects the company’s industrial core, not a short-term balance-sheet fix that poisons labour relations for years.

### Our position
The union is prepared to negotiate a **time-limited, conditional contribution** by employees, but only under strict conditions:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures in Germany**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees**
4. **Collective-bargaining framework preserved**
5. **Temporary employee contribution only**
6. **Management restraint and owner contribution**
7. **Investment and product commitments tied to Germany**
8. **Capacity reduction without closure**

If these conditions are met, we can discuss:
- deferred compensation,
- reduced bonuses,
- moderate wage restraint for a limited period,
- working-time flexibility,
- and voluntary measures to reduce headcount over time.

If they are not met, then there is no shared sacrifice — then there is just employee sacrifice. And that is unacceptable.

### The real test
So I will put the question back to management, the family, and the investors:

**Are you prepared to finance a closure-free restructuring with real contributions from capital and management, or are you insisting that workers alone pay for the turnaround?**

If it is the first, we can negotiate. If it is the second, then you should expect industrial resistance.
