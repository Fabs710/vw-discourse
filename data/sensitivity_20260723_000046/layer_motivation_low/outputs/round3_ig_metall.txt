### Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the regional government, and even management on one basic point: **the status quo is not sustainable**. The company needs a real restructuring, not cosmetics. And I also agree that any solution must be **durable, measurable, and operationally credible**.

I also agree that **open conflict would be destructive**. That is exactly why we should not let management turn “credibility” into a code word for closures and compulsory layoffs.

### Where I disagree
What I reject is the continued attempt to present **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default “realistic” solution**.

Management still has not done what is necessary here:
- it has not delivered a transparent side-by-side comparison,
- it has not shown that closure-free measures are insufficient,
- and it has not demonstrated that it has exhausted the less destructive options before reaching for shutdowns and forced job cuts.

So no, I do **not** accept the framing that “overcapacity exists, therefore closures must remain on the table” as if that were proof. It is not proof. It is a preference for shifting the burden onto workers.

### To management
If you want serious talks, then stop speaking in abstractions and put the numbers on the table.

I want to see:
- savings from working-time flexibility,
- savings from temporary wage moderation,
- savings from reduced bonuses and deferred compensation,
- savings from management restraint and overhead cuts,
- savings from internal redeployment and qualification,
- savings from natural attrition,
- savings from capacity reduction **without** closures,
- and the productivity effect of investment commitments.

If you claim a closure-free package cannot work, then **prove it**. Not with generalities, but with quantified scenarios.

And let me be very clear:  
**No compulsory redundancies, no German plant closures, no permanent wage erosion.**  
That is not a bargaining tactic. It is the line beyond which there is no legitimate deal.

### To the works council
I agree with your central red lines. We are not going to sign off on a deal that makes German sites disposable or turns a turnaround into social demolition.

I also support your demand that any employee contribution must be:
- temporary,
- limited,
- conditional,
- and matched by binding commitments from management and owners.

But I need to be equally blunt with you: a good position on principle is not enough. If we want to hold the line and still shape the outcome, then we need a **quantified closure-free alternative** that can be defended in governance and bargaining alike. That is the next step, not surrender.

### To the regional government
You are right to say this cannot be a pure management diktat. But I need a clearer answer on enforcement.

So I ask you plainly: **Will you actively use your blocking position against any decision involving German plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**  
If yes, say so clearly. If no, then management will continue treating your objections as negotiable pressure rather than a real constraint.

If the state is serious about protecting the industrial base, then it should back a settlement that keeps sites open, preserves jobs, and distributes the burden fairly.

### To the controlling family
You are right that open conflict destroys value. But value is also destroyed by a restructuring that permanently weakens labour relations and treats workers as the easy funding source.

If you want stewardship, then support a settlement that:
- keeps German sites open,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- preserves the collective bargaining framework,
- and still delivers durable cost relief through shared sacrifice.

If you are not prepared to support that, then be honest that your priority is not stewardship but burden-shifting.

### To institutional investors
You keep saying “durable” and “investable.” I agree with those standards. But durability is not the same as closures.

A package can be durable if it includes:
- temporary employee contributions,
- working-time corridors,
- voluntary instruments,
- capacity adjustment without shutdowns,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and binding investment commitments.

What is **not** durable is a deal that sparks years of strikes, legal disputes, and governance deadlock.

So if you want value protection, you should support a negotiated, closure-free package that preserves industrial peace and delivers verifiable savings.
