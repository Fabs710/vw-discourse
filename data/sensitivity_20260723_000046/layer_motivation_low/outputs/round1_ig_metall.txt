We share the core diagnosis that the company needs a real turnaround, not cosmetic measures. But I need to be equally clear: **any “solution” built on plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or the one-sided destruction of collective wage standards is a non-starter for us.**

Management may see structural overcapacity and high costs. We do not deny the pressure. But **we will not allow the workforce to carry the full burden** while owners, top management, and capital markets are insulated. If the company wants a durable settlement, then the sacrifice must be shared and the future in Germany must be secured.

Where we align with the works council:
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **Binding site and job guarantees.**
- **Alternative capacity measures instead of shutting plants.**
- **Investment commitments for future products and technologies.**

Where we are especially firm:
- The **collective bargaining framework must remain intact**. We will not accept a precedent that permanently undermines wage standards across the workforce.
- Any employee contribution must be **temporary, proportionate, and conditional** on hard guarantees.
- We will not sign up to an open-ended “transformation” that creates permanent insecurity.

What we can discuss, in principle, if the company is serious:
- A negotiated labour-cost contribution for a limited period.
- Working-time adjustments.
- Reduced bonuses or deferred compensation.
- Internal redeployment, training, and natural attrition.
- Capacity reductions that do **not** mean closure or forced dismissals.
- A credible investment and production commitment for German sites.

But this only makes sense if management and the owners also contribute materially. That means:
- Real management restraint and accountability.
- A fair contribution from shareholders/owners.
- A binding commitment that there will be **no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures** as part of the deal.

So my message is simple: **we are ready to bargain hard on a shared-sacrifice package, but not on a surrender package.** If management insists on closures or mass layoffs, it will face industrial resistance. If it comes with a balanced, reversible, and job-protecting plan, then we can negotiate seriously.
