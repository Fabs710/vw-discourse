We share the diagnosis that the status quo is not acceptable, and I will not waste time pretending otherwise.

Where I agree with the other stakeholders:
- The company needs a **real turnaround**, not a cosmetic one.
- **Open conflict would destroy value** and damage the brand, the balance sheet, and the company’s ability to invest.
- Any solution must be **durable, measurable, and credible**.

Where I disagree — and this is important:
- I do **not** support a settlement that simply preserves the current cost structure and calls that stability.
- I do **not** support a package that protects every existing structure while leaving the company uncompetitive.
- I do **not** support an outcome where the burden falls only on management and owners while the operating economics remain unchanged.

### To the works council
I understand your red lines on job security and site closures. I also understand why you want binding guarantees. But I cannot endorse a position that says, in effect, “all sites remain, all jobs remain, and the company must somehow absorb the structural gap.” That is not a credible stewardship position.

If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must deliver **equivalent, structural savings**. Not intentions, not goodwill, not temporary optics. I need to see:
- how much capacity is removed,
- how much cost comes out permanently,
- what headcount reduction is achieved,
- and what that means for competitiveness.

If that can be done without closures, then it is worth serious support. If it cannot, then insisting on “no closures” as a principle becomes a constraint on the company’s future.

### To the union
I take your point that permanent wage dumping is unacceptable. I am not asking for that. But a **temporary** contribution is only useful if it is part of a package that actually restores margins. If the contribution is too small or too short to matter, then it merely postpones the problem.

So I agree with you on one thing: any employee contribution must be **shared, time-limited, and conditional**. But I will not support a deal that preserves collective standards in form while leaving the company structurally weakened in substance.

### To the regional government
I respect your role and your concern for the regional industrial base. But I need to be blunt: a veto posture that blocks necessary adjustment is not stewardship, it is delay. If the company is structurally overcapacity, then the question is not whether adjustment happens, but whether it happens in a controlled way or through a worse crisis later.

I am open to a solution that preserves German industrial capability. I am **not** open to a solution that freezes the problem in place.

### To management
Your case is stronger when you talk about economics, not confrontation. The family holding will support a turnaround that is:
- economically real,
- governable,
- and not value-destructive in execution.

But if you cannot show a closure-free package that meets the required savings, then you should not expect the owners to back a fake solution. We will not underwrite permanent underperformance.

### My position
I would support a package if it includes all of the following:
1. **Quantified, durable savings**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **Meaningful workforce adjustment, preferably socially managed**
4. **Temporary employee contribution**
5. **Clear investment and product commitments**
6. **A governance structure that avoids open conflict and preserves control**

What I will **not** support:
- a no-change compromise,
- a deal that simply defers hard choices,
- or a settlement that leaves the company unable to compete.

### The practical next step
Bring forward the numbers. If the closure-free alternative is strong enough, I will back it through governance. If it is not, then we need to be honest that the company cannot be kept competitive without structural site decisions.

That is the line I am holding.
