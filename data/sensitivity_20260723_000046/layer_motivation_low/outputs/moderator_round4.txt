### Moderator Summary — Round 4

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree that **a cosmetic compromise is not acceptable**.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must deliver **real, durable restructuring** and not merely postpone the problem.
- The parties also agree that the next step must be a **numbers-based comparison** rather than continued slogan-based argument.
- There is common ground that a serious package must address:
  - fixed-cost reduction,
  - capacity rationalization,
  - workforce adjustment over time,
  - and investment/productivity measures.
- Several parties accept that **temporary employee concessions alone are insufficient** unless matched by binding contributions from management and owners.
- There is also shared recognition that **industrial conflict and governance deadlock would destroy value**.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**
  - Employee-side and state actors reject plant closures as a starting point and, in some cases, as an outcome.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out in advance without proof that alternatives are sufficient.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee-side actors reject them categorically.
  - Management and capital-side actors view them as potentially necessary if the economics require it.
- **Job guarantees / 2030 framework**
  - Employee-side actors want binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
  - Management and capital-side actors say such guarantees are unacceptable if they freeze the cost base and prevent structural adjustment.
- **Governance / veto role**
  - The state and employee-side actors insist their governance rights should be used to block closures and forced layoffs.
  - Management, family holding, and investors argue that political vetoes should not override an economically necessary restructuring.
- **Whether a closure-free package can be sufficient**
  - The central unresolved issue is whether a no-closure route can produce **economically equivalent, permanent savings** at the required scale.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Permanent savings vs. social disruption**
  - The parties have not yet quantified whether closure-free measures can match the structural savings of closures.
- **One-time costs vs. long-term gains**
  - There is no agreed comparison of restructuring costs, execution risk, and steady-state EBIT impact.
- **Temporary relief vs. durable reset**
  - It remains unclear how much of the proposed relief would persist after temporary concessions expire.
- **Capacity reduction method**
  - The debate remains open on whether capacity should be reduced through closures or through shift/line consolidation and redeployment.
- **Burden-sharing**
  - The parties have not settled what binding contributions each stakeholder group would make, or how those would be enforced.
- **Governance and enforceability**
  - There is still no agreed mechanism for how co-determination, state involvement, and shareholder oversight would operate in a final package.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- The next round should be limited to a **side-by-side scenario comparison**:
  1. deep-cut / closure scenario,
  2. closure-free scenario.
- For each, the discussion should specify:
  - permanent savings,
  - temporary savings,
  - one-time restructuring costs,
  - headcount impact,
  - capacity impact,
  - timing of savings,
  - and steady-state EBIT/cash-flow effect.
- The key question to answer next is:
  **Can the company restore competitiveness without plant closures and without compulsory redundancies, at the required scale and on a durable basis?**
- The discussion should avoid repeating positions and instead focus on **verifiable numbers, assumptions, and trade-offs**.
