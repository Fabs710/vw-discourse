We are entering this discussion with a very clear mandate from the workforce: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework**.

Management has chosen confrontation by putting closures and large-scale job cuts on the table. That is unacceptable. We will not accept a restructuring that shifts the full burden onto employees while destroying sites, livelihoods, and regional industrial value.

Our priorities are straightforward:

1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **Rule out forced layoffs.**
3. **Secure binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Preserve co-determination and the workforce’s real say in any restructuring.**
5. **Use alternative measures instead of closure**: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, training, natural attrition, and a fair negotiated contribution from management and owners.

We are prepared to discuss a serious turnaround plan, but only on the basis that it is **socially responsible and reversible**. If the company needs cost relief, then management must first show that it is taking its share of the burden and that investment, product commitments, and future production in Germany are secured.

Let me be equally clear: if this discussion is being used to present a fait accompli — plant closures, mass redundancies, or the breaking of past guarantees — then we will resist it with all the means at our disposal, including mobilising the workforce and escalating industrial action.

So our starting point is not compromise on closures. Our starting point is **protecting jobs, protecting sites, and protecting the future of German manufacturing in this company**.
