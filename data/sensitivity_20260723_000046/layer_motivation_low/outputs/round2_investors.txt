We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

### Where we agree
- The current structure is **not investable** if it leaves German operations structurally overcosted.
- A **real turnaround** is necessary, not a symbolic truce.
- Any outcome must be **durable, measurable, and credible to capital markets**.
- Open-ended conflict would destroy value, cash flow, and management focus.

### Where we disagree
To the works council, union, and regional government: your insistence on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding guarantees through 2030** is understandable politically, but it is **not sufficient financially** unless you can show equivalent, quantified, permanent savings.

From our perspective, what matters is not the label on the package. It is whether it delivers:
- lower unit costs,
- real capacity reduction,
- improved margins,
- and certainty that the savings will stick.

A settlement that preserves the same cost base and merely delays the pain is **unacceptable**. So is a deal that creates years of conflict while pretending to protect value.

### To management
We hear your point that a cosmetic restructuring is useless. That is correct. But you have not yet justified why closures must be the default instrument rather than the last resort. If you want shareholder support, you need to present a **side-by-side comparison** with numbers:
1. the closure / deep-cut path,
2. a closure-free alternative,
3. and the margin impact, timing, and execution risk of each.

At this point, your case is still incomplete if it rests on “closures may be necessary” without proving that less destructive measures cannot get close enough.

### What would move us
We would support a package that credibly restores competitiveness, even if it avoids closures, **if and only if** it contains:
- permanent and verifiable cost reduction,
- genuine capacity adjustment,
- a meaningful workforce contribution,
- management restraint,
- and a clear timetable to margin recovery.

We would also support a closure-based plan **only if** it is clearly the least disruptive route to durable competitiveness and not an avoidable escalation.

### What would not move us
- A purely symbolic settlement
- Temporary concessions with no structural effect
- A prolonged strike or governance deadlock
- A plan that leaves the company uncompetitive and then asks capital markets to wait

### Our position
We are not taking sides in the social dispute. We are taking sides for **risk-adjusted value creation**. That means:
- no cosmetic compromise,
- no indefinite delay,
- no value-destructive conflict,
- and no outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired.

So the next step should be numerical, not rhetorical:
**Show us the savings, the permanence, and the execution path.** If the closure-free package is strong enough, we will back it. If not, then the company must not pretend it has solved the problem.
