### 1) What has been agreed
- **All sides agree the status quo is not sustainable** and that the company needs a real turnaround.
- There is broad agreement that **costs must come down** and that savings need to be **durable and measurable**.
- Several stakeholders accept that a serious solution should include some mix of:
  - capacity adjustment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - internal redeployment,
  - training/qualification,
  - natural attrition,
  - and some form of **temporary, shared contribution**.
- There is also shared recognition that **investment in future products and German operations matters** for any credible settlement.
- Most parties signal preference for a **negotiated outcome** over open conflict, if it can be made economically credible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works council, union, and regional government reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management, capital markets, and to a lesser extent the family holding, keep closures on the table if needed for structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Employee side and regional government oppose them as a first resort or at all.  
  - Management and investors do not rule them out if the economics require it.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees:**  
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030.  
  - Management says blanket guarantees are not compatible with a deep restructuring unless enough savings are secured.
- **Collective bargaining / wage framework:**  
  - The union insists the collective framework must remain intact and any concessions must be temporary and limited.  
  - Management and investors want a more permanent labour-cost adjustment.
- **Governance and state role:**  
  - The regional government insists it cannot be bypassed and will oppose decisions that sideline its role.  
  - Management wants enough flexibility to act without governance deadlock.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** for the turnaround to be credible, and how much each party must contribute.
- Whether the company can reach the needed savings through **non-closure measures alone**, or whether closures are genuinely necessary.
- The balance between:
  - **job security now** versus
  - **competitiveness and investment capacity later**.
- Whether employee concessions can be structured as:
  - temporary and reversible, or
  - permanent and structural.
- How to ensure any solution is **credible to capital markets** while still being **socially and politically acceptable**.
- What governance mechanism could prevent backsliding while still allowing management to implement change.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **quantified options**: required savings, timing, and the operational impact of each measure.
- Ask management to present a **closure-free alternative** with numbers, if one exists.
- Ask the employee side to specify **which concessions are acceptable, for how long, and under what guarantees**.
- Ask the regional government to clarify **what restructuring forms it would support** and under what conditions.
- Keep the next round focused on a **side-by-side comparison** of at least two packages:
  1. management’s structural restructuring path, and  
  2. a negotiated alternative centered on shared sacrifice and site preservation.
