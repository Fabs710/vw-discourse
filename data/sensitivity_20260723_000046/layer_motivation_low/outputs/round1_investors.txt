We agree with the core diagnosis from management and the family: **the status quo is not acceptable**. If the German operations remain structurally overcosted and under-margined, that destroys value for shareholders and ultimately weakens the company’s ability to invest. From a capital-markets perspective, we need a **credible, durable path back to competitive margins**, not a temporary truce.

That said, we are **not** endorsing a value-destructive confrontation or a cosmetic settlement.

### Where we agree
- **A real turnaround is necessary.**
- **The cost base must come down.**
- **Savings must be durable, not symbolic.**
- **A plan that preserves current overcapacity and current economics is not investable.**

### Where we disagree
To the works council and the union: your red lines may be understandable socially, but **“no plant closures, no forced redundancies, no structural change” is not a viable financial answer** unless the alternative delivers equivalent, quantified savings. Right now, what matters to us is not the form of the adjustment but the outcome:
- lower unit costs,
- better capacity utilization,
- a return to target margins,
- and certainty that the measures will actually stick.

If the package simply shifts costs into the future, or relies on vague redeployment and temporary concessions without structural improvement, then it does **not** solve the investment case.

To management: we agree that hard choices cannot be postponed. But we also need to be clear that **open-ended conflict and a drawn-out strike would destroy shareholder value** and delay any recovery. If you pursue closures as the default without a credible negotiated path, you may win a headline but lose value through prolonged disruption, reputational damage, and governance deadlock. We would not back a plan that is operationally disruptive but financially ambiguous.

To the regional government: your insistence on preserving industrial capability is understandable, but **blocking necessary restructuring simply because it is painful is not acceptable**. If German sites remain structurally uncompetitive, then delaying adjustment protects neither jobs nor returns. We need a solution that is economically real, not politically comfortable.

### Our position
We would support **whatever package credibly restores competitiveness**, provided it has these features:
1. **Quantified and verifiable cost reduction**
2. **Durable capacity adjustment**
3. **A clear path to margin improvement within a defined time frame**
4. **Deal certainty, with minimal risk of prolonged industrial conflict**
5. **No purely cosmetic concessions that leave the cost base structurally impaired**

### What would move us
A serious alternative to closures could be acceptable **if** it demonstrably matches the economics:
- permanent labour-cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- measurable headcount reduction through a credible mix of attrition, redeployment, and, if unavoidable, forced measures,
- binding governance to prevent backsliding,
- and investment commitments tied to performance milestones.

### What would not move us
- A settlement that preserves excess capacity and high German costs
- A deal built on temporary goodwill with no structural effect
- A prolonged conflict that burns margin and cash
- Any outcome where management and owners ask shareholders to accept permanent underperformance

So our message is simple: **show us the numbers and the durability**. If the alternative to closures is genuinely strong enough, we will back it. If not, then we will not support a compromise that leaves the company uncompetitive.
