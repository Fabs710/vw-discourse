I will be equally direct.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: the company needs a **real** turnaround, not a cosmetic pause. And I accept that the cost base in Germany must come down if the company is to remain competitive.

But I do **not** accept the logic that the only credible route is plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That is too blunt, too risky, and too damaging for the regional economy. You have not yet shown that every less destructive option has been exhausted.

From the state’s perspective, a solution must satisfy three tests:
1. **Protect the German industrial base**
2. **Deliver durable competitiveness**
3. **Preserve governance stability and co-determination**

A plan that breaks the first or third test is not acceptable, even if it looks neat on a spreadsheet.

So if you want my support, I need a **closure-free package first**:
- capacity reductions without plant shutdowns,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- natural attrition,
- a temporary and proportionate employee contribution,
- and a real contribution from management and owners.

If that package genuinely does not close the gap, then show the numbers. But until then, closures remain a last resort, not a starting point.

### To the works council
I agree with your core position: **no forced layoffs, no dismantling of job security, no treating German plants as disposable**. Those are not just bargaining points; they are central to the company’s role in the region.

Where I want to be careful is this: a hard “no change at all” position will not hold if the company can prove that some adjustment is necessary. What I can support is **site-preserving restructuring with binding guarantees**, not frozen structures regardless of economic reality.

So I support your demand for:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job and site guarantees,
- and co-determination in implementation.

But I also need you to engage on the economics. If you want the state to use its veto power responsibly, then we need a package that is credible to the business side as well as socially responsible.

### To the union
I appreciate that you are talking about **temporary, conditional contributions** rather than permanent wage erosion. That is the right direction. A temporary, shared-sacrifice package can be part of a responsible settlement.

But I will not support any deal that turns a crisis into a permanent weakening of collective standards. The social partnership model only works if the burden is shared and the gains are real.

What I could support:
- temporary wage moderation,
- reduced bonuses or deferred compensation,
- working-time corridors,
- voluntary and attrition-based headcount reduction,
- and an investment commitment tied to German production.

What I cannot support:
- permanent wage dumping,
- forced redundancies as the default,
- or closures presented as inevitability before alternatives are fully tested.

### To the controlling family
I agree that uncontrolled conflict destroys value. That is precisely why we should not be pushed into a closure-first escalation. A settlement that protects industrial peace while restoring competitiveness is better for everyone, including ownership.

But I also need clarity from you: are you prepared to back a **socially managed restructuring without German plant closures** if it delivers sufficient savings? If yes, then use your influence to make management take that path seriously. If not, then you are helping to shift the burden onto the state and workforce.

### To the institutional investors
I hear the demand for quantifiable, durable savings. That is legitimate. But let me say clearly: **durability does not automatically mean closure**. A package can be durable through capacity trimming, labour flexibility, and structural efficiency gains without destroying sites.

You are right that open-ended conflict would be value-destructive. That is why the state will push for a negotiated solution. But a solution that saves the quarter and weakens the region is not acceptable.

### My position
The regional government will support a restructuring only if it:
- keeps German sites open,
- avoids mass compulsory redundancies,
- preserves the state’s co-determination role,
- and still delivers measurable competitiveness improvements.

That means I am open to:
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- capacity adjustment without closures,
- and a binding investment plan.

I am **not** open to:
- plant closures in Germany as the default answer,
- being sidelined in governance,
- or a restructuring that hollows out the regional industrial base.

### What I want next
Management should put forward, in numbers, a **closure-free restructuring scenario** with:
- cost savings by measure,
- timing,
- expected headcount effects,
- investment commitments,
- and safeguards for German sites.

If that scenario is credible, I will engage constructively. If not, then you are asking us to endorse industrial damage that is neither necessary nor politically acceptable.

So my message is simple: **adjustment yes, destruction no.**
