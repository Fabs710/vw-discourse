We share the basic assessment that a real restructuring is needed, and that it cannot be done by simply loading all the pain onto the workforce.

But I need to be very clear where we stand:

- **Plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable starting point.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **Any attack on the collective wage framework is unacceptable unless it is part of a fair, time-limited, and reciprocal deal.**

If management is serious about competitiveness, then it must stop framing the workforce as the sole cost item to be cut. Employees did not cause the strategic errors, market shifts, or underperformance alone, and they will not finance the turnaround alone.

What we can discuss, and what we have already signaled willingness to discuss, are **shared-sacrifice measures**, such as:

- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- reduced or suspended bonuses,
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit options,
- and contributions tied to a binding investment and site-security package.

But that only works if the deal is balanced. If management wants concessions, then we expect:

1. **Binding protection for German sites**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **A credible future industrial plan**
4. **Management and owners also taking responsibility**
5. **No one-sided dismantling of long-standing protections**

We are not impressed by claims that “there is no alternative” if the alternative is to make workers carry the whole burden while ownership and management remain untouched. That is not a restructuring solution; that is a transfer of risk downward.

So our position is this:  
**We are ready to negotiate a durable settlement, but only one based on fairness, job security, and real shared sacrifice.**  
If plant closures or compulsory redundancies remain on the table, then you should expect strong resistance, including industrial action.
