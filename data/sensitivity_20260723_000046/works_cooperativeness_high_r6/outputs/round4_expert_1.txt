## 1) Core financial logic

For each site, the economic question is:

**Which option maximizes 3-year probability-weighted value, after one-time costs and execution risk?**

The comparison should include:

- **Annual gross savings**
- **One-time restructuring cash costs**
- **Timing of savings realization**
- **Probability of delivery**
- **3-year NPV / probability-weighted NPV**
- **Residual risk** from underdelivery or reversibility

A useful simplification is:

\[
\text{3-year PW NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{3} \frac{(\text{Annual savings}_t \times p_t)}{(1+r)^t} - \text{One-time costs}
\]

Where:
- \(p_t\) = probability-weighted realization in year \(t\)
- \(r\) = discount rate, often 8%–12% in turnaround analysis; I will use **10%** as a neutral restructuring hurdle for illustration

## 2) Important operating assumptions

Because site-level data is unavailable, I use the following **illustrative assumptions**, not verified facts:

### A. Closure economics
A closure typically yields:
- **high permanent fixed-cost removal**
- but also **high one-time cash costs**

Typical one-time items:
- severance / social plan
- decommissioning
- site remediation
- asset write-downs
- tooling relocation
- supply-chain disruption costs
- transition costs for rerouting volume

### B. Deep no-closure restructuring
This usually includes:
- shift reduction
- line consolidation
- product reallocation
- internal mobility / voluntary exits
- working-time changes
- temporary wage/bonus moderation
- overhead cuts

It usually achieves:
- **50%–80% of closure-level savings**
- with **lower one-time costs**
- but often **less complete fixed-cost removal**

### C. Hybrid sequencing
This has a:
- **short-term moratorium / protection phase**
- followed by a review-triggered fallback

Economically, this often creates:
- lower near-term conflict and cash outflow
- but delayed savings and some option value lost
- slightly worse NPV than an immediate, clean package unless the moratorium improves execution enough to offset delay

## 5) Illustrative 3-year NPV comparison

### A. Full closure / shutdown

**Assumptions**
- Savings start after 12 months of transition
- Year 1 savings: 25% of full annual savings
- Year 2 savings: 75%
- Year 3 savings: 100%
- One-time cash cost: **€350m**
- Probability-weighted delivery: **80%**

**Cash savings stream**
- Year 1: 0.25 × 212.5 = **€53.1m**
- Year 2: 0.75 × 212.5 = **€159.4m**
- Year 3: 1.00 × 212.5 = **€212.5m**

Discounted and probability-weighted:

- Y1: 53.1 × 0.80 / 1.10 = **€38.6m**
- Y2: 159.4 × 0.80 / 1.21 = **€105.4m**
- Y3: 212.5 × 0.80 / 1.331 = **€127.7m**

Total PV savings = **€271.7m**

Less one-time costs = **€350m**

**3-year PW NPV = -€78.3m**

This result is not unusual: a closure can be economically superior over a longer horizon, but under a 3-year lens the **cash cost often outweighs the early savings**.

---

### B. Deep no-closure restructuring

**Assumptions**
- Savings begin earlier, because there is no closure transition
- Year 1 savings: 50% of annual savings
- Year 2: 85%
- Year 3: 100%
- One-time cash cost: **€120m**
- Probability-weighted delivery: **75%**

**Cash savings stream**
- Year 1: 0.50 × 127.5 = **€63.8m**
- Year 2: 0.85 × 127.5 = **€108.4m**
- Year 3: 1.00 × 127.5 = **€127.5m**

Discounted and probability-weighted:

- Y1: 63.8 × 0.75 / 1.10 = **€43.5m**
- Y2: 108.4 × 0.75 / 1.21 = **€67.2m**
- Y3: 127.5 × 0.75 / 1.331 = **€71.9m**

Total PV savings = **€182.6m**

Less one-time costs = **€120m**

**3-year PW NPV = +€62.6m**

This is the key economic point: **deep no-closure restructuring can be strongly positive in 3-year NPV terms because the upfront cash burden is much lower**, even though the annual savings are smaller than closure.

### C. Hybrid sequencing with moratorium/backstop

**Assumptions**
- A one- to two-year moratorium on closure and compulsory redundancies
- Savings delayed relative to deep no-closure because of sequencing and negotiation
- Year 1 savings: 35%
- Year 2: 70%
- Year 3: 90%
- One-time cash cost: **€150m**
- Probability-weighted delivery: **70%**

**Cash savings stream**
- Year 1: 0.35 × 148.8 = **€52.1m**
- Year 2: 0.70 × 148.8 = **€104.2m**
- Year 3: 0.90 × 148.8 = **€133.9m**

Discounted and probability-weighted:

- Y1: 52.1 × 0.70 / 1.10 = **€33.2m**
- Y2: 104.2 × 0.70 / 1.21 = **€60.3m**
- Y3: 133.9 × 0.70 / 1.331 = **€70.4m**

Total PV savings = **€163.9m**

Less one-time costs = **€150m**

**3-year PW NPV = +€13.9m**

So hybrid sequencing can be value-positive, but usually **less attractive than a clean, immediately executable no-closure package** unless the moratorium materially improves labor peace and execution certainty.

## 7) Site-by-site decision rules

Without actual site data, the correct restructuring rule is:

### Closure is economically favored when:
- the site has **high fixed-cost density**
- little or no future product allocation
- high ongoing capex requirement
- low redeployment value
- high duplicated overhead
- low probability that internal measures can remove enough cost

### Deep no-closure is economically favored when:
- the site has **still-viable product lines**
- there is meaningful ability to cut shifts, overhead, and labor cost
- the closure one-time cost is very large
- the company needs cash preservation
- labor peace is materially valuable

### Hybrid is economically favored when:
- immediate closure would cause severe strike / political / execution losses
- but management needs a credible backstop
- and there is a reasonable chance the internal package will deliver close enough economics

## 8) Quantifying “50%–80% of closure economics”

The conversation already references a useful rule of thumb: no-closure measures can often capture **50%–80%** of closure economics.

Translated financially:
- If closure saves €200m/year, a deep no-closure package may save roughly **€100m–€160m/year**
- But the one-time cost may fall from, say, **€300m–€500m** to **€50m–€150m**

That is why the **3-year result can favor non-closure even if the long-run annual run-rate remains somewhat weaker**.

## 9) Main uncertainty drivers

The biggest unknowns that could materially change the answer are:

1. **Actual site fixed-cost base**
2. **How much labor cost can be permanently reset**
3. **Whether products can be reallocated internally**
4. **How much demand recovery is embedded**
5. **Strike duration / disruption cost**
6. **Asset write-downs and decommissioning**
7. **Pension / social plan liabilities**
8. **Supply-chain and logistics redesign costs**

If strike disruption is prolonged, closure can become much worse in the short run.  
If internal concessions are temporary only, no-closure NPV deteriorates quickly after year 3.

## 10) Decision conclusion

### Financial conclusion
On a 3-year probability-weighted basis, **a serious deep no-closure restructuring can easily outperform closure**, because closure’s one-time costs are so high.

### Strategic-financial conclusion
A closure is financially justified only if:
- the site truly cannot be made profitable internally,
- the internal package cannot permanently remove enough cost,
- or management needs the capacity reduction more than it needs short-term cash preservation and labor peace.

### Practical conclusion for the dispute
The relevant question is not “Can no-closure equal closure in annual savings?”  
It is:

**Can no-closure deliver enough durable savings, quickly enough, with sufficiently low execution risk, to beat closure on risk-adjusted 3-year value?**

Very often, the answer is **yes** for the 3-year lens — especially where plant closure costs are large.
