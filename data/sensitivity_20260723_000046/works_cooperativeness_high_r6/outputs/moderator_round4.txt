Core impasse, as reflected in round 4:

## 1) Where the impasse lies
The negotiation is stuck on **one central question**:

**Can the company accept a restructuring package that delivers most of the economics without preserving closure and compulsory-redundancy rights as fallback tools?**

That splits the room into two camps:

- **Labour / works council / union / state shareholder**
  want a **binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff framework**, with internal restructuring only and enforceable investment commitments.

- **Management / owners / investors**
  want **structural flexibility retained**, because they do not yet accept that a non-closure package can be sufficient or durable enough on its own.

So the immediate deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **the enforcement mechanism and the residual management backstop**.

---

## 2) Facilitator proposals for possible compromise routes

### Proposal A: Time-bound moratorium with measurable milestones
A negotiated package could include:
- a **moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies** for a defined period,
- a **quantified internal restructuring plan** with site-by-site milestones,
- and a **pre-agreed review after 12–24 months** based strictly on measurable delivery.

To address labour concerns:
- the review would **not automatically reopen closure rights**;
- any fallback would require a **fresh negotiation** and defined trigger thresholds.

To address management concerns:
- if the package misses agreed milestones by a material margin, a **predefined escalation process** would begin.

---

### Proposal B: Binding “equivalent savings” package with asymmetric protections
A second route could be:
- labour accepts **temporary, reciprocal concessions**,
- management and owners commit to **investment, overhead reduction, and internal capacity cuts**,
- and the company accepts **no closures / no compulsory redundancies during the agreement term**.

In return:
- the agreement would include **hard savings targets**, audited quarterly,
- and **automatic corrective measures** if the package underdelivers, such as deeper internal cuts, additional management contributions, or extended wage moderation.

This tries to shift the dispute from “closure rights versus no closure” to **verified delivery versus underdelivery**.

---

### Proposal C: Protected core / adjustable periphery
A third compromise could distinguish between:
- a **protected German core** of sites guaranteed open for a set period,
- and a **limited adjustable periphery** where internal reallocations, product shifts, and capacity changes are allowed.

This would mean:
- no closures of core sites,
- no forced redundancies as the default,
- but greater room for internal restructuring in less strategic units.

This may be useful if the parties can agree that **not every site carries the same strategic role**.

## 3) One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific internal restructuring measures, quantified by site, would you accept as economically equivalent to closure savings if management formally renounces closure rights for the agreement period?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What exact employee contributions and enforcement mechanisms would the union accept so that “no closure” is not just a principle but a durable, measurable settlement?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What minimum quantified savings gap would still justify retaining closure and compulsory-redundancy backstops, and what evidence would you accept that a no-closure package closes that gap?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a binding no-closure settlement with concrete governance tools, even if that limits future management flexibility during the agreement period?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a time-bound no-closure commitment if the package delivers audited, probability-weighted savings close to the closure case and includes strict corrective triggers?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a risk-adjusted internal restructuring package if it demonstrates credible 3-year value creation, even without closure rights as a fallback?**

---

## 4) Facilitator summary of the next needed move
For the dialogue to move forward, the parties likely need to answer three bridge questions:

1. **What level of savings is “close enough” to closure economics?**
2. **What enforcement mechanism replaces closure as a backstop?**
3. **What period of binding protection is acceptable if labour makes concessions?**
