As the state shareholder, I need to be equally clear: **a restructuring that protects the company only by hollowing out our German industrial base is not acceptable.**

I do agree on one point raised by management: **the status quo is not a solution**. The company does need a durable path back to competitiveness, and I am not pretending that cost and capacity issues do not exist. A negotiated restructuring can be legitimate.

But I disagree sharply with the idea that the only credible route is to put **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and the erosion of long-standing protections** on the table as a normal management choice.

### Where I agree
I support:
- a serious restructuring, if it is truly necessary;
- capacity adjustments where they are economically justified;
- qualification, redeployment, and working-time solutions;
- voluntary instruments before forced measures;
- a future-oriented investment plan for German sites;
- and a contribution from the workforce, provided it is fair, limited, and reciprocal.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- treating plant closures in Germany as the default restructuring lever;
- making compulsory redundancies an ordinary bargaining chip;
- or demanding that employees absorb the main burden while owners and management preserve strategic flexibility untouched.

That is not a balanced turnaround. That is a transfer of risk downward onto workers and regions that have carried this company for decades.

### My core concern
This company is not just any cost unit. It is a central industrial anchor in a region with real employment, supplier networks, and social stability at stake. If we break sites or gut employment, the damage will not be temporary. It will weaken the company’s production base, regional resilience, and political legitimacy for years.

And let me be very direct: **there is no path I will support that removes the state’s co-determination or veto role, or that amounts to a disguised exit from the company’s regional responsibilities.**

### What I am prepared to support
I can support a negotiated package if it contains all of the following:
1. **binding protection for German sites** as the starting point, not an afterthought;
2. **no compulsory redundancies** as the primary instrument;
3. **shared sacrifice**, including a real but proportionate employee contribution;
4. **capacity rationalization through non-destructive means first**;
5. **credible investment commitments** for future products and technologies in Germany;
6. **long-term employment perspectives**, not a short-term cost cut followed by decline.

### On management’s position
Management says closures “remain on the table.” I understand the leverage logic, but I warn against using that as a negotiating tactic in a way that normalizes unacceptable outcomes. If closures are treated as the starting assumption, you will harden resistance and destroy trust before a serious compromise is possible.

If management genuinely wants a solution, then it needs to come back with a package that shows:
- where savings come from,
- how jobs are protected,
- how sites remain viable,
- and how the burden is shared fairly.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that there be no fait accompli, no forced redundancies, and no one-sided dismantling of protections. That is the right line.

At the same time, I would encourage both of you to be prepared for a negotiated contribution if it is:
- temporary or time-limited,
- tied to binding site and investment guarantees,
- and designed to preserve the company’s long-term industrial base.

### Bottom line
I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring built on German plant closures or mass layoffs.  
I **am** prepared to back a serious negotiated restructuring that protects sites and jobs, preserves co-determination, and restores competitiveness through fair, durable measures.

If the discussion cannot meet those conditions, then my position will remain oppositional.
