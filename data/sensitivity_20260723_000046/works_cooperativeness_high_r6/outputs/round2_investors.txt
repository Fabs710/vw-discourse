We share the core diagnosis that the company needs a **real structural reset**. On that point, management, the family holding, and even the labour side’s recognition of the problem are broadly right: the current cost base and capacity profile are not compatible with competitive margins.

But for us the test is not whether a deal is politically comfortable. The test is whether it **restores risk-adjusted value** through **durable** and **enforceable** cost reduction.

### Where we agree
We agree with:
- the need for a **serious restructuring**, not a cosmetic deal;
- the need for **capacity reduction**;
- the use of **redeployment, qualification, and working-time flexibility** where they create real savings;
- the value of **voluntary exits** over avoidable confrontation;
- the importance of a **credible investment plan** for future products;
- and the fact that prolonged strikes and uncertainty would be value destructive.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that simply preserves the current German footprint, blocks compulsory redundancies in all circumstances, and leaves legacy labour costs largely intact. That would leave the company with an uncompetitive cost base and structurally impaired margins. For capital markets, that is fundamentally unacceptable.

At the same time, we also do **not** support plant closures being used as a default or symbolic threat if an alternative can achieve the same economics. The key question is not ideology; it is **equivalent financial effect**.

### On the labour side’s position
The works council and union keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no unilateral dismantling of protections.” We understand the industrial and social logic, but from an investor perspective that is only acceptable **if** they can produce a package that is truly comparable on savings, timing, and durability.

So far, what we are hearing is mainly:
- temporary measures,
- voluntary mechanisms,
- and broad commitments to shared sacrifice.

That is not enough by itself. A time-limited contribution that expires before the structural problem is fixed does not restore returns. We need:
- quantified savings,
- a clear capacity reduction path,
- enforceability,
- and evidence that the cost reset is durable.

### On management
Management is right to insist that the company cannot accept managed decline. But we want to be clear: **credibility matters**. If closures and layoffs are simply used as leverage without a disciplined, quantified restructuring case, the market will discount the entire process.

What we need from management is not just severity, but precision:
- what savings are required,
- what capacity must come out,
- what measures are truly non-negotiable,
- and how the package affects returns on sales and free cash flow.

### On the family holding
We broadly align with the family’s long-term value focus. But long-term ownership cannot become an excuse for open-ended bargaining. If a negotiated alternative to closures is possible, we would support it — but only if it is **credible, durable, and economically equivalent**. If not, then the company should not be trapped by political red lines into preserving an uncompetitive structure.

### On the state shareholder
Your concern for regional stability is understandable and legitimate. But investor capital will not support a settlement that protects every site at the expense of the business model. If the final outcome cannot restore competitiveness, then the regional base is not being protected — it is being delayed into a deeper crisis.

### Our position
We are prepared to support **whatever package credibly restores competitiveness**. That means:
- a material and durable cost reduction,
- structural capacity adjustment if needed,
- no purely symbolic compromise,
- and no settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the employee side can produce an alternative to closures that matches the economics, we can back it. If not, then the company must retain the ability to act decisively.

### What would move us
We would be materially more comfortable with:
1. **Quantified savings and timing**
2. **A durable capacity reduction plan**
3. **A binding investment path**
4. **A real employee contribution that is not merely temporary window dressing**
5. **A settlement that is enforceable and not dependent on goodwill alone**

### Bottom line
We are not aligned with red lines for their own sake. We are aligned with **financial outcomes**:
- competitive margins,
- credible restructuring,
- and stable value creation.

If the eventual package fails that test, we will treat it as value destructive and will say so through our capital allocation and analyst channels.
