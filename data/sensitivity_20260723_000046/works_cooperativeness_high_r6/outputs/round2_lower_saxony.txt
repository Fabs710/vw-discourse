As the state shareholder, I need to be very clear: I agree that the company needs a serious restructuring, but I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible route is plant closures, mass redundancies, and dismantling the protections that have kept this company stable and rooted in the region.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one central point: the status quo is not sustainable. The cost base, capacity, and margin situation clearly need action.

I also agree with labour on something important: this cannot be solved by pushing the full burden onto employees. A durable settlement needs:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary measures,
- and a fair, time-limited employee contribution.

That is a legitimate basis for negotiation.

### Where I disagree
I strongly disagree with the repeated framing that **closures and compulsory redundancies must remain normal tools** unless labour produces something “equivalent.”

That is too narrow and too risky. It treats the company’s German industrial base as disposable, when in reality these sites are part of the company’s long-term value, supply chain strength, and social legitimacy. Once a plant is closed, that capacity, skills base, and trust are lost for good.

I also reject the idea that the state can be asked to simply accept a settlement that weakens regional employment and then call that a success because margins improved on paper. We are not here to facilitate hollowing out the company’s home base.

### To management
You say you need a structural reset. I understand that. But you have not yet shown that closures are the only way to get there.

Before any closure or forced redundancy is treated as unavoidable, I expect a serious examination of alternatives:
- capacity reduction through internal reallocation,
- shift and line adjustments,
- product allocation changes,
- targeted efficiency measures,
- voluntary departures,
- and temporary labour-cost contributions tied to investment commitments.

If you want us to take closure threats seriously, then bring **numbers**, not just pressure:
- what savings are needed,
- what capacity must come out,
- and why this cannot be achieved with a negotiated package that protects sites and employment.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that there be no fait accompli and no one-sided burden shifting. That is the right line.

But I also want to be constructive and realistic: if we want to avoid the harshest measures, then labour side proposals must be specific enough to be judged against the economic problem. “No closures” is a legitimate red line, but it cannot remain just a slogan. It has to be matched by a package that is credible, quantified, and enforceable.

That said, I am **not** asking workers to pay for management’s mistakes alone. If there is a contribution from the workforce, then it must be:
- temporary,
- limited,
- reciprocal,
- and tied to binding guarantees on sites, jobs, and investment.

### To the family holding and investors
I understand the long-term value argument. But long-term value does not mean maximizing short-term restructuring speed at the cost of regional legitimacy, industrial trust, and workforce stability.

A settlement that triggers lasting industrial conflict or destroys the company’s role as a regional anchor may look disciplined in the board papers but weaken the business in practice.

### My position
I can support a negotiated restructuring if it contains all of the following:
1. **No plant closures as the first resort**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the default tool**
3. **A real employee contribution that is temporary and fair**
4. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**
6. **A credible industrial plan that protects the company’s regional anchor function**

What I cannot support is a package that:
- normalizes closures,
- treats forced layoffs as routine,
- and shifts the company away from its German industrial base.

### Bottom line
We need restructuring, yes.  
We do **not** need to destroy the company’s regional foundation to achieve it.

If management wants the state shareholder’s support, it must present an alternative that protects jobs and sites while restoring competitiveness. If it cannot, then I will continue to oppose any outcome built on closures and mass layoffs.
