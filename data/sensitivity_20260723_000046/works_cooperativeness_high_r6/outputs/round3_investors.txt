We are prepared to support a restructuring that **materially and durably restores margins**. That is the only test that matters for capital markets.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert that:
- the current cost base is too high,
- the status quo is not sustainable,
- a cosmetic settlement would destroy value,
- and **real capacity reduction and durable cost reduction** are necessary.

We also agree with labour and the state on one point:
- a prolonged strike, reputational damage, or a conflict that bleeds cash and output would be value destructive.

So yes, we want a deal. But not any deal.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that leaves:
- the German footprint unchanged,
- labour costs largely intact,
- and margins still structurally impaired.

That would be a cosmetic compromise dressed up as stability. For us, that is fundamentally unacceptable.

At the same time, we also do **not** support plant closures as a symbolic gesture if a serious non-closure package can get close enough on economics. The expert has made clear that non-closure alternatives can often capture **most, but not all**, of the closure savings. That means the right question is not ideology; it is **risk-adjusted equivalence**.

## What the expert’s model means for us
The specialist’s comparison is useful because it confirms three things:

1. **Closure** gives the strongest permanent savings, but with high one-time costs and high disruption risk.
2. **No-closure restructuring** can deliver a large share of the savings, often with better near-term cash and lower execution risk.
3. **Hybrid sequencing** only works if the milestones are real and the fallback is credible.

That leads to a clear capital-markets position:
- We will support **whatever package credibly restores competitiveness**.
- We will reject any package that merely defers the problem.
- We will also reject any package that relies on conflict to force compliance.

## Response to the works council
We understand the industrial logic of protecting sites and jobs. But your current line — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful structural change to legacy protections — is not enough unless it delivers comparable economics.

The expert’s assessment is clear: a non-closure package can often get you **50%–80%** of the savings, not always 100%. That is important. It means your side cannot dismiss cost and capacity reductions as if they were purely optional.

If you want capital markets support for a no-closure route, you need:
- quantified savings,
- durable savings,
- enforceability,
- and a real capacity reduction path.

Otherwise, you are asking investors to accept structurally impaired returns in the name of industrial peace. We will not do that.

## Response to the union

But temporary concessions alone are not enough. What matters is whether the package permanently fixes the cost base. If your proposal expires before the problem is solved, it is not a turnaround.

So the union needs to answer one simple question:
- What combination of wage moderation, working-time change, voluntary exits, and capacity reduction delivers a **durable** result?

If you can show that, we will consider it seriously. If not, then the company must retain structural options.

## Response to the state shareholder
Your concern for regional stability is legitimate, but stability only matters if the company remains investable.

We agree that closures should not be the first reflex. But political concern cannot become a veto on economic reality. The state should support a solution that:
- protects what is economically defensible,
- enables investment,
- and restores competitive margins.

If a no-closure plan can do that, we would welcome it. If not, delaying adjustment will simply deepen the eventual crisis.

## Response to the family holding
Your long-term value focus is broadly aligned with ours. We agree that a reckless headline closure strategy can destroy trust and execution capacity.

But the same logic also applies to preserving every site and every legacy guarantee. Long-term owners should not back a compromise that leaves the business structurally weaker.

Your support should be conditional on:
- quantified savings,
- durable capacity reduction,
- enforceable implementation,
- and a credible path to improved returns.

## Our bottom line
We are not here to choose between “closures at any price” and “no change at any price.”

Our position is simple:

- **Yes** to a serious, quantified restructuring.
- **Yes** to non-closure alternatives if they deliver durable, enforceable economics that are close enough on a risk-adjusted basis.
- **No** to cosmetic settlements.
- **No** to outcomes that leave margins structurally impaired.
- **No** to open-ended conflict that destroys value.

If management and labour can table a package that restores competitive margins through durable internal restructuring, we would support it. If they cannot, then the company must keep structural measures available.
