I hear the positions from labour, the union, management, and the state shareholder.

Let me be equally clear from the ownership side: **we support a serious restructuring, but not a performative one**. The family holding’s overriding concern is long-term enterprise value, and that means the company must restore competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity. If that does not happen, then all the employment guarantees in the world only postpone a worse outcome.

### Where I agree
I agree with several points made here:

- **The status quo is not acceptable.** Management is right that structural overcapacity and a weak cost base cannot simply be wished away.
- **This cannot be solved by dumping the burden entirely on employees.** A turnaround that is seen as one-sided will not be durable.
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.** Prolonged industrial strife would destroy value, damage the brand, and undermine future investment.
- **Voluntary measures, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and a real contribution from the workforce should be part of the package.**

### Where I disagree
I do **not** agree with the implication from the works council and state shareholder that:

- German site protection can be treated as absolute,
- compulsory redundancies must be ruled out under all circumstances,
- and the company can remain competitive while preserving every legacy structure untouched.

That is not a credible business plan. It may be politically appealing, but it does not answer the margin gap.

I also do **not** accept a restructuring logic in which management presents plant closures as the only serious tool without first exhausting all credible alternatives. That is too blunt an instrument if it is used as the opening move rather than the last resort. But I will be equally blunt: **if there is no alternative package that is materially equivalent in cost and capacity effect, then the company must retain the option to act structurally.**

### What matters to the owners
The family holding will not support any outcome that:

- permanently weakens the company’s industrial footprint without restoring competitiveness,
- creates a cost structure that remains uncompetitive after the settlement,
- or turns governance into a mechanism for freezing decline.

We are long-term owners. That means we can accept short-term pain for a durable reset. What we cannot accept is a settlement that preserves symbolism while eroding value.

### On plant closures
I understand why closures are emotionally and politically explosive. But they cannot be ruled out in principle if they are necessary to secure the company’s future. The crucial question is not whether closures are ideologically pleasant; it is whether the final package produces a genuine, durable improvement in economics.

So my position is:

- **Closures should not be the first choice.**
- **They should be a real option if no equivalent alternative is offered.**
- **But any closure decision must be tied to a credible overall restructuring plan, not used as a threat without purpose.**

### On compulsory redundancies
I would strongly prefer to avoid compulsory redundancies because they can trigger prolonged conflict and reputational damage. But I will not make them a taboo if the alternative is a paper compromise that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

### On the state shareholder’s position
I respect the concern for regional stability. It is legitimate. But the state cannot insist on every site being protected regardless of economics while also expecting the company to compete globally. Those two goals are not always compatible.

If the state wants durable jobs, it should help secure a durable business, not just preserve the current footprint.

### What would move us
What could move the family holding is a package that is clearly credible on four dimensions:

1. **Structural capacity reduction**
2. **Material and durable labour-cost savings**
3. **A viable investment path for future products and technology**
4. **Labour peace and governance stability**

If an alternative to closures can achieve those four outcomes with equal durability, we would support it. If not, we will not block necessary structural action simply to avoid difficult decisions.

### Bottom line
We are **not** here to preserve the past at any price.  
We are here to preserve the company’s long-term value, which may require tough restructuring.

So my message is:

- To management: bring forward a package that is credible, not just aggressive.
- To labour: if you want to avoid closures and forced measures, you must offer a solution that matches them in effect.
- To the state shareholder: regional legitimacy matters, but competitiveness matters too. Protecting jobs in the long run requires a competitive company, not just protected sites.

We will judge the outcome by whether it restores the business, not by whether it avoids hard decisions on paper.
