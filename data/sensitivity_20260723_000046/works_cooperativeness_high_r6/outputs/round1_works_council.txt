We are prepared to discuss a serious restructuring, but not at the expense of the workforce’s existence.

Our position is clear:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No unilateral dismantling of the historic job-security framework.
- Any savings must be achieved through negotiated, socially responsible measures, not by sacrificing entire sites or forcing people out.

If management says competitiveness requires action, then let us talk about alternatives that preserve production and jobs:
- capacity adjustments,
- flexible working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary departure options only,
- temporary or negotiated employee contributions,
- and a credible investment plan for the future of German sites.

But there is a hard line here: we will not accept a fait accompli, and we will not sign off on closing plants that are part of the company’s industrial backbone. If management wants the workforce to contribute, then management must also commit—bindingly—to site security, job security, and genuine co-determination in shaping the restructuring.

We are ready to negotiate, but only on the basis of:
1. securing every German plant,
2. protecting jobs to 2030,
3. preserving co-determination rights,
4. and ensuring that any burden is shared fairly, not dumped on employees alone.

If those conditions are not on the table, then there is no acceptable solution from our side.
