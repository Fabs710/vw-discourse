Management, owners, and investors are making the same mistake in different words: they treat **closures and forced layoffs** as if they were normal instruments of restructuring. They are not. For us, they are the point where a “turnaround” becomes the destruction of the industrial base and a breach of trust with the workforce.

So let me respond directly to the arguments on the table.

### Where I agree
I agree with the shared diagnosis that the company needs a serious restructuring and that the current cost position is not acceptable. I also agree that a cosmetic deal would help nobody, including the workforce. We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a temporary, negotiated employee contribution.

But those are only acceptable if they are tied to **binding protection for German sites and jobs**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the claim that plant closures must remain a real option unless workers deliver an “equivalent” alternative. That framing is one-sided. It assumes the burden of proof lies only with labour, while management’s own strategic errors, ownership decisions, and prior investment choices are treated as fixed.

Nor do I accept compulsory redundancies as an ordinary restructuring tool. Once you normalize forced layoffs, the whole logic of co-determination and the historic job-security framework is undermined.

And I am not impressed by talk of “durable savings” if the means to achieve them are socially destructive and irreversible.

### What is non-negotiable for us
Our position remains:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No unilateral dismantling of job-security protections**
4. **Preserve co-determination as a real shaping power, not a ritual consultation**

If these are not respected, then there is no agreement from our side.

### On numbers and comparability
You ask for a concrete alternative package. Fine. Here is the direction we are prepared to discuss:
- temporary capacity reductions through shift and line adjustments rather than site shutdowns,
- redeployment across the German network,
- qualification and skill transformation,
- voluntary exit pathways only,
- moderated working-time and wage-related contributions for a defined period,
- and a binding investment commitment for German plants through 2030.

But the key point is this: **we will not validate a package that “matches the economics” by permanently sacrificing plants or forcing people out.** If management says the target is a structural cost reset, then management must also accept structural commitments on site security and future production.

### What we need from management and owners
If you want labour to contribute, then we need:
- a **binding no-closure commitment** for all German plants,
- **no compulsory redundancies** through 2030,
- a clear investment plan for products and technologies in Germany,
- and a genuine role for the works council in shaping implementation, not just reacting to it.

If you cannot offer that, then your call for sacrifice is not credible.

### What I reject from the other sides
- **From management:** the idea that closures are merely “options” and not a threat.
- **From owners/investors:** the idea that value creation can be measured only by short-term margin repair while ignoring the social and industrial consequences.
- **From the state shareholder:** support is welcome, but political concern alone does not solve the economic problem unless it is translated into binding protection for jobs and sites.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a real restructuring, but only one that keeps every German plant open, avoids compulsory redundancies, and preserves the job-security framework.

If management wants a contribution from the workforce, then put a binding offer on the table:
- security to 2030,
- real co-determination,
- and investment for the German industrial base.

Without that, there is nothing acceptable here from the works council’s side.
