We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic settlement.
- The current cost base is too high, and **margins must improve materially and durably**.
- A prolonged strike or endless uncertainty would destroy value.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and temporary employee contributions are legitimate tools.
- A package that is **quantified, enforceable, and durable** is the right standard.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support any outcome that leaves the German footprint essentially unchanged, the labor cost base structurally uncompetitive, or the margin problem unresolved.

At the same time, we do **not** buy the idea that closure has to be the default answer if a serious internal restructuring can get close enough on economics. The expert’s assessment matters here: non-closure measures can often capture **50%–80%** of closure-level savings with lower one-time costs and lower disruption risk. That is not trivial. It means the real test is **risk-adjusted economic sufficiency**, not ideological purity.

## To the works council and union
Your insistence on no closures and no compulsory redundancies is understandable, but it is **not sufficient by itself**. If you want capital-markets support for a no-closure route, you need to show:
- how much cost comes out,
- how much capacity comes out,
- how long it lasts,
- and how it is enforced if performance misses.

A promise of protection without a credible savings plan is just a delay tactic. We will not back that.

## To management
You are right that flexibility must remain if the package underdelivers. But you still need to be more precise and disciplined. We need a side-by-side case:
1. full closure,
2. deep no-closure restructuring,
3. hybrid sequencing with a strict backstop,

showing:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- timing,
- execution risk,
- and probability-weighted NPV.

If the no-closure package is genuinely close enough on a risk-adjusted basis, we would favor it. If it is not, then structural measures remain justified. But we will not endorse vague severity for its own sake.

## To the state shareholder
Regional stability matters, but it cannot become a veto over competitiveness. The state’s best role is to support a solution that preserves what is economically defensible while allowing real internal adjustment where needed. “No closures ever” is too rigid if it prevents a durable turnaround.

## To the family holding
We agree that the company cannot afford a weak compromise. We also agree that symbolic closures would be bad governance if a serious alternative exists. But the alternative must be **quantified and enforceable**, not just politically attractive.

## Our position
We will support a restructuring only if it delivers:
- **material and durable cost reduction**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **credible margin improvement**
- **enforceable implementation**
- **no cosmetic deal**

We will **not** support:
- a settlement that preserves the current economics,
- an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired,
- or open-ended conflict that destroys value.
