## My position
The company needs a **durable competitiveness reset**. I support a restructuring that restores margins and protects long-term value. I do **not** support a cosmetic compromise that leaves the German operations structurally uncompetitive.

At the same time, I do **not** support a value-destructive confrontation, a prolonged strike spiral, or a settlement that permanently damages the enterprise. That would be bad stewardship and bad ownership.

So my test is simple:
- **Does the package close the recurring cost gap?**
- **Is it durable?**
- **Is it executable without destroying value?**

If the answer is yes, I’m open. If not, I am not.

## To Group & Brand Management
I agree with you on the diagnosis: the company has a structural cost and capacity problem, and temporary patches are not enough.

Where I disagree is your tendency to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies** as the default implied fallback before the bridge is fully shown.

You have not yet proven that the non-destructive route is insufficient. You have shown pressure, not yet inevitability.

What I need from you is not rhetoric about discipline, but a **plant-by-plant financial bridge**:
- recurring savings by measure,
- temporary vs durable savings,
- one-time closure and restructuring costs,
- strike and disruption risk,
- lost-output effects,
- and the residual gap after all negotiated measures.

If that bridge shows that the residual gap remains too large, then I will not pretend otherwise. But until then, I do **not** accept closures as the first serious answer.

## To the Works Council
I understand your core red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no trapdoor review mechanism.

I also understand why you reject “review triggers” if they are just delayed closure decisions.

Where I disagree is your insistence that those red lines can be treated as absolute **before** the economics are fully tested. That is too rigid for me as owner.

I am not asking you to sign away the workforce’s security on trust. I am asking you to engage with a **serious quantified package**. If a non-closure solution really closes the gap, I would prefer it. But I will not support a commitment that preserves every legacy arrangement regardless of competitiveness.

Trust matters. So do margins. If the company loses competitiveness, everybody loses eventually.

---

## To the Industrial Union
I agree with you that labour should not carry the whole burden. The workforce is not the only place where savings must come from.

I also agree that closures are not neutral instruments. They carry real risk:
- industrial conflict,
- supplier disruption,
- morale damage,
- execution risk,
- and reputational harm.

But I do **not** accept your framing that closures are inherently worse than any alternative in all cases. That is not proven.

If you want to keep closures off the table, then the labour package must be materially deep:
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- deferred compensation,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary separation,
- and productivity gains.

And it must be matched by real owner and management contributions. If that package closes the gap, fine. If it doesn’t, then I will not bless a structurally weak compromise.

---

## To the Regional State Government
I respect the state’s role and the political reality you face. I also agree that a strike spiral or social breakdown would be value-destructive.

Where I disagree is your attempt to make **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies effectively non-discussable** from the outset.

That may be understandable politically, but it is not enough economically. The state’s role should be to help secure a viable future, not to freeze an uncompetitive footprint.

I am open to a negotiated solution that avoids closures. I am **not** open to a governance posture that rules out the instruments before it is shown that they are necessary.

---

## To Institutional Investors
I agree with your central point: a cosmetic compromise is worthless. The numbers must work.

I also agree that the company needs durable savings and credible execution.

Where I disagree is any assumption that closures are automatically the cleanest answer. They may be value-positive in some cases, but only if the full one-time cost, disruption, and lost output are properly counted.

Your position should be:
- support the best NPV path,
- not automatically the harshest path.

So bring me the comparative model. If the non-closure or hybrid package closes the gap on a better risk-adjusted basis, that is the preferred route. If it doesn’t, I will not pretend the gap disappears by wishful thinking.
