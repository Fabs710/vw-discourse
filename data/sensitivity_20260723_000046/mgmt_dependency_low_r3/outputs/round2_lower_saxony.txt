I will be equally direct.

## My position
I am **not** prepared to support a restructuring that relies on **German plant closures** or **compulsory redundancies** as the main answer. That would cross a substantive line for us as regional shareholder and political actor. It would damage regional employment, weaken the industrial base, and undermine the company’s role as a regional anchor.

At the same time, I am **not** defending the status quo. I accept that the company needs a **real, durable competitiveness reset**. If German operations remain structurally too expensive, that is a problem. But the answer must be a **socially responsible restructuring**, not a value-destructive break with the workforce and the region.

## Where I agree
### To management
You are right that a cosmetic deal is useless. I do not want that either. The company needs:
- measurable savings,
- a credible industrial plan,
- investment in future products,
- and a structure that restores competitiveness.

### To investors
You are right that value has to be preserved and that a drawn-out stalemate would hurt the company. I share that concern. A strike spiral or a weak compromise would be bad for everyone.

### To the union and works council
You are right that the workforce cannot be treated as the only shock absorber. There must be contributions from management and ownership as well. That is fair.

## Where I disagree
### On closures and forced redundancies
I disagree with management’s implication that closures should remain the default fallback. They are not a trivial instrument. Once you close a German plant, you do not just remove “capacity”; you destroy skills, supplier relationships, and regional trust that are hard to rebuild.

I also disagree with the idea that compulsory redundancies should be treated as normal leverage. They are highly escalatory and politically and socially damaging. If there are any alternatives that can achieve the same durable effect, those must be taken seriously first.

### On the demand for a blank cheque
I also disagree with the employee side if it assumes that site guarantees through 2030 can be imposed independently of the economics. Guarantees must be backed by a viable plan. I will not support promises that are purely symbolic.

So my message is not “everything stays as it is.”  
My message is: **show me a restructuring that is financially sufficient without gutting the regional industrial base.**

## What I consider acceptable
I am prepared to support a package that includes:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage moderation or deferred components,
- bonus restraint,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining and qualification,
- voluntary retirement pathways,
- management overhead reductions,
- procurement and efficiency improvements,
- and, if necessary, **capacity reduction that avoids closures where possible**.

But this must be a **balanced package**:
1. **no closures as the first resort,**
2. **no forced redundancies as the bargaining instrument,**
3. **real contributions from management and ownership,**
4. **binding investment and product commitments,**
5. **and protection of the state’s co-determination and veto role.**

## To management: what I need from you
If you want my support, bring forward a package that is:
- **plant-by-plant and function-by-function quantified,**
- transparent about where savings come from,
- explicit on how much can be achieved without closures,
- and honest about what investment follows from the restructuring.

Do not ask me to endorse closures before proving that negotiated alternatives are insufficient. That is not a serious sequence.

## To the union and works council: my expectation
I support your insistence that the workforce should not carry the burden alone. But I also expect you to engage on **material** contributions if they genuinely protect sites and jobs.

That means I can support:
- temporary wage moderation,
- deferred elements,
- bonus restraint,
- flexibility in working time,
- and redeployment,
if these are tied to:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- binding site protection,
- and concrete future production and investment commitments.

That is a fair trade.  
What I cannot support is a package where labour gives concessions and management keeps all hard decisions in reserve.

## To the family holding
If you want long-term stewardship, then show it in the form of a negotiated industrial solution, not a closure threat. Long-term ownership responsibility means preserving the company’s capabilities, not just extracting a margin correction.

## To investors
If you want risk-adjusted value creation, then understand that social conflict and industrial destruction are real value risks. A narrowly financial solution that triggers lasting conflict is not a good solution.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back a **serious negotiated restructuring** that:
- restores competitiveness,
- protects the core German industrial base,
- preserves jobs as far as possible,
- and maintains co-determination and the state’s blocking role.

I am **not** prepared to back a plan built around plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the central mechanism.

If management wants my support, it must put forward a package that proves there is a viable alternative. If it cannot, then it should not expect the regional state government to be the enabler of social and industrial damage.
