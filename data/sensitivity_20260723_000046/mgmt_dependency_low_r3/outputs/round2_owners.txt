I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the investors, and the state on one central point: **this company needs a real structural reset, not a symbolic compromise**. If the German footprint remains permanently too expensive, then the enterprise will keep bleeding competitiveness and value. That is not acceptable for us as owners.

I also agree with the employee side that **a prolonged confrontation would be destructive**. A strike spiral, reputational damage, and a broken labour relationship would hurt the company far beyond this round of restructuring. That risk is real, and it matters.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** support turning “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute, unconditional vetoes before the numbers are fully tested.

That position may be understandable from the workforce perspective, but from an ownership perspective it is not enough to say, “We do not like the hard options.” If the economics require structural capacity reduction, then the company cannot be forced to preserve an uncompetitive structure simply because it is painful to change.

At the same time, I do **not** support management treating closures and forced redundancies as the default answer. That would be too blunt and too risky. A value-destructive conflict or a rushed closure program could destroy more value than it creates.

## What I want to see
Before anyone locks themselves into red lines, I want a **fully quantified restructuring case**:
- how much savings are required,
- what can be delivered through working-time flexibility, redeployment, voluntary measures, procurement, management overhead, and other internal efficiencies,
- what durable labour-cost contribution is realistic,
- and what capacity reduction is still needed after that.

That is the right sequence. Not slogans. Not moral posturing. Numbers.

## My position on the current dispute
### To management
You have my support for a serious competitiveness agenda. But support is not blank cheque support. If you want the owners behind you, then you must show:
- a credible industrial logic,
- not just a closure logic,
- and a package that restores margins without igniting a long, destructive conflict.

I will not back a settlement that is “fast” but value-destructive, or one that pretends the problem is solved while leaving the cost base structurally impaired.

### To the works council and union
I hear your point that the workforce should not be the sole shock absorber. I agree. But I will not accept a position that effectively says the company must absorb all current structures and still become competitive.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward an alternative that is **material, durable, and sufficient**. That means real savings, not just deferred pain:
- working-time changes,
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and, if needed, other negotiated contributions.

But it must close the gap in the numbers. If it does not, then you are asking the company to pay for a future it cannot finance.

### To the state government
I respect the regional responsibility here, but I cannot endorse a governance stance that effectively makes every plant untouchable. The company must remain capable of investing, innovating, and earning its cost of capital. That sometimes requires hard choices. If the state blocks all meaningful structural change, it may preserve appearances while weakening the company’s future.

### To investors
I agree that cosmetic compromise is not enough. But I also reject any idea that value creation is achieved simply by forcing through closures and calling it discipline. If the outcome triggers a major strike or lasting labour warfare, that is not value creation — that is value destruction with a spreadsheet attached.

## What can move me
I am prepared to support a negotiated package if it delivers all of the following:
1. **durable reduction in the cost base,**
2. **real capacity adjustment,**
3. **measurable workforce contribution,**
4. **credible investment and product commitments,**
5. **and a stable industrial peace that does not destroy value.**

If the employee side can show that these goals can be met **without closures**, that is preferable. If not, then we should not pretend there is no hard decision to be made.

## What will not move me
What I will not support is:
- a permanent no-closure rule regardless of economics,
- compulsory guarantees that ignore the business case,
- a cosmetic compromise that preserves the status quo,
- or a closure-driven rush that creates a costly and destabilising fight.

## Bottom line
My position is not “close plants at any cost.”  
My position is: **restore competitiveness, protect long-term value, and avoid self-inflicted instability**.

If management, labour, and the state can produce a package that is financially sufficient and socially stable, I will support it. If the package is built on denial, or on forcing through a destructive confrontation, I will not.
