I will answer directly.

## To management
You keep repeating “structural necessity,” but you still have not shown that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only way to achieve it. That is the key issue.

I **accept** that the company needs a durable solution and that the cost base must come down. I **do not accept** that the workforce should be the default reservoir for savings while management, ownership, and other cost centers are protected.

If you want the union to consider concessions, then bring a **fully quantified restructuring package** that shows, plant by plant and function by function:

- the exact savings required,
- what working-time measures can deliver,
- what can be achieved through redeployment and qualification,
- what management and overhead reductions are on the table,
- what owner-side contribution is on the table,
- and what investment and product commitments come in return.

Right now, you are asking us to accept closures and layoffs as a threat in advance. That is not a serious bargaining basis.

## To the Works Council
I share your red lines on the essentials:
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no German plant closures,**
- **no dismantling of co-determination,**
- and no one-sided transfer of risk onto employees.

That said, we should remain precise: workforce contributions can be discussed if they are **temporary, negotiated, and matched by binding guarantees**. I support that. What I do not support is any package where employees give up wages or flexibility and management later says the guarantees were only “conditional” or “aspirational.”

So yes, we are aligned on the core boundary: if the deal does not protect jobs and sites, then it is not a deal.

## To the state government
Your position is useful only if it is converted into hard leverage in governance. We need you to do more than express concern. We need you to make clear that you will not back any plan involving:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced redundancies,
- or a restructuring that destroys regional industrial capacity.

If the state is serious about long-term stewardship, then it should support a negotiated alternative that preserves the industrial base and gives the company a future without social dumping.

## To the family holding
You say you want long-term value. Then long-term value means preserving the workforce, skills, and industrial capabilities that make this company what it is. A plan that relies on breaking trust and driving through closures may look efficient on paper, but it risks years of conflict, strikes, and reputational damage.

If you want the union to help stabilize the company, then ownership must also show willingness to contribute materially. That means real acceptance of:
- wage moderation or deferred components,
- limits on bonus payouts,
- a contribution from capital,
- and a binding commitment to future production in Germany.

## To investors
You are right about one thing: cosmetic compromise is not enough. We are not offering one.

But you are wrong if you think value creation comes from forcing through closures and dismissing the social and industrial consequences. A prolonged conflict, warning strikes, and a broken labour relationship are not “execution risk” in the abstract — they are direct value destruction.

If you want a credible path to margin recovery, then support a package that is:
- financially meaningful,
- socially balanced,
- and durable.

That means savings from **all sides**, not just labour.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We remain open to a negotiated package that includes:
- temporary wage moderation or deferred components,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary retirement models,
- and capacity adjustments that do **not** rely on closures or forced layoffs.

But this must be tied to:
1. **no German plant closures,**
2. **no compulsory redundancies,**
3. **binding site and employment guarantees,**
4. **preserved collective-bargaining standards,**
5. **and concrete management/owner contributions.**

## What we will not accept
Let me be explicit:
- We will **not** accept a deal built on fear of closures.
- We will **not** accept compulsory redundancies.
- We will **not** accept a restructuring where workers bear the pain alone.
- We will **not** accept an open-ended erosion of the collective wage framework.

If management cannot come forward with a balanced package that protects jobs and sites, then the conflict will escalate. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to use the leverage we have. We prefer a negotiated settlement, but not at any price.

## Bottom line
There is a path to agreement:
- shared sacrifice,
- management and owner contributions,
- binding guarantees,
- and a serious industrial plan.

If that path is rejected, then the union will not be the party that keeps pretending this can be solved by squeezing employees harder.
