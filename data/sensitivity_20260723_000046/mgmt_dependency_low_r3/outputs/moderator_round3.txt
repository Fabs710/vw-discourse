## Core impasse

The dispute has narrowed to one central fault line:

**Can the company close the recurring cost gap without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**

Everything else now hangs off that question.

More specifically, the parties are deadlocked on four linked issues:

1. **Threshold of sufficiency**
   - Labour-side actors say a non-destructive package is the only acceptable basis.
   - Management and investors say that may be insufficient if the gap is large.
   - The expert says both views are plausible, depending on the size of the gap.

2. **Role of closures and forced redundancies**
   - Works council, union, and regional state government treat them as unacceptable tools.
   - Management and investors treat them as potential fallback instruments if savings are otherwise insufficient.

3. **Nature of guarantees**
   - Labour-side actors want binding site and job protection through 2030.
   - Management and investors say unconditional guarantees may freeze an uncompetitive structure.
   - The state and family want stability, but not at the expense of viability.

4. **Timing and sequencing**
   - Labour-side actors reject trigger-based escalation as a backdoor closure mechanism.
   - Management and investors want some conditionality in case initial measures do not fully close the gap.

So the main impasse is not “restructuring versus no restructuring.”
It is **whether restructuring can be made credible without using closures/forced layoffs as an explicit fallback**.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise position 1: Quantified non-closure first, with bounded review
**Proposal framing:**  
Start with a fully quantified non-closure package for a fixed initial period, paired with a jointly audited review at a defined milestone.

**Core elements**
- Immediate workforce and management measures:
  - working-time flexibility
  - temporary wage moderation / deferred components
  - bonus restraint
  - management overhead cuts
  - procurement savings
  - internal redeployment and retraining
  - voluntary exit / early retirement schemes
- Jointly verified savings bridge:
  - plant-by-plant and function-by-function
  - recurring vs one-time savings
  - transparent residual gap
- Review after a fixed period:
  - triggered only by jointly verified financial metrics
  - no automatic closure outcome
  - escalation must return to negotiation first

**Why it could bridge**
- Gives labour a real non-closure test first.
- Gives management/investors a structured way to revisit the numbers if the gap remains.
- Gives the state and family a governance channel short of immediate conflict.

---

### Compromise position 2: No closures / no forced redundancies, but deeper variable contributions and capacity adjustment by non-closure means
**Proposal framing:**  
A binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitment, matched by a deeper package of variable cost and capacity measures.

**Core elements**
- Binding commitments:
  - no German plant closures
  - no compulsory redundancies
  - preserved co-determination
- In exchange:
  - deeper temporary wage moderation
  - more substantial bonus restraint
  - longer-duration working-time flexibility
  - voluntary retirement / attrition targets
  - product allocation and line-shift changes
  - stronger owner and management contributions
- Independent financial test:
  - external modeler confirms whether the package closes the gap
  - if not, parties revisit non-destructive options only

**Why it could bridge**
- Protects the red lines of labour, state, and partly the family.
- Gives management and investors a chance to test whether the gap can be closed without closures.
- Forces the package to be serious enough to matter financially.

---

### Compromise position 3: Hybrid restructuring with limited structural adjustment short of closure
**Proposal framing:**  
Accept capacity reduction, but avoid plant closures and forced layoffs by using internal restructuring levers first.

**Core elements**
- Capacity reduction through:
  - shift reductions
  - line consolidation
  - product reallocation
  - automation / productivity investments
  - site specialization
- Workforce adjustments:
  - redeployment
  - qualification
  - voluntary exits
  - partial retirement
- Conditional safeguard:
  - if measurable targets are not met, parties return to the table before any further step

**Why it could bridge**
- Gives management some structural correction without triggering the most contested instruments.
- Gives labour a chance to preserve sites and jobs in adapted form.
- May satisfy investors if the savings are credible and recurring.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact recurring savings gap remains after you quantify labor moderation, overhead cuts, procurement savings, redeployment, and owner-side contributions plant by plant?**

---

### To the Works Council
**Would you accept a time-limited, jointly audited non-closure package if the model shows it closes the recurring gap without German plant closures or forced redundancies?**

---

### To the Industrial Union
**What depth of temporary labor contribution are you prepared to put on the table if it is matched by binding owner, management, and investment commitments?**

---

### To the Institutional Investors
**What minimum recurring savings level, after all non-destructive measures, would you regard as sufficient to rule out closures?**

---

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you commit to backing only those restructuring options that explicitly exclude German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, even if that limits management’s fallback options?**

---

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to underwrite a non-closure solution with measurable reinvestment and product commitments, even if that means accepting lower short-term margin recovery?**

---

### To the Independent Restructuring Expert
**Can you produce a side-by-side model showing the NPV, one-time cash cost, and disruption risk of: non-closure, hybrid capacity reduction, and closure scenarios under the same assumptions?**
