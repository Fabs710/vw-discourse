I agree with the Works Council on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not negotiable for us either.

Management cannot expect the workforce to pay for every strategic error while ownership and the top layer protect themselves. If the company wants a restructuring that is socially acceptable and industrially durable, then the burden must be shared fairly. That means:

- **no closure agenda disguised as “capacity optimization,”**
- **no forced layoffs,**
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework,**
- and **real commitments from management and owners** on investment, future products, and site security.

We are open to difficult but fair measures if they are negotiated properly. That includes things like:
- temporary wage moderation or deferred components,
- waiving bonuses,
- shorter working time,
- internal mobility,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and possibly a limited workforce contribution if it is part of a balanced package.

But I want to be very clear: **the workforce will not accept being the sole shock absorber**. If management is asking for labour cost reductions and capacity cuts, then the company leadership, the owners, and the capital side must also contribute materially. There has to be something concrete on the other side: binding guarantees, investment decisions, and a credible industrial plan for the German sites.

If management insists on plant closures or compulsory redundancies, we will not treat that as a starting point for bargaining. We will treat it as an attack on the workforce and respond accordingly. The peace obligation is over, and our members expect us to use the leverage we have.

So our position is straightforward:
**shared sacrifice, negotiated solution, site security, no forced job destruction.**  
Anything else is unacceptable.
