As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear as well.

I **do** agree with management on one point: the company cannot ignore structural competitiveness problems. If the German operations remain permanently too expensive, that eventually puts jobs, investment, and the company’s long-term role in the region at risk.

But I **do not** accept the idea that the only credible answer is plant closures or broad compulsory redundancies. That would be a failure of strategy, not a serious restructuring plan. It would also ignore the company’s responsibility as an industrial anchor in the region.

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a short-term patch.
- A restructuring must include **real cost improvements** and **credible investment commitments**.
- The workforce cannot be asked to carry the entire burden alone.
- Any solution must be judged on whether it secures the company’s future, not just this year’s margin.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with the framing that closures are somehow the default benchmark for seriousness.

Germany is not just a cost item on a spreadsheet. These plants are part of a regional industrial ecosystem, with skilled labour, supplier networks, and public infrastructure built around them. If you close plants, you do not simply “adjust capacity” — you destroy capabilities that are difficult to rebuild, and you create long-term damage well beyond the company itself.

So my position is:

- **No agreement that cuts through plant closures in Germany as the first resort.**
- **No forced layoffs as a bargaining threat.**
- **No restructuring that weakens co-determination or sidelines the state’s blocking role.**

Those are not tactical preferences. They are substantive limits.

## What I would consider
I am open to a negotiated restructuring if it is built on:
- capacity reductions **without closures where possible**,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- temporary labour-cost moderation,
- management and owner contributions,
- and above all **binding investment and product commitments for the German sites**.

If management wants sacrifices from the workforce, then the company must put something durable on the table:
- clear industrial commitments,
- a transparent business case,
- and protection for the core sites and employment base.

## To the union and works council
I support your insistence that the workforce cannot be the sole shock absorber. That is the right principle.

At the same time, I would urge both of you to engage constructively on **reversible and negotiated adjustments** if they genuinely help secure the sites. If there is a package that preserves the plants, protects employment as far as possible, and restores competitiveness, then that is the path I would back.

## Bottom line
I am **not** prepared to endorse a restructuring built around plant closures in Germany.  
I **am** prepared to support a serious negotiated alternative that preserves regional industrial capacity, protects co-determination, and puts the company on a sustainable footing.

If management wants my support, it needs to come back with a plan that meets those conditions.
