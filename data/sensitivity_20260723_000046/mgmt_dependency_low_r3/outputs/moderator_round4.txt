Core impasse, as it stands:

1. **Management / investors** are asking for a structurally durable cost and capacity reset, but have not yet provided a fully comparable model showing why non-closure measures are insufficient.
2. **Works council / union / state** are treating **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies** as non-negotiable red lines, and reject any review mechanism that could reopen closures later.
3. **The dispute is therefore no longer mainly about intent**; it is about whether a non-destructive package can close the recurring gap with enough durability and credibility to satisfy the balance-sheet and competitiveness requirements.

The deadlock is concentrated in one question:
**Can the recurring savings gap be closed without plant closures, and if not, what kind of structural adjustment is acceptable without crossing the employee and state red lines?**

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Verified non-closure package with a hard information test
A first-stage package would include:
- working-time flexibility
- temporary wage moderation / deferred compensation
- bonus restraint
- management overhead cuts
- procurement savings
- redeployment / retraining
- voluntary exits

This would be paired with:
- a **plant-by-plant financial bridge**
- independent verification
- a fixed timeline
- and a binding rule that **closure options cannot be triggered automatically by the review**

Purpose: to test whether the gap can be closed without destructive measures before any structural fallback is discussed.

---

### Proposal 2: No closures / no compulsory redundancies, but deeper employer-side and ownership-side contributions
This compromise would preserve the employee red lines while requiring:
- measurable management pay and overhead restraint
- owner-side financial support or reinvestment
- product allocation commitments
- capex commitments
- internal redeployment and capacity optimization

Purpose: to make the non-closure package materially deeper and more credible, so the burden is not shifted only to labor.

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid restructuring without plant closure
If the gap remains too large for a pure non-closure deal, a middle path could allow:
- line consolidation
- shift reductions
- site specialization
- product reallocation
- voluntary exits
- selective asset rationalization

With explicit limits:
- no German plant closure
- no compulsory redundancies
- no disguised closure trigger through review

Purpose: to permit structural cost reduction while staying short of full site shutdowns.
