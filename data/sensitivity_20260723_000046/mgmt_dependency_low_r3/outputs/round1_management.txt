Let me be equally clear: I hear the red lines you are drawing, but I cannot accept a package that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

We are facing a hard reality:
- German factory costs are far above target,
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we do nothing structural, we will not protect jobs — we will simply delay a much bigger crisis.

So, on the core points:

### Where I agree
I agree that this must be negotiated and that the workforce cannot be treated as the only party making sacrifices. I also agree that investment, product commitments, and transparency matter. A restructuring that only cuts costs without a credible industrial plan is not good enough.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept a blanket exclusion of plant closures, and I do **not** accept a hard “no compulsory redundancies” position as an absolute starting point if there is no sustainable alternative.

Why? Because management is not asking for cosmetic change. We need a **durable, structural reduction in cost and capacity**. If that can be achieved through other measures, I am open to them. But if those alternatives do not deliver enough, then we cannot rule out closures or job reductions simply because they are difficult.

Likewise, long-term site and job guarantees through 2030 are not credible if the cost base remains uncompetitive. Guarantees without a viable business model are just postponing the problem.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am prepared to consider:
- working-time measures,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- pay moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- and other negotiated labour-cost contributions,

**but only if they add up to a genuine structural cost reset**.

That means:
1. **clear and measurable cost reductions,**
2. **a permanent capacity adjustment,**
3. **a real workforce contribution,**
4. **and a package that restores margins and funds future investment.**

### What is not acceptable
What I will not accept is a deal that preserves every current structure, promises everything, and achieves savings only on paper. That would be a cosmetic compromise, and it would fail the company.

### My position
I am not coming here to threaten for effect. I am saying plainly that the company needs a solution that changes the economics of the German operations. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it has to bring forward a package that delivers comparable durable savings. If it cannot, then we will have to pursue deeper structural measures.

So I invite you to come back with something that is:
- concrete,
- quantifiable,
- and sufficient to close the competitiveness gap.

Anything less will not solve the problem.
