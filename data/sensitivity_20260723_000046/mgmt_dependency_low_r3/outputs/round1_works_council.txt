As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and abandoning entire sites. A company of this size has alternatives: capacity adjustments, shifts in working time, negotiated flexibility, internal redeployment, training, and investment commitments. But these must be real alternatives, not a cover for unilateral cuts.

Our priorities are:

1. **Every German plant must remain open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework.**
5. **A restructuring plan that is negotiated, fair, and socially responsible.**

If management wants contributions from the workforce, then it must offer something in return: credible long-term product and investment commitments, full transparency on the business case, and binding guarantees for sites and employment. Any savings must be achieved through agreement, not through threats of closure.

We are prepared to discuss solutions that protect the company’s future and the workforce’s future together. But if the proposal is built on closing plants or forcing workers out, then we will resist it with all the means available to us.
