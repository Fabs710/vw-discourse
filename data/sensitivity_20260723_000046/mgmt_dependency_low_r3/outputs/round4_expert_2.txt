## Relative merits of the three restructuring paths

### 1) Non-closure package
Typical measures:
- working-time flexibility
- temporary wage moderation / deferred compensation
- bonus restraint
- redeployment and retraining
- voluntary exits / early retirement
- management overhead cuts
- procurement savings
- productivity improvements

**Financial strengths**
- Lowest one-time cash cost
- Lowest disruption risk
- Usually fastest to negotiate if there is trust
- Preserves operational continuity

**Financial weaknesses**
- Some savings are temporary or reversible
- Savings ceiling may be too low if overcapacity is structural
- May not fully fix fixed-cost burden

**Best case**
- Moderate cost gap
- Overcapacity is more labor-linked than site-linked
- Management can deliver real overhead and procurement reductions

**Main risk**
- Package looks large on paper but does not produce durable recurring savings

---

### 2) Hybrid capacity reduction
Typical measures:
- shift reduction
- line consolidation
- product allocation changes
- site specialization
- selective voluntary exits
- asset rationalization without full plant closure

**Financial strengths**
- More durable than a pure labor-concession package
- Can remove real structural cost without maximum disruption of full closure
- Often a better NPV compromise when fixed cost is the issue but closure is too costly

**Financial weaknesses**
- Requires implementation complexity
- May still involve sizable one-time costs
- Savings may be slower to realize
- Can leave stranded cost if the footprint is only partially reduced

**Best case**
- Structural overcapacity exists
- Full closures are too destructive or too difficult to execute
- The company needs permanent capacity correction, not just wage relief

**Main risk**
- A partial measure that neither closes the gap nor avoids the pain of structural change

---

### 3) Closure scenario
Typical measures:
- full plant shutdown
- major site exit
- workforce reduction
- asset impairment/write-down
- relocation of output
- supply-chain restructuring

**Financial strengths**
- Highest potential recurring savings
- Strongest direct reduction in fixed cost and overcapacity

**Financial weaknesses**
- Highest one-time cash cost
- Highest strike/disruption risk
- Highest lost-output risk
- Can create stranded costs elsewhere in the network
- Execution risk is significant in a co-determined setting with veto players

**Best case**
- A site is clearly structurally redundant
- Alternative measures are insufficient
- The recurring savings are large enough to overcome transition costs on an NPV basis

**Main risk**
- The closure destroys more value in transition than it saves in steady state

## NPV framework
The right comparison is:

**NPV = Present value of recurring savings  
minus one-time restructuring cash costs  
minus disruption / strike losses  
minus lost-output costs  
minus incremental capex / relocation costs**

### Important implications
- A closure can be financially inferior if:
  - one-time severance and shutdown costs are high
  - strikes or work stoppages are likely
  - ramp-down is slow
  - output is lost rather than moved
  - key products must be relocated at cost

- A non-closure package can be financially inferior if:
  - savings are mostly temporary
  - overcapacity remains
  - the company still carries a structurally high fixed-cost base
  - margin recovery remains too weak to fund future investment
