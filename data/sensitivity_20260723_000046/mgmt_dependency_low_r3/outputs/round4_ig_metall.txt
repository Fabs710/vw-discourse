I’ll respond directly.

## To the Works Council
We are aligned on the core red lines: **no compulsory redundancies, no German plant closures, and no trapdoor review model** that only delays the same outcome. I support your refusal to let “review triggers” become a disguised closure mandate.

Where I would sharpen the line is this: we should not let management redefine “capacity adjustment” as a euphemism for layoffs. Any capacity measure must be **non-destructive**, transparent, and jointly verified. If it is not, it is not a restructuring tool — it is a threat.

I also agree that the workforce should not be asked to give up wage and job security first while management keeps the right to break promises later. That sequencing is unacceptable.

## To Group & Brand Management
You keep saying the numbers may require closures if the gap is large. Fine — then **show the gap** in a plant-by-plant, function-by-function model.

Right now, you are asking the workforce to concede first on the basis of a possibility, not a proven necessity. That is not a bargaining position; it is pressure.

What I want to see, concretely:
- the recurring gap after all non-destructive measures,
- the contribution from management overhead cuts,
- procurement savings,
- executive restraint,
- product allocation changes,
- working-time measures,
- redeployment,
- and owner-side contribution,
- plus the one-time and disruption costs of any closure scenario.

Until that is shown side by side, I do **not** accept closures as justified.

And let me be clear: if your model only works by breaking the collective wage framework and threatening sites, then the model is too narrow, not the workforce too expensive.

## To the Institutional Investors
You are right about one thing: cosmetic compromise is worthless.  
But you are wrong if you treat closures as neutral efficiency tools and employee security as a distortion.

A closure is not just a cost item. It brings:
- strike risk,
- supplier disruption,
- loss of skills,
- morale damage,
- execution risk,
- and long-term trust destruction.

Those are real costs. If your own standard is value creation, then you should not support a plan that destroys more value than it saves.

You ask what savings level would be enough. My answer remains: show the full bridge first. If the non-destructive package closes the recurring gap, that is the superior solution. If it does not, then management must prove that honestly — not use closure threats to force concessions upfront.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
You speak of stewardship, but stewardship is not compatible with using closure threats as bargaining leverage against the workforce.

If you want long-term value, then act like an owner who understands that the industrial base is not just machinery and capital. It is also trust, skills, and co-determination.

So I ask you plainly:
- Will you back a solution that keeps every German plant open?
- Will you support binding reinvestment and product commitments?
- Will you insist that management stops treating closures as the first resort?

If the answer is no, then “stewardship” is just a word for pressure.

## To the Regional State Government
Your line against closures and forced redundancies is correct, and I support it.

But it has to be made real in governance, not just stated in principle. That means:
- no endorsement of plant closures,
- no support for compulsory redundancies,
- no approval of a review framework that can later reopen the same outcome,
- and no acceptance of a settlement that shifts the whole burden onto labour.

If the state wants an industrial future, then it must defend the industrial base, not just call for balance while accepting irreversible damage.

Your earlier assessment was useful, but it did **not** prove closures are necessary.

You showed that non-closure measures can plausibly generate meaningful recurring savings — roughly in the range of **€150m to €370m annually** if deep enough. That is exactly why closures are not automatically justified.

What was missing was the full comparison of:
- NPV,
- one-time closure costs,
- strike and disruption risk,
- lost output,
- and long-term trust and execution effects.

So yes, I want the side-by-side comparison. But I will not accept a model that counts closure savings and ignores the social and operational destruction they create.

## To the moderator’s compromise lines

### 1) Quantified non-closure first, with bounded review
Not acceptable if “review” means a delayed closure trigger.  
A review is only legitimate if it cannot be used as a backdoor to the outcomes we have rejected.

### 2) No closures / no forced redundancies, but deeper variable contributions
This is the only serious direction. If management wants deeper labour contributions, then the company must offer real guarantees in return:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- binding product and investment commitments,
- and measurable owner/management contribution.

### 3) Hybrid restructuring with limited structural adjustment short of closure
If this means productivity, allocation, working-time, and voluntary instruments, that is discussable.  
If it means closures by another name, then no.

## What I am prepared to discuss
I remain prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage moderation,
- deferred compensation,
- bonus restraint,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary retirement,
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement savings,
- and plant-level productivity improvements.

But only within a framework that guarantees:
- every German plant remains open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- co-determination is preserved,
- and the collective wage framework is not broken.

## What I will not accept
Let me restate the boundary plainly:
- No plant closures.
- No forced redundancies.
- No disguised closure trigger through “review.”
- No one-sided sacrifice from labour.
- No bargaining under the shadow of a pre-decided closure.

## Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a serious restructuring.  
We are **not** ready to legitimise closures or forced layoffs as “realism” without proof.

If management, owners, and investors want agreement, they must bring a **fully quantified, comparable, and non-destructive package** first. Until then, the workforce will not give up job security and wage standards on trust alone.
