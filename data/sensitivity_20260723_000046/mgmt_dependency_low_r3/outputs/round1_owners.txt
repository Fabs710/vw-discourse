I will be direct.

I agree with management on the central diagnosis: **the current cost base and capacity are not sustainable**. If the company cannot restore margins, it will damage the entire group for years. That is unacceptable from an ownership perspective. We are not here to preserve every structure regardless of economics.

But I also agree with the union, the works council, and the state government on one essential point: **a value-destructive confrontation would be the worst possible outcome**. A prolonged strike, reputational damage, and a breakdown in trust would destroy value faster than any restructuring plan can restore it.

So my position is this:

## Where I agree
- The company needs **real structural improvement**, not a cosmetic exercise.
- Labour, management, and owners all have to contribute.
- Investment and product commitments matter, because cuts without a future model are pointless.
- Any solution must be durable, not just a temporary truce.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** support a package that simply rules out all plant closures and all compulsory redundancies as absolutes, regardless of the economics.

Those are understandable red lines in bargaining, but they cannot become a permanent veto on reality. If the business case demands structural change, then the question is not whether something is politically uncomfortable — it is whether it is necessary to protect the long-term enterprise.

At the same time, I do **not** support using closures as the first and only answer. That would be too blunt, too destabilising, and potentially value-destructive if there are serious alternatives.

## What I want to see
Before anyone claims there is no alternative, I expect management to present a **credible, quantified restructuring package** showing:
- what savings are needed,
- what can be achieved through working-time, redeployment, and labour-cost measures,
- what capacity adjustment is actually required,
- and why any proposed closures are unavoidable rather than convenient.

If there is a negotiated alternative that genuinely delivers comparable durable savings, protects competitiveness, and avoids unnecessary instability, I would regard that as preferable.

But I will be equally clear: **I will not back a settlement that preserves the status quo and postpones the problem.** That would erode the company’s value and ultimately weaken the very job security everyone wants to protect.

## On the workforce contribution
I think it is reasonable that the workforce contributes to the turnaround, but that contribution must be:
- **negotiated,**
- **temporary where possible,**
- **measurable,**
- and tied to concrete commitments on investment and future production.

I am open to:
- pay moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and retraining,
- and other negotiated measures.

I am not open to a pure cost-cutting exercise that strips the company of capability while leaving no credible industrial future behind.

## On ownership and governance
Let me also be explicit: **ownership control must not be weakened** by this process. The family holding is committed to the long-term health of the company, and that includes the governance structure that allows patient capital and long-term stewardship. Any restructuring that creates instability in control or invites permanent strategic drift would be unacceptable.

## Bottom line
My support goes to a **serious, evidence-based restructuring** that restores competitiveness and preserves long-term value.

I will not support:
- a symbolic compromise that changes nothing,
- a purely closure-driven solution chosen for speed,
- or a settlement that locks in uncompetitive costs under the banner of social peace.

If management can show that deeper cuts are truly unavoidable, then I will not pretend otherwise.  
If the workforce can bring forward a package that genuinely closes the competitiveness gap without destroying the enterprise, I will support that.  
But I will not reward denial, and I will not accept delay disguised as compromise.
