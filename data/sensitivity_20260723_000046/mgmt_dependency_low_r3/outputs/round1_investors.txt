We agree on the diagnosis more than on the remedies: the German operations need a **credible, durable reset** if margins are to recover and the share price is to reflect a viable business again.

Where we **agree**:
- Management is right that this cannot be a cosmetic exercise.
- The company’s cost base is clearly under pressure relative to competitors.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged confrontation that destroys value.
- Workforce contributions, if they are real and measurable, can be part of the answer.
- Investment and product commitments matter, because cuts without a future plan are not investable.

Where we **disagree**:
- We do **not** accept a restructuring that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.
- We do **not** support “no plant closures” or “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute red lines if they block a solution that is otherwise necessary to restore competitiveness.
- We do **not** support a package that preserves every site and every legacy guarantee while claiming to solve the margin problem.
- We also do **not** want a drawn-out strike or political stalemate that erodes operating performance and investor confidence.

Our position is straightforward from a capital-markets perspective: **what matters is risk-adjusted value creation**. If the company can achieve the required savings through working-time flexibility, redeployment, pay moderation, and other negotiated measures, that is preferable to closures because it reduces execution risk. But if those measures do **not** close the competitiveness gap, then the board must be prepared to take harder structural decisions.

So the test for us is simple:
1. **Are the savings large enough and durable enough?**
2. **Do they materially improve margins and return on sales?**
3. **Is the package credible enough to support investment and capital allocation?**
4. **Does it avoid prolonged conflict that destroys value?**

If the answer is yes, we can support it. If the answer is no, then we will not back a settlement that simply delays the necessary restructuring.

To the union and works council: we hear the concern about social impact, but investor support will not be bought with promises that preserve the status quo. To management: if you want backing, bring a package with real numbers, not just rhetoric about competitiveness. To the family and the state: long-term stewardship is only valuable if it delivers a company that can actually earn its cost of capital.

Bottom line: **we support a serious restructuring that restores competitive margins; we do not support a cosmetic compromise or a cost base that remains structurally impaired.**
