We agree with the core diagnosis: the company has a **structural competitiveness problem**, and a cosmetic compromise would be unacceptable. We also agree that **prolonged industrial conflict destroys value**. That part is clear.

Where we differ is on what counts as a credible solution.

## To management
You are right that the company needs **durable cost and capacity reduction**. We support that objective.

But you have not yet shown that **closures and compulsory redundancies are the only way** to get there. Saying “we need structural savings” is not enough. We need to see the economics:
- how much savings are required,
- how much can be achieved through non-closure measures,
- what capacity can be rationalized internally,
- and what headcount reduction is truly necessary.

If you push closures too hard without proving necessity, you risk a **value-destructive strike spiral** and a governance deadlock. That would damage margins, cash flow, and investor confidence. We will not support a strategy that creates industrial war just to demonstrate toughness.

## To the works council and union
We understand your refusal of closures and forced layoffs, and we agree that the burden cannot fall only on employees.

But your current stance — **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full guarantees through 2030** — is not sufficient unless it is paired with **real, measurable economics**. If it simply preserves the existing cost base and capacity base, then it is a freeze, not a restructuring. We cannot back that.

So if you want investor support, you need to bring a non-closure package that genuinely closes the competitiveness gap:
- material capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- meaningful headcount reduction,
- and clear investment commitments.

Temporary gestures are not enough.

## To the regional state government
We support your rejection of a reckless closure-first approach. But a blanket political veto over all significant adjustment is also not acceptable if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. The company cannot be insulated from economic reality indefinitely.

## To the controlling family owners
We agree with the priority of protecting long-term enterprise value. But value is also destroyed by **delay** and by a deal that merely postpones hard decisions. We need decisive action, but it must be **governable and economically real**, not a headline-driven confrontation.

## Our position
We will support **the solution that credibly restores competitive margins**.

That means:
- **yes** to durable restructuring,
- **yes** to serious cost reduction,
- **yes** to capacity rationalization,
- **yes** to headcount reduction if required,
- **yes** to investment commitments tied to savings,
- **no** to a cosmetic settlement,
- **no** to a freeze that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- **no** to a prolonged strike that destroys value.

## What we want next
We need a concrete comparison of scenarios:
1. **Site-preserving plan** — only acceptable if it delivers real savings and capacity reduction, not just promises.
2. **Mixed plan with limited rationalization** — potentially the most credible route if it achieves durable economics.
3. **Closure-inclusive plan** — acceptable only if management proves non-closure options cannot deliver comparable results.

Bottom line: we are not aligned with any maximalist position. We will back the package that **actually restores margins, protects enterprise value, and avoids a long destructive conflict**. If none of the proposals can do that, then the process has not yet produced a credible restructuring.
