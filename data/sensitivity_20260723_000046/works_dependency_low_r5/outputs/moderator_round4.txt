### Core impasse

The central impasse is **not over whether restructuring is needed**. Nearly everyone accepts that it is.

The impasse is over **the restructuring instrument**:

- **Works council, union, and state**: want restructuring **without German plant closures and without compulsory redundancies**.
- **Management, controlling family, and investors**: say they need **quantified, durable cost and capacity reduction**, and will not accept a solution that merely preserves the current footprint and cost base.
- The two sides therefore disagree on **whether non-closure measures can deliver sufficient savings**, and on **who must carry the burden if they cannot**.

A second, related impasse is **sequencing**:
- employee representatives want **binding site/job guarantees first**,
- management and capital want **quantified structural reductions first**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### 1) Time-bound non-closure restructuring with hard measurement points
A first-phase package could be built around:
- all German plants remaining open during phase 1,
- no compulsory redundancies in phase 1,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, working-time flexibility, and internal consolidation,
- a **fixed review date** with audited plant-by-plant numbers,
- and **predefined escalation into phase 2 only if agreed targets are missed**.

This bridges the gap by giving the employee side a genuine non-closure phase while giving management and investors a measurable test of whether it works.

#### 2) Site-preserving specialization model with differentiated roles
A second option is a binding footprint plan in which:
- all German plants stay open,
- each site receives a defined production or development role,
- capacity is reduced through specialization and internal reallocation,
- some volumes shift between sites,
- and investment is tied to each site’s assigned role.

This addresses management’s demand for real capacity change while staying within the no-closure/no-forced-redundancy red lines of the works council, union, and state.

#### 3) Temporary burden-sharing package paired with binding guarantees and shared contributions
A third option is a temporary contribution package that includes:
- time-limited wage or working-time concessions,
- matched contributions from management and owners,
- hard end-dates,
- and binding job/site guarantees through a defined horizon.

This would make employee contribution contingent on reciprocal concessions and give capital a clearer economic bridge without immediate structural rupture.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap remains after voluntary exits, redeployment, internal consolidation, specialization, and working-time measures are fully quantified on a plant-by-plant basis?

#### To the Works Council
Which specific non-closure measures are you prepared to accept as binding if they are paired with measurable savings and a fixed review mechanism?

#### To the Industrial Union
What level of temporary employee contribution would you consider acceptable if it is matched by management and owner contributions and protected by hard end-dates?

#### To the Regional State Government
If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what measurable economic conditions must a non-closure plan satisfy for you to support it?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you support a phase-1 non-closure restructuring if it includes audited savings targets, a strict review date, and automatic escalation only if those targets are missed?

#### To the Institutional Investors
What minimum quantified capacity and cost reductions would make a site-preserving plan credible enough for capital markets to support it?
