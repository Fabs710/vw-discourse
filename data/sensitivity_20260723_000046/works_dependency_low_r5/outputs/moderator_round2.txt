## 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not a cosmetic deal.
- All parties agree that **prolonged industrial conflict would be damaging**.
- There is broad support for some combination of:
  - investment in future products/technology,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - internal redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - and some form of burden-sharing.
- There is also shared recognition that any solution must be **durable** and economically credible.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and state reject German plant closures.  
  - Management, owners, and investors keep closures on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Labour representatives reject them.  
  - Management and capital-side actors consider them potentially necessary.
- **Scope of restructuring:**  
  - Labour side favors a **site-preserving** solution with guarantees.  
  - Management, owners, and investors argue that such a solution must still produce **substantial capacity, cost, and headcount reduction** to be credible.
- **Role of governance/politics:**  
  - The state insists its veto/co-determination role must be respected and used to prevent closures.  
  - Management and capital-side actors argue governance should not block economically necessary change.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings can realistically be achieved without closures or compulsory layoffs.**
- **What level of capacity reduction is acceptable without plant shutdowns.**
- **How far temporary wage/working-time concessions can go, and for how long.**
- **Whether voluntary exits, redeployment, and internal reorganization can cover the required headcount reduction.**
- **What binding investment commitments can be credibly financed and linked to restructuring.**
- **How to balance site/job protection against the need for competitiveness and margin recovery by 2030.**

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and scenarios**.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - the minimum acceptable savings,
  - the maximum acceptable capacity reduction without closures,
  - the feasible headcount reduction from voluntary/internal measures,
  - the scope and duration of any temporary employee contribution,
  - and the investment commitments tied to each scenario.
- Compare the three options explicitly:
  1. site-preserving plan,
  2. mixed plan with limited rationalization,
  3. closure-inclusive plan.
- Require a clear statement of **what each party would accept, and what it would not accept**, so the negotiation can test whether a non-closure package is economically viable.
