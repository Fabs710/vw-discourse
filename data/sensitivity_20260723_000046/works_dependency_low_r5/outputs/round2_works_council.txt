Management is still trying to frame this as if closures and forced layoffs are the only serious tools. They are not. They are the easiest way to destroy trust, trigger conflict, and gamble with the future of the company.

Let me be very clear again: **we will not accept German plant closures or compulsory redundancies.** That remains our red line.

## Where I agree
I agree with the basic diagnosis that the company needs a real restructuring and that a cosmetic solution is not enough. I also agree that we need investment, future products, qualification, and a path to competitiveness through 2030.

But that is exactly why management’s approach is wrong. If you damage the workforce, break the social contract, and hollow out the German industrial base, you do not create a competitive future — you weaken it.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You are still not presenting a credible non-closure alternative. You keep saying the cost base must come down, but you have not shown why that requires plant closure or forced layoffs.

A serious package must start from the premise that:
- all German plants stay open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- capacity is adjusted through working-time, redeployment, internal transfers, qualification, and voluntary measures,
- labour contributions are temporary, negotiated, and fairly shared,
- management and owners also contribute materially.

If you cannot make the numbers work without destroying sites and jobs, then the problem is not the workforce’s refusal — it is the inadequacy of your plan.

### To the family owners and investors
You keep speaking as if “value creation” were neutral. It is not neutral when the method is industrial destruction. A deal that may look neat on paper but tears apart the workforce and triggers years of conflict is not value-creating.

If you want competitiveness, then support a package that preserves industrial capacity in Germany and gives the workforce security to cooperate. If you back closures, you are choosing escalation and making the outcome less stable, not more.

### To the state government
We appreciate your clear line that closures and forced redundancies are not acceptable. But let me be equally clear: we do not need encouragement to be “more flexible” when the first demand from management is the dismantling of guarantees. Flexibility without security is just a code word for insecurity imposed on employees.

## Concrete position on the scenarios
### 1) Site-preserving plan
This is the only scenario we can seriously engage with.

It would need:
- all German plants remaining open,
- no forced redundancies,
- binding job and site guarantees through 2030,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit options only,
- temporary working-time and pay-related contributions only if clearly limited in time and matched by management/owner contributions,
- and binding investment in products and technology.

This is the minimum basis for discussion.

### 2) Mixed plan with limited rationalization
If “rationalization” means hidden closures, site erosion, or a backdoor to shutting down plants, then no. We will not sign up to that.

If it means temporary capacity adjustment, shift changes, and internal reorganization without plant closure or forced job cuts, then that belongs in the site-preserving plan — but only with guarantees.

### 3) Closure-inclusive plan
Not acceptable. Full stop.

A closure-inclusive plan violates the historic job-security framework and crosses the line for the workforce. We will resist it using all available co-determination rights and industrial means.

## What would be required for us to even continue talks
If management wants a real negotiation, then bring a concrete package with:
- written site guarantees through 2030,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- a transparent calculation of savings from non-closure measures,
- a fair contribution from management, owners, and workforce,
- and a binding investment program.

Without that, you are not negotiating — you are applying pressure.

## Final point
I also want to remind everyone: with co-determination and the governance rules here, a closure decision is not something management can simply force through. If you continue down that path, you are choosing confrontation you may not be able to win.

So I am putting the choice back where it belongs:

- **A fair restructuring with all German plants open and no forced layoffs**, or
- **a prolonged conflict that management will have brought on itself**.

We are ready for the first. We will fight the second.
