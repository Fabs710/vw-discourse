We share the works council’s core line: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable starting point for us.

Let me be equally clear: the workforce does **not** deny that the company is under real pressure. We know the market situation, the investment needs, and the competitiveness gap. But a restructuring that shifts the full burden onto employees — through closures, layoffs, wage cuts, and the dismantling of protections — is not a solution. It is a one-sided transfer of the crisis onto the people who built this company.

Our position is:

- **No closures**
- **No forced redundancies**
- **No destruction of the collective wage framework**
- **Binding site and job security**
- **A fair contribution from management, owners, and capital**
- **Temporary, negotiated measures only if they are genuinely shared and reversible**

We are prepared to talk about:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and other measures that preserve jobs and sites.

But those discussions only make sense if they are tied to **credible guarantees**. If management wants concessions from the workforce, then management and owners must also accept sacrifices. That means no slogans about “competitiveness” while asking employees alone to pay for the transformation.

To management: if you come with plant closures and mass job cuts as the first answer, you should expect resistance, including industrial action. That is not escalation for its own sake; it is the consequence of breaking trust.

To the works council: we are aligned on the red lines. We support a common front against closures and compulsory redundancies. Where a negotiated package can help secure the future of sites and jobs, we are open — but only on the basis of shared sacrifice and binding guarantees, not vague promises.

If there is a serious offer that protects jobs, preserves the collective agreement, and spreads the burden fairly, we will engage. If not, we are prepared to use our leverage.
