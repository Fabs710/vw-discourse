### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a real competitiveness and cost problem.
- There is broad agreement that a purely cosmetic or delay-based solution is not acceptable.
- There is general openness to negotiated measures such as:
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification and retraining,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary or reversible contributions,
  - and investment commitments for future products.
- Several parties agree that prolonged conflict would be value-destructive and should be avoided if possible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state reject them as a starting point.
  - Management says closures remain on the table and may be necessary.
  - Investors and family owners do not rule them out if economically justified.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee side and state reject them.
  - Management, investors, and family owners see them as potentially necessary if voluntary measures are insufficient.
- **Job and site guarantees**
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees through 2030.
  - Management says blanket guarantees are incompatible with restructuring.
  - Other parties are not endorsing full guarantees.
- **Scope and depth of restructuring**
  - Management/investors/family owners want material capacity and headcount reduction.
  - Employee side and state want restructuring without site destruction and with job protection.
- **Balance of burden**
  - Employee side insists the burden must not fall mainly on workers.
  - Management and capital-side actors want substantial labor-side contributions as part of any deal.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is actually needed**, and whether it can be achieved without closures.
- **Whether voluntary measures alone can deliver sufficient headcount reduction**, or whether compulsory measures are unavoidable.
- **What level of labor-cost relief is acceptable**, and whether it is temporary or structural.
- **What investment commitment would be credible enough to justify employee concessions.**
- **How governance constraints and political vetoes interact with economic restructuring needs.**
- **Whether there is a package that can both preserve key German sites and restore margins materially.**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to a **concrete package comparison**:
- Define the minimum acceptable savings/capacity reduction target.
- Test two or three restructuring scenarios:
  1. site-preserving plan,
  2. mixed plan with limited rationalization,
  3. closure-inclusive plan.
- For each scenario, specify:
  - job impact,
  - site impact,
  - labor contributions,
  - investment commitments,
  - timing,
  - and governance feasibility.
- Aim to identify whether a non-closure alternative can meet the required economics, or whether the parties are still too far apart for a durable deal.
