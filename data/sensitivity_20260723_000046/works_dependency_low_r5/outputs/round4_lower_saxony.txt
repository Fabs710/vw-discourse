I will be direct.

## My position
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I **cannot support German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**. That remains the line. It is not just politics; it is about protecting the company as a regional economic anchor, preserving co-determination, and avoiding lasting damage to employment, supplier networks, and industrial stability in the state.

At the same time, I do **not** support a frozen status quo. The company needs a real restructuring. But it must be a restructuring that is:
- economically credible,
- socially durable,
- and compatible with site protection and co-determination.

## Where I agree
### With management
You are right about the diagnosis:
- the cost gap is real,
- capacity and margins need adjustment,
- and a cosmetic deal would be worthless.

I also agree that prolonged conflict would be harmful. No one benefits from a strike spiral.

### With the family holding and investors
You are right that a weak, symbolic compromise would be irresponsible. The company must restore competitiveness and investment capacity.

### With the works council and union
You are right that workers cannot be made the sole shock absorber. Any employee contribution must be:
- limited,
- fair,
- and matched by contributions from management and owners.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You still have **not** shown that closures and compulsory redundancies are unavoidable.

You keep saying the numbers demand “durable structural change,” but that is not the same as proving that German sites must close. Before you ask for closures, I need a plant-by-plant comparison showing:
- utilization,
- product allocation,
- investment requirements,
- savings from internal reorganization,
- savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and the residual gap after all non-closure measures.

Until then, closures are a choice, not a necessity.

And I will say this plainly: a closure-led path is not low-risk. It risks strikes, political escalation, and long-term damage to the company’s legitimacy in Germany. That is not responsible restructuring.

### To the works council and union
I support your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But your position must also be operational, not just defensive.

A non-closure solution must still deliver:
- real capacity reduction,
- productivity gains,
- internal consolidation,
- and a credible investment path.

If it is only “keep everything as it is,” then it will not be enough. I am not asking for a freeze; I am asking for a serious site-preserving restructuring.

### To the family holding and investors
I agree that economics matter. But preserving German sites is not anti-economic by definition.

A deal that protects industrial capacity, avoids mass layoffs, and restores margins through negotiated change can be the more durable solution in a co-determined company with blocking minorities and a powerful workforce.

I do not accept the idea that social acceptability and competitiveness are mutually exclusive. They are both required.

## My view on the scenarios

### 1) Site-preserving plan
This is the only direction the state can seriously support.

But it must be real restructuring:
- all German plants remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- voluntary exits and internal transfers first,
- qualification and retraining,
- working-time flexibility,
- targeted productivity measures,
- and a fair, temporary employee contribution if management and owners also contribute materially.

It must also include a binding investment plan for future products and technology.

### 2) Mixed plan with limited rationalization
This is acceptable only if “rationalization” means:
- internal consolidation,
- site specialization,
- shift reorganization,
- lower volumes at some sites,
- and better capacity utilization,

without closures and without forced redundancies.

If it is a euphemism for a backdoor shutdown path, then no.

### 3) Closure-inclusive plan
Not acceptable.

I am not prepared to endorse a solution that guts regional employment or weakens the company’s role as an industrial anchor in the state.

## What would move me
What would move me is not rhetoric about inevitability. It is a credible non-closure package with hard numbers:
- quantified savings,
- quantified capacity reduction,
- quantified headcount adjustment through voluntary/internal measures,
- a temporary and fair burden-sharing arrangement,
- and binding investment commitments.

If management can show that such a package closes the competitiveness gap, then we have a basis for agreement.

If it cannot, then the burden is on management to explain why every internal tool has been exhausted before asking for stronger measures.

## My bottom line
I remain open to a negotiated solution, but only within these limits:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **yes to real restructuring**
- **yes to shared burden**
- **yes to investment for the future**
- **yes to preserving co-determination and the state’s governance role**

If the next step is serious, bring the numbers and a non-closure package that can actually work. If that cannot be done, then the obstacle is not the state.
