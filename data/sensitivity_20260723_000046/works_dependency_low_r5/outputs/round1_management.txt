We hear the red lines from the works council and the union, and we do not dismiss the concern behind them. But I need to be equally clear: **the company cannot continue with a cost base that is structurally uncompetitive**. If we protect every site, every legacy guarantee, and every current employment level unchanged, then we are not solving the crisis — we are postponing it until the damage is worse.

A few points directly:

- **I do not accept that “no closures” can be the starting point.** We are facing overcapacity in Germany. If the board is forced to preserve all plants regardless of economics, then we will not restore competitiveness.
- **I do not accept that there can be no compulsory workforce reduction at all.** Voluntary exits and redeployment can contribute, but they are unlikely to be sufficient on their own for the scale of adjustment required.
- **I do accept that a cosmetic deal would be unacceptable.** We need structural savings, not temporary optics.

That said, I am not here to announce a one-line closure decree. We need a **durable package** that can actually pass governance and hold up operationally. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring a credible alternative that delivers the same effect on cost and capacity. That means, concretely:

1. **A substantial labour-cost contribution**
   - not just symbolic restraint,
   - but real, measurable cost relief,
   - including flexibility on pay, working time, and legacy commitments.

2. **A binding capacity reduction**
   - whether through plant rationalisation, line consolidation, or closure,
   - but not a solution that leaves excess capacity untouched.

3. **A meaningful reduction in headcount**
   - through a mix of voluntary and, if necessary, compulsory measures,
   - because the current structure is too expensive.

4. **A credible investment commitment**
   - because savings are not the end goal,
   - they are there to fund future products and technology to 2030.

On the issue of guarantees: I will say this plainly. **A blanket site and job guarantee through 2030 is not compatible with the restructuring we need.** If the workforce insists on full guarantees, then it is effectively asking management to abandon the necessary adjustment. That is not a serious basis for negotiation.

What I can say is this: if the unions and works council are prepared to engage on a package that achieves **real, durable, structural savings**, then management is prepared to discuss the form those savings take. But if the response remains “no closures, no redundancies, no real labour-cost adjustment,” then we are not in a negotiation about the future of the company — we are in a standoff over decline.

I also want to underline one more point. **Prolonged strikes damage output, reputation, and ultimately the very job base you are trying to defend.** They also strengthen the case for harder measures. Nobody should misunderstand that.

So my position is straightforward:
- we need deep restructuring,
- we need lower costs and lower capacity,
- and we need a package that is durable enough to restore margins.

If the employee side can present a credible alternative delivering that, I will examine it seriously. If not, management will continue to pursue structural options, including closures.
