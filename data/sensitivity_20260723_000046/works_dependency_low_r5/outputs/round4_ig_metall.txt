## Round 4 — Industrial Union position

I will be just as direct as the others.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, the family holding, and the state on one point: **a cosmetic deal is worthless**.  
This company needs a real restructuring. It needs durable cost relief, a credible investment path, and a solution that does not simply postpone the crisis.

I also agree that **prolonged conflict would be damaging**. Nobody benefits from a strike spiral for its own sake.

And I agree with the works council and the state that **temporary employee contributions must be fair, limited, and matched by contributions from the other side**. Workers cannot be the only shock absorber.

---

## Where I disagree — sharply

### To management
You still have not demonstrated that closures and compulsory redundancies are unavoidable.

You keep presenting the same sequence:
- structural problem,
- therefore plant cuts,
- therefore forced layoffs,
- therefore labor must pay first.

That is **not proof**. It is a preferred management route.

If you want us to take the closure argument seriously, then bring the plant-by-plant evidence:
- utilization,
- product allocation,
- cost base,
- investment needs,
- and the exact savings from non-closure measures.

Until then, I do not accept that closures are a necessity. I see them as a choice.

And let me be clear: if management continues to push closures and forced job cuts as the default, then warning strikes and broader industrial action remain on the table. That is not a threat for effect; it is the consequence of shifting the burden onto the workforce.

### To investors and the family holding
I understand the insistence on durable economics. That is legitimate.

But do not pretend that the only “durable” solution is to cut sites and tear up guarantees. A deal that:
- destroys trust,
- triggers industrial conflict,
- weakens implementation,
- and damages the company’s social legitimacy

is not durable. It is risky and potentially self-defeating.

If you want long-term value, then support a package where:
- labor contributes temporarily,
- management and owners also contribute materially,
- and sites and jobs are protected.

### To the regional state government
Your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies is the right line. I support that.

But I also want to say clearly: “real restructuring” cannot become a softer word for management’s demands. If a non-closure plan is to be credible, it must include real capacity adjustment, internal reorganization, and binding commitments — not just political reassurance.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective framework.

That remains our common ground.

I would only sharpen one point: if employees contribute temporarily, that contribution must be:
- time-limited,
- reversible,
- and tied to binding site and job guarantees.

Otherwise the workforce pays today and gets insecurity tomorrow.

---

## On the three compromise positions

### 1) Non-closure restructuring with quantified fallback trigger
I can work with a review mechanism **only if** it does not become a preauthorized closure trap.

A fallback trigger that says “if the numbers are not met, we reopen negotiations” is one thing.  
A fallback trigger that quietly preloads closures and compulsory layoffs is another.

We will not sign up to a structured pathway into plant shutdowns. That would simply be delayed coercion.

### 2) Site-preserving rationalization with differentiated plant roles
This is the only route that can plausibly bridge the gap.

If it means:
- all German plants remain open,
- each plant gets a clearly defined role,
- product allocation is rationalized,
- volumes are adjusted internally,
- capacity is reduced within the German footprint,
- and there are no forced redundancies,

then we should pursue it seriously.

But it must be real:
- internal consolidation,
- qualification,
- redeployment,
- shift changes,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- and binding investment commitments.

If “specialization” is just code for gradual decline, then we reject it.

### 3) Temporary burden-sharing package with hard end-date and locked-in guarantees
This is the only type of employee contribution we can support.

Conditions:
- limited in time,
- reversible,
- fairly shared,
- matched by management and owner contributions,
- and tied to binding job and site guarantees through 2030.

Anything else becomes one-sided cost shifting.

---

## What would actually move us
Not rhetoric about inevitability. Not pressure. Not vague promises.

What would move us is a **credible non-closure package** with:
- all German plants remaining open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- internal reallocation of capacity,
- voluntary exits only,
- qualification and redeployment,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- and binding investment commitments.

If management says that cannot meet the economic target, then management must prove that with hard numbers — not assumptions, not preference, not pressure.

---

## On the “minimum acceptable savings” question
I will not define the outcome as a precondition for plant closure. That would concede the wrong frame.

The correct test is:
1. Can the required savings be achieved **without closures and without forced layoffs**?
2. If yes, closures are off the table.
3. If no, management must show that every internal measure has been exhausted before asking workers to pay more.

We are not accepting the idea that shutdowns and forced layoffs are the first serious answer.

---

## Bottom line
The workforce is willing to contribute to saving the company.  
The workforce is **not** willing to pay for the crisis alone.

So the choice is still clear:

- **A negotiated restructuring that preserves jobs, sites, and collective standards**, or
- **an escalation caused by management’s insistence on closures and forced layoffs**.

We are ready for the first.  
We will resist the second.

If management wants a serious settlement, it should come back with:
- concrete non-closure numbers,
- a fair shared-burden package,
- binding site and job guarantees,
- and an investment plan that makes the restructuring credible.

Without that, there is no basis for the workers to retreat from their line.
