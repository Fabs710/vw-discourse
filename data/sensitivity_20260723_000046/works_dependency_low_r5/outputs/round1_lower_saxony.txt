The regional state government takes this situation very seriously, and I want to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys a German plant, guts regional employment, or sidelines the co-determination rights that are part of this company’s governance model.**

I do recognize one point from management: the company does need a credible path back to competitiveness. Pretending that the cost and market pressures do not exist would be irresponsible. But the answer cannot be to start with closures and compulsory redundancies as the default solution. That is economically risky, socially damaging, and politically unacceptable.

### To management
Your argument that the company needs structural savings is not wrong in principle. What I reject is the framing that **plant closure and forced layoffs are the necessary first answer**. That is too blunt, too irreversible, and too damaging to the regional economy.

If you want our support, then you need to show a restructuring that:
- preserves German sites wherever possible,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- uses voluntary measures, working-time adjustments, redeployment, and qualification first,
- and includes a real industrial future for the affected plants.

A plan that treats jobs and sites as expendable assets will not get my backing. And given the special statute and our blocking position, you should understand that this is not a rhetorical objection.

### To the works council and the union
I support your core demand that the burden must not fall one-sidedly on the workforce. The state will stand behind efforts to protect jobs, preserve sites, and keep the company anchored in the region.

At the same time, I would encourage both of you to stay open to a negotiated solution that is tough but fair. A complete refusal to discuss any capacity adjustment or labour contribution could leave the door open to a more damaging confrontation later. We should be looking for:
- temporary and reversible measures rather than permanent destruction,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- fair contributions from management and owners as well as employees,
- and binding investment commitments that secure the company’s future.

### My line
What is **not acceptable** is:
- plant closures in Germany as a first resort,
- compulsory redundancies as the main adjustment tool,
- or any attempt to reduce the state’s co-determination and veto role.

What **would be discussable** is a negotiated restructuring that preserves the company as a regional industrial anchor while improving competitiveness through:
- capacity rationalization short of closure where possible,
- voluntary exit programs,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary contributions from the workforce and shareholders,
- and strong commitments to future products and investment.

So I am not endorsing management’s current direction. I am saying plainly: **bring a serious alternative, because “closures and layoffs” is not a solution we can support.** If the company wants durability, it must be built on social partnership, not on breaking the social contract.
