We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the clear position of the works council.

We acknowledge that the company is under pressure and that change is necessary. But the burden cannot be placed one-sidedly on the workforce while management destroys trust by breaking historic job-security guarantees and presenting closures as the starting point. German sites are not disposable. Thousands of families, entire regions, and the future industrial base of the company are at stake.

Our priorities are clear:

- Every German plant must remain open.
- No forced layoffs.
- Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
- A real co-determination role for the workforce in shaping any restructuring.
- A credible industrial plan that secures investment, future products, and long-term competitiveness without sacrificing employees.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification, voluntary schemes, and negotiated contributions if they are fairly shared and truly temporary. But we will not negotiate away the existence of plants or the security of employment.

If management comes with a fait accompli, we will resist it with all available means, including industrial action and using our co-determination rights. If there is to be a solution, it must be built on binding guarantees, not threats.
