### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between **site/job protection commitments** and **management/capital’s demand for durable structural cost and capacity reduction**.

In practical terms:
- **Works Council / Union / State** insist that German sites remain open and compulsory redundancies be avoided.
- **Management / Investors / Family Holding** insist that the company must achieve substantial, lasting savings and capacity reduction, and are not willing to rule out closures or headcount cuts if needed.

The dispute is no longer about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **whether restructuring can be made economically sufficient without closures or compulsory redundancies**, and if so, **what level of burden-sharing and guarantees would make that credible**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to test

#### 1) Non-closure restructuring with quantified fallback trigger
A package could be structured as:
- all German plants remain open for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies in the first phase,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, shift changes, and working-time flexibility are used first,
- management and owners make defined financial and investment contributions,
- and the parties agree in advance on **quantified trigger points**: if agreed savings or capacity reductions are not achieved by a set review date, then a second-stage negotiation opens on additional measures.

This may bridge:
- the **works council/union/state** need for site protection,
- with the **management/investor** demand for measurable economics.

#### 2) Site-preserving rationalization with differentiated plant roles
A second option is a **specialization model**:
- no German closures,
- but each plant receives a clearly differentiated role,
- some sites take on higher volume, others fewer product lines,
- internal consolidation and capacity reduction occur within the German footprint,
- and the workforce contribution is paired with binding investment and product allocation commitments.

This may bridge:
- the **site-preservation** demand,
- with the **capital-side** demand for real footprint reduction and lower cost.

#### 3) Temporary burden-sharing package with hard end-date and locked-in guarantees
A third option is a time-limited bargain:
- temporary wage, time, or flexibility contributions from labor,
- matched by management/owner contributions,
- in exchange for binding site and employment guarantees through a fixed date,
- plus mandatory investment commitments and a review mechanism tied to business performance.

This may bridge:
- the **union/work council** requirement that labor concessions not become permanent erosion,
- with the **capital-side** insistence on immediate savings and implementation certainty.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
**Which specific combination of internal measures, quantified savings, and implementation timeline would you accept as sufficient to keep all German plants open while still delivering the restructuring targets management claims are required?**

#### Industrial Union
**What temporary labor contribution, in what exact form and for what maximum duration, would the union consider legitimate if it were matched by binding management and owner contributions?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction you require, and what precise non-closure package would you accept as sufficient to meet that threshold before pursuing closures or compulsory redundancies?**

#### Regional State Government
**What concrete restructuring measures can the state support within its blocking and governance role, and what measurable conditions must be met for you to treat a non-closure plan as credible?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**What specific non-closure economics would persuade the holding that site-preserving restructuring is preferable to closures, and by what metrics would you judge that comparison?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What hard numerical evidence would you need to see to conclude that a non-closure package is financially credible and that closures are not required?**
