We agree on the end state, but not yet on the means.

## Where we agree
- The **status quo is not viable**.
- A **prolonged strike or symbolic compromise would destroy value**.
- Employees should contribute, and so should **management and owners**.
- Any settlement must be **quantified**, not rhetorical.
- A deal must deliver a **durable** improvement in margins, not a temporary pause.

## Where we disagree
### 1) To the union and works council
Your position is still too absolute:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no permanent labour-cost change.

That may be politically understandable, but from a shareholder perspective it is **not sufficient** unless you can show, in hard numbers, that it closes the structural gap at scale. So far, that has not been demonstrated.

The expert was clear: the non-closure package may help materially, but it is **not proven sufficient** if the cost gap is truly structural and large. That means your proposal is, at best, a partial bridge. We will not support a “solution” that preserves every site and every legacy protection while leaving margins structurally impaired.

### 2) To the state government
We respect the state’s role in preserving industrial legitimacy. But “no closures as a first resort” is only acceptable if the alternative is actually **credible and fully quantified**. Otherwise it becomes a political shield for declining competitiveness.

The state should push for social mitigation, yes. But it should not block economically necessary adjustment if the numbers say the footprint is still too large.

### 3) To the family holding
We agree that cosmetic compromise is not enough. But the answer is not to treat closures as the default moral high ground either. The right sequence is:
1. employee contribution,
2. management/owner contribution,
3. attrition/redeployment/working-time flexibility,
4. then footprint rationalization if the gap remains.

That sequence protects value better than either denial or all-out confrontation.

### 4) To management
You are right to insist on numbers. But you still need to publish a **clear bridge**:
- required annual savings,
- savings from the labour package,
- savings from management/owner discipline,
- residual gap,
- and what capacity reduction is still needed.

Without that, the market cannot judge whether the restructuring is real or merely aspirational.

## Our position
We will support a restructuring only if it is **economically credible and durable**.

That means:
- material, quantified cost savings,
- a real reduction in overcapacity,
- visible management/owner contribution,
- and a clear path to competitive margins.

We are open to:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- and time-limited employee contributions.

We are **not** prepared to back a package that:
- leaves structural overcapacity untouched,
- substitutes political reassurance for operational correction,
- or preserves all sites at the expense of returns.

## What needs to happen next
We support the call for a **side-by-side scenario test**:
1. non-closure package,
2. hybrid package,
3. partial closure / workforce reduction package.

For each, we need:
- annualized savings,
- one-time cash costs,
- headcount impact,
- capacity impact,
- and implementation risk.

If the non-closure package closes the gap, fine — support it.  
If it does not, then the other stakeholders need to stop pretending that hard structural measures can be avoided indefinitely.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend closures for their own sake.  
We are here to protect **risk-adjusted equity value**.

A socially softer deal that leaves the company uncompetitive is **unacceptable**.  
A hard restructuring that restores durable margins, even if it is politically uncomfortable, is acceptable.
