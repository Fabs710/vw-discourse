Let me be very clear from the start: **the workforce will not accept plant closures in Germany or compulsory redundancies as the price of restructuring.** That line is not a bargaining tactic. It is the boundary of what is politically, socially, and industrially acceptable.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **the status quo is not an option**. The company needs a future-proof industrial plan, and we are not denying the pressure on margins, market share, and investment. I also agree that a cosmetic solution would be pointless.

I also agree that:
- employees can make a contribution,
- a negotiated settlement is better than escalation,
- investment commitments matter,
- and the company needs to regain competitiveness.

### Where I disagree
But I fundamentally reject the idea that competitiveness can only be restored through:
- closing German plants,
- forcing redundancies,
- and tearing up hard-won job-security and collective protections.

That is not a “hard but necessary” solution. It is a one-sided attempt to shift the burden onto the workforce.

To management:  
You talk about structural overcapacity and cost gaps, but you are still presenting the workforce with a fait accompli. That is exactly what I will not accept. If you want cooperation, then stop treating closures as the default answer. Show us a plan that preserves German sites, protects jobs, and uses socially responsible instruments first.

To the family holding and capital-market investors:  
You are speaking as if the only credible path is aggressive cutbacks. That may satisfy short-term financial models, but it ignores industrial reality, social stability, and the long-term damage of destroying trust. **A company cannot build a future by breaking the backbone of its workforce.** If you want savings, then owners and management must also bear visible responsibility.

To the regional state government:  
I appreciate the clear rejection of closures and mass layoffs, and I expect you to use your position accordingly. But that support must be more than words. The state should make clear that it will not tolerate a restructuring that permanently weakens the industrial base or bypasses co-determination.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are not saying “no change.” We are saying **no closures and no forced layoffs**.

We can discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- bonus restraint,
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- retirement pathways,
- training and qualification,
- and capacity adjustments that do not destroy sites.

We can also discuss a **binding, time-limited contribution from the workforce** if and only if it is matched by:
1. **binding site guarantees through 2030**,  
2. **a clear commitment to future products and investment in Germany**,  
3. **management contribution**,  
4. **owner contribution**, and  
5. **a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring plan**.

### What is not acceptable
Not acceptable are:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- a permanent dismantling of the wage framework,
- or a restructuring that asks workers to pay while preserving strategic flexibility only for management and owners.

### My message to management and owners
If you want labour peace, then bring a plan that protects the German footprint. If you continue pushing closures and forced cuts, you should expect fierce resistance — in the supervisory board, in the plants, and publicly. We are already mobilised, and we will not stand down for a settlement that sacrifices jobs and sites.

The workforce is prepared to contribute to a fair solution. **It is not prepared to finance its own insecurity.**
