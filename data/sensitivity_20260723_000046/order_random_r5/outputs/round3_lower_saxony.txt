I will be direct.

## My position
I support a **serious restructuring** because the status quo is not sustainable. But I do **not** support a restructuring that is simply a coded way to close plants, shed jobs, and weaken co-determination in the state.

For me, the non-negotiable core is still:
- **protecting regional employment**,  
- **preserving the company as an industrial anchor in the state**,  
- **avoiding plant closures as long as viable alternatives exist**,  
- **avoiding compulsory redundancies**, and  
- **maintaining the state’s co-determination and veto role**.

That is not sentimental. It is the basis of long-term industrial stability.

## Where I agree with the other sides
### To management
I agree that this cannot be a cosmetic deal. The company needs a durable competitiveness reset, and the workforce cannot be asked to contribute nothing.

### To the family holding and investors
I agree that burden-sharing must be real, not symbolic. Owners and management must contribute visibly, and investment commitments must be concrete.

### To the union and works council
I agree that irreversible steps should not be taken before reversible measures are properly tested. You are right to insist on quantified evidence, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
### With management
You keep implying that closures and compulsory redundancies are the natural end point if the non-closure package is not instantly proven sufficient. I reject that sequencing.

The expert did **not** prove that closures are necessary. The expert said the labor-side package may be insufficient on its own, and that further capacity reduction might still be needed. That is not the same as saying plant closures are unavoidable.

So I do **not** accept any attempt to use “structural overcapacity” as a blanket justification for closing German plants before the non-closure route has been tested seriously and in numbers.

### With the family holding and investors
You are right that a durable cost reduction is needed. But you are still treating closures and workforce reduction as if they were the default instruments. They are not.

If capital wants legitimacy for hard restructuring, then capital must show:
- what it will contribute financially,
- what returns it is willing to forgo in the transition,
- what investment is committed to the German sites,
- and how many jobs and which sites are actually secured.

If the only concrete contribution from capital is pressure for cuts, that is not shared responsibility.

### With the union and works council
I support your refusal of closures and forced layoffs as a starting point. But I also need to be frank: it is not enough to say “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no permanent labor-cost change” and stop there.

That becomes credible only if you bring a **quantified alternative** that can actually close the gap at scale.

So I need to hear, in numbers:
- what the employee contribution saves annually,
- how much of the gap it closes,
- how long it lasts,
- and how capacity is adjusted without hollowing out the industrial base.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert was useful, and I want to be clear about what it did and did not say.

It said:
- a non-closure package can contribute meaningfully,
- it is not yet proven sufficient,
- and some residual capacity reduction may still be required if the gap remains.

That is precisely why I insist on a **side-by-side test** before anyone reaches for closures or forced redundancies.

What I will not accept is this leap:
> “Non-closure measures might be insufficient, therefore closures are justified now.”

No. First test the non-closure path properly. Then discuss what, if anything, remains.

## What I can support
I am prepared to support a package with:
- temporary wage and bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- a temporary solidarity contribution,
- and binding investment/product commitments for the German footprint.

I would also support:
- a defined review period,
- clear trigger points,
- and a return to normal terms once the restructuring has delivered.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a plan that makes plant closures the starting assumption,
- compulsory redundancies as a routine tool,
- permanent erosion of collective protections,
- or a restructuring that leaves the state as a spectator.

I also cannot support a “deal” that preserves the appearance of industrial peace while quietly hollowing out employment in the state over time.

## To management: what I need from you
If you want my support, bring a **site-by-site quantified package** showing:
1. the required savings target,
2. what the labor package contributes,
3. what management contributes,
4. what the owners contribute,
5. what investment is guaranteed,
6. which jobs are protected,
7. and what residual gap remains.

Only then can we judge whether any further structural step is truly necessary.

## To the union: what I need from you
If you want to avoid harsher measures, then bring a quantified alternative that is:
- durable,
- large enough,
- and credible beyond the next bargaining round.

I am open to a strong non-closure settlement. But “open to discussion” is not the same as “automatic approval.” It must actually solve the problem.

## My line in the sand
Let me be explicit:

- **No outcome that guts regional employment.**
- **No outcome that removes the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
- **No premature plant closures while a credible non-closure alternative has not been fully tested.**
- **No forced settlement that treats the workforce as the sole adjustment buffer.**

If we stay within those boundaries, I am prepared to back a tough but socially responsible restructuring. Outside them, I will oppose it.
