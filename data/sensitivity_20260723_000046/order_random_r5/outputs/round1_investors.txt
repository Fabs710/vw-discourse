From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the message is simple: **we need a deal that credibly restores competitiveness, not a symbolic compromise that preserves structural weakness.**

### Where we agree
- **The status quo is unacceptable.** Margins are too weak, the German cost base is too high, and the brand needs a credible return to target profitability.
- **A prolonged conflict would destroy value.** A strike spiral, customer disruption, and management drift would all be costly.
- **Any solution should include investment commitments.** Cost reduction without a future industrial plan would just buy time, not value.
- **Employee contributions are legitimate.** If the company needs a reset, labor cannot be completely insulated from the adjustment.

### Where we disagree
To the **union**:  
Your red lines are too rigid **if they rule out the measures needed to restore margins**. No compulsory redundancies, no closures, and no meaningful weakening of labor costs may sound socially fair, but financially it risks leaving the company stuck with an uncompetitive footprint. That is not acceptable to us. If the savings come only from natural turnover and working-time tweaks, we doubt they will be sufficient. We will not support a deal that merely delays the problem.

To the **regional state government**:  
We understand the political constraints, but **a blanket rejection of closures as a first resort is not enough**. If a site is structurally non-competitive, preserving it indefinitely is not a value-preserving strategy. The state’s role should be to insist on a credible industrial future, not to protect every existing footprint regardless of economics.

To the **controlling family**:  
We agree with your diagnosis that cosmetic measures are not enough. But we would also caution against letting “structural reset” become a justification for open-ended disruption. A settlement that is too confrontational, too slow, or too uncertain will also destroy value. We need a plan that is economically forceful **and** executable.

To **management**:  
We support decisive action, but we will judge it on outcomes, not rhetoric. You need to show:
1. **quantified cost savings**,  
2. **durable capacity rationalization**,  
3. **a clear timeline to margin recovery**, and  
4. **limited execution risk**.

If the proposal does not credibly restore the return-on-sales target, then it is not good enough.

### Our bottom line
We are **open to substantial restructuring, including closures if they are genuinely necessary**, but only if the package is:
- financially credible,
- durable,
- and tied to a real path back to competitive margins.

We will **not** back a cosmetic settlement that leaves the cost base structurally impaired. At the same time, we do not support a reckless confrontation that destroys value through prolonged industrial conflict.

So the burden of proof is on management and the controlling stakeholders: **show us a settlement that actually fixes the economics.** If you cannot, capital will continue to discount the business accordingly.
