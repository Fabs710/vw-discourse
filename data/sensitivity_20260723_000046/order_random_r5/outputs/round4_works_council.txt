I will be equally direct.

## My position has not changed
I support a **serious restructuring** only if it is compatible with:
- **every German plant remaining open,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **preservation of co-determination,**
- **and protection of the job-security framework.**

Anything else is not a “solution” from the workforce’s perspective; it is a transfer of risk and pain onto employees and their families.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that the status quo is not viable. The company needs a durable reset and quantified facts, not slogans.

### With the union
I agree that the workforce is prepared to contribute, but only if that contribution is **temporary, fair, and matched** by real commitments from management and owners.

### With the state government
I agree that the industrial base in Germany must be protected, and that irreversible steps should not be taken before reversible measures are properly tested.

### With the expert
I agree with the basic industrial logic: non-closure measures can meaningfully reduce the gap. That is exactly why they must be tested seriously first.

---

## Where I disagree — and strongly

### 1) Management is still trying to turn “possible residual gap” into a blank cheque for closures
You keep saying the non-closure package may not be enough, therefore closures must remain available.

That is **not** a decision. That is a preference.

The expert did **not** prove that closures are necessary. The expert said:
- the non-closure package can contribute materially,
- it may still leave a residual gap,
- and the question is whether that residual gap can be closed without destroying sites.

So the burden is still on you to show, in hard numbers, why the site-preserving route fails. Until then, closures are not justified.

### 2) The family holding and investors are still shifting the burden downward
You talk about “shared sacrifice,” but your version of it still sounds like:
- workers accept cuts,
- sites absorb uncertainty,
- and capital keeps the right to demand structural destruction if it wants.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is asymmetric risk transfer.

If owners want credibility, then show:
- concrete financial contribution,
- lower near-term return expectations,
- binding investment commitments,
- and acceptance of a settlement that protects sites and jobs.

Otherwise, you are asking labour to subsidize competitiveness while capital stays protected.

### 3) The state is right to resist destruction, but “avoid as far as possible” is too weak
If the state’s stance is only “avoid closures as far as possible,” that is not enough if it becomes the path to eventual closure under pressure.

The state should insist on:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- concrete investment commitments,
- and a restructuring path that preserves the industrial base.

If it merely softens the blow after management has already chosen destruction, it has not used its leverage.

### 4) The expert’s assessment is being overread by management, owners, and investors
You are all using the same move:
- “non-closure measures may be insufficient”
- therefore structural action, including closures, is legitimate.

No. That is a leap.

The expert said the non-closure route should be tested first and properly. That means:
1. quantify the savings target,
2. quantify employee contributions,
3. quantify management and owner contributions,
4. then test whether any residual gap remains.

You are skipping directly to the hardest instrument before proving the softer ones fail.

---

## On the call for a side-by-side model

Yes, we need numbers. But the model must be built **fairly**, or it becomes a device to rationalize closures.

It must include:
- annualized savings from employee measures,
- management cost discipline,
- owner contribution,
- investment commitments,
- the residual gap,
- and the employment/site impact of each option.

And it must answer one question plainly:
**Can the gap be closed without plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**

If that answer is not yet proven false, then closures are not legitimate.

---

## What a fair package would look like from my side

I can discuss:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- solidarity contributions,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- and temporary capacity reductions that do **not** destroy plants.

I can also discuss:
- a time-limited peace clause,
- a review mechanism,
- and jointly verified triggers.

But the package must contain:
1. **binding no-closure commitment for the German plants during the agreement period**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **visible management and owner contribution**
4. **binding investment and product allocation**
5. **preservation of the collective wage framework**
6. **a fair employee contribution that is temporary and limited**

That is the minimum acceptable basis.

---

## What I reject outright

I reject:
- treating plant closures as a default option,
- compulsory redundancies as a built-in contingency,
- permanent erosion of job-security guarantees,
- and any “deal” where the workforce bears the structural burden while management and owners preserve their options.

I also reject the idea that co-determination should be weakened in the name of restructuring. That would not create legitimacy; it would destroy it.

- savings from each measure,
- the residual gap,
- the cost of closures,

We need a model that shows:
1. what the non-closure package saves,
2. what management and owners contribute,
3. what the residual gap is,
4. and whether that gap can be closed through additional social measures **without** closures or forced layoffs.

If the model still says “partial closure is better,” then it must also show why plant destruction is unavoidable **after** all social instruments have been used. We are nowhere near that proof yet.

We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing a restructuring where:
- employees pay first,
- jobs are cut first,
- sites are sacrificed first,
- and capital’s contribution remains vague.

If management, owners, and investors want a durable settlement, then bring a package that:
- protects every German plant,
