## Where I disagree

### 1) Management is still trying to turn “possible residual gap” into a license for closures
Your argument is still:
- the gap is structural,
- non-closure measures may not be enough,
- therefore closures must remain available.

That is not a conclusion. That is a preference.

The expert did **not** prove that closures are necessary. The expert said the labor-side package may be insufficient **on its own** if the gap is large. That means the next step is not “therefore close plants.” The next step is:
1. quantify the gap,
2. quantify the labor contribution,
3. quantify management and owner contribution,
4. and then test whether the remaining gap can be solved without closures.

You are skipping the hard part.

### 2) The family holding and investors are still shifting the burden downward
You speak about “shared sacrifice,” but your version of it still sounds like:
- workers accept restraint,
- sites absorb uncertainty,
- and capital keeps the right to demand structural destruction if it wants.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is asymmetric risk transfer.

If owners want legitimacy, they must state clearly:
- what financial contribution they make,
- what returns they forgo,
- what investment they commit,
- and what future volumes and products they secure for Germany.

Otherwise, workers are being asked to pay for a turnaround they did not create.

### 3) The state is right, but “avoid as far as possible” is too soft
I appreciate the state’s role and its co-determination leverage. But I need to be blunt: if the state’s stance is only “avoid closures as far as possible,” then that is not a serious industrial protection line.

The state should insist on:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- concrete investment commitments,
- and a restructuring path that preserves the industrial base.

If it merely manages the political fallout after the fact, it is not using its leverage.

## On the moderator’s “staged restructuring with binding triggers”

This is the only part of the current discussion that has some real substance — but only if the triggers are meaningful and not a trap door.

### What I can support
I can support a **staged agreement** if it includes:
- immediate employee contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- bonus restraint,
- temporary wage moderation,
- attrition and early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- and binding owner/management contributions.

I can also support a defined review period.

### What I cannot support
I cannot support a staged agreement where “phase 2” is simply a preloaded path to:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or permanent dismantling of collective protections.

If phase 2 is written to make closure inevitable whenever management says the target was not met, then the whole thing is just a delayed attack on jobs and sites.

So if you want triggers, then they must be:
- transparent,
- jointly verified,
- and tied first to additional social instruments and owner contributions,
not to automatic structural destruction.
