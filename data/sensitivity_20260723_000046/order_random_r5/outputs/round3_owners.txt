The union is still trying to turn a necessary restructuring into a veto over the tools needed to fix the business. I cannot accept that.

## Where I agree
I agree on three points:
1. **The status quo is not viable.**
2. **A strike spiral would destroy value and weaken the company.**
3. **Employee contributions should be part of a settlement**, not just management discipline alone.

I also agree that any settlement must be **quantified** and not built on slogans.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not a credible economic rule
You keep repeating that as a minimum condition. From a controlling owner’s perspective, that is exactly the problem: it is a political line, not a restructuring solution.

If the German footprint is structurally too expensive and oversized, then:
- some capacity must be removed,
- some workforce adjustment may be unavoidable,
- and some legacy protections may need to change.

If you want to rule out closures and compulsory redundancies entirely, then you must show a quantified alternative that closes the cost gap at scale and durably. So far, that has not been shown.

### 2) The burden-sharing test cuts both ways
You ask owners and management to “put skin in the game.” Fair enough — but that does not mean owners should subsidize an unchanged structure indefinitely.

A real ownership contribution is:
- backing a credible turnaround,
- accepting lower returns during the adjustment,
- and funding investment where there is a viable future.

It is **not** accepting that every site and guarantee remain untouched regardless of economics.

### 3) The expert did not validate your conclusion
The restructuring expert said the non-closure package may help, but is unlikely by itself to close a large structural gap if management’s diagnosis is correct. That matters.

You are reading the expert as if it endorsed a fully site-preserving outcome. It did not. It said the package should be tested first. Fine. Test it. But do not confuse “test first” with “therefore no closures ever.”

## On what I can support
I can support a package that includes:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- and a time-limited employee contribution,

**if** it delivers meaningful, durable cost relief and a credible path to competitiveness.

I can also support:
- binding investment commitments,
- product allocation for German sites that remain,
- and a clear implementation timeline.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- an absolute no-closure rule,
- an absolute no-compulsory-redundancy rule,
- or a deal that preserves every existing structure while asking the business to somehow become competitive anyway.

That is not stewardship. That is delay.

## To the state government
I appreciate the state’s insistence on social responsibility and regional stability. Those concerns are legitimate. But if the state uses its position to block any economically necessary adjustment, it risks preserving decline instead of preserving industry.

A viable role for the state is to insist on:
- legally executable restructuring,
- strong social cushioning,
- and binding investment commitments,

not to pretend that every plant can be guaranteed forever.

## To management
You still need to put the numbers clearly on the table:
- required savings,
- savings from the labor package,
- residual gap,
- and what combination of capacity and workforce adjustment closes it.

But you should also be prepared to demonstrate that you have genuinely exhausted the non-closure path before insisting on harsher measures. That is not a concession to the union; it is governance discipline.

## My position in plain terms
The family holding wants:
- **durable competitiveness,**
- **material cost reduction,**
- **capacity adjustment,**
- **a real employee contribution,**
- **management discipline,**
- **and labour peace after settlement.**

If the non-closure package can get us there, I will support it. If it cannot, then I will not shield the company from necessary structural action just because it is politically uncomfortable.

## What I want next
I support the moderator’s call for a side-by-side test, but it must be real:
1. quantified savings from the labor package,
2. quantified savings from site-preserving adjustments,
3. residual gap,
4. and the cost/benefit of any additional closure or workforce action.

If the union’s package truly closes the gap, it will show up in the numbers. If it does not, then the company must not be trapped by red lines that preserve all sites but destroy competitiveness.

So my message is simple:
- **Test the non-closure route.**
- **Do not assume it is sufficient.**
- **And do not demand that owners accept permanent value erosion in the name of social comfort.**
