We are now at the point where the discussion must stop hiding behind abstractions.

## My position, clearly
I am prepared to negotiate **real employee contributions**. I am **not** prepared to negotiate away:
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no plant closures,**
- **preservation of the collective wage framework,**
- **and a settlement where workers carry the whole burden.**

If management and the owners want serious cooperation, they must show a package that protects jobs and sites **and** makes them put real skin in the game. Anything else is just pressure dressed up as restructuring.

## Where I agree
I agree with almost everyone on one thing: **the status quo is not viable**. The company needs a durable reset. I also agree that any settlement has to be **quantified**, not rhetorical.

And yes, I accept that:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- qualification,
- and a temporary workforce contribution

can all be part of a serious agreement.

But those instruments are only acceptable if they are used to **avoid forced job destruction**, not to prepare it.

## Where I disagree — and strongly
### 1) Management is still skipping the burden-sharing test
Management keeps saying the non-closure package is “likely insufficient,” but that is not a quantified conclusion — it is an assumption.

If you want us to accept irreversible measures, then show:
- the exact savings target,
- the savings from each proposed measure,
- the residual gap,
- and why that gap cannot be closed through social instruments, redeployment, attrition, and capacity adjustments that do **not** destroy sites.

You have not made that case yet.

### 2) The family holding and investors are still trying to normalize layoffs and closures
You keep saying closures must remain “on the table.” Of course they are on the table in your minds — but that does not make them legitimate or necessary.

What I hear is:
- workforce first,
- sites second,
- ownership untouched.

That is not shared sacrifice. That is cost shifting.

If the owners want credibility, then they should quantify:
- their financial contribution,
- the investment they will commit,
- and what returns they are willing to forgo during the restructuring period.

Otherwise, you are asking employees to subsidize competitiveness while capital stays protected.

### 3) The state is right to reject destruction, but must be more than a brake
I appreciate that the state government is drawing a line against plant destruction and mass job loss. That is correct.

But the state should not stop at “avoid closures as far as possible.” It should insist on:
- binding site guarantees,
- no forced redundancies,
- concrete investment commitments,
- and a restructuring path that does not hollow out the regional industrial base over time.

If the state’s role is only to soften the blow after management has chosen destruction, that is too weak.

## On the expert assessment
The expert was useful, but let’s not overread it.

What the expert actually said was:
- the non-closure package can **contribute meaningfully**,
- but it is **not proven insufficient**,
- and capacity reduction may still be needed if a gap remains.

That is **not** a mandate for closures. It is a mandate for **testing the non-closure route first and properly**.

The sequencing matters:
1. concessions,
2. attrition,
3. redeployment,
4. working-time solutions,
5. capacity adjustment without closures,
6. and only then, if management can actually prove there is no alternative, further discussion.

Management and investors are trying to jump from “not proven sufficient” to “therefore closures and forced redundancies.” I reject that leap.

## What a serious package would have to contain
I will restate the minimum, because it is still the core of the issue:

1. **Binding no-closure commitment for German sites**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Time-limited employee contribution**
4. **Measurable management and owner contribution**
5. **Concrete investment and product allocation**
6. **Capacity reduction through social instruments first**
7. **Protection of the collective wage framework**

That is not maximalism. That is the minimum for industrial legitimacy.

## What I am prepared to discuss in concrete terms
We are prepared to discuss:
- a temporary waiver or reduction of bonuses,
- temporary moderation of wage components,
- working-time flexibility,
- a solidarity contribution,
- internal redeployment,
- early retirement,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- and temporary capacity reduction.

We can also discuss **time limits**, **review clauses**, and **trigger points** that would reassess the package after a defined period.

But I will not accept:
- hidden exit clauses leading to plant closures,
- forced redundancies as a “contingency” built in from the start,
- or a permanent weakening of the collective framework.

## My challenge to management
If you insist that the non-closure path cannot work, then put your numbers on the table now:
- what savings are required,
- what the labor package saves,
- what remains,
- and why that remainder cannot be covered without closures or forced layoffs.

If you cannot do that, then your preferred solution is not proven — it is merely preferred.

## My challenge to the family holding and investors
If you want to be taken seriously on “durable competitiveness,” then stop treating workers as the first and easiest lever.

Show:
- your cash contribution,
- your willingness to accept lower near-term returns,
- your investment commitments,
- and your acceptance of a deal that protects sites and jobs.

If the only concrete contribution from capital is pressure for cuts, then you are not sharing responsibility.

## My challenge to the state
Use your veto power and your governance role not just to block the worst outcomes, but to force a genuine alternative:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- and no hollowing out of the industrial base.

That means standing firm, not merely expressing concern.

## Bottom line
We are not rejecting restructuring. We are rejecting a restructuring where:
- employees pay first and most,
- sites are sacrificed,
- and management/owners avoid real burden-sharing.

If you want a deal, bring a **quantified, site-preserving package** with real contributions from all sides.  
If you do not, then do not expect the workforce to quietly absorb the damage.

And let me be equally clear: if this turns into a forced-cut agenda, the union has the means to escalate. We would rather settle — but not at the price of surrendering jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework.
