We agree on one central point: **the company needs a durable competitiveness reset**, not cosmetic measures. If German operations remain structurally too expensive and underutilised, that will erode value for everyone — including employees, the state, and the family owners. That reality cannot be wished away.

But I have to be equally clear: **a solution that rules out all plant closures, all forced adjustments, and any meaningful structural change is not credible**. If the cost base does not come down materially, then the enterprise will continue to bleed margin and investment capacity. That would be worse for long-term employment than a hard but workable restructuring now.

Where I agree:
- **No value in conflict for its own sake.** A prolonged strike spiral would damage the brand, customers, and industrial trust.
- **A negotiated settlement is preferable** to a unilateral confrontation.
- **Employees should not be treated as the only party making sacrifices.** Management must show discipline, and owners will support a credible long-term plan, not short-term window dressing.
- **Investment and future product commitments matter.** Any restructuring must be tied to a real industrial future in Germany.

Where I disagree:
- **No forced redundancies as an absolute principle** is too rigid. If overcapacity is real, then the workforce cannot be fully insulated from the consequences.
- **No plant closures under any circumstances** is not something I can support if the numbers show that one or more sites are structurally non-competitive.
- **Protecting the collective framework cannot mean preserving all legacy guarantees unchanged** when they are part of the cost problem.

From the family holding’s perspective, the non-negotiable point is this: **the enterprise must regain competitiveness without triggering a permanent destruction of value**. That means:
1. real cost relief,
2. durable capacity adjustment,
3. labour peace once a settlement is reached,
4. and protection of the company’s strategic future.

So I would support a plan that combines:
- targeted capacity reductions,
- significant employee contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- selective job reductions through socially responsible mechanisms where possible,
- and binding investment commitments for the German footprint.

But I would **not** support a package that preserves every site and every existing cost guarantee while asking the company to absorb the burden alone. That is not stewardship; that is slow decline.

If the union wants to avoid harsher measures, it should come forward with a proposal that delivers **quantifiable savings and capacity correction** at a scale that actually restores the business. Otherwise, management’s restructuring case will remain the stronger one.
