### Moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the **status quo is not viable** and that the company needs a **durable competitiveness reset**.
- There is broad agreement that a **negotiated settlement** is preferable to confrontation, strike escalation, or unilateral action.
- Most parties accept that **employee contributions** may be part of the solution, provided they are matched by **management/owner discipline** and **investment commitments**.
- There is general recognition that any settlement should include some mix of:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - temporary concessions,
  - internal redeployment,
  - retirement/attrition measures,
  - training/qualification,
  - and future product/investment commitments.

#### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Union, works council, and state government reject closures.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs:**  
  - Union and works council reject them.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors view them as potentially necessary.
- **Scope of labor-cost and structural change:**  
  - Employers/capital want **material, durable cost reductions** and capacity rationalization.  
  - Employee and political sides want **site protection** and limited, socially managed adjustment.
- **Collective framework / co-determination:**  
  - Labor side wants the collective wage framework and co-determination preserved.  
  - Employers and investors want more flexibility and are open to altering existing protections if needed for competitiveness.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  - All acknowledge burden-sharing is needed, but there is no agreement yet on **how much** each side contributes and in **what form**.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to achieve sufficient cost savings without closures or forced layoffs.**
- **What concrete quid pro quo employees would receive** for concessions: site guarantees, product allocation, investment, or time-limited protections.
- **Whether capacity reduction can be done via non-forced means** at the scale management and investors say is required.
- **How to define “socially responsible” restructuring** in operational terms, including timelines, thresholds, and safeguards.
- **What owner and management contributions are visible and measurable**, rather than symbolic.
- **How to reconcile state/regional political constraints with economic demands** for restructuring.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers, scenarios, and package options**.
- Ask each side to specify:
  1. minimum acceptable savings/capacity reduction,
  2. acceptable adjustment instruments,
  3. required safeguards or concessions,
  4. and the exact commitments they would exchange.
- Narrow the discussion to **one or two viable restructuring models** that can be tested against:
  - financial credibility,
  - job/site impact,
  - and implementation feasibility.
