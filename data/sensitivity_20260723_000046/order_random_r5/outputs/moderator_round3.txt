## Core impasse

The central blockage is not whether restructuring is needed; all sides accept that it is. The impasse is over **sequence, proof, and irreversibility**:

- **Union / works council / state** want a **quantified, site-preserving test first** and reject closures and compulsory redundancies as starting assumptions.
- **Management / owners / investors** want to preserve the option of **capacity reduction, including closures**, if the gap remains.
- The sides disagree on whether the non-closure package is merely a first step or could be the final solution.

In short:  
**The dispute is about whether “test non-closure first” is a decision rule, or only a negotiating step.**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### 1) Staged restructuring with binding triggers
A compromise could be a **two-phase agreement**:

- **Phase 1:** immediate non-closure package
  - temporary wage/bonus restraint
  - working-time flexibility
  - redeployment
  - early retirement
  - qualification and transfer measures
- **Phase 2:** only if agreed financial triggers are not met after a defined review period, the parties reopen discussion on footprint measures

**Facilitator framing:** This gives the union and state a real non-closure test, while preserving management’s claim that unresolved structural gaps cannot be ignored.

---

### 2) Site guarantees paired with capped adjustment rights
A second compromise could be:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No immediate plant closures**
- but with a jointly defined list of **socially managed capacity tools**:
  - voluntary exits
  - retirement pathways
  - product reallocation
  - function consolidation
  - temporary reduced utilization
- plus a **narrowly defined fallback mechanism** if quantified targets are missed

**Facilitator framing:** This separates “site preservation now” from “future flexibility later,” reducing the all-or-nothing conflict.

---

### 3) Shared-burden package with matched contributions
A third compromise could require that each side’s contribution be **matched and visible**:

- workforce: time-limited labor concessions
- management: executive cost discipline and operational simplification
- owners/investors: defined financial support and lower near-term returns
- state: facilitation, guarantees, and governance oversight

**Facilitator framing:** This addresses the burden-sharing dispute directly by making each contribution explicit and comparable.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To the Industrial Union / Works Council
**What quantified savings would you accept as sufficient to keep all sites open, and what specific measures would you support if the numbers show a residual gap after the non-closure package?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to a side-by-side model that tests the non-closure package first and only then defines any residual gap, with plant closures excluded unless that gap is proven and jointly reviewed?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What concrete financial and investment contribution are you prepared to make, and how much near-term return are you willing to forgo to make a site-preserving agreement credible?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What governance condition or veto threshold will you apply so that the non-closure test is meaningful, while still allowing a quantified fallback if the restructuring gap remains?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a settlement that preserves all sites for a defined period if it produces quantified, durable savings and a credible return to competitiveness, even if the footprint adjustment is deferred?**

### To the Expert
**Can you produce a decision-grade comparison that isolates how much of the gap can be closed by social instruments alone, and at what residual gap footprint measures become economically unavoidable?**
