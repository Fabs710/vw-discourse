The expert’s assessment confirms what we have been saying: **a non-closure labor package may help, but it is not proven sufficient to close the structural cost gap**. That is the key point for capital-market shareholders.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the expert on the central diagnosis:
- the status quo is unacceptable,
- margins are too weak,
- the German cost base is too high,
- and some form of durable capacity and cost correction is necessary.

We also agree that:
- a prolonged strike or chaotic conflict would destroy value,
- employee contributions are legitimate,
- and investment commitments matter.

## Where we disagree
### To the union and works council
Your position still reads as **a refusal of the main adjustment tools** while asking everyone else to somehow absorb the gap.

You say:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no permanent labor-cost change.

But the expert has just made the important point: these tools are useful, yet **not enough on their own if the structural gap is large**. That means your package is, at best, a partial bridge. It is not yet a credible full solution.

From our perspective, that is not acceptable if it leaves the company with:
- structurally uncompetitive margins,
- a bloated fixed cost base,
- and continued market underperformance.

We do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that delays the restructuring and lets equity continue to underperform.

### To the regional state government
You are right that legitimacy and industrial stability matter. But your position still leans too far toward protecting sites as a principle.

A state-backed refusal to countenance closures “as a first resort” is understandable politically, but financially it only works if the alternative truly closes the gap. So far, that has **not** been demonstrated. If the alternative remains unproven, then preserving every footprint becomes value-destructive.

We are not asking for social indifference. We are asking for economic realism.

### To the family holding
We agree with your insistence that cosmetic measures are not enough. But we would also caution against framing closures as the only serious route. The expert’s sequence is the right one:
1. concessions,
2. attrition/retirement/redeployment,
3. capacity reduction,
4. then closures or involuntary reductions if the gap remains.

That is the right discipline. It protects value better than either denial or confrontation-first tactics.

### To management
You are right to demand numbers, but at this stage you still have not given the market the full quantified bridge between:
- the current cost base,
- the labor package,
- and the margin target.

Without that, the market cannot assess whether your proposed restructuring is sufficient. We need a concrete path, not just a narrative.

## Our position
We will support a restructuring only if it is **credible on economics and durable in execution**.

That means:
- real and quantified cost savings,
- a verified reduction in overcapacity,
- a clear timeline to margin recovery,
- and a package that does not rely on endless temporary concessions.

We are open to:
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- and targeted capacity rationalization.

But we are **not** prepared to back a deal that:
- preserves structural overcapacity,
- leaves the company uncompetitive,
- or substitutes political comfort for operational correction.

## What we need next
The expert has identified the missing piece: **quantification**. So the next step should be a side-by-side test of:
1. the exact savings from the non-closure package,
2. the residual gap to the target,
3. and what remaining capacity reduction is required.

If that test shows the labor package closes the gap at scale, then fine — we will support it.  
If it does not, then management and the controlling stakeholders must be honest that **some combination of closures, workforce reduction, or deeper structural change remains necessary**.

## Bottom line
We are not here to preserve every site at any price. We are here to protect **risk-adjusted equity value** by restoring competitive margins and a durable cost base.

A deal that is socially smoother but economically insufficient is **not** good enough for us.
