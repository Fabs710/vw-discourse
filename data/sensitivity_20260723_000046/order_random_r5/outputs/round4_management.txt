I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the **diagnosis**:
- the status quo is not viable,
- the company needs a durable reset,
- and a cosmetic deal would be worse than no deal.

I also agree that a prolonged strike spiral would damage output, reputation, and the company’s ability to invest for 2030.

And yes: employee contributions, management discipline, owner participation, and investment commitments all belong in the package.

## Where I disagree — fundamentally
### 1) “Test non-closure first” must not become “no structural action”
I am willing to test a non-closure package. I am **not** willing to accept a framework that effectively rules out closures and compulsory reductions in advance.

That is not a restructuring strategy. That is a political wish.

The expert’s point was clear:
- a non-closure package can contribute materially,
- but it is **not proven sufficient** if the cost gap is structural and large,
- and residual capacity reduction may still be required.

That means management has every right — and duty — to keep footprint rationalization on the table. If the gap remains after serious social measures, we cannot pretend the problem disappears because closures are unpopular.

### 2) Absolute no-closure / no-redundancy red lines are not compatible with the economics
To the union and works council: I understand your position, but I cannot accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as a permanent rule.

Why? Because it pre-emptively removes the only tools that can remove fixed costs at scale if non-closure measures fall short.

You keep saying the non-closure route has not been disproven. That is true. But that is not the same as proving it is sufficient. And until it is sufficient, we cannot rule out deeper structural measures just because they are difficult.

### 3) The burden-sharing demand cuts both ways
To the labor side: you are right that workers should not carry the whole burden. They won’t.

But burden-sharing does **not** mean workers get a veto over any meaningful structural change. It means everyone contributes:
- workforce through temporary concessions and flexibility,
- management through operational discipline and overhead cuts,
- owners through investment and lower near-term returns,
- and, if needed, site rationalization where the footprint is still too large.

Shared sacrifice is not synonymous with “everyone keeps everything they want.”

## On the state government
I respect the state’s role and its responsibility for regional stability. But I need to be blunt: protecting industry does not mean freezing the footprint forever.

If the state uses its leverage to block necessary adjustment, it will preserve decline, not jobs. The right role for the state is to insist on:
- social cushioning,
- investment commitments,
- and legal execution,

not to impose a permanent no-closure doctrine regardless of competitiveness.

## On the family holding and investors
I agree that owners and investors must contribute visibly. But I reject the implication that capital’s role is to underwrite an unchanged structure indefinitely.

A real ownership contribution is:
- backing a credible turnaround,
- accepting lower returns during the transition,
- funding future product and technology investment,
- and allowing the company to resize where economics require it.

It is **not** demanding that every site remain untouched while margins stay below target.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert has been helpful, but let’s not overstate what was said.

What was said:
- non-closure measures can generate meaningful savings,
- but they often do not fully solve a large structural overcapacity problem,
- and hybrid solutions are often the most plausible outcome.

That is exactly why I am pushing for a side-by-side test. But it is also why I will not accept any precommitment that says closures are off limits before the numbers are known.

## My position in plain terms
I am prepared to support:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus moderation,
- working-time flexibility,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transfer measures,
- temporary solidarity contributions,
- and binding investment commitments.

I am prepared to test a non-closure package first.

I am **not** prepared to accept:
- an absolute no-closure rule,
- an absolute no-compulsory-redundancy rule,
- a permanent erosion of labour-cost competitiveness,
- or a deal that leaves the company structurally overbuilt.

## What I need from the other sides
### To the union and works council
Bring a **quantified package**:
- annual savings,
- duration,
- participation rate,
- redundancy/attrition volumes,
- and how it closes the gap at scale.

If that package truly closes the gap, then fine — prove it. But until then, your objections to closures are not a solution, just a boundary.

### To the family holding and investors
Show me:
- your concrete financial contribution,
- the near-term return you will forego,
- the investment you will lock in,
- and the product commitments you will make for German sites.

If your only concrete contribution is pressure on labor, then you are not sharing responsibility.

### To the state government
If you want a socially responsible settlement, support one that is **economically credible**. That means allowing the company to right-size if the residual gap remains.

## The decision rule I am standing on
Here is the line:
1. Test the non-closure route seriously.
2. Quantify the savings and residual gap.
3. If the gap is closed, we can settle on that basis.
4. If the gap is not closed, then structural footprint action remains available.

I will not accept any framework that makes step 4 illegal or taboo from the outset.

## Why I am holding this line
Because the company must be able to:
- restore competitiveness,
- hit the brand’s return-on-sales target,
- and free funds for future products and technology to 2030.

A compromise that preserves every site but leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not a compromise. It is a delay.

So yes: let’s test the non-closure path.  
But no: I will not sign up to a solution that rules out the tools needed if that path fails.
