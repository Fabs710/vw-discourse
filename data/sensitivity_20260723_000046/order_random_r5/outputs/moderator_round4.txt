## Facilitator readout: core impasse

The discourse has converged on a **single central blockage**:

### Core impasse
There is no shared agreement on whether **plant closures / compulsory redundancies are**
1. **still off the table while alternatives are tested**, or
2. **a legitimate fallback if quantified gaps remain**.

Everything else is downstream of that split.

### More precisely, the conflict has three layers
1. **Burden-sharing dispute**  
   The workforce wants visible, quantified contributions from management and owners before any structural cuts.  
   Management and capital want labor measures, but do not want a permanent no-closure constraint.

2. **Sequencing dispute**  
   Labour/stakeholder defenders want: quantify non-closure measures first, then test residual gap.  
   Management/investors want: retain structural tools from the start in case the gap remains.

3. **Governance dispute**  
   The state and works council want binding protection for sites, jobs, and co-determination.  
   Management and investors want enough flexibility to preserve competitiveness and avoid being boxed in.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-closure moratorium with quantified fallback review
A compromise package could be structured as:

- **Phase 1 (fixed period, e.g. 6–12 months):**
  - no plant closures
  - no compulsory redundancies
  - employee contribution begins immediately
  - management overhead cuts and owner contribution are applied in parallel
  - investment/product commitments are locked in

- **Phase 2 review trigger:**
  - jointly verified financial test
  - side-by-side model showing remaining gap after all social and ownership measures
  - if a threshold gap remains, parties enter a second round of negotiation before any structural action

**Purpose:** gives labor a real test-first guarantee while preserving a defined route for management if the gap is still material.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with protected sites and targeted capacity reduction
A second compromise is to avoid an all-or-nothing closure debate and instead agree to:

- preserve all German plants during the agreement period,
- but allow:
  - shift reductions,
  - line consolidation,
  - function relocation,
  - product reassignment,
  - attrition,
  - early retirement,
  - and internal redeployment.

This would be paired with:
- management cost cuts,
- owner-funded transition support,
- and a binding investment schedule.

**Purpose:** reduces cost and capacity without immediate plant closure, while still allowing meaningful structural adjustment.

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional no-closure commitment tied to measurable burden-sharing
A third bridge is a conditional package:

- **No closures / no compulsory redundancies** remain in force **if and only if**:
  - employee concessions reach an agreed savings target,
  - management meets its own overhead-cut target,
  - owners provide defined financial support,
  - and the residual gap falls below a jointly accepted threshold.

If those conditions are not met, the parties reopen negotiations on structural measures.

**Purpose:** converts the no-closure demand from a slogan into a performance-based agreement.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the exact annual savings target that must be met, and what is the minimum residual gap at which you would still refrain from proposing plant closures?**

### To the controlling family holding
**What concrete financial, investment, and return-sacrifice contribution are you prepared to commit in writing, and for how long?**

### To the state government
**What binding site- and job-protection commitments are you prepared to insist on before accepting any restructuring that risks footprint reduction?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of residual margin improvement would you regard as sufficient to support a non-closure or hybrid package, even if it leaves some overcapacity in place?**

### To the works council
**What quantified employee measures can you credibly deliver that would narrow the gap enough to justify a binding no-closure test period?**

### To the union
**Can you specify the maximum duration and scale of employee concessions you are prepared to accept if management and owners make equivalent quantified contributions?**

### To management and investors jointly
**Will you accept a jointly verified scenario model as the sole basis for deciding whether a second-stage structural response is needed, instead of treating closures as a preloaded conclusion?**

## Facilitator synthesis
A workable next step would be to ask all sides to endorse one of these procedural frameworks:

1. **Test-first moratorium**
2. **Hybrid restructuring with no immediate closures**
3. **Conditional no-closure agreement with hard financial triggers**

Each of these preserves room for negotiation while directly addressing the unresolved question:  
**Can competitiveness be restored without immediate plant destruction, and if not, under what verified conditions does the conversation move to structural action?**
