We share the diagnosis that the company needs a durable competitiveness reset. We also agree that conflict for its own sake would be damaging and that a negotiated solution is preferable to a destructive confrontation.

But I need to be equally clear: **a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany, mass job losses, or a one-sided dismantling of co-determination is not acceptable**. As a regional anchor shareholder, my responsibility is not only to balance the books in the short term, but to protect the industrial base, employment, and long-term economic stability of the state.

Where I agree with the other sides:
- **The status quo cannot simply be preserved unchanged.** The company does need a credible cost and capacity adjustment.
- **Employee contributions are legitimate**, provided they are fair, temporary where possible, and tied to secure commitments.
- **Investment and future product allocation are essential.** If workers are asked to contribute, the company must commit to a real industrial future in Germany.
- **A negotiated settlement is better than escalation.** A strike spiral or unilateral move would be harmful for everyone.

Where I disagree:
- I cannot support the idea that **plant closures are just a normal management tool** here. In this company and under this governance structure, closures are not merely a technical efficiency measure; they would be a profound social and political rupture.
- I also do not accept the framing that the workforce must absorb the main burden while owners and management remain largely protected. A fair restructuring must visibly include **management discipline, owner contribution, and a serious commitment to the regional footprint**.
- Most importantly, I will not back any plan that **removes jobs and capacity in a way that permanently weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor**.

So my position is this:

1. **Yes to restructuring.**
2. **No to closures as a first resort.**
3. **No to compulsory redundancies if there are workable alternatives.**
4. **Yes to a negotiated package of capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, early retirement, training, and temporary concessions.**
5. **Yes only to a solution that includes binding investment, site commitments, and a credible path to profitability.**

If management believes savings are necessary at a significant scale, then it must demonstrate that it has exhausted alternatives before putting closures on the table. And if the family holding wants a durable solution, it should support a social-partnership model that preserves the company’s legitimacy, not one that forces a confrontation with the workforce and the state.

In short: **I am open to a hard restructuring, but not to a destructive one.** The burden must be shared, the region must not be sacrificed, and the state’s co-determination and veto role cannot be treated as an obstacle to be bypassed.
