1) **Agreed so far**
- All sides agree the current situation is unsustainable and that a durable restructuring is needed.
- All sides agree a prolonged conflict or strike would be damaging.
- There is broad support for some combination of employee contributions, management discipline, owner participation, and investment commitments.
- There is also broad acceptance that any solution should be based on quantified facts rather than general statements.

2) **Core disagreements remaining**
- **Closures and layoffs:** Management, investors, and the family holding insist closures and/or compulsory redundancies must remain available; the union and works council reject them, and the state wants them avoided as far as possible.
- **Sufficiency of non-closure measures:** Labor argues that working-time flexibility, wage/bonus restraint, redeployment, early retirement, and similar tools should be tried first; management and investors say these are likely insufficient at the required scale.
- **Role of collective protections:** Labor wants to preserve job-security and collective frameworks; capital-side actors want more flexibility and structural change.
- **State role:** The state supports restructuring but seeks to prevent plant destruction and mass job losses, while management and investors want the state to acknowledge economic necessity more openly.

3) **Key trade-offs still unaddressed**
- The exact **savings target** and how it breaks down by measure.
- How much the proposed labor package would actually save, in annualized and durable terms.
- What **capacity reduction** is still required if non-closure measures fall short.
- Whether capacity reduction can be achieved through social instruments alone, or whether some sites would still need to be closed.
- What concrete **owner and management contributions** would look like in financial terms.
- Which **investment and product commitments** are binding enough to make any compromise credible.
- The time horizon for any temporary concessions and how a return to normal terms would be handled.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to a **side-by-side quantified scenario test**: one with the non-closure package, one with partial closures or direct workforce reductions.
- Require each side to specify numbers for costs, savings, capacity, timing, and risks.
- Focus discussion on what package closes the gap **at scale** while preserving as much industrial stability as possible.
- Keep the next round centered on a single question: **What combination of measures actually restores competitiveness without creating a larger long-term conflict?**
