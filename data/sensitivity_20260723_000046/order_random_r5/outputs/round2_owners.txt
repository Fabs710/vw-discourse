The union’s position is clear, and I will answer just as clearly.

### Where I agree
I agree that **a negotiated settlement is preferable** to a strike spiral or unilateral management action. Prolonged conflict would destroy value, weaken the brand, and damage trust across the group. I also agree that **employees should not be the only party contributing**. If the company needs a reset, management, owners, and the broader governance structure must all show discipline and commitment.

I also agree that **temporary and time-limited employee contributions** can be part of a serious package if they are tied to a credible industrial future.

### Where I disagree
What I cannot accept is the union’s attempt to make **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no permanent labor-cost change”** into a universal rule.

That is not a restructuring model. It is a request to keep the cost problem intact and hope the company somehow absorbs it. It will not.

If the German footprint remains structurally too expensive and underutilized, then:
- some capacity must go,
- some workforce adjustment is unavoidable,
- and some legacy protections will have to change.

I am not saying this to be provocative. I am saying it because **preserving every site and every guarantee regardless of economics would erode the company’s long-term value**, which is unacceptable to the family owners and, ultimately, harmful to employees as well.

### On your demand for proof
You say management must first prove that alternatives cannot deliver the savings. Fair enough — but that proof cuts both ways. If the union wants to rule out closures and forced reductions entirely, then it must show how the required savings will be achieved **at sufficient scale and with durable effect**.

At the moment, I do not see that answer. Working-time changes, natural attrition, internal transfers, and bonus restraint may help, but I do not believe they are enough on their own to restore competitiveness. If you believe they are, then present quantified numbers, not just categories of measures.

### On burden-sharing
You are right that burden-sharing must be real. But burden-sharing does **not** mean that one side sets absolute vetoes while the others absorb the economic reality.

So from the family holding’s perspective, a credible package would need all of the following:
1. **material cost reduction**,  
2. **durable capacity adjustment**,  
3. **a fair but real employee contribution**,  
4. **management discipline**,  
5. **binding investment commitments**,  
6. **and a stable industrial peace after settlement**.

What we will not back is a deal that leaves the company with the same structural problems, just wrapped in better language.

### On closures and layoffs
Let me be specific.

I am **not** saying every site must close. I am saying that **closures must remain on the table** where a site is structurally non-competitive. Similarly, I am **not** demanding mass layoffs as a first resort. But I am also not prepared to accept “no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute principle if the economics do not support it.

That is the point where the union must move from a political stance to an economic one.

### What I would regard as a serious pathway
A workable settlement would likely need:
- targeted capacity reduction,
- meaningful workforce adjustment through a mix of social measures and, where unavoidable, direct reductions,
- temporary labour-cost concessions,
- working-time flexibility,
- and binding investment/product commitments for the German sites that remain.

If the union can bring forward a **quantified** alternative that closes the cost gap at scale without closures or forced redundancies, I will examine it seriously. But I will not pretend that slogans are a substitute for economics.

### Bottom line
We want a durable solution, not a symbolic one.  
We want to protect the company’s long-term value, not freeze its cost structure in place.  
And we want labour peace — but not peace bought by guaranteeing stagnation.

So my message is simple: **bring a credible, quantified plan that restores competitiveness, or accept that deeper structural measures remain necessary.**
