# 3) Scenario comparison

## A. Non-closure package
### Typical measures
- temporary wage restraint / bonus moderation
- working-time flexibility
- reduced overtime premiums
- internal redeployment
- early retirement / attrition
- qualification and transfer measures
- management overhead cuts

### Likely annual savings
**Order of magnitude: €300m–€700m per year**

### One-time costs
**Order of magnitude: €100m–€300m**
- severance top-ups
- retraining
- transfer costs
- administration

### Capacity impact
- **Low to moderate**

### Employment impact
- **Moderate reduction through attrition**
- likely no large immediate layoffs if negotiated

### Residual risk
If the structural gap is large, this package likely leaves:
- **material residual overcapacity**
- and a **continuing margin drag**

### Assessment
**Feasible politically, but often insufficient economically if the required reset is large.**

---

## B. Hybrid package
This is the most plausible compromise in many German industrial restructurings.

### Typical measures
- all measures in A, plus
- product reallocation
- shift/line consolidation
- consolidation of functions across sites
- selective site resizing
- partial externalization of non-core activities
- targeted workforce reduction without immediate full closures

### Likely annual savings
**Order of magnitude: €600m–€1.3bn per year**

### One-time costs
**Order of magnitude: €300m–€900m**
- severance
- relocation
- decommissioning of lines
- duplicate-running costs
- IT / footprint reconfiguration
- retraining / transfer

### Capacity impact
- **Moderate to material**
- can remove fixed cost and some physical capacity without necessarily announcing broad plant closures

### Employment impact
- **Material workforce reduction**
- can often be achieved through:
  - natural attrition,
  - retirement programs,
  - transfer,
  - and socially managed downsizing

### Residual risk
- implementation complexity is high
- if governance blocks deeper site changes, the package may still underdeliver

### Assessment
**Most credible path if outright closures are politically blocked but the company still needs a genuine cost reset.**

## C. Partial closure / direct workforce reduction package
### Typical measures
- closure of one or more sites or major site sections
- direct workforce reduction
- concentration of production in fewer plants
- substantial fixed-cost removal

### Likely annual savings

### One-time costs

### Residual risk

### Assessment
**Best on pure cost reduction, but likely the least executable in your governance setting.**

# 5) What this means for the “residual gap”

If management’s target is a **large durable cost reset**, the non-closure package often closes only **part** of the gap.

A practical rule of thumb:
- if the required structural saving is **below €500m per year**, non-closure measures may be enough;
- if it is **€700m–€1.2bn+**, non-closure measures are usually **not enough alone**;
- above that, you generally need **some footprint action** or equivalent capacity removal.

That is an estimate, not a fact, because the actual target is missing.

# 6) Implementation risk

## Non-closure package risks
- savings can be temporary rather than structural
- too much reliance on short-term wage restraint
- insufficient reduction in fixed costs
- delayed competitiveness gains

## Hybrid package risks
- complex to execute
- can be diluted by site-by-site exemptions
- may not fully remove capacity if management cannot reallocate product enough

## Closure-based package risks
- very high governance resistance
- industrial conflict
- long execution timelines
- possible loss of know-how and future flexibility
