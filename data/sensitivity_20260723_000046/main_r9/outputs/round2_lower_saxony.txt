I will respond directly and plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic patch**. The cost base is too high, the market is tougher, and the brand must return to sustainable profitability. Pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

I also agree that:
- capacity must be adjusted,
- working-time flexibility should be used,
- redeployment and qualification are important,
- and all sides must contribute in some form.

So yes, there must be a real package. I am not defending inertia.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only credible path is plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

That is not a neutral technical conclusion. It is a choice. And from the state’s perspective, it is the wrong choice if it destroys regional employment, industrial capacity, and trust in the company’s social partnership model.

### To management
You keep saying that if the footprint is too large, closures are unavoidable. I challenge that as a first conclusion, not because I deny the problem, but because you have not yet shown that every alternative has been exhausted.

If you want support for structural change, then put forward a package that proves the same durable savings can be achieved through:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a fair negotiated employee contribution.

If that is truly impossible, then show the numbers transparently. But “closures first” is not good governance; it is the fastest route to escalation.

### To the union and works council
I support your red lines on **no compulsory redundancies** and the protection of the German industrial base. Those are not empty slogans; they are legitimate conditions for any settlement with political and social legitimacy.

That said, I also need you to engage seriously with quantified savings. If the goal is to avoid closures, then the alternative package must be real, measurable, and durable. Saying “we reject closures” is not enough unless the replacement package closes the gap.

### To the family holding
I appreciate that you are cautious about a destructive conflict. I agree. A long confrontation would erode value. But that is exactly why I am wary of a maximalist restructuring plan that starts with closures and forced layoffs. It may look decisive on paper, but it risks a prolonged fight, political damage, and lasting harm to the company’s legitimacy.

The owners should use their governance power to push management toward a negotiated solution that protects long-term enterprise value **and** the regional anchor role.

### To institutional investors
You are right that savings must be quantified and durable. I do not dispute that. But I reject the implication that the only measurable savings are those created by shutting plants. That is too narrow.

If alternative measures can close the gap credibly, then that is the economically rational path. Investors should support the solution that is durable, not merely the one that appears harshest.

## My position, clearly
The state can support a tough restructuring **only if it meets these conditions**:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A genuine shared-sacrifice package**
4. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies**
5. **Protection of the company’s co-determination and regional anchor role**

Those are not bargaining ornaments. They are the minimum basis for political and industrial legitimacy.

## What I would like to see next
I want management to come back with a **side-by-side comparison** of:
- the savings from closures,
- the savings from a no-closure restructuring,
- the time profile,
- the risks,
- and the impact on jobs, sites, and investment.

If the no-closure route truly cannot work, then the burden of proof is on management to explain why—not to assume it. And even then, I would expect every possible alternative to be exhausted before any discussion of closures.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back a difficult turnaround. I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s co-determination role. If this company wants the state’s support, it must present a plan that preserves sites and jobs in Germany while restoring competitiveness. Anything else will meet our opposition.
