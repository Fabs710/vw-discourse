I hear the core message from labour side clearly: you want security, no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and a burden-sharing approach rather than a one-sided cut on the workforce.

Let me be equally clear from management’s side: **I cannot accept a solution that preserves every site and every guarantee while leaving the cost base structurally uncompetitive.** That would not be a turnaround. It would be a delay.

We are facing a real crisis:
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our German cost base is far above target,
- and the brand is not meeting its return-on-sales objective.

If we do not make a **durable structural reduction in cost and capacity**, we will not free the funds needed for future products, technology, and the 2030 transition. That is not a theoretical risk; it is the core business problem.

Where I agree:
- We need a negotiated solution, not chaos.
- We need workforce contribution to be part of the package.
- We need redeployment, qualification, working-time changes, and capacity adjustments on the table.
- We need to protect the company’s long-term industrial base.

Where I disagree:
- **“No closures under any circumstances” is not an acceptable starting point.**
- **“No compulsory redundancies” cannot be treated as absolute if we are left with no other credible route to the required cost reduction.**
- A purely defensive package that avoids the hard decisions would simply preserve overcapacity and lock in underperformance.

I also reject the idea that management is asking the workforce to carry everything alone. We are prepared to discuss a broader package. But it must meet one basic test: **does it actually fix the cost and capacity problem?** If not, it is cosmetic.

So my position is this:
1. We need a **sustainable reduction in capacity and cost**.
2. I am open on the **method** — closures, consolidation, working-time changes, labour-cost measures, or a combination.
3. Any agreement must produce **real, measurable, durable savings**, not symbolic concessions.
4. If the workforce side wants guarantees, those can only be discussed in a package that also delivers the structural change the company needs.

If you want me to move, bring me a proposal that closes the gap on cost and capacity in a way that survives beyond the next bargaining round. Otherwise, we are just postponing the crisis.
