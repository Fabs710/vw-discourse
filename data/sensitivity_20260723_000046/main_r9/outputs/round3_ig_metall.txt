I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even management on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix**.  
I also agree that the package must be **quantified**, and that workforce measures alone cannot do all the work.

I am still prepared to discuss:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary or deferred wage components,
- and an employee contribution.

That remains the union’s constructive offer.

## Where I disagree
### 1) No plant closures
I reject the idea that German plant closures are a necessary or acceptable instrument here.  
A closure is not a technical inevitability; it is a management decision. If management and investors want to claim that closures are unavoidable, then they must prove that **all non-closure alternatives fail**.

So far, that has not been demonstrated.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies
I am equally clear: **we will not accept forced layoffs**.  
That is not a tactical preference. It is a substantive limit. Workers cannot be made to carry the crisis by losing their jobs while the company, owners, and management keep their options open.

### 3) No one-sided burden shift
A “credible” package is not one where labour gives up wages, flexibility, and security while capital asks for applause.  
If employees contribute, then management and ownership must contribute materially as well. Otherwise this is not restructuring; it is transfer of pain downward.

## To management
You keep asking for recognition that the footprint is too large. I do recognize the problem. What I do **not** accept is your leap from “there is an overcapacity problem” to “therefore closures and forced redundancies must stay on the table.”

That leap is exactly what the burden of proof is meant to test.

So here is my challenge back to you:
- Show the **exact savings gap**.
- Show the **alternative package** you actually tested, not just a list of buzzwords.
- Show the **management contribution**: overhead cuts, executive restraint, strategic reprioritization.
- Show the **owner contribution**: lower payout expectations, capital support, governance commitment.
- Show how the package protects the workforce **without** closures and **without** forced layoffs.

If you cannot do that, then you are not offering a restructuring plan we can support.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site security,
- co-determination in implementation.

I support that line.  
At the same time, I want us to remain serious about a quantified settlement. If an alternative package is genuinely under pressure, then the answer is not to surrender to closures; the answer is to deepen the shared-sacrifice package on all sides **except** job destruction.

## To the state
You are right to insist that legitimacy matters. A solution that destroys industrial trust will come back as a bigger problem later.

But I will also say this: political opposition to closures is not enough unless it is backed by real governance pressure on management and owners to pursue the non-closure route seriously. You should use your leverage for that.

## To the family holding
You are right that a destructive conflict would destroy value. Exactly. That is why I do not understand any instinct to keep closures and forced redundancies available as an easy fallback.

If you want long-term value protection, then support a negotiated package that preserves the industrial base and avoids a labour conflict that will poison the company for years.

## To institutional investors
You say you want durable savings. Fine. Then judge the package by whether it closes the gap **without** ripping up the workforce and the plant structure.

Do not turn “durable” into code for “painful enough to satisfy the market.”  
Durable means:
- measurable savings,
- stable implementation,
- and a viable company after the conflict ends.

If a no-closure, no-forced-layoff package achieves that, then support it.

## My position
The union remains ready to negotiate a tough but fair settlement based on:
- workforce contributions,
- capacity adjustment,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity gains,
- and investment commitments.

But our limits remain clear:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no destruction of the collective wage framework**
- **no one-sided burden shift onto employees**

## What would move me
The only thing that would change my position is a package that **genuinely meets those limits while still closing the savings gap**.  
Not rhetoric. Not pressure. Not repetition.

If management cannot deliver that, then the conflict will not be solved by asking workers to accept job losses “for the greater good.” We will not do that.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to contribute.  
We are not prepared to surrender jobs, sites, or wage standards to solve a problem that must be shared by all sides.

Bring forward a real no-closure package with quantified burdens from management and owners. If you can do that, we can move. If not, then management is choosing confrontation.
