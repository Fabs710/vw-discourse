We agree with the Works Council on the core point: **no plant closures in Germany, and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable detail for us; it is the minimum condition for any serious settlement.

Let me be equally clear about what this means from the union side:

- **We will not accept a restructuring that dumps the full burden on the workforce.**
- **We will not accept breaking the collective wage framework as if workers were the only variable cost.**
- **We will not accept “competitiveness” arguments used as a blanket justification for closing sites and tearing up long-standing protections.**

Management may be under pressure, but so are the people who built this company’s products, quality, and reputation. If the company needs a durable turnaround, then the sacrifice must be **shared** and **reversible**, not imposed one-sidedly.

Where we are open:
- capacity reductions and smarter utilization
- working-time adjustments
- redeployment and qualification
- temporary wage moderation or deferred components
- an employee contribution, **if** management, owners, and other stakeholders also contribute
- productivity and cost measures that avoid breaking the company’s social compact

What is unacceptable:
- plant closures
- forced layoffs
- stripping away job-security guarantees
- lowering labour costs only through employee sacrifice while management and ownership avoid responsibility

If management wants our cooperation, it needs to come with a package that includes:
1. **binding site security**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **a fair burden-sharing contribution from capital and management**
4. **credible investment commitments for the future of the plants**
5. **a negotiated path to cost reduction that preserves the collective wage framework**

We have already shown willingness to engage constructively by putting forward a counter-proposal. That openness remains—but only for a settlement that protects jobs, sites, and collective standards. If the proposal remains centered on closures and layoffs, then you should expect resistance, including industrial action.
