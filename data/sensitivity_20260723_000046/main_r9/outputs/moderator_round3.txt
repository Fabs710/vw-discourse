### Core impasse
The central deadlock is this:

- **Labour side / state:** no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies, with binding site and job security.
- **Management / capital side:** no advance exclusion of closures or forced redundancies if they are needed to close a structural competitiveness gap.
- **All sides:** agree the company needs durable restructuring and quantified savings, but disagree on **which instruments may be used before the numbers are proven**.

So the dispute is not about whether change is needed; it is about **whether restructuring can be pre-committed to a no-closure, no-forced-layoff path** or whether hard options must remain available as fallback instruments.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
#### 1) Time-bound no-closure test with a defined review gate
Facilitator proposal:
- Agree a **strictly time-limited no-closure package** for a defined first phase.
- During that phase, management, owners, union, works council, and state jointly test the full alternative package.
- Predefine a **review gate** with measurable savings, investment, and capacity targets.
- If targets are met, the no-closure path continues and gains stronger guarantees.
- If targets are missed, the parties return to the table with a jointly agreed escalation process.

Why this may bridge positions:
- Gives labour and the state a real no-closure effort.
- Gives management and investors a performance test rather than a permanent veto.
- Avoids immediate closure decisions while not pretending the savings gap does not exist.

#### 2) Binding non-closure package with a quantified burden-sharing ladder
Facilitator proposal:
- Create a package that includes:
  - management overhead cuts,
  - owner contribution,
  - working-time and productivity measures,
  - qualification and redeployment,
  - employee contribution,
  - and protected investment commitments.
- Set these in a **burden-sharing ladder** with explicit euro values and implementation deadlines.
- Add a clause that any additional action requires a fresh joint assessment and documented exhaustion of prior measures.

Why this may bridge positions:
- Converts the debate from principles to measurable contributions.
- Gives labour concrete job and site protection in exchange for real quantified concessions from other parties.
- Gives capital a structure to verify whether the alternative is truly sufficient.

#### 3) Conditional security framework without automatic closure triggers
Facilitator proposal:
- Establish **binding site and employment security** for a defined period, but make that security conditional on:
  - verifiable delivery of savings,
  - documented progress milestones,
  - and continuous co-determination in implementation.
- Instead of automatic plant closures or layoffs upon underperformance, require:
  - mandatory joint remediation talks,
  - independent verification,
  - and a predefined sequence of corrective measures.

Why this may bridge positions:
- Addresses labour’s concern about a built-in threat mechanism.
- Addresses management and investors’ concern that commitments must remain enforceable.
- Preserves pressure for delivery without hard-wiring closures as the immediate fallback.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after management overhead cuts, owner contribution, and non-closure workforce measures are fully quantified—by euro amount and by date?**

#### To the works council
**Which specific additional non-closure measures, if independently verified and quantified, would you accept as sufficient to close the gap without reopening the question of site shutdowns?**

#### To the union
**What minimum quantified labour contribution can the union commit to now, and how would you verify that it is large enough to materially affect competitiveness through 2030?**

#### To the regional state government
**What concrete leverage will the state use to ensure that a serious no-closure alternative is tested and enforced, rather than merely politically endorsed?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**Will you commit to supporting a time-bound no-closure trial if the parties agree on quantified milestones, even if it reduces management’s immediate flexibility?**

#### To institutional investors
**What numeric threshold would convince you that a no-closure package is economically equivalent to restructuring through closures, so you can support it on governance grounds?**
