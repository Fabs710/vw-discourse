## Core impasse
The central deadlock is **not** about whether restructuring is needed. All parties now accept that it is.

The impasse is about **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies may remain available as fallback instruments**:

- **Works council + union + state**: want a restructuring path that is explicitly **no closures / no forced layoffs**, with binding site and job security.
- **Management + investors + family holding**: want **hard instruments to remain available** if the non-closure package does not fully close the savings gap.
- All sides also want the package to be **quantified, durable, and shared**.

So the conflict is essentially:
1. **Red line vs fallback option**
2. **Political/social legitimacy vs economic contingency**
3. **Whether “test the alternative first” can be agreed without pre-authorizing job destruction**

---

## Possible compromise positions to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Time-bound no-closure test with agreed trigger point
A structured compromise could be:

- A **defined test period** for a non-closure package.
- During that period: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**.
- The package must include quantified contributions from:
  - workforce,
  - management,
  - owners,
  - productivity,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification,
  - working-time flexibility.
- A **pre-agreed review date** and **objective trigger metrics** determine whether the package closes the gap.
- If targets are missed, parties return to the table with a predefined escalation process.

This gives labour and the state a protected testing phase, while giving management and investors a formal review mechanism.

---

### Proposal 2: “No closures” package plus contingent non-job-destructive escalation ladder
A second bridge option is to agree that the first escalation steps, if the gap remains, must still avoid closures and forced redundancies.

For example, the sequence could be:
1. deeper management overhead cuts,
2. higher owner contribution,
3. further working-time adjustments,
4. additional internal redeployment,
5. stronger productivity measures,
6. targeted investment commitments,
7. only then a new governance review.

This would not settle the closure issue permanently, but it would establish a **strong non-closure escalation ladder** before any hard structural measures are considered.

---

### Proposal 3: Side-letter on job and site security with conditional governance review
A third compromise is to separate the issue into two documents:

- **Main restructuring agreement**: quantified savings, burden-sharing, investment, and implementation rules.
- **Side-letter / protocol**: binding commitments on
  - consultation,
  - co-determination,
  - review milestones,
  - use of redeployment and qualification first,
  - and a defined governance process before any closure or compulsory redundancy is considered.

This could preserve the hard positions rhetorically while creating a practical framework that makes closures a last-resort governance decision rather than an automatic fallback.

### To the union
**Would you accept a temporary, measurable workforce contribution if it were paired with binding management and owner contributions and a formally protected no-closure test period?**

### To the regional state government
**What concrete leverage will the state use to enforce a serious non-closure restructuring, and what outcome metrics will determine whether that route is succeeding?**

### To the controlling family holding
**If a non-closure package demonstrably closes the savings gap in hard euros, will you commit to supporting it even if it excludes closures and compulsory redundancies?**

### To institutional investors
**If the quantified non-closure package meets the same savings and timing targets as a closure scenario, will you support it on value grounds?**
