We are at the point where slogans must give way to a concrete, fair package.

## First, where we agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state, and even parts of management on one basic point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.

I also agree that:
- the German cost base must come down,
- capacity has to be adjusted,
- working-time and redeployment instruments should be used,
- training and qualification matter,
- and an employee contribution can be part of the solution.

So let me be clear: the union is **not** refusing adjustment. We are refusing **one-sided adjustment**.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not “the inevitable answer”
Management and investors keep speaking as if closures are the natural conclusion of any serious restructuring. I reject that.

A closure is not a technical necessity; it is a decision with massive social, industrial, and political consequences. If there are alternatives that can achieve the required savings, then closures are not justified. And those alternatives exist and must be tested honestly.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
I want to state this plainly: **we will not accept forced layoffs**.

If management says that no agreement is possible without compulsory redundancies, then management is saying it wants workers to pay for the crisis by losing their livelihoods. That crosses a line. We are prepared for tough measures, but not for a solution that abandons the workforce.

### 3) The burden cannot sit on employees alone
I hear the call for “durable savings.” Fine. But then the burden must be shared by:
- management,
- owners,
- and capital,
not just by the workforce.

If employees are asked for wage restraint, flexibility, or deferred pay components, then management and owners must also contribute materially. Otherwise, this is just a transfer of pain downward.

## What we are prepared to discuss
The union remains open to a serious package that includes:

- capacity reductions without plant closures
- working-time adjustments
- redeployment within the group
- qualification and training
- temporary or deferred wage components
- a negotiated employee contribution
- productivity improvements
- investment commitments for future products and technologies

That is the kind of shared-sacrifice package we can discuss.

## What must be part of any serious settlement
A durable agreement needs to include:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site security**
4. **Protection of the collective wage framework**
5. **A fair contribution from management and ownership**
6. **Clear investment commitments for the future**
7. **A quantified savings package, not vague threats**

If management cannot deliver savings without closures and forced layoffs, then the burden of proof is on management to show why all other instruments have failed. Merely repeating “competitiveness” is not enough.

## To management
You say you need structural savings. Then bring forward the exact numbers:
- what savings are required,
- what has already been tested,
- what the alternative package would deliver,
- and how much management and ownership are contributing.

If the answer is “employees must give more, and in addition lose sites and jobs,” then there is no balanced bargaining basis. That is not a turnaround strategy; that is a unilateral extraction.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding protection for jobs and sites. I support that line.

At the same time, we should be ready to negotiate real contributions if they are part of a package that protects the workforce and the company’s future. That means being specific, not symbolic.

## To the state and the family holding
Your rejection of closures and forced redundancies is welcome, but it must be backed by real governance pressure on management to pursue alternatives seriously. If you want a social partnership solution, then do not leave the workforce alone to absorb the adjustment.

## To the investors
You say you are agnostic on the instrument. Then prove it. Support the alternative if it delivers the savings. Do not use “market confidence” as a code word for pushing the entire adjustment onto labour.

## Bottom line
The union is prepared to negotiate a tough, quantified, shared-sacrifice package. But our limits are clear:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no destruction of the collective wage framework**
- **no one-sided burden shift onto workers**

If those conditions are ignored, then the conflict will not be solved at the bargaining table.
