I will respond plainly, because the discussion has reached the point where ambiguity is costly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the investors, the union, the works council, and the state on one fundamental point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.

I also agree that:
- the cost base is too high,
- capacity must come down somewhere,
- workforce flexibility and redeployment must be part of the package,
- the solution must be quantified,
- and a prolonged open conflict would destroy value.

So the family holding is **not** defending the status quo. We are defending the long-term value of the enterprise.

## Where I disagree with labour and the state
I do **not** accept a permanent rule of **“no plant closures”**.

If the German footprint is structurally too large for the market and the cost base remains uncompetitive, then management must have the ability to resize. That is not ideology; it is industrial reality. I would strongly prefer that this is achieved without closures, but I will not support turning “no closures” into an absolute veto before the economics are proven.

I also do **not** accept an unconditional **“no compulsory redundancies”** rule in every scenario.

I prefer negotiated solutions. I would rather avoid forced layoffs entirely. But I cannot endorse a framework that strips management of the last credible restructuring tool if every alternative fails to close the gap. That would be governance failure, not stewardship.

## Where I disagree with management
At the same time, I do **not** support a maximalist closure strategy as the default answer.

Management is right to insist on measurable savings, but wrong if it treats closures as the primary proof of seriousness. A plan that is too harsh, too fast, or too politically blind can destroy value, trigger long conflict, and damage the company’s legitimacy for years.

So my message to management is: **bring the numbers, but do not confuse force with value creation**.

## Where I disagree with the union and works council
You are right to demand burden-sharing. You are right to reject a one-sided burden shift onto labour. But you are not entitled to demand binding site protection and job security through 2030 **without first showing that the alternative package closes the gap**.

You keep asking management and investors to prove that non-closure options fail. Fair enough. But that same standard applies to your preferred alternative: it must be shown, not merely asserted, that a no-closure package can deliver **equivalent durable savings**.

A promise of social peace is not a cost reduction plan.

## Where I agree with investors
I agree with investors that **durable, quantified savings** are the benchmark.

I also agree that a cosmetic compromise would be worse than no deal. If the package does not materially improve margins and competitiveness, it is not a solution.

Where I push back is if “durable savings” becomes a code word for “use the harshest available instrument.” That is too simplistic. The instrument matters because some instruments create lasting savings, while others create lasting conflict.

## What would move me
The family holding would support a settlement if it meets all of the following tests:

1. **It restores competitiveness and margins durably**
2. **It avoids unnecessary instability and prolonged conflict**
3. **It shares the burden fairly**
4. **It protects the company’s long-term industrial capability**
5. **It is quantified, timed, and enforceable**

That means we are open to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity measures,
- deferred or temporary employee contributions,
- management overhead reductions,
- and owner-level discipline around returns and governance.

We are **not** open to:
- a deal that permanently impairs enterprise value,
- a cosmetic compromise that just delays the problem,
- or a structure that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## On the facilitator’s proposed sequencing
A **no-closure first** package is worth testing **only if** it is real and time-bound.

I would support:
- a quantified alternative package,
- with explicit milestones,
- with verified savings,
- and with a review point if the targets are missed.

What I will **not** support is pretending that an incomplete package automatically becomes acceptable just because it avoids difficult choices.

## To the Works Council
I respect your insistence on protecting jobs and sites. But “not acceptable” is not a business plan.

If you want to keep all German plants open, then show how the required savings are achieved with the same durability, not just with the same volume of rhetoric. If you cannot do that, then you are asking the owners to accept an unproven structure and call it partnership.

## To the Union
I appreciate your willingness to discuss real employee contributions. That is constructive.

But the package must be substantial enough to matter. If the workforce contributes through flexibility, wage restraint, or deferred components, then that contribution must be large enough to materially change the economics. Otherwise it is symbolic, not structural.

## To the State
You are right to emphasize legitimacy and regional stability. But long-term legitimacy also depends on whether the company survives competitively. A plan that protects every site today while leaving the business weak tomorrow is not a win for the region.

So yes, reject reckless closure-first thinking. But do not block hard decisions before the non-closure route has been proven sufficient.

## My position, clearly
The family holding will back a restructuring only if it is:
- **credible**
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **fairly shared**
- and **consistent with long-term enterprise value**

We will not support:
- a cosmetic deal,
- endless delay,
- or a settlement that preserves comfort while eroding competitiveness.

## What I want next
I want management to present a **side-by-side quantified package** showing:
- the exact savings target,
- the savings from non-closure measures,
- the savings from structural consolidation,
- the timeline to 2030,
- and the verified impact on margins and investment capacity.

If the no-closure route closes the gap credibly, I would prefer it. If it does not, then the hard options remain on the table.

That is my position.
