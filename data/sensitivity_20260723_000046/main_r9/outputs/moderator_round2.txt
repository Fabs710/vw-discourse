### Core impasse
The central deadlock is between:

1. **Labour/state/family holding**, which insist on **no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**, with binding job and site protection; and  
2. **Management/institutional investors**, which insist that **those hard instruments remain available** if they are needed to deliver durable savings and restore competitiveness.

In procedural terms, the conflict is not about whether restructuring is needed — all sides accept that it is. The impasse is about **which instruments are permissible before the required savings are demonstrated**, and **who carries the burden if alternatives do not fully close the gap**.

A second, related impasse is evidentiary:  
- labour and state want management to **prove that alternatives fail before considering closures/forced layoffs**;  
- management and investors want labour to **prove that alternatives can close the gap without those instruments**.  

So the table is stuck in a **mutual burden-of-proof stalemate**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to test

#### 1) Quantified “no-closure first” package with review trigger
A time-limited agreement could be tested in which:
- no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies are applied initially;
- all parties implement a quantified package of capacity, working-time, redeployment, wage, and productivity measures;
- a **pre-agreed review trigger** is set for a later date (e.g., if savings or utilization targets are missed).

This does not decide the hard question permanently; it creates a **sequenced test** of whether the alternative package is sufficient.

#### 2) Conditional security framework tied to verified savings
A second compromise is a **conditional site-and-job security pact**:
- sites/jobs are protected **up to a defined savings milestone**;
- if the milestone is met, enhanced protections continue through 2030;
- if the milestone is not met, the parties reopen talks on additional measures, with a structured escalation ladder before any closure or compulsory layoff decision.

This allows management/investors to insist on measurable performance, while labour/state obtain meaningful protection and procedural barriers against abrupt cuts.

#### 3) Burden-sharing package across all constituencies
A third bridging position is a **four-way contribution matrix**:
- workforce: wage restraint, deferred components, flexibility, redeployment;
- management: overhead reduction, leadership cost cuts, strategic delivery commitments;
- ownership: capital support / lower payout expectations / governance commitments;
- company: investment guarantee for future products and technologies.

The idea is that **no single group carries the adjustment alone**, and any structural measure is only considered after these contributions are independently quantified and verified.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If a quantified alternative package falls short of the required savings by a measurable margin, what specific additional instrument are you prepared to discuss before rejecting the package outright?

#### Industrial Union
What is the minimum savings and capacity-adjustment threshold at which you would consider a no-closure, no-forced-layoff agreement credible enough to recommend to members?

#### Group & Brand Management
Will you present a side-by-side comparison of alternatives with the same rigor you apply to closure scenarios, including explicit assumptions, timing, and the point at which you would conclude that no-closure options have been exhausted?

#### Regional State Government
What concrete governance or support measures are you prepared to use to make a no-closure restructuring executable, beyond expressing political opposition to closures and forced redundancies?

#### Controlling Family Holding
If a negotiated no-closure package is credible but slightly less fast than a closure plan, what is your decision rule for choosing between near-term disruption and long-term value preservation?

#### Institutional Investors
What objective evidence would be sufficient for you to support a no-closure restructuring, and how much implementation risk are you prepared to tolerate if the quantified savings are otherwise met?
