We are prepared to engage on the numbers, but our benchmark is unchanged: **credible, durable margin repair**. Anything that does not materially and permanently lower the cost base will not be supported by capital markets.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the company has a structural competitiveness problem.
- **The family owner is right** that a prolonged conflict destroys value.
- **The union, works council, and state are right** that a purely one-sided solution risks escalation and implementation failure.
- **All sides are right** that vague promises or a cosmetic settlement would be worse than no deal.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a prior
We do **not** accept a blanket no-closures position. If the footprint is genuinely oversized, then capacity must come out somewhere. If a no-closure package can produce the same durable savings, fine — but that needs to be proven with real numbers, not asserted as a moral entitlement.

A solution that protects every site by default but leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive is not acceptable to us.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be absolute
We prefer negotiated adjustment, because strikes and disorder destroy near-term value. But an unconditional veto on workforce reduction would leave management with no credible restructuring tool if alternatives fail.

We are not demanding forced layoffs. We are saying they cannot be ruled out in advance if they are part of the only credible route to restore returns.

### 3) Alternative measures must close the gap, not merely soften it
We are open to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- training/qualification,
- temporary or deferred employee contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- and management/owner contributions.

But these only matter if they produce **quantified, durable savings** sufficient to restore competitive margins. “Shared sacrifice” is not enough if the business remains structurally weak.

## What would move us
We would support a settlement if it delivers all of the following:
1. **A quantified savings plan**
2. **Durable reduction in fixed costs and/or capacity**
3. **Clear implementation certainty**
4. **A credible path back to return-on-sales target**
5. **Minimal disruption and limited strike risk**

## Our limit
We will not back:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves overcapacity,
- or a structure that leaves margins impaired and capital returns below cost.

If the employee side wants to argue that closures and forced redundancies are unnecessary, then bring a side-by-side package showing:
- the exact savings target,
- the exact savings from alternatives,
- the timing,
- and why those savings are durable through 2030.

Until then, we remain focused on one thing: **restore competitiveness, or capital will continue to exit.**
