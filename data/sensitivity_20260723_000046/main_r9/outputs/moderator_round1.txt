### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a serious structural competitiveness and cost problem.
- There is broad support for a negotiated rather than chaotic solution.
- Several instruments are seen as acceptable in principle: working-time adjustments, redeployment, qualification/training, capacity reductions, productivity measures, and some form of employee contribution.
- There is shared concern that a purely cosmetic or short-term fix would fail.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Labour, the state, and the family holding reject them; management and institutional investors keep them on the table if needed for durable savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and the state reject them; management will not rule them out absolutely; owners/investors are cautious but not fully committed to a no-forced-layoffs line.
- **Scope of job/site guarantees:** Labour wants binding site guarantees and job security through 2030; management says it cannot preserve all guarantees if the cost base remains uncompetitive.
- **How much burden should be borne by employees vs. capital/management:** Labour insists on shared sacrifice; investors emphasize measurable savings and competitiveness, while the exact balance is unresolved.
- **Whether there are credible alternatives to closures:** Labour and the state believe there are; management and investors are unconvinced unless they deliver equivalent durable savings.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. social protection:** How to achieve enough durable cost reduction without closures or forced layoffs.
- **Speed vs. legitimacy:** Faster structural change may increase conflict; a more consensual route may reduce savings certainty.
- **Flexibility vs. guarantees:** Strong site/job guarantees constrain management’s ability to resize the footprint.
- **Short-term pain vs. long-term viability:** Parties disagree on how much immediate adjustment is necessary to protect future investment and industrial presence.
- **Who contributes how much:** The package lacks a concrete, quantified burden-sharing formula across employees, management, owners, and potentially the state.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and instruments**: quantify the required savings, identify which measures produce them, and compare alternatives side by side.
- Test whether a **no-closure / no-forced-layoff** package can still close the cost gap credibly.
- If not, clarify what limited adjustments might be acceptable in exchange for stronger guarantees and investment commitments.
- Aim for a single structured package with: savings target, implementation timeline, site protections, workforce measures, and ownership/management contributions.
