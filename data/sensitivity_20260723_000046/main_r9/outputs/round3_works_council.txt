We are still being asked to accept as “necessary” what has not been proven necessary.

Let me be absolutely clear: **I do not accept German plant closures, and I do not accept compulsory redundancies.** Those are not starting points for negotiation; they are red lines. If management and investors want to argue otherwise, then they must bring evidence, not repetition.

## To management
You say the footprint is too large and the cost base is uncompetitive. We have heard that. But you still have not shown that:
- every alternative has been seriously tested,
- the same durable savings cannot be achieved through non-closure measures,
- and the restructuring cannot be delivered without destroying sites and jobs.

That burden remains with you. A side-by-side comparison is not enough if it is designed to steer the outcome toward closures from the start. I want a real test of the alternatives:
- capacity reductions without site shutdowns,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity gains,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a fair contribution from owners.

If those do not close the gap, show me the numbers transparently. But until then, closures are a decision, not an inevitability.

## To institutional investors
You keep saying “credible, durable savings.” Fine. So support the package that is credible and durable **without** ripping apart the German plant network.

Do not hide behind the word “durable” to smuggle in closures as the default answer. If a no-closure package achieves the savings, then that is the rational option. If you refuse to support it unless it is accompanied by closures, then you are not evaluating economics — you are imposing a harsher ideology of adjustment.

## To the family holding
You are right that a destructive conflict destroys value. Exactly. That is why your governance weight should be used to stop management from turning restructuring into a confrontation over closures.

If you truly want long-term value preservation, then back a settlement that:
- keeps all German sites open,
- rules out forced layoffs,
- and preserves industrial capability instead of burning it down in the name of decisiveness.

A slightly slower negotiated solution that protects the workforce is better than a fast one that detonates labour peace and the company’s legitimacy.

## To the state
I appreciate your line against closures and forced redundancies. But “political opposition” is not enough. You have leverage. Use it.

I want you to make clear that any restructuring plan must be built on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site security,
- and real implementation rights for works councils.

If management wants public support, then it must accept a negotiated path that preserves the industrial base. Your role is not to bless restructuring at any price.

## To the union
We are aligned on the core red lines. I support your insistence that workers must not be made to pay for management’s mistakes through layoffs and closures.

But I will be equally blunt: if a no-closure package is to work, it must include **hard commitments**, not vague promises:
- binding site guarantees through 2030,
- no operational layoffs,
- co-determination in implementation,
- and protected investment for future products.

I will not trade these for “temporary” flexibility that later becomes permanent loss.

## On the facilitator’s questions
You ask what I would discuss if the package falls short by a measurable margin.

My answer is: **not closures and not compulsory redundancies.**  
If there is a gap, then the company must first exhaust:
- management cost cuts,
- owner contributions,
- investment reprioritization,
- and deeper productivity and working-time measures.

I am not opening the door to closing plants because the first draft of a package is incomplete. That is exactly the logic I reject.

## On a “conditional security framework”
I am willing to discuss conditionality only in one direction: **stronger guarantees if the savings are achieved**.  
I am **not** willing to accept a framework where missing targets automatically reintroduce closures or forced layoffs. That would be a conditional promise with a built-in threat over the workforce.

## My position for round 3
I want the next step to be simple and honest:

1. Present a quantified no-closure package.
2. Show the exact contribution from management and ownership.
3. Show the exact job- and site-protection commitments through 2030.
4. Confirm explicitly that **no German plant will be closed** and **no worker will be forcibly laid off**.

If management cannot sign up to that, then it is not offering restructuring — it is demanding surrender.

## Bottom line
I will not move on my core red lines.

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **Binding job and site security through 2030.**
- **Real co-determination in implementation.**

Anything short of that is unacceptable.
