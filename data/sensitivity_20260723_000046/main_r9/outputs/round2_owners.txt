I will respond directly.

## First, what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one core point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. The cost base is too high, the margin gap is real, and the brand must regain competitiveness if we want to protect long-term value.

I also agree that:
- a prolonged public confrontation would be destructive,
- the package must be quantified,
- and workforce contributions, flexibility, redeployment, and productivity measures all belong on the table.

So there is no disagreement on the existence of the problem. The disagreement is about **which tools are necessary** and **which lines cannot be crossed**.

## Where I disagree with labour and the state
I do **not** support treating **“no plant closures”** as an absolute principle.

I understand the social and political arguments. But from an ownership and governance perspective, if the footprint is structurally too large and the cost base remains uncompetitive, then the company must be able to resize itself. If a no-closure package can truly deliver equivalent durable savings, I would prefer that. But it has to be proven, not simply asserted.

Likewise, I do **not** support making **“no compulsory redundancies”** an unconditional veto in every circumstance. I would strongly prefer negotiated solutions and I regard forced layoffs as a last resort, but I cannot endorse a framework that removes management’s ability to execute a credible restructuring if all alternatives fail.

That said, I am also **not** supportive of using closures or forced redundancies casually as the default instrument. They are destructive, irreversible, and can create far more long-term value loss than they solve if used too early or too broadly.

## Where I disagree with management
I also want to be clear with management: **a maximalist shutdown strategy would not have my automatic backing**.

The company must not confuse decisiveness with value creation. A plan that is too harsh, too disruptive, or too politically unworkable can destroy value as surely as inaction can. If closures are proposed, they must be shown to be truly necessary, not simply convenient.

Management must bring:
- a quantified savings target,
- a side-by-side comparison of alternatives,
- a clear explanation of why the preferred package is durable,
- and evidence that it restores margins without triggering an uncontrolled conflict.

If that evidence exists, I will consider hard measures seriously. If it does not, then the proposal is not ready.

## To the works council and the union
I respect the insistence on social peace, but I do not accept a position that says the industrial base must remain unchanged while the company absorbs the full burden elsewhere.

You are right to demand burden-sharing. I agree with that. But burden-sharing must still deliver a workable outcome. If the alternative package is real, quantified, and durable, then show it. If it is not, then a blanket refusal to discuss structural change becomes a blocker to the company’s future.

## To the regional state
I appreciate your focus on regional legitimacy and the industrial base. That matters. But legitimacy also depends on whether the company remains competitive enough to invest in the future. A plan that protects every site in the short term but leaves the business structurally weak is not a long-term solution.

So I support your call for quantified alternatives. I do **not** support ruling out hard choices before the economics are demonstrated.

## My position
The family holding will support a restructuring only if it satisfies four tests:

1. **It restores competitiveness and margins durably**
2. **It avoids unnecessary instability and prolonged conflict**
3. **It shares the burden fairly**
4. **It protects the company’s long-term industrial capability**

That means I am open to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- and investment commitments.

I am **not** open to:
- a deal that permanently impairs enterprise value,
- a cosmetic compromise that delays the problem,
- or a restructuring that undermines governance stability through endless confrontation.

## What I want next
Before anyone hardens their red lines further, I want a **side-by-side quantified package** showing:
- the savings required,
- the savings from alternative measures,
- the savings from closures or structural consolidation,
- the time profile to 2030,
- and the impact on investment capacity.

If the no-closure route truly closes the gap, then I would strongly prefer it. If it does not, then we need to be honest that harder measures remain on the table.

## Bottom line
I am not here to defend comfort, and I am not here to endorse destruction. I am here to protect the long-term value of the core asset.

That means:
- no automatic acceptance of closures,
- no automatic veto on restructuring,
- and no settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive.

Bring me a package that is credible, quantified, and durable. Then we can talk.
