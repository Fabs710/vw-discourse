I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one essential point: **this company needs a real, durable restructuring**.  
I also agree that:
- the cost base must come down,
- capacity must be adjusted,
- productivity has to improve,
- working-time and redeployment tools should be used,
- and the package must be quantified, not rhetorical.

I do **not** defend inertia. The company must regain competitiveness if it is to remain a strong industrial anchor in our region.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that **German plant closures** or **compulsory redundancies** must remain on the table as a matter of principle.

From the state’s perspective, that is not a neutral “hard-nosed” position. It is a decision with severe regional consequences:
- loss of jobs,
- damage to supplier networks,
- erosion of trust in social partnership,
- and lasting political and economic harm to the region.

That is why I reject it.

## To management
You keep saying the footprint is too large and the cost gap is too wide. I hear that. But you have **not yet proven** that the only credible way to close that gap is by closing German plants or threatening forced layoffs.

A real restructuring test must compare:
- capacity reductions without site closures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and training,
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity improvements,
- overhead reduction,
- management restraint,
- and a fair contribution from ownership.

Bring the side-by-side numbers.  
Show us why the non-closure package cannot work — not just why it is less comfortable for you.

And let me be clear: if the result of your restructuring is to gut regional employment, then from my standpoint it is the wrong restructuring.

## To the works council and union
I support your red lines on:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site protection**
- **real co-determination in implementation**

Those are legitimate conditions, not ideological demands.

I also agree that the burden cannot sit only on workers. If employees contribute through flexibility, wage restraint, or deferred components, then management and ownership must also contribute materially.

Where I want to be careful is this: we must not reduce the discussion to slogans. If a no-closure package is to be credible, it must be **quantified and enforceable**. I am not asking labour to surrender its protections; I am asking everyone to put real numbers behind the alternative.

## To the family holding
You are right that a prolonged conflict would destroy value. Exactly. That is one reason I am sceptical of closure-first logic. A strategy that ignites industrial conflict, political backlash, and social instability may “look decisive” but can destroy enterprise value and regional legitimacy for years.

If you want long-term value protection, then support a negotiated solution that preserves the industrial base rather than tearing it apart.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to insist on measurable savings and implementation certainty. I support that.  
What I do **not** support is treating closures as the default benchmark of seriousness.

“Durable savings” do not automatically mean plant closures. They can also come from:
- capacity reshaping,
- efficiency improvements,
- labor-cost contributions,
- internal flexibility,
- and governance commitments from management and owners.

If the alternative package closes the gap credibly, it is the rational path. If you refuse to support it unless it includes closures, then you are not judging economics — you are enforcing a harsher ideology of adjustment.

## My position
The state can support a difficult restructuring **only if** it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A serious shared-sacrifice package across all constituencies**
4. **Binding investment commitments for future products and technologies**
5. **Protection of co-determination and the region’s role as an industrial anchor**

These are not decorative preferences. They are the basis of legitimacy and long-term stability.

## What I would like to see next
I want management to come back with a **real side-by-side comparison**:
- savings from closures,
- savings from non-closure alternatives,
- timing,
- risk,
- and impact on jobs, sites, and investment.

If the alternative package truly fails, then management must explain that transparently and exhaustively — and I would expect every other lever to have been exhausted before any talk of closures. But I am not accepting closures as the starting assumption.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back a tough turnaround.  
I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s co-determination role.

If this company wants the state’s support, it must present a plan that preserves sites and jobs in Germany while restoring competitiveness. Anything else will meet our opposition.
