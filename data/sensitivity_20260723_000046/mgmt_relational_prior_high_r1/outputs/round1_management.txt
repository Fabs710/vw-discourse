I hear the red lines being set, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic restructuring is not an option**.

We are in an acute competitiveness crisis. German factory costs are structurally above target, the European market is shrinking, and we are facing intense pressure from lower-cost competitors. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target, and without a real correction we will not have the funds to invest in the next generation of products and technology through 2030.

So let me state my position plainly:

- **We need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.**
- **We need a meaningful labour-cost contribution.**
- **We need structural change, not just temporary pain relief.**

Where I agree with you:
- I do agree that the burden should not be borne by one side alone.
- I do agree that qualification, redeployment, and internal adjustment should be used where they are genuinely effective.
- I do agree that we need a negotiated settlement, because we cannot simply dictate outcomes in this governance structure.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** accept that every German plant must be kept open regardless of economics.
- I do **not** accept a settlement that preserves all sites and all guarantees while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.
- I do **not** accept that the company can fund its future while avoiding the hard decisions that make that future possible.

If the answer is “no closures under any circumstances,” then you are effectively asking management to preserve overcapacity and hope the market improves. That is not a plan. That is delay.

That said, I am not fixated on closures as an end in themselves. What matters is the outcome: **structural savings at a scale that restores competitiveness**. If you can bring forward an alternative package that delivers comparable, durable cost and capacity reduction — not just a short-term bridge — then I will engage seriously. But it must include real numbers, not slogans:

- measurable capacity reduction,
- a genuine labour-cost contribution,
- binding productivity improvements,
- and a credible path to restore margins and investment capacity.

Without that, we are left with a proposal that protects every existing structure except the company’s future. That I cannot support.

And let me be explicit on time: prolonged confrontation, strikes, and reputational damage help no one. But neither does a settlement that postpones the problem. If we are to avoid escalation, we need a package that actually changes the cost base, not one that merely softens the optics.
