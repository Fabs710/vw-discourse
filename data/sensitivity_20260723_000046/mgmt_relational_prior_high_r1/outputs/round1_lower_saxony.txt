Management is right on one point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company does need a durable improvement in competitiveness, and I do not dispute that Germany’s cost base must be brought closer to reality.

But I need to be just as clear: **plant closures in Germany are not an acceptable first resort**, and neither are compulsory redundancies or a one-sided dismantling of job-security guarantees. From the regional state’s perspective, that would not be a responsible restructuring — it would be a reckless destruction of industrial capacity, skilled employment, and regional stability.

So my position is this:

- **I agree** that the status quo is not sustainable.
- **I agree** that savings must be real, measurable, and durable.
- **I agree** that all stakeholders must contribute.
- **I disagree fundamentally** with using German plant closures as the default instrument to achieve that.

What I need to see instead is a **serious negotiated restructuring package** that protects sites and jobs while delivering genuine savings. That means:
- capacity reductions through internal restructuring, not closures;
- working-time and flexibility solutions;
- redeployment and qualification;
- a fair employee contribution that is time-bound and linked to binding investment and site guarantees;
- management and ownership contributions as well, not just labour’s contribution.

A package that preserves every current cost structure except labour is not fair, and a package that shifts all risk to the workforce is politically and socially unacceptable. The burden must be shared.

I also want to be explicit about governance: **the state will not accept being sidelined**. Under the special statute, we have a legitimate veto role for a reason. Any solution must respect co-determination and the regional public interest. That is not obstructionism; it is part of the company’s governance and social contract.

So if management wants our support, it needs to come forward with:
1. a concrete alternative to closures,
2. quantified savings,
3. binding guarantees on German sites and employment,
4. and a credible investment path for future products.

If those conditions are met, I am prepared to support a tough but socially responsible restructuring. If not, then I will stand with the workforce against any attempt to force through closures and mass layoffs.
