I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the finance expert, and the investors on the central point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a symbolic one**. The current cost base is too high for the market reality, and if we do not restore competitiveness and margins, we will erode long-term value for everyone, including the controlling family.

I also agree with labour and the state on one important issue: **a prolonged conflict would be value-destructive**. Strikes, reputational damage, and operational disruption are not acceptable outcomes. A negotiated solution is clearly preferable if it is credible.

And I agree that internal measures should be fully used:
- redeployment
- qualification
- working-time flexibility
- shift rationalisation
- productivity improvements
- overhead and management contribution

Those are legitimate first-line measures.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the attempt to make **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no material change to guarantees”** into an absolute framework.

That is not a governance position I can support unless the numbers prove that it still closes the savings gap. So far, the discussion has not done that. What we have heard is:
- management says the gap may be too large for internal measures alone,
- labour says closures and compulsory redundancies are unacceptable,
- the state says closures should not be the first resort,
- and the expert says the feasibility of a non-closure solution depends on the size of the gap.

That is not yet a case for preserving every site and every guarantee.

## On management’s case
Management is right to insist on reality. If the company needs a step-change in cost and capacity, then we cannot hide behind slogans.

But I also think management should be careful not to overstate its case. Saying “closures may be necessary” is not the same as proving that they are necessary. The burden is still to show:
- the actual EBIT uplift required,
- the savings available from non-closure measures,
- the residual gap,
- and what footprint reduction is truly needed.

Without that, I will not give a blank cheque for closures.

## On the works council and union
I understand and respect the insistence on job protection and co-determination. It is not in my interest to create a destructive confrontation.

But I do **not** accept:
- no closures in all circumstances,
- no compulsory redundancies in all circumstances,
- and no meaningful change to long-standing guarantees if they block competitiveness.

Those positions may be politically understandable, but they are not sufficient if the company’s economics do not work. Long-term value cannot be protected by preserving every structure regardless of market conditions.

## On the regional state
I respect the state’s role and its legitimate concern for regional stability. But the state cannot use its position to freeze the company in an uncompetitive shape.

The public interest is not served by protecting every current site if the result is a weaker company with less investment capacity. The state should support a viable industrial future, not an illusion of permanence.

## On the investors
I agree with the investors that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. They are right to demand a real margin reset. But I do not support a reflexive embrace of closures either. The right answer is the one that closes the gap with the least overall damage.

## What I need to see next
At this point, the discussion needs a **quantified scenario table**. I want the finance and operations teams to put forward a plant-by-plant comparison of:

1. **Non-closure scenario**
   - labor-cost reset
   - shift cuts
   - redeployment and attrition
   - overhead reduction
   - productivity gains
   - annual savings
   - one-time costs
   - employment impact
   - residual gap

2. **Hybrid scenario**
   - internal measures plus selective footprint rationalisation
   - annual savings
   - one-time costs
   - employment impact
   - capacity removed
   - impact on margins

3. **Closure-based scenario**
   - savings
   - one-time restructuring cost
   - headcount impact
   - execution risk
   - effect on competitiveness

## My position
Let me be explicit:

- I support a serious restructuring.
- I support burden-sharing.
- I support internal measures where they genuinely work.
- I support a non-closure solution **if** it is sufficient.
- I do **not** accept “no closures” as an absolute principle.
- I do **not** accept a deal that permanently weakens competitiveness or traps the company in structural overcapacity.
- I do **not** accept governance control being diluted or bypassed through pressure.

If the numbers show that a non-closure package truly closes the gap, I will prefer it because it likely reduces conflict and preserves more of the industrial base. But if the numbers do not close the gap, then we must not pretend otherwise.

## Bottom line
We should stop debating principles in the abstract and force the arithmetic.

If internal measures can deliver the required durable savings, good.  
If they cannot, then some form of capacity rationalisation must remain available.  

That is the only responsible position for a controlling shareholder committed to long-term value, stability, and competitiveness.
