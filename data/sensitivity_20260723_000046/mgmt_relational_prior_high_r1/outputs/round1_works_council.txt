As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for a restructuring that management failed to prepare responsibly.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. If the company needs a restructuring, then it must come through negotiated, socially responsible measures: capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, working-time solutions, qualification and transformation programs, and a fair contribution from management and shareholders — but with every German plant kept open and with binding guarantees for jobs and sites.

Our priorities are straightforward:
- preserve every German plant,
- prevent forced layoffs,
- secure employment and investment through 2030,
- maintain co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and ensure the burden is shared fairly.

If management wants cooperation, it must come with a credible alternative to closures and dismissals. If it comes with a fait accompli, then it should expect fierce resistance — in the supervisory board, in collective bargaining, and, if necessary, through industrial action.
