I agree with the Works Council on the core points: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of job-security guarantees**.

Let me be equally clear from the collective-bargaining side: the workforce will not be asked to carry a one-sided austerity program while management and owners remain untouched. If the company wants a durable settlement, then it needs a **shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs and sites and distributes the burden fairly.

What I can support:
- **Capacity reduction through negotiated, reversible measures**, not closures.
- **Working-time adjustments**, short-time style solutions, internal redeployment, and qualification.
- **Temporary or targeted wage moderation**, but only if it is part of a broader package.
- **Employee contributions** if they are limited, time-bound, and tied to binding guarantees.
- **Management and owner contributions** as a condition, not an afterthought.

What I reject:
- Plant closures as a restructuring tool.
- Forced layoffs.
- Open-ended insecurity dressed up as “flexibility.”
- Cuts imposed only on labour while capital is spared.

So if management’s proposal is simply “more pain for workers to fix strategic mistakes,” that will fail. If, however, there is a serious alternative that achieves savings **without destroying sites and jobs**, backed by investment commitments and fair contributions from all sides, then we are ready to negotiate hard and constructively.

And to be explicit: we have already begun mobilising, and if the company tries to force through closures or redundancies, we will escalate further.
