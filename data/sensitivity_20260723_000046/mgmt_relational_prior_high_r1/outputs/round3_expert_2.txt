\[

## 4) Plant-by-plant economics framework

You asked for a plant-by-plant bridge. Since no plant-specific data is disclosed here, the correct way to structure it is by **site economics**:

For each German plant, estimate:

1. **Current annual fixed cost**
   - plant overhead
   - maintenance
   - utilities
   - local management
   - depreciation/lease burden
2. **Variable cost per unit**
3. **Current utilization**
4. **Break-even volume**
5. **Savings available from non-closure measures**
6. **Incremental savings from line consolidation or partial shutdown**
7. **Exit cost if the plant is closed**

### Decision rule by plant
A plant becomes a candidate for deeper action if:

\[
\text{Utilization} < \text{Break-even utilization}
\]

and

\[
\text{Savings from internal measures} < \text{Required cost reduction}
\]

In practical terms:

- **High utilization, strong product pipeline** → candidate for non-closure measures only
- **Medium utilization, shrinking volumes** → candidate for hybrid footprint reduction
- **Low utilization, high fixed cost** → likely needs closure or equivalent consolidation

## 6) Minimum capacity reduction logic if closures are avoided

If the company wants to avoid closures entirely, the minimum required capacity reduction usually has to come from:

- shift removal
- line shutdown for specific models
- fewer operating days
- more efficient sequencing
- internal transfer of volume
- lower staffing density

However, those measures typically remove **effective capacity**, not all site cost.

### Rule of thumb
To materially restore margins **without closure**, the company would often need:

- **10%–15% effective capacity reduction**
- plus a meaningful labor-cost reset
- plus overhead cuts
- plus at least moderate productivity gains

If the needed savings gap exceeds what those measures can deliver, the residual gap remains.

## 8) Cost base decomposition: what matters most

From an operations finance perspective, the cost base typically breaks into:

### Fixed / semi-fixed
- plant overhead
- maintenance
- depreciation
- engineering support
- site management
- utilities base load
- logistics infrastructure
- salaried labor

### Variable
- direct labor tied to output
- purchased materials
- energy usage per unit
- logistics per unit
- consumables

### Why this matters
If the problem is mainly variable cost, then:
- productivity
- shift patterns
- labor concessions
- supplier renegotiation
can solve much of it.

If the problem is mainly fixed cost and underutilization, then:
- closures
- line transfers
- footprint rationalization
are far more effective.

The conversation strongly suggests the latter is a major issue.
