## Core impasse

The plateau appears to rest on **one unresolved structural conflict**:

- **Labour / state / parts of the shareholder side** want to **remove closures and compulsory redundancies from the default option** until a verified plant-by-plant bridge proves they are unavoidable.
- **Management / family / investors** want to **keep closures and forced reductions available in principle now**, arguing that otherwise the restructuring cannot credibly restore competitiveness.

So the deadlock is not over the need for restructuring. It is over **sequence and optionality**:
1. **Do we first test an all-sites-open scenario**, or
2. **Do we keep closure and compulsory redundancy as live fallback instruments from the start**?

A second, related impasse concerns **trust and verification**:
- all sides demand numbers,
- but no one will relax red lines before the numbers are provided.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Two-stage decision framework
**Facilitator proposal:** Adopt a formally sequenced process:
1. **Stage 1:** A verified plant-by-plant bridge tests the **all-sites-open** scenario first.
2. **Stage 2:** Only if the bridge shows a residual gap above an agreed threshold do the parties reopen discussion on footprint reduction.

**Safeguard elements:**
- closures are **not ruled in** at Stage 1,
- but they are also **not permanently ruled out**,
- the threshold and methodology must be agreed in advance,
- no unilateral announcements during Stage 1.

This may bridge the gap between “no closures as first resort” and “closures must remain available if needed.”

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional non-closure pact with hard safeguards
**Facilitator proposal:** A time-limited non-closure pact for a defined period, paired with strict conditions:
- all German plants stay open for the pact period,
- no compulsory redundancies during that period,
- management and owners commit quantified contributions,
- labour accepts limited, temporary contribution only,
- independent verification of savings and utilization occurs plant by plant.

If the agreed savings target is not reached, the parties automatically return to negotiations with the verified gap, rather than predetermining closure.

This may bridge the gap between protection for sites/jobs and management’s need for a serious restructuring test.

---

### Proposal 3: Hybrid capacity reduction without immediate plant closures
**Facilitator proposal:** Replace the binary choice of “all open” vs “closures” with a hybrid package:
- site specialization,
- shift elimination,
- line consolidation,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- reduced temporary labour,
- overhead cuts,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management/owner contributions.

This keeps plants open but still reduces capacity and fixed cost materially. It may give management enough cost relief while preserving labour’s red lines against immediate closures and compulsory layoffs.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Works Council
**If the verified bridge shows that an all-sites-open package still leaves a material residual gap, what specific additional non-closure measures would you accept, and what is the maximum level of workforce reduction you would tolerate without triggering your red line on closures?**

### To Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited, quantified employee contribution if it is paired with binding site guarantees, no compulsory redundancies, and a verified all-sites-open test first? If yes, what exact conditions would make that acceptable?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to a verified all-sites-open scenario as the first test, with no closure announcement before the plant-by-plant bridge is complete, and if not, what concrete evidence do you have that the non-closure path cannot close the gap?**

### To Regional State Government
**Will you use your governance role to insist that the all-sites-open scenario is fully tested before any closure option is activated, and if the numbers remain short, what specific protections will you require before you would even consider footprint reduction?**

### To Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to support a non-closure test period with binding job and site guarantees, if that improves trust and reduces conflict risk, even if it temporarily limits management’s freedom to announce closures?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified cost and margin improvement would make an all-sites-open restructuring acceptable to you, and would you support a hybrid solution if it meets that threshold even without closures?**

### To the Expert
**What exact data points are missing from the plant-by-plant bridge to determine whether the non-closure path is sufficient, and what threshold would you use to distinguish “moderate” from “large” savings gaps?**
