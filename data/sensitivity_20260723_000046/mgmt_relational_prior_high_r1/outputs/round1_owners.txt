I appreciate the positions being laid out clearly. From the controlling shareholder perspective, I want to be equally direct: **the company needs a restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins, and it cannot be a purely symbolic exercise**.

Where I agree:
- Management is right that **the current cost base is not sustainable**.
- The workforce and the state are right that **a disorderly, one-sided solution would destroy value**.
- I also agree that **burden-sharing matters**. This is not a problem that labour alone can solve.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** support a settlement that simply preserves the status quo and defers the adjustment.
- I do **not** support an outcome that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive while giving everyone a political headline.
- I also do **not** want a prolonged confrontation that damages the brand, burns cash, and weakens the enterprise for years.

My key point is this: **value preservation comes first, but value preservation is not the same as preserving every existing structure.** If the business needs capacity reduction, productivity gains, and labour-cost adjustments to survive and invest, then those must happen. The question is how, not whether anything changes.

At the same time, I am **not** in favour of plant closures as a default or first-choice instrument if comparable savings can be achieved by other durable means. Closures may be economically justified in some cases, but they are also highly destructive, legally complex, and likely to trigger exactly the kind of instability we must avoid. A settlement that avoids closures while still delivering real structural savings would be preferable — **but only if it is credible and quantified**.

So my position is:
- I will support **serious restructuring**.
- I will support **capacity reduction** if it is real.
- I will support **labour contributions** if they are meaningful and durable.
- I will support **investment commitments** if they are tied to a viable cost base.
- I will **not** support an agreement that permanently weakens governance control or locks in uncompetitive economics.

To the works council and the union: if you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that actually replaces the savings management says are needed. “No closures” is not enough by itself. I need to see:
- quantified cost reductions,
- credible productivity improvements,
- site-specific solutions,
- and a timetable that restores margins without endless interim bargaining.

To management: if you want support from owners and governance, then you must show that the restructuring is not just a blunt-cut exercise. You need a plan that is financially convincing and politically survivable. If you push for closures without exhausting serious alternatives, you may win a confrontation and lose the company’s stability.

To the regional state: your role is important, and I respect the governance structure. But that veto cannot become a veto on necessary change. The company must remain competitive, or the entire industrial base erodes over time. Public interest is served by a viable enterprise, not by postponing hard decisions indefinitely.

So my message is simple: **I am open to a negotiated solution, but only if it delivers durable competitiveness.** If the alternative to closures is real, measurable, and sufficient, I am prepared to back it. If not, then we should not pretend that preserving every current site and guarantee is a sustainable strategy.
