Facilitator assessment: the discourse has plateaued because the parties are no longer disputing the need for restructuring; they are disputing **the threshold for irreversible measures**.

## Core impasse
The main deadlock is between:
- **Labour / Works Council / State**: will only accept a quantified package that preserves job security, sites, and co-determination, with closures and compulsory redundancies excluded unless fully disproven.
- **Management / Family / Investors**: insist that closures and forced redundancies must remain available if the savings gap cannot be closed otherwise.
- **Expert**: has narrowed the issue to a numbers problem, but has not yet supplied the plant-level bridge needed to determine whether non-closure is sufficient.

So the impasse is not “restructuring or no restructuring.” It is:
**What level of cost gap justifies footprint reduction, and what binding protections can exist while that question is tested?**

## Facilitator proposals for compromise
These are not positions, but possible bridging options for discussion:

### 1) Conditional no-closure pact with objective triggers
A time-limited agreement could state:
- no closures or compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- in exchange for a quantified employee contribution,
- but only if a jointly agreed plant-by-plant bridge shows the remaining gap stays below a pre-defined threshold.

If the residual gap exceeds that threshold, the parties move automatically to a second-stage negotiation rather than immediately to unilateral action.

### 2) Two-track restructuring package
Negotiate two linked scenarios at once:
- **Track A:** a non-closure package with labour, management, and owner contributions;
- **Track B:** a fallback package with deeper footprint rationalisation, but only activated if Track A fails to close the gap after verification.

This would let each side test seriousness without committing immediately to the most conflictual outcome.

### 3) Binding site guarantees with controlled flexibility
Instead of absolute permanence or open-ended closure rights, the parties could discuss:
- site guarantees for a defined period,
- protected employment transition instruments,
- and a jointly monitored capacity adjustment mechanism.

This would preserve core assurances while giving management a structured path to adjustment if volumes or savings targets are missed.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after all non-closure measures are fully and transparently counted, and what is your plant-by-plant threshold for declaring closures unavoidable?**

### To the Works Council
**If a verified scenario shows that internal measures close most, but not all, of the gap, what limited and time-bound flexibility would you consider in exchange for binding site guarantees?**

### To the union
**What concrete employee contribution could you support that is temporary, measurable, and reversible, while still preserving your no-compulsory-redundancies red line?**

### To the regional state
**Would you support a conditional restructuring framework that preserves your veto role but allows a pre-agreed escalation path if the quantified gap cannot otherwise be closed?**

### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to accept a staged process that delays footprint decisions until the plant-level evidence is complete, even if that limits immediate strategic flexibility?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you endorse a non-closure solution if it is verified to close the EBIT gap with acceptable execution risk, even if it preserves more capacity than you initially prefer?**

### To the expert / finance lead
**Can you provide a single, audited scenario table that separates savings from non-closure measures, the residual gap, and the incremental value of footprint reduction site by site?**
