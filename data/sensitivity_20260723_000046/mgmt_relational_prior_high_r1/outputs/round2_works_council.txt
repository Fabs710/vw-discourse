Let me respond very clearly.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. I also agree that burden-sharing is necessary and that internal redeployment, qualification, working-time solutions, and productivity improvements must be part of the answer.

But I will not move on the core issues.

## What is non-negotiable
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework**

Those are not bargaining chips for the works council. They are the minimum conditions for any settlement that can even be discussed seriously.

## On management’s argument
Management keeps saying the cost base is uncompetitive and therefore closures may be necessary. I do not accept that logic as the starting point.

You have not yet shown that all non-destructive options are exhausted. Before anyone talks about closing a German plant, I expect a **quantified alternative package** that demonstrates:
- how much savings are required,
- how much can be delivered through capacity adjustments without closures,
- how much can be achieved through internal redeployment and working-time changes,
- what management itself contributes,
- and what owners contribute.

Until that is on the table, “closures are necessary” is just a pressure tactic.

## On the shareholders
The family shareholder and the capital markets are both talking about competitiveness and value preservation. Fine. But value is not preserved by destroying the company’s industrial base and social legitimacy.

A “durable” solution that tears up trust, triggers strikes, and breaks co-determination will not be durable in practice. It will create conflict, legal risk, and operational damage. That is not responsible governance.

## On the regional state
I welcome the state’s clear rejection of closures and forced layoffs. But I need more than general support. The state should use its blocking position to prevent any attempt to force through plant closures under the guise of restructuring. If there is no non-closure package, then there should be no approval.

## What a serious alternative must contain
If management wants cooperation, then bring a package that includes:

1. **Binding site guarantees for all German plants through 2030**
2. **No forced redundancies**
3. **Capacity reduction through shifts, allocation, and internal restructuring only**
4. **Qualification and redeployment commitments**
5. **Management and shareholder contributions**
6. **A negotiated employee contribution only if it is temporary, limited, and explicitly linked to job and site guarantees**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework**

Without these elements, there is nothing to negotiate from our side.

## What I reject in the other arguments
- To management: “structural change” does **not** automatically mean closures.
- To the shareholders: “value preservation” does **not** justify forcing workers to pay for strategic mistakes.
- To anyone suggesting flexibility: flexibility is acceptable only if it protects jobs and sites; otherwise it is just insecurity by another name.

## The real test
If the company can present a non-closure scenario that truly closes the savings gap, then let us examine it. But if the proposal still contains plant closures or compulsory layoffs, then it fails at the outset.

So my position remains unchanged:
**keep every German plant open, rule out compulsory redundancies, and negotiate a restructuring that protects jobs, sites, and co-determination through 2030.** If that is not possible, then management is not asking for restructuring — it is asking the workforce to accept dismantlement. And that we will resist.
