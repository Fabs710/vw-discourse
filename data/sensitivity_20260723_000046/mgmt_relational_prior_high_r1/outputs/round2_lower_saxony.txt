I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family shareholder, and the investors on one essential point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company does need a durable improvement in competitiveness, and the expert has confirmed that purely temporary measures may be insufficient if the savings gap is large.

I also agree that:
- burden-sharing is necessary,
- working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and productivity measures must be part of the package,
- and the company needs a quantified plan, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the conclusion that plant closures are the default answer.

The finance expert’s assessment does **not** prove that closures are inevitable. It says something narrower: if the gap is very large, non-closure solutions may be insufficient. That is not the same as saying closures must happen. It means management still has to prove, with plant-level numbers, that all other options are inadequate.

And that proof has not been provided.

### On the works council and union
I respect the clear red lines on:
- no forced redundancies,
- no one-sided burden on labour,
- no dismantling of the social partnership model.

Those positions are legitimate, and I will not treat them as obstructionism. The workforce did not create this crisis alone.

### On management
Management keeps speaking in generalities about structural change, but the burden of proof remains with you. If you want even the possibility of closures, then you need to show:
- the savings gap by plant,
- what internal measures can realistically deliver,
- what portion of the gap remains,
- and why that remainder cannot be handled through partial footprint adjustment, shifts, redeployment, and negotiated labour contributions.

Right now, the argument is still too abstract to justify plant closures in Germany.

## My line
From the regional state’s perspective, I can support **hard restructuring**. I cannot support **industrial shrinkage at the expense of German sites and regional employment**.

That means:

- **No German plant closures as a first resort**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No sidelining of the state’s veto and co-determination role**
- **No settlement that destroys the company’s role as a regional economic anchor**

If management wants my support, it must come forward with a package that preserves sites and jobs **while still delivering credible savings**. That package must be concrete, not rhetorical.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a tough compromise if it contains all of the following:
1. **Binding site guarantees**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **A quantified employee contribution that is limited and time-bound**
4. **Management and owner contributions**
5. **Internal capacity reduction before any talk of closures**
6. **Investment commitments tied to the restructuring**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s governance role**

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures presented as inevitable before alternatives are exhausted,
- mass layoffs,
- a restructuring that hollowes out regional employment,
- or a governance outcome that treats the state as a nuisance rather than a legitimate anchor shareholder.

## My challenge to management
Before asking for closure authority, bring a **plant-by-plant non-closure scenario** with numbers:
- expected savings,
- capacity reduction,
- utilization changes,
- employment effects,
- and the gap versus your target.

If that scenario truly fails, then we can discuss the remaining options. But not before.

## Final position
I am open to a negotiated restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and jobs as the easy way out.

The company’s future matters. So do the people and regions that built it.
