### Neutral Moderator Summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- **The company needs a real restructuring**, not a cosmetic one.
- **Burden-sharing is necessary**.
- **Internal measures should be used first where possible**, including:
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - shift rationalisation,
  - productivity improvements,
  - overhead and management contributions.
- A **negotiated solution is preferable to prolonged conflict**.
- The next step must be **quantified**, not rhetorical: parties want a **plant-by-plant, numbers-based savings bridge**.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works council, union, and state oppose closures as a first resort and in some cases categorically.  
  - Management, family shareholder, and investors keep closures or footprint rationalisation on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Labour representatives reject them.  
  - Management and some shareholders will not rule them out if required by the economics.
- **Job-security / co-determination framework**:  
  - Labour seeks preservation through 2030 or longer.  
  - Management and shareholders see current guarantees as potentially blocking necessary change.
- **Whether a non-closure solution is sufficient**:  
  - Labour and the state believe this has not been disproven.  
  - Management and investors argue the burden of proof must be met with hard numbers, and that non-closure measures may fall short if the savings gap is large.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
The discussion still has not resolved:
- **How large the savings gap actually is**.
- **How much can realistically be achieved without closures**.
- **How much capacity reduction is possible through shifts, line consolidation, or internal reallocation alone**.
- **What the employment impact would be under each scenario**.
- **What contributions each side is prepared to make**:
  - labour,
  - management,
  - owners,
  - and, where relevant, the state.
- **What combination of measures would be sufficient to restore competitiveness while limiting conflict and preserving legitimacy**.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Next round should move from principle to **quantified comparison**. The key ask is a **side-by-side scenario table** covering:
- **non-closure**,
- **hybrid**,
- **closure-based** options,

with:
- annual savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- headcount impact,
- capacity removed,
- utilization effects,
- and expected margin/ROS improvement.

The discussion should then focus on **which package closes the gap with the least overall damage and the clearest implementation path**.
