## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one essential point: **the company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. The cost gap, the weak ROS, and the structural pressure from competition are real. I also agree that:
- burden-sharing is necessary,
- internal measures must be fully exhausted,
- redeployment, qualification, working-time flexibility, and productivity gains belong on the table,
- management and owners must contribute,
- and we need a **verified plant-by-plant savings bridge**, not more abstract claims.

So far, that is the only common ground I see.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept management’s attempt to keep **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** permanently available as a default answer while the numbers are still unverified.

That is not a neutral position. It is a pressure tactic.

The expert has been careful: **non-closure can work if the savings gap is moderate**. That is not the same as saying closures are necessary. It means the burden is still on management to prove, transparently and site by site, that the non-closure route is insufficient.

Until that proof is on the table, I will not treat closures as inevitable.

## To management

- the exact savings gap,
- the fixed-cost burden by site,
- what non-closure measures deliver,
- what management and owner contributions actually are,
- and what residual gap remains.

Without that, your closure argument is premature.

You want flexibility; I want credibility. Credibility comes first.

And I need to be explicit: **I will not support German plant closures as a first resort, and I will not support compulsory redundancies as the default instrument.** If you cannot restructure without gutting regional employment, then the restructuring design is flawed, not the workforce.

## To the Works Council and Union
I agree with your insistence that workers should not be the sole shock absorber. I also agree that no settlement should be built on fear, forced layoffs, or a permanent erosion of guarantees.

But I want to sharpen the point: I support your position **only if** we stay disciplined and quantified. If the non-closure package is viable, it must be shown in numbers, not asserted in principle.

I am also open to a **time-limited, reversible employee contribution** if — and only if — it is tied to:
- binding job and site guarantees,
- preservation of co-determination,
- and real management/owner contribution.

That is the fair part of burden-sharing. What I will not accept is a deal where labour gives and everyone else keeps optionality.

## My political and governance line
I need to state this clearly: **the state’s co-determination role and veto rights are not bargaining chips.** They are part of the company’s governance architecture and part of its social legitimacy.

I will not support any path that:
- sidelines the state,
- weakens its blocking role,
- or uses restructuring as a pretext to erode the region’s stake in the company.

That is not just a political preference. It is a non-negotiable part of preserving the company as a regional economic anchor.

## What I will support
I can support a serious restructuring if it includes:
1. a quantified non-closure package first,
2. binding site and job guarantees,
3. no compulsory redundancies,
4. management and owner contributions,
5. internal capacity reduction as the primary tool,
6. preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role,
7. and a time-limited employee contribution only in exchange for real security.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures presented as “inevitable” before the numbers are proven,
- forced layoffs as a default instrument,
- or a deal that sacrifices regional employment to preserve managerial flexibility.

I am not here to block restructuring. I am here to prevent a bad restructuring from destroying the company’s regional base and social legitimacy.

If the verified numbers show that an all-sites-open package can close the gap, then that is the best outcome: it protects jobs, protects the sites, and avoids an unnecessary conflict spiral.

If the numbers do **not** show that, then we discuss the residual gap — but only after the non-closure route has been honestly and fully tested.

- **no compulsory redundancies as the default**
