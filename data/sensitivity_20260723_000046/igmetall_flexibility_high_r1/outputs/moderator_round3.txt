## Core impasse

The main blockage is **not agreement on the need for restructuring**. Most parties accept that the company needs lower cost, better productivity, and more investment capacity.

The impasse is over **the acceptable instruments and the order in which they are tested**:

1. **Worker-side stakeholders** insist on:
   - no German plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - preserved job security,
   - protected co-determination.

2. **Management and capital-side stakeholders** insist that:
   - closures and redundancies must remain available,
   - the restructuring must deliver durable structural savings,
   - guarantees cannot be unconditional.

3. **The state and ownership** are split between:
   - avoiding political/social damage from closures,
   - and ensuring the package is economically credible.

So the unresolved core is:

**Can the savings and capacity reduction target be met by non-closure measures alone, or must structural footprint reduction remain available as a fallback?**

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Sequenced restructuring with a “non-closure first” test
A time-bound process could be established in which all parties agree to test internal measures first, before any closure decision is considered.

**Elements:**
- site-by-site savings plan,
- quantified contributions from labor, management, and owners,
- 6–12 month implementation window,
- closure decisions suspended during the test period,
- predefined trigger point if savings gap remains materially open.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It addresses worker concerns by preventing immediate closure action, while preserving management’s claim that structural options remain available if quantified targets are not met.

---

### Proposal 2: Partial footprint adjustment plus binding site guarantees for the remaining footprint
Instead of “all sites stay” versus “closures on the table,” parties could consider a package that:
- protects strategically essential sites,
- permits limited consolidation or partial site reduction elsewhere,
- pairs that with binding investment and job-security commitments for the retained footprint.

**Elements:**
- identify core sites to be guaranteed,
- identify sites or functions open to consolidation,
- no compulsory redundancies where possible, with voluntary exit mechanisms first,
- investment commitments tied to the retained plants.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It gives management and investors some structural savings, while giving labor and the state a clearer guarantee that the industrial base is not being broadly dismantled.

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional guarantees tied to measurable performance milestones
A compromise could link job and site protections to explicit performance and savings milestones.

**Elements:**
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies unless pre-agreed financial triggers are missed,
- milestone-based review at fixed intervals,
- binding commitments on productivity, overhead, and labor-cost relief,
- management and owner contributions formally quantified,
- automatic escalation mechanism if targets fail.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It converts the conflict from a binary yes/no over closures into a rules-based framework with measurable conditions.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact quantified savings gap remains after you apply every non-closure instrument site by site, and what minimum threshold would make you formally rule out closures for the duration of the agreement?**

### To the Works Council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies are excluded as absolute red lines, what specific quantified labor and capacity contributions are you prepared to commit to so the non-closure package remains economically credible?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What binding concessions on labor cost, working time, and voluntary workforce reduction would you accept in exchange for a formal non-closure commitment through a defined period?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Would the state support a time-limited non-closure test period with objective savings triggers, even if that means accepting a predefined fallback mechanism after the test period ends?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Under what exact financial and operational conditions would you commit to a no-closure settlement, and what management/owner contributions are you prepared to place on the table first?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a settlement that avoids closures if it delivers a quantified, enforceable EBITDA improvement by 2030, even if the path to that improvement is staged rather than immediate?**

### To the Expert
**Can you provide a simple site-by-site template that compares non-closure and closure scenarios using the same cost and capacity assumptions, so the parties can test whether the gap is genuinely bridgeable?**
