As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will respond directly and clearly.

## First: what I agree with
I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one basic point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.  
The cost base is too high, capacity is too loose, and future investment must be financed somehow.

I also agree with the works council and the union that:
- the workforce cannot be the sole adjustment variable,
- burden-sharing must be real,
- and the company’s long-term industrial capability matters.

So I am not denying the problem, and I am not pretending that nothing has to change.

## Where I disagree sharply
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.  
I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the default instrument.  
And I do **not** accept a restructuring logic that treats the regional industrial base as the first place to cut.

That is not a responsible “hard truth.” It is a political and strategic choice — and one I cannot support.

### To management
You keep saying closures and forced redundancies cannot be ruled out if alternatives do not close the gap. I hear that, but I reject making that the benchmark before the alternatives are fully tested.

You have not yet shown, in a credible site-by-site form, that:
- working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- training and reskilling,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity improvements,
- overhead reduction,
- management restraint,
- and targeted labour-cost moderation

cannot close the gap first.

So my answer is: **show the non-closure package in numbers before you ask the region to accept irreversible damage**.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce should not be made to pay alone. I also support binding protection for sites and jobs as far as possible.

But I want to be equally clear: a posture of “all German plants stay open no matter what” only works if it is paired with a serious restructuring mechanism. I am not asking you to surrender your red lines. I am asking you to engage with a **quantified non-closure plan** that proves the company can improve competitiveness without dismantling its regional footprint.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that the package must be durable and margin-accretive. A feel-good compromise that leaves the economics unchanged would be irresponsible.

But you are wrong if you think the state should simply acquiesce to closures and forced layoffs because they are administratively neat. A package that weakens the company’s home base, trust, skills, and supplier network may look efficient short term, but it can destroy value long term.

## What I want to see next
I want a **site-by-site, numbers-based comparison** of:
1. a non-closure package,
2. a closure-inclusive package,
3. the savings from each instrument,
4. the timing of the effects,
5. the contribution from management and owners,
6. and the residual gap in each scenario.

That is the only serious next step.

## My red lines
Let me state them plainly:
- **No German plant closures as the first instrument**
- **No compulsory redundancies before all internal and negotiated alternatives are exhausted**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No package that hollows out the company’s regional anchor role**

Those are not rhetorical preferences. They are the basis on which the state can continue to support a settlement.

## What I am prepared to support
If the numbers show that the gap can be closed without closures, I am ready to back:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- training and reskilling,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- productivity and overhead measures,
- and investment commitments tied to German sites.

That is the path to a viable compromise.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures as a pressure tactic,
- forced redundancies as a shortcut,
- or a deal that weakens the state’s co-determination and anchor role.

If management wants my support, bring a genuine non-closure plan with figures.  
If that cannot be done, then the company must explain why it has not exhausted the instruments available in a co-determined, socially embedded enterprise before asking the region to absorb permanent damage.

If needed, I can help broker that structured discussion once the figures are on the table. But I will not sign off on a course that starts from closures and forces the region to accept industrial decline as the price of adjustment.
