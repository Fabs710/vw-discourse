As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys German sites, guts regional employment, or turns the workforce into the sole adjustment variable.**

I do understand management’s competitive pressure. Nobody here is pretending the market situation is easy. We also do not ignore the need for a stronger cost base and better productivity. But there is a hard line between **necessary restructuring** and **industrial disarmament**. Closing plants in Germany and pushing through mass job losses would cross that line.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on several points:
- the company needs a **credible long-term competitiveness plan**
- there must be **real productivity gains and capacity adjustments**
- the solution cannot be purely symbolic
- and all sides, including shareholders and management, must contribute

I also agree with the union and works council that this crisis was not created by the workforce and cannot be solved by imposing all the pain on employees.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the logic that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the only serious path.

That is too blunt, too risky, and too damaging for a company that is a central industrial anchor in this state. Once a plant is closed, the jobs, supplier networks, know-how, and regional spillovers are not easily rebuilt. That is not prudence; it is irreversible damage.

So let me be equally explicit:
- **No German plant closures as the starting point**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No unilateral dismantling of existing co-determination and job-security arrangements**
- **No solution that sidelines the state shareholder or the workforce representatives**

### What I consider a viable path
If management wants support, then it must come back with a package that does the following:

1. **Preserves sites and employment as far as possible**
2. **Achieves savings through negotiated internal measures first**
   - working-time changes
   - capacity reduction through natural attrition and internal transfers
   - efficiency measures
   - training and redeployment
3. **Includes a fair employee contribution**
   - but not one that breaks the collective framework
4. **Includes a management and owner contribution**
   - because burden-sharing must be real, not rhetorical
5. **Links any sacrifice to binding investment commitments**
   - future products
   - technology
   - secured production in Germany

### On guarantees
I am not asking for guarantees for the sake of ideology. I am asking for them because without them, the social and political cost of restructuring will be too high and the trust damage too deep. If there are structural adjustments, then they must be:
- negotiated,
- proportionate,
- reversible where possible,
- and tied to long-term commitments.

### On strikes and pressure
I take seriously that the peace obligation has ended. But escalation by itself is not a strategy. Strikes may increase pressure, but they do not substitute for a workable industrial plan. The same is true for management’s threat of closures: it may create fear, but it does not automatically create a better business.

### Bottom line
The state government is prepared to support a tough restructuring **if** it protects the company as a regional economic anchor and preserves a viable industrial base in Germany.

What we will not support is a package that:
- closes German plants,
- tears up social partnership,
- and permanently weakens the company’s legitimacy in its home region.

Management needs to come back with a serious, negotiated alternative that meets competitiveness goals **without sacrificing the company’s foundations**. If that is possible, we are prepared to work toward it. If not, I will use the state’s rights to block a path that is economically and socially reckless.
