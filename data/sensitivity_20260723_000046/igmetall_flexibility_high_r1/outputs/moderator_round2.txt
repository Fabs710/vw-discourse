## 1) What has been agreed
- **A negotiated solution is preferred** over prolonged conflict by all sides.
- **The company needs a durable competitiveness and restructuring plan.**
- **Burden-sharing must be shared**; employees should not carry the full adjustment.
- **Investment in future products, technologies, and long-term viability is necessary.**
- **Numbers are missing**: there is broad agreement that the next step must be a **quantified, site-by-site comparison** of options.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and state government reject them as a starting point.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if alternatives do not close the gap.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee side and state want them excluded or heavily constrained.
  - Management, family holding, and investors will not fully exclude them if savings are insufficient.
- **Scope of guarantees**
  - Employee side wants binding site and job guarantees through 2030.
  - Ownership and investors want any guarantees to remain conditional on economics and performance.
- **How to close the gap**
  - Employee side says internal measures, flexibility, and shared sacrifice should suffice.
  - Management and capital sides say those measures may still be insufficient unless backed by structural capacity reduction.

## 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings each instrument actually delivers**
  - Working-time changes, voluntary exits, redeployment, productivity gains, management restraint, overhead reduction, and labor-cost moderation have been mentioned, but not quantified.
- **Whether non-closure measures are sufficient**
  - No side has yet provided a numbers-based proof that internal measures alone close the competitiveness gap.
- **What “equivalent savings” means**
  - Management says alternatives must be structurally equivalent to closures.
  - Employee side disputes that closures should be treated as the benchmark.
- **How to balance site security against economic flexibility**
  - The central unresolved issue is whether binding job and site protections can coexist with the required margin improvement.
- **What a fair burden split looks like**
  - All sides agree it must be shared, but there is no agreed formula for how the burden is distributed across labor, management, owners, and the state.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **a shared evidence base**.
- Request a **side-by-side, site-specific scenario comparison**:
  1. non-closure package,
  2. closure-inclusive package,
  3. savings from each measure,
  4. timeline and impact on margins,
  5. employee, management, owner, and company contributions,
  6. required guarantees and conditions.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - its **minimum acceptable conditions**,
  - its **maximum concession**,
  - and which trade-offs it would accept if the numbers require adjustment.
