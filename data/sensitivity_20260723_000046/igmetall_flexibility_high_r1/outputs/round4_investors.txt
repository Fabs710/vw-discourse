We agree with the basic diagnosis that this is a **real restructuring problem**, not a branding exercise. The company’s margins are too weak, the cost base is too high, and prolonged conflict would destroy value.

Where we **agree**
- Management is right that durable competitiveness requires **material cost reduction** and better capacity utilisation.
- The expert is right that the decision must be based on **site-by-site economics**, not slogans.
- The workforce is right that this cannot be a one-sided squeeze on labour alone.
- The state and the family are right that a cosmetic peace deal would be value-destructive.

Where we **disagree**
- We do **not** support an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive just to preserve every current footprint.
- We do **not** accept unconditional site/job guarantees if they freeze the economics and prevent meaningful restructuring.
- We do **not** accept a settlement that relies only on temporary optics while excess fixed cost remains in place.

So our position is straightforward: **non-closure measures are acceptable only if they credibly close the gap on a durable basis**. If they do not, then management must retain the ability to take harder structural actions, including plant rationalisation.

What would move us
We need a quantified package showing, for each site:
1. current output and utilization,
2. fixed and variable cost by plant,
3. savings from working-time changes,
4. savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
5. savings from productivity and overhead cuts,
6. management and owner contribution,
7. the residual gap under a non-closure scenario,
8. and the net savings / margin impact versus a closure-inclusive scenario.

Until that comparison is on the table, we are not persuaded that closures can be ruled out. But we also will not support closures as a first move without proof that the alternatives fail.

## To management
You are right that the restructuring must be **durable and margin-accretive**. But you still have not shown that the non-closure package cannot work. Bring the numbers, site by site, and show the residual gap.

## To the works council and union
Your insistence on burden-sharing is legitimate. But an absolute “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is only sustainable if you can prove the alternative closes the economics. Otherwise it simply shifts the burden onto shareholders and future earnings.

## To the state and family owners
We agree that a cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. But you should also avoid turning closure threats into the starting premise before the non-closure case has been properly tested. That increases execution risk and may destroy more value than it saves.

### Bottom line
We will back whatever arrangement **credibly restores competitive margins, reduces fixed costs, and gives execution certainty**.  
We will **not** back a deal that preserves all current sites while leaving the company structurally weak.  
And we will **not** back a reckless closure strategy unless the numbers show it is truly necessary.
