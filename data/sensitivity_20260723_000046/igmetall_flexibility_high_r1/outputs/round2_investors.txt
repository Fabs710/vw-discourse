We share the diagnosis that the company needs a **durable competitiveness fix**, not a cosmetic compromise.

## Where we agree
- The German cost base is too high.
- Overcapacity has to come down.
- Management cannot place the whole burden on employees.
- Prolonged conflict, strikes, and uncertainty destroy value.

We also agree with the family owners on one important point: **the package must be economically credible and margin-accretive**, not just politically acceptable.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures, no compulsory redundancies” cannot be an absolute precondition
I understand why the works council and union are drawing hard lines. But from an equity-holder perspective, an absolute ban on closures and forced reductions is not acceptable if it prevents the company from restoring margins.

If every site is effectively protected regardless of economics, then we are not restructuring — we are **locking in structural weakness**. That would mean:
- continued margin erosion,
- weaker cash generation,
- less investment capacity,
- and lower valuation.

That is not something we can support.

### 2) Management still needs to prove the numbers
At the same time, I am not prepared to endorse a closure-heavy strategy just because it is the most dramatic option. Management has not yet demonstrated, in a sufficiently quantified way, that closures and compulsory redundancies are the only route to the required savings.

We need a **site-by-site, instrument-by-instrument comparison**:
- how much savings from working-time changes,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity measures,
- targeted labor-cost relief,
- overhead reduction,
- and, if necessary, structural capacity cuts.

“Closures may be needed” is not enough. Show us the gap and show us why other instruments do or do not close it.

## What would move us
We would support a deal if it clearly delivers:
1. **durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity rationalization**
3. **credible labor-cost relief**
4. **execution certainty**
5. **a clear path back to competitive margins**

That can include avoiding closures — but only if the alternative is **quantified, binding, and sufficient**.

## On guarantees
We are sceptical of broad unconditional guarantees. They only make sense if they are tied to:
- measurable savings,
- productivity improvements,
- investment commitments,
- and a clear review mechanism.

A guarantee that freezes the cost base while expecting shareholders to absorb the downside is not acceptable.

## On the employee side
The union and works council are right that the workforce should not be the sole adjustment variable. But saying “all plants stay open” is not, by itself, a financial solution. If the economics do not work, then insisting on total site protection simply pushes the burden onto shareholders through lower returns.

That is not a sustainable outcome.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated restructuring, but only if it is:
- **numbers-based**
- **durable**
- **credible**
- and **sufficient to restore margins**

We will **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves structural inefficiency.

We also will **not** support a prolonged conflict that destroys value while management and labour argue about principle instead of economics.

So the next step should be very concrete: bring a **quantified comparison of the non-closure and closure scenarios**, with the savings, timing, and margin impact laid out plainly. If the non-closure route really closes the gap, then that is preferable. If it does not, then no one should pretend otherwise.
