Yes. Here is a concise **site-by-site scenario model template** the parties can use to test whether the non-closure package is economically sufficient versus a closure-inclusive alternative.

## 1) Decision question
For each plant, compare:

- **Scenario A: Non-closure package**
  - working-time changes
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment
  - productivity / overhead reduction
  - labor-cost moderation

- **Scenario B: Closure or footprint reduction**
  - volume transfer
  - elimination of fixed costs
  - severance / wind-down costs
  - logistics and transition costs

The key test is whether **Scenario A closes enough of the EBITDA gap on a durable basis**.

## 4) Financial comparison
For each site, calculate:

### Non-closure net benefit
**Annual EBITDA uplift**
minus
**annualized implementation cost**

### Closure net benefit
**Annual EBITDA uplift**
minus
**annualized transition cost**
minus
**extra logistics / ramp-up cost**

Then compare:

> **NPV(non-closure)** vs **NPV(closure)** over 5–7 years

Also compare:
- payback period
- 2030 margin impact
- cash burn in years 1–2
- capacity risk at receiving plants

---

## 5) Interpreting the result
### Non-closure is credible if:
- the site’s savings gap is modest to medium
- utilization can be improved materially
- fixed cost burden left behind is manageable
- recurring labor concessions are limited and durable
- no major capacity bottleneck is created elsewhere

### Closure is economically stronger if:
- the site is structurally underutilized
- fixed costs remain too high after all internal measures
- the residual gap is persistent
- receiving plants have spare capacity
- annualized closure savings clearly exceed the recurring cost of keeping the plant open

---

## 6) Practical rule
If the restructuring problem is mainly **fixed-cost and underutilization driven**, closure or consolidation usually delivers the larger long-run EBITDA gain.

If the gap is **smaller and mostly recoverable through flexibility and productivity**, a non-closure package can be viable.
