We have heard enough generalities. The next step must be a **credible, quantified restructuring package** that shows whether the gap can be closed **without** destroying value through prolonged conflict.

From the controlling family’s perspective:

## Where we agree
- The company has a real competitiveness problem.
- The cost base and overcapacity issue cannot be waved away.
- A negotiated settlement is far better than escalation.
- Employees, management, owners, and the company itself must all contribute.
- Future-product and technology investment must be protected.

## Where we disagree
### With the Works Council and union
I do **not** accept an absolute “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no structural change” doctrine.

That is not stewardship. It is a freeze on adjustment, and it risks locking in weakness. If the non-closure route is truly sufficient, prove it with numbers. But if it is not sufficient, then refusing all structural instruments simply shifts the burden onto the company’s future earnings and therefore onto all shareholders, including the family holding.

I also do **not** accept that binding guarantees can be unconditional. Guarantees only have value if they are tied to:
- measurable savings,
- productivity gains,
- investment commitments,
- and a realistic path to margin restoration.

### With management
I also do **not** accept management’s tendency to treat closures and redundancies as the default benchmark before alternatives are properly tested.

That is too blunt and, frankly, value-destructive if it hardens resistance and triggers avoidable industrial conflict. If management wants support from ownership, it must show:
- the actual cost gap,
- the savings from each instrument,
- what management itself will contribute,
- and why the remaining gap cannot be closed otherwise.

## What we need now
Not more position papers. A **site-by-site comparison**.

I want a side-by-side view of:
1. **Non-closure scenario**
2. **Closure-inclusive scenario**
3. Savings from:
   - working-time changes
   - voluntary exits
   - redeployment
   - training/reskilling
   - productivity measures
   - management overhead cuts
   - labour-cost moderation
4. Residual gap in each case
5. Investment capacity under each case
6. Impact on margins through 2030

## What would move us
A non-closure package would need to demonstrate all of the following:
- durable savings, not temporary optics
- material capacity reduction
- credible labour-cost relief
- enforceable productivity improvements
- real investment in future products
- industrial peace for a meaningful period

If that can be achieved, it is clearly preferable to closures.

If it cannot, then we cannot pretend otherwise. In that case, the company must have the freedom to consider harder structural options. I am not going to endorse a settlement that protects every current footprint while eroding the company’s economic future.

## On the red lines stated by others
I hear the state, works council, and union on site protection. I understand the political and social reasons. But I will not support red lines that are absolute **before the economics are tested**.

I am not saying “close plants now.” I am saying: **do not rule out the instruments needed to restore competitiveness if the figures show they are required.**

## On labour peace
A long strike or a drawn-out confrontation would be damaging. That is precisely why I am pushing for numbers, not slogans. But labour peace is not enough by itself; it has to be bought with a plan that actually restores earnings power.

## Bottom line
The family holding will support a settlement only if it is:
- economically credible,
- margin-accretive,
- durable,
- and capable of restoring competitiveness without permanently impairing enterprise value.

So my position is:
- **Yes** to a negotiated solution.
- **Yes** to shared burden.
- **Yes** to serious non-closure alternatives.
- **No** to unconditional site/job guarantees.
- **No** to a cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to a package that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.
