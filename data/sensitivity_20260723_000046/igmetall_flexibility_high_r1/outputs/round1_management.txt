I hear the position from the Works Council and the Union, but I need to be equally clear: **a purely defensive answer is not enough**.

We are not in a normal bargaining round. We are facing a structural competitiveness gap:
- German factory costs are far above target,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and our brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we do not reduce the cost base and capacity in a durable way, we will not have the financial room to invest in future products, software, battery technology, and the platform work needed through 2030. In that case, we do not preserve the industrial base — we erode it over time.

So let me respond directly:

### Where I agree
I agree that the solution must be **negotiated**, not imposed by rhetoric.
I also agree that we need **future investment in Germany** and that we should use every serious tool available:
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- training,
- productivity measures,
- and a real labor-cost contribution.

I am also prepared to say plainly that **management must be part of the burden-sharing**. This is not being asked of employees alone.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that:
- **no plant closures**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- and **full site guarantees everywhere**
can be fixed in advance as untouchable red lines while still expecting the company to restore competitiveness.

That position may be understandable politically, but it does not solve the arithmetic.

If the cost base stays structurally too high, then “protecting everything” becomes a short-term comfort at the expense of long-term viability. I am not willing to sign up to a cosmetic package that preserves today’s structure and leaves us uncompetitive tomorrow.

### What I need from the employee side
To be concrete, a credible deal must deliver:
1. **A substantial and durable reduction in labor costs**
2. **A meaningful reduction in overcapacity**
3. **A lasting increase in flexibility and productivity**
4. **A package that restores margin and investment capacity**

That means I am open to alternatives to closures, but only if they produce the same structural effect. If you want to avoid plant closures, then you must help deliver equivalent savings through:
- real capacity cuts,
- permanent cost reductions,
- and a workforce contribution that is more than symbolic.

### On guarantees
I am not dismissing guarantees outright. But guarantees only make sense **if they are paired with measurable savings and a credible path back to competitiveness**. A blanket guarantee with no structural adjustment is not a solution.

### On industrial action
I would rather reach a negotiated settlement. But let me be equally explicit: prolonged strikes will damage the company, the brand, and ultimately everyone’s room for maneuver. They will not change the underlying need for restructuring.

### Bottom line
Management is ready to negotiate a serious package, but not a cosmetic one.

If the employee side is prepared to discuss:
- substantial labor-cost relief,
- durable capacity reduction,
- internal flexibility,
- and investment-linked site commitments,

then we can talk about how the burden is shared and how to avoid unnecessary plant closures. But if the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no change to the current framework,” then there is no viable restructuring path on the table.

I need a proposal that actually closes the competitiveness gap. Not a promise to postpone it.
