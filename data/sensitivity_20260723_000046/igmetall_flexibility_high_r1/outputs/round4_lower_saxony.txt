I will be very clear.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one central point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.  
I also agree with the works council and the union that:
- the workforce must not carry the full burden,
- future investment must be protected,
- and burden-sharing has to be real.

So yes, there is a shared recognition that something has to change.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.  
I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the default instrument.  
And I do **not** accept a restructuring logic that treats the regional industrial base as the first place to cut.

That is not responsible realism. It is a political and strategic choice — and one I cannot support.

### To management
You keep saying that closures and redundancies must remain available if the alternatives do not close the gap. But you still have **not** shown, in a credible site-by-site way, that the non-closure instruments have been fully exhausted.

So my answer is simple: **show the non-closure package in numbers first**.

I want to see:
- the exact cost gap by site,
- savings from working-time changes,
- savings from redeployment and training,
- savings from voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- savings from productivity and overhead restraint,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and the residual gap.

Only then can anyone seriously judge whether structural footprint changes are unavoidable. Until then, closure is a choice, not a necessity.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that jobs and sites cannot be treated as disposable. That is absolutely right.

But I also need to be honest: a posture of “all German plants stay open no matter what” is only sustainable if a serious restructuring path is proven to work. I am not asking you to surrender your red lines. I am asking you to engage with a **quantified non-closure plan** that proves the company can improve competitiveness without dismantling its regional footprint.

I will not support any settlement that:
- guts employment in the state,
- weakens co-determination in practice,
- or makes workers the first and easiest adjustment variable.

### To the controlling family holding and investors
You are right that the package must be durable and margin-accretive. A feel-good compromise that leaves the economics unchanged would be irresponsible.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible route is to keep closures and forced layoffs on the table from the outset. That approach may look disciplined on paper, but it risks destroying trust, provoking industrial conflict, and weakening the company’s long-term capability.

A company’s value is not just a margin line. It is also skills, continuity, supplier networks, and labour peace. If you break those, you may win a spreadsheet and lose the industrial base.

## My position
Let me state it plainly:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No settlement that hollows out the company’s regional anchor role**

Those are not rhetorical preferences. They are the basis on which the state can continue to support a settlement.

## What I am prepared to support
If the numbers show that the gap can be closed without closures, I am ready to support:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- training and reskilling,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- temporary and fair employee contributions,
- productivity and overhead measures,
- and investment commitments tied to German sites.

That is the path to a viable compromise.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures as a pressure tactic,
- forced redundancies as a shortcut,
- or a deal that weakens the state’s co-determination and anchor role.

If management wants my support, bring a genuine **non-closure plan with figures**.  
If that cannot be done, then the company must explain why it has not exhausted the instruments available in a co-determined, socially embedded enterprise before asking the region to absorb permanent damage.

## Final word
I am ready to help broker a serious, quantified discussion.  
But I will not sign off on a course that starts from closures and treats regional decline as the price of adjustment.
