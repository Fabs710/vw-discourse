### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge the company faces a serious structural competitiveness problem.
- There is broad agreement that a negotiated solution is preferable to an imposed or purely confrontational one.
- Most parties accept that **burden-sharing** is needed and that management and owners must also contribute.
- There is general openness to non-closure instruments such as:
  - working-time adjustments
  - internal redeployment
  - training
  - productivity measures
  - voluntary exits
  - targeted labor-cost relief
- There is shared recognition that future investment in products, technology, and German operations matters.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works Council, union, and state government reject them; management, family holding, and investors say they cannot be ruled out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state oppose them; management, owners, and investors want them available as a last resort if alternatives fail.
- **Extent of guarantees:** Employee and state side want binding site/job guarantees; management and capital side argue guarantees must be conditional and cannot block structural adjustment.
- **Depth of restructuring:** Management, owners, and investors want substantial, durable cost and capacity reductions; employee and state side accept adjustment only if it preserves sites, jobs, and co-determination.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required, and from which measures specifically**: no shared numbers yet.
- **Whether alternatives to closures can fully replace closure-related savings**: no quantified comparison has been presented.
- **How to balance job security against competitiveness**: the parties have not agreed on which protections can be made binding and which must remain flexible.
- **Who bears what share of the adjustment**: the balance between employee concessions, management restraint, owner contribution, and structural cuts remains unresolved.
- **What investment commitments are credible and enforceable**: future-product and site-investment promises have not yet been tied to concrete conditions or timelines.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to a **numbers-based package**:
- specify required cost savings and capacity reductions,
- identify which sites and instruments are affected,
- set out what each party is prepared to concede,
- and define which guarantees can be made conditional, time-limited, or site-specific.

The next round should aim to test whether a **quantified, enforceable compromise** can replace the current stand-off between “full protection” and “structural flexibility.”
