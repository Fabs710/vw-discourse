We share the core line on this: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral dismantling of the collective framework**.

The workforce did not create this crisis, and it is unacceptable if management now tries to solve a structural problem by putting the full burden on employees while owners and leadership remain protected. That is not a restructuring; that is a transfer of risk onto the people who built the company.

That said, I want to be clear about the trade space from the union side:

- **We are prepared to contribute** to a negotiated solution.
- **We are prepared to discuss labor-cost relief**, including temporary or targeted wage moderation, reduced bonuses, working-time instruments, and capacity adjustments.
- **We are prepared to accept internal flexibility** and redeployment if this protects jobs and sites.
- We are **not** prepared to accept a deal that destroys the collective wage framework or creates open-ended insecurity.

So my message to management is: if you want savings, we will negotiate savings. But they must be **shared fairly** and **tied to binding guarantees**:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Site and investment commitments**
4. **A durable collective agreement**, not a one-sided rollback
5. **Real burden-sharing** from management, shareholders, and, where necessary, the company itself

If the company insists on deep cuts but refuses enforceable job and site protection, then you should expect the workforce to respond with the full industrial leverage available to us. The peace obligation is over, and our members will not accept being made the sole shock absorber.

We are ready for a serious negotiated package. But it must protect the industrial base and treat the workforce as a partner, not as the adjustment item.
