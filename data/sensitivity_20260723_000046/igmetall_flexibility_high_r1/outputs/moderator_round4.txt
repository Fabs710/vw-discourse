Core impasse
- The central deadlock is between **binding site/job security** on one side and **maintaining full restructuring flexibility** on the other.
- The works council, union, and state want a **non-closure-first settlement** with no compulsory redundancies and strong guarantees.
- Management, the family holding, and investors want **credible access to closures and workforce reduction** if the numbers require it.
- The expert input shifted the discussion to **site-by-site economics**, but the parties still disagree on the **threshold for proving necessity** and on whether closures/redundancies can remain available as a fallback.

Possible bridge proposals

1. Quantified non-closure first, with a delayed fallback
- Agreement to a **site-by-site savings model** using the expert template.
- For a defined period, only non-closure measures are implemented:
  - working-time changes,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - productivity and overhead cuts,
  - management and owner contributions.
- Closures and compulsory redundancies are **not used during this phase**.
- A jointly agreed review date is set, with pre-defined metrics for whether the gap is closed.
- This addresses labour’s demand for a genuine non-closure test while preserving management’s insistence on an evidence-based second step.

2. Conditional guarantees tied to measurable triggers
- A package of **conditional job and site guarantees through 2030** for sites that meet agreed cost, utilization, and productivity milestones.
- If milestones are met, sites remain protected.
- If milestones are not met, a second-stage negotiation opens automatically on specific sites, not the whole network.
- This could narrow the conflict by replacing a binary “all plants / no plants” dispute with **site-specific conditionality**.

3. Differentiated treatment of sites
- Separate sites into categories:
  - **core sites**: protected, no closures, no compulsory redundancies,
  - **at-risk sites**: open to partial consolidation or stronger restructuring tools,
  - **transition sites**: eligible for special support, retraining, and investment commitments.
- This allows the parties to preserve a political and social anchor while acknowledging that not every site may have the same economic profile.

Pointed questions to each stakeholder

To the Works Council
- If the site-by-site model shows that one or more plants cannot reach the target without some form of footprint change, what specific non-closure threshold would you accept before agreeing to reopen the question?

To the Union
- Would you accept a time-limited concession package if it were paired with binding investment, measurable management/owner contributions, and a jointly audited non-closure test?

To Management
- Will you commit to presenting a fully quantified non-closure scenario first, with management and owner contributions clearly specified, before any closure or compulsory redundancy option is treated as the preferred path?

To the Regional State Government
- Are you prepared to support a phased process that protects sites initially, but allows a site-specific second-stage review if the agreed non-closure measures fail to close the gap?

To the Controlling Family Holding
- What exact economic trigger would justify, in your view, moving from a protected non-closure phase to structural measures at specific sites?

To Institutional Investors
- Would you accept a deal that delays any closure decision, provided the company receives a credible, audited path to durable margin improvement and the option to revisit underperformance later?

To the Expert
- What minimum dataset and forecast horizon would you require so the parties can distinguish a genuinely sufficient non-closure plan from one that merely postpones restructuring?
