We share the fundamental line that has just been stated: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no restructuring that pushes the entire burden onto the workforce**.

From the union side, I want to be equally explicit: the lapse of the wage agreement and the end of peace obligation mean the company is now in a phase where industrial action is on the table, and members expect us to use that leverage if necessary. We already signaled openness to a negotiated contribution by employees — but only **within a fair package** and only if management, owners, and the company itself also carry their share.

Where I agree with the Works Council:
- We both reject closures as a restructuring instrument.
- We both reject forced layoffs.
- We both insist on a durable job-security framework.
- We both see that transformation needs investment, qualification, and a negotiated path rather than a one-sided cut.

Where I need to draw a clear line:
- A vague promise of “competitiveness” is not enough to justify destroying jobs and wage standards.
- If management wants savings, it must show that **other stakeholders are contributing materially**: owners, leadership, and capital must not be sheltered while labor absorbs the pain.
- Any attempt to weaken the collective bargaining framework permanently, or to make workers pay through unilateral wage dumping, would not be acceptable.

Our position is simple:
1. **Protect sites and jobs.**
2. **Reduce costs through negotiated, reversible measures first** — working-time solutions, capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, qualification, and if necessary a limited employee contribution.
3. **Bind management and owners to real commitments** on investment, product allocation, and future capacity in Germany.
4. **No compulsory redundancies, no closures, no erosion of collective standards.**

If management is serious, then the discussion should be about a balanced package: temporary concessions by employees in exchange for hard guarantees on employment, investment, and site security. If it is serious only about cutting labor and closing plants, then it should not expect cooperation from us.

So I am not here to endorse a restructuring at any price. I am here to make clear that **a fair settlement is possible, but only if the burden is shared and the core red lines are respected**.
