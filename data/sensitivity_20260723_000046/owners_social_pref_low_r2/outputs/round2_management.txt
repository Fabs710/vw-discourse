Let me respond directly.

I appreciate that the employee side is willing to discuss alternatives to an immediate shutdown. But I need to be equally direct: **a “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful change to guarantees” position does not solve the economic problem**. It simply preserves the problem and shifts the risk forward.

## Where I agree
I agree on three points:

1. **A drawn-out confrontation would damage everyone.**  
   That is precisely why we need a serious settlement now.

2. **Investment and future products in Germany are essential.**  
   This restructuring is not about weakening the company; it is about making room to invest in its future.

3. **Reversible measures should be part of the package.**  
   Working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and natural turnover all matter.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No plant closures” is not a serious starting point
You keep treating closures as if they were a political choice management simply prefers. They are not. We are facing:
- structural overcapacity,
- a shrinking European market,
- and factory costs in Germany that are far above target.

If every site is declared untouchable, then the company is left with an uncompetitive cost base. That is not a sustainable business model.

### 2) Temporary measures alone are not enough
Working-time changes, attrition, and internal transfers can help, but they do **not** deliver the durable savings we need. We need a package that changes the structure of the business, not just delays hard decisions.

### 3) Binding guarantees through 2030 cannot be unconditional
I will not pretend that we can lock in every existing arrangement and still restore margins. That is not credible. A guarantee has to sit within a restructuring that actually improves the economics. Otherwise it is just a promise to preserve decline.

## On the burden of proof
You are right about one thing: the burden is on management to show that the package is credible. I am doing that now.

What I need is not a slogan of “no closures.” What I need is:
- a **meaningful labour-cost contribution**,
- a **durable reduction in capacity**,
- **clear productivity and flexibility improvements**,
- and a **deal that restores return on sales and investment capacity to 2030**.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that achieves **equivalent, durable savings**. So far, I have heard alternatives, but not an equivalent solution.

## What I can accept
I remain open to a negotiated solution that combines:
- capacity reductions,
- site consolidation or footprint rationalisation,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- redeployment and natural attrition,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

But I need to say this plainly: **if the package does not materially reduce overcapacity and labour cost, it will not be enough**. A cosmetic deal is not acceptable.

## To the works council and union
I understand your commitment to jobs and sites. But you cannot ask management to carry the entire burden of restructuring while ruling out the instruments that actually change the cost base.

If the response is simply:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- no real guarantee changes,
- limited temporary concessions only,

then there is no viable restructuring to approve.

## To the regional state
I appreciate your focus on industrial stability, but stability without competitiveness is fragile. Protecting the region in the long run requires a company that can earn its money, invest, and survive the next decade. That means making difficult structural choices now.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would be unacceptable. We need a durable package. But durability does not come from preserving every legacy arrangement; it comes from a structure that can actually compete.

## My line is simple
I am prepared to negotiate on the **form** of restructuring. I am not prepared to abandon the **need** for restructuring.

So the next step is not to repeat “no closures” as an absolute. The next step is to define a package that delivers:
- real savings,
- real capacity adjustment,
- real labour-cost contribution,
- and real investment capability.

If that can be done without a closure, fine — but it must be proven, not asserted. If it cannot, then we cannot pretend that the current model is sustainable.
