### Moderator summary after Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree that the company faces **real competitive and structural pressure** and cannot simply remain unchanged.
- There is broad agreement that a **negotiated solution is preferable** to open conflict or a prolonged strike.
- Most parties support using **non-forced measures first**, such as:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - internal redeployment,
  - natural turnover,
  - qualification/training,
  - and some form of employee contribution if it is negotiated.
- There is also shared recognition that **future investment and product commitments in Germany** are part of any viable settlement.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works Council, union, regional state, and to a large extent the family holding reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management and institutional investors say some form of **durable capacity reduction** is necessary and do not accept an absolute no-closure position.
- **Compulsory redundancies and job guarantees**:  
  - Employee representatives and the state insist on no compulsory redundancies and strong job-security guarantees.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors say binding guarantees through 2030 are too rigid if structural change is required.
- **How savings are achieved**:  
  - Labor side wants savings mainly through reversible, negotiated measures.  
  - Shareholders and management want **durable structural savings** and do not view temporary measures as sufficient.
- **Burden-sharing**:  
  - Labor and state insist the burden must be shared by owners and management, not only employees.  
  - Management and investors want a meaningful labor contribution and structural adjustment before committing to protections.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. job protection**: how to secure enough cost reduction without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- **Temporary vs. permanent measures**: whether reversible concessions can add up to the durable savings management and shareholders want.
- **Credible guarantees vs. flexibility**: how far employment and site-security commitments can go without eliminating restructuring options.
- **Employee contribution vs. investment commitment**: what level of workforce concession would be matched by binding commitments on products, investment, and future capacity.
- **Governance feasibility**: what package could realistically pass the supervisory board and satisfy political, labor, and shareholder constraints.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **concrete package design**:
- identify the minimum savings target that must be met,
- specify which measures are reversible and which are structural,
- quantify what employee contribution would be acceptable,
- and state what binding guarantees or investment commitments would be exchanged in return.

The next round should aim to test whether a package can be built that is both **economically durable** and **socially/politically acceptable**.
