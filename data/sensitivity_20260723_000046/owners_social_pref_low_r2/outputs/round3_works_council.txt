I will be equally clear.

The conversation has finally become more concrete, but the core issue remains unchanged: **management and shareholders are still treating closures as the benchmark solution, while we are being asked to prove that workers can somehow compensate for a strategic failure they did not cause.** That is not acceptable.

## Where I agree
I agree with the experts, and with the state and even parts of the shareholder side, on one crucial point: **temporary measures alone are not enough** if the company truly has a structural cost problem. A hiring freeze, wage moderation, and a few flexibility concessions do not solve overcapacity. On that narrow point, yes — the debate has moved forward.

I also agree that we need something durable and enforceable, not a symbolic truce that collapses in a year.

## Where I strongly disagree
### 1) I do not accept “equivalent to closure” as the standard
The repeated framing that the employee side must produce an “equivalent” to a plant closure is fundamentally biased. A closure is not a neutral reference point; it is a drastic measure with massive social destruction.

We do **not** accept the premise that the only serious benchmark is the destruction of a site.

If management wants to compare options, then it must compare them honestly:
- What savings come from internal restructuring?
- What savings come from shift reduction?
- What savings come from redeployment, attrition, and productivity measures?
- What are the industrial and social costs of closures?

That full balance sheet has not been presented convincingly.

### 2) I reject any backdoor closure logic
The phrase “no closure, but footprint rationalisation” is only acceptable if it truly means preserving sites and jobs. If it means:
- stripping a site down until it is a shell,
- transferring out core production,
- hollowing out employment,
- and then calling that “no closure,”

then that is closure by another name. We will not fall for that word game.

### 3) I reject compulsory redundancies outright
This is not a negotiating preference. It is a red line.

If the company needs adjustment, then adjustment must come through:
- internal redeployment,
- qualified conversion,
- natural turnover,
- voluntary exits,
- shorter working time,
- and negotiated flexibility.

Forced layoffs are not an acceptable instrument. Full stop.

### 4) I reject closing German plants as a restructuring instrument
Management has still not demonstrated that every alternative has been exhausted. The company has not proved that a no-closure package cannot work. What we hear is pressure, not proof.

And let me say this plainly to the family holding and investors: **a settlement that breaks the historic job-security framework will not preserve trust, and without trust there is no durable industrial model.**

## What a real solution would require
If management is serious, it should stop demanding that the workforce pre-accept its preferred pain distribution and instead put forward a package based on these principles:

1. **Every German plant remains open.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Real structural savings through internal measures, not closures.**
4. **Binding investment and product commitments for German sites.**
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework.**
6. **A fair, temporary employee contribution only if matched by real owner and management concessions.**

That is the minimum basis for serious negotiations.

## On the moderator’s proposed compromise paths
### “No immediate closure, but binding footprint rationalisation”
This is the most dangerous formulation because it is vague enough to hide a closure agenda.

If “rationalisation” means:
- removing a shift,
- consolidating support functions,
- and reallocating production **within the existing sites**,

then that can be discussed.

If it means shrinking plants until closure becomes inevitable, then it is unacceptable.

### “Time-bounded no-redundancy pact with trigger-based review”
We are not interested in a temporary freeze followed by a built-in threat of reopening closures later. That is not security; that is a countdown clock.

A time-bounded pact only makes sense if it strengthens job security, not if it creates a delayed strike against it.

### “Two-tier package: immediate cash relief + structural second phase”
We can discuss immediate stabilization measures, but not as a prelude to later plant closures or forced redundancies. Any second phase must still respect the no-closure and no-compulsory-layoff framework.

## To management
You still have not answered the central question: **why are closures necessary if durable structural savings can be built through other means?**

Do not tell me “it is the benchmark.” Tell me why the alternatives are insufficient, with actual evidence, not just assertion.

If you cannot do that, then you should stop pushing closures and start negotiating in good faith on a no-closure restructuring.

## To the experts
Your assessments are useful, but they risk normalizing the idea that large social destruction is just one option among many. It is not.

If a no-closure package can capture 60–85% of closure economics, then the burden is on management to explain why the remaining gap justifies closing German plants and breaking job guarantees. That threshold has **not** been met.

## To the state, union, and family holding
I appreciate that some of you now explicitly reject cosmetic deals. Good. But do not let that slide into accepting closures as the “realistic” answer.

Realistic for whom? For management’s convenience? For capital markets’ speed? That is not our standard.

Our standard is:
- protect jobs,
- keep sites open,
- preserve co-determination,
- and secure the company’s future without sacrificing the workforce.

## Bottom line
Let me state the works council position without ambiguity:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No erosion of the historic job-security framework.**
- **No settlement built on hollow “rationalisation” that becomes closure by stealth.**

We are prepared to discuss real structural adjustments **inside the existing plant network**, and we are prepared to discuss a temporary, fair employee contribution **if and only if** the company and owners make binding commitments on investment, product allocation, and site security.

If management wants a settlement, it must abandon the closure-first mindset. If it does not, then it should expect resistance — in the supervisory board, in the plants, and publicly.
