I will be direct.

The works council has rightly restated the red lines, and I support them: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures in Germany, and no one-sided restructuring that makes the workforce absorb all the pain**. On that core point, there is no gap between us.

But I want to be equally clear to management, the family holding, and investors: **“durable savings” is not a magic word that overrides social responsibility, collective bargaining rights, or the company’s long-term industrial trust.** If your answer to every problem is closures or tearing up guarantees, then you are not presenting a restructuring plan — you are presenting a conflict strategy.

### Where I agree
- **The company does need a credible future path.** That is not in dispute.
- **A drawn-out confrontation would damage everyone.** True.
- **Some form of employee contribution may be part of a solution.** Yes — we said that already.
- **The savings must be durable enough to support investment.** Also true.

### Where I disagree
#### 1) Management’s “closure first” logic is not acceptable
You say you need a structural reduction in capacity. Fine. But you have not shown that **German plant closures are the only way** to achieve it. That is the key failure in your argument.

Capacity reduction can be achieved through:
- shift reductions,
- internal transfers,
- production reallocation,
- temporary working-time changes with long-term design,
- attrition and retirement pathways,
- qualification and conversion,
- and, where necessary, limited negotiated cost contributions.

If management refuses to treat these as serious alternatives, then it is choosing the most destructive instrument first, not the most responsible one.

#### 2) Investors are oversimplifying the financial test
You keep saying the package must be “big enough” and “durable.” That is correct in principle, but you are treating social destruction as if it were a neutral accounting entry. It is not.

A plan that triggers strikes, destroys trust, weakens the labor-relations model, and damages production stability will not preserve value. It may create the appearance of savings while imposing high hidden costs. So if you want durability, then you need a settlement that is durable **in industrial relations too**.

#### 3) The family holding and management cannot demand labor sacrifice in isolation
We are willing to discuss a fair employee contribution. But it must be matched by real contributions from the top:
- management restraint,
- owner backing,
- investment commitments,
- product allocation,
- and a credible long-term industrial footprint in Germany.

If labor gives and ownership simply takes, that is not a settlement; that is extraction.

### What we can discuss
Let me be concrete, because vague talk is not enough.

We are prepared to discuss a package with:
1. **No compulsory redundancies.**
2. **No plant closures.**
3. **A temporary, negotiated employee contribution** — for example through wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, or a solidarity component — but only if it is limited and reversible.
4. **Capacity reduction through non-destructive measures**, not shutdowns.
5. **Binding investment and product commitments** for German sites.
6. **A jointly monitored transformation agreement** with union, works council, and management participation.

That is the framework within which a fair deal could exist.

### What we will not accept
- No “guarantee” language that is later hollowed out by plant shutdowns in disguise.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No closure threats used as leverage to force wage cuts.
- No indefinite erosion of the collective wage framework.
- No settlement where employees pay while owners and executives stay protected.

### On the burden of proof
The burden is on management to prove that your preferred solution is not just simpler for you, but better for the company as a whole. So far, I have not seen that proof.

If you want our cooperation, then bring a **credible no-closure package** that delivers the necessary savings through negotiated means. If you cannot, then do not expect us to soften our position because you repeat the same pressure points.

### Bottom line
My position is unchanged:
- **protect jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework;**
- **share the burden fairly;**
- **use reversible and negotiated measures first;**
- **reject compulsory redundancies and closures.**

If management wants a real solution, it should stop asking the workforce to accept structural damage as the price of “competitiveness” and start treating shared sacrifice as a two-way street.
