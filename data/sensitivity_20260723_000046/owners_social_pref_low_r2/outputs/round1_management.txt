We hear the red lines clearly. Let me be equally clear about ours.

I agree on one point: a transformation of this scale cannot be done by slogans or by shifting every burden onto employees. We also need investment, qualification, and a credible path to future products in Germany. But I reject the core premise that the current cost and capacity structure can be preserved and that the company can remain competitive without deep structural change.

### Where I agree
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict**. Strikes help no one if they drag on and damage the company further.
- **Investment in future products and technology is essential**. We are not restructuring for its own sake; we are trying to secure the company’s future.
- **Natural turnover, redeployment, working-time measures, and qualification** should all be part of the toolbox.

### Where I disagree
- **No plant closures at all is not a realistic starting point.**  
  We have German factory costs that are far above target, and we have overcapacity in a shrinking European market. If every site is declared untouchable, then the cost base remains structurally uncompetitive and the problem is only postponed.
- **A binding job guarantee through 2030, untouched by structural adjustment, is not something management can accept in the present situation.**  
  We cannot sign up to preserving every existing structure while asking investors and owners to fund losses indefinitely.
- **Temporary or reversible measures alone will not be enough.**  
  A cosmetic package that avoids hard decisions would fail the company, the brand, and the workforce in the medium term.

### What management needs
To be concrete: we need a **durable reduction in cost and capacity**, not a short-term truce.

That means:
1. **A genuine labour-cost contribution**.
2. **A structural reduction in overcapacity**.
3. **Clear productivity and flexibility improvements**.
4. **A package that restores return on sales and frees funds for investment to 2030**.

I am open on the exact design. I am not insisting that the only route is immediate closure of a specific plant. But I am saying plainly: if the employee side’s answer is simply “no closures, no job cuts, no guarantees touched,” then there is no viable restructuring.

### On ownership and governance
It is also important to be realistic about the governance context. We need a solution that can actually pass the supervisory board and withstand economic scrutiny. A package that protects every current site regardless of economics will not satisfy the duty to preserve the group’s competitiveness.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to negotiate, but only on the basis of a **real structural package**:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a substantial labour-cost contribution,
- and a credible agreement that restores competitiveness.

If the workforce side wants to avoid closures, then it must come forward with an alternative that delivers equivalent savings and durability. Otherwise, management will continue to press for the structural measures it considers necessary.
