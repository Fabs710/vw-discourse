I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the state, the union, the works council, investors, and the expert input on the central fact: **the company needs a structural reset, not a cosmetic truce**.

I also agree that:
- temporary measures alone are insufficient,
- a prolonged strike would destroy value,
- and any settlement must be **durable** and **governable** under our co-determination structure.

That is the right starting point.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept a closure-first mindset
Management still speaks as if plant closure is the natural benchmark and all other options must justify themselves against it. I do **not** accept that framing.

Closure is not the neutral baseline. It is the most disruptive instrument. In this governance structure, with co-determination and the special statute, a closure strategy that triggers conflict, deadlock, and political escalation may be value-destructive even if it looks neat on a spreadsheet.

So I will not endorse closure as the default answer.

### 2) I do not accept cosmetic no-closure packages
At the same time, I do **not** accept a weak compromise dressed up as reform.

If “no closure” means only:
- a hiring freeze,
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- some attrition,
- and vague efficiency promises,

then that is not enough. That would preserve the problem and simply delay the next crisis.

## What the expert analysis changes
The expert input is useful because it confirms the real trade-off:

- **Labour-only compromise**: too weak.
- **Deep no-closure rationalisation**: potentially strong enough.
- **Closure**: highest theoretical savings, but also highest execution and governance risk.

That tells me the real question is not whether the company needs change. It does.  
The question is whether we can get **most of the closure economics without closures**.

My answer is: **potentially yes, but only if the package is genuinely deep**.

## What I can support
I would support a package only if it includes all of the following:

1. **No immediate German plant closures as the restructuring starting point**
2. **Real structural capacity reduction**
   - shift elimination
   - line consolidation
   - model reassignment
   - overhead reduction
3. **Material headcount reduction primarily through non-forced means**
   - voluntary exits
   - retirement pathways
   - redeployment
   - attrition
4. **Meaningful employee contribution**
   - temporary wage moderation
   - bonus restraint
   - working-time flexibility
5. **Binding investment and product commitments in Germany**
6. **A package that can actually pass governance and preserve labour peace**

That is the only route I see as potentially value-preserving.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- indefinite delay,
- a deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- or a solution that threatens ownership control or makes the enterprise ungovernable.

I also will not accept forced closure as a show of strength. If management wants closure, it must explain why a deep internal restructuring is insufficient and why the value destruction from conflict is justified. So far, that case has **not** been made.

## To management
You need to stop arguing in absolutes and bring a quantified package.

Show us:
- the minimum permanent savings required,
- how much comes from internal measures,
- how much headcount reduction can realistically be achieved without compulsory redundancies,
- and what the remaining gap is.

If the remaining gap is small, then closure is not justified.  
If the gap is large, then management must also explain why no-closure has not been designed to close it.

But repeating “closures are necessary” is not an argument. It is a demand.

## To the works council and union
I respect your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no closure by stealth. Those are legitimate concerns.

But I also need to be clear: **you cannot ask for full preservation of every legacy arrangement and still expect a competitive turnaround**.

If you want no closures, then the no-closure package must be **hard**:
- real shift cuts,
- real overhead cuts,
- real productivity gains,
- real headcount reduction through non-forced means,
- and a meaningful employee contribution.

That is the price of keeping the sites open.

## To the regional state
I appreciate your role and your concern for industrial stability. But stability without competitiveness is just postponement.

If you support a no-closure path, then support a **real** no-closure path. Not a symbolic one.

## To investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic peace deal. I share that view. But do not assume the only serious solution is closure. A well-designed deep internal restructuring may preserve more value than a conflict-driven closure strategy.

## My position
My position is now clear:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to durable savings.
- **Yes** to a deep no-closure package if it is structurally strong enough.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to open-ended drift.
- **No** to any outcome that weakens governance control or permanently impairs the enterprise.
