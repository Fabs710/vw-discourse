As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for management’s restructuring.

We recognise that the company faces real pressure: weak demand, high costs, and the transition to electric vehicles. But the answer cannot be to force the workforce to pay for strategic failures through shutdowns, layoffs, and the destruction of trust. That would be unacceptable socially, economically, and industrially.

Our priorities are:

1. Keep every German plant open.  
2. Protect every job through 2030 with binding guarantees.  
3. Preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework.  
4. Pursue restructuring only through alternatives that avoid forced layoffs and closures.  
5. Ensure any employee contribution is fair, limited, and matched by real commitments from management on investment, future products, and site security.

If management wants savings, then we are prepared to discuss alternatives such as:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- internal reallocation,
- natural turnover,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- and a negotiated, temporary employee contribution.

But I want to be equally clear: any plan built on closing German sites or pushing out workers against their will will meet fierce resistance. We are prepared to defend these jobs and plants with all the means available to us, including industrial action and use of our governance rights.

If there is to be a solution, it must be a negotiated one that secures the German sites, protects employment, and gives the workforce a real role in shaping the transformation.
