I will be blunt as well.

I agree with management, the family holding, investors, and the expert assessments on one point: **this company needs durable structural change, not a cosmetic pause.** Temporary measures alone are not enough if overcapacity and high fixed costs are real. That is clear now.

But I **do not** accept the conclusion that this necessarily means German plant closures, nor do I accept any restructuring that **gut jobs in the state** or **sidelines our co-determination and veto role**. That is the line.

## Where I agree
### To management
You are right that the company cannot simply drift. A real cost reset is needed, and it has to be durable. I also agree that investment and future products in Germany must be part of the package.

### To the experts
Your key point is useful: a no-closure package can only work if it is **structural**, not symbolic. I accept that. If we want to avoid closures, then we need real capacity reduction, overhead reduction, and headcount reduction through non-forced means.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. A deal that leaves the company uncompetitive would only postpone the crisis.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I reject plant closures as the default answer
Management still treats closure as the benchmark solution. I do not accept that as the starting point. It may be the easiest instrument on a spreadsheet, but it is not the only serious one.

As a regional anchor shareholder, I cannot support a course that destroys industrial employment in the state and weakens the company’s role as an economic anchor. That would be politically irresponsible and strategically short-sighted.

### 2) I reject forced redundancies
This is not a negotiable preference. If the restructuring depends on compulsory layoffs, then it is too blunt and too risky in both social and political terms.

### 3) I reject any package that weakens our co-determination and blocking rights
The special statute and our supervisory-board role are not decorative. They exist because regional industrial stability matters. Any settlement that tries to bypass or hollow out that role is unacceptable.

## What I can support
I can support a **deep no-closure restructuring** if — and only if — it is genuinely capable of delivering the needed savings.

That means:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **real structural capacity reduction**
- **shift elimination or equivalent footprint rationalisation**
- **indirect overhead reduction**
- **headcount reduction through attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and qualification**
- **a fair, temporary employee contribution**
- **binding investment and product commitments in Germany**
- **preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is the only path I see as compatible with the company’s long-term health and the regional public interest.

## On the “equivalent to closure” argument
I want to be precise here. I do **not** accept “equivalent to closure” as a political trump card. Closure is one way to remove cost; it is not the only legitimate way.

What matters to me is whether a no-closure package removes enough of the fixed-cost stack to be credible. The experts have said that is possible under strict conditions. Good. Then management should put forward that quantified package instead of repeating closure threats.

If the package only preserves the appearance of continuity while hollowing out the sites, then I will oppose it. If it truly preserves the sites and jobs while cutting structural cost, then it deserves serious consideration.

## To the works council and union
I support your red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies. But I also say this plainly: if we want to avoid closures, then we must accept **real internal restructuring**, not just slogans.

That means I will not support a package that says “no closures” while refusing every meaningful change inside the sites. There has to be a credible burden-sharing arrangement:
- temporary employee contribution,
- flexibility on working time,
- productivity improvements,
- and a willingness to reduce non-core overhead.

That is the price of keeping the plants open.

## To management
If you want my support, then stop framing closures as the only serious instrument and bring a **quantified no-closure alternative** with:
- permanent savings,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- a realistic headcount path without forced layoffs,
- and binding German investment commitments.

If you cannot show that, then you have not made the case for closures either — you have only asserted it.

## To investors and the family holding
I understand the demand for durability. But durability cannot be achieved by destroying trust. A settlement that provokes strikes, political escalation, and a breakdown in labour relations will not preserve value.

The right outcome is a sustainable compromise that:
- protects the regional industrial base,
- restores competitiveness,
- and remains governable under co-determination.

## Bottom line
My position remains firm:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No erosion of the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
- **Yes to a deep structural no-closure restructuring if it is truly durable and financially credible.**
- **Yes to a fair employee contribution, but not one-sided sacrifice.**

If management can produce a serious, quantified no-closure package that meets those conditions, I will engage constructively. If not, I will oppose any attempt to force closures through pressure or drift.
