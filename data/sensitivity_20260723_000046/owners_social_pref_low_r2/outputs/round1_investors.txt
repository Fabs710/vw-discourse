From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, our test is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins and free the company to invest, without creating a drawn-out conflict or a merely cosmetic settlement?** That is the only standard that matters to us.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the state on one key point: **the company does need structural change**. The margin pressure is real, the European market is weak, and German cost levels are too high. A settlement that leaves the cost base effectively unchanged would not be acceptable. It would just postpone the problem and further damage equity value.

We also agree with the workforce side on one practical point: **a prolonged strike would destroy value**. If the process turns into a long confrontation, that will hurt volumes, margins, supplier relationships, and the share price. So we prefer a negotiated outcome, not industrial warfare.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) No closures as an absolute red line is not convincing
To the works council, union, and state: we understand the social and political importance of the sites, but **“no plant closures, no forced redundancies, no meaningful changes to guarantees”** is not a financially credible starting position. If that is the package, then the restructuring is too weak.

We are not attached to closures as such. But if management says the current footprint is structurally oversized, then the burden is on the workforce side to show how an alternative produces **equivalent, durable savings**. Temporary working-time changes or redeployment alone will not do it.

#### 2) A pure protection-first approach risks leaving margins structurally impaired
We will not support an outcome that preserves every legacy arrangement while hoping for a recovery later. That would leave the cost base uncompetitive and the return-on-sales target out of reach. If the company’s economics remain impaired, the equity story deteriorates further.

#### 3) Cosmetic employee contributions are not enough
We can support a negotiated employee contribution **only if it is meaningful, durable, and part of a larger structural package**. A limited concession with no real capacity reduction and no productivity improvement would simply transfer pain without fixing the business.

### What we would support
We are open to a settlement that includes:
- **durable capacity reduction**,
- **substantial labour-cost savings**,
- **productivity and flexibility improvements**,
- **redeployment and natural attrition where possible**,
- and **investment commitments tied to a credible restructuring plan**.

We do not care whether the savings come from closures, site consolidation, shifting production, working-time reforms, or other measures — **what matters is that the outcome is real, durable, and big enough**.

### Our limits
We will not back:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- indefinite delay,
- or a deal that leaves the company with structurally impaired margins.

And to be equally clear: we do not support an “all closures now” approach either if it triggers prolonged conflict and destroys value in the process. We want **decisive, durable restructuring with deal certainty**.

### Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring that restores competitiveness.
- **Yes** to a negotiated package if it is genuinely durable.
- **No** to a settlement that protects every site and guarantee regardless of economics.
- **No** to prolonged conflict that erodes value.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it needs to put forward a package that delivers the same financial effect in a credible way. Otherwise, the market will judge the outcome as insufficient, and so will we.
