The core impasse now appears to be this:

## Core impasse
**There is agreement that the cost base must come down structurally, but no agreement on the form, depth, or safeguards of that reduction.**

More specifically, the deadlock is between four positions that have not yet been reconciled:

1. **Management / investors / family holding** want a package that is **durable, materially large, and board-credible**.
2. **Works council / union** accept structural change only if it is **inside the existing plant network**, with **no closures and no compulsory redundancies**.
3. **State / shareholder-side actors** want **industrial stability and political acceptability**, and are unwilling to back either empty symbolism or value-destructive escalation.
4. **The expert input** suggests that a no-closure path is possible only if it is **deep enough to approximate most of the closure savings**.

So the stalemate is not “change vs no change.” It is:
**how much structural cost reduction is required, through which instruments, and with what binding protections.**

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Deep no-closure restructuring with measurable savings floor
A compromise package could be structured around:
- **no German plant closures for a defined planning horizon**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding capacity reduction targets**
- **shift elimination / line consolidation**
- **material overhead reduction**
- **voluntary exits and redeployment**
- **temporary employee contribution**
- **binding investment and product commitments**

To bridge the conflicting sides, this could include a **minimum savings threshold** and a **review point**:
- if the package reaches the agreed savings floor, closures are off the table;
- if it does not, the parties reopen only the unresolved elements, not the whole framework.

This may bridge:
- employee side’s site-security red lines,
- management’s demand for measurable structural savings,
- investor/family demand for seriousness.

---

### Proposal 2: Tiered restructuring with site-specific protections
A second path is a **differentiated site approach**:
- all sites remain open,
- but not all sites retain the same footprint,
- selected lines, shifts, or support functions are consolidated,
- vulnerable sites receive stronger investment and product commitments,
- stronger sites absorb more of the consolidation burden.

This could be paired with:
- a **no-forced-layoff rule**,
- **voluntary exit windows**,
- **qualification/redeployment guarantees**,
- and a **joint monitoring mechanism**.

This may bridge:
- works council and union concerns about closure-by-stealth,
- management’s need for actual footprint change,
- state’s concern for regional stability.

---

### Proposal 3: Time-bound employee contribution with anti-closure safeguard
A third compromise could combine:
- immediate employee contribution measures,
- binding owner/management commitments on investment,
- and a **time-bound no-closure clause**.

For example:
- employee-side concessions apply for a fixed period,
- in return, management commits not to pursue closures during that period,
- if economic triggers are breached, the parties must first exhaust internal restructuring tools before any closure consideration.

This may bridge:
- management’s need for fast relief,
- employee side’s demand for security,
- investors’ demand for enforcement,
- and the state’s desire for industrial continuity.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is your quantified minimum structural savings requirement, and which specific internal measures would you accept as sufficient if they deliver that number without closures?**

### To the works council
**If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are guaranteed, what level of permanent capacity reduction, shift elimination, and site-level restructuring are you prepared to accept to make the package economically credible?**

### To the union
**Which mix of voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and temporary labor-cost concessions would you support if it were explicitly tied to binding investment and no-closure guarantees?**

### To the regional state
**Are you prepared to back a deep internal restructuring package even if it requires visible footprint reduction at some sites, provided jobs and ownership of the plants remain in Germany?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Would you support a no-closure compromise if the quantified savings reach a clearly defined threshold and the supervisory-board governance position remains intact?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of permanent cost reduction and execution certainty would make a no-closure package investable for you, even if it is somewhat below closure-level savings?**

### To the expert side
**What specific combination of shift cuts, overhead reduction, and voluntary exits would you regard as the minimum credible “deep no-closure” package, and where do you see the savings floor below which it becomes non-viable?**
