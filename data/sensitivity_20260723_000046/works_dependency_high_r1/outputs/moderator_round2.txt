### 1) Agreed so far
- **A real restructuring is necessary**; the status quo is not sustainable.
- **Cosmetic or delay-only solutions are rejected** by all sides.
- There is broad openness to a package combining:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - voluntary exits / retirement bridges,
  - redeployment and qualification,
  - working-time tools,
  - management restraint,
  - owner/shareholder contribution,
  - and investment commitments for future products.
- All parties accept that any settlement should be **durable, measurable, and implementable**.

### 2) Core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: employee representatives, state government, and family strongly oppose them; management and investors keep them on the table unless an equivalent alternative is proven.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: the employee side and state reject them; management/investors seek a credible headcount reduction if needed.
- **Scope and bindingness of guarantees**: employee side wants binding site and job guarantees to 2030; management/investors resist blanket guarantees.
- **Burden-sharing**: all say everyone should contribute, but there is no agreement on the scale, form, or sequencing of contributions.
- **What counts as “equivalent” restructuring**: management/investors require hard, permanent cost and capacity reductions; employee side insists this must be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required exactly**, and how much must come from labor versus management, owners, procurement, and capital allocation.
- **Whether non-closure measures can deliver the same durable economic effect** as closures and forced redundancies.
- **How to balance job/site protection against competitiveness and margin restoration**.
- **What concessions are conditional on what commitments**: e.g., employee contributions linked to investment, product allocation, and governance guarantees.
- **How to make any agreement verifiable** while preserving trust and industrial peace.

### 4) Brief refocus for next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**:
  1. required savings gap,
  2. measures by source,
  3. site-by-site capacity implications,
  4. employment impact under each option,
  5. binding commitments and milestones.
- Ask each side to table **one concrete package** and **one fallback package**.
- Keep the discussion centered on a single test: **Can the company reach durable competitiveness without plant closures and compulsory redundancies, and if so, by which exact measures?**
