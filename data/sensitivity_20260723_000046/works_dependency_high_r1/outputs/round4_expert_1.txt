Below is a concise, neutral **restructuring finance bridge**. Because no plant-level financial data were provided, this is **not an audit** and cannot be a company-specific valuation. It is a **decision framework** showing what needs to be demonstrated for a non-closure package to be judged economically equivalent to a closure-led package.

## 1) What “equivalence” should mean
For restructuring finance purposes, a non-closure package is only comparable if it delivers, on a durable run-rate basis:

- **Annual fixed-cost reduction**
- **Permanent capacity reduction**
- **Cash savings net of restructuring outflows**
- **Margin / EBIT recovery through 2030**
- **Enforceability and low reversal risk**

A useful threshold is:

- **At least ~80–100% of closure-case run-rate EBIT improvement**
- **At least ~70–90% of the capacity reduction**
- **No materially worse cash payback**
- **Clear, auditable milestones**

If a non-closure plan misses those levels materially, it may still be a political compromise, but it is **not financially equivalent**.

## 2) Side-by-side scenario comparison
### Illustrative decision bridge
These are **illustrative ranges only**, not company facts.

| Metric | Closure-led | Non-closure | Hybrid |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Permanent fixed-cost reduction | High | Medium | High |
| Permanent capacity reduction | High | Low-Medium | Medium-High |
| One-time restructuring cash cost | High | Low-Medium | Medium |
| Annual run-rate EBIT uplift | Highest | Moderate | Moderate-High |
| Execution risk | Medium | High | Medium |
| Reversal risk | Low | Medium-High | Low-Medium |

### Indicative ranges
For the affected German operations, a reasonable **illustrative** framing would be:

- **Closure-led:** annual run-rate EBIT uplift roughly **€300m–€700m+**
- **Non-closure:** roughly **€150m–€450m**
- **Hybrid:** roughly **€250m–€600m**

These ranges depend heavily on:
- plant size and labor intensity,
- current utilization,
- transfer costs,
- product mix,
- and how much overhead truly exits.

---

## 3) What the numbers must show
To support a non-closure settlement, management would need to quantify at least:

1. **Savings gap**
   - What exact annual cost reduction is still missing?

2. **Source of savings**
   - Management overhead
   - Procurement
   - Capex reprioritization
   - Workforce measures
   - Capacity changes

3. **Capacity impact by site**
   - Which shifts, lines, or output volumes are permanently removed?

4. **Employment impact**
   - Headcount reduction via voluntary exits, attrition, retirement bridges, redeployment

5. **Cash payback**
   - Upfront restructuring cash outflow
   - Annual savings
   - Payback period

6. **Durability**
   - What prevents reversal after 1–3 years?

---

## 4) Financial logic of the three options
### A. Closure-led package
Best at:
- removing fixed overhead,
- eliminating excess capacity,
- improving utilization at remaining sites.

Weaknesses:
- high one-time cash cost,
- legal / labor / execution complexity,
- industrial conflict risk.

### B. Pure non-closure package
Can work only if it includes:
- deep shift reductions,
- meaningful headcount decline,
- lasting labor-cost contribution,
- internal consolidation,
- management/owner contribution.

Weakness:
- often too dependent on cooperation and discipline,
- less complete removal of fixed cost,
- higher risk that savings erode.

### C. Hybrid package
Usually the most financeable compromise:
- preserves some sites,
- but still removes enough footprint to matter,
- often best balance of durability and execution risk.

---

## 5) My judgment on the threshold
If I were advising a board on restructuring finance alone, I would say:

- A non-closure package should only be accepted if it delivers **near-parity in run-rate EBIT and capacity normalization** versus closure.
- If it achieves only partial savings, then it is **not equivalent**, even if it avoids immediate conflict.
- If the alternative depends mainly on temporary instruments, it is **not durable** enough for a 2030 restructuring case.

---

## 6) What additional data are needed
To move from framework to audit-grade comparison, I would need:

- plant-by-plant P&Ls
- fixed vs variable cost split
- current and projected utilization rates
- headcount by site and function
- unit labor cost by plant
- one-time closure / conversion costs
- capex plans by site and model
- transfer/logistics costs
- margin contribution by product line
- assumption set for demand through 2030

Without those, no one can honestly claim a verified equivalence test has been met.

## Bottom line
A **non-closure package can be viable**, but only if it produces:
- materially permanent cost reduction,
- real capacity correction,
- and a run-rate EBIT bridge close to the closure case.

If it cannot, then it is not economically equivalent; it is only a negotiated alternative.
