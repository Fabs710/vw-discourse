We are not moving off the core point: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no hollowing out of co-determination**.

I hear management, investors, and the family all repeating the same word: “structural.” Fine. But “structural” is not a magic word that justifies breaking the social contract and pushing the cost of strategic mistakes onto the workforce.

## Where I agree
I agree that:
- the company needs a serious restructuring,
- the current cost base is not sustainable,
- and a credible plan must exist for 2030 and beyond.

I also agree that a cosmetic solution would be irresponsible. If the business is genuinely in trouble, then we need real answers — not slogans.

## Where I strongly disagree
### To management
You are still speaking as if closures are the benchmark for seriousness. They are not. They are the benchmark for failure of imagination and failure of negotiation.

You say voluntary exits, attrition, and working-time tools are not enough. Then bring forward a **real alternative package** instead of threatening jobs and plants. We will not accept the logic that the only way to prove seriousness is to destroy livelihoods.

And let me be very clear: after the historic job-security pledge was broken, trust is already damaged. You do not repair that by escalating pressure and expecting the workforce to quietly absorb it.

### To institutional investors
You keep demanding “verifiable” savings, but you are not the ones who bear the social consequences of closures and forced redundancies. If you want durable savings, then you must accept that they can be achieved through:
- capacity reduction,
- redeployment,
- internal transfers,
- qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- management restraint,
- and owner contributions.

What you are not entitled to demand is an outcome that is only “acceptable” if it looks tough enough on the balance sheet, regardless of whether it tears apart the company’s industrial base.

### To the family holding
You speak about long-term value, and I take that seriously. Then support a settlement that actually preserves the company’s industrial strength in Germany. A “viable footprint” cannot mean sacrificing one or more plants and calling it stability.

If the family wants stability and value preservation, it should back:
- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- real investment commitments,
- and a restructuring that does not break the company’s legitimacy with its workforce.

### To the state government
You are right to resist closures and compulsory layoffs. But “no closures as the starting point” is not enough. The workforce needs an explicit commitment that German plants remain open and that employment is protected. Anything less keeps us in permanent uncertainty.

## What we will accept
We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments **without** plant closures,
- internal redeployment across sites,
- voluntary exits only,
- retirement bridges,
- qualification and training,
- temporary and fair working-time measures,
- a limited, negotiated employee contribution if and only if management and owners contribute materially too,
- a binding industrial plan with investment and product allocation for Germany.

## What we will not accept
- Any plant closure
- Any compulsory redundancy
- Any “temporary” solution that quietly prepares the next wave of cuts
- Any deal that removes guarantees and then calls that modernization
- Any agreement that weakens co-determination or turns consultation into theatre

## The concrete test
If management wants to show this is not just a one-way cut, then put real numbers on the table:

1. **How much cost reduction is needed exactly?**
2. **How much can come from management overhead, owner contribution, procurement, and capital allocation?**
3. **How much can come from voluntary and internal measures without forced layoffs?**
4. **What investment and product commitments are tied to German sites?**
5. **What binding job and site guarantees are offered through 2030?**

Until those questions are answered honestly, we are not discussing a balanced restructuring — we are discussing pressure tactics.

## Bottom line
Our position remains firm:

- **German plants stay open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees to 2030**
- **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners**
- **Real co-determination in shaping the plan**

If you want industrial peace, bring a plan that respects those conditions.  
If you want the workforce to pay for every problem, then you will face resistance.
