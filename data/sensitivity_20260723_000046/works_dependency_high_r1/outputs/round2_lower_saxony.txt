I will be direct.

The state shareholder **does not accept plant closures in Germany as the default solution**, and we **do not accept compulsory redundancies**. Those are not abstract preferences; they are unacceptable because they would damage the regional economy, destroy trust, and weaken the company’s long-term foundation.

At the same time, I am **not** defending the status quo. The company clearly needs a restructuring that is real, measurable, and durable. If we simply postpone the problem, we will end up with a worse crisis later. So I do agree with management, the family, and investors on one point: **there must be structural improvement**. But the question is whether that improvement must come through closures and forced job cuts. My answer remains no.

## To management
I understand the pressure on margins and competitiveness. But I do **not** accept your framing that closures are the benchmark of seriousness.

What I disagree with:
- I do not accept a closure-first logic.
- I do not accept blanket resistance to site guarantees.
- I do not accept that the state’s co-determination role can be treated as an obstacle to be worked around.

What I do accept:
- The cost base must come down.
- Capacity must be adjusted.
- Labour-cost measures may be part of the package.
- The solution must be durable and enforceable.

But if you want support from me, then bring **numbers and instruments**, not threats. Show me a package that achieves real savings through:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time instruments,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

If that cannot be done, then I will not endorse a plan simply because it is presented as unavoidable.

## To the works council and union
I support your core red lines:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no hollowing out of co-determination**

I agree that the workforce cannot again be made the sole shock absorber for strategic failures.

Where I would be careful is that “no closures” must not mean “no change at all.” The company does need operational adjustment. So I will support a solution only if it includes real efficiency gains and a credible industrial plan for Germany. That means:
- capacity reduction without site shutdowns,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- qualification,
- and a limited, negotiated contribution if and only if management and ownership also contribute materially.

But I will be very clear: **I will not support an arrangement that turns site protection into a euphemism for forcing the workforce to carry everything alone.**

## To the controlling family
I respect your focus on long-term value. On that, we are aligned. But long-term value is not preserved by hollowing out the company’s German base or by weakening the social partnership that has kept it stable.

I do agree that the solution must be economically credible. I do **not** agree that this automatically means plant closures. You need to support a settlement that preserves the company’s industrial anchor in Germany while still delivering the necessary savings.

## To institutional investors
You are right that the company must return to competitive margins. I do not dispute that.

Where I disagree is your implication that only severe structural cuts count as “verifiable” savings. That is too narrow. Verifiable savings can also come from:
- operational redesign,
- capacity reductions without closures,
- procurement improvements,
- management restraint,
- capital allocation discipline,
- and negotiated workforce measures.

You want certainty and durability. So do I. But certainty is not created by triggering a socially destructive confrontation. It is created by an enforceable, balanced settlement.

## My position
The state is prepared to support a restructuring that:
1. protects German sites,
2. avoids compulsory layoffs,
3. delivers real capacity adjustment,
4. secures future investment and products in Germany,
5. preserves co-determination,
6. and makes the company competitive again.

What we will **not** support:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced redundancies,
- a one-sided workforce sacrifice,
- or any weakening of the state’s veto and co-determination role.

## The concrete test
If management wants our support, then put forward a package with:
- quantified savings,
- specific site-by-site capacity adjustments,
- a timetable,
- management and owner contributions,
- and binding commitments on jobs, investment, and future products.

If you cannot produce that without closures, then the burden is on you to prove why no other route exists. At present, I do **not** see that proof.

So my message is simple:
**We are open to a serious restructuring. We are not open to a dismantling of Germany’s industrial base.**
