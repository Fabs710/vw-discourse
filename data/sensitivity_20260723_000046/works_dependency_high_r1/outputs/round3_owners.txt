## Where I agree
I agree with the broad premise from management, investors, the union, the works council, and the state: **this company needs a real restructuring**. The current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable if we want to restore margins, fund future products, and protect the long-term value of the business.

I also agree that:
- a cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible,
- industrial peace has real value,
- and everyone should contribute in some form.

That includes labour, management, ownership, and capital.

## Where I disagree

### To management
I support the need for structural improvement. I do **not** support presenting plant closures or hard cuts as the only serious language of discipline.

Your point about overcapacity and labour costs is real. But you still owe the board and the owners a **properly quantified alternative comparison**:
- what savings come from capacity change,
- what from labour measures,
- what from procurement,
- what from management overhead,
- what from capital allocation discipline,
- and what from ownership contribution.

If you cannot show why a non-closure package fails, then you do not yet have the case to force closures. If you can show it, then say it in numbers, not in generalities.

What I will **not** back is a prolonged confrontation that destroys brand value, cash, and trust. That would be value-destructive and unacceptable.

### To the works council and union
I respect your red lines, but I do **not** accept red lines by themselves as a substitute for an economically equivalent solution.

“No plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are understandable, but they are not enough on their own. If the business remains structurally uncompetitive, then the result is not job security — it is delayed job loss.

So if you want the no-closure path to be credible, it must be shown to deliver:
- durable capacity reduction,
- real labour-cost improvement,
- measurable productivity gains,
- and a footprint that the company can afford long term.

I am open to alternatives to closures, but only if they are **economically equivalent and enforceable**. Otherwise, they are just a delay mechanism.

I also reject the idea that management and owners can simply “trim the top” and somehow close the gap. They must contribute, yes — but it is not realistic to pretend that overhead trimming alone solves the structural problem.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your concern for regional stability and industrial legitimacy. That is legitimate and important.

But I do **not** accept a position that effectively freezes industrial adjustment because closures are politically difficult. The state cannot ask the company to preserve every site and still be competitive unless it is prepared to show how the economics are fixed another way.

If you want our support, the state should help push for a **credible, enforceable, non-cosmetic restructuring** — not simply prohibit the instruments that may be necessary if no equivalent solution exists.

### To institutional investors
You are right to insist on durable, verifiable savings. I agree with that standard.

Where I differ is that I do not accept a market test that only rewards the harshest-looking headline. The test is whether the business is actually stronger afterward.

So yes: if a non-closure package can credibly restore margins, reduce excess capacity, and strengthen the balance sheet, I will support it. If it cannot, then we should not pretend otherwise.

## My position as controlling shareholder
Let me be explicit:

- I support **a serious restructuring**
- I support **alternatives to closures** if they are truly equivalent
- I do **not** support a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive
- I do **not** support value-destructive instability
- I do **not** support any outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control

That is the boundary.

1. **Measurable and durable capacity reduction**
2. **A substantial permanent labour-cost contribution**
3. **Real productivity gains**
4. **Management and owner contributions in parallel**
5. **Binding investment and product commitments**
6. **A credible path back to competitive margins and ROS**

That is the standard. Not slogans, not goodwill, not temporary relief.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that merely postpones the adjustment,
- a package that preserves overcapacity,
- or a settlement that forces the owners to accept permanent value erosion.

If the employee side can show a package with the same economic effect as closures and forced redundancies, I am open. If not, then I will not pretend the problem is solved.
