We are aligned on the core red lines: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**.

I want to be equally clear on what follows from that: if management insists that the only “solution” is to make the workforce pay for strategic and managerial failures through closures, layoffs, and unilateral wage destruction, then that is not a basis for agreement. It would be unacceptable and will be resisted.

That said, I do agree with the works council on one important point: **a restructuring is necessary**. We are not denying the pressure on the company, the cost gap, or the need for a credible path to the future. But restructuring must be done **fairly and industrially**, not by turning employees into the shock absorber for everything.

Our position as the trade union is:

- **Protect jobs and sites**
- **Preserve the collective bargaining framework**
- **Avoid forced redundancies**
- **Avoid plant closures**
- **Use negotiated instruments instead**: working-time adjustments, capacity reductions, voluntary exits, qualification, internal redeployment, and temporary employee contributions if they are balanced and limited
- **Expect management and owners to contribute materially as well**

Where I differ from any approach that simply talks about “pain sharing” in the abstract is this:  
**employees cannot be the only side asked to sacrifice.** If there is to be an employee contribution, then management compensation, bonuses, overheads, investment priorities, and owner responsibility must also be part of the package. Shared sacrifice means exactly that — shared.

So the union is prepared to engage on a serious alternative package if it contains:

1. **Binding security for German sites**
2. **No compulsory dismissals**
3. **A durable collective agreement**
4. **A fair, time-limited labour-cost contribution, if necessary**
5. **Real commitments on future products and investment in Germany**
6. **A restructuring that does not break the social compact**

If those conditions are met, there is room for a negotiated settlement.  
If not, then the workforce will not accept being told that closures and layoffs are “inevitable.”

I would ask management and the owners to stop presenting this as a one-way cost-cutting exercise. If they want industrial peace, they need to put something substantial on the table: security, investment, and fairness.
