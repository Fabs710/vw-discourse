## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the core diagnosis: **the business needs a real restructuring**. The status quo is not sustainable, and a cosmetic deal would only postpone the next crisis. I also agree that:
- savings must be **measurable and durable**,
- management, owners, and labour all need to contribute,
- and the company needs a credible path to restore margins and fund future products through 2030.

That part is not in dispute.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the attempt to turn **“no plant closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** into absolute barriers regardless of the economics.

I have said consistently: the controlling family supports **alternatives to closures if they are truly economically equivalent and enforceable**. That is the standard. Not slogans, not trust me, not political red lines by themselves.

### To the works council
I respect the role of co-determination and I understand the damage trust has suffered. But I do **not** accept the claim that because management previously broke trust, every future structural adjustment must now be ruled out. That would be a recipe for stagnation and long-term value erosion.

You say closures are just leverage. They are not just leverage if the numbers show excess capacity and a structural cost gap that cannot be closed otherwise. Then they are an instrument of last resort. I am not endorsing closures as a default. I am saying they remain on the table unless a credible alternative closes the economics gap.

What I reject is your attempt to make a **permanent site guarantee to 2030** the starting point. If the footprint is frozen regardless of economics, then we are not restructuring; we are deferring impairment.

### To the union
I agree that workers should not bear the whole burden. I do **not** accept, however, that “fairness” can be used to avoid hard structural change. A fair settlement must be judged by whether it restores competitiveness, not by whether it feels balanced in the abstract.

You ask for a collective-bargaining compatible solution. Fine. Then show me the exact package:
- labor-cost contribution,
- permanent headcount reduction,
- productivity gains,
- capacity adjustment,
- and management/owner contributions.

If those numbers add up, I will support it. If they do not, then the refusal of closures is simply an obstacle, not a solution.

### To the regional state government
I respect your role and your regional responsibility. But I do **not** accept a position that effectively says “no closures, no forced redundancies” and expects the company to become competitive by force of will.

That is not an industrial strategy. It is a political constraint. And the company cannot be asked to carry a structurally uncompetitive footprint just to avoid political discomfort.

### To institutional investors
You are right to insist on quantified, durable savings. I agree with that. But I also want to be clear: I do **not** support using market pressure as a way to force an outcome without proving the alternative fails.

The burden of proof still matters. If a non-closure package is truly equivalent, it can be supported. If it is not, then saying “we oppose closures” is not an acceptable substitute for economics.

### To management
I agree with your basic diagnosis, but I do **not** accept vague assertions of necessity. You still owe the board and owners a proper bridge:
- exact savings gap,
- plant-by-plant capacity implications,
- labor cost reductions,
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement savings,
- capex reprioritization,
- and owner contribution.

Without that, you have not yet proven that the hard instruments are necessary. And without proof, I will not support a value-destructive confrontation.

## What would move me
I remain open to a no-closure settlement **only if** it includes all of the following:
1. measurable and durable capacity reduction,
2. a substantial permanent labour-cost contribution,
3. real headcount reduction,
4. verifiable productivity gains,
5. meaningful management and owner contributions,
6. binding investment and product commitments,
7. and a credible return to target margins.

If those conditions are met, I can support a non-closure path. If they are not, then I will not pretend the problem is solved.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a permanent footprint freeze,
- a deal that preserves overcapacity,
- or a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value.

I also will not support a prolonged conflict that destroys brand value, cash, and trust. That is precisely why I am insisting on a credible, durable, quantified solution rather than a political declaration.

We are open to a negotiated settlement. We are **not** open to a weak one.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then bring a package that can be audited and shown to be economically equivalent in substance, not just in aspiration. If that cannot be done, then we should be honest: some form of hard structural adjustment remains necessary.
