I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone that **the company needs a real restructuring**. The current situation is not sustainable. The cost base is too high, the European market is shrinking, and we cannot fund the product and technology transition to 2030 if we keep carrying structural overcapacity and above-target labour costs.

I also agree that a purely cosmetic solution would be a mistake. A deal that leaves the business structurally weak is not an option.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to turn “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute red lines **without** offering an equivalent structural solution.

That is the central issue.

### To the works council
You say plant closures are a failure of imagination. I say refusing to address overcapacity is a failure of responsibility.

You ask for binding site guarantees to 2030, no layoffs, and no closures. I need to be blunt: **I cannot support blanket guarantees that freeze the current footprint irrespective of economics**. If we do that, we simply lock in a cost base that is already out of line with competitors and we postpone the next crisis.

You also ask for management and owner contributions. Fine — they will have to contribute. But let’s be honest: **no amount of overhead trimming or symbolic sacrifice at the top closes the structural gap**. The problem is not only “management failure”; it is an industrial footprint and labour-cost structure that are no longer competitive enough.

### To the trade union
I appreciate that you are prepared to discuss working-time changes, voluntary exits, qualification, and negotiated contributions. That is better than pretending nothing needs to change.

But I do not accept your premise that **closures are merely a bargaining failure**. If the alternative package does not deliver comparable, durable savings, then it is not an alternative. It is delay.

And I want to be very clear: **voluntary exits, retirements, redeployment, and temporary measures are not sufficient by themselves** if we are serious about restoring margins. We need a permanent and measurable reduction in cost and capacity. Anything less leaves us uncompetitive.

### To the regional state government
You are right that co-determination matters and that the company must remain a regional industrial anchor. I respect that.

But I do **not** accept that regional political sensitivity can override the economic reality of the business. If the state supports a package that protects every site but fails to fix the cost problem, it will not be preserving the company — it will be protecting an uncompetitive structure.

### To the controlling family
I agree that we need long-term value, not short-term theatre. But long-term value requires hard choices now. Support for “competitiveness with stability” is only meaningful if it includes a real footprint correction, not just a more polished version of the status quo.

### To institutional investors
You are right to insist on verifiable, durable savings. I agree with that. But then be consistent: **verifiable savings require real structural measures**. If you want the margin problem fixed, you cannot simultaneously rule out the instruments that actually move the economics.

## My position
Let me state it plainly:

- **Yes** to a negotiated solution
- **Yes** to alternatives to closures if they truly deliver equivalent savings
- **No** to a settlement that leaves overcapacity in place
- **No** to a deal that preserves every site unchanged while asking for only temporary workforce measures
- **No** to a “guarantee” that prevents necessary restructuring
- **Yes** to management, owners, and labour all contributing
- **Yes** to investment in future products and technology
- **No** to using that as a reason to avoid hard capacity decisions

## What would actually move me
I am prepared to work with an alternative **if** it includes all of the following:

1. **A measurable and durable reduction in capacity**
2. **A substantial permanent labour-cost contribution**
3. **A real reduction in headcount, not just natural attrition over time**
4. **A footprint that is economically viable, not politically comfortable**
5. **Clear commitments to future products and investment to 2030**
6. **A settlement that restores the brand’s return-on-sales target**

If you want me to accept no closures, then you need to show me a package that produces the same economic effect. Not slogans. Not principles. Numbers.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a promise of job security that prevents structural correction,
- a settlement that keeps all current sites protected while the business remains uncompetitive,
- or a deal that pushes the company further behind competitors in the name of social peace.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it needs to engage on the actual economics: **where capacity comes out, how labour costs come down permanently, and how fast we can restore competitiveness**.

I am not moving off the need for deep structural change.  
If you want a deal, bring a credible alternative that fixes the numbers.
