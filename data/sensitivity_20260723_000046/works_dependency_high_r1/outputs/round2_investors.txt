We appreciate the clarity from all sides, but from a capital-markets perspective the test is still very simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins, or does it merely postpone the adjustment?**

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family, the state, and the employee side on one point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. The company needs a real restructuring, and industrial peace has value only if it is durable and economically credible.

We also agree that:
- a cosmetic compromise is useless,
- everyone may need to contribute,
- and the German footprint must remain investment-capable over the medium term.

## Where we disagree
### To the works council and union
We respect the no-closure/no-compulsory-redundancy line as a political and social position. But from our side, that is acceptable **only if** it produces **equivalent, measurable, durable savings**.

Right now, what you are offering is a direction of travel, not yet a financeable outcome. If the package relies mainly on:
- attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time tweaks,
- internal redeployment,

then we need to see hard numbers proving that this closes the cost gap fast enough. Otherwise, it is not a solution — it is a delay mechanism.

We will not back a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive or margins permanently impaired. That is a hard limit for us.

### To management
We support decisive restructuring, but we do **not** support a value-destructive showdown. Prolonged uncertainty, strikes, and brand damage are all heavily discounted by the market.

So while we agree that the business needs real structural savings, we do not want management using confrontation as a substitute for a credible plan. If the only path offered is “accept closures or accept chaos,” that is not good governance and not good capital allocation.

What we need from management is:
- quantified savings,
- a clear timetable,
- a credible footprint/capacity plan,
- and proof that the measures will restore ROS, not just cut headlines.

### To the state government
We understand the political constraints and the importance of co-determination. But political red lines do not change the economics.

If the state blocks any meaningful capacity adjustment, then investors will bear the cost through lower returns and continued underperformance. That is not acceptable to us. Regional stability matters, but not at the expense of a structurally weak equity story.

### To the controlling family
We agree with your focus on long-term value and governance integrity. But “competitiveness with stability” cannot become a slogan that preserves too much of the current structure.

If the company needs permanent cost reduction, then the package must deliver it in ways the market can underwrite. That means hard evidence of:
- footprint rationalisation,
- labour-cost improvement,
- and enforceable productivity gains.

## What would move us
We could support a non-closure settlement **if** it is genuinely equivalent in economic effect. That means, at minimum:

1. **A quantified, durable reduction in the cost base**
2. **A clear reduction in excess capacity**
3. **A verifiable path back to competitive margins**
4. **No prolonged industrial conflict**
5. **Implementation milestones that can be monitored**
6. **A credible contribution from management, owners, and labour**

## What we will not support
- A cosmetic compromise
- A deal that leaves overcapacity in place
- A settlement that depends on vague future promises
- A prolonged conflict that destroys value
- A package that protects every site but weakens the company’s economics

## Bottom line
We are open to alternatives to closures. We are **not** open to alternatives to competitiveness.

If the employee side can show that capacity reductions, productivity gains, and labour-cost measures will actually restore margins, we can support that. If not, then management should not be prevented from taking the actions needed to fix the business.

Our loyalty is to risk-adjusted equity value. The burden of proof is on the side proposing “no closures” to show that it is financially equivalent.
