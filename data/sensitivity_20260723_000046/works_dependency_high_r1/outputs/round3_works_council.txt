I will be equally clear in round 3: **we will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies in Germany, and we will not accept a restructuring that breaks the job-security framework under the pretext of “equivalence.”**

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one essential point: **the company needs a real restructuring**.  
I also agree that the status quo is not sustainable and that the future to 2030 must be secured with a serious industrial plan.

I further agree that:
- savings must be measurable,
- the solution must be durable,
- management, owners, and labour all have to contribute,
- and a purely cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible.

So no one should misunderstand us: **we are not denying reality**.

## Where I disagree — strongly

### To management
You keep saying “structural change” as if that automatically proves closures are necessary. It does not.

What I hear from you is still the same old logic:
- declare overcapacity,
- threaten plants,
- demand concessions,
- then call that a “credible alternative.”

That is not a plan. That is pressure.

You say voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, and qualification are not enough. Then give us the **full quantified package** that includes:
- management overhead reductions,
- procurement savings,
- postponement or reshaping of capital spending,
- owner contribution,
- productivity gains,
- and internal capacity adjustments **without closures**.

Do not start by demanding that workers first surrender their security and then claim the result is economically necessary. That is not negotiation.

And let me be explicit: after the historic job-security pledge was broken, trust is damaged. You do not restore trust by keeping closures on the table as a disciplinary instrument.

### To institutional investors
Your insistence on “verifiable” and “durable” savings is understandable, but you are still treating the workforce as if the only credible numbers are layoffs and plant shutdowns.

That is false.

Verifiable savings can also come from:
- permanent working-time instruments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and productivity programs,
- management restraint,
- procurement discipline,
- and fair burden-sharing at the top.

What you are not entitled to demand is a package that looks harsh enough for the market while destroying industrial trust and regional stability. A deal that weakens the company’s social foundation is not value creation.

### To the controlling family
You say you want long-term value. Then back a settlement that preserves the industrial base that makes the company valuable in the first place.

I do not accept the idea that “economic equivalence” is only possible through closures or forced redundancies. If you truly want stability and value preservation, you should support:
- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- real investment commitments,
- and a restructuring that shares the burden fairly.

### To the state government
I appreciate that you reject closures and compulsory redundancies. That is the correct line.

But I want to be blunt: we do not need vague political sympathy. We need **binding protection**.  
Anything less leaves the workforce in permanent uncertainty and allows management to keep pressure alive site by site.

You should use your position to insist on:
- open German plants,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a real industrial plan that does not sacrifice the workforce to prove toughness.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a serious restructuring if it includes all of the following:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity reductions without shutting plants**
4. **Voluntary exits only**
5. **Retirement bridges**
6. **Redeployment across sites**
7. **Qualification and upskilling**
8. **Temporary and fair working-time measures**
9. **A negotiated employee contribution only if management and owners also contribute materially**
10. **Binding investment and product commitments for Germany to 2030**
11. **A real role for co-determination in shaping and monitoring implementation**

That is not obstruction. That is a workable path if anyone is serious.

## What we need to see from the others
Before anyone keeps repeating “equivalent economic effect,” put the numbers on the table:

- What exact cost reduction is required?
- What is the contribution expected from management and owners?
- How much can be achieved through procurement, overhead, and capital allocation?
- What productivity gains are assumed?
- What capacity reduction is actually necessary, and where?
- What binding job and site guarantees are offered through 2030?

If you cannot answer those questions transparently, then “closures may be necessary” is just a bargaining threat dressed up as analysis.

## Our red lines
Let me restate them without ambiguity:

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No hollowing out of co-determination**
- **No deal that turns job security into a temporary slogan**
- **No restructuring that leaves workers as the sole shock absorber**

If management and the owners want industrial peace, they need to stop treating worker protections as the problem. The problem is the business model, the cost structure, and the allocation of burden — not the existence of co-determination.

## Bottom line
We are ready for a serious restructuring. We are not ready to trade away the company’s German plants, jobs, and social legitimacy.

If you want a deal, bring:
- a quantified, non-closure package,
- binding guarantees to 2030,
