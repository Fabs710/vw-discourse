We are still at the same core point: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a bargaining ornament; it is the basis on which any settlement can even be discussed.

## Where I agree

I agree with the works council, the state government, and even parts of management and the owners on one important point: **the company needs a real restructuring**. This is not a debate about whether change is necessary. It is. The cost base is too high, the market is shrinking, and the company needs a durable path to 2030.

I also agree that a purely cosmetic solution would be irresponsible. If the restructuring does not deliver real effects, then it simply postpones the next crisis.

And I agree that **everyone must contribute**. The workforce is not saying “someone else pays everything.” We have already signaled openness to a negotiated contribution, working-time adjustments, and capacity measures. That remains true.

## Where I disagree

### To management
I disagree very clearly with the way you are framing this as if **closures are the benchmark of seriousness**. They are not. They are the benchmark of a failed bargaining outcome if you cannot find a fair alternative.

You say voluntary exits, attrition, and working-time tools are not enough. Then bring forward a quantified alternative that does not destroy jobs and sites. Do not demand that the workforce first accept the disappearance of protections and then call that “credibility.”

You also keep speaking about structural savings as if that automatically justifies shifting the burden downward. It does not. If the workforce is supposed to contribute, then management overhead, compensation, investment priorities, and group responsibility must also be part of the package — not just declared in principle.

### To institutional investors
You keep asking for “verifiable” savings, which is understandable. But verifiable does not mean socially destructive. It means measurable. We are prepared to discuss measures that are measurable and durable:
- capacity reduction without closures,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time changes,
- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- management restraint,
- and owner contributions.

What you cannot demand is a solution that is only acceptable if it looks harsh enough on paper. If the result is a stronger margin but a weakened industrial base, you have not solved the problem — you have shifted it.

### To the family holding
I take seriously your focus on long-term value and stability. But long-term value will not be created by hollowing out the workforce’s trust or by treating a no-closure stance as unrealistic by definition.

If you want “competitiveness with stability,” then support the conditions that make it possible:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- real investment commitments in Germany,
- and a fair contribution from ownership and management.

A viable footprint is not a euphemism for closures. If the company’s industrial strength in Germany is sacrificed, then the value story weakens, not strengthens.

### To the state government
I agree with you that closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable as a default path. But I want to be equally clear: **“no closures as the starting point” is not enough**. We need explicit, binding protection for the German sites and for employment. Otherwise uncertainty continues, and the workforce is expected to live under permanent threat.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to negotiate on a serious package if it contains all of the following:

1. **German sites remain open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity adjustments without plant closures**
4. **Voluntary exits, retirement bridges, redeployment, and qualification**
5. **A fair but limited employee contribution**
6. **Management and owner contributions in parallel**
7. **Binding investment and product commitments for Germany**
8. **A durable collective agreement, not a one-off pressure deal**

That is a genuine restructuring path. It is not a refusal of reality. It is a refusal to make employees the only shock absorber.

## What I need answered concretely
Before we talk about “structural necessity,” I want real numbers on the table:

- How much cost reduction is actually required?
- How much can come from management overhead and group-level contribution?
- How much can come from procurement, capital allocation, and investment shifts?
- How much can come from internal measures without forced layoffs?
- What exact capacity reduction is needed, and where?
- What binding job and site guarantees are offered through 2030?

Without those answers, “closures may be necessary” is just a pressure tactic.

## Bottom line
The union is prepared to bargain hard, but not to surrender the core protections that define decent collective bargaining in this company.

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Fair burden-sharing**
- **Binding guarantees**
- **A serious, measurable alternative to layoffs**

If management and the owners want industrial peace, they need to put a real package on the table — one that protects jobs and sites while delivering credible savings. If they do not, then they should not expect the workforce to quietly accept the destruction of its security.
