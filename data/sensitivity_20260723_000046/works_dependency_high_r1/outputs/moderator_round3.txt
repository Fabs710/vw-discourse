## Core impasse

The main deadlock is between two different standards of “acceptable restructuring”:

1. **Employee-side and state-side line:**  
   No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies in Germany, with restructuring achieved through voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, qualification, and shared burden.

2. **Management / investor / family line:**  
   Any solution must deliver **economically equivalent, durable, and enforceable** capacity and cost reduction, and they do not yet accept that a no-closure package can match the financial effect of closures and forced layoffs.

So the impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed — **all sides agree it is**.  
The impasse is over **which instruments are allowed** and **whether a non-closure package can be proven to close the gap**.

A second layer of impasse is **trust**:
- labour and the state do not trust that “equivalence” is being used fairly and not as a pressure tool,
- management and investors do not trust that red lines will still allow enough permanent savings,
- the family wants value protection, while labour wants binding protection for jobs and sites.

---

## Possible compromise positions

### 1) Hybrid restructuring package with conditional capacity adjustment
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Keep the principle of **no compulsory redundancies**, but allow **selective capacity reduction without immediate plant closure**, using:
- internal consolidation of production lines,
- shift reductions,
- product reallocation across sites,
- and phased output reductions at some sites.

This would be paired with:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and management/owner contributions.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Labour and the state preserve the no-forced-layoffs line.  
- Management and investors get real structural change.  
- The family gets a more durable economic adjustment than temporary concessions.

---

### 2) Time-limited no-closure moratorium with review triggers
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Agree to a **binding moratorium on plant closures for a defined period**, for example until a mid-plan review, while implementing a quantified restructuring package now.

At the review point, if agreed savings, margins, and capacity targets are not met, all sides reopen the footprint discussion on the basis of a jointly audited financial model.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Gives workers and the state immediate protection and certainty.  
- Gives management and investors a hard performance test rather than a permanent veto.  
- Reduces the current “closure threat as leverage” problem.

---

### 3) “Equivalence test” with independent verification and escalation ladder
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Create a **jointly mandated independent panel** to test three scenarios:
- closure scenario,
- non-closure scenario,
- hybrid scenario.

If the non-closure plan reaches a predefined threshold of equivalence on:
- fixed-cost reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- labor-cost reduction,
- cash impact,
- and ROS/margin recovery,

then closures are off the table.  
If it does not, parties move to a negotiated escalation ladder, starting with deeper internal measures before any site-level decision.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Satisfies investor and management demands for numbers.  
- Gives works council, union, and state a procedural safeguard against premature closure decisions.  
- Converts the dispute from political assertion into a verified comparison.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**Question:**  
If a jointly verified package shows that some German sites must lose production volume and shifts permanently, but without closures or compulsory redundancies, would you accept that as a lawful and durable form of capacity reduction?

### Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What is the minimum quantified labor-cost and headcount reduction you would accept as “substantial permanent contribution” from labour, and by when would you require it to be delivered?

### Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
What exact financial gap remains after management overhead cuts, procurement savings, capex reprioritization, and owner contributions — in numbers, not descriptors?

### Regional State Government
**Question:**  
If the independent numbers show that no-closure restructuring only works with strict site-by-site capacity reductions and enforceable performance milestones, would you support that compromise even if it means major operational change at some German plants?

### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Would you commit to backing a no-closure settlement if it meets a jointly audited equivalence test on margin, cash, and capacity — even if it requires visible owner-level trade-offs?

### Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
If a non-closure package is independently verified as financially equivalent on a cash and margin basis, would you support it even if it does not include plant closures or compulsory redundancies?

### Independent Industrial Restructuring Finance Advisor
**Question:**  
What specific threshold would you recommend for “economic equivalence” so that all parties can judge whether the non-closure package truly matches the closure scenario?
