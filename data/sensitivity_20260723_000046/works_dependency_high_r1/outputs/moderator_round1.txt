1) **What has been agreed**
- All sides agree the company needs a **serious restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that the current situation is **not sustainable** and that the business must become more competitive.
- Several parties accept that **everyone may need to contribute** in some form, including management/owners and the workforce.
- There is general openness to a **negotiated solution** rather than an open-ended conflict.
- There is some shared interest in **investment, future products, and a credible path to 2030**.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Works council, union, state government, and the family holding all strongly resist closures as the default path; management and investors insist real capacity adjustment is necessary and do not rule out closures if required for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state government reject them; management and investors want meaningful structural change, but have not accepted a no-compulsory-layoff settlement unless equivalent savings are achieved.
- **Scope of restructuring:** Employee side wants job/site protection with negotiated instruments; management and investors argue that voluntary exits and attrition alone are insufficient.
- **Who bears the burden:** Workforce representatives insist the burden must be shared fairly; management and investors emphasize that labour costs must come down materially, while not committing to specific management/owner sacrifices yet.
- **Role of governance/co-determination:** Employee side and state government insist co-determination and regional interests must remain central; management and investors want faster, more decisive restructuring and warn against governance constraints that prevent necessary change.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- **Job/site security vs. measurable cost reduction:** No agreement yet on how to guarantee German sites and employment while still achieving the savings management and investors require.
- **No layoffs vs. real headcount/capacity adjustment:** The parties have not bridged the gap between avoiding compulsory redundancies and delivering sufficient structural downsizing.
- **Alternative instruments vs. equivalence of effect:** There is no shared definition of what level of savings, productivity gains, or capacity reduction would make an alternative to closures acceptable.
- **Short-term industrial peace vs. long-term competitiveness:** The parties differ on how much immediate stability can be preserved without weakening the company’s future economics.
- **Contribution burden:** No agreement yet on the relative contributions of labour, management, owners, and investors, or on what would count as a fair balance.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to a **concrete option set**: what specific combination of measures could achieve the required savings without plant closures or compulsory redundancies.
- Ask each side to specify **minimum acceptable conditions** and **non-negotiables** in operational terms.
- Require a clearer definition of **“equivalent structural savings”** and what metrics would verify them.
- Separate the discussion into two linked tracks:  
  1. **industrial footprint and cost measures**, and  
  2. **employment/security and governance guarantees**.
- Encourage proposals that are **time-limited, measurable, and enforceable**, rather than broad statements of principle.
