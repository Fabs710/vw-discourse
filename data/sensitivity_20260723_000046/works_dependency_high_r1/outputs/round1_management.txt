I’ll respond very clearly.

We do agree on one point: **the company needs a restructuring, and it needs to be credible for the future, not just a short-term patch**. That part is not in dispute.

Where I fundamentally disagree is on the idea that we can solve this crisis **without real structural change to capacity and cost**.

The current German cost base is not competitive enough, the European market is shrinking, and we are losing ground to faster, lower-cost competitors. If we pretend that natural attrition, voluntary exits, and working-time tweaks alone will close the gap, we are not protecting jobs — we are delaying a bigger problem and weakening the entire brand.

So let me be explicit:

- **I do not accept a cosmetic compromise**
- **I do not accept a settlement that leaves overcapacity in place**
- **I do not accept a deal that preserves every site unchanged while the business continues to underperform**
- **I do not accept that the burden falls only on management or owners while labour costs remain structurally above target**

On the red lines you have both stated: I hear the rejection of compulsory redundancies and plant closures. But I must also be clear in return: **if no meaningful structural savings are possible, then the alternative is not safety — it is continued deterioration**. That is not acceptable either.

What I am prepared to discuss is a **durable package** that achieves the necessary effect by a combination of measures, provided it actually delivers:

- a **substantial and permanent labour-cost contribution**
- **capacity reduction** that is real, not symbolic
- **reallocation of production and investment** to make the footprint viable
- a **credible contribution from the workforce**, but also from management and the broader group
- a path that restores the brand’s **return on sales** and frees investment capacity toward 2030

That means I am open to alternatives to closures **only if they produce comparable structural savings**. But I will not sign up to a solution that merely postpones the hard decisions.

On guarantees: I am not in a position to promise blanket job security for all sites irrespective of economics. If there is to be security, it must be earned by a settlement that makes the business competitive. A guarantee with no structural correction is not responsible.

So my message is straightforward:

1. **Yes, we need a negotiated solution**
2. **No, it cannot be a preserve-everything compromise**
3. **Yes, we can discuss alternatives to closures**
4. **But only if they deliver a durable reduction in cost and capacity**
5. **And yes, everyone will have to contribute — including labour**

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced measures, then it needs to engage on the substance: where the capacity comes out, how labour costs come down permanently, and how the company regains competitiveness fast enough to survive.

I’m prepared to negotiate on the instrument. I am **not** prepared to negotiate away the need for structural change.
