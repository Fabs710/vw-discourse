Core impasse, in procedural terms:

## Where the impasse lies
The discussion is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **what counts as an acceptable restructuring instrument**.

More specifically, there are three locked positions:

1. **Management / investors / family** want a package that delivers a **durable structural effect** comparable to closures, with enough permanent cost and capacity reduction to restore competitiveness through 2030.

2. **Works council / union / state** reject **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** as acceptable baselines and want any alternative to preserve every German plant, job security, and co-determination.

3. The **expert** has clarified that equivalence is **not yet proven**, but has not resolved whether a non-closure package can be made equivalent in practice.

So the real deadlock is:
- **economic equivalence demanded by one side**
vs.
- **non-closure / no-forced-layoffs as a hard red line by the other side**.

That makes the debate circular: each side is asking for proof that depends on conditions the other side refuses to accept in advance.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified non-closure package with binding fallback
A structured alternative could be:
- no immediate plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- but a **quantified capacity and cost reduction package** with:
  - shift reduction,
  - voluntary exits,
  - retirement bridges,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time measures,
  - management overhead cuts,
  - owner contribution,
  - and binding investment commitments.

This would be paired with:
- quarterly verification,
- predefined correction steps if targets are missed,
- and a time-limited review point.

Why it may bridge:
- It gives labour-side stakeholders a **non-closure path**.
- It gives management/investors a **measurable structural commitment** rather than a promise.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid footprint adjustment without immediate closure
A second option is a **hybrid**:
- every German plant remains formally open for now,
- but one or more sites undergo **capacity reduction, line consolidation, or partial product transfer**,
- with the state, union, and works council receiving **binding protection terms** for employment and co-determination,
- while management gets a credible permanent cost base reduction.

Why it may bridge:
- It avoids immediate closure language,
- but still delivers more than temporary labor measures,
- and may be closer to the expert’s “economic equivalence” threshold.

---

### Proposal 3: Protected restructuring covenant to 2030
A third compromise structure could be a **covenant-based settlement**:
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- in exchange for:
  - hard productivity targets,
  - voluntary workforce adjustment,
  - management and owner financial contribution,
  - procurement and capex discipline,
  - and a binding investment roadmap.

This would only be valid if:
- the package is financially auditable,
- the savings are enforceable,
- and there is a clearly defined trigger for reopening talks if the company misses external market assumptions.

Why it may bridge:
- It gives the workforce security,
- while giving capital providers a time-bound performance framework.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If closures and compulsory redundancies remain non-negotiable, what **specific permanent capacity and cost reductions** are you prepared to accept that would still meet the company’s restructuring requirement?

### Industrial Union
What is the **quantified labour contribution** you are prepared to support, and how would you ensure it is durable enough to substitute for the economic effect of closures?

### Group & Brand Management
What exact **savings gap** remains after management, overhead, procurement, and capex measures, and what minimum non-closure package would you accept as economically sufficient?

### Regional State Government
What **binding leverage** are you prepared to use to support a non-closure settlement, and under what measurable conditions would you consider the package no longer credible?

### Controlling Family
What minimum combination of **capacity reduction, labour-cost savings, and owner contribution** would you accept as a genuine alternative to closures?

### Institutional Investors
What verified metrics would you require before you would regard a non-closure package as **economically equivalent** to closure-led restructuring?

### Independent Expert
What would be the most defensible **threshold test** for declaring a non-closure package sufficiently equivalent in fixed-cost reduction, capacity impact, cash effect, and durability?
