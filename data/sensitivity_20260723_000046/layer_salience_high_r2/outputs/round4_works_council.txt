I’ll respond directly.

## First: what remains clear
We all agree the company has a serious competitiveness problem and that a temporary patch would be irresponsible. We also agree that conflict is costly. Fine.

But let me be equally clear: **that does not justify German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**. Those are still being presented as if they were the only serious option. They are not.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and the finance team on one point: we need **durable, enforceable, quantified** measures. I also agree that a superficial compromise would fail.

I agree with the state government that this is not just a balance-sheet issue; it is an industrial and regional issue. I also agree with the union that labour contributions must be matched by management, ownership, and capital contributions.

And yes, I agree a hybrid package is often the realistic shape of a settlement in Germany.

But let me draw the line there: **a hybrid that includes closures or forced layoffs is still not acceptable**.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “Closures may be necessary” is still an assertion, not a proof
Management, the family, and investors keep saying the footprint must be reduced. That may be true in abstract. But you have still not shown that this can only be done by closing German plants.

The expert was right to say the key question is the **minimum savings target** and what closure-free measures can deliver. That is exactly the missing piece.

So I ask again:
- What is the exact annual savings target?
- What savings come from working-time changes?
- What from remuneration moderation?
- What from internal reallocation?
- What from overhead cuts?
- What from management and owner contribution?
- And what is the **marginal savings difference** between closure and non-closure options?

Until that is on the table, closures are not “necessary.” They are a management preference.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not unrealistic
It is only unrealistic if management insists on solving the problem by cutting workers first.

We are not refusing contribution. We are refusing to let employees pay for a crisis they did not create by being thrown out of their jobs. That is the point.

There are still real alternatives:
- voluntary departure programmes,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transition,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary remuneration measures,
- and plant-level capacity adjustment without closures.

If management actually wants a durable deal, these are the instruments that must be exhausted first. Not layoffs. Not threats.

### 3) Blanket guarantees are not the problem — broken promises are
We are being asked to accept “flexibility” in exchange for promises that can be reopened later. We’ve seen that movie before.

Our position remains:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site guarantees**
- **job guarantees through 2030**
- **preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**

That is not “rigidity.” That is the minimum for any credible employee contribution.

## To management
You say you need freedom to realign the footprint. I say you need to stop treating the workforce as the default adjustment variable.

If you want labour to contribute, then bring a serious package that includes:
- measurable working-time changes,
- real remuneration restraint,
- overhead reductions,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and investment commitments.

Then we can talk.

But if your “solution” still depends on closing plants and cutting jobs forcibly, then there is no deal. Not now, not later.

And let me remind you: under co-determination, you do **not** have a free hand. For plant closures and relocations, you need the board majority you do not have. So this is not just a moral issue; it is a governance reality.

## To the union
We are aligned on the red lines:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- no dismantling of the collective framework.

I support negotiated flexibility only if it is tied to binding guarantees. But I will not support temporary concessions that buy management time to come back later with the same closure agenda.

So the union should be very careful not to turn “burden-sharing” into a backdoor acceptance of site rationalisation.

## To the state government
Your stance against closures is not enough by itself. Use your leverage properly:
- demand transparent cost calculations,
- demand a closure-free restructuring path,
- demand binding investment,
- demand enforcement through 2030,
- and defend the regional industrial base.

Do not let this become a staged drift into plant closures by another name.

## To the controlling family and investors
You say you want durable value. Then understand this:
- shutting German plants destroys trust,
- triggers industrial conflict,
- weakens skills,
- damages the brand,
- and creates political risk that outlives the savings.

If you believe closures are worth that, prove it with full economics, not slogans.

And if a closure-free package delivers slightly lower gross savings but is more executable and less conflict-ridden, then that may well be the better value outcome. But only if it preserves jobs and sites. That is the key.

## My position, plainly
The works council will support only a package that contains all of the following:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site guarantees**
4. **Job guarantees through 2030**
5. **Real burden-sharing from management and capital**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technology**
7. **Respect for co-determination in implementation**

Anything less is not a compromise. It is an attempt to shift the burden onto labour while keeping the option to dismantle the German footprint later.

## What I need next
If management genuinely wants an agreement, then present:

- the **minimum savings target**
- the **site-by-site cost gap**
- the **savings from closure-free measures**
- the **savings from management and ownership contribution**
- and the **marginal value of closures versus non-closure options**

Without that, we are not negotiating a solution. We are being asked to approve plant closures by stealth.
