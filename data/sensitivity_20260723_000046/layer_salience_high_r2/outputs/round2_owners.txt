I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one fundamental point: **the status quo is not acceptable**. The company has a real structural competitiveness problem, and if we do nothing, value will erode further. That would be a failure of stewardship.

I also agree that **a prolonged conflict would be destructive**. Strikes, public escalation, and governance deadlock would damage the company, the workforce, and the shareholder base alike.

Where I **do not** agree is with any position that turns “no closures” into a blanket answer **without an economically equivalent alternative**.

## To the works council
Your insistence on protecting jobs and sites is understandable, and I respect the role of co-determination. But I cannot support a package that effectively says:

- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- and only limited labour contribution,

if that does **not** close the cost gap in a durable way.

That would simply lock in an uncompetitive structure and shift the problem into the future. From an ownership perspective, that is not protection — it is deferred damage.

If you want to rule out closures, then you need to show a **quantified alternative** that achieves the same savings with the same durability. Not a principle. Not a political statement. Numbers.

## To the union
I appreciate that you are at least discussing burden-sharing, working-time flexibility, and remuneration adjustments. That is constructive.

But I will not pretend that modest, temporary concessions are enough. If the company’s fixed-cost base remains structurally too high, then the package fails. We need a solution that is:

- durable,
- enforceable,
- and large enough to restore margins.

If the only answer is “employees contribute a bit, but every site stays open and every job is protected,” then the economics do not work.

## To management
You are right that the cost base must come down materially. But I want to be equally clear: if you present closures as the only serious option without exhausting a credible closure-free alternative, you will meet resistance — and deservedly so.

The company needs a restructuring that is not only forceful, but also **stable**. If the final package triggers ongoing resistance or destroys trust, then the savings will not hold. I want management to be sharper on the minimum economics required, and more disciplined on what is truly non-negotiable.

## To the state government
I understand your political responsibility and your concern for the regional industrial base. But preserving the industrial base requires a company that can compete. If we preserve every site regardless of economics, we may end up preserving decline.

So I support your insistence on negotiation and burden-sharing. I do **not** support turning that into an implied veto over any real restructuring.

## On what would move me
I could support a closure-free restructuring **if** it contains all of the following:

1. **Clear quantified savings** that materially restore competitiveness  
2. **Durable capacity reduction** through non-destructive means  
3. **Real labour-cost relief** beyond symbolism  
4. **Enforceable implementation and timing**  
5. **A credible investment plan for future products and technologies**  
6. **No governance instability or uncontrolled escalation**

If that package can be shown, I am open to it. If not, then I will not pretend that sentiment can substitute for economics.

## My bottom line
I am **not** here to defend plant closures as an ideology.  
I am **not** here to protect management from hard questions.  
But I am also **not** prepared to back a settlement that preserves all current guarantees while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

So the next step is not another statement of red lines.  
It is a **quantified, enforceable proposal** that closes the gap.

If the employee side wants to rule out closures, bring the numbers. If management wants closures, prove they are unavoidable. Until then, I do not support any premature conclusion.
