### Core impasse
The impasse now appears to be **not whether restructuring is needed**, but **what kind of restructuring can be accepted as both economically sufficient and governance-feasible**.

Specifically, the conflict has crystallized around four unresolved questions:

1. **Are German plant closures actually necessary, or merely the preferred management/owner solution?**  
2. **Can a closure-free package produce enough durable savings through 2030?**  
3. **Are compulsory redundancies and blanket site closures off the table, or only negotiable later?**  
4. **Who bears the burden of proving the economics, and on what board-ready model?**

At present, the parties are stuck because:
- labor and state actors are asking for proof that closures are unavoidable,
- management and capital are asking for proof that they are avoidable,
- and no shared site-by-site model has yet been accepted as the common factual basis.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible compromise paths

#### Proposal 1: “Closure-last” sequencing with verified trigger points
A compromise could be structured so that **closures are not ruled out in principle, but are contractually postponed until a staged sequence of non-closure measures has been fully implemented and independently reviewed**.

Elements:
- immediate labor contribution package;
- management and owner contribution package;
- overhead and procurement reductions;
- voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time measures;
- independent review after a fixed period;
- closures only permitted if pre-defined savings thresholds are still missed.

This may bridge:
- **works council / union / state government** concerns about premature closures,
- while giving **management / family / investors** a conditional path to footprint reduction if targets are not met.

---

#### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with explicit “site protection core” and limited rationalization perimeter
A second compromise could divide sites into:
- a **protected core** of strategically critical plants with binding guarantees through 2030, and
- a **rationalization perimeter** where limited adjustments, line consolidation, or partial capacity reductions can occur.

Elements:
- no compulsory redundancies in the protected core;
- no blanket closures;
- voluntary and negotiated workforce adjustment tools;
- targeted restructuring only at sites shown by the model to have the worst economics;
- binding investment commitments for retained sites.

This may bridge:
- **employee-side red lines** on broad job and site security,
- with **management/capital** demands for actual structural savings.

---

#### Proposal 3: Independent model first, decision second
A procedural compromise could require that **all sides commit in advance to one shared independent site-by-site model**, and that no final position on closures or guarantees is taken until that model is complete.

Elements:
- one agreed data room;
- one independent expert team;
- one scenario set: closure-free, hybrid, closure-based;
- agreed metrics: annual savings, one-time charges, implementation risk, durability through 2030, and job/site impact;
- then a second negotiation round based only on the model.

This may bridge:
- the current trust gap,
- and reduce accusations of “closure by stealth” or “refusal to confront economics.”

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
**If the independent model shows that a closure-free package closes only, say, 60–70% of the required savings gap, what additional concessions would you consider acceptable without crossing your red lines?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**What is the maximum duration and scale of labor-cost relief you would support if it is tied to enforceable job and investment guarantees through 2030?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target and margin outcome would make you accept a closure-free or low-closure alternative as economically sufficient?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**Would you support a time-limited, conditional restructuring package if it legally binds the company to investment, job protection, and transparent review milestones, even without an absolute no-closure guarantee?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you be willing to accept a hybrid package that preserves strategic German sites but reduces footprint selectively, if the independent model shows that this maximizes long-term value and lowers execution risk?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**Would you back a lower gross savings package if it is demonstrably more executable, more durable, and has a better risk-adjusted NPV than a closure-heavy plan?**

#### To the Expert
**What minimum data threshold would you require before concluding that closures are genuinely unavoidable rather than merely optimal?**
