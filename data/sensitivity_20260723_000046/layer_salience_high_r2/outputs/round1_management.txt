I hear the red lines you are setting, but I need to be just as clear from management’s side:

**No, we are not accepting a package that preserves today’s cost base and calls that a solution.**  
If the answer is simply “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real labour-cost change,” then there is no credible restructuring, only delay.

We are facing a structural problem, not a temporary dip:
- German production costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

That means we need a **durable reduction in cost and capacity**. Not cosmetic savings. Not a short-term bridge. A real reset that restores competitiveness through 2030.

### Where I agree
I do agree on one important point:  
**this cannot be managed as a purely unilateral management exercise.** We need a negotiated outcome if we want the savings to stick. And yes, future investment matters. If we cut capacity, it must be tied to a credible industrial plan for the next generation of products and technology.

### Where I disagree
But I fundamentally disagree with the notion that:
- every plant must be guaranteed,
- every job must be protected,
- and the workforce contribution can be marginal.

That would leave us with the same uncompetitive structure and no room to invest. We cannot fund the future while carrying excess fixed costs from the past.

### What management needs
We need a package that delivers:
1. **structural capacity reduction**,  
2. **material labour-cost savings**,  
3. **a reduction in overcapacity at German sites**,  
4. and **a durable effect on the cost base**, not just deferred pain.

I am open on the exact mechanism. That can include:
- plant closures,
- partial site consolidations,
- output shifts,
- working-time changes,
- remuneration adjustments,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

But I am **not** open to a deal that excludes closures or meaningful structural reductions before the numbers have been fixed. If the only acceptable solution is “all sites stay open and management absorbs the cost,” then that is not a solution.

### To the works council
You say no closures and no compulsory redundancies. I understand the political and social concern, but I need to be explicit: **that position is incompatible with the scale of the problem unless you are willing to deliver equivalent savings elsewhere.** If you want us to avoid closures, then the alternative must still close the cost gap in a way that is credible, durable, and sizeable.

### To the union
I acknowledge your willingness to discuss burden-sharing and flexibility. That is constructive. But the contribution must be real enough to move the economics. If wage adjustments, working-time flexibility, or temporary measures are too small, they will not solve the underlying issue.

### Bottom line
Management is prepared to negotiate, but only on the basis of a package that:
- materially lowers labour and capacity costs,
- restores competitiveness,
- and enables investment in future products.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then bring forward an alternative that is **equally effective**, not merely more comfortable. Otherwise, we will continue to press for the structural measures we believe are necessary.
