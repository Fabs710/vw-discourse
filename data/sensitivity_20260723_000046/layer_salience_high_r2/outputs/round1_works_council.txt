As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

Management cannot solve a strategic problem by throwing thousands of workers and entire sites overboard, especially after years of asking the workforce to deliver productivity, flexibility, and the transition to electric mobility. We are not prepared to sign off on a restructuring that destroys jobs, breaks the historic job-security framework, and weakens co-determination.

What we will support is a serious alternative that protects the German industrial base:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- a negotiated solution on capacity adjustments, working time, and internal flexibility,
- a fair contribution from labour only if jobs and sites are secured,
- and a credible commitment to future investment, new models, and technology at the German locations.

If management wants savings, it must find them through cooperation, efficiency, and a fair burden-sharing arrangement — not through closures and unilateral cuts. We are ready to negotiate, but only on the basis that the plants stay open and the workforce is protected.

If those core conditions are not met, we will resist with full force, including industrial action and using every co-determination right available to us.
