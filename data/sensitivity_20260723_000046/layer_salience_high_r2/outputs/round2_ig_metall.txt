I will answer directly.

### To management
You are still arguing as if **closures and compulsory redundancies are the default solution**, and I do **not** accept that framing.

I do accept that the company has a structural competitiveness problem. I accept that a durable solution must reduce costs and capacity. I also accept that a negotiated settlement has to be economically real, not symbolic.

But what I do **not** accept is:
- making employees pay for the whole restructuring,
- using the threat of plant closures to force wage concessions,
- dismantling the collective wage framework as if that were just an efficiency measure,
- or presenting forced layoffs as inevitable before all alternatives have been exhausted.

If you want a deal, then show a **quantified closure-free alternative**. Not just principles — numbers and mechanisms.

### What a serious alternative must contain
From the union side, a viable package can include:
- a negotiated labour-cost contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary or limited wage-component adjustments,
- capacity reduction through internal measures,
- reallocation of production,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- and possibly forgoing certain bonus or special payments.

But that is only acceptable if it is paired with:
1. **no compulsory redundancies**,  
2. **no German plant closures**,  
3. **binding site security**,  
4. **a durable investment commitment**, and  
5. **a fair contribution from management and owners**.

If management insists that the only “real” savings are achieved by closing plants and cutting jobs, then you are not negotiating — you are demanding surrender.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines: no forced layoffs, no closures, no unilateral destruction of job security.

Where I want to be careful is this: I support a hard stance, but we should keep the door open for a **socially balanced restructuring package** if it genuinely protects jobs and sites. The point is not to preserve every current cost element at any price; the point is to protect the workforce from bearing all the pain.

### To the state government
Your position is important, and I welcome your rejection of closures as the starting point. But support for a closure-free solution must be matched by pressure on management and owners to contribute materially. “No closures” alone is not enough if the burden simply shifts to wages and working conditions.

### To the controlling family and investors
You say you are not ideologically attached to closures. Good. Then prove it by backing a package that solves the economics **without** making workers absorb the losses alone.

From our perspective, fairness means:
- employees contribute,
- management contributes,
- capital contributes,
- and the company still protects jobs and future industrial capacity.

If capital is only willing to support restructuring when the workforce takes the deepest cuts, then that is not a balanced solution. That is cost shifting.

### Our bottom line
The union is prepared to negotiate a durable, quantified burden-sharing package. We are **not** prepared to accept:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures,
- or the breakup of the collective wage framework.

If a closure-free plan with real savings is not put on the table, then the conflict will continue — and we will use the means available to us to defend the workforce.

If management wants to avoid escalation, then bring the numbers for a fair settlement.
