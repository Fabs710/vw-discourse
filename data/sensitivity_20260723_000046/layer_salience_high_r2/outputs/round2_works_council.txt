Management, I will answer you directly: **a restructuring that relies on plant closures and compulsory redundancies is not acceptable**. Not as a starting point, not as a bargaining chip, and not as a hidden threat to force acceptance of wage concessions.

You keep saying “numbers,” “durable savings,” and “competitiveness.” We are not ignoring the numbers. But you are still refusing the central truth: **there are alternatives to destroying sites and livelihoods**. If you want a serious negotiation, then present a concrete closure-free plan with quantified savings — not just the assertion that closures are “necessary.”

### Where I agree
I agree with the union, the state government, and even parts of what the family and investors have said:
- the company needs a **durable** solution,
- the answer cannot be a temporary patch,
- and prolonged industrial conflict would damage value.

I also agree that burden-sharing must be real. But burden-sharing means **all sides** contribute, not that the workforce is forced to pay with jobs while management and owners frame it as inevitability.

### Where I disagree
I fundamentally disagree with the notion that:
- all sites must be “reassessed” through the lens of closure,
- forced redundancies are somehow a normal tool,
- or job guarantees can be treated as negotiable collateral.

That is exactly the line we will not cross. The historic job-security framework was not a decoration; it was a core part of industrial peace and trust. Management’s move against it is why trust is so low now.

### To management
If you want our support, stop talking in generalities and put numbers on the table for a **closure-free restructuring**:
- How much capacity reduction is needed?
- What savings come from working-time adjustments?
- What savings come from temporary remuneration measures?
- What savings come from internal reallocation, training, and product allocation?
- What contribution are executives and shareholders making?

If you cannot show that the gap can only be closed by shutting German plants, then your closure agenda is a choice, not a necessity.

### To the union
We are aligned on the red lines:
- no forced layoffs,
- no German plant closures,
- no dismantling of co-determination and job security.

Your willingness to negotiate flexibility is useful only if it is tied to firm guarantees. I support discussing:
- working-time changes,
- internal mobility,
- temporary sacrifice,
- training and qualification,
- and capacity adjustments that do not involve closure.

But those discussions must be anchored by **binding site guarantees and job guarantees through 2030**. Without that, employees are being asked to surrender security for promises that can be reversed later.

### To the state government
You are right to reject closures as the default answer. But that position must now translate into concrete governance pressure:
- insist on a closure-free plan,
- insist on binding commitments,
- and use your influence to prevent a fait accompli.

This company is not just a balance sheet. It is a regional industrial base and a social compact. That must be reflected in the decision.

### To the controlling family and investors
You say you care about enterprise value and durable savings. Then understand this: **forced closures and mass layoffs destroy value too**. They destroy know-how, weaken the workforce, trigger strikes, and damage the brand.

If you want a credible turnaround, support a package that preserves capacity and protects labour peace. If you push closures against the employee side’s red lines, you should expect resistance on the supervisory board and in the plants. We are not passive observers here; co-determination gives us real power, and we will use it.

### Our position
We will support only a package that includes:
1. **binding site guarantees for all German plants,**
2. **job guarantees through 2030,**
3. **no compulsory redundancies,**
4. **capacity adjustments through non-destructive means,**
5. **real burden-sharing from management and capital,**
6. **investment commitments for future products and technology,**
7. **and respect for co-determination in shaping the restructuring.**

Anything else is not a negotiated solution; it is an attempted imposition.

So my message is simple: **bring a closure-free, no-layoff package with real numbers, or do not expect the works council to accept your restructuring.**
