As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will be very clear: **I do not accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point, and I will not support a restructuring that destroys regional jobs or sidelines co-determination.**

I do not deny the cost problem. I do not deny that the company needs a durable competitiveness reset. But management is still treating closures and compulsory redundancies as the natural answer, and I reject that framing.

## To management
You keep saying “equivalent economics” and “structural reduction.” That is not enough by itself.  
If you want support from us, you must show a **closure-free restructuring path** that is:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- durable,
- and compatible with the German industrial base.

If you cannot prove that closures are unavoidable, then they are a choice — and I will not back that choice.

What I would need to see from you is:
1. the **minimum savings target** you believe is required,
2. which parts of that target are actually non-negotiable,
3. what savings can come from working-time changes, internal reallocation, and temporary remuneration measures,
4. what management and owners are contributing themselves,
5. and how you will secure future investment in German plants.

Do not ask the state to bless a fait accompli.

## To the works council
Your red line against closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable, and on the core point I am with you: **German plants are not disposable assets**.

But I also need you to engage on a package that is not just declarative.  
If you want binding site and job guarantees through 2030, then I need to see a credible operational plan that closes the cost gap without bankrupting the future. That means real numbers on:
- capacity reduction without closure,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a fair employee contribution if jobs and sites are secured.

I support a strong social-partnership solution. I do **not** support a solution where workers are asked to give up security while management keeps the right to later reopen the same threats.

## To the union
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss burden-sharing, but I want to be explicit: **I will only support labour contributions if they are matched by comparable contributions from management and capital.**

A restructuring that means:
- workers take wage and security hits,
- while owners merely demand “competitiveness,”

is not fair and is not politically sustainable.

If there is to be a negotiated adjustment, it must include:
- no forced layoffs,
- no German plant closures,
- binding employment protection,
- and a real contribution from executives and shareholders.

## To the controlling family and investors
You are right that cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. But you are wrong if you think the only serious option is to pressure the workforce until it accepts closures.

That is not value creation; that is social and political risk transfer.

You speak of enterprise value. Then understand this: **prolonged conflict, loss of trust, and damaged regional employment also destroy value**. A company like this cannot be managed as if the plants were interchangeable cost blocks with no regional consequences.

If you want a stable turnaround, support a package that preserves viable German capacity and secures industrial peace.

## My position
I can support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:

1. **No plant closures in Germany as the default or starting solution**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding protection for jobs and sites**
4. **Real burden-sharing from management, owners, and labour**
5. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies**
6. **Respect for co-determination and the state’s veto role**

If those conditions are not met, then I will not call it a balanced restructuring. I will call it an attempt to force the adjustment onto workers and the region.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to broker and back a serious negotiated solution.  
I am **not** prepared to endorse closures and mass layoffs as the price of competitiveness.

Bring forward a closure-free, quantified package that protects the company’s long-term health **and** the regional industrial base — or do not expect my support.
