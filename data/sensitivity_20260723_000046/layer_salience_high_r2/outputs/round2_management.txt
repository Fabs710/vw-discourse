I’ll be equally direct.

I hear the employee side repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding job guarantees, no change to the cost base.” That is **not** a restructuring. That is an attempt to freeze an uncompetitive structure and shift the adjustment problem into the future.

We are **not** in a normal bargaining round. We are in a structural crisis:
- German production costs are too high,
- capacity is too high for the market we now have,
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is still not where it needs to be on return on sales.

If we do not make a **durable** correction now, we will bleed margin, lose investment headroom, and weaken the company further into 2030.

## Where I agree

I agree with all parties on one point:  
**A purely imposed solution would be unstable and destructive.**

I also agree that:
- prolonged strikes would damage output and reputation,
- the company needs future investment commitments,
- and burden-sharing must be part of the package.

And I will also acknowledge this: a solution that only cuts jobs without a plan for future products and industrial utilization would be short-sighted. We do need a credible industrial path beyond the restructuring.

## Where I disagree

### 1) “No plant closures” as a hard line
I do **not** accept that line.

If the numbers show permanent overcapacity, then some sites, lines, or functions may need to be reduced, consolidated, or closed. If the employee side wants to rule that out entirely, then you must show an **equivalent** alternative that is:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- durable,
- and large enough to close the cost gap.

So far, what I’m hearing is not an equivalent alternative. I’m hearing a political refusal.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as an absolute
I understand why you state it. But I will not sign up to a package that guarantees every job regardless of economic reality while expecting management to absorb the entire burden.

If the alternative measures do not add up to the required savings, then we cannot rule out employment adjustments. I prefer negotiated solutions, but I am not going to pretend the company can keep every current guarantee and still become competitive.

### 3) “All sites guaranteed through 2030”
That is incompatible with serious restructuring unless you can show that all sites remain viable under the new economics. I am not prepared to grant blanket guarantees that eliminate management’s ability to realign production.

## On the closure-free alternative

The works council and union keep asking for a quantified closure-free package. Fair enough: I will respond in the same terms.

A closure-free solution would only be credible if it delivers:
1. **material capacity reduction**
2. **durable labour-cost relief**
3. **lower fixed overhead**
4. **flexibility in working time and remuneration**
5. **enforceable implementation**
6. **a return to target margins**

If you propose internal mobility, working-time changes, temporary remuneration adjustments, and reallocation, those may help — but unless the savings are large and permanent enough, they do not solve the problem.

Let me be explicit:  
**Temporary sacrifices are not enough. Deferred pain is not a turnaround.**

## To the works council

You say closures are a choice, not a necessity. That may be true in the abstract. But in the actual economics of this company, choice is constrained by market reality.

You also say management should put the numbers on the table. We have: the cost gap is real, and it is not small. What I need from you is not another declaration of principle, but a plan that closes that gap **without** just moving costs around.

If you want site guarantees through 2030, then tell us:
- how much cost you will take out,
- from where,
- on what timeline,
- and how it is made durable.

Without that, the guarantee becomes a promise to preserve an unaffordable structure.

## To the union

You say you are willing to discuss burden-sharing and flexibility. That is the right direction. But the package must be serious.

I am prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- adjustments to remuneration elements,
- internal reallocation,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- capacity reductions without unnecessary disruption,
- and a managed transition path.

What I am **not** prepared to do is accept a package where the workforce makes symbolic concessions while the company’s cost base remains structurally above target.

If you want a closure-free settlement, it must be economically equivalent to what closures would deliver. Not “some savings.” Not “a fair feeling.” **Equivalent economics.**

## To the regional state government

I appreciate your concern for the industrial base. But preserving the industrial base requires a company that can compete.

If we force a solution that leaves the cost structure intact, we are not protecting jobs — we are postponing a larger problem. Your role should be to support a viable restructuring, not to freeze the current footprint regardless of economics.

## To the controlling family and investors

You are right that cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. And you are right that value destruction from conflict matters. But I need clarity: if you all agree the problem is structural, then we cannot pretend that every plant and every guarantee is sacrosanct.

The ownership side must not hide behind general calls for competitiveness while refusing the hard choices that make competitiveness possible.

## My line

Management is open to negotiation, but only for a package that produces:
- a **structural reduction in cost and capacity**,
- a **real labour-cost contribution**,
- **durable margin improvement**,
- and **investment capacity for future products and technology**.

If the employee side can bring forward a closure-free package that truly closes the economics, I will engage seriously.  
If not, then I will continue to insist on structural measures including plant rationalization and workforce reduction.

That is the reality.  
We are not here to preserve the status quo.  
We are here to restore competitiveness.
