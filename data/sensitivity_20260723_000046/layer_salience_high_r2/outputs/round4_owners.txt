I’ll be direct as well.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the institutional investors on the main diagnosis: **the company has a structural competitiveness problem**, and a **temporary patch would just postpone the damage**. I also agree with the union, works council, and state government that a **prolonged strike or governance deadlock would destroy value**. That would be bad for the company, the workforce, and the ownership base.

I also agree that we need this to be **durable**. A one-year truce that leaves the cost base fundamentally unchanged is not a restructuring.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees through 2030” is automatically a viable restructuring plan. That is a demand, not yet a solution.

But I also do **not** accept management’s implied claim that closures are already proven unavoidable. That has still **not** been demonstrated in a board-ready, site-by-site way.

So I am not endorsing either extreme.

## My view on the current debate
What I take from the expert and finance input is sensible:
- **closures** are the cleanest way to remove fixed cost if overcapacity is real;
- **closure-free measures** can contribute materially, but often only partially;
- a **hybrid** is often the most realistic outcome in German co-determination.

That is the right framework.

But the crucial point is this:  
**I will not support a settlement that preserves every site, blocks every workforce adjustment, and still leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.**  
That would be value destruction by another name.

## To the works council
I respect the insistence on protecting jobs and sites. But you are still asking us to accept your red lines before you have shown the economics.

If you want a no-closure path, then bring a **board-ready, site-by-site package** that demonstrates:
- the minimum annual savings target,
- the savings from working-time changes,
- remuneration moderation,
- internal reallocation,
- overhead cuts,
- voluntary exits,
- and management/ownership contribution,
- plus the marginal difference versus closures.

Without that, “no closures” is not a restructuring plan. It is a refusal to confront the scale of the problem.

And to be clear: if your position is that **every German plant must remain open regardless of economics**, then I cannot support that. That would risk permanent impairment of the enterprise.

## To the union
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss flexibility, temporary remuneration relief, redeployment, and qualification. That is constructive.

But temporary concessions are only useful if they are **large enough and durable enough**. If the fixed-cost base stays too high, then we are just buying time.

I am open to a closure-free or low-closure package **if** it is economically equivalent in substance and enforceable through 2030. I am not open to a package that depends on recurring re-negotiation and permanent uncertainty.

## To management
You are right that the status quo is not acceptable. But do not overstate inevitability.

If closures are necessary, prove why. If closure-free measures can close the gap, accept them. Right now I do **not** see a fully quantified comparison that proves which mix is required.

What I want is simple:
- minimum savings target,
- site-by-site gap,
- savings from closure-free measures,
- marginal savings from closures,
- restructuring charges,
- and margin impact through 2030.

Without that, we are still in a positional fight, not a decision.

## To the state government
I understand your concern for the regional industrial base. But preserving the industrial base requires a company that can compete over time. If the economics are not fixed, the region will not be protected in the long run.

So I support transparent numbers and serious burden-sharing. But I cannot support a stance that effectively rules out the tools needed to restore competitiveness.

## My ownership position
I am prepared to support a restructuring only if it delivers:
- durable margin improvement,
- real cost reduction,
- credible future investment,
- and industrial peace.

That can come from:
- a closure-light or hybrid package, or
- if truly unavoidable, selective footprint reduction.

What I will **not** support is:
- an all-closure-free settlement that fails the economics,
- or a closure-first rush without proving there is no equivalent alternative.

## What I need next
I want the **site-by-site and measure-by-measure model**. Specifically:
1. minimum annual savings required  
2. what closure-free measures can actually deliver  
3. what management and ownership contribute  
4. what the marginal savings from closures really are  
5. what package is durable enough to hold through 2030  

Until I see that, I will not endorse either side’s preferred extreme.

## Bottom line
My position is:
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No permanent impairment of enterprise value**
- **No governance deadlock**
- **No blanket acceptance of closures without proof**
- **No blanket refusal of closures if the economics require them**

Bring a package that genuinely restores competitiveness and preserves long-term value. That is the standard I will judge this by.
