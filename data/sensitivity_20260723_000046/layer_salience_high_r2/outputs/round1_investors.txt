From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the only question that matters is whether this package can credibly restore **risk-adjusted value** through **competitive margins**, **durable cost reduction**, and **deal certainty**.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, and partly with the controlling family, on one point: **the current cost base is not sustainable**. If German operations remain structurally above competitors, then shareholder value will continue to erode. We do not support cosmetic measures, temporary bridges, or a settlement that simply delays the same margin pressure by 12–24 months.

I also agree with the works council, union, and state government that a prolonged strike or a chaotic imposed restructuring would be destructive. Value leakage from industrial conflict, lost production, and reputational damage is real. We are not interested in a “victory” that leaves the company weaker.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept an outcome that preserves the current employment and site structure while calling that a restructuring. If the package leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired, we will not view it as credible.

That means:
- **No acceptance of a purely political compromise**
- **No settlement that lacks measurable, durable savings**
- **No promise structure that protects everything except shareholder returns**
- **No drift into delay while margins keep slipping**

The works council’s red line of no closures and no compulsory redundancies may be understandable, but it is **not** enough on its own. If that position means the company cannot reach competitive economics, then it is not investable.

## What would move us
We are open to an alternative to plant closures **only if** it is financially equivalent and enforceable. Specifically, we would need to see:

1. **A quantified reduction in fixed costs and capacity**
2. **A credible path back to target return-on-sales**
3. **Durable labour-cost relief, not temporary optics**
4. **Clear timing and enforcement**
5. **Evidence that the company can invest in future products without being trapped by legacy costs**

If the employee side proposes capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, remuneration changes, and internal reallocation, that can be acceptable **only if the numbers truly close the gap**. “Some sacrifice” is not enough. We need a package that visibly resets the economics.

## Our position on closures
We are **not ideologically attached** to closures. But we are also not prepared to reject them if they are the only credible way to restore competitiveness. The burden is on management and the other stakeholders to demonstrate that any closure-free alternative is at least as effective, as durable, and as enforceable.

## Bottom line
Our support is conditional and outcome-based:

- **We support deep, durable restructuring**
- **We do not support a cosmetic settlement**
- **We do not support structural underperformance**
- **We do not support prolonged conflict that destroys value**
- **We will back whichever path credibly restores margins and competitiveness**

So to all parties: bring forward a package that is financially real, not politically comfortable. If you cannot do that, then you should not expect capital markets to reward the outcome.
