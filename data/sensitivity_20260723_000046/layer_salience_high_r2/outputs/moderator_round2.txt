### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **structural competitiveness and cost problem**.
- All parties agree that a **durable** solution is needed, not a temporary patch.
- All parties recognize that a **prolonged conflict/strike would damage value** and should be avoided.
- There is broad openness to some form of **burden-sharing**, including working-time flexibility, remuneration adjustments, internal mobility, qualification measures, and investment commitments.
- There is general agreement that any solution should also protect the company’s **future industrial capacity** and not be purely short-term.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Employee-side and state-side speakers reject closures as the starting point; management, family, and investors say closures may be necessary if the economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  The employee side rejects them categorically; management and capital will not rule out employment reductions if needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:**  
  The employee side wants binding guarantees; management and capital say they cannot accept blanket guarantees without proof that they are economically sustainable.
- **What counts as a credible alternative:**  
  Employee-side actors demand a quantified closure-free plan; management and capital say such a plan must match the savings and durability of closure-based restructuring.
- **Distribution of burden:**  
  Employees insist management, owners, and executives must materially contribute; management and capital argue employee concessions alone are insufficient if structural overcapacity remains.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How large the cost gap actually is** and what minimum savings are required.
- **Whether closure-free measures can close that gap durably** or only partially.
- The trade-off between **higher immediate disruption** from closures and **lower but less certain savings** from non-closure measures.
- The balance between **employment protection** and **management flexibility to realign capacity**.
- The extent to which **management, ownership, and labor** each contribute in a way that is both politically and financially credible.
- The question of whether the likely outcome is **pure closure-based, pure closure-free, or a hybrid restructuring**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **site-by-site and measure-by-measure numbers**.
- Clarify the **minimum savings target**, the **cost gap by plant/function**, and which measures are **durable vs temporary**.
- Ask each side to specify **what they will concretely concede** and **what they require in return**.
- Focus discussion on whether a **hybrid package** could meet both competitiveness and job-security concerns.
- Keep the next round centered on **quantified trade-offs, implementation durability, and enforceability** rather than further restating red lines.
