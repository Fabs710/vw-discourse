**Facilitator assessment: core impasse**

The central deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed; it is over **the form of restructuring and the burden allocation**.

More specifically, the impasse sits on three linked points:

1. **Management / owners / investors** want structural cost reduction with the option of footprint reduction, including closures if needed.
2. **Employee-side stakeholders** accept the need for durable savings but reject closures and compulsory redundancies, and want binding guarantees.
3. **No side has yet produced a fully quantified, site-by-site package** showing that its preferred path closes the economic gap with acceptable durability and implementation risk.

So the dispute is now less about diagnosis and more about:
- **how much savings are required,**
- **which measures are durable,**
- **who carries the burden,**
- and **whether a hybrid package can satisfy both economics and governance constraints.**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified hybrid package with conditional site security
**Frame:** Keep closures off the table initially, but make job and site guarantees conditional on meeting a verified savings target.

**Core elements**
- A site-by-site savings model is agreed within a short deadline.
- Employee-side flexibility measures are implemented first:
  - working-time adjustments
  - remuneration moderation
  - internal redeployment
  - qualification and voluntary exits
- Management and capital contribute:
  - executive restraint
  - reduced payout expectations
  - overhead cuts
  - investment commitments
- **Closure option remains suspended** for a defined period, but only if the quantified savings target is met and maintained.

**Why it may bridge**
- Gives labor a real no-closure pathway.
- Gives management a measurable test of whether alternatives work.
- Avoids an immediate binary fight over closures.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with limited footprint adjustment plus binding protections
**Frame:** Accept that some footprint reduction may be necessary, but limit it to a narrowly defined, jointly reviewed scope.

**Core elements**
- One or more limited plant/line rationalizations are considered only after full exhaustion of non-closure measures.
- Any workforce reduction is through:
  - voluntary departures,
  - attrition,
  - redeployment,
  - and qualification-led transition,
  not compulsory redundancies.
- In exchange, labor receives:
  - binding site-security commitments for the remaining plants
  - a durable employment framework through 2030
  - concrete investment and product commitments.

**Why it may bridge**
- Provides management with structural savings.
- Protects the employee-side red line against forced layoffs.
- Creates a compromise path if a purely closure-free package falls short.

---

### Proposal 3: Two-stage agreement with review gate and enforcement triggers
**Frame:** Separate immediate stabilization from final footprint decisions.

**Core elements**
- Stage 1: all sides sign a durable interim package:
  - working-time changes
  - cost concessions
  - management contribution
  - investment commitments
  - industrial peace
- Stage 2: an independent review after a defined period assesses whether the company has met the savings target.
- Only if the target is missed does the parties reopen the question of stronger structural measures.

**Why it may bridge**
- Reduces immediate conflict.
- Creates a structured proof mechanism.
- Avoids irreversible decisions before the numbers are agreed.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) To Management
**What exact savings target must be met site by site, and what share of that target can you accept being delivered through non-closure measures before you consider closures unavoidable?**

### 2) To the Works Council
**If closures remain off the table, what quantified and enforceable savings package will you accept that is durable through 2030 and credibly closes the gap?**

### 3) To the Union
**Which combination of wage, working-time, and flexibility measures would you formally support, and what minimum guarantees must accompany them for you to endorse the deal?**

### 4) To the Regional State Government
**What concrete leverage will you use to require transparent numbers and a closure-free or hybrid solution, and what commitments are you prepared to demand from the company in return for political support?**

### 5) To the Controlling Family
**Will you commit to backing a hybrid or closure-free package if it is independently shown to close the economic gap, even if it falls short of your preferred footprint reduction?**

### 6) To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a settlement that delivers slightly lower gross savings than closures but offers higher execution certainty and lower conflict risk, if the risk-adjusted value outcome is better?**

### 7) To the Expert / Finance Team
**Can you produce a single comparative model showing closure-free, hybrid, and closure-based options side by side, with annual savings, restructuring charges, durability, and implementation risk through 2030?**

---

## Facilitator next step
To move the discourse beyond repetition, the next round should require each stakeholder to answer one of two things:

- **either** the minimum acceptable terms for a hybrid package,
- **or** the specific data that would change their position.

That will convert the debate from positional repetition into a testable negotiation.
