### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a solution should be **negotiated rather than imposed** if possible.
- All sides accept that the package must include some form of **burden-sharing** and **future investment commitment**.
- There is shared concern that **prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Management, the controlling family, and investors see closures as potentially necessary; the works council, union, and state government reject them as the starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee representatives reject them categorically; management and owners say they may be needed if equivalent savings are not found elsewhere.
- **Extent of labor contribution:**  
  All accept some employee contribution in principle, but disagree sharply on how large and durable it must be.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees:**  
  Works council and union want binding guarantees; management and capital say full guarantees may block a viable restructuring.
- **Definition of a “credible” solution:**  
  Management and investors require measurable, durable cost reductions; employee and political stakeholders require those reductions to be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to balance **cost reduction** against **job security and site preservation**.
- Whether the needed savings can be achieved through **working-time flexibility, remuneration changes, capacity reductions, or internal reallocation** without closures.
- What level of **management, ownership, and capital contribution** is sufficient to make burden-sharing credible.
- How to secure **future investment and product commitments** while reducing current capacity and costs.
- Whether there is a package that is both **financially durable** and **socially/ քաղաքականically acceptable**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from stated red lines to **specific packages and quantified alternatives**. In particular:
- Management should present the **minimum savings and capacity reduction required**, and what parts are negotiable.
- Works council and union should present a **closure-free alternative** with estimated savings and implementation tools.
- State government and shareholders should clarify **what they would regard as sufficient burden-sharing** and which outcomes they can support.
- The next round should focus on **numbers, mechanisms, and enforceability**, not just principles.
