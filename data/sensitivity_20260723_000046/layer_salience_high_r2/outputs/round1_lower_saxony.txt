As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be equally direct:

We **do** recognize the seriousness of the cost problem and the competitive pressure. Nobody here should pretend that the status quo can simply be carried forward unchanged. But I **do not accept** the framing that the only credible path is plant closures in Germany and a large-scale destruction of jobs.

That would be economically shortsighted, socially damaging, and politically unacceptable in a state whose industrial base depends on this company.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one essential point:  
**there must be a restructuring that restores competitiveness and creates room for future investment.**  
I also agree that this cannot be solved by slogans or by denying the numbers.

And I agree with the union and works council that any sustainable solution has to be negotiated, not imposed. A conflict that ends in a damaged company, broken trust, and prolonged industrial action would hurt everyone.

### Where I disagree
I disagree sharply with the idea that **German plant closures** are the necessary first instrument. Given this company’s role in the regional economy, closures are not a neutral efficiency measure; they are a decision with long-term consequences for jobs, supplier networks, skills, and public trust.

I also reject any approach that treats the workforce as the main adjustment variable while capital and management make only limited contributions. If employees are asked to accept sacrifices, then **the burden must be shared fairly**.

And let me be clear: I will not support any outcome that sidelines the state’s role, weakens co-determination, or strips away the protections that have helped this company weather past crises.

### What I can support
I am prepared to back a restructuring package if it meets three conditions:

1. **No plant closures in Germany as the default solution**  
   Capacity adjustments and site reconfiguration can be discussed, but closure must not be the starting point.

2. **Binding protection for employment and sites**  
   A serious alternative must include job security, social safeguards, and a credible commitment to keep the German industrial footprint intact.

3. **A balanced burden-sharing agreement**  
   That means labour contribution, yes — but also contributions from management, owners, and the company’s broader financing structure. If savings are needed, they cannot all come out of wages and headcount.

### To management
If you want my support, bring forward a restructuring that is **socially sustainable and industrially credible**. Show me how you close the cost gap without tearing up the regional economic base. If you ask for our vote on closures, you should expect resistance.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and on preserving the sites. But I also urge you to stay constructively engaged on working-time, flexibility, and efficiency measures. A total refusal to discuss adjustment would leave the field open to the most aggressive options.

### Bottom line
I am **not** prepared to accept a restructuring built around plant closures and mass layoffs in Germany.  
I **am** prepared to support a negotiated restructuring that preserves the company’s long-term health while protecting jobs, sites, and co-determination.

That is the path we should be working toward.
