We share the core point: **no restructuring can be legitimate if it is built on compulsory redundancies and plant closures while the workforce is made to carry the full burden**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **Forced layoffs are unacceptable.**
- **German plant closures are unacceptable.**
- **The collective wage framework must not be dismantled as a one-way cost-cutting exercise.**

That said, we are **not refusing adjustment in principle**. We submitted a counter-proposal precisely because we understand the pressure on the company and the need for a durable solution. We are prepared to discuss:

- a **negotiated labour-cost contribution**,
- **capacity reduction** without closing sites,
- **working-time flexibility**,
- temporary or limited adjustments to remuneration elements,
- and an orderly transformation path that secures future products and investment.

But this only works if the sacrifice is **shared fairly**. If management and owners expect employees to give up wages, guarantees, and security while they themselves offer no comparable contribution, then there is no basis for an agreement.

So to management and the owners: if you want a deal, bring forward a package that includes:

1. **binding job security**,  
2. **site security for the German plants**,  
3. **no compulsory redundancies**,  
4. **a real burden-sharing contribution from management and capital**, and  
5. **investment commitments for the future of production in Germany**.

If the proposal remains “jobs and sites first on the chopping block, then workers pay,” we will oppose it decisively. The peace obligation has ended, and we are prepared to escalate further if necessary.

To the works council: we are aligned on the key red lines. Where we can be constructive is in shaping the alternative—one that preserves the industrial base and avoids a destructive confrontation. But I want to stress: if management insists on closures, we should not treat that as a normal bargaining position. It is a line we must resist.
