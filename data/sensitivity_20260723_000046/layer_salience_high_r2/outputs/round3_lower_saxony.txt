I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: **this is a structural problem, not a temporary one**. The company needs a durable turnaround, and a cosmetic compromise would simply postpone the crisis. I also agree that a prolonged strike or governance deadlock would damage the company, the workforce, and the region.

But I do **not** accept the management line that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the natural or default answer. And I do **not** accept any package that removes jobs and industrial capacity from the state in a way that undermines the region’s economic base or sidelines our co-determination role.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the cost gap is real,
- the current structure is not competitive enough,
- and temporary measures alone will not solve the problem.

That is the honest starting point.

### With the works council and union
You are right that:
- burden-sharing must be real,
- workers should not carry the whole adjustment,
- and industrial peace is worth protecting.

### With the expert and finance views
The analytical point is also correct: a closure-free solution is **harder**, not impossible. It can work only if the gap is manageable and the measures are durable and enforceable. A purely symbolic package is worthless.

## Where I disagree

### 1) I do not accept closures as the starting point
Management keeps speaking as if closures are the rational baseline and everything else is an exception. For me, that is the wrong sequence.

Before anyone talks about shutting German plants, the company must show:
- the **minimum savings target**,
- the **site-by-site gap**,
- what can be done through working-time, remuneration, internal reallocation, and overhead cuts,
- and what management, ownership, and capital will contribute themselves.

If that work has not been done, closures are not “necessary.” They are only an option management prefers.

### 2) I do not accept compulsory redundancies as inevitable
I will be very clear: forced job cuts are not an acceptable first instrument. They create political damage, industrial conflict, and long-term trust loss that can easily wipe out short-term savings.

If workforce reduction is ever discussed, it must be through:
- natural attrition,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- and negotiated transitions.

Not as a blunt instrument.

### 3) I do not accept a restructuring that weakens the regional industrial base
This company is not just another balance-sheet story. It is a regional anchor:
- for direct employment,
- for suppliers,
- for training,
- for municipal economies,
- and for social stability.

A package that hollows out German sites may improve a short-term ratio and still damage the company’s long-term social license and industrial strength. That is not a responsible outcome.

## What I need to see

If management wants my support, I need a **quantified, closure-free or near-closure-free alternative** on the table, with the following elements:

1. **Minimum savings requirement**
   - State the exact annual savings needed.
   - Separate fixed-cost from variable-cost relief.

2. **Site-by-site contribution**
   - What does each German plant contribute to savings?
   - What capacity reduction is possible without closure?

3. **Labor contribution**
   - Working-time flexibility
   - Remuneration adjustments
   - Bonus restraint
   - Qualification and redeployment measures

4. **Management and capital contribution**
   - Executive restraint
   - Lower payout expectations
   - Concrete ownership contribution
   - No passing the entire burden to employees

5. **Durability**
   - Any concession must be enforceable through 2030 or for a similarly meaningful period.
   - No vague promises that can be reopened at the next downturn.

6. **Investment commitments**
   - Future products
   - Technology
   - Plant modernization
   - Clear linkage between contribution today and industrial future tomorrow

## My bottom line on the current debate

### To management
If you can show that a closure-free package truly fails economically, then say so with numbers. But do not ask us to accept closures before that test is complete.

### To the works council and union
I support your refusal to let the workforce be sacrificed. But you also need to engage the savings question honestly. “No closures” is a position, not yet a full restructuring plan.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right that value destruction is unacceptable. But so is forcing the adjustment mainly onto labor and the region. If you want a stable turnaround, support a solution that preserves viable German capacity and industrial peace.

### To the state government
Your leverage should be used to ensure:
- no closures as the default,
- transparent economics,
- real burden-sharing,
- and binding commitments to German sites.

That is the political and economic line I will hold.

## My position
I can support a restructuring only if it:
- protects jobs and sites in the state,
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- keeps co-determination meaningful,
- and still delivers a durable competitiveness improvement.

I will **not** support a package that:
- starts from plant closures,
- imposes compulsory redundancies,
- or strips the state of its practical veto role over major site decisions.

So the next step is not more rhetoric about inevitability.  
The next step is a **serious, quantified, closure-free or hybrid proposal** that proves it can restore competitiveness without gutting regional employment.
