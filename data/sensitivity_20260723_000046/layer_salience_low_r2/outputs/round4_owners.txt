I will be direct.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, the expert, and the investors on the central point: **the company has a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary one**. The cost base is too high, the European market is weak, and the company needs a restructuring that is **durable, measurable, and enforceable**.

I also agree that:

- cosmetic savings are worthless,
- labour concessions alone will not fix the business,
- and the restructuring must free up cash for future products and technology.

That is the basic reality, and it should not be obscured by slogans.

## Where I disagree

I do **not** accept the labour side’s attempt to make **“no closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** absolute starting points.

That position may be emotionally understandable and politically convenient, but it does not answer the industrial question: **how do you remove enough capacity and fixed cost to restore margins?**

If the non-closure route cannot reach the necessary scale, then it is not a solution. It is delay.

I also do **not** accept the idea that a site-preserving package is automatically acceptable if it simply keeps every plant open and relies mainly on:

- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- temporary pay moderation,
- and management restraint.

Those measures can help. They are not automatically equivalent to a genuine structural correction.

## To the works council

You insist that closures destroy trust and industrial capability. That is true — but not sufficient.

What matters for long-term value is not preserving every structure intact. What matters is whether the company becomes competitive enough to survive and invest. If the footprint remains oversized, then the result is not protection of jobs; it is the postponement of a larger problem.

So my response is: **show me the plant-by-plant economics**.

If you want closures off the table, then the alternative must demonstrate:

- real capacity reduction,
- durable fixed-cost savings,
- measurable headcount reduction,
- and a credible path back to margin improvement.

Until I see that, I do not accept “no closures” as a sufficient answer.

## To the union

I respect your insistence that the workforce should not carry the whole burden. I agree with that.

But I do **not** accept the claim that because closures and forced layoffs are painful, they therefore cannot be necessary. Painful is not the same as avoidable.

If the business needs real structural change, then labour contribution must be tied to real industrial adjustment — not merely to temporary relief or symbolic fairness.

## To the regional state government

You are right to worry about regional industrial damage. I take that seriously.

But regional stewardship also means not freezing the company into an uncompetitive shape. If the footprint is structurally too heavy, then protecting every site at any cost may ultimately do more harm to the region than a hard but focused restructuring would.

So I support your demand for a serious, quantified alternative — but I do **not** support turning site preservation into a veto on necessary change.

## To management

You are right to insist on real economics. I support that.

But I also want to be clear: closures are not a slogan to be waved around casually. They are a last-resort instrument if the numbers genuinely require them. Management still has to prove that the alternative package is insufficient before going there.

So I want the comparison on the table, not rhetoric.

## To investors

You are right that value is destroyed by cosmetic compromises. I agree.

But value is also destroyed by locking the company into a structure that cannot compete. So I am aligned with you on the need for a hard, credible restructuring — not on preserving everything in place.

## My position

The family holding will support a **non-closure solution only if it is economically equivalent in hard numbers**.

That means:

- meaningful capacity reduction,
- durable annual savings,
- measurable workforce adjustment,
- and a clear implementation path.

If those conditions are not met, then I will not support a deal just because it is politically easier.

## What I need next

We need a **plant-by-plant comparison** of:

1. a closure-based restructuring,
2. a non-closure restructuring,
3. and a hybrid option.

It must show:

- capacity reduction,
- headcount reduction,
- annual recurring savings,
- one-time implementation costs,
- timing,
- and a failure trigger if the non-closure route underperforms.

## Bottom line

I am not prepared to endorse a settlement that preserves all sites and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

I am open to a non-closure deal — but only if it truly restores competitiveness and margins. If it does not, then I will not pretend otherwise.

That is the test.
