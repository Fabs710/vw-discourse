From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the key question is not who “wins” this confrontation, but whether the company returns to a credible, durable margin structure fast enough to protect enterprise value.

I agree with management, and with the family holding, on the central diagnosis: the current cost base is not competitive enough. If that is not corrected, the market will continue to punish the share price, and capital allocation into the future will be constrained. We cannot support a solution that preserves structurally impaired margins or treats overcapacity as if it were merely a temporary inconvenience.

That said, I do not automatically endorse plant closures as the only credible route. What matters to us is the economic result:
- durable capacity reduction,
- meaningful and lasting labour-cost improvement,
- and a clear path back to competitive returns on sales.

If management can only get there through closures, then closures may be justified. But if the same structural effect can be achieved through a negotiated package with equivalent savings and enforceability, we would prefer that. Not because we are sentimentally opposed to restructuring, but because prolonged industrial conflict, execution risk, and legal/board veto risk all destroy value.

To the works council and union: I understand the insistence on no forced redundancies and no cosmetic deal. On that point, I agree that a purely symbolic settlement would be worse than no settlement. But your red lines become a problem if they amount to preserving the existing cost structure under a different label. No-closure and no-layoff positions are only credible if they are matched by a package that genuinely restores competitiveness. Otherwise you are asking shareholders to finance delay.

To the regional state government: I respect the public-interest concern and the need to avoid reckless damage to the German industrial base. But “protecting sites” has to be judged against the economic reality of a shrinking European market and international competition. If keeping every site intact leaves the company uncompetitive, that does not protect the region in the medium term. We would support site preservation only if it is part of a plan that clearly improves margins.

To the controlling family: your focus on long-term value is aligned with ours, and your rejection of cosmetic compromise is exactly right. But long-term stewardship also means being willing to take decisive action now. Delay, ambiguity, or a deal that merely shifts the pain into the future would be unacceptable.

So our position is:

- We support a restructuring that credibly restores competitiveness and margins.
- We do not support a cosmetic settlement that leaves the cost base structurally too high.
- We are open to either closures or non-closure alternatives, but only if the result is economically equivalent and enforceable.
- We would view prolonged conflict, weak implementation, or governance stalemate as value-destructive.

In practical terms, we will be watching for three things:
1. measurable capacity reduction,
2. durable labour-cost savings,
3. and sufficient certainty that the plan can actually be implemented.

If those conditions are not met, we will not regard the outcome as investment-grade, regardless of how politically attractive it may be.
