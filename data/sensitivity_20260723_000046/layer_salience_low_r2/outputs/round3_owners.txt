I appreciate that the discussion has finally become concrete. That matters. But I will be equally clear: I am not prepared to endorse a settlement that preserves every site, rules out compulsory redundancies absolutely, and leaves management without a credible way to restore the cost base. That would protect comfort, not value.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the crisis is structural, not cyclical,
- temporary measures alone will not solve it,
- and the outcome must be durable, measurable, and enforceable.

I also agree that the company needs room to invest in future products and technology. Without that, we are just extending decline.

### With the expert
The expert has clarified the central issue correctly: a non-closure package is only credible if it delivers **real capacity reduction** and **durable fixed-cost savings**. I accept that. Labor concessions alone are not enough if the network remains oversized.

### With investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. A deal that avoids hard choices but does not restore margins is value-destructive.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
I do **not** accept “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute outcomes unless you can demonstrate a truly equivalent industrial result.

So far, I have not seen that proof.

You keep saying non-closure options exist. I do not dispute that they exist. The question is whether they are sufficient in scale and durability. The expert’s assessment suggests the answer is: only if they include substantial capacity removal, headcount reduction, and recurring savings in the hundreds of millions. That is a demanding standard, and it should be.

What I do **not** accept is a deal that:
- preserves all sites unchanged,
- relies mainly on temporary pay moderation and working-time flexibility,
- and then calls that a restructuring.

That is postponement.

### With the regional state government
I understand the political need to defend the regional industrial base. But “no closures as a basis” is not the same as “no closures ever.” If the economics require a hard rationalization and the alternative package fails to match it, then blocking that adjustment simply shifts the damage into the future.

Regional stewardship also means not allowing a structurally weak footprint to drag the company down.

## What would move me

What would move the holding is not rhetoric about social partnership; it is a quantified plan that proves three things:

1. **capacity comes out of the system**
2. **the annual cost base falls durably**
3. **the company retains enough flexibility to adapt later if the market worsens**

If a site-preserving package can do that, then I am open to it. But it must be real, not aspirational.

## What I need to see
Before I can support any non-closure route, I want a plant-by-plant comparison of:

- which lines or shifts are removed,
- what recurring savings are achieved,
- how headcount declines,
- what the one-time implementation costs are,
- and what the failure trigger is if the plan misses its targets.

[SUMMON: independent industrial restructuring advisor to convert the non-closure and closure options into plant-level capacity, cost, headcount, and milestone comparisons, including a failure trigger and margin impact]

That is the only way to separate a genuine alternative from a delay mechanism.

## My position on guarantees
I am **not** prepared to support a full employment and site guarantee through 2030 across all German sites if that prevents needed adjustment. That would lock in the very problem we are trying to fix.

I am open to:
- phased protection,
- conditional guarantees,
- and site-preserving solutions,
but only if they are tied to verified performance and do not remove management’s ability to act if the restructuring under-delivers.

## My minimum acceptable outcome
- real structural improvement in capacity and cost
- measurable and durable margin recovery
- no cosmetic compromise
- no permanent impairment of flexibility
- no value-destroying instability

## My maximum concession
- openness to a non-closure solution
- acceptance of working-time and flexibility measures
- support for temporary, negotiated labor contributions
- willingness to back site-preserving restructuring **if it is economically equivalent**

## Single most important condition
The single most important condition for the family holding remains:

**The restructuring must produce a durable improvement in competitiveness and margins without creating long-term instability or trapping the company in an uncompetitive structure.**

If a package cannot meet that test, I will not support it — regardless of how insistently it is presented as the only politically acceptable option.

## Bottom line
I am not taking closures off the table just because they are unpopular. I am also not endorsing them as a first choice. I am saying the same thing I have said throughout: show me a package that truly fixes the economics, and I will support the path that does that.

Until then, I remain unconvinced that the current “no closures, no forced layoffs” line is sufficient to protect long-term value.
