# 1) What “equivalent” means in practice

For a large premium-volume carmaker in Germany, a non-closure deal is only economically equivalent if it achieves, in the affected footprint:

- **Capacity reduction:** about **10–20%**
- **Headcount reduction:** typically **several thousand FTE**
- **Recurring annual savings:** typically **hundreds of millions of euros**
- **Implementation certainty:** through plant-specific, enforceable measures
- **One-time cost:** materially lower than closure-led, but not zero

If the package is below that range, it is not a real substitute. It is a delay tactic.

# 2) Plant-by-plant scenario logic

Because no site data were provided, I have to use a **model-based template** rather than actual plant numbers.

## Scenario A: Closure-led restructuring
### Typical mechanics
- One or more plants close, or major plant units are shut permanently
- Products are moved to remaining plants or external suppliers
- Workforce reduction occurs through closure, transfers, and exit programs

### Typical industrial outcome
- **Capacity reduction:** highest
- **Recurring savings:** highest
- **Headcount reduction:** highest
- **One-time restructuring cost:** highest
- **Execution risk:** high due to labor conflict and transition complexity

### Practical use
This is the fastest way to remove a large fixed-cost block. It is also the most politically and socially disruptive.

---

## Scenario B: Non-closure restructuring
### Typical mechanics
- All plants remain formally open
- Capacity is reduced by:
  - eliminating shifts,
  - consolidating models,
  - lowering operating days,
  - transferring volume,
  - freezing hiring,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - productivity programs,
  - management overhead reduction,
  - wage moderation / benefit changes

### Typical industrial outcome
- **Capacity reduction:** moderate
- **Recurring savings:** moderate to high only if executed hard
- **Headcount reduction:** moderate
- **One-time cost:** lower than closure-led
- **Execution risk:** high, because savings are easy to dilute

### Practical use
This can work if the package is **site-specific and numerically tight**. It usually fails if it remains a loose collection of promises.

---

## Scenario C: Hybrid restructuring
### Typical mechanics
- Preserve core sites
- Reduce shifts, lines, and overlapping functions at weaker sites
- Specialize plants by model or technology
- Use partial footprint reductions rather than whole-site closure

### Typical industrial outcome
- **Capacity reduction:** medium to high
- **Recurring savings:** medium to high
- **Headcount reduction:** medium to high
- **One-time cost:** intermediate
- **Execution risk:** lower than full closure, higher than a soft non-closure deal

### Practical use
This is the most realistic compromise if governance blocks outright closure but management still needs real change.

# 3) What a credible non-closure package must contain

To be economically credible, it should include all four buckets below.

## A. Capacity measures
- fixed removal of one or more shifts at named sites
- line or module consolidation
- product transfer out of low-utilization lines
- operating-day reduction
- closure of sub-units, not just “optimization”

## B. Workforce measures
- attrition targets
- voluntary exit programs
- early retirement / bridging where legal and affordable
- redeployment across plants
- hiring freeze in affected functions

## C. Cost measures
- management overhead cuts
- procurement/logistics rationalization
- maintenance and energy efficiency
- labor-cost contribution through working-time and compensation changes
- productivity improvements with measured output/FTE

## D. Governance measures
- plant-specific milestones
- independent verification
- review gates at 12–18 months
- automatic consequences if targets are missed

Without all four, the deal is not robust.

# 6) The failure trigger

A non-closure package needs a **hard failure trigger**, otherwise it will lose credibility.

## Recommended trigger structure
If, by a predefined review date:
- capacity reduction is below target by more than, say, **2–3 percentage points**,
- savings are below target by more than **10–15%**,
- or headcount reduction is materially behind plan,

then:
1. the guarantee period expires or narrows,
2. management regains the right to propose deeper footprint action,
3. closure or partial-closure options reopen for the underperforming sites.

That is the only way to make the package enforceable without forcing an immediate closure decision.
