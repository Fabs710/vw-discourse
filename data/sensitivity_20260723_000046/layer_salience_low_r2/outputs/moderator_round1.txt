**Moderator summary**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious competitiveness and cost crisis**.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable restructuring** is needed, not just cosmetic or temporary measures.
- Most parties support the importance of:
  - **investment in future products/technologies**,
  - **some form of burden-sharing**,
  - **working-time / flexibility solutions** as part of the package,
  - and **avoiding unnecessary industrial conflict** if a workable settlement can be found.
- There is also shared recognition that **a purely one-sided approach** would be unstable and likely fail.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Management, the family holding, and investors see closures as a possible tool if needed.
  - Works council, union, and regional government reject closures as a first choice and want to preserve sites wherever possible.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Works council, union, and regional government oppose them.
  - Management, family holding, and investors do not rule them out if required for structural change.
- **Scope of guarantees**
  - Labour side seeks binding job and site security commitments.
  - Management and shareholders oppose guarantees that would prevent necessary restructuring.
- **Form of restructuring**
  - Labour/public side prefer negotiated, site-preserving adjustment.
  - Management/shareholders insist the outcome must deliver **real, durable capacity and cost reductions**.
- **Role of co-determination / state influence**
  - Employee representatives and the state want meaningful participation.
  - Management and capital-side actors emphasize operational decisiveness and enforceability.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required**, and how much of it must come from labour versus management/owners.
- **Whether equivalent savings can be achieved without closures**, and what “equivalent” means in measurable terms.
- **Which sites/capacities are protected, reduced, or transformed**, and on what criteria.
- **How to balance job security against strategic flexibility** over the medium term.
- **What binding commitments are credible** without eliminating management’s ability to restructure later if market conditions worsen.
- **How investment commitments are to be linked** to concessions on wages, working time, or capacity.
- **How burdens are shared at the top** versus on the shop floor, including possible restraint on distributions or executive measures.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **concrete package design**:
- specify the **target savings/capacity reduction** needed,
- identify **which measures can deliver them without closures** and which cannot,
- outline **what guarantees are possible** and for how long,
- and clarify **what each side is prepared to contribute** in a combined settlement.

A useful next step would be for each side to state:
1. its **minimum acceptable outcome**,  
2. its **maximum concession**, and  
3. the **single most important condition** for supporting a deal.
