I want to be very clear: I share the diagnosis that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem. I do not dismiss the pressure from shrinking demand, high costs, and international competition. If we ignore that reality, we endanger the company’s future and with it the jobs in the region.

But I do not accept management’s conclusion that plant closures in Germany are the necessary or preferred solution, at least not as a first resort and not without exhausting alternatives. For me, closures are not a neutral “structural measure”; they are a permanent damage decision with deep consequences for employment, the regional economy, and the company’s social legitimacy.

Where I agree:
- The company needs a durable restructuring, not cosmetic adjustments.
- The current cost base has to come down.
- Any settlement must be credible to capital markets and capable of restoring competitiveness.
- Investment in future products and technologies is essential.

Where I disagree:
- Closing German plants should not be treated as the default answer.
- Forced redundancies would be economically and politically damaging and should be avoided.
- A one-sided burden on the workforce is neither fair nor sustainable.
- A restructuring that weakens co-determination or sidelines the state’s role would be unacceptable.

From the regional and public-interest perspective, the right approach is a negotiated restructuring that preserves sites and core industrial capabilities in Germany while delivering real savings. That means:
- capacity adjustment through reorganization, not closure where avoidable,
- working-time flexibility and targeted cost contributions,
- use of natural attrition, internal redeployment, and retirement schemes,
- binding investment commitments for future platforms and products,
- and a fair sharing of burdens across management, owners, and workforce.

To the union and works council: your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no unilateral plant closures is legitimate and important. I support that as a negotiating baseline. But I would add: we also need a solution that is economically durable, not just defensively protective. If there are ways to reduce capacity without eliminating sites, they should be seriously explored.

To management: if you want the state and workforce to accept sacrifices, you must show that you are not just cutting labour, but also contributing through strategic discipline, restraint at the top, and a genuine industrial plan. A restructuring that destroys regional trust will make the company weaker, not stronger, over the long term.

So my position is:
- No rash plant closures.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Yes to a serious restructuring package if it protects jobs and sites while restoring competitiveness.
- Yes to negotiated burden-sharing, but not to a solution that guts the company’s regional footprint or sidelines co-determination.

If management can present a plan that achieves the needed savings with site preservation, investment, and social partnership, I am prepared to work toward that. If the proposal remains centered on closures and mass layoffs, then I will oppose it.
