## Core impasse

The conflict has narrowed to one central issue:

**Whether the required structural savings can be achieved without plant closures and compulsory redundancies, while still preserving a meaningful job- and site-security framework.**

That impasse has three layers:

1. **Different definitions of “equivalent savings”**  
   - Management, investors, and the family holding require a quantified, durable reduction in cost and capacity.
   - Works council and union insist that alternatives to closures must be tried first, and that guarantees are part of the deal, not a later outcome.

2. **Different views of risk allocation**  
   - Labour wants the adjustment burden shared and bounded.
   - Capital-side actors want flexibility to ensure the turnaround can still be executed if market conditions worsen.

3. **Different thresholds for acceptability**  
   - Labour’s red lines are “no closures, no forced layoffs.”
   - Management’s red line is “no deal that fails to change the economics.”
   - The state’s red line is “no outcome that permanently damages the regional base or sidelines co-determination.”

---

## Possible compromise positions

### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional guarantees
**Structure:**  
- Immediate agreement on no compulsory redundancies for a defined initial period.
- Temporary site guarantees for all German plants during that same period.
- In parallel, a binding savings and capacity-reduction program with milestones.
- If milestones are met, the guarantee period extends; if not, a pre-agreed escalation mechanism is triggered.

**Why this could bridge stakeholders:**  
- Gives labour and the state concrete protection up front.
- Gives management and investors measurable enforcement and an exit if the plan fails.
- Converts the dispute from “yes/no closures” into “performance-conditional preservation.”

---

### 2) Hybrid package: no closures now, but hard capacity cuts elsewhere
**Structure:**  
- No plant closures in the initial settlement.
- No compulsory redundancies as a general rule.
- Achieve structural savings through:
  - shift elimination,
  - product-line consolidation,
  - site specialization,
  - headcount reduction via attrition and voluntary exits,
  - management overhead cuts,
  - and negotiated working-time/cost contributions.

**Why this could bridge stakeholders:**  
- Meets the labour side’s core red lines in the short term.
- Gives management and shareholders real capacity reduction if the cuts are specific and quantified.
- Requires detailed plant-by-plant implementation, not a general promise.

---

### 3) Site-guarantee package linked to enforceable counter-concessions
**Structure:**  
- Binding preservation of named German sites through a medium-term horizon.
- In return, workforce accepts:
  - temporary working-time flexibility,
  - limited pay moderation,
  - voluntary departure instruments,
  - redeployment rules.
- Management and owners commit:
  - executive restraint,
  - investment in future products,
  - transparent reporting of savings,
  - no distributions that contradict the restructuring burden-sharing.

**Why this could bridge stakeholders:**  
- Addresses the fairness dispute directly.
- Makes the deal legible as shared sacrifice rather than one-sided adjustment.
- Still requires precise numerical proof that the savings are sufficient.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific combination of capacity cuts, redeployment, and labor contributions would you accept as a credible substitute for closures, and what measurable savings would make that substitute defensible to your constituency?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited no-compulsory-redundancy and no-closure framework if it included binding milestones, verified savings, and an automatic review clause tied to performance?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings, capacity reductions, and implementation milestones would be sufficient for you to withdraw closure and forced-layoff demands and proceed with a site-preserving deal?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to support a medium-term site guarantee even if it requires temporary workforce contributions and tighter management commitments, provided the package is independently verified as economically credible?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What level of quantified, enforceable cost and capacity improvement would lead you to support a non-closure settlement, even if it limits short-term strategic flexibility?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you back a restructuring that preserves German sites if it is independently audited, milestone-based, and demonstrably sufficient to restore margins and utilization without closures?**

### To the Independent Expert
**Can you define the minimum measurable capacity reduction and fixed-cost improvement that would make a non-closure package genuinely equivalent, so the parties have a shared benchmark rather than competing claims?**
