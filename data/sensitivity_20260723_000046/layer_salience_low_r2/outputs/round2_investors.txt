We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the implementation.

## Where we agree
The expert’s assessment reinforces our core view:
- the company has a **real overcapacity and cost problem**,
- a **cosmetic settlement would destroy value**,
- and the outcome must deliver **durable margin improvement**, not just labour peace.

That is exactly the right lens for shareholders. We are not interested in symbolic compromise if it leaves the return-on-sales problem intact.

## Where we disagree
The employee side keeps making **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full guarantees”** sound like a reasonable baseline. From a capital-market perspective, that is only acceptable if the alternative is **economically equivalent** in hard numbers.

Right now, I have not heard a package that convincingly proves that.

The expert was clear:
- labor concessions alone do **not** equal capacity reduction,
- attrition and flexibility are usually **too slow** for a severe structural problem,
- and if overcapacity is real, then some hard rationalization is often necessary.

So I do **not** accept a settlement that simply preserves all sites and postpones the adjustment while calling it transformation.

## What would move us
What would move institutional shareholders is not the political form of the deal, but whether it includes all three of these:

1. **A quantified capacity cut**
   - specific lines, shifts, or volumes removed

2. **A durable cost-base reduction**
   - not a temporary pay moderation that expires in the next bargaining round

3. **Implementation certainty**
   - a deal management can actually execute under German governance constraints

If those can be delivered without closures, fine. If not, then closures remain a legitimate tool.

## Our view on each party

### To management
We support the need for a hard restructuring, but we are not endorsing a vague threat posture. Bring a package with numbers:
- target savings,
- plant-by-plant impact,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and the path to ROS recovery.

If you cannot show that, the market will discount the whole plan.

### To works council and union
Your insistence on social protection is understandable, but **absolute red lines** are only credible if you can replace them with a restructuring that is numerically equivalent.

So far, the burden you are asking to avoid is not magically disappearing. If the company still has excess capacity, then refusing closures and forced layoffs without a fully costed substitute is asking shareholders to accept underperformance.

### To the regional state government
We respect the concern for jobs and regional stability. But regional stability is not preserved by keeping an uncompetitive footprint alive indefinitely. That only delays pain and lowers enterprise value.

We would support site preservation only if it is part of a package that clearly improves economics.

### To the controlling family
Your focus on long-term stewardship is aligned with ours. But the stewardship test is not “can we avoid conflict for a year?” It is “can we restore competitive margins fast enough to protect value?”

If the answer is no, then the longer management waits, the more value is destroyed.

## Our current position
We are prepared to support:
- a **site-preserving solution** if it truly achieves the required savings,
- **working-time and flexibility measures**,
- **targeted voluntary exits and redeployment**,
- and even a **temporary employee contribution**, if it is material and enforceable.

We are **not** prepared to support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a framework that leaves the cost base structurally impaired,
- or a deal that locks in guarantees so rigidly that management cannot fix the business later.

## Single most important condition
The single most important condition for us is still:

**measurable, durable, enforceable improvement in margins and capacity utilization.**

If the package does not do that, then it is not an investment-grade solution, no matter how acceptable it sounds politically.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated alternative to closures — but only if it is real, quantified, and durable.

If the employee side wants us to reject closures, it must show the numbers that prove the alternative works. Otherwise, shareholders will conclude that the company is being asked to pay for delay, and we will not support that.
