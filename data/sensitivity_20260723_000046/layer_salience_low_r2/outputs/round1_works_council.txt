As the Works Council, my position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. The workforce has already made major contributions over the years, and management cannot now solve strategic problems by destroying jobs, breaking site security, and abandoning the historic job-security framework.

What we are prepared to discuss are alternative measures that secure the company’s future without sacrificing our people:
- binding employment guarantees through 2030,
- preservation of all German sites,
- capacity adjustments through non-destructive measures,
- working-time and flexibility solutions negotiated with the workforce,
- and a genuine co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping the restructuring and investment plan.

If management wants support for a transformation, then it must be credible, fair, and reversible where possible. That means burden-sharing across all stakeholders, not one-sided cuts on the shop floor. We need a plan that protects jobs, preserves industrial capability in Germany, and secures long-term competitiveness together — not by setting the workforce against the company’s own future.

So my line is: no closures, no forced layoffs, no unilateral fait accompli. If there is to be a settlement, it must come with binding guarantees and real participation for the workforce.
