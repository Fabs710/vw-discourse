I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the **diagnosis**:
- the crisis is real,
- the current cost base is too high,
- the company needs a **durable** restructuring,
- and any package that is only cosmetic will fail.

I also agree with the expert that **labor concessions alone are not enough** if the company genuinely has structural overcapacity. We need real industrial change, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
I strongly disagree with the repeated attempt to turn **“no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies”** into absolute starting points.

That is not a restructuring strategy. It is a constraint that may make the company’s economics impossible to fix.

If the German footprint remains structurally oversized, then:
- fixed costs stay too high,
- capacity remains uncompetitive,
- margin recovery will be too weak,
- and the company will have less cash to invest in future products and technology.

That is exactly what I cannot accept.

## To the Works Council
You say closures are destruction and that the workforce should not carry the burden. I understand the concern, but your answer does not solve the industrial problem.

You keep saying:
- shift elimination,
- product consolidation,
- attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- management cuts.

Fine. Those are parts of the answer. But you still have not shown that they are **equivalent** to the savings and capacity reduction required.

The expert has already made the key point: a credible non-closure package requires roughly:
- **10–20% capacity reduction** in the relevant German footprint,
- **several thousand jobs reduced over time**,
- and **hundreds of millions of euros of recurring annual savings**.

If you cannot deliver that scale without closures, then “no closures” is not a solution. It is a refusal to face the numbers.

## To the Union
You argue that closures and forced redundancies break trust and the collective framework. That may be true politically, but it does not answer the competitiveness problem.

You also say you are willing to contribute through working-time flexibility and temporary moderation. That is welcome, but it is not enough on its own.

What I need is:
- a **plant-by-plant plan**,
- quantified savings,
- clear headcount impact,
- and proof that the package fixes the cost base structurally.

Without that, the claim that a site-preserving deal is sufficient remains unproven.

## To the Regional State Government
You say closures should not be the basis of the settlement. I can accept that closures are not the **first choice**.

But I do not accept any political line that makes closures impossible **even if the numbers demand them**.

A state shareholder must also confront reality:
- a company that remains uncompetitive will eventually destroy more jobs, more investment, and more regional value than a hard restructuring would.

So yes, protect the regional base where possible. But do not block the measures required to restore the business.

## To the Controlling Family
You are right that a weak compromise would damage value. On that point we agree.

But value is not protected by preserving every site at all costs. Value is protected by restoring the economics. If the non-closure package can do that, I am open to it. If it cannot, then we need the ability to act.

I am not interested in “comfort.” I am interested in competitiveness and margin recovery.

## To Institutional Investors
You are right to demand measurable, enforceable improvement. That is exactly what I am demanding as well.

But you should be careful not to confuse flexibility with weakness. A real restructuring package may require closures or compulsory separations if the alternatives cannot produce the same structural effect.

What I cannot support is a deal that sounds peaceful but leaves the company with:
- too much capacity,
- too much fixed cost,
- and too little ability to compete.

## On the expert’s contribution
The expert has helped clarify the terms of the debate:
- a non-closure solution is possible only if it produces **real capacity cuts**,
- **durable fixed-cost reduction**,
- and **measurable headcount decline**.

That is useful because it moves us from slogans to thresholds.

But let me be clear: the expert’s threshold does **not** prove that closures are unnecessary. It proves the opposite: that if a non-closure plan cannot hit those numbers, then it is not a viable substitute.

## What management is prepared to consider
I am prepared to consider alternatives to closures if they meet all of the following:

1. **Real capacity reduction**
   - not rearrangement on paper

2. **Durable labor-cost savings**
   - not temporary relief

3. **Plant-level implementation**
   - named actions by site, not generalities

4. **Meaningful burden-sharing**
   - workforce, management, and owners all contribute

5. **Enforceability**
   - milestones, monitoring, and consequences if targets are missed

6. **Investment linkage**
   - restructuring savings must free funds for future products and technology to 2030

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a blanket ban on closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- a full employment guarantee through 2030 regardless of market evolution,
- or a package that is politically attractive but economically insufficient.

That would not restore competitiveness. It would postpone the next crisis.

## On the “conditional guarantee” idea
I am open to a **conditional** framework only if it is truly conditional on measurable performance and does not erase management’s ability to fix the business later.

But I reject the idea that the company should sign away all flexibility up front and then hope the cost problem solves itself.

If a package under-delivers, we must have a predefined consequence. Otherwise, there is no discipline and no credibility.

## My position, stated plainly
- I support a **durable restructuring**
- I support **real capacity reduction**
- I support **burden-sharing**
- I support **investment**
- I do **not** accept a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive
- I do **not** rule out closures or compulsory redundancies if they are required to achieve the necessary economics
