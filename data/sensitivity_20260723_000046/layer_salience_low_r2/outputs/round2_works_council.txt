We should move from general statements to what is actually on the table.

My position remains unchanged:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework**
- **No settlement that sidelines co-determination**

That is not a rhetorical line; it is the minimum basis for any serious negotiation.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: the company needs a durable restructuring and real competitiveness improvements. I am not denying the crisis, and I am not asking for cosmetic measures. We also agree that investment in future products matters.

### Where I strongly disagree
What I do **not** accept is the conclusion that closing German plants and threatening forced layoffs is the only credible way forward.

That is the usual management logic when it wants the workforce to absorb the entire adjustment. We reject that logic.

If the company truly wants a sustainable solution, then it must show that it is willing to act on all sides of the balance sheet and all levels of the organization, not just the shop floor.

### Concrete package logic
If management wants our support, then the package must contain:

1. **Binding site guarantees for all German plants**
   - no closures,
   - no relocation disguised as restructuring,
   - no erosion by gradual hollowing-out.

2. **Binding employment guarantees through 2030**
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - staffing changes only through natural attrition, retirement, redeployment, and voluntary instruments.

3. **A real employee contribution**
   - yes, we are prepared to discuss working-time flexibility,
   - yes, we are prepared to discuss temporary, negotiated cost contributions,
   - but only as part of a fair package with guarantees.

4. **Management and owner contribution**
   - restraint on executive pay and top-level cost structures,
   - no value extraction through distributions while asking workers to make permanent sacrifices,
   - investment commitments for the German sites and future products.

5. **Genuine co-determination**
   - the workforce must have real influence in shaping the restructuring and investment plan,
   - not just be informed after the fact.

### On the “equivalent savings” argument
Management and the capital side keep saying “equivalent structural effect.” Fine — then show it.

But let me be very clear: **equivalent savings do not automatically justify closures or layoffs**. If there are alternative measures that can deliver cost reduction without destroying sites and livelihoods, then those alternatives must be taken first.

That means:
- capacity reductions through reorganization,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary schemes,
- and a negotiated labour contribution.

What it does **not** mean is: “We prefer closures, but if you object, you must solve our problem anyway.” That is not negotiation; that is coercion.

### To management
You say permanent guarantees block necessary restructuring. I say: if your restructuring only works by breaking the job-security framework, then your plan is not a future-proof industrial strategy — it is a short-term cost cut with long-term social damage.

### To the family holding and investors
You talk about long-term value. Then understand this: long-term value is not created by provoking conflict, destroying trust, and gutting the German industrial base. Value also depends on stable social partnership, skilled workforce retention, and production capability.

A company that burns trust and tears out its employment foundation does not become stronger.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your stance against rash closures. But “supporting restructuring” cannot become a gateway to job destruction. If you are serious about regional responsibility, then you should back the defence of all German sites and oppose compulsory redundancies clearly and publicly.

### My minimum acceptable outcome
- all German plants remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- genuine co-determination,
- and a negotiated transformation package with fair burden-sharing.

### My maximum concession
- targeted working-time flexibility,
- temporary cost moderation,
- voluntary instruments and redeployment,
- and a structured productivity agenda,
**but never closures and never forced layoffs**.

### Single most important condition
If there is to be any deal at all, it must contain **binding job and site guarantees**. Without that, there is nothing for us to sign.

So I will say it plainly: if management continues to insist on closures and compulsory redundancies, then there is no basis for agreement. We will defend the workforce and the German sites by every means available under co-determination and collective action.
