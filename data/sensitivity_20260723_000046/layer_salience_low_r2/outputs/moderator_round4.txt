**Facilitator assessment: core impasse**

The discussion has converged on a narrow but serious bottleneck:

1. **Labour-side red line:** no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.
2. **Management / investor / family side red line:** they need a restructuring that is economically sufficient, and they are not convinced that a site-preserving plan can deliver enough capacity and fixed-cost reduction.
3. **State’s position:** no closures as the basis of settlement, but it wants a credible industrial alternative.
4. **Expert’s filter:** a non-closure deal is only acceptable if it produces plant-level, quantified, durable savings and capacity reduction comparable enough to the closure scenario.

So the impasse is **not over whether restructuring is needed**; all sides now accept that.  
The impasse is **whether sufficient restructuring can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs, and if so, under what enforceable conditions**.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### Proposal 1: Conditional site-preservation package with hard milestones
A possible bridge is a **phased, site-preserving restructuring** in which:

- all German plants remain open in phase one,
- specific shifts, lines, or volumes are removed at named sites,
- workforce reduction is achieved primarily through voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and working-time changes,
- savings and capacity targets are set plant by plant,
- and a **pre-agreed failure trigger** opens the door to deeper structural options if targets are missed.

**Purpose:** preserves the no-closure principle initially while giving management and investors enforceable performance safeguards.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with protected core sites and targeted footprint reduction
A second bridge is a **hybrid model**:

- a limited number of core German sites are explicitly protected,
- other sites undergo stronger consolidation, shift cuts, or partial line shutdowns,
- no compulsory redundancies are used in the first phase except by mutual agreement,
- and the company commits to transparent transfer of volumes, products, and investment into the remaining footprint.

**Purpose:** gives management a real capacity reduction path while allowing labour and the state to avoid a full closure-led outcome.

---

### Proposal 3: Exchange package linking labour flexibility to binding investment and governance guarantees
A third compromise is an exchange structure:

- labour provides time-limited flexibility on working hours, pay moderation, redeployment, and voluntary separation,
- management, owners, and investors provide binding commitments on investment, overhead cuts, and governance restraint,
- the state supports the package with regional industrial safeguards,
- and the package includes a jointly monitored review point with automatic escalation if savings fall short.

**Purpose:** aligns burden-sharing with enforceable commitments and reduces the sense that workers are carrying the adjustment alone.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**If a plant-by-plant non-closure plan can demonstrate the expert’s required capacity and cost reductions, what specific quantified threshold would you accept as sufficient to keep closures off the table?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-limited package of working-time flexibility and voluntary exits if it were tied to binding plant-level savings targets and a failure trigger that protects jobs in the first instance?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you present a plant-by-plant non-closure scenario with verified savings, headcount effects, and timing before insisting that closures are unavoidable?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a conditional site-preservation package even if it requires strong shift cuts, product consolidation, and temporary employee contributions, provided no immediate closures occur?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you accept a hybrid plan that preserves the German footprint initially if the financial model shows durable, comparable savings and an enforceable fallback mechanism?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**If the package delivers measurable recurring savings and capacity reduction through non-closure measures, would you support it even if it leaves some operational flexibility constrained for a fixed period?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum plant-level combination of shift cuts, volume transfers, voluntary exits, and overhead reduction would you regard as sufficient to qualify a non-closure package as industrially equivalent for negotiation purposes?**
