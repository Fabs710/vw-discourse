The union shares the core line of the Works Council on the non-negotiables:

- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and no dismantling of the collective wage framework.

We will not accept a restructuring that pushes the full burden onto the workforce while management and owners preserve their position. That is neither fair nor sustainable, and it will meet resistance.

That said, I want to be precise about what a viable settlement could look like. We are not blocking change for the sake of it. We know the company faces real competitive pressure. But any solution must be a shared-sacrifice package, not a one-sided cost-cutting exercise.

What we can discuss includes:
- a negotiated labour-cost contribution,
- temporary or targeted moderation on pay elements,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions that do not mean closure,
- and a credible industrial strategy with investment and future-product commitments.

What we cannot accept:
- forced redundancies,
- site closures,
- and an erosion of the collective agreement that becomes permanent under the label of “restructuring.”

If management is serious about a durable settlement, then it must show that it is prepared to contribute itself — through savings at the top, restraint on distributions, binding investment commitments, and a restructuring concept that gives the workforce a future instead of just cutting today.

So our position is clear: we are ready to negotiate, but only on the basis of fairness, job security, and co-determined transformation. If management insists on closures and compulsory layoffs, then it should expect industrial conflict, because the workforce will not pay for this crisis alone.
