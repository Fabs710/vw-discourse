I hear the core message from the works council and the union: you want transformation, but you reject closures and compulsory redundancies. I need to be equally clear: that position does not solve the cost crisis we are facing.

We are not in a normal cycle. German factory costs are structurally too high, the European market is shrinking, and competition is intensifying fast. If we leave the current cost and capacity structure in place, we will not restore competitiveness, we will not hit the brand’s return-on-sales target, and we will not free the capital needed for the next generation of products and technology.

So let me state management’s position plainly:

- We need a durable reduction in capacity.
- We need a significant and lasting reduction in labour costs.
- We need structural change, not temporary measures that preserve excess capacity.
- And yes, that includes plant closures as a real option if that is what it takes to remove overcapacity.

I do not accept the idea that all German sites can be preserved unchanged and that the burden can be limited to voluntary flexibility or short-term pay moderation. That would be a cosmetic deal, not a restructuring.

That said, I am not closing the door on negotiation. If the employee side can bring forward a package that delivers the same structural effect — real capacity reduction, credible cost savings, and a durable contribution on labour costs — then we can discuss how that is achieved. But the outcome must be measurable and sustainable, not symbolic.

To be specific, what management needs to see is:

- a genuine and sizeable labour-cost contribution,
- reductions in overcapacity that actually change the cost base,
- no permanent guarantee that blocks necessary restructuring,
- and a framework that restores competitiveness through 2030, not just until the next bargaining round.

On investment and future products: yes, that matters. But investment is only possible if the underlying economics improve. We cannot fund the future by keeping an uncompetitive structure intact today.

So my message is simple: I respect that the workforce wants security, but security cannot be built on a cost base the company cannot sustain. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it must put something equally strong on the table — not a refusal, but a credible alternative that achieves the same structural result.
