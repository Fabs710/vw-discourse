## Core impasse
The discourse is blocked on **one structural question**:

**Can the company achieve the required durable capacity and cost reduction without plant closures and compulsory redundancies?**

That question has split the table into two camps:

- **Labour / state side:** closures and forced layoffs are unacceptable starting points; any restructuring must be site-preserving and job-secure.
- **Management / owners / investors:** non-closure proposals are only acceptable if they are quantitatively equivalent in savings, capacity reduction, and implementation certainty.

So the impasse is not about whether restructuring is needed — everyone accepts that.  
It is about **the form of capacity reduction and the enforceability of the trade-off**.

A second, related impasse is:

**Should the deal include an absolute guarantee, or a conditional guarantee with a failure trigger?**

Labour wants binding protection.  
Capital wants retained flexibility if the package under-delivers.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tested in the next round.

### 1) Phased site-preservation package with milestone-based review
**Concept:** All German plants remain open in the initial phase, but the package includes specific shift removals, model consolidation, and headcount reduction targets by site. A review gate is built in after 12–18 months.

**Core trade-off:**
- Labour gets: no immediate closures, no compulsory redundancies in phase one.
- Management / owners get: a real structural adjustment plan and a reopening of options if milestones fail.

**Key feature:** A predefined failure trigger:
- if savings, capacity reduction, or headcount targets are missed, closure options reopen.

---

### 2) Conditional employment and site-security agreement
**Concept:** A time-limited guarantee on sites and employment is granted only in exchange for quantified burden-sharing and verified productivity/capacity measures.

**Core trade-off:**
- Labour gets: stronger job security than a pure market-based restructuring.
- Capital gets: the guarantee is not absolute; it is tied to performance and implementation.

**Key feature:** The guarantee is not “through 2030 no matter what,” but rather:
- “no closures / no compulsory redundancies unless predefined economic triggers are breached.”

---

### 3) Hybrid package with differentiated treatment by plant type
**Concept:** Not every site is treated identically. Core plants receive stronger protection; low-utilization or overlapping functions are addressed through shift elimination, product transfer, or consolidation without immediate closure.

**Core trade-off:**
- Labour gets: a site-preserving framework where possible.
- Management / owners get: the ability to concentrate reductions where capacity is most excessive.

**Key feature:** A plant-by-plant matrix:
- protected sites
- restructuring sites
- consolidation sites
- each with measurable output, staffing, and savings targets
