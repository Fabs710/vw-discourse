As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and large parts of the regional workforce as the first or only solution.**

We understand the company’s competitive pressure. We are not denying the need for action. But management’s framing is too narrow if it treats closures and layoffs as the default answer.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a **durable industrial reset**, not a short-term patch.
- The current cost base and capacity situation cannot simply be ignored.
- A workforce contribution can be legitimate if it is part of a fair, negotiated package.
- The company must remain capable of investing in future products and technology.

### Where we disagree
What we cannot accept is the idea that “structural savings” automatically justify:
- closing German plants,
- forcing large job losses,
- or weakening the state’s and workforce’s co-determination role.

That is not a sustainable restructuring; it is a high-risk rupture of the social and industrial fabric that has made this company strong in the first place. Once a site is closed, the damage is permanent. Once trust is broken, it is very hard to rebuild. From the perspective of the region, that is not prudence — it is escalation.

### To management
If you want our support, you must show that you have exhausted the alternatives before touching sites or jobs:
- capacity reductions through smarter utilization,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary measures and attrition,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity gains and process improvements,
- and a real contribution from the ownership side as well.

You say the economics must be fixed. Fine. But that does **not** mean the adjustment burden can be placed primarily on the workforce and the regions that host the plants.

### To the works council and union
I support the core principle that **forced redundancies and plant closures should not be the starting point**. At the same time, we should be honest: a company in this situation will likely need a real restructuring package, not just a slogan of “everything stays as it is.”

That means we should be prepared to discuss:
- temporary employee contributions,
- flexible working-time instruments,
- capacity alignment,
- and productivity measures,

but only if they are tied to:
- binding site security,
- protection against compulsory redundancies,
- investment commitments,
- and a credible future for the German operations.

### Our bottom line
The state’s position is straightforward:
- **No unilateral closures.**
- **No forced layoffs as the chosen solution.**
- **No sidelining of co-determination or the regional anchor role.**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring if it protects jobs, preserves sites, and restores competitiveness.**

If management comes forward with a package that tries to make German plants the main adjustment variable, we will resist it. If, however, there is a serious social-partnership solution that shares the burden fairly and secures the company’s future, we are prepared to work on it.

Management should come back with a concrete alternative package before demanding that the region and workforce accept irreversible damage.
