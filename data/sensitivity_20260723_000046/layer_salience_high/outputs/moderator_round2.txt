### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs **durable restructuring**, not a temporary patch.
- There is broad agreement that the business needs:
  - **future investment**
  - **improved competitiveness**
  - **quantified, plant-level transparency**
  - **some form of shared burden**, not a one-sided solution
- Several stakeholders accept that **working-time tools, voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, productivity gains, and some employee contribution** should be part of the discussion.
- There is shared recognition that **conflict costs** and **reputational damage** matter and should be considered.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: management, some investors, and the controlling family keep them on the table; works council, union, and state oppose them as a starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: employee-side and state representatives reject them; management and owners treat them as possible if needed for structural savings.
- **Binding job/site guarantees**: employee-side wants strong guarantees through 2030; management and investors say unconditional guarantees may block needed restructuring.
- **Extent of employee concessions**: workers accept contributions only if tied to binding protections; management and investors say contributions alone may be insufficient.
- **Role of the state/co-determination**: the state and workforce want these powers used to block irreversible cuts; management and investors argue this can’t prevent necessary adjustment.
- **Whether alternatives are economically equivalent**: employee-side says yes in principle; management and investors say this has not yet been proven.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings versus permanence**: whether temporary measures can close a structural cost gap.
- **Capacity reduction versus preservation of sites**: whether competitiveness requires fewer sites or can be achieved within the current footprint.
- **Cost of restructuring versus cost of conflict**: one side emphasizes direct savings; the other emphasizes strikes, disruption, and trust erosion.
- **Short-term relief versus long-term viability**: temporary wage/time concessions may help cash flow but may not solve fixed-cost underabsorption.
- **Protection of jobs versus investment flexibility**: strong guarantees may stabilize the workforce but reduce management’s room to restructure.
- **Regional/industrial base versus shareholder value**: whether preserving sites supports long-term value or delays necessary adjustment.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **plant-by-plant scenarios**.
- Require a **side-by-side comparison** of:
  - closure/consolidation,
  - negotiated no-closure restructuring,
  - and status quo.
- Ask each side to quantify:
  - recurring savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - capacity effects,
  - implementation timeline,
  - and conflict risk.
- Focus the next round on identifying a **durable package that closes the savings gap** while clarifying which protections, if any, are conditional rather than absolute.
