We need to move from vague “competitiveness” rhetoric to a real, enforceable package. On the core issue, my position remains unchanged:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework**
- **No fait accompli by management**

Management says the arithmetic does not work. Fine — then put the arithmetic on the table transparently, plant by plant, and stop using jobs as the first adjustment variable.

### Where I agree
I agree with the union, the state, and even management on one point: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a temporary patch. I also agree that the business needs future investment and that the current cost base cannot simply be ignored.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that the only “serious” answer is plant closure or forced layoffs. That is not a law of economics; it is a management choice. And it is a choice we will resist.

If management wants to claim that no alternative exists, then it must explain why the following are supposedly insufficient:
- working-time reductions and flexibility,
- internal redeployment across sites,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary departures and natural attrition,
- temporary, negotiated employee contributions,
- productivity improvements,
- management and overhead reductions,
- and a fair contribution from owners.

You cannot start by declaring closures “inevitable” and then call that a negotiation.

### On numbers and conditions
If we are now being asked for a concrete framework, here is ours:

#### Minimum acceptable package from the works council side
1. **Binding site guarantee through 2030** for all German plants  
2. **No compulsory redundancies through 2030**  
3. **Preservation of co-determination rights and the job-security framework**  
4. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies** at German sites  
5. **A negotiated capacity adjustment plan** using working-time and voluntary tools only  
6. **Transparent burden-sharing**, including management and owner contributions

#### Fallback option
If management insists on savings beyond what can be achieved through working time, productivity, and voluntary measures, then the fallback is still **not** closures or forced layoffs. The fallback must be:
- shorter working hours temporarily,
- internal redistribution of work,
- staged investment,
- and a time-limited wage or bonus contribution tied to a binding job guarantee.

That is painful, but it is socially responsible. Mass redundancies and site closures are not “fallbacks”; they are ruptures.

#### Non-negotiable red line
- **No closure of any German plant**
- **No compulsory redundancy program**
- **No erosion of the historic security framework as the price of management’s mistakes**

### To management
You demand “economically equivalent” alternatives. Then define what you mean instead of hiding behind the phrase. If your benchmark is just short-term cost cutting, then of course closures look attractive. If your benchmark includes long-term industrial capability, labour peace, and the cost of destroying trust, then your case is much weaker.

You also need to answer this: what is the cost of a protracted conflict, warning strikes, reputational damage, and a damaged employer brand? Because that cost is real, and it is not borne by the works council alone.

### To the controlling family and investors
You say governance and value discipline matter. Agreed. But value is not protected by burning down social capital. A company that tears up its historic guarantees and destabilizes its workforce may win a spreadsheet argument and lose the industrial base.

If you want discipline, then exercise it where it belongs:
- management accountability,
- overhead reduction,
- product mix and execution,
- capital allocation,
- and investment quality.

Do **not** tell the workforce to pay for strategy errors through closures and layoffs.

### To the regional state
I appreciate your support, but “no unilateral closures” is not enough. The issue is not just unilateral action; the issue is any attempt to force closures or job cuts through a board majority. Your role should be to block that, not merely comment on it.

### Our message is simple
We are prepared to contribute to a fair solution. We are **not** prepared to sacrifice:
- sites,
- jobs,
- or co-determination.

If management wants a deal, it must come with:
- binding guarantees,
- real investment,
- and a restructuring that does not destroy the workforce to save the balance sheet.

Otherwise, we will continue to use every instrument available to us to defend the plants and the jobs.
