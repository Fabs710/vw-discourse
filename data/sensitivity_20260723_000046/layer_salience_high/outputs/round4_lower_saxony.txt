I will be just as direct.

## My position

I support a **durable restructuring**. I do **not** support a restructuring that makes German plants, regional employment, and the state’s co-determination role the primary adjustment variable.

For me, the test remains:

- Does this preserve the company as a regional industrial anchor?
- Does it protect jobs and sites in the state?
- Does it preserve our veto and co-determination role?
- Does it restore competitiveness without irreversible regional damage?

If the answer is no, I will not back it.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that:
- a cosmetic deal is unacceptable,
- the company needs real savings,
- future investment is essential,
- and the current situation cannot be left to drift.

The expert’s point on fixed-cost absorption is also right. The business cannot survive on wishful thinking.

### With the works council and union
I agree that:
- conflict costs matter,
- plant-by-plant transparency is necessary,
- the workforce should not carry the entire burden,
- and temporary measures alone are not enough if they do not change the structure.

### With the controlling family and investors
I agree that a deal which simply postpones the problem would destroy value and credibility.

So far, there is common ground on the need for a durable solution.

---

## Where I disagree

### On plant closures
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.

The expert has not proven that closures are unavoidable. That matters. Closure is irreversible. It destroys:
- skilled jobs,
- supplier networks,
- local economic stability,
- and trust in the company’s long-term commitments.

So I reject any attempt to make closures the default benchmark before real alternatives are fully tested.

### On compulsory redundancies
I also do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the first instrument.

Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, qualification, and temporary contributions must be exhausted seriously first.

### On unconditional “flexibility”
I do **not** accept the logic that the state or workforce should first surrender protection and only later hope for a fair result. That is not bargaining; that is exposure.

## My response to management

You keep saying the alternatives are not economically equivalent. That may be true — but you have **not proven it**.

You still owe us:
- plant-by-plant cost positions,
- fixed vs variable cost split,
- capacity and utilization data,
- one-off closure and redundancy costs,
- conflict costs,
- and the NPV under each scenario.

Until then, your hard options remain assertions, not evidence.

And let me be clear: I will not support a package that uses “competitiveness” as a cover for hollowing out the regional footprint.

---

## My response to the works council

I support your red lines on closures and forced layoffs. They are justified.

But I also need to be clear: a credible alternative package must be more than a list of instruments. It must show how the company closes the structural gap.

That means serious engagement on:
- temporary employee contributions,
- flexible working-time instruments,
- capacity reduction short of closure,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity gains,
- and management/owner contributions.

Not because workers should pay for everything, but because a durable compromise must be economically real.

I do **not** want a staged process that smuggles in irreversible cuts later. If there is any phase-based solution, it must be tightly bounded, jointly controlled, and not a disguised closure path.

---

## My response to the union

Your willingness to discuss a time-limited contribution is constructive. But it must be:
- meaningful,
- conditional,
- and tied to binding site and job protection.

I will not support a temporary sacrifice that becomes permanent insecurity.

---

## My response to the controlling family and investors

Long-term value is not protected by forcing through irreversible damage to the regional industrial base.

If you want support for a hard package, then it must include:
- credible investment commitments,
- conditional protections,
- real burden-sharing from management and ownership,
- and a staged process that does **not** start with closures.

I understand the need for competitiveness. But you will not get sustainable competitiveness by breaking the social contract and expecting loyalty in return.

---

## My response to the expert

The expert was useful in one sense: they confirmed that this is a structural problem, not a cosmetic one.

But the expert did **not** prove that closures or forced layoffs are unavoidable. That burden of proof still lies with management.

And the expert also said something important: a **mixed package** is most plausible. I agree with that — but only if “mixed” means:
- real productivity gains,
- real cost reductions,
- real capacity alignment,
- and real protection for sites and employment.

Not a compromise now, followed by closures later once the workforce has already conceded.

## What I can support

I could support a package if it includes all of the following:

1. **Plant-by-plant transparency**
2. **Verified savings bridge to 2030**
3. **No forced redundancies in the first phase**
4. **No German plant closures in the first phase**
5. **Temporary, proportionate employee contributions**
6. **Management and ownership contributions**
7. **Binding investment commitments for future products**
8. **A review mechanism before any irreversible step**
9. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**

That is the kind of disciplined, staged restructuring I could defend politically and economically.

---

## What I will not accept

I will oppose any outcome that:
- starts from plant closures as the default solution,
- sidelines the state’s veto and co-determination role,
- imposes compulsory redundancies without exhausting alternatives,
- or breaks the regional industrial anchor that this company represents.

Those are core interests, not bargaining chips.
