We are in broad agreement with the Works Council on the core line: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of the collective wage framework, and no unilateral management diktat**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade union side: if management’s answer to every problem is to push the pain onto employees while preserving strategic freedom, then we will not accept that. The workforce did not create the overcapacity alone, and it will not carry the burden alone.

### Where we agree
- **Sites must remain open.**
- **Forced layoffs are unacceptable.**
- **A credible restructuring must be socially responsible.**
- **The company needs a future strategy, not just cuts.**
- **Employees can contribute, but only as part of a fair package.**

### Where we draw the line
We are not prepared to sign off on a solution that:
- closes German plants,
- imposes compulsory redundancies,
- permanently weakens the collective bargaining framework,
- or turns “competitiveness” into a one-sided wage and job cut program.

If management wants flexibility, then it must also show restraint and take responsibility. That means:
- real job security commitments,
- alternatives to layoffs such as working-time reductions, redeployment, qualification, and natural attrition,
- a negotiated employee contribution that is time-limited and matched by contributions from management and owners,
- and binding investment commitments for future products and technologies.

### On the substance
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary labour-cost measures,
- reductions in capacity,
- voluntary exit options,
- working-time instruments,
- and a shared-sacrifice package,

but only if these are tied to **site security and protection against forced redundancies**. That is the minimum basis for serious negotiations.

### What will happen if management refuses
If the company insists on closures and compulsory layoffs, then it is choosing confrontation. The peace obligation has ended. We have already shown with warning strikes that the workforce is prepared to act. We will use that leverage again if needed.

### Our bottom line
A durable settlement is possible, but only on a fair basis:
- **workers contribute**, yes,
- **management and owners contribute as well**,
- **jobs and sites are protected**,
- and **the collective agreement remains the backbone** of the solution.

If management wants us to help secure the company’s future, it must stop treating the workforce as the cost item of last resort.
