I will be equally direct.

## My position

I support a **durable restructuring**. I do **not** support a restructuring that treats German plants and regional employment as the default adjustment variable.  
As the regional state and anchor shareholder, my core test is simple:

- **Does this preserve the company as a regional industrial anchor?**
- **Does it protect jobs and sites in the state?**
- **Does it maintain co-determination and our veto role?**
- **Does it restore competitiveness without irreversible regional damage?**

If the answer to any of these is no, then I cannot support it.

---

## Where I agree

### With management
I agree that:
- the current situation is not sustainable,
- the company needs real savings,
- future products and technology investment are essential,
- and a cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.

The expert also confirmed the key point: **the issue is structural, especially fixed-cost absorption and capacity utilization**. So yes, we need hard numbers and a credible bridge to 2030.

### With the works council and union
I agree that:
- jobs matter,
- sites matter,
- trust matters,
- conflict costs matter,
- and the workforce should not be asked to carry the entire burden.

You are right to insist that any contribution from employees must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**, not a one-sided cut.

### With the controlling family and investors
I agree that we cannot freeze a structurally weak setup and call it stewardship.  
A weak deal that postpones the problem would hurt everyone, including the region.

---

## Where I disagree

### On plant closures
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.

The expert was careful, and that matters: **closures may be economically justified in some scenarios, but they are not yet proven to be necessary** on the information presented. That is an important distinction.

Closing a plant is irreversible. It destroys:
- skilled jobs,
- supplier networks,
- local economic stability,
- and trust in the company’s long-term commitments.

So I reject any attempt to make closures the default benchmark before genuine alternatives are fully tested.

### On compulsory redundancies
I also do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as the first instrument.

Voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, qualification, and temporary contributions must be used first and seriously. If management says those are insufficient, it must prove that with plant-level economics, not with general claims.

### On “economically equivalent”
Management keeps using this phrase, but no one has yet shown that closures and layoffs are the only way to reach it.  
The expert explicitly said a **mixed package is the most plausible outcome**. That is where I see the opening: not in unconditional cuts, but in a negotiated package that combines several instruments while protecting the industrial base.

---

## My response to each party

### To management
You are right to demand quantified savings. But you still owe us a **plant-by-plant case**.

I want to see:
- current cost position by site,
- target cost position,
- fixed versus variable cost split,
- savings from working-time, voluntary exits, productivity and overhead reduction,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- conflict costs,
- and the NPV under each scenario.

Until then, your hard options remain assertions, not proof.

And let me be very clear: I will not back a package that uses the language of competitiveness to hollow out the company’s regional footprint.

### To the works council
Your red lines are understandable, and I share most of them.  
But if you want our blocking role to hold, we must be able to show that there is a credible alternative package, not just a refusal.

That means I need you to engage seriously on:
- temporary employee contributions,
- flexible working-time instruments,
- capacity reduction short of closure,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity gains,
- and management/owner contributions.

Not because the workforce should pay for everything, but because a durable compromise must be economically real.

### To the union
Your willingness to discuss a time-limited contribution is constructive.  
But it must be:
- meaningful,
- conditional,
- and tied to binding site and job protections.

I will not support a “temporary sacrifice” that turns into a permanent weakening of the workforce’s position.

### To the controlling family and investors
I hear your insistence on long-term value.  
But long-term value is not created by forcing through irreversible damage to the regional industrial base.

If you want my support for a hard package, then it must include:
- credible investment commitments,
- conditional protections,
- real burden-sharing from management and ownership,
- and a staged process before any irreversible step is taken.

---

## My line on the expert’s findings

The expert’s assessment is useful because it confirms something important:  
**We do not yet have enough information to conclude that closures or forced layoffs are unavoidable.**

That means the burden of proof remains with management.

The expert also said a mixed package is most plausible. I agree — but only if “mixed” means:
- real productivity gains,
- real cost reductions,
- real capacity alignment,
- and real protection for sites and employment.

Not a compromise now, followed by closures later once the workforce has already given concessions.

---

## What I can support

I could support a package if it includes all of the following:

1. **Plant-by-plant transparency**
2. **Verified savings bridge to 2030**
3. **No forced redundancies in the first phase**
4. **No German plant closures in the first phase**
5. **Temporary, proportionate employee contributions**
6. **Management and ownership contributions**
7. **Binding investment commitments for future products**
8. **A review mechanism before any irreversible step**
9. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**

That is the kind of disciplined, staged restructuring I could defend politically and economically.

---

## My red lines

I will oppose any outcome that:
- starts from plant closures as the default solution,
- sidelines the state’s veto and co-determination role,
- imposes compulsory redundancies without exhausting alternatives,
- or breaks the regional industrial anchor that this company represents.

Those are not bargaining points for me. They are core interests.

I am prepared to help broker a hard solution.

If not, I will use the rights and leverage the state has to block an outcome that is unacceptable for the region.
