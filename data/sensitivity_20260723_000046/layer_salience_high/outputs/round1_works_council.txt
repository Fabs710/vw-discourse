As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework**
- **No unilateral fait accompli from management**

We are prepared to discuss a restructuring **only if it is built on binding guarantees** for jobs, sites, and co-determination through **2030**, and only if management is willing to pursue alternatives that protect the workforce instead of sacrificing it.

### What we will argue for
- **Keeping every German plant open**
- **Securing employment through socially responsible measures**
- **Capacity adjustments through working-time instruments, internal redeployment, qualification, and voluntary solutions**
- **A genuine industrial strategy with investment, future products, and a credible plan for long-term site security**
- **A negotiated package in which the workforce contributes, but not by paying for management mistakes with closures and layoffs**

### What we will not accept
- Plant closures under the pretext of “competitiveness”
- Forced job cuts
- Permanent erosion of pay, standards, or guarantees without reciprocal security commitments
- Any restructuring that treats workers as the adjustment variable while management keeps strategic freedom

Management lost trust when it moved against the historic job guarantees. That matters. If the company wants cooperation, it must first restore credibility with **binding commitments**, not slogans about urgency. We understand the pressure on the business, but we will not allow the burden to be shifted onto the workforce alone.

If management wants a viable path forward, it must come to the table with a plan that protects sites, protects jobs, and gives employees a real say in shaping the future. Otherwise, we will resist this restructuring with all the means available to us.
