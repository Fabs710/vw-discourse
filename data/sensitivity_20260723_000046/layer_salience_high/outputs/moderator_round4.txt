## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the means, timing, and reversibility** of the restructuring:

1. **Management / investors / family** want a package that produces **durable, verified savings**, and they insist that **closures and compulsory redundancies remain available** if the numbers require them.
2. **Works council / union** insist that the restructuring must be achieved **without German plant closures, without compulsory redundancies, and without weakening the job-security / co-determination framework**.
3. **Regional state** wants to preserve the industrial footprint and its governance role, and is unwilling to endorse irreversible regional damage.
4. **The expert** has clarified that the decisive test is a **plant-by-plant, risk-adjusted NPV comparison**, but that data has not yet been jointly accepted as the basis for a settlement.

So the impasse is:  
**one side demands optionality for hard measures; the other side demands binding protection against them.**  
Without a shared decision basis, each side treats the other’s proposal as either too risky or too restrictive.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Joint audit first, then binding decision rules
A jointly appointed, independent plant-level auditor is commissioned immediately, with access to internal cost, utilization, product allocation, one-off cost, and conflict-cost data.

**Compromise features:**
- all parties agree in advance on the audit scope
- the auditor produces a plant-by-plant savings bridge and 3–5 year risk-adjusted NPV comparison
- the parties pre-agree what thresholds would justify:
  - no closure restructuring,
  - limited consolidation,
  - or more intrusive measures
- no irreversible step is taken before the audit is complete

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives management and investors the evidence standard they want, while giving labor and the state a jointly controlled process rather than unilateral closure logic.

---

### Proposal 2: Two-phase restructuring with hard legal guardrails
A first phase is implemented immediately using **non-destructive tools only**:
- working-time changes
- voluntary exits
- redeployment
- qualification
- management / owner contribution
- bonus restraint
- product reallocation

This phase is paired with **binding guardrails**, such as:
- no compulsory redundancies in Phase 1
- no plant closures in Phase 1
- no unilateral activation of closure options
- a fixed review date and jointly defined performance metrics

A second phase can only be considered if agreed metrics are missed and only after a joint review.

**Compromise features:**
- gives management immediate operational relief
- gives workers and the state protection against a disguised closure path
- keeps hard measures contingent, not automatic

---

### Proposal 3: Conditional protection in exchange for quantified burden-sharing
A settlement is built around:
- **time-limited job and site protection**
- **employee contribution**
- **management overhead cuts**
- **owner capital contribution / investment commitment**
- **binding productivity and utilization targets**

The protection is not absolute, but neither is the restructuring mandate blank-check.  
Any later escalation would require:
- jointly audited failure against metrics
- renewed bargaining
- and a defined escalation ladder

**Compromise features:**  
This can bridge the divide between “no closures ever” and “closures are on the table now,” by making protection conditional on a real, audited package rather than on trust alone.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact plant-level savings gap remains after excluding closures and compulsory redundancies, and can you show that gap in a jointly auditable bridge through 2030?**

### To the works council
**Which quantified, time-bound concessions would you accept if the audit shows that a non-closure package closes most, but not all, of the structural gap?**

### To the union
**Would you support a binding first-phase package with temporary employee contributions if it includes hard, enforceable no-closure and no-forced-layoff protections for a defined period?**

### To the regional state
**What specific conditions would make you support a staged restructuring without immediate closures, while still preserving your ability to block irreversible damage later?**

### To the controlling family
**Are you prepared to accept a joint audit and a conditional no-closure settlement if it produces verified savings, even if it limits your ability to keep closures as a governance fallback?**

### To institutional investors
**What minimum risk-adjusted NPV improvement would make you support a negotiated restructuring that avoids closures, and what one-off / conflict-cost assumptions must be included for you to regard the analysis as credible?**

### To the expert
**What exact threshold in fixed-cost reduction, utilization improvement, and conflict-cost exposure would allow you to classify a no-closure package as economically defensible rather than insufficient?**
