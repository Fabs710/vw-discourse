### Core impasse

The plateau is not about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **what counts as an acceptable form of restructuring**.

More specifically, the parties are split on four linked questions:

1. **Evidence threshold**
   - Management and capital providers want proof that alternatives can match the economics.
   - Works council and union want proof that closures and forced redundancies are truly necessary.

2. **Irreversibility**
   - Employee-side actors reject irreversible measures now.
   - Management and owners want options kept open if economics require them.

3. **Burden-sharing**
   - All sides say sacrifice must be shared.
   - They disagree on whether worker-side concessions can be paired with binding job/site guarantees.

4. **Sequence**
   - Employee-side actors want guarantees first, then restructuring.
   - Management-side actors want restructuring flexibility first, then commitments.

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise

#### Proposal 1: Staged restructuring with trigger-based escalation
A compromise could be structured in two phases:

- **Phase 1:** No immediate plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.
- **Phase 2:** If agreed financial targets are missed after a defined review period, then a pre-agreed escalation menu becomes available.

This bridges:
- the **works council/union** demand for immediate protection,
- the **management/investor** demand for a credible adjustment mechanism,
- and the **state/family** desire to avoid premature irreversibility.

Key feature: any escalation would require **jointly verified plant-level metrics**.

---

#### Proposal 2: Binding protection package paired with quantified workforce contribution
A second compromise could combine:

- binding site and job protections for a defined period,
- a time-limited employee contribution,
- management overhead reductions,
- owner/investor burden-sharing,
- and a transparent savings bridge by plant.

This bridges:
- the employee-side demand for security,
- the management-side demand for measurable savings,
- and the investor demand for durability.

Key feature: concessions on labor cost would be matched by **equally explicit commitments from management and owners**.

---

#### Proposal 3: Mixed restructuring without default closures, but with conditional plant review
A third compromise could agree that:

- closures are **not the default solution**,
- each German site is reviewed individually,
- the company must first exhaust redeployment, working-time, voluntary exits, and overhead cuts,
- but if a site cannot meet agreed economic thresholds, it enters a jointly governed review process.

This bridges:
- the works council/union refusal of blanket closure logic,
- management’s insistence on economic realism,
- and the expert’s point that the solution is likely mixed, not binary.

Key feature: **no automatic closure, but no automatic immunity either**.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If plant closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table, what specific quantified savings and capacity reductions would your alternative package deliver at each site, and by when?

#### To the Trade Union
What level of time-limited workforce contribution would you regard as acceptable, and what binding protections would have to accompany it for members to support it?

#### To Group & Brand Management
Which plant-level figures would you accept as sufficient proof that alternatives are inadequate, and are you prepared to subject your preferred route to joint verification before any irreversible step?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete conditions would you require before using your blocking role, and are you willing to conditionally support a staged process if it preserves sites in the first phase?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to back a package that preserves the industrial base even if it delivers less immediate simplification, provided it shows a credible path to sustainable returns?

#### To the Institutional Investors
Would you support a mixed package with delayed structural measures if it produces a verified, risk-adjusted NPV improvement and includes clear escalation triggers?

#### To the Expert
What minimum plant-level metrics would you require to distinguish between a viable negotiated restructuring and a case where consolidation becomes economically unavoidable?
