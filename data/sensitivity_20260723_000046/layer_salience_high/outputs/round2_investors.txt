We appreciate the expert’s intervention because it clarifies the central point: **so far, no side has proven its case with plant-level economics**. That matters. But it does **not** change our core position.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the controlling family, and the expert on one thing: **this cannot be a cosmetic settlement**.

A deal that:
- preserves structural overcapacity,
- leaves fixed costs underabsorbed,
- and only delays the margin problem

would be value-destructive. We would not support that.

We also agree with the expert that:
- the question is **not** ideology,
- it is **NPV, durable savings, and implementation certainty**,
- and the answer must be tested plant by plant.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the employee side’s repeated claim that closures and compulsory redundancies are unnecessary simply because alternatives are named.

Saying “working time, redeployment, voluntary exits, and productivity improvements” is not the same as showing those measures close the savings gap. The expert is right: **they may help, but they may not be enough**.

At the same time, we also do **not** accept management’s implication that closures are automatically the cleanest or best answer. If closures trigger prolonged industrial conflict, supplier disruption, and reputational damage, then they can destroy value as well as create it.

So our position is narrower and more demanding:
- **prove the savings**
- **prove the durability**
- **prove the execution path**
- **prove the net value case**

## What the expert confirmed
The expert basically confirmed our financial concern:
- the real issue is **fixed-cost absorption**,
- and if capacity remains too high, soft measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient.

That supports our red line: **no settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive**.

But the expert also confirmed something else important:
- a **mixed package** may be the most plausible economically,
- not an all-or-nothing choice.

That means we are open to alternatives **only if** they are real, quantified, and durable.

## Our response to each party

### To the Works Council
Your position is understandable, but your minimum package is not yet an investable solution.

A binding guarantee through 2030, with no compulsory redundancies and no closures, is not acceptable **unless** it is matched by a quantified and durable savings bridge that closes the structural gap. Right now, you have not shown that.

If your counterproposal cannot demonstrate:
- exact recurring savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- and a credible route back to target margins,

then it is not a serious alternative to restructuring. It is a demand to preserve the current structure and hope the problem fades.

We will not support that.

### To the Trade Union
Your willingness to discuss time-limited contributions is more constructive. But the same issue remains: **the contribution must be big enough and durable enough**.

Temporary wage moderation or bonus restraint may help cash flow. It does not automatically solve:
- excess installed capacity,
- weak fixed-cost absorption,
- or persistent margin underperformance.

If the package is mostly symbolic, we would oppose it.

### To Management
You have the stronger diagnosis, but you still owe the market a more disciplined case.

“Arithmetic” is not enough as a slogan. We need:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- the exact savings gap,
- scenario comparison between closure, consolidation, and negotiated adjustment,
- and the full cost of conflict.

You are right that hard decisions may be necessary. But if you want capital-market support, you need to show the **relative value case**, not just the urgency case.

### To the Regional State
We respect the need for social stability and regional industrial capacity. But stability is not the same as viability.

If the state blocks every meaningful adjustment, it may preserve jobs in the short run while weakening the company’s ability to fund future investment. That is not a good outcome for shareholders, workers, or the region.

Your role should be to insist on:
- credible alternatives,
- conditional protections,
- and real performance discipline,

not to rule out all hard choices in advance.

### To the Controlling Family
We agree that no cosmetic compromise is acceptable. We also agree that unconditional guarantees are not the answer.

But “some sites, some capacity, and some jobs may have to go” is only acceptable if management demonstrates that it is the **least value-destructive** route. We are not buying the notion that severity alone proves discipline.

If the family wants investor support, it should push for:
- hard numbers,
- clear milestones,
- conditional protections,
- and a package that is strong enough to restore returns without creating open-ended conflict.

## What would move us
We could support a package if it includes all of the following:

1. **Verified plant-level savings**
2. **Durable capacity reduction**
3. **Recurring labour-cost savings**
4. **A credible implementation timetable**
5. **Clear margin improvement**
6. **A conflict-risk assessment**
7. **Conditional, not unconditional, site/job protections**

## What would not move us
We would not support:
- a cosmetic no-closure compromise with no fixed-cost solution,
- an open-ended strike settlement that just buys peace,
- or a restructuring that damages margins structurally.

## Our practical request
Before anyone asks shareholders to back a final deal, we want a **decision matrix** by plant showing:
- current cost position,
- savings from alternative measures,
- savings from consolidation or closure,
- one-off costs,
- conflict costs,
- and the NPV outcome over 3–5 years.

Without that, the debate remains underpowered.

## Bottom line
Our position is unchanged, but sharpened by the expert’s analysis:

- **We will back deep restructuring.**
- **We will not back cosmetic compromise.**
- **We are open to a mixed package if it is economically equivalent and durable.**
- **We will oppose any outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.**

So: prove the numbers, prove the durability, and prove the value case. Otherwise, capital will not be patient.
