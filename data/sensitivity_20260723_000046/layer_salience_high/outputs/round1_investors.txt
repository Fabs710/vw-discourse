From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the test is simple: **does the package credibly restore competitive margins and protect long-term equity value?** If yes, we can support it. If not, we will not back a cosmetic settlement that preserves structural overcapacity and delays the pain.

### Where we agree
We agree with management and the controlling family on the core diagnosis:

- the current cost base is too high,
- the German capacity footprint is too large for demand,
- and the return-on-sales gap is a real problem, not a communications issue.

We also agree that **the company needs durable savings, not a temporary truce**. A deal that simply buys labor peace while leaving the economics unchanged would be value-destructive. We would treat that as failure, not compromise.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the idea that the only credible route is immediate plant closures and compulsory redundancies.

Not because we are sentimentally attached to every site, but because from a shareholder point of view the key question is **net value creation**. If there is a restructuring path that delivers equivalent savings through capacity reduction, working-time changes, productivity gains, voluntary exits, and negotiated labour-cost contributions, then that is preferable to a protracted conflict that damages cash flow, brand execution, and investor confidence.

So we are **not aligned with the works council or union on preserving every plant and every guarantee regardless of economics**. That would be structurally uncompetitive and therefore unacceptable to us.

But we are also **not giving management a blank cheque for closures** if the result is a long, expensive confrontation, execution risk, and reputational damage without a clean savings case. A tough restructuring only works if it is **credible, enforceable, and fast**.

### Our red lines
We will not support any outcome that:

- leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- fails to deliver durable margin improvement,
- relies on symbolic savings,
- or postpones restructuring into the next cycle.

A deal with nice language but no hard savings is not investable.

### What we would support
We would support a package that has all of the following:

- **clear quantified cost savings**
- **durable capacity reduction**
- **a realistic timeline**
- **a credible workforce contribution**
- **protection against endless conflict**
- **visible improvement in medium-term margins**

That could include a combination of:
- site rationalisation,
- voluntary redundancy programs,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labour-cost measures,
- productivity commitments,
- and targeted investment tied to performance milestones.

What matters is not the instrument but the outcome.

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no erosion of guarantees is understandable from a labor perspective, but from a shareholder perspective it is only acceptable if you can show an alternative that matches the economics. If you cannot, then you are asking investors to finance continued underperformance.

### To management
We share your concern about cosmetic settlements, but you will not win investor support by framing every objection as resistance to reform. If you want capital markets to stay patient, you need to present:

- a quantified bridge to competitive margins,
- a credible implementation path,
- and proof that the plan is not just a strike-management exercise.

### To the controlling family and state
We hear your concern about social stability and co-determination. Fine. But social stability cannot become a cover for permanent underperformance. If the company’s value erodes further, all stakeholders lose. The ownership bloc should not confuse preserving governance harmony with preserving shareholder value.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to back a tough restructuring, but only if it is **real**.

- **No cosmetic settlement.**
- **No structural impairment of margins.**
- **No open-ended conflict.**
- **Yes to a credible, durable, measurable turnaround.**

If the final package does not clearly restore competitiveness, we will oppose it through capital-market pressure, analyst scrutiny, and our allocation choices.
