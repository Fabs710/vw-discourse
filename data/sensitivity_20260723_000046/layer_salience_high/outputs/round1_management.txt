Let me be direct.

I **do** agree on one point: the company needs a future strategy, not just symbolic cuts. But that is exactly why I cannot accept a package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

### Where I agree
- We need investment in future products and technology.
- We need a durable industrial strategy, not a temporary patch.
- A workforce contribution can be part of the solution.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to endless escalation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the premise that we can preserve everything and still restore competitiveness.

- **No closures at any German site** is not credible as a blanket position.
- **No compulsory redundancies** may sound socially desirable, but if it blocks any meaningful structural adjustment, it becomes a refusal to solve the crisis.
- **No changes to the job-security framework** is not compatible with the situation we are in.

The problem is not rhetoric; it is arithmetic. German factory costs are too high, capacity is too high relative to demand, and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target. If we only rely on voluntary attrition, internal redeployment, and working-time tweaks, we will not get the scale of savings needed.

### What is not acceptable to management
I am not here to negotiate a cosmetic agreement that preserves excess capacity and pushes the real adjustment into the future. That would damage the company further, undermine investor confidence, and leave us unable to fund the next product cycle.

### What I am prepared to consider
If the employee side wants to avoid plant closures and forced layoffs, then it must put forward a **credible, measurable, durable** alternative that achieves the same economic effect. That means:
- substantial and lasting labour-cost contribution,
- real capacity reduction,
- binding productivity improvements,
- and a package that restores the brand’s medium-term margins.

If those conditions are met, I am open on the **form** of the adjustment. I am not open on the need for structural savings.

### On guarantees
I will not give a blanket 2030 site guarantee in exchange for nothing material. That would simply lock in the current cost problem. Any security commitment must be conditional on a settlement that actually fixes the economics.

So my position is this:  
**We need deep, durable restructuring.**  
If the workforce and union want to avoid closures and compulsory redundancies, they need to help deliver a solution that is economically equivalent—not just politically more comfortable.
