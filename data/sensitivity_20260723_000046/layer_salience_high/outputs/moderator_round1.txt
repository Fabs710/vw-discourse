1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties accept that the company needs a **durable industrial and restructuring strategy**, not just symbolic cuts.
- There is broad agreement that the company needs:
  - **investment in future products and technology**
  - **credible medium-term competitiveness and margin improvement**
  - **a negotiated settlement rather than open-ended confrontation**
- There is also shared openness, in different forms, to discussing:
  - **working-time instruments**
  - **voluntary exit options**
  - **redeployment and qualification**
  - **productivity improvements**
  - **some form of workforce contribution**, if it is part of a broader package

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and state reject closures as the starting point and want sites protected.
  - Management, owners, and investors will not rule out closures if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs**
  - Employee side and state oppose them.
  - Management, owners, and investors say they remain possible if no equivalent alternative is delivered.
- **Job-security framework and guarantees**
  - Employee side seeks binding protection through 2030.
  - Management and owners reject unconditional long-term guarantees.
  - Investors want guarantees only if tied to measurable restructuring outcomes.
- **How to achieve savings**
  - Employee side wants alternatives that avoid closures and layoffs.
  - Management, owners, and investors insist the savings must be **deep, durable, and economically equivalent**, not merely postponed.
- **Role of co-determination and political constraints**
  - Works Council, union, and state want co-determination and social protection preserved.
  - Management, owners, and investors prioritize flexibility and rapid structural change if needed.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- No agreed **quantified target** yet for:
  - cost savings
  - capacity reduction
  - headcount impact
  - investment commitments
- No shared framework on **what counts as an “economically equivalent” alternative** to closures and layoffs.
- No clear distribution of burden between:
  - workforce
  - management
  - owners
  - regional/public stakeholders
- No settled answer on whether any job/security commitments would be:
  - **conditional**
  - **time-limited**
  - **site-specific**
  - or **performance-linked**
- No common basis yet for balancing:
  - **social stability and site security**
  - against
  - **speed, scale, and certainty of restructuring**

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to **numbers and conditions**:
  - What exact savings are required?
  - What exact alternatives can match them?
  - What protections can be made conditional, time-limited, or performance-based?
- Ask each side to table:
  - one **minimum acceptable package**
  - one **fallback option**
  - one **non-negotiable red line**
- Focus the next round on designing a package that is:
  - **socially acceptable enough to be implemented**
  - and **economically credible enough to satisfy management, owners, and investors**
