I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state, and even parts of management and the investors on one basic fact: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a temporary patch**.

I also agree that labour cannot be the only side contributing. If the company needs relief, then management, owners, and shareholders must also make material sacrifices. That is fair, and it is the only basis on which I am prepared to negotiate seriously.

I further agree with the expert that a capital-intensive auto business with too much fixed cost and too much overcapacity usually needs structural adjustment. I am not denying the economics.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the move being made here by management, the family holding, and investors: treating **plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default solution** and then asking the workforce to prove a perfect alternative.

That is backwards.

If you want the union to consider concessions, then first you must show a credible restructuring path that does **not** force people out of their jobs and does **not** destroy the collective wage framework. Until then, closures and compulsory layoffs remain unacceptable.

## My non-negotiables
I will say them plainly again:

1. **No compulsory redundancies.**
2. **No plant closures as part of this settlement.**
3. **Protection of the collective wage agreement and collective bargaining framework.**
4. **No one-sided sacrifice by the workforce.**

Those are not tactical talking points. They are the line.

## What I am prepared to put on the table
I have already signaled openness to a fair contribution, and that remains true. If the package is balanced, we can discuss:

- working-time adjustments,
- temporary or structural flexibility,
- capacity reductions without closures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- partial retirement,
- and an employee financial contribution if it is part of a broader shared-sacrifice settlement.

But let me be equally clear: **employees will not pay for the restructuring alone**. Any contribution from labour must be matched by real contributions from:
- management,
- owners,
- and the company’s own cost base and capital allocation.

That means:
- executive restraint,
- management overhead cuts,
- procurement and central-function savings,
- and binding investment commitments for the German plants.

## On the expert’s intervention
The expert was useful in one respect: they confirmed that the real issue is whether alternative measures can close the structural gap. Exactly.

That is why the next step is not to brand closures as “inevitable,” but to present a **plant-by-plant business case** showing:
- utilisation,
- fixed-cost gaps,
- alternative savings,
- and what exact residual gap remains after non-closure measures.

If management cannot show that transparently, then there is no basis for demanding closures or compulsory cuts.

And if management does show a gap, then the question becomes: **what are management, owners, and investors prepared to contribute to close it without breaking the workforce?**

## To management
Your argument has one serious weakness: you keep asserting that the footprint must change, but you have not yet demonstrated that the only way to achieve sufficient savings is to shut plants or force people out.

You say the cost base is too high. Fine. Then cut the cost base:
- reduce overhead,
- reduce executive and administrative burden,
- improve procurement,
- rebalance product allocation,
- improve capacity utilisation,
- and negotiate labour flexibility.

If you still believe more is needed, then bring us the numbers. But do not start by demanding that workers absorb the pain through closures and layoffs.

## To the Works Council
I support your refusal to accept forced redundancies and plant closures. That is the correct line.

At the same time, I want to be careful not to confuse defensiveness with strategy. If we can secure:
- jobs,
- sites,
- collective bargaining,
- and a durable industrial future,

then we should be willing to consider flexibility on instruments. But not on the core red lines.

## To the Regional State Government
You are right that the company matters for the region and for industrial capability. Use your leverage accordingly.

That means: do not let “competitiveness” become a code word for transferring the entire adjustment burden onto workers. If you want stability, then insist on a settlement that preserves jobs, sites, and co-determination.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
If you want long-term stewardship, then prove it with long-term choices. Stewardship means:
- protecting the industrial base,
- sharing the burden,
- and not treating the workforce as the easiest adjustment variable.

If the family wants labour peace, it needs to back a fair deal — not just call for discipline from below.

## To Investors
You are right that a fake compromise would destroy value. I agree.

But you are wrong if you think the only value-preserving route is to force closures or accept mass job cuts. A deal that tears up trust, triggers prolonged conflict, and damages industrial capability may look clean on paper and still be value-destructive.

## About the moderator’s compromise paths
- **Phased restructuring with review trigger:** I am not interested in a trap that postpones closures until later. If a review is just a way to reopen the same confrontation after the workforce has disarmed, then no.
- **Hybrid footprint reduction:** If this is a euphemism for closures, then no.
- **No closures but equivalent savings:** That is the only route I will seriously engage with, provided the savings are real and fairly shared.

## Bottom line
I am willing to help solve the crisis. I am **not** willing to sign up to a settlement where the workforce carries the cost through closures, compulsory layoffs, and the erosion of collective bargaining.

If management wants agreement, it must bring a credible no-closure, no-forced-redundancy package with real contributions from all sides.

If it refuses, then we will use the tools available to us.
