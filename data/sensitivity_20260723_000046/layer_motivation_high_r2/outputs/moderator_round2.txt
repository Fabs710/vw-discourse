### Core impasse
The deadlock is not over the need for restructuring; nearly everyone accepts that the company needs durable cost relief. The impasse is over **which instruments may be used**:

- **Works Council / Union / Regional State:** insist that restructuring must not include German plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and must preserve co-determination, site security, and job guarantees.
- **Management / Investors / Controlling Family:** insist that a credible turnaround likely requires real capacity reduction, structural footprint change, and potentially closures or compulsory measures if alternatives do not close the cost gap.

So the central conflict is:

**How can the company achieve enough durable savings to restore competitiveness without crossing the stakeholders’ red lines on closures, layoffs, and legacy guarantees?**

---

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise
Below are three structured compromise paths that could bridge the most conflicting positions. These are not endorsements, just options for further bargaining.

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a review trigger
- Agree now on a **time-limited protection period** for German plants and no forced redundancies during that period.
- In parallel, implement **immediate cost measures**: working-time adjustments, overtime reductions, voluntary exits, redeployment, training, and management overhead cuts.
- Set a **binding review point** after agreed milestones and audited savings data.
- If the savings target is not met, a second-stage negotiation opens on more difficult measures, potentially including footprint changes.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It gives labour protection upfront while giving management and investors a credible path to deeper adjustment if the softer tools prove insufficient.

#### 2) Hybrid footprint reduction with job-security buffer
- Identify only the **most clearly underutilised or structurally weak lines/sites**, rather than opening all sites at once.
- Combine any footprint reduction with:
  - voluntary exits only,
  - strong internal redeployment,
  - extended transfer support,
  - retraining,
  - partial retirement,
  - and a no-forced-redundancy buffer for the majority of the workforce.
- Pair this with **binding investment and product commitments** for the remaining German plants.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It allows management and investors to obtain real structural savings while limiting the social damage and preserving most of the job-security architecture.

#### 3) “No closures, but equivalent savings” package with audited economics
- Commit to **no immediate plant closures**.
- Ask labour, management, owners, and suppliers to each deliver quantified contributions.
- Require management to publish a **plant-by-plant business case** showing:
  - utilization,
  - unit cost,
  - overhead burden,
  - and savings from alternative measures.
- If equivalent savings can be proven through labor flexibility, product allocation, procurement, and overhead cuts, closures are avoided.
- If not, all parties agree in advance to reopen the footprint question in a tightly defined process.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It respects the “prove alternatives first” demand while making the no-closure position conditional on actual economics rather than principle alone.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If the audited numbers show that the company still needs a substantial fixed-cost reduction, **what specific non-closure measures do you accept in advance as sufficient, and what verified threshold would trigger reconsideration of footprint changes?**

#### To the Industrial Union
If no forced redundancies are allowed, **what concrete wage, time, or flexibility contributions are you prepared to put on the table, and for how long, to make the package economically credible?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
Before asking for closures or compulsory measures, **will you publish plant-level scenarios showing the savings from non-closure alternatives and the minimum savings gap that remains after those measures?**

#### To the Regional State Government
If the company presents a credible business case that avoids broad closures but needs limited restructuring, **what exact support can the state provide to secure investment, training, and labor peace without becoming a blocker to adjustment?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
If preserving the company’s long-term industrial core is the priority, **what owner-side capital, governance, or investment commitments are you prepared to make to help finance a no-forced-redundancy solution?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
If management delivers a restructuring with smaller direct savings but far lower execution risk, **what minimum economic metrics would you accept before rejecting it as insufficiently value-accretive?**

#### To the Expert Advisor
Based on your scenario modelling, **what is the smallest package of capacity reduction and labor-cost relief that would still plausibly close the structural gap, and what measures would be inadequate on their own?**
