I will be equally direct.

I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **this company does need a credible restructuring**. Pretending the current cost base and capacity can simply remain unchanged is not responsible. If the business keeps underperforming, that will ultimately threaten jobs, investment, and the regional industrial base.

But I **do not** accept the conclusion some of you are trying to force from that premise: that German plant closures and forced redundancies must remain the default answer.

### Where I agree
- **A cosmetic deal is not enough.**
- **Competitiveness matters.**
- **Labour cannot be the only side contributing.**
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to escalation and a strike spiral.**
- **The company needs room to invest in future products and technology.**

### Where I disagree
I disagree very clearly with the repeated framing that says:  
“either closures and compulsory redundancies, or decline.”

That is too narrow, and politically and industrially too dangerous. The company is not just a cost sheet. It is a regional anchor, a supplier network, and a co-determined industrial institution. If you tear out too much of the German footprint, you may improve a model on paper while damaging the ecosystem that makes long-term success possible.

### My non-negotiable line
I cannot support any package that:
- **guts regional employment,**
- **removes the state’s co-determination and veto role,**
- or treats German sites as disposable.

That is not a bargaining preference. That is a limit.

### What I could support
I am open to a serious package if it is built around:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- redeployment and training,
- voluntary exits where needed,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and binding investment and product commitments for German operations.

If savings are genuinely required, then they must be achieved in a way that **preserves the company’s industrial core in Germany**.

### To management
You say some sites are structurally uncompetitive. Fine — then show the full case, including alternatives that avoid closures. At this point I have not heard a convincing demonstration that the only path is plant shutdowns and compulsory cuts. That burden is still on management. Until that is shown, I will not treat closures as justified.

And let me be clear: if the restructuring is designed in a way that risks permanent damage to the company’s regional legitimacy and labor peace, then it will be harder, not easier, to implement.

### To the Works Council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce must not carry the whole burden. That is legitimate. But I also want a solution that is durable, not just defensive. If alternatives can preserve jobs and sites while still producing meaningful savings, they should be on the table. What I will not support is turning every discussion into a total blockade with no room for a negotiated industrial future.

### To the family holding
Long-term stewardship means more than saying the company must be “competitive.” It means accepting responsibility for preserving the company’s role as a regional economic anchor. If restructuring becomes a vehicle for hollowing out the domestic base, then stewardship has failed its test.

### To investors
You are right to demand measurable improvements. But you do not get a blank check to demand the fastest numerical fix if it undermines the company’s long-term social and industrial foundation. A deal that tears up co-determination, destroys trust, and destabilizes the core sites may satisfy a quarterly narrative and still be bad ownership.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to support a **balanced restructuring** that:
- restores competitiveness,
- preserves as many jobs and sites as possible,
- respects co-determination,
- and keeps the company anchored in the region.

I am **not** prepared to support a package whose real logic is plant closure by pressure and workforce reduction by ultimatum.

If management wants my support, it must bring forward a concrete plan that shows:
1. which savings are needed,
2. how they can be achieved without destroying the regional base,
3. what management, owners, and investors are contributing,
4. and how German sites remain viable beyond this crisis.

Until then, I remain firmly opposed to any solution that treats closures as unavoidable and the state’s role as negotiable.
