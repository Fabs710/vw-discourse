I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally direct, from the perspective of the controlling family holding:

We are **not indifferent** to employment, sites, or the company’s regional footprint. Quite the opposite: as long-term owners, our interest is the preservation and strengthening of the enterprise over generations, not a short-term extraction of value. That said, I will not support a posture that treats every structural adjustment as illegitimate. If competitiveness is not restored, then the company will bleed value, and that ultimately endangers jobs, sites, and the ownership position itself.

So my position is this:

### Where I agree
- **The company needs a credible turnaround.**  
  Management is right that the current cost base and capacity cannot remain untouched if the business is to regain margin and investment capacity.

- **A negotiated solution is preferable to escalation.**  
  Prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value, damage reputation, and weaken the company’s ability to invest in the future.

- **A package should not place the burden solely on employees.**  
  If labour is asked to contribute, management, shareholders, and the wider governance structure must also show seriousness.

### Where I disagree
- **I do not accept a blanket “no plant closures ever” position.**  
  If certain sites are structurally uncompetitive, then refusing to address that reality is not protection; it is delay. Delay can become value destruction.

- **I also do not accept a simplistic closure-first strategy.**  
  Plant closures are a blunt instrument. They may be necessary in limited form, but only if they are part of a broader, durable package and not used as leverage for a quick fix.

- **I reject any outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise’s value or weakens governance stability.**  
  A restructuring that triggers uncontrolled conflict, reputational damage, or a governance breakdown would be unacceptable.

### What would be credible from my side
A settlement could be supported if it meets three tests:

1. **Real competitiveness gains**  
   The package must materially improve margins and lower structural costs.

2. **Durable labour peace**  
   We need stability, not a temporary truce that collapses after a few months.

3. **Protection of long-term industrial capacity**  
   This means preserving the company’s strategic core in Germany, with clear investment and product commitments.

### What that implies in practice
I would be open to a package built around:
- significant capacity reduction,
- voluntary exit schemes and socially responsible workforce adjustment,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- a negotiated labour-cost contribution,
- and binding investment commitments linked to a viable industrial footprint.

But I am **not** prepared to support a position that says management must prove competitiveness **without any structural change**. That is not realistic.

To the works council and union: your concern for job security is legitimate, but you cannot demand absolute guarantees while also expecting the company to absorb all the economic pain. If you want site security, you have to engage with the economic facts.

To management: you will not get support for a purely hardline approach that treats closures and layoffs as the only credible language. You need a package that can survive governance scrutiny and industrial resistance. If you bring forward something that looks like permanent value destruction dressed up as restructuring, I will not back it.

To the regional state government: I respect the state’s role and its blocking position. But that role must be exercised in the interest of the enterprise as a whole, not as a veto on every uncomfortable adjustment. Industrial continuity requires competitiveness first.

So my message is simple:  
**We are prepared to support a serious restructuring, but only one that is economically credible, socially responsible, and stable in governance terms.** Anything less would be a bad result for the company and for the family’s long-term stewardship.
