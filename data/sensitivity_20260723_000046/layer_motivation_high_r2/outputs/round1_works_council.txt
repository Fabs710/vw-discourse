We are in a crisis, but let me be absolutely clear from the outset: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures in Germany and no forced redundancies**.

The workforce has already shown that it will not quietly absorb the cost of management’s strategic mistakes. We reject any so-called “solution” that destroys jobs, breaks up sites, or tears up the historic job-security framework that has given this company stability for decades.

Our core priorities are straightforward:

1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **No operational layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees, at least through 2030.**
4. **Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring, not a fait accompli.**
5. **Any savings must come from alternatives**: capacity adjustments, internal efficiency, working-time solutions, redeployment, training, and a fair negotiated contribution from management, owners, and—if necessary—employees, but not through plant closures or firing people.

If management believes competitiveness requires a long-term restructuring, then it must prove that **without sacrificing German locations and without breaking the social contract**. We are prepared to discuss measures that secure the future of the company and the transition to new technology, but only on the basis of **security for the workforce and respect for co-determination**.

If the proposal remains a package of closures, compulsory layoffs, and unilateral cuts, then I will say plainly: **we will resist it with all the means available to us**.
