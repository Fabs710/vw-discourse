I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one basic point: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**.  
I also agree with the expert that the cost and capacity problem is likely genuine and that labour alone cannot be expected to carry the burden.

And I agree with the union and works council on one essential point: **a solution that destroys trust, industrial peace, and the company’s regional legitimacy will not be durable**.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the framing from management, the family holding, and investors that German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default answer and that everyone else must simply adapt to that.

That is too narrow, too blunt, and too risky for a company that is not just another listed asset, but a regional industrial anchor with a special governance structure and co-determination rights.

Equally, I do **not** accept a position from labour that treats “no closures” as a slogan without showing a credible economic path.  
If the company has a structural gap, then the answer cannot be wishful thinking. But the burden is also on management to prove, transparently and plant by plant, why non-closure measures are insufficient.

## My position
I remain firmly opposed to:
- **German plant closures as the starting point or default outcome,**
- **compulsory redundancies as an easy escape hatch,**
- **any restructuring that guts regional employment,**
- and **any solution that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role**.

Those are not negotiating preferences for me. They are limits.

At the same time, I am **not** defending stagnation. I am prepared to support a hard but socially managed restructuring if it:
- preserves the company’s industrial core in the state,
- protects as many jobs as possible,
- keeps sites viable,
- and restores competitiveness through a shared burden.

## On management’s argument
You say the economics force footprint reduction. Maybe. But you have not yet shown that the **only** credible route is closure and compulsory redundancy.

The expert was right to say the crucial question is whether the structural gap can be closed through a negotiated hybrid package. That is exactly where the discussion should be, not at the assumption that closures are inevitable.

So yes: bring the plant-by-plant business case.  
But do it honestly:
- utilization,
- fixed-cost gap,
- labour-cost per unit,
- overhead burden,
- procurement effects,
- capacity scenarios,
- and savings from alternatives.

If the numbers show a gap, then show why it cannot be bridged by:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- partial retirement,
- product allocation,
- overhead cuts,
- and management restraint.

Until that is done, I will not treat closures as justified.

## On the works council and union
I support your refusal to let the workforce become the sole shock absorber. That is legitimate, and politically and socially necessary.

I also support your insistence on:
- internal redeployment,
- training,
- flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- and fair contributions from all sides.

Where I would caution you is this: if you make “no closures” sound like a closed door in every conceivable scenario, you risk losing the credibility needed to force management into a real compromise. Your strongest argument is not ideological refusal — it is demanding proof that alternatives can work first.

That proof matters.

## What I would support
I could support a package built around:
- **no immediate German plant closures,**
- **no compulsory redundancies in the initial phase,**
- **capacity adjustments through shifts, working-time, and internal reallocation,**
- **voluntary exit instruments and partial retirement,**
- **management and owner contributions,**
- **investment and product commitments for German sites,**
- and **a binding review with independently audited economics**.

That is the kind of balanced solution that could preserve jobs and still create room for competitiveness.

## What I would not support
I will not support:
- a closure-first strategy,
- a plan that uses “competitiveness” as a euphemism for hollowing out German production,
- a deal that preserves shareholder optics while damaging the regional industrial base,
- or a settlement that turns co-determination into a decorative afterthought.

And I will be frank: if the final package is just a polite way of moving jobs away from the state and weakening the company’s anchor role, I will use the blocking rights we have.

## To management
If you want support, do not come with general warnings. Come with a quantified path that shows:
1. what savings are needed,
2. what alternatives were tested,
3. what management, owners, and investors contribute,
4. and why the remaining gap cannot be closed without breaking the social contract.

## To the family holding
If you are serious about stewardship, then this is the moment to prove it with more than statements about discipline. Stewardship means preserving industrial substance, not just financial neatness.

## To investors
You are right to demand durability. But durability is not served by a settlement that detonates labour relations and regional legitimacy. A deal that saves a little on paper but creates a lasting governance crisis is not value-accretive.

## To the state’s own role
I will be clear: I am not prepared to see the state’s veto and co-determination role eroded in exchange for a harsher restructuring. That would be unacceptable. Our role exists precisely to prevent the company from being treated as a disposable cost center.

## Bottom line
I am open to a hard, negotiated restructuring. I am **not** open to a deal that:
- closes plants in the state by pressure,
- shifts the burden almost entirely onto workers,
- or weakens the company’s regional anchor function.

If management wants a serious settlement, it must bring forward a **credible no-immediate-closure package** with real savings, real shared sacrifice, and a real future for German sites.

If it cannot, then it should not pretend that closure is the only responsible answer.
