As dispersed capital-market shareholders, we are listening for one thing above all: **a credible path back to competitive margins and durable cash generation**. On that test, I agree with management, and I disagree with the employee side and, so far, with any position that treats German restructuring as optional.

### Where I agree
- **The company has a structural cost problem.**  
  If the German cost base is above peers and return on sales is missing target, then preserving the status quo is not an option.

- **A negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged strike.**  
  Value destruction from extended industrial conflict is real. We do not want a cosmetic truce that leaves the economics unchanged, but we also do not want a long strike that burns cash and weakens the business.

- **Employees should not be the only contributors.**  
  If labour is asked to give ground, management, owners, and the wider governance structure should also show discipline.

### Where I disagree
- **I do not accept “no plant closures ever” as a serious starting point.**  
  If a site is structurally uncompetitive, keeping it open because it is politically difficult is not protection — it is a commitment to ongoing margin erosion.

- **I do not accept job and site guarantees that freeze the industrial footprint until 2030 if they prevent meaningful restructuring.**  
  Long guarantees have value only if the business behind them remains competitive. Otherwise they simply defer the problem and lower equity value.

- **I do not view a purely shared-sacrifice package as sufficient unless it materially changes the cost base.**  
  Fairness matters, but fairness without competitiveness is not a solution. We need measurable, durable savings.

### What would move us
We would support a package if it includes:
1. **Real and durable capacity reduction**
2. **Meaningful labour-cost reduction**
3. **Voluntary exits and redeployment where possible, but not an absolute veto on closures or compulsory adjustments**
4. **A credible implementation timeline**
5. **Clear evidence that margins will improve, not just stabilize**
6. **Investment commitments tied to a leaner, viable footprint**

### What would not move us
- A deal that avoids closures by substituting only temporary measures
- A settlement that preserves all sites but leaves structural overcapacity intact
- A strike-driven outcome that produces headlines but no margin repair
- A governance compromise that protects everyone’s optics while undermining the share price

To management: if you believe the structure must change, then bring forward a package that is **specific, enforceable, and financially quantifiable**.  
To the works council and union: if your answer is effectively “no closures, no forced redundancies, no fundamental change,” then you are asking investors to fund permanent underperformance. That is not acceptable.  
To the state and family owners: your role should be to ensure the restructuring is disciplined and credible, not to veto every hard decision.

Our bottom line is simple:  
**We will back the outcome that credibly restores competitiveness. If that requires plant closures or major capacity cuts, then so be it. If it does not, show us the numbers.** Without that, we will continue to treat the current setup as value-destructive.
