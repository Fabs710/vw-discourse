I agree with the Works Council on the core line: **plant closures and compulsory redundancies in Germany are not acceptable starting points**. If management wants a durable solution, it cannot be built on forcing workers to pay for strategic errors while owners and management keep their distance.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No closure of German plants as the default “solution.”**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
- **No one-sided sacrifice by the workforce.**

We have already entered warning strikes because the peace obligation has ended and our members expect us to act. That was not theatre; it was a signal that there is real resistance if management tries to ram through a bad package.

At the same time, I am not ignoring the business reality. If capacity is genuinely too high and costs must come down, then we are prepared to discuss **fair contributions from labour** — but only in a package that is balanced and gives something concrete back to employees. That means:

1. **Job and site security** for the duration of the agreement.
2. **No compulsory redundancies**; only voluntary, socially responsible instruments if workforce reduction is unavoidable.
3. **Capacity reduction through alternatives first**: working-time adjustments, redeployment, training, internal transfers, early retirement, and partial use of flexibility tools.
4. **Management and owners must contribute materially** — not just ask employees to give up pay, bonuses, or protections.
5. **A durable industrial strategy** with commitments on future products, investment, and production allocation in Germany.

Where I disagree with the Works Council is not on the protection of jobs, but on the risk of leaving the company with no acceptable path if the economic pressure is real. We should not pre-emptively close every door to negotiated adjustments. But any adjustment must be **shared-sacrifice**, not a disguised transfer of the crisis onto the workforce.

So my message to management and the shareholder side is simple:  
If you want peace, you need to bring a serious package that protects jobs and sites, respects collective bargaining, and shows that leadership and capital are also taking pain. Otherwise, expect escalated industrial action and a very difficult road to any agreement.
