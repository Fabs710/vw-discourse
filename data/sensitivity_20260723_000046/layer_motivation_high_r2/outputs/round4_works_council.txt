## My position remains unchanged
I do **not** accept:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or dismantling the job-security framework.

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are red lines. If management, investors, or the family believe repeating “competitiveness” makes those lines disappear, they are wrong.

---

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and, to a limited extent, with management and investors on one point:  
**the company needs real restructuring, not theatre.**

I also agree that labour cannot be the only side contributing. A fair solution must include:
- management cuts,
- owner contribution,
- investment commitments,
- and a serious industrial plan.

So no, I am not defending inertia. I am defending a **real** restructuring that does not sacrifice German sites and jobs.

---

## Where I disagree with management, investors, and the family
### 1) You still have not proven that closures are necessary
You keep treating footprint reduction as if it were self-evident. It is not.

If you want closures on the table, then show the numbers:
- plant-by-plant utilization,
- fixed-cost burden,
- alternative savings,
- model allocation,
- overhead cuts,
- procurement savings,
- and the exact residual gap.

Until then, closures are not a necessity. They are a management choice.

### 2) “No closure” is not “no restructuring”
This keeps being misstated.

We are prepared for hard restructuring through:
- working-time flexibility,
- shift adjustments,
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- partial retirement,
- capacity reduction **without closures**,
- overhead cuts,
- and negotiated employee contributions if the deal is balanced.

What we reject is using restructuring as a euphemism for shutting plants and forcing people out.

### 3) A staged “review” mechanism is only acceptable if it cannot be used as a trap
I reject any scheme that says:
“Accept this now, and we’ll reopen closures later if management decides the savings are still not enough.”

That is not a fair compromise. That is a delayed closure pathway dressed up as process.

If there is any review at all, it must be based on:
- independently audited data,
- agreed metrics,
- and a guarantee that non-closure measures are fully exhausted first.

And even then: I do **not** accept a built-in escape hatch for closures as the default outcome.

## On the moderator’s proposals
### 1) “No immediate closures” with audited trigger mechanism
This is still too close to a closure corridor for my comfort.

I will not sign up to a structure whose hidden purpose is to normalise eventual closures.  
If the company wants savings, it must find them **without plant closures**.

### 2) Shared-sacrifice package with quantified contribution layers
This is the only sensible direction, but only if labour is not the only side being quantified.

I would expect equal specificity from:
- management,
- owners,
- investors,
- and suppliers,
not just from the workforce.

### 3) Capacity adjustment without closure, with optional site rationalization corridor
I reject the “optional rationalization corridor.”  
That is just a polite phrase for future closures.

No corridor, no backdoor, no hidden conditionality.
