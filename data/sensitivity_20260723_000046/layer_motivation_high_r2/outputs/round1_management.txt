Let me respond plainly.

I hear the rejection of **forced redundancies** and the insistence on **socially responsible instruments**. That is noted. But I need to be equally clear: **a package that rules out plant closures and structural capacity reduction from the outset does not address the crisis we are in**.

We are not dealing with a normal negotiation. We are dealing with a company whose **German cost base is structurally too high**, whose **European market is shrinking**, and whose **brand return is below target**. If we do not materially change the structure, then we are not solving the problem — we are postponing it until it becomes worse.

So my position is this:

1. **I agree** that a durable solution must be negotiated and cannot be imposed by management alone.  
   We need an agreement that can actually be implemented.

2. **I disagree** with the claim that there will be no plant closures under any circumstances.  
   If a site is permanently uncompetitive, then keeping every plant open simply preserves overcapacity and weakens the whole group.

3. **I agree** that layoffs should not be the first or only tool.  
   But I do **not** accept “no compulsory redundancies” as a blanket principle if it blocks the structural correction required for survival.

4. **I agree** that labour should not be the only side making sacrifices.  
   Management, the company, and the owners must also contribute. But those contributions must be part of a package that delivers **real cost relief**, not symbolic gestures.

5. **I reject** any arrangement that gives us a short-term peace deal while leaving the underlying cost problem untouched.  
   We need durable savings, not a cosmetic compromise.

What I am prepared to discuss is a **serious restructuring package** with multiple levers:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- site rationalisation where necessary,
- labour-cost adjustments,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- voluntary exit tools where they are sufficient,
- and investment commitments linked to a viable future industrial footprint.

But I will not pretend that all of this can be achieved while preserving the current structure everywhere. That is not credible.

To the union side: if you want job and site security through 2030, then you need to help create the economic basis for it. Security cannot be detached from competitiveness. Otherwise we are guaranteeing the decline of the company, not its future.

So my message is simple:  
**I am open to negotiation, but only on the basis of a package that delivers structural savings and a durable reduction in cost and capacity.** If the answer is simply “no closures, no redundancies, no fundamental change,” then we do not have a solution. We have a dead end.
