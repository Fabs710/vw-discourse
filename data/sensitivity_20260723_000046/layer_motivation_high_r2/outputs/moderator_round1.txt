**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge that the company is in a serious crisis and that a durable solution is needed.
- There is broad agreement that a negotiated outcome is preferable to escalation or prolonged conflict.
- Most parties accept that employee-side measures alone are insufficient; management, owners, and shareholders must also contribute.
- There is openness across several sides to discuss:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment and training,
  - voluntary exit instruments,
  - capacity adjustments,
  - investment and product commitments tied to any restructuring.
- There is common recognition that any agreement must be implementable and economically credible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  The works council, union, and regional state government reject closures as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if sites are structurally uncompetitive.
- **Forced redundancies:**  
  Labour-side actors reject compulsory layoffs; management and investors do not accept an absolute ban if restructuring requires it.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030:**  
  Labour and the state want strong security commitments; management and investors say such guarantees may block necessary structural change.
- **Scope of restructuring:**  
  Labour wants restructuring without breaking the social contract; management and investors insist that real capacity reduction and cost reduction are required.
- **Governance and co-determination:**  
  The state, works council, and union want meaningful participation and veto-sensitive consultation; management wants room to act decisively; owners and investors want governance to support competitiveness.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is actually needed, and where.**
- **Which measures can deliver durable savings without closures or layoffs, and whether they are sufficient.**
- **What concrete sacrifices management, owners, and shareholders are prepared to make in parallel to labour concessions.**
- **Whether site and job security can be guaranteed in a limited form without undermining the restructuring target.**
- **How to balance long-term industrial continuity in Germany with short-term financial recovery.**
- **What the implementation sequence would be: immediate crisis relief, medium-term restructuring, and longer-term investment commitments.**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package elements**: quantify needed savings, capacity changes, and workforce instruments.
- Ask each side to state **one non-negotiable** and **one possible concession**.
- Require management to present **a concrete restructuring scenario** with alternatives, assumptions, and expected effects.
- Ask labour-side actors what forms of flexibility or contribution could be acceptable **if paired with binding guarantees**.
- Ask owners, state, and investors what **material contributions** they are prepared to make to support a balanced deal.
- Keep the discussion centered on **measurable trade-offs, sequencing, and implementation conditions** rather than general principles.
