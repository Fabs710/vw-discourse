I appreciate that management is naming the core problem: the company does need a durable path back to competitiveness. But I need to be equally clear from the outset: **I will not support a restructuring that destroys regional employment, undermines social stability, or sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking role.**

So, to each of you:

**To the works council and the union:**  
I understand and share the concern that workers must not be made the sole shock absorber of a strategic crisis. The regional state will not stand by if “restructuring” becomes a euphemism for hollowing out German sites. I strongly support the principle that any solution must preserve jobs as far as possible, rely first on alternatives to forced redundancies, and respect co-determination. That is not sentimentality; it is also in the long-term interest of the company and the region.

**To management:**  
I agree that wishful thinking is not a strategy. But your current framing is too one-sided. If you come to the table with plant closures as an assumed prerequisite, you are creating exactly the kind of conflict that can derail the entire restructuring. The company has obligations beyond short-term cost cutting: it is a major industrial anchor, embedded in a regional ecosystem of suppliers, workers, and public finances. A plan that “solves” competitiveness by permanently damaging that base is not a serious solution from my perspective.

I also want to be explicit on one point: **the state’s role is not decorative**. Under the special statute, we are not a passive capital holder. We have a legitimate stake in industrial continuity, employment, and governance balance. Any attempt to sideline that role would meet firm resistance.

Where I see room for movement is this:
- **capacity adjustments and working-time measures** can be part of the solution;
- **voluntary** instruments may be acceptable if truly necessary;
- **targeted, negotiated employee contributions** can be discussed if they are balanced by meaningful contributions from management and owners;
- **investment commitments, product commitments, and site guarantees** are essential if the workforce is asked to help carry the burden.

What I cannot support is:
- blanket acceptance of **plant closures in Germany** as the default answer;
- **forced redundancies** as a first-line tool;
- a deal that preserves financial flexibility for management while stripping away security for employees and weakening the regional industrial base.

So my position is pragmatic, but firm:  
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring **only if** it keeps German sites viable, protects jobs to the maximum extent possible, and comes with credible long-term industrial commitments. If that is not on the table, then I will use every right available to block a bad outcome.
