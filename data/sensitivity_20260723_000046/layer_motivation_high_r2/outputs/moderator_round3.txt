## Core impasse

The central blockage is between two different definitions of a “credible restructuring”:

- **Labour side:** restructuring is only acceptable if it preserves all German plants, avoids compulsory redundancies, and keeps the job-security framework intact.
- **Management / investor side:** restructuring is only acceptable if it delivers durable fixed-cost and capacity reduction, and they see footprint reduction and possibly closures as necessary if alternatives do not close the gap.

So the impasse is not simply “how much saving” but **whether the restructuring may include irreversible footprint change at all**.  
A second, related impasse is **burden-sharing**: labour is asking management/owners to prove alternatives and contribute first, while management/investors want labour to accept that the economics may require deeper structural change.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### 1) “No immediate closures” with audited trigger mechanism
**Proposal:**  
Agree now to **no immediate German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies in the first phase**, paired with a **plant-by-plant audited restructuring plan** and a **binding review point** after implementation of agreed savings measures.

**How it could bridge conflict:**  
- Gives labour a real near-term protection against closure and forced layoffs.
- Gives management/investors a credible mechanism to revisit the question if the numbers still do not work.
- Lets the expert’s point about plant-level data be operationalized.

**Key design element:**  
The review trigger should be tied to **pre-agreed financial and operational metrics**, not open-ended management discretion.

---

### 2) Shared-sacrifice package with quantified contribution layers
**Proposal:**  
Build a restructuring package in which each side commits to a quantified contribution:
- management: overhead and executive-cost reduction,
- owners/family: capital support or investment guarantees,
- investors: willingness to accept a staged margin recovery path,
- labour: working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and possibly temporary sacrifice instruments.

**How it could bridge conflict:**  
- Addresses labour’s demand that it not be the sole adjustment variable.
- Addresses management/investor demands for real savings.
- Creates a measurable burden-sharing framework rather than a principle debate.

**Key design element:**  
Each contribution should be **numerically specified** and **sequenced**, so the package can be tested against the savings gap.

---

### 3) Capacity adjustment without closure, with optional site rationalization corridor
**Proposal:**  
Start with a **no-closure operational package**: shift changes, working-time reductions, redeployment, and product reallocation to improve utilization, while defining a **narrow corridor** under which limited site rationalization could be discussed later if agreed savings are not achieved.

**How it could bridge conflict:**  
- Preserves labour’s immediate no-closure red line.
- Gives management a structured route if non-closure measures fall short.
- Avoids a binary “all or nothing” confrontation.

**Key design element:**  
Any later footprint discussion would require **supermajority governance approval** and **independent verification** that all non-closure measures were exhausted.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**If independent plant-by-plant numbers show a residual structural gap after all non-closure measures, what quantified additional contribution would you accept from labour that still preserves the no-forced-redundancy principle?**

### To the Union
**What specific combination of working-time, voluntary exit, redeployment, and wage/flexibility measures do you consider sufficient to close the gap without closures, and what numerical threshold would make you say the package is no longer viable?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact plant-level savings bridge can you present, and which measures have you already ruled in or out before concluding that footprint reduction is required?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete conditions would you attach to your support so that you can defend no immediate closures while still ensuring the company reaches a viable cost base?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What level of capital support, investment commitment, or governance commitment are you prepared to provide to make a no-closure restructuring credible if the numbers are close but not yet sufficient?**

### To Institutional Investors
**If the package delivers audited, durable savings but does not include closures, what minimum financial metrics would satisfy you that the restructuring is still value-preserving?**

### To the Expert
**What plant-level data set would you require to determine whether a no-closure solution is genuinely sufficient, and what threshold would make footprint reduction economically unavoidable?**
