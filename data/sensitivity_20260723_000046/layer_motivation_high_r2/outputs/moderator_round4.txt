Here is a procedural readout as facilitator.

## Core impasse
The central deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed; most stakeholders now accept that it is.  
The impasse is over **the permissible instruments**:

- **Labour side / state:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of job-security and co-determination.
- **Management / investors / family:** no credible turnaround if all sites and protections are fully frozen; they want room for footprint change if the numbers require it.
- **Expert:** says the issue turns on whether non-closure measures can close the structural gap; if not, capacity reduction or rationalization becomes economically hard to avoid.

So the conflict is essentially:
**“Can the gap be closed with non-closure measures and shared sacrifice, or must footprint change remain available as a last resort?”**

A second, related impasse is **trust and sequencing**:
- Labour fears a “review” or “trigger” mechanism will become a backdoor closure path.
- Management and investors fear a blanket no-closure stance becomes a de facto veto on viable restructuring.

---

## Possible compromise positions
Below are facilitator proposals, not opinions.

### 1) Sequenced restructuring with hard data gates
**Structure:**
- Immediate commitment: no closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined initial period.
- In parallel: plant-by-plant audited business case, with agreed metrics and independent verification.
- First wave of measures must exhaust non-closure tools: working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, partial retirement, management cuts, overhead cuts, product reallocation.
- Only if pre-agreed financial milestones are missed would parties reconvene on additional measures.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Gives labour a real no-closure phase.
- Gives management/investors a disciplined path to test whether the gap can actually be closed.
- Reduces fear of a hidden closure corridor by requiring independent data and pre-agreed sequencing.

---

### 2) Shared-sacrifice package with asymmetrical protections
**Structure:**
- Labour contribution: flexibility, voluntary exits, qualification, limited temporary concessions.
- Management contribution: executive restraint, overhead reduction, procurement savings, internal efficiency targets.
- Owner/shareholder contribution: funding commitment, investment pledge, perhaps temporary governance concessions or capital support.
- State contribution: political and institutional support for investment, possibly facilitation for training, energy, or site modernization.
- Protective rule: no forced redundancies during the agreed restructuring window, subject to review only after all other measures are proven insufficient.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Addresses labour’s objection that workers are being asked to carry the whole burden.
- Addresses investors’ concern that symbolic measures alone are insufficient.
- Creates a quantified burden-sharing framework rather than a binary yes/no fight over closures.

---

### 3) Capacity adjustment without closure, with repurposing option
**Structure:**
- Keep all German sites formally open in the first phase.
- Reduce capacity through shift changes, line consolidation, or model reassignment.
- Identify weaker sites for repurposing rather than immediate closure, for example toward components, specialty production, or other industrial functions.
- Include a review point only for repurposing feasibility, not automatic closure.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Preserves the “no closures” red line in a literal sense.
- Gives management an instrument for real cost reduction.
- Provides investors with evidence that footprint change is being addressed economically, not merely deferred.

---

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**If independent plant-level data show that non-closure measures do not fully close the structural gap, what exact additional non-closure measures would you accept before you still rule out any footprint change?**

### To the Union
**What quantified labour contribution are you prepared to put on the table if management, owners, and investors also make measurable sacrifices and no forced redundancies remain off the table only as a last resort?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to a published plant-by-plant savings bridge that separates what can be achieved by non-closure measures from what would only be achieved by footprint change?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Under what specific conditions, if any, would you accept a non-closure plan being tested first, provided it is independently audited and time-limited?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What concrete owner-side contribution are you prepared to make to preserve viable German sites before asking labour to accept deeper concessions?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a time-bound no-closure phase if it came with audited milestones, a quantified savings bridge, and a clearly defined fallback if targets are missed?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum dataset and threshold would you require to determine whether the company can credibly avoid footprint reduction, rather than simply postpone it?**

## Facilitation note
A productive next round would be to require each party to respond in one of three ways only:

1. **Accept the sequenced-data path**
2. **Reject it and explain what condition is missing**
3. **Specify the exact numbers or safeguards needed to make it acceptable**

That would force the discourse from principle-based repetition into negotiable structure.
