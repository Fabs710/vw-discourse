I will speak plainly.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the regional state on the central diagnosis: **the company has a real competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic solution would be value-destructive**. If the German cost base stays structurally above target, if overcapacity remains, and if margins do not recover, then we are not protecting jobs or the enterprise — we are postponing deterioration.

I also agree that **employees should not be the only party asked to contribute**. A durable settlement must include concessions and discipline from management, ownership, and the company itself, not just from labour.

And I agree with the state that **industrial peace matters**. A prolonged strike or public breakdown would damage the enterprise, the brand, and the family’s long-term asset.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the works council and union position that any serious restructuring must start from the premise of:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and preservation of every existing guarantee as if the economic reality did not exist.

That position is understandable politically, but it is not a credible stewardship of the enterprise if it blocks a durable competitiveness reset. If some sites are structurally uncompetitive, then refusing to address that because it is painful is not protecting the company; it is locking in decline.

I also do **not** accept the state’s implication that the special role of co-determination or the regional interest can be used to freeze the footprint indefinitely. Governance rights matter, but they are not a veto against economic reality.

### On management’s case
Management is right to insist that **real structural savings** are needed. I support that direction. But I want to be equally clear:  
**I will not back a crude closure-first strategy that creates uncontrolled conflict, reputational damage, or governance paralysis.**

If management wants support from the controlling owner, it needs a package that is:
- economically credible,
- implementable under co-determination,
- and durable enough to restore margins rather than just trigger a one-off cut.

So I support the need for structural change, but not recklessness.

### What I need to see
For me to support a settlement, it must meet three tests:

1. **Durable competitiveness gains**  
   Not symbolic savings, not temporary truce economics. Real margin improvement.

2. **Governability and labour peace**  
   The package must be implementable without endless escalation or a permanent legitimacy crisis.

3. **Protection of the enterprise’s long-term industrial core**  
   The company’s strategic capacity in Germany must remain viable, not hollowed out.

### What that means in practice
I am open to:
- significant capacity reduction,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and targeted investment commitments tied to a leaner footprint.

I am **not** open to:
- a pledge that every plant remains open regardless of economics,
- a blanket ban on compulsory adjustments if the package genuinely requires them,
- or a settlement that preserves all current guarantees while leaving the cost base untouched.

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on dignity and security is legitimate. But your current line risks becoming a refusal to engage with the numbers. If you want site and job security, you have to help create the economic basis for it. That may require flexibility, workforce adjustment, and acceptance that not every current structure can survive intact.

### To management
You will not get a blank cheque for blunt-force restructuring. If you want the controlling shareholder’s support, bring a plan that is disciplined, specific, and credible under the company’s governance realities. Do not present value destruction as toughness.

### To the regional state government
I respect your role, but it must be exercised in the interest of a viable enterprise, not as a permanent shield against uncomfortable decisions. If you help secure a balanced restructuring that preserves competitiveness and industrial strength, you will have our support. If you insist on blocking all real adjustment, then you will also be blocking the company’s future.

### To investors
You are right to demand measurable improvement. But a deal that maximizes short-term numerical simplification while creating long-term instability is not a good outcome. We need a settlement that actually survives implementation.

### My bottom line
The family holding will support a **serious restructuring** that restores competitiveness and margins, even if it includes painful measures. But we will **not** support an arrangement that permanently freezes the footprint, preserves uncompetitive structures, and thereby weakens the enterprise over time.

If the parties want our support, they need to bring forward a package that is:
- economically real,
- socially managed,
- and governance-stable.

Otherwise, we will not sign up to it.
