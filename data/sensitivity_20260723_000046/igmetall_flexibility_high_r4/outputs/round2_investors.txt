We agree with the expert’s core conclusion: **the company needs a large, durable structural savings package, not a cosmetic deal**. That is the right frame.

Where we part company with the employee side is simple: **we will not support a settlement that preserves all sites, rejects compulsory redundancies outright, and leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.** If that is the outcome, margins stay impaired and the equity story remains broken.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current cost base and capacity are unsustainable.
- **The family is right** that the restructuring must be economically real, not symbolic.
- **The expert is right** that overhead cuts alone will not close the gap.
- **The state and workforce are right** that a pure one-sided shock treatment would be unstable and value-destructive.

We also agree that a prolonged strike would destroy value. We want decisive action, but we do **not** want drawn-out industrial conflict.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is still too absolute.

The expert has now made the crucial point: if the problem is fixed-cost overcapacity, then **someone has to remove capacity and cost in a way that is large enough to matter**. Voluntary exits, working-time changes, and wage restraint may contribute, but they are not automatically sufficient.

So we reject any position that:
- guarantees every German plant through 2030 regardless of economics,
- rules out compulsory redundancies in all circumstances,
- and assumes management can somehow produce competitive margins without hard structural change.

If you can present a **quantified non-closure package** that truly matches the savings of closures and workforce reduction, we will consider it. But it must be measurable, durable, and enforceable. So far, we have heard principles, not numbers.

### To the State Government
We respect your role, but we do **not** support using veto power to freeze an inefficient footprint in place. The region’s long-term interests are not served by preserving every site if that means ongoing margin erosion and underinvestment.

A state-backed solution is only credible if it helps restore competitiveness, not just delay hard choices.

### To Management
We support your insistence on real savings, but we also need discipline on execution. “Closures are on the table” is not enough by itself. The market will want to know:
- what savings are required,
- which sites are the weakest nodes,
- what the capacity reduction path is,
- and how quickly the margin improvement flows through.

If management cannot quantify the savings and the implementation path, investor support will weaken.

## What would move us
We would back an outcome if it contains all of the following:

1. **A quantified structural savings target**
   - large enough to restore peer-competitive margins.

2. **Real capacity reduction**
   - if not through closures, then through an alternative that is economically equivalent, not just politically easier.

3. **A credible workforce reduction mechanism**
   - voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, retirement pathways are welcome,
   - but only if the numbers add up.

4. **Durable labour-cost relief**
   - temporary wage restraint alone is not enough unless it is substantial and paired with productivity gains.

5. **No prolonged disruption**
   - we want certainty, not a never-ending confrontation.

6. **Clear governance certainty**
   - a package that can actually be implemented under the co-determination constraints, not one that collapses later.

## What we will not support
- A cosmetic compromise.
- A preserve-everything settlement.
- A deal that leaves German operations structurally too expensive.
- A restructuring that protects jobs in the short term but destroys returns in the medium term.
- Any outcome where shareholders fund the transition while the operating model remains broken.

## Our position in plain terms
We are **not** asking for brutality; we are asking for **economic reality**.

If a non-closure solution can genuinely deliver the same savings, fine — we support the economics, not the symbolism. But if the employee side cannot produce that, then management’s deeper restructuring case becomes the only credible route.

So our message is unchanged:
**show us the numbers, show us the durability, and show us the margin reset — or expect investors to back the harder cost-cutting path.**
