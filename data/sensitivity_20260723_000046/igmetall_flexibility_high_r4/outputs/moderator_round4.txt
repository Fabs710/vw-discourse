## Core impasse
The discussion is stalled on a single structural fault line:

**Stakeholders disagree on whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies are legitimate fallback instruments, or whether the restructuring must be designed as a binding non-closure path first.**

That disagreement then cascades into three secondary impasses:

1. **Sequencing**
   - Labor-side actors want management to prove the non-closure path first.
   - Management and capital want closures to remain available if savings do not materialize.

2. **Guarantees vs. flexibility**
   - Labor and state want binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy commitments.
   - Management and investors refuse to remove hard options in advance.

3. **Definition of “credible restructuring”**
   - Labor-side actors define credibility as preserving every German plant.
   - Capital-side actors define credibility as achieving equivalent structural savings, even if that requires selective closure.

## Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete compromise frames that could bridge the biggest gaps.

### Compromise 1: Time-limited non-closure phase with jointly verified trigger rules
**Proposal frame:**  
A defined non-closure phase runs for a fixed period, during which closures are not pursued. During that phase, all parties commit to a quantified restructuring package with site-by-site targets.

**Core elements:**
- fixed duration, e.g. 12–24 months
- binding monitoring by a joint committee
- independent verification of savings, utilization, and headcount progress
- any reopening of closure discussion only if pre-agreed, objective trigger thresholds are met

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It gives labor and the state a real protection period, while giving management and investors a narrow, rule-based fallback instead of a permanent ban.

---

### Compromise 2: “No closures during the agreement term” in exchange for a quantified labor and ownership package
**Proposal frame:**  
The workforce accepts temporary, limited contributions if management and owners provide parallel burdens and the agreement contains explicit no-closure / no-forced-redundancy protections for the term.

**Core elements:**
- temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, and working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits and redeployment only
- management overhead cuts and executive restraint
- ownership-side contribution, such as dividend restraint or capital support
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies for the agreement period

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It aligns the labor-side demand for guarantees with the capital-side demand for real structural savings, without deciding the long-term footprint in advance.

---

### Compromise 3: Site-by-site preservation/consolidation map with protected core and jointly reviewed weaker sites
**Proposal frame:**  
Instead of an all-or-nothing footprint decision, the parties agree to a site-by-site model that classifies sites into preserve, consolidate, or review categories.

**Core elements:**
- each site assessed on utilization, fixed cost, capex need, and future product allocation
- protected core sites receive explicit investment and product commitments
- weaker sites are not closed automatically, but are subject to defined consolidation measures first
- closure discussion only if a site fails jointly defined recovery tests

**Why this may bridge conflict:**  
It avoids an immediate sacrifice list while giving management a route to address structural underperformance through consolidation before closure.

### To the controlling family holding

### To institutional investors
