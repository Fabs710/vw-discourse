## Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with the state that this is a **serious structural problem**, not a cosmetic one. I also agree that overhead cuts alone will not solve it. And yes, if we want a durable settlement, we need a site-by-site picture.

That said, the existence of a problem does **not** justify a fait accompli against the workforce.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You keep saying closures must remain “available.” That is exactly the problem: you are treating plant closure as an ordinary management tool instead of the most destructive option on the table.

If you want even to *argue* for closures, then you must first show, site by site, that:
- the site has no viable future product allocation,
- internal capacity reduction cannot absorb the adjustment,
- voluntary instruments cannot deliver the workforce reduction,
- and the closure does not damage the wider network.

Until that is proven, closures are not “necessary.” They are a managerial choice to offload the crisis onto workers and regions.

And I reject your framing that no compulsory redundancies somehow “blocks change.” It does not. It blocks **forced social destruction**. There is a huge difference.

### To the family and investors
You say you want economic reality. Fine. Then here is the reality: **a workforce that is treated as disposable will not deliver a stable turnaround**.

If you insist that “equivalent savings” must mean permanent job destruction and plant closure, then you are defining equivalence in a way that makes negotiation impossible. The company has other levers:
- management overhead,
- executive restraint,
- investment sequencing,
- working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification,
- temporary labour-cost moderation.

If those are not being taken seriously, then do not tell me the workforce is the only rigid actor in the room.

### To the state government
I appreciate your resistance to closures. But I need to be frank: saying closures are a last resort is not enough unless you are prepared to **use your blocking position to stop premature closure logic**.

You cannot say “we oppose closures” in principle and then leave the door wide open for management to use them as leverage in practice. If you want the region, suppliers, and industrial base protected, then you must insist on a genuine non-closure model first.

### To the expert
I accept the expert’s core point that the numbers matter. But I also want to stress: the expert’s “hybrid with limited closure” being called the highest-probability compromise is an observation, not a mandate.

A model is only “realistic” if it is socially and industrially acceptable. A closure may be economically cleaner on paper, but if it breaks trust, triggers conflict, and destroys co-determination, it is not a viable solution.

## My position on the proposed scenarios

### 1) Status quo
Not acceptable. Nobody here is defending doing nothing.

### 2) Non-closure restructuring
This is the only route I am prepared to seriously examine.

But it must be real, not decorative. That means:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site and job guarantees through 2030,
- and a genuine plan for capacity reduction through internal measures.

If management and capital want a labour contribution, then it must be **temporary, limited, and explicitly linked to guarantees**.

### 3) Hybrid with limited closure
I reject this as a starting point. It is not a compromise if the “compromise” begins with sacrificing a plant and a community.

If anyone wants to argue for it, they need to show why every other lever fails. So far, that proof has not been delivered.

## What I want to see next
If management is serious about a non-closure route, then bring forward a **site-by-site model** with these items:
- utilization,
- next product allocation,
- fixed cost burden,
- capex needs,
- headcount impact,
- savings over 3–5 years,
- and what is achieved through voluntary measures versus management contributions.

And I want the model to include a **credible no-closure, no-forced-redundancy scenario** as a full alternative, not a token appendix.
