I’ll be direct.

## My position
As the regional state and anchor shareholder, I support a **durable restructuring**. I do **not** support a restructuring that uses the crisis to normalize plant closures, forced redundancies, or the sidelining of the state’s co-determination and veto role.

The company’s cost problem is real. The expert’s assessment confirms that. But the burden of proof is still on management and capital to show, **site by site**, why less destructive options are insufficient. Until that proof exists, closures are not the default answer — they are a last resort, if ever justified.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- the cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and cosmetic fixes will fail.

I also agree that the package must deliver **real structural savings**, not just a public-relations compromise.

### With the expert
The expert has clarified the right frame:
- a non-closure restructuring can be credible,
- but only if it includes capacity reduction, workforce reduction through voluntary means, labor-cost moderation, management contributions, and binding investment commitments.

That is the correct bargaining basis.

### With the works council and union
You are right that:
- workers should not carry the burden alone,
- management and ownership must contribute,
- and a blunt closure-first approach would damage trust, the regional industrial base, and the social partnership that has made this company strong.

## Where I disagree

### To management
I do **not** accept your framing that closures must remain a normal baseline option from the start.

A plant closure in this state is not an abstract efficiency tool. It has consequences for:
- regional employment,
- suppliers,
- municipalities,
- training pathways,
- and the company’s social legitimacy.

If you want me to even consider hard measures, you must first prove that:
- non-closure capacity reductions have been exhausted,
- voluntary instruments cannot deliver the headcount path,
- and the same savings cannot be achieved through internal reorganization, working-time changes, redeployment, and product shifts.

Right now, that proof is not there.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right that the package must be economically real. But you are **not** entitled to define “real” as “whatever removes labor cost fastest, even if it guts the regional footprint.”

A restructuring that damages the company’s long-term legitimacy, triggers protracted conflict, and weakens its regional anchor role is not a sound value strategy. It may look cleaner on a spreadsheet, but it is not durable.

### To the works council and union
I also need to be equally clear with you: a blanket “all plants stay open, no compulsory redundancies” line is only sustainable if it is matched by a **credible, quantified non-closure plan**.

Saying “no” to closures is not enough by itself. If the company needs material capacity reduction, then the question is how to achieve it without destroying the industrial base. That means you must engage on:
- shift reductions,
- product reallocations,
- voluntary exit volumes,
- working-time changes,
- productivity measures,
- and internal redeployment.

A pure blocking position is not enough to save the company.

## What I can support
I can support a **non-closure restructuring package** if it contains all of the following:

1. **A site-by-site quantified model**
   - utilization,
   - fixed costs,
   - labor cost per unit,
   - capex needs,
   - headcount impact,
   - and savings over 3–5 years.

2. **Capacity reduction without plant closures as the first option**
   - shift cuts,
   - line rationalization,
   - product consolidation,
   - internal footprint reduction,
   - and lower output at weak sites.

3. **Workforce reduction mainly through voluntary instruments**
   - natural attrition,
   - retirement pathways,
   - voluntary severance,
   - redeployment,
   - qualification.

4. **A fair labor contribution**
   - temporary wage restraint,
   - bonus moderation,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - productivity improvements.

5. **Meaningful management and ownership contribution**
   - overhead cuts,
   - executive restraint,
   - investment discipline.

6. **Binding job, site, and investment safeguards**
   - no closures as long as viable alternatives exist,
   - protection of the state’s veto and co-determination role,
   - clear commitments to German production and future products.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- plant closures as the default answer,
- compulsory redundancies as the presumed solution,
- a deal that weakens the state’s special-law rights,
- or any attempt to sideline the regional interest as though it were a nuisance.

Those are not bargaining preferences. They are core interests.

## On the moderator’s compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Non-closure first, closure as triggered fallback”
This is only acceptable if the trigger is **exceptionally high, objective, and jointly verified**.

If the fallback is vague, discretionary, or management-controlled, then it is not a protection phase. It is simply a delayed closure threat. I will not support a fake non-closure phase.

### Proposal 2: “Conditional protection in exchange for measurable labor contribution”
This is the most promising direction, provided the conditions are binding.

I can support temporary labor contribution if it is matched by:
- no plant closures during the agreement term,
- no compulsory redundancies during the agreement term,
- material management and ownership contributions,
- and enforceable site-by-site commitments.

### Proposal 3: “Hybrid package with protected core sites”
This may be economically plausible in the abstract, but politically and socially it is only acceptable if the core sites are truly safeguarded and the weaker sites are not simply sacrificed by default.

I would not endorse it as an opening move. It can only be discussed after the non-closure path has been seriously tested and found insufficient.

## What should happen next
I support the request for a **site-by-site quantitative model** comparing:
- status quo,
- non-closure restructuring,
- and hybrid restructuring.

But I want that model to include not only economics, also:
- regional employment impact,
- supplier consequences,
- investment commitments,
- and the effect on co-determination and governance stability.

## Final word
I am open to a durable deal. I am not open to a settlement that treats the state, the workforce, and the regional economy as disposable.

If management can show a credible non-closure path with real savings, I will help broker it.  
If not, then it must explain why it is choosing a route that destroys jobs, weakens the company’s legitimacy, and undermines the long-term foundation of the business.
