As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

Management cannot solve a strategic problem by destroying livelihoods, breaking trust, and dismantling the industrial base that made this company strong. We also reject any attempt to tear up established job-security arrangements or force through a one-sided cost-cutting programme onto the workforce.

What we are prepared to discuss is a different path: a restructuring that protects all German sites, avoids forced layoffs, and secures jobs through alternatives such as capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, qualification, working-time solutions, and a fair negotiated employee contribution. But any contribution from employees must be matched by binding guarantees from management and owners.

Our priorities are:
- keep every German plant open,
- protect every employee from forced layoffs,
- preserve co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- secure binding guarantees through 2030 for sites, jobs, and investment,
- ensure the burden of restructuring is shared fairly, not dumped on the workforce alone.

If management wants cooperation, it must put a credible, binding package on the table:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral downgrading of labour conditions,
- clear product and investment commitments for German sites,
- and a genuine role for employee representatives in shaping the future plan.

Without that, there is no basis for agreement. We are prepared to resist decisively, including through industrial action and by using every rights-based lever in co-determination.
