### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides acknowledge the company faces a **serious cost and competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable restructuring** is needed, not a cosmetic fix.
- Several parties accept that **some form of employee contribution** may be part of a deal, provided it is matched by safeguards.
- There is shared interest in preserving **German industrial capability**, future **investment**, and a viable long-term business.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state strongly oppose closures; management, family, and investors will not rule them out.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee side and state reject them; management, family, and investors want the option left open if needed.
- **Scope of job security:** Employee side seeks binding guarantees through 2030; management and capital-side actors view that as too rigid without structural change.
- **Degree of labour contribution:** Employee side is open to sacrifice only with binding counter-guarantees; capital-side actors say the contribution must be substantial enough to deliver real savings.
- **Governance power:** The state insists on its role and rights; management, family, and investors want enough flexibility to implement restructuring effectively.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is necessary** and whether it can be achieved without closures.
- **What “equivalent savings”** would look like if closures and forced redundancies are avoided.
- **Who bears what share of the burden** among workers, management, owners, and investors.
- **What binding guarantees** can credibly be offered in exchange for employee concessions.
- **How to preserve investment and future product plans** while also generating immediate cost savings.
- **Which parts of the restructuring are reversible or temporary** versus permanent.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **specific package design**:
- identify **concrete savings measures**,
- quantify **capacity and labour-cost reductions**,
- specify **what guarantees or protections** would accompany employee concessions,
- and clarify **which alternatives to closures and compulsory redundancies** are realistically on the table.

The next round should aim to compare **named options** rather than restating red lines.
