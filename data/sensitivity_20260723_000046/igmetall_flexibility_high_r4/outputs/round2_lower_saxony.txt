As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will be direct.

I agree with management, the family, and investors on one central point: **this company needs a credible restructuring and a real cost reset**. I also agree with the Works Council and the union that **a solution that simply dumps the burden onto the workforce is politically, socially, and economically unacceptable**.

But I need to be equally clear about the limits of my position: **I cannot support German plant closures as the starting point**, and I cannot support compulsory redundancies if there is a serious alternative path that preserves the company’s regional role. Our blocking minority and board seats are not decorative; they exist to protect exactly these interests.

## Where I agree
### With management
- Yes, there is a genuine competitiveness and capacity problem.
- Yes, the company needs durable savings, not a cosmetic deal.
- Yes, future investment in products and technology depends on restoring margins.

### With the workforce side
- Yes, the company cannot be rebuilt by destroying trust, sites, and jobs.
- Yes, any employee contribution must be matched by binding commitments from management and owners.
- Yes, German industrial capability is part of the company’s long-term strength.

## Where I disagree
### On the “closures are just another option” line
I do **not** accept closures as a normal first-response instrument. A plant closure in this state would have consequences far beyond one site: it would hit suppliers, municipalities, skilled employment, and the company’s reputation as a regional anchor. That is not something to treat as a routine efficiency measure.

So to management and investors: if you want the state to even consider difficult structural changes, you must first demonstrate, transparently and site by site, that alternatives have been exhausted.

### On the “no-closure/no-redundancy is impossible” claim
I do **not** accept that as proven. You keep asserting that closures and forced redundancies are necessary, but assertion is not evidence. What I need to see is a package that shows:
- how much savings are required,
- how much can be achieved through capacity adjustments,
- how much through working-time and flexibility measures,
- how much through voluntary exits and redeployment,
- how much through management and overhead restraint,
- and only then, if necessary, what remains unresolved.

Right now, I do not see a convincing proof that the hardest measures are unavoidable.

## What I can support
I can support a **serious non-closure restructuring package** if it is concrete and enforceable. That package must include:

1. **A binding commitment to avoid German plant closures unless no viable alternative exists**
   - Not a permanent taboo in the abstract, but a clear rule that closure is the last resort, not the opening move.

2. **No compulsory redundancies as a principle of the settlement**
   - Workforce reduction, if needed, must come through voluntary exits, retirement pathways, redeployment, qualification, and working-time instruments.

3. **A fair employee contribution**
   - Temporary wage restraint, bonus moderation, or working-time adjustments can be part of the deal.
   - But only if the burden is shared and the outcome is durable.

4. **Comparable contributions from management and ownership**
   - executive restraint,
   - overhead cuts,
   - disciplined investment sequencing,
   - and a visible commitment from capital that the workforce is not financing everyone else’s mistakes.

5. **Binding site, job, and investment commitments**
   - If employees make sacrifices, the company must commit to future production and investment in Germany.
   - Otherwise this becomes a hollow restructuring with delayed decline.

6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
   - I will not accept any attempt to sideline the state or weaken the special statutory protections.
   - Those rights are part of the governance balance and part of the region’s economic security.

## What I cannot accept
Let me state this plainly:
- no outcome that guts regional employment,
- no outcome that removes the state’s co-determination or veto role,
- no settlement that treats closures as the default answer,
- and no one-sided labour-cost cut that leaves management and ownership untouched.

## My message to each side

### To management
If you want support, bring a **real alternative package**, not a threat. Show me a site-by-site plan with quantified savings that avoids closures and forced redundancies. If that cannot be done, say exactly why and where. But do not expect the state to endorse plant closures without exhausting other routes.

### To the Works Council and union
I support your resistance to closures and compulsory redundancies. But I will also say this: the state cannot back a position that denies any operational adjustment at all. If we are to protect the company and the regional base, there must be some capacity reduction and some negotiated contribution. The question is how to do it without ripping out the industrial core.

### To the family and investors
You are right that the company needs durability. But durability is not created by forcing through the hardest cuts at the first moment. If you want the state to facilitate a settlement, it must be a balanced one: serious savings, yes, but also serious protection for jobs, sites, and long-term industrial presence in Germany.

## Where I stand
So my position is:

- **Yes** to restructuring.
- **Yes** to a real, measurable cost reduction.
- **Yes** to negotiated employee contributions.
- **No** to closures as the default path.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies as the presumed solution.
- **No** to any weakening of the state’s co-determination and veto rights.

If a non-closure, non-forced-redundancy package can be shown to deliver the required structural savings, I am prepared to back it and help broker it.

If not, then the burden is on management and the controlling shareholder to explain why they are choosing a route that would damage the regional economy and the company’s long-term social license. I will not approve that lightly, and under the statute I will use the leverage available to me.
