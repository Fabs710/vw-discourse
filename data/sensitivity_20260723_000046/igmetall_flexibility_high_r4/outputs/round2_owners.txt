I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree with others

### With management
You are right that the company’s current cost base and capacity are not sustainable. If we leave the structure untouched, we will continue to erode margins and weaken the company’s ability to invest in future products. That would be irresponsible ownership, and I will not support it.

### With the works council, union, and state
You are also right that a one-sided restructuring imposed on the workforce would be unstable and value-destructive. A settlement that destroys trust, triggers prolonged industrial conflict, and undermines execution would not restore competitiveness; it would delay the pain and amplify it.

So yes: **we need a durable solution, not theatre**.

## Where I disagree

### To the works council and union
Your position remains too absolute.

A blanket **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies”** line may be understandable politically, but it is not yet an economic answer. If a site cannot be made competitive, then ruling out every hard option in advance does not protect the enterprise — it risks locking in structural losses and making the eventual adjustment worse.

I do not accept a deal that preserves every historical structure regardless of economics.

### To the state government
I respect the special role and the regional interest, but I cannot accept the idea that the state’s blocking position turns necessary restructuring into a no-go zone. The company has to remain viable first; otherwise the regional base you are defending will be damaged anyway.

### To management
You are right to insist on real savings, but I also caution against turning “closures” into the first and only credible answer. If you cannot show, site by site, that alternatives have been exhausted, then a closure-driven approach will be hard to support and may be unnecessarily destructive.

## What would move me
I am prepared to back a serious restructuring package if it delivers all of the following:

1. **A real structural cost reset**
   - not symbolic savings, but savings large enough to restore competitive margins.

2. **A credible capacity reduction**
   - whether through closures or an alternative, it must be economically equivalent.

3. **A meaningful labour contribution**
   - wage restraint, bonus moderation, working-time flexibility, productivity measures, redeployment, voluntary exits.

4. **Protection of the core German industrial base**
   - not every site at any cost, but a clearly viable long-term footprint.

5. **Stability in execution**
   - no drawn-out stalemate that destroys value through strikes and uncertainty.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a preserve-everything settlement,
- a deal that leaves the company permanently overstaffed and uncompetitive,
- a cosmetic compromise that buys peace but not performance,
- or a restructuring where the workforce bears all the burden while management and ownership are insulated.

## My position in plain terms
I am open to an alternative to closures and forced redundancies — **but only if it achieves the same structural result**. That means quantified savings, durable capacity adjustment, and a cost base that can compete.

If the employee side and the state want to block the hard measures, then they must help produce a package that actually solves the problem. If they cannot, then management’s restructuring case remains necessary in principle.
