We are prepared to be pragmatic, but we are not prepared to bless a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
- With management, the family, the expert, and partly the state: this is a **real structural problem**, not a cosmetic one.
- With everyone: **overhead cuts alone are not enough**.
- With the expert: **capacity reduction, labor-cost moderation, and workforce reduction** all matter.
- With the union and works council: **industrial conflict is value-destructive** and should be avoided.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council / Union
Your position still reads as: “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and preserve every German site through 2030.” We do **not** support that as a default bargaining outcome.

Why not?
- Because it may sound protective, but it can leave the company **overcapacity-heavy and margin-poor**.
- Because “voluntary only” is not enough unless the numbers show it can actually deliver the required savings.
- Because a deal that protects sites but does not restore competitiveness is not a win for shareholders, employees, or the company.

We do not dismiss your non-closure pathway. But we need proof it is **economically equivalent**, not just politically preferable.

### To the State Government
We respect the state’s role and the regional stakes. But the state cannot be allowed to freeze the footprint if that means the business stays structurally weak.

A company that remains uncompetitive will eventually damage the region anyway. So the state should support a **credible restructuring**, not a guarantee that every site survives regardless of economics.

### To Management
We agree you need real structural savings. But you still have not shown, with credible site-level numbers, the **residual gap after non-closure measures**.

You keep saying closures must remain available. Fine — but that is not yet a substitute for a quantified case. If you want capital-market backing, show:
- site-by-site utilization,
- fixed cost burden,
- labor cost per unit,
- headcount reduction path,
- capex implications,
- and 3–5 year margin impact.

Without that, “closures are available” is just leverage, not a plan.

### To the Controlling Family
We agree with the principle of a durable reset. We do **not** agree to a cosmetic compromise.  
But we also do **not** accept a posture where the company’s long-term legitimacy is traded away for faster labor pain.

A destructive strike war or a politically unworkable closure plan would also destroy value. We want hard savings, but we also want **execution certainty**.

## What would move us
We would back a settlement if it includes:

1. **A quantified structural savings target**
   - enough to restore competitive margins.

2. **A site-by-site model**
   - utilization,
   - fixed costs,
   - labor cost per unit,
   - capex,
   - headcount,
   - and 3–5 year savings.

3. **A credible non-closure scenario**
   - if it truly delivers equivalent savings, that is our preferred route.

4. **A meaningful residual fallback**
   - if the non-closure route fails on the numbers, then limited hard measures remain on the table.

5. **Material labor contribution**
   - wage restraint,
   - bonus moderation,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - voluntary exits,
   - productivity gains.

6. **Management and ownership contribution**
   - overhead cuts,
   - executive restraint,
   - tighter investment sequencing.

7. **Implementation certainty**
   - no symbolic deal,
   - no endless renegotiation,
   - no settlement that merely postpones the problem.

## Our bottom line
We are **not** demanding closures.  
We are demanding **credible competitiveness**.

So:
- if a non-closure package can truly close the gap, we will support it;
- if it cannot, then the company must be allowed to make the harder structural changes required.

What we will **not** support is an arrangement that:
- preserves every site,
- avoids meaningful structural cost reduction,
- and leaves margins impaired.
