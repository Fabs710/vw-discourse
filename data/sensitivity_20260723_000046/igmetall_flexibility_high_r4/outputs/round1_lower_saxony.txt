I appreciate the clarity from both sides, and I will be equally clear from the perspective of the regional state government as a shareholder with a responsibility to the public interest.

### Where I agree
Management is right on one point: **the company does need a credible restructuring**. Pretending that the current cost base and capacity can remain untouched would be irresponsible. The market reality is serious, and a durable solution must restore competitiveness.

I also agree with the works council and the union that **a crisis cannot simply be dumped on the workforce**. This company is not an abstract cost model; it is a major employer, a regional anchor, and part of the industrial fabric of this state. Any restructuring that destroys that base would be economically and politically damaging, and it would undermine trust in the whole governance model.

### Where I disagree with management
What I cannot support is the framing that plant closures in Germany are simply “on the table” as if they were a normal technical option.

From our side, that is not acceptable as a starting point. A closure decision would have deep consequences for jobs, suppliers, municipal finances, and the region’s industrial future. It would also be a very serious step in a company that has long benefited from social partnership and strong public support. If management wants room to maneuver, it must first show that it has exhausted alternatives.

Equally, I will not support a restructuring logic that makes the workforce carry the burden while ownership and management are insulated. A sustainable settlement has to be shared and balanced.

### Where I disagree with the works council and union
I understand the refusal of forced redundancies and the resistance to closures. That position is politically and socially understandable. But I cannot say that **absolutely no capacity adjustment and no meaningful internal reallocation** can happen.

If the company is structurally overcapacity, then some adjustment is unavoidable. The key question is not whether there will be change, but **how it is designed**:
- avoiding plant closures if possible,
- avoiding compulsory redundancies,
- using redeployment, qualification, working-time instruments, and voluntary exits,
- and preserving industrial capability in Germany.

So I support a strong defensive line against closures and mass layoffs, but I do not support a position that denies any operational change whatsoever.

### What I am prepared to back
I would support a negotiated package if it contains all of the following:

1. **No plant closures as the first resort**
   - Management must present a serious alternative before any closure is considered.
   - If a site-specific issue exists, it must be proven with transparent criteria, not used as leverage.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - Job reductions, if unavoidable, should be handled through voluntary instruments, retirement pathways, redeployment, and qualification.

3. **A fair employee contribution**
   - Temporary restraint, working-time measures, and other negotiated contributions can be part of the deal.
   - But only if they are matched by binding commitments from management and ownership.

4. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - If workers make sacrifices, the company must commit to future industrial activity in Germany.
   - Otherwise, we would simply be buying time for a hollowing-out process.

5. **Protect co-determination and the special shareholder role**
   - The state will not accept being sidelined.
   - Our seat and veto role exist to protect regional interests and balanced governance, and that must remain intact.

### What I cannot accept
- A restructuring that leads to German plant closures as a default solution.
- Mass layoffs presented as unavoidable before alternatives are seriously tested.
- A one-sided labour-cost cut with no comparable contribution from management and ownership.
- Any attempt to weaken the state’s co-determination and blocking rights.
- A “competitiveness” argument that ignores the long-term damage to the company’s regional anchor role.

### My position
So my position is this:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to negotiated sacrifices if they are fair and reversible where possible.
- **Yes** to protecting jobs, sites, and regional industrial capacity.
- **No** to closures and compulsory redundancies as the solution.
- **No** to a settlement that strips the state of its legitimate co-determination and veto role.

If management comes forward with a credible plan that achieves the required savings through **alternative measures**, with **binding guarantees** and **shared burden**, then I am prepared to work constructively toward that outcome.

If, however, the plan remains fundamentally based on closures and unilateral cuts, then I will not support it. Under the statute, I can and will use the leverage available to me to protect the regional interest.
