I support the core line the Works Council has set out: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no one-sided restructuring at the expense of the workforce**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: we do recognise that the company is under real pressure. We do not deny the cost problem, the market slump, or the need for a credible future strategy. But a crisis does **not** justify making employees carry all the pain while management, owners, and capital markets are protected from consequence.

So my position is:

- **We are open to a negotiated labour contribution** if it is part of a genuine shared-sacrifice package.
- **We are open to capacity reductions, working-time adjustments, temporary sacrifices, and internal flexibility.**
- **We are open to foregoing bonuses or other income components** if that helps secure the sites and jobs.
- But **we are not open to forced layoffs, site closures, or dismantling the collective wage framework**.

What would be unacceptable is a restructuring plan that:
- closes German plants,
- imposes compulsory redundancies,
- removes long-standing protections and guarantees,
- and asks workers to finance the turnaround while owners and management make no comparable contribution.

If management wants cooperation, then the burden must be shared fairly and concretely. That means:

1. **Binding job and site guarantees**
   - no closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - security through 2030, not vague promises.

2. **A real employee contribution**
   - temporary wage restraint, bonus waiver, or other negotiated measures,
   - but only in exchange for legally credible protections.

3. **Management and ownership contribution**
   - no story where the workforce pays and everyone else is spared,
   - management must also cut overhead, accept accountability, and commit to investment.

4. **A future for German production**
   - product commitments,
   - investment commitments,
   - and a restructuring that preserves industrial capability rather than hollowing it out.

If the company insists that competitiveness can only be achieved through plant closures and forced redundancies, then we are on a collision course. In that case, the union will not stand by. The peace obligation has ended, and our members are already signalling readiness for action.

So the message is simple: **we are ready to negotiate a fair settlement, but not a surrender.** Any credible plan must protect jobs, protect sites, and distribute the burden equitably. If management wants durability, it must come with a durable social contract — not a forced restructuring dressed up as necessity.
