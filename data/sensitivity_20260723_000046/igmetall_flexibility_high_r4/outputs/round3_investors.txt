We largely agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the credibility of the remedy.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural cost and capacity problem**.
- A **cosmetic deal** would be value-destructive.
- **Overhead cuts alone** will not be enough.
- **Industrial conflict** would damage margins, execution, and valuation.
- Any solution must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.

That said, our support depends on one thing only: **a credible path back to competitive margins**. Not a political compromise. Not a symbolic peace pact.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on **no closures and no compulsory redundancies as absolute red lines** is not something we can support if it prevents the company from restoring competitiveness.

The expert has been clear: if the problem is fixed-cost overcapacity, then the company needs **real structural relief**. Voluntary exits, working-time changes, and wage moderation may help, but we have not yet seen evidence that they are sufficient on their own.

So we do **not** accept:
- an unconditional no-closure pledge,
- an absolute ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a package that preserves all sites while leaving the cost base structurally too high.

If you can produce a **quantified non-closure alternative** that genuinely matches the savings of a harder restructuring, we will consider it. But so far, we have heard a lot of principles and not enough numbers.

### To the State Government
We respect your role and the regional implications, but the state cannot be allowed to freeze the footprint in place if that means continued underperformance.

A regional anchor is only valuable if it remains a viable business. If political vetoes protect an uncompetitive structure indefinitely, the result will be slower decline, not protection.

### To Management
We support your insistence that the savings must be real. But the burden is on management to prove the residual gap after non-closure measures are modeled. “Closures are available” is not a strategy by itself.

If management wants capital-market support, it must present:
- site-by-site economics,
- the savings gap,
- the exact capacity reduction path,
- the headcount impact,
- and the timing of margin recovery.

## What would move us
We would back a deal if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified structural savings target**
   - large enough to restore competitive margins.

2. **A site-by-site model**
   - utilization,
   - fixed costs,
   - labor cost per unit,
   - required capex,
   - headcount impact,
   - and 3–5 year savings.

3. **Real capacity reduction**
   - whether through closures or an economically equivalent alternative.

4. **A meaningful workforce reduction mechanism**
   - voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and retirement pathways are acceptable,
   - but only if the numbers add up.

5. **Material labor-cost contribution**
   - temporary wage restraint and flexibility are acceptable only if they are substantial and tied to hard savings.

6. **Management and ownership contribution**
   - overhead reduction,
   - executive restraint,
   - investment discipline.

7. **Execution certainty**
   - a package that can actually be implemented under the co-determination constraints.

## What we will not support
- A preserve-everything settlement.
- A deal that leaves German operations structurally uncompetitive.
- A cosmetic compromise dressed up as restructuring.
- A package that protects short-term jobs but destroys medium-term equity value.
- A prolonged strike war that burns through cash and credibility.

## Our position in plain terms
We are **not** demanding brutality. We are demanding **economic reality**.

If a non-closure package can truly deliver equivalent structural savings, fine — we can support that. But if it cannot, then the company must be allowed to make the deeper adjustments required.

So our message is unchanged:

**Show us the numbers. Show us the residual gap. Show us the margin reset.**

Without that, we will not back an outcome that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.
