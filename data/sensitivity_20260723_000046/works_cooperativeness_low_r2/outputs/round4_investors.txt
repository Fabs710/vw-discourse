We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on a solution that is sufficiently credible for capital.

## Where we agree

- **The cost problem is real.**
- **Temporary measures alone are not enough** if the savings gap is structural.
- A **site-by-site comparison** is the right way to separate viable consolidation from true overcapacity.
- A prolonged strike would be **value-destructive**, and no one here should pretend otherwise.
- Management, ownership, and labor should all contribute in some form.

## Where we disagree

### 1) A blanket “no closures / no compulsory redundancies” line is not acceptable
To the works council and union: I understand the social logic, but from a shareholder perspective it is not enough to say “preservation first” and stop there.

If a site is structurally uncompetitive, then **closures and involuntary headcount reductions must remain possible**. Otherwise we risk locking in an impaired cost base and depressing margins for years. That is not a temporary discomfort; it is a permanent value problem.

### 2) “Presumption of preservation” cannot override economics
To the state and family holder: I support a careful, evidence-based process. But a presumption of preservation is only useful if it is a **test**, not a veto.

If it becomes a political filter that prevents hard outcomes even when the numbers are clear, then it simply delays the adjustment and increases the eventual cost.

### 3) Temporary employee concessions are only meaningful if they are paired with durable structural change
Bonus waivers, wage moderation, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, and redeployment can help. But they are not a substitute for **permanent capacity and cost reduction**.

A package that preserves every site, avoids real headcount adjustment, and relies mainly on temporary savings would be cosmetic. We would not support that.

## What we would support

We could support a package if it has all of the following:

1. **Quantified site-by-site review**
   - utilization
   - future model allocation
   - fixed-cost burden
   - savings from shift/line reduction
   - voluntary-exit potential
   - one-time closure costs
   - timing to savings

2. **Durable labor-cost relief**
   - temporary wage moderation
   - bonus moderation/waivers
   - working-time corridors
   - voluntary exits
   - redeployment and qualification

3. **A real structural fallback**
   - if the site-by-site analysis shows a plant cannot be made viable, then closure or substantial headcount reduction must remain on the table

4. **No cosmetic settlement**
   - the final package must materially improve margins and structurally lower the cost base, not just reduce conflict for a year

## Direct responses

### To management
You are right that we need permanent savings, not just bridge measures. But you still owe the market a **quantified comparison**. Repeating that closures are possible is not enough. Show the numbers by plant, and show that you have tested the alternatives honestly.

### To the works council
You are right that the workforce should not be the only shock absorber. But your refusal to accept closures or compulsory redundancies in principle is too rigid. If the business case supports hard measures, then refusing them outright is not protecting value; it is preserving inefficiency.

### To the union
We can accept temporary contributions only if they are tied to binding, measurable structural improvements. Otherwise the company gets short-term relief while the underlying margin gap remains.

### To the state
Your demand for a site-by-site test is sensible. What we need from you is a clear threshold that is **economically credible**, not a disguised commitment to block any hard step.

### To the family holder
You are right that value destruction from strikes matters. But so does value destruction from inertia. Stewardship means accepting hard choices when the numbers justify them, not merely avoiding conflict.

## Our line

We are **not** attached to closures as an ideology.  
We are attached to **risk-adjusted value creation**.

So:

- If non-closure measures can genuinely deliver equivalent, durable savings, fine.
- If they cannot, then closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available.
- A deal that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive will not earn our support.
