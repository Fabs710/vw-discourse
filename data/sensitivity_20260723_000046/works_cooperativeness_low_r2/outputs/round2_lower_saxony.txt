I will be direct.

The operational expert has confirmed something important: **temporary concessions alone will not solve a structural overcapacity problem**. I accept that. But the same expert also made clear that **the degree of permanent capacity exit required depends on how large the savings target actually is**. That matters, because management has not yet shown that closures are the only credible route.

## To management
You have made the diagnosis. You have **not yet justified the most extreme remedy**.

I agree that:
- the company needs a durable competitiveness reset,
- the cost base is too high,
- and some permanent structural adjustment may be necessary.

But I disagree with your repeated implication that closures and compulsory redundancies are simply the “realistic” answer. That is too convenient.

What you still owe this board and the public is:
- a **site-by-site comparison** of closure versus non-closure options,
- the **quantified savings** from each path,
- the **one-time costs** and implementation risks,
- and a clear explanation of why alternatives like shift elimination, footprint reduction, product reallocation, redeployment, and voluntary exits are insufficient.

Until then, plant closures are not a proven necessity; they are management’s preferred instrument.

And I need to say this clearly: **I will not support a restructuring that guts regional employment or treats co-determination as an obstacle to be routed around.** The state’s role is not decorative. Our blocking minority and board seats are there for exactly this kind of decision.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce must not be made the sole shock absorber of this crisis.

I agree with you on these points:
- no automatic closures,
- no forced redundancies as a default,
- no hollowing out of the German industrial base,
- and no one-sided burden shifting onto employees.

Where I want to be careful is this: a blanket “nothing may close, nothing may go” stance is only sustainable if the alternatives can genuinely deliver the required savings. The expert has said that non-closure options can work **if the savings target is moderate**. So I need you to be prepared for a more precise discussion than slogans.

That means:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- capacity reduction,
- and a temporary employee contribution,

but all **within a settlement that preserves sites, preserves jobs as far as possible, and protects the state’s long-term industrial base**.

I am **not** open to a deal that simply turns “temporary” into permanent workforce pressure. Employee contributions must be limited, reversible where possible, and matched by real commitments from management and owners.

## To the controlling family holding
You are right about one thing: endless delay is not a strategy. But neither is an aggressive reset that fractures the company’s social compact and destroys trust in its home market.

I can support a hard restructuring only if it is:
- **proportionate**,
- **evidence-based**,
- and **less destructive than closure-first management would be**.

Your call for alternatives before closures is sensible. I support that. But if you then drift toward accepting closures as the easy exit, I will not follow you there unless management proves there is no viable way to preserve sites and jobs.

Family stewardship should mean defending long-term enterprise value — and in this company, that includes the German industrial base and social peace, not just margin optics.

## To institutional investors
You are right to insist on durability and implementation certainty. I do not dismiss that.

But you are wrong if you think the only way to get certainty is to push through closures at any cost. This company’s governance structure is not a nuisance; it is part of the asset. If you force a value-destructive conflict, you will not get a cleaner company — you may get a weaker one.

What I can support is a package that delivers:
- real cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- and real earnings improvement,

**without** unnecessary plant closures and without compulsory redundancies where alternatives still exist.

What I will not support is a settlement that satisfies a quarterly model at the expense of regional stability, workforce trust, and long-term industrial legitimacy.

## My position as state shareholder
Let me state our line clearly:

### We can support:
- working-time measures,
- redeployment,
- qualification and internal mobility,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- targeted capacity reductions,
- site-specific restructuring where it is truly unavoidable,
- and a negotiated transformation package with binding investment commitments.

### We cannot support:
- plant closures as the starting point,
- compulsory redundancies as a default tool,
- relocation out of Germany,
- or any deal that removes or sidesteps the state’s co-determination and veto role.

That is not a negotiation tactic. That is a boundary.

## What I would require next
Before anyone talks about closures, I want management to produce a **site-by-site matrix** with:
- current utilization,
- future model allocation,
- savings from shift or line reductions,
- savings from footprint shrinkage,
- savings from voluntary exits and working-time changes,
- and the added cost/risk of closure.

If that analysis shows a site truly cannot be preserved, then we can discuss that case — **as a last resort**, not as a strategic reflex.

## Bottom line
I am prepared to back a tough restructuring.

I am **not** prepared to back a restructuring that:
- destroys jobs in the state,
- removes production from Germany without exhausting alternatives,
- or treats the state’s governance role as an inconvenience.

The company needs competitiveness, yes.  
But it also needs legitimacy, industrial stability, and a future as a regional economic anchor.

If management wants my support, it must prove that it can achieve that balance. Without that proof, I will use the blocking rights and board position available to me.
