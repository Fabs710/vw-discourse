## Moderator summary

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **serious competitiveness problem** and that **inaction is not an option**.
- There is broad support for a **durable** solution rather than a cosmetic one.
- Several possible tools are on the table in principle: **working-time flexibility, redeployment, training/qualification, voluntary exits, and selective capacity adjustment**.
- Most parties agree that any settlement must be **legally and operationally workable** and should avoid prolonged disruption.
- There is shared recognition that **skills and future investment capacity** matter.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures in Germany:** employee representatives, the state, and the family holder reject closures as a starting point; management and institutional investors want closures kept available if needed for sufficient savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** works council, union, and state reject them; management and investors see them as potentially necessary if overcapacity persists.
- **Scope of employee concessions:** unions accept only limited, temporary contributions; management and investors want a more durable and meaningful cost reduction.
- **Role of governance constraints:** the state and co-determination actors insist on a strong say; management and investors warn against governance blocking necessary restructuring.
- **Threshold for acceptable restructuring:** employee side focuses on job and site protection; capital side focuses on quantified savings, certainty, and margin restoration.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to reconcile job/site guarantees with the need for a structurally lower cost base.**
- **Whether alternatives to closures can credibly deliver savings at the same scale and durability.**
- **How much burden each constituency should bear**: workforce, management, shareholders, and the state.
- **What counts as “last resort”** for closures or redundancies, and who decides that threshold.
- **How to secure industrial peace** while still allowing enough restructuring to satisfy financial and competitive demands.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **site-by-site and measure-by-measure options**.
- Ask management to present a **quantified comparison** of:
  - closures/redundancies,
  - versus non-closure alternatives,
  - including savings, timing, and implementation certainty.
- Ask employee and public stakeholders to specify **which concessions are possible, for how long, and under what binding guarantees**.
- Aim to identify a package that is **economically sufficient, socially limited, and governance-feasible**.
