We need to be very clear in Round 2: **we agree on the diagnosis, but not on the remedy being forced onto the workforce.**

## To management
You keep saying “structural necessity,” but that is still not an argument for:
- **plant closures in Germany**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **tearing up the collective wage framework**
- **permanent insecurity for employees**

If you want the union to engage constructively, then bring **numbers**, not slogans:
- What savings are you claiming from each closure or headcount cut?
- What savings can be achieved through **working-time flexibility**, **redeployment**, **qualification**, **voluntary exit**, **capacity adjustments**, and **management/owner contributions**?
- What is the **time horizon** and what is **reversible** if market conditions improve?
- What is the exact burden each side is taking?

You say the workforce must prove alternatives are equal. No. The burden is not on us to justify why jobs should exist. The burden is on management to justify why its preferred tools are the **least destructive** and **truly necessary**.

If you cannot show that, then closures are not “necessity”; they are a choice.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- **no closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no relocation out of Germany**
- **binding protection for jobs and sites**
- **shared sacrifice instead of one-sided cuts**

I also agree with you that employee contributions must be **limited, temporary, and conditional**. That is exactly right.

Where I want to sharpen the point is this: if management is serious, we need a package that protects the collective bargaining framework while allowing a **fair, temporary labour-cost contribution**. That can include:
- forgoing bonuses,
- temporary wage moderation,
- shorter working time with partial compensation,
- internal mobility and qualification,
- and a jointly governed transformation fund.

But only if it is paired with:
- binding site security,
- no forced layoffs,
- and clear investment commitments.

## To the regional state government
You are right that this cannot be decided by management alone. And yes, your governance role matters.

But I want to be explicit: if the state wants social partnership to mean anything, then it must back a package that **stops closures and forced redundancies**, not merely “moderates” them. A vague middle ground where jobs are still lost and plants are still endangered is not enough.

The state should use its position to press for:
- regional industrial presence,
- training and reskilling,
- investment commitments,
- and a restructuring path that does not hollow out the workforce.

## To the controlling family holding
You talk about stewardship and avoiding value destruction. Fine. Then stewardship means **not** supporting a plan that destroys the company’s labour peace and social legitimacy.

A long strike would be costly, yes — but so would a restructuring that permanently poisons relations with the workforce while offering no credible long-term industrial commitment. The easiest way to destroy value is to create a fight that management can only “win” by breaking the company’s social compact.

If ownership wants stability, then it should support:
- no plant closures as long as alternatives exist,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a balanced contribution from all sides.

## To institutional investors
You keep demanding certainty and durability. We hear that. But “certainty” cannot mean certainty that workers carry the pain alone.

A package that cuts jobs, closes plants, and weakens collective standards may produce short-term optics, but it also creates:
- industrial conflict,
- lower commitment,
- skill loss,
- reputational damage,
- and long-term operational fragility.

If you want durable value, then support a restructuring that is:
- economically sufficient,
- socially fair,
- and implementable without open-ended conflict.

That means accepting that **employee contributions can be real**, but they must be part of a balanced package — not the substitute for one.

## Our concrete position
We remain prepared to discuss a serious package, but only under these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
4. **Binding job and site guarantees**
5. **Temporary, limited employee contributions only**
6. **Management and owners also contribute meaningfully**
7. **Investment and future-product commitments in Germany**
8. **Internal solutions first: working time, redeployment, qualification, capacity adjustment**

We can talk about:
- temporary pay moderation,
- bonus waivers,
- working-time corridors,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- and a transformation fund.

We cannot accept:
- closure threats as leverage,
- a permanent downgrade of labour standards,
- or a restructuring that simply transfers the company’s strategic mistakes onto workers.

## Bottom line
If the company wants cooperation, it needs a **fair shared-sacrifice deal** with binding guarantees.  
If it insists on closures and forced layoffs, then it should not expect industrial peace.

We are ready for a negotiated solution.  
We are **not** ready to sign off on a one-sided cutback plan dressed up as restructuring.
