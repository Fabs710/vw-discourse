We share the core line on this: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be asked to pay for it alone. If management wants a durable settlement, then the burden must be shared fairly across **management, owners, and the workforce**. That means real concessions on the cost side, yes — but only in a package that **protects jobs, preserves sites, and keeps the collective bargaining framework intact**.

Where I agree with the Works Council:
- **No closures in Germany**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **Protection of skills and industrial capacity**
- **A negotiated, socially responsible restructuring**
- **Internal alternatives before any cuts**

Where I need to be equally clear:
- We will not accept a “restructuring” that is simply a euphemism for tearing up standards, hollowing out the workforce, and shifting all risks onto employees.
- We will not accept open-ended insecurity or a permanent low-wage downgrade.
- Any employee contribution must be **limited, temporary, and part of a balanced package**.

If management is serious, then the bargaining table needs concrete alternatives:
- working-time flexibility instead of dismissals,
- capacity adjustments instead of closures,
- redeployment and qualification instead of job destruction,
- a fair contribution from employees **matched by contributions from management and shareholders**,
- and binding guarantees on sites, investment, and future product allocation.

We have already signaled willingness to discuss labour-cost relief and capacity reduction. That is not weakness; it is responsibility. But let there be no misunderstanding: **if the company insists on closures or compulsory redundancies, we will escalate.** The peace obligation is over, and our members expect us to act accordingly.

So our message is simple:  
**A fair shared-sacrifice deal is possible. A one-sided austerity plan is not.**
