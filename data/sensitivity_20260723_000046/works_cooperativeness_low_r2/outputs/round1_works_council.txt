As Works Council, our position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the line. The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden of transformation, and management cannot now solve strategic failures by destroying jobs and breaking historic guarantees. If the company wants a viable future, it must be built with the workforce, not against it.

Our priorities are:

- Binding job and site guarantees for all German plants through 2030
- No forced layoffs
- No plant closures or relocation of production out of Germany
- A real co-determination role for employee representatives in shaping any restructuring
- Fair, negotiated measures that preserve industrial capacity and skills

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that genuinely protect jobs and sites: working-time solutions, internal redeployment, capacity adjustments, and a fair contribution from all sides — including management, shareholders, and the company itself. But any plan that relies on closures, mass layoffs, or the tearing up of existing protections is unacceptable.

Management must stop treating the workforce as a cost item to be cut. If there is to be restructuring, it has to be socially responsible, legally robust, and future-oriented. Otherwise, we will resist it with all the means available to us.
