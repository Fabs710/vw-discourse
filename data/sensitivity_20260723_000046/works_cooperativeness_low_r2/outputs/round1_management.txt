I hear the position from the works council and the union, but I need to be very direct: **a deal that only protects the status quo is not a solution**.

We are not talking about abstract cost discipline. We are talking about a company that is under real structural pressure:
- German factory costs are **well above target**,
- the European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and the brand is **missing its return-on-sales target**.

If we do not restore competitiveness now, we will not have the financial base to invest in new products, technology, and the 2030 plan. That would put **far more** jobs at risk later.

So let me be clear on our side:

- **We need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.**
- **We need a real labour-cost contribution.**
- **We need structural measures, not cosmetic adjustments.**

On the points where you and I may overlap:
- I agree that **skills must be preserved** where they are needed for the future.
- I agree that **short-term chaos or uncontrolled disruption helps no one**.
- I agree that any outcome has to be **legally and operationally viable**.

But where I disagree is fundamental:
- **No plant closures** as a blanket red line is not credible if some sites are structurally uncompetitive.
- **No compulsory redundancies** cannot be the starting point if overcapacity is the problem.
- A package that only offers working-time tweaks and temporary concessions, while leaving the cost base fundamentally unchanged, is **not sufficient**.

The workforce has indeed carried transformation burdens. But management cannot be asked to carry the full burden of past strategic mistakes while still funding the future. Shareholders and the company need a settlement that actually changes the economics.

So my position is this:
1. We are prepared to discuss **alternative ways to achieve savings** if they are truly equivalent in scale and durability.
2. We are prepared to consider **capacity reductions, redeployment, and working-time measures** if they produce a real structural effect.
3. We are also prepared to make this confrontation explicit if necessary, because we cannot accept a cosmetic compromise.

What we will **not** accept is a package that:
- preserves every site regardless of economics,
- avoids hard decisions,
- and still expects the company to meet future investment needs.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward a package that matches the required savings in a **credible and lasting** way. Not symbolically. Not temporarily. Structurally.

That is the standard we need to discuss from here.
