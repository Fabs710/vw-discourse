## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not the general diagnosis**; most parties accept that costs, capacity, and long-term competitiveness must change.

The impasse is over **the sequencing and necessity of structural measures**:

- **Management / investors / partly ownership** want **credible permanent savings now**, and keep **closures and compulsory redundancies** available if needed.
- **Works council / union** demand **binding no-closure and no-forced-layoff commitments**, with only temporary and conditional employee contributions.
- **The state shareholder / political actor** is trying to split the difference by requiring a site-by-site proof before accepting any closure, while still resisting outright taboos.

So the real deadlock is:

1. **Whether closures are a last resort or an unacceptable starting point**
2. **Whether job guarantees through 2030 can coexist with structural reset**
3. **Whether temporary measures can be enough if the savings target is large**

---

## Concrete compromise positions to bridge the gap

### 1) Two-stage agreement: immediate bridge package + gated structural review
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Adopt a two-stage settlement:

- **Stage 1:** immediate temporary measures for 12–18 months:
  - working-time flexibility
  - bonus moderation / limited wage contribution
  - voluntary exits
  - redeployment and qualification
  - temporary management/owner contribution
- **Stage 2:** a jointly agreed, site-by-site review with predefined criteria:
  - utilization
  - model allocation
  - savings from line/shift reductions
  - closure costs
  - social impact
  - implementability

**Bridge value:**  
This gives management near-term relief while giving labor and the state a procedural safeguard before any closure decision is finalized.

---

### 2) “No forced redundancies first” with a conditional closure threshold
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Agree that for a fixed period:

- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no site closure decision** unless a quantified threshold is met showing that:
  - non-closure measures fall short by a defined margin, and
  - the site is structurally unviable even after footprint/shift reduction

If the threshold is met, then the parties move to a **site-specific restructuring protocol** rather than immediate closure.

**Bridge value:**  
This preserves the union’s priority of exhausting alternatives, while giving management a defined path if the numbers truly do not work.

---

### 3) Exchange model: job and site security in return for deeper, measurable labor-cost relief
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Tie stronger employee protections to stronger employee contributions:

- If management and owners provide:
  - binding investment commitments
  - a defined future-production plan for Germany
  - no relocation out of Germany during the agreement period
- then labor can provide:
  - temporary wage moderation
  - bonus waivers
  - flexible working-time corridors
  - targeted redeployment

But the employee contribution would be **sunsetted**, not permanent, and the agreement would include a **trigger for automatic review** if investment or volume commitments are not met.

**Bridge value:**  
This links labor concessions to enforceable countercommitments, reducing the fear of one-sided burden shifting.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a site-by-site matrix within a fixed deadline that compares closure, partial footprint reduction, shift deletion, voluntary exits, and working-time measures—so the parties can test whether closures are truly unavoidable?

### To the works council
**Question:**  
If the quantified review shows that one or more sites cannot be preserved even after full use of non-closure tools, what exact non-closure savings threshold would you accept as evidence that the company has exhausted alternatives?

### To the industrial union
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to accept temporary wage moderation or bonus sacrifice if, and only if, it is paired with binding site protection, no forced redundancies, and a verified investment program?

### To the regional state government
**Question:**  
Will you define in advance the concrete criteria under which you would support, tolerate, or block a site adjustment—so your veto is used as a rule-based instrument rather than only a political reserve?

### To the controlling family holding
**Question:**  
Will you state explicitly whether you prefer a negotiated no-closure package even if it yields somewhat lower short-term savings, or whether you will back closures if the site-by-site analysis shows them necessary?

### To institutional investors
**Question:**  
Would you support a restructuring that delivers slightly slower savings in exchange for materially lower labor conflict and higher implementation certainty, provided the savings are still durable and quantified?
