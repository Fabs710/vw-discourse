We agree on the **diagnosis** and we are now focused on whether the remedy is credible enough to restore value.

## To management
We support the need for a **durable competitiveness reset**. The company’s cost base, overcapacity, and weak return-on-sales cannot be allowed to persist. On that core point, management is right.

But we do **not** support rhetoric in place of evidence. Repeating “structural necessity” is not enough. If you want capital markets to back a painful restructuring, you need to show, site by site, that the package is:
- quantitatively sufficient,
- durable,
- implementable,
- and fast enough to stop margin erosion.

What is still missing is a clear comparison of:
- full closure,
- partial footprint reduction,
- shift/line elimination,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and labour-cost contributions.

If non-closure measures can truly deliver equivalent savings and permanence, we would prefer that route. If they cannot, then you should say so plainly. What we will not support is a cosmetic compromise that protects every footprint and leaves the enterprise structurally impaired.

## To the Works Council and Union
We understand your red lines, but we cannot accept an outcome that leaves the company with an uncompetitive cost base simply to preserve status quo employment structures.

That said, we do agree with you on one point: **temporary-only concessions are not enough for the workforce, and temporary-only concessions are also not enough for us** if they do not reduce the structural cost base. So we are aligned on the need for **real, durable savings**.

Where we disagree is your blanket refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies. Those cannot be ruled out in advance if the company’s German network genuinely cannot meet the savings target otherwise. Protecting every site regardless of economics would destroy value for all shareholders, including the workforce’s long-term interests.

We are open to a balanced package that avoids unnecessary closures, but only if it delivers:
- permanent cost reduction,
- capacity matching demand,
- and a credible path to margin recovery.

## To the Regional State Government
We respect the governance role and the need to protect regional industrial strength. But the state’s veto cannot become a mechanism for freezing necessary restructuring.

We agree that closures should not be the first resort. We also agree that alternative measures should be fully tested. But if those alternatives do not add up financially, then blocking adjustment will only prolong weak margins and weaken the share price further.

The state should use its influence to secure:
- a quantified restructuring plan,
- realistic burden sharing,
- and commitments on future investment.

What we cannot accept is a settlement that privileges regional optics over enterprise value.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
We agree with your rejection of a cosmetic fix and your concern about prolonged value destruction from conflict. We also agree that the governance structure means any solution has to be operationally and politically workable.

Where we press you is this: “exhaust alternatives” must not become code for delay. If the evidence shows that non-closure options cannot achieve the required savings, then ownership should not block the hard decision.

At the same time, if management can truly demonstrate equivalent savings through non-closure measures, then that is preferable because it reduces disruption and implementation risk. We are not ideologically attached to closures. We are attached to **results**.

## Our position
We would support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:
1. **Credible and quantified path back to competitive margins**
2. **Durable cost reduction, not temporary optics**
3. **Capacity reduction aligned with demand**
4. **High implementation certainty**
5. **No endless industrial conflict**
6. **Protection of future investment capacity**

That means we can support a package built from:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- targeted capacity reductions,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- and, where necessary, closures or other permanent structural exits.

What we will **not** support is a compromise that avoids hard decisions, leaves structural overcapacity in place, and hopes the market will forgive weak execution.

## Bottom line
We are not here to defend one instrument. We are here to defend **enterprise value**.

So our line is simple:
- **Yes** to a serious, durable restructuring.
- **Yes** to alternatives if they genuinely match the required savings.
- **No** to cosmetic settlements.
- **No** to structurally impaired margins.
- **No** to open-ended delay.

If management brings a quantified package that convincingly restores competitiveness without closures, we will back it.
If not, then closures and other hard measures must remain on the table.
