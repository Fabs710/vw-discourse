## Core impasse
The stakeholders are no longer divided on whether change is needed. The deadlock is over **which instruments may be used to achieve it** and **what must be guaranteed in return**.

In particular, three fault lines are blocking convergence:

1. **Management / capital** want closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available as real options.
2. **Works council / union** reject closures and forced layoffs as acceptable instruments and want binding job and site guarantees first.
3. **State / family holder** are willing to consider hard measures only after a quantified site-by-site test, but they are not aligned on how protective the threshold should be.

So the dispute is not about “restructuring vs. no restructuring.”  
It is about **whether restructuring can be made conditional, reversible, or site-specific enough to avoid a general conflict over plant fate and job security**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Phased restructuring with a hard sequencing rule
A compromise package could be structured in three steps:

- **Phase 1:** Immediate non-closure measures only  
  working-time flexibility, management cost cuts, redeployment, qualification, voluntary exits, bonus moderation, and temporary employee contributions.

- **Phase 2:** Binding site-by-site review with agreed criteria  
  utilization, future product allocation, capex needs, logistics cost, and savings potential.

- **Phase 3:** Only if a site fails the agreed threshold after Phase 1, then limited structural measures may be considered, with co-determination and state oversight.

This would not settle the closure question in principle, but it would prevent closures from being the starting move.

---

### Proposal 2: “No closure / no forced layoffs” pact for core sites, with conditional relief elsewhere
A second bridge could separate the company into categories:

- **Core sites:** protected through a binding no-closure, no-forced-redundancy commitment through a defined horizon.
- **At-risk sites or lines:** subject to a stricter review, but only with enhanced employee protections, transition funding, and verified alternative testing.

That would give the workforce a meaningful protection zone while preserving some management flexibility where the business case is weakest.

---

### Proposal 3: Employee contribution tied to enforceable countercommitments
Any temporary contribution from employees could be made conditional on a package of countercommitments such as:

- no compulsory redundancies for a fixed period,
- no plant closures during that period,
- defined investment in German sites,
- product allocation commitments,
- and joint monitoring with works council, union, state, and ownership.

This does not eliminate hard decisions forever, but it converts employee sacrifice into a time-limited exchange rather than an open-ended subsidy.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact quantified threshold, by plant and by timeframe, would convince you that non-closure measures are sufficient — and if you cannot name one, on what basis do you claim closures are already necessary?**

### To the Works Council
**If a site-by-site analysis shows that one or two plants cannot be kept viable without major structural reduction, what is your fallback position that protects as many jobs as possible without turning every hard case into a total veto?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What concrete combination of temporary concessions would you accept, and for how long, if it were paired with binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy guarantees?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you define in advance the exact criteria under which your veto would be used to block a closure, or will you reserve discretion until the plant-by-plant evidence is complete?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to underwrite a binding employment-and-investment covenant if it lowers short-term flexibility, or do you regard structural exit options as indispensable in every scenario?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a package that delivers slightly slower savings in exchange for materially lower industrial conflict risk, and if not, what level of conflict risk are you willing to tolerate for faster cost reduction?**

### To the Expert
**Can you translate the site-by-site framework into a simple decision matrix with minimum thresholds for utilization, savings, and future capex that would distinguish retention, consolidation, and closure?**
