From the perspective of dispersed institutional investors, the key question is not who “wins” the industrial-politics fight. It is whether the company gets back to a **credible, durable, competitive cost base** fast enough to protect enterprise value.

So let me be direct.

## Where we agree

We agree with management on the core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- structural overcapacity is a real issue,
- and the brand is not earning an adequate return.

We also agree with the family owners that this cannot be a cosmetic fix. If the outcome merely preserves the current structure while adding a little flexibility at the margin, that does not restore value.

We agree, too, that prolonged conflict is value-destructive. A long strike, ongoing uncertainty, and repeated brinkmanship all damage margins, market share, and investor confidence.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council and Union
Your position is understandable from a labour perspective, but as shareholders we cannot support a settlement that makes **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies”** an absolute starting point if that leaves the company with a structurally uncompetitive cost base.

That is not acceptable to us.

If the company’s current footprint cannot deliver competitive margins, then insisting in advance that every site must remain open and every adjustment must be voluntary is not a viable capital-allocation answer. It may protect employment in the short term, but it risks destroying the value of the business itself.

We are not interested in a deal that simply shifts the pain into the future by preserving excess capacity and high fixed costs.

### To the Regional State Government
We respect the governance role and the regional responsibility, but we do not accept the idea that the state’s veto should effectively freeze necessary restructuring. The company is listed capital, not a regional employment preservation scheme.

If the state blocks any serious adjustment, it may protect jobs temporarily, but it will also keep margins weak and depress the share price further. That is not sustainable.

### To the Family Holding
We agree that avoiding reckless value destruction matters. But “exhaust all alternatives” cannot become a code for delay. If alternatives do not produce equivalent, durable savings, then they are not good enough.

What matters to us is **outcome, magnitude, and certainty** — not the political elegance of the mechanism.

## Our position

We would support a restructuring only if it delivers all of the following:

1. **A credible, quantified path back to competitive margins**
2. **Durable cost reduction**, not temporary concessions that unwind later
3. **Clear capacity reduction**, matched to realistic demand
4. **High certainty of implementation**
5. **No open-ended strike risk**
6. **Protection of investment capacity for future products**

We can accept a mix of measures:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- labour-cost contributions,
- selective capacity reduction,
- and, if necessary, site closures.

But the package has to be **economically sufficient**. If it is not, then we will view it as a weak compromise, and we will say so publicly through analyst commentary, engagement, and capital allocation.

## What would make us move

We are prepared to back a painful restructuring if it is clearly the lesser evil versus continued margin erosion. But we need:
- hard numbers,
- durability,
- and implementation certainty.

If management can demonstrate that a non-closure package really matches the savings of closures, then fine — we will support the economics, not the symbolism.

If it cannot, then we will not support a settlement that preserves every red line and leaves shareholders with the bill.

## Bottom line

Our threshold is simple:
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No structurally impaired margins**
- **No endless conflict**
- **No value-destroying delay**

If the final deal restores competitiveness, we will support it.  
If it does not, we will not pretend otherwise.
