We take the pressure on competitiveness seriously. No one here should pretend the cost and capacity problems are fictional.

But I need to be equally clear: **a restructuring that solves the balance sheet by destroying regional jobs, closing German plants, and bypassing the state’s co-determination role is not acceptable.** That would damage the company’s long-term legitimacy and its industrial base in the regions that have supported it for decades.

Where I agree with management:
- The company needs a **durable** competitiveness solution, not a cosmetic one.
- The current cost base and capacity structure must be addressed.
- Any agreement must be operationally and legally robust.

Where I disagree sharply:
- **Plant closures cannot be the default answer.** They are a last resort, not a starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies are not an acceptable planning assumption** when other restructuring tools have not been exhausted.
- A deal that shifts the adjustment burden mainly onto employees while protecting every other constituency is neither fair nor politically sustainable.

To the works council and union, I also want to say: your insistence on protecting jobs and sites is legitimate, and we will support that. But protection alone is not enough unless there is a credible path to restore competitiveness. We cannot simply freeze the current structure and hope the market improves.

So from the state’s perspective, the acceptable path is:
1. **No closures as long as viable alternatives exist**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **A negotiated restructuring package** with working-time flexibility, redeployment, training, and selective capacity adjustments
4. **A fair contribution from management and shareholders as well**
5. **Binding commitments on investment, future products, and regional industrial presence**

If management believes savings are needed, then it must show, site by site, why alternative measures are insufficient and why the social and regional costs of closure would be justified. That burden of proof is on management, not on the workforce or the regions.

And let me add one governance point: **the state will not be sidelined in a decision of this magnitude.** Our veto and board role exist for exactly this kind of case, and we intend to use them to protect the regional interest, social partnership, and the company’s long-term stability.

We are open to a hard restructuring. We are **not** open to a restructuring that hollows out the company’s German base. If there is to be sacrifice, it must be shared, proportionate, and aimed at preserving the company as a regional economic anchor.
