The core impasse is now clear:

1. **Management and capital** want to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies available** as real fallback instruments for durable cost reduction.
2. **Works council and union** want **binding exclusion of closures and forced layoffs**, with a presumption of preservation.
3. **State and family holder** want a **site-by-site, evidence-based process**, but are not yet aligned on how far hard restructuring tools may go if the numbers justify them.
4. Everyone agrees the problem is structural and temporary measures alone are insufficient, but there is no shared rule yet for **when a site becomes truly non-viable**.

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridging positions

### Proposal 1: Conditional preservation pact
A temporary pact could be built around:
- no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- in exchange for temporary employee concessions,
- management overhead cuts,
- owner contributions,
- and binding investment/future-product commitments for Germany.

This would bridge the worker side’s demand for security with management’s need for measurable adjustment, while deferring irreversible measures unless a later review proves them unavoidable.

### Proposal 2: Site-by-site “retain / consolidate / last-resort close” matrix
Parties could agree to a common decision matrix with:
- utilization,
- future product allocation,
- fixed-cost burden,
- savings from shift/line reduction,
- voluntary-exit potential,
- and one-time closure costs.

A site would be classified as:
- **retain** if non-closure measures close most of the gap,
- **consolidate** if a reduced footprint works,
- **last-resort close** only if even a downsized footprint remains structurally uncompetitive.

This bridges the gap between “preservation first” and “hard measures available” by requiring a common evidentiary threshold.

### Proposal 3: No-forced-layoff period with a hard review trigger
Parties could agree to:
- a time-limited no-compulsory-redundancy phase,
- aggressive voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and shift reduction,
- and a pre-agreed review trigger if savings or utilization targets are not met.

That would give the workforce protection now, while preserving management’s ability to revisit harder measures later based on agreed performance data rather than open-ended pressure.

**Would you accept a time-limited preservation pact if it included binding concessions, a site-by-site viability matrix, and a clearly defined review threshold rather than an open-ended closure threat?**

**What temporary employee contribution package would you consider legitimate if it were paired with enforceable no-closure, no-forced-layoff, and investment commitments through a defined period?**

### To the regional state government
**What objective threshold would you use to determine that a site has crossed from “preserve” to “last-resort close,” and will you publish that threshold in advance?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Are you prepared to commit to preserving German industrial capacity for a defined period if management and labor can agree on a quantified restructuring package, even if that limits short-term optionality?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you support a settlement that delays closures and compulsory redundancies if it produces verified, durable cost reductions and lowers the risk of labor conflict and execution disruption?**
