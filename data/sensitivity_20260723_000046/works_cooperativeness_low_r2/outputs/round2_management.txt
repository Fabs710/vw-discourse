## To the Works Council
You keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as if declaring a red line makes the underlying economics disappear. It doesn’t.

I agree with you on one point: **the company does need future investment, skills, and a viable long-term industrial base in Germany**. That is exactly why we cannot leave the current cost and capacity structure untouched.

But I disagree fundamentally with your core position:
- **A blanket ban on closures is not credible** if some sites are structurally uneconomic.
- **A blanket ban on compulsory redundancies is not credible** if overcapacity is real and voluntary measures do not close the gap.
- **Binding job guarantees through 2030** are not compatible with the scale of structural adjustment we need unless the workforce is prepared to deliver equivalent permanent savings elsewhere.

You ask for the burden of proof to be on management. Fine — we accept that burden. But then the evidence has to be taken seriously:
- German factory costs are far above target.
- The market is shrinking.
- We have excess capacity.
- The brand is not meeting its return-on-sales target.

That is not rhetoric. That is the basis on which we are acting.

What I reject is your attempt to define the outcome in advance and then call every hard measure “unacceptable.” If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that is **equivalent in scale, durable in effect, and implementable without simply pushing the problem into the future**. Otherwise, your position is not a solution; it is a veto on reality.

## To the Industrial Union

If all we get are short-term wage moderation, bonus waivers, or flexible working time while the structural cost base remains intact, then we have not solved the crisis. We have postponed it.

I agree with you that the burden should be shared. Yes, management, owners, and the workforce all have to contribute. But the contribution has to be meaningful and durable:
- not just symbolic wage restraint,
- not just temporary flexibility,
- not just a transformation fund with no lasting cost effect.

You also say closures are a choice, not a necessity. That is only true if you can show a non-closure package with **the same magnitude and certainty of savings**. So far, you have not done that.

If you want to keep every German site open, then show me how you achieve:
- the required cost reduction,
- the required capacity reduction,
- the required margin improvement,
- and the required investment room for 2030.

Until then, “no closures” is not an industrial strategy. It is a demand.

## To the Regional State Government
