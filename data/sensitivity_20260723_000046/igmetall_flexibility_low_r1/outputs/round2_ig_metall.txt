We are aligned on the core red lines, and I want to be explicit about that:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided restructuring where employees carry all the pain**

So I agree with the Works Council on the essentials. But I also want to sharpen the debate, because management and shareholders keep hiding behind the phrase “structural change” as if it automatically justifies the harshest instruments.

### To management
You say the cost gap is structural. Fine. Then show the full picture.

Right now, what I hear is:
- plant closures are “on the table,”
- job cuts are “necessary,”
- labour must contribute,
- and everything else is secondary.

That is not a restructuring concept; that is a demand for the workforce to absorb the crisis first and deepest.

If you want credibility, put forward a **complete burden-sharing calculation**:
- management cost reductions,
- executive compensation restraint,
- procurement and overhead savings,
- owner contribution,
- labour-cost contribution,
- productivity and working-time measures,
- and only then any residual gap.

Until that is on the table, closures and forced layoffs are not “necessary”; they are simply your preferred instrument.

I also reject the idea that a settlement based on voluntary measures, capacity reductions, and temporary concessions is automatically insufficient. If the package is designed properly, those measures can be durable enough — especially if management, owners, and investors also contribute. The issue is not whether the workforce contributes. The issue is whether the burden is **shared fairly**.

### To the family holding and investors
You are both talking as if the only legitimate test is whether the company gets the exact margin profile you want. But a company is not a spreadsheet in isolation. It is a production system, a skills base, and a social contract.

I agree that cosmetic compromises are useless. I do **not** agree that “economically equivalent” means “employees should accept closures or permanent insecurity until investors are satisfied.”

If you want an alternative to closures, then accept that the alternative must include:
- a real employee contribution,
- a management contribution,
- lower short-term returns for owners,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is what burden sharing means. Not just “workers give, everyone else waits.”

### To the state government
You are right to push for industrial stability. But that means more than moderation. It means using your governance role to block a one-sided solution.

If the company’s owners and management want social peace, they need to stop treating it like a cost item to be minimized. Social peace is not a gift from labour; it has to be earned through fair terms.

### What we can support
From the union side, we remain open to a serious package that includes:
- capacity reduction **without plant closures**
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exit instruments
- temporary employee contributions
- training and redeployment
- investments in future products and German sites
- and a binding agreement that **no compulsory redundancies** will be used

We are also prepared to discuss a **temporary sacrifice element** for the workforce — but only if it is:
1. time-limited,
2. measurable,
3. matched by management and ownership contributions,
4. and tied to site and job security.

### What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- closures disguised as “site consolidation”
- permanent erosion of the collective wage agreement
- open-ended insecurity
- a restructuring where employees pay today and still face shutdowns tomorrow

### On the claim that we are “protecting an uncompetitive status quo”
No. We are protecting the conditions for a sustainable industrial future:
- skilled workers,
- stable sites,
- a functioning collective bargaining system,
- and a company that does not destroy trust in order to save a few percentage points in the short run.

A turnaround built on fear and unilateral sacrifice is not durable. It will produce conflict, not competitiveness.

### Bottom line
If management wants a deal, it must stop using closures and layoffs as the starting point and instead negotiate a **fair, quantified shared-sacrifice package**.

If that happens, we can move.  
If not, then the conflict will intensify — and we have the means to make sure that the workforce is not the only side feeling pressure.
