### Core impasse
The deadlock is no longer about whether the company needs restructuring. All parties broadly accept that it does.

The impasse is about **the instrument and the burden split**:

1. **Employee side (Works Council + Union)**
   - demands: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding site guarantees, preservation of co-determination/job security
   - accepts: temporary employee contributions, flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, training

2. **Management + ownership + investors**
   - accept: burden sharing and negotiated solutions
   - insist: the package must produce **durable, quantified, economically equivalent** savings
   - keep plant closures / footprint reduction on the table if no-closure options do not close the cost gap

3. **State government**
   - wants restructuring, but not at the expense of German sites and social stability
   - can support pressure for compromise, but not a one-sided closure plan

So the precise impasse is:

> **Can a no-closure package produce the same durable economic effect as footprint reduction, and if not, what level of site/capacity change is unavoidable while still preserving the social contract?**

---

### Possible compromise positions
Below are three facilitator-style bridging options that could be tested in negotiation.

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a conditional no-closure period
A package could be built around:
- a **time-limited closure moratorium** for defined German sites,
- immediate cost reductions via working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, procurement/overhead cuts, and management restraint,
- a **mid-term review gate** tied to hard financial and operational targets,
- and pre-agreed fallback measures if targets are missed.

**Why it may bridge:**  
It gives employees site security now, while giving management and owners a credible performance test and a future contingency if savings do not materialize.

---

#### 2) “Capacity reduction without closure” through redeployment and product allocation
Instead of closures, parties could negotiate:
- reduced output at selected sites,
- reallocation of specific product lines/platforms,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and investment commitments that keep the sites operational but smaller and more specialized.

**Why it may bridge:**  
It addresses overcapacity and cost without immediate shutdowns, which may satisfy the social and political red lines better than formal closures.

---

#### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with quantified burden caps
A structured burden-sharing deal could include:
- management compensation restraint,
- owner acceptance of lower short-term returns,
- temporary employee concessions,
- procurement and overhead savings,
- and a clearly defined residual gap after all non-closure measures.

If a residual gap remains, parties could agree in advance on a narrow set of last-resort measures, with strong procedural safeguards.

**Why it may bridge:**  
It turns the debate from abstract principles into a measurable sequence: who contributes what, in what order, and with what remaining gap.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
**What specific quantified savings package would you accept as proving that “no German plant closures” is economically viable, and what residual gap—if any—would still require further concessions?**

#### Industrial Union
**What is the maximum temporary employee contribution you would recommend, and what measurable management/ownership contributions must be in place before the workforce accepts it?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings shortfall remains after overhead, procurement, executive, and labour-side measures, and at what numerical threshold do you conclude that footprint reduction is unavoidable?**

#### Regional State Government
**What concrete governance or political leverage are you prepared to use if management presents a closure-first plan that lacks a fully tested no-closure alternative?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Would you commit to a defined no-closure trial period if the employee side accepts quantified temporary concessions, and if so, what performance triggers would end that trial?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What minimum combination of cost reduction, capacity adjustment, and margin improvement would make you support a no-closure restructuring package as economically equivalent?**
