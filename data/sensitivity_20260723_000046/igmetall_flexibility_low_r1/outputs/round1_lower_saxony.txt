As the Regional State Government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **we share the diagnosis that the company needs a credible restructuring**, but we do **not** accept the conclusion that the only real solution is plant closures and mass job cuts in Germany.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a temporary patch.
- The company must improve its **competitiveness** and restore profitability.
- There has to be a serious discussion about **capacity, productivity, and cost base**.
- The outcome must be **legally and governance-wise workable**; pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

### Where we disagree
What we cannot support is a restructuring logic that starts from:
- **German plant closures**
- **forced redundancies**
- **a one-sided burden on employees**
- **damage to regional industrial stability**

That would be economically risky and politically unacceptable. It would also ignore the fact that the company’s strength has long rested on **social partnership, site loyalty, and co-determination**, not on treating German locations as disposable cost items.

### Our position
If management wants our support, then the plan must be built around:
1. **Protecting sites in Germany**
2. **Avoiding compulsory layoffs**
3. **Preserving co-determination and the special veto/anchor role**
4. **Achieving savings through negotiated, reversible, and socially responsible measures**
5. **Backed by credible investment commitments for future products and technology in Germany**

That means we are open to:
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- productivity improvements,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- and management/owner contributions as well.

But we are **not** open to a “closures first” approach.

### On management’s argument
Management says the footprint must shrink structurally. I understand the pressure. But a structurally weaker company is not made stronger by destroying its industrial base and regional support. If the company wants long-term resilience, it needs a solution that **keeps the social fabric intact** and preserves the skills and production capacity that made it successful.

### On the union and works council
Your refusal to accept plant closures and forced layoffs is understandable and, in our view, legitimate. But it will only help if it is paired with a **serious alternative restructuring concept** that actually delivers savings and competitiveness. Saying “no” is not enough by itself; the company needs a path forward. We are prepared to help broker that path.

### Bottom line
We are **not** prepared to endorse a restructuring that guts employment or removes strategic production from Germany. We will support a negotiated turnaround only if it:
- protects jobs and sites,
- preserves the state’s co-determination role,
- and secures the company’s future through balanced burden-sharing rather than unilateral cuts.

So my message is simple:  
**Open the books, present a real alternative to closures, and we can work toward a durable settlement. If not, you should expect resistance from our side.**
