I will respond directly.

To management, the family holding, the state government, the works council, and the union: I agree on the diagnosis that this is a structural competitiveness problem. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would be value-destructive. But I do **not** accept a solution that preserves the current footprint simply because closures are politically uncomfortable.

### To the Works Council
I hear your refusal of plant closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand why you are drawing a hard line. But from an ownership perspective, **a blanket “no closures” position is not credible unless it is matched by an economically equivalent alternative**.

Right now I have not yet seen that alternative. You are asking for:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- site guarantees to 2030,
- and preservation of the existing job-security framework.

That is a very large ask. If it is to be accepted, it must be backed by hard numbers showing:
- how the cost gap closes permanently,
- how capacity is reduced materially,
- and how the margin target is restored.

Without that, you are not presenting a solution; you are presenting a veto on restructuring.

### To the Union
I appreciate the willingness to discuss burden-sharing, temporary contributions, and flexibility. That is more constructive than a purely defensive stance. But I need to be equally clear: **temporary measures alone are not enough**.

If the package does not produce a durable reduction in fixed cost and capacity, then it only postpones the problem. We cannot ask the company to fund future investment while the current structure remains materially uncompetitive.

If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be:
- quantified,
- durable,
- and economically equivalent.

Not “less painful.” Equivalent.

### To Management
I am aligned with you on the need for real structural change, but I need discipline here as well. I do **not** want management to use “restructuring” as a blanket justification for unnecessary destruction of value.

What I need from you is a precise case:
- the size of the cost gap,
- the savings from overhead, procurement, and executive restraint,
- the savings from labour-side measures,
- the residual gap after those steps,
- and why that residual gap cannot be closed without deeper footprint change.

That is the standard. If closures or site consolidation are truly necessary, prove it. If a no-closure package can match the economics, I will support it. But I will not support a compromise that is merely politically easier and economically weak.

### To the Regional State Government
Your role is important, and I respect the need for regional stability. But stability cannot become a synonym for preserving every site regardless of economics.

If the company’s cost base remains structurally too high, the result will be weaker margins, less investment, and ultimately less security for the region. That is the long-term risk we must not ignore.

So I support your insistence on:
- durable restructuring,
- negotiated implementation,
- and social peace.

But I do **not** support turning the special governance structure into a barrier against necessary change.

### My position
The family holding’s position is this:

1. **We support a serious restructuring.**
2. **We are open on the instruments.**
3. **We are not open to preserving the current structure by default.**
4. **We will support a negotiated no-closure solution only if it is economically equivalent and durable.**
5. **We will not support any outcome that permanently weakens the company’s value or leaves governance unable to act.**

### What would move us
A credible package would need to show:
- a material and permanent capacity reduction,
- a durable labour-cost contribution,
- management and owner participation,
- and a clear path back to target margins with room for future investment.

If the employee side can bring that, I am prepared to engage seriously. If not, then we should be honest that the disagreement is not about tone, but about whether the company is allowed to become competitive again.

I will not sign off on a solution that protects every legacy structure and calls that stewardship. Stewardship means preserving the business for the long term, not freezing it in place.
