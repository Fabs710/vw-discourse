I hear the message from both the Works Council and the union, but I need to be equally clear: **the company is in a structural cost crisis, not a temporary discomfort.** We do not have the luxury of preserving every site, every cost position, and every legacy guarantee unchanged while the market shrinks and competitors move faster and cheaper.

Where I agree:
- We need a **durable solution**, not a headline-only exercise.
- We need **investment in future products and technology**.
- We need a path that is **negotiated and legally workable**, not imposed without the required governance majorities.

Where I disagree strongly:
- **“No plant closures” as a starting point is not credible** if the current footprint remains materially uncompetitive.
- **“No compulsory redundancies” alone is not enough** if we cannot structurally reduce capacity and fixed cost.
- A package that only uses working-time tweaks, temporary measures, and voluntary instruments will **not close the cost gap**. That would be a delay tactic, not restructuring.

Let me state the core issue plainly:  
**German factory costs are above target, capacity is too high for the market, and the brand must restore its return on sales.** If we do not make a real structural change now, we will underinvest in the next product cycle and weaken the company further.

So my position is this:
1. **Substantial, permanent cost reduction is non-negotiable.**
2. **Capacity must come down structurally.**
3. **Labour cost must contribute meaningfully.**
4. **I am open on the exact mix** — closures, partial conversions, site consolidation, working-time arrangements, voluntary exits, and negotiated pay/cost measures — but only if the result is a **durable** reduction in the cost base.

If the employee side comes with a package that genuinely delivers:
- a sustained capacity reduction,
- a material labour-cost contribution,
- and real flexibility to align output with demand,

then we can discuss avoiding certain closures. But if the answer is simply to freeze the current footprint and ask management to absorb the gap, that is not acceptable and it will not solve the crisis.

I also want to address the idea that management, owners, and capital markets can simply “take the pain” while the footprint remains untouched. That is not realistic. Investors will not fund a business model that keeps excess capacity and above-market labour costs in place. We need a structure that can stand up through 2030, not just through the next bargaining round.

So:  
**I am prepared to negotiate hard on the form. I am not prepared to retreat on the substance.**  
A real turnaround requires deep structural change, and that must be reflected in the final package.
