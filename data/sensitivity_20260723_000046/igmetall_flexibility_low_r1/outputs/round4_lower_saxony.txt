## To management
I agree with you on one point: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not slogans. I also accept the expert conclusion that a no-closure package is **not automatically sufficient** unless it is large, permanent, and operationally credible.

But you still have **not** shown that plant closures are the unavoidable starting point. You keep saying “structural change” and “material footprint reduction,” but that is still not the same as proving that German closures are necessary.

What you have not yet delivered is:
- a fully itemized cost bridge,
- the exact residual gap after management, overhead, procurement, and executive restraint,
- and a clear explanation of why that residual gap cannot be addressed through deeper specialization, lower volumes, redeployment, and product reallocation **without formal closures**.

The production expert said something important that you are underplaying: **preserving all German plants is operationally feasible in principle** if the network is reconfigured properly. That means closures are not mechanically required. They are one instrument, not the benchmark.

So my position is:
- **Yes** to real restructuring.
- **Yes** to burden-sharing.
- **Yes** to quantified capacity reduction and specialization.
- **No** to closures as the default answer before alternatives are fully tested.
- **No** to any plan that gut-punches regional employment when a serious no-closure industrial plan may still exist.

If you want us to take closure seriously, bring the verified bridge and show why every non-closure route fails. Until then, I will not endorse German plant closures.

## To the works council
Your insistence on protecting sites and jobs is legitimate, and I support the core of it. But I also want to be careful not to turn “no closures” into a political slogan divorced from operational reality.

The specialist assessments say a no-closure path can work **if** it is not cosmetic:
- deeper specialization,
- product reallocation,
- lower duplication,
- reduced shifts,
- stronger utilization,
- and durable savings.

That is the route worth pushing. But it has to be real, not a promise that keeps every structure untouched.

So I support your red lines on:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preservation of co-determination,
- protection of the social contract.

I also agree that temporary worker contributions must remain temporary and paired with binding protections.

Where I would caution you is this: if you insist on preserving every current pattern, every product, and every internal structure, then you weaken the no-closure case. The company will need a genuine operational redesign if all German sites are to remain.

That said, I do **not** accept management’s framing that closures must come first. They do not. A no-closure package has not been disproved.

## To the industrial union
You are right to demand burden-sharing from management, ownership, and investors. I support that. I also support temporary worker contributions, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, and training — **if** they are tied to site security and no forced layoffs.

But I disagree with any suggestion that “temporary measures” are automatically too weak. If combined with:
- product reallocation,
- specialization,
- overhead cuts,
- management restraint,
- and an investment commitment,
they can form a credible no-closure restructuring.

The key is that the burden must be fairly shared. The workforce cannot be the only adjustable variable.

The experts have said:

- and the residual gap must be proven.

That means closures are not the only credible route. They remain one possible route — but not the only one, and not yet the proven one.

A deal that destroys that legitimacy is not stewardship.

## My reading of the expert assessments
The experts did not settle this in favour of closures. They narrowed the question.

### What they confirmed
- A pure, soft no-closure package is not enough.
- The restructuring must be **durable**.
- The network likely needs **real specialization** and **capacity rationalization**.
- Temporary concessions alone are not sufficient.

### What they did **not** prove
- That plant closures are unavoidable.
- That preserving all German plants is impossible.
- That a no-closure package cannot be economically credible if the network is reconfigured properly.

That distinction matters.
