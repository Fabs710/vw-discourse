### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem** and needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement that the future package must include **investment in products/technology** and a **legally workable, negotiated solution**.
- Most stakeholders accept that some form of **capacity reduction, productivity improvement, and cost reduction** is necessary.
- There is general support for exploring **voluntary measures, working-time flexibility, temporary arrangements, and negotiated contributions** as part of a package.
- All sides prefer **avoiding prolonged conflict** if a workable settlement can be found.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Employee representatives and the state government reject closures as a starting point; management, the family holding, and investors insist closures or equivalent structural footprint changes remain on the table.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** The employee side and state government reject them; management and shareholders do not rule out measures that could lead to permanent workforce reduction if needed.
- **Depth of restructuring:** Workers and the state favor preserving sites and jobs through negotiated flexibility; management and capital providers argue that this is insufficient unless it produces a materially lower fixed-cost base.
- **Burden-sharing:** Employee representatives want management, owners, and investors to share the pain more visibly; management and shareholders argue employee-side concessions must be economically meaningful and durable.
- **Role of co-determination / governance:** Employee representatives and the state insist these protections must remain intact; management and shareholders emphasize the need for governance room to execute structural change.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to achieve meaningful, permanent cost reduction without plant closures or forced layoffs.**
- **Whether voluntary exits, working-time measures, and temporary concessions can add up to a durable solution at the required scale.**
- **What specific investment and product commitments can be credibly linked to restructuring concessions.**
- **How burden-sharing would be measured and balanced across employees, management, owners, and investors.**
- **What governance safeguards can coexist with the degree of structural flexibility management says it needs.**

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from principles to **quantified options**:
- identify the **size of the cost gap** to be closed;
- test which **non-closure measures** could realistically close part of it;
- specify what **employee, management, owner, and investor contributions** would look like in numbers;
- and define what **site/job protections** are feasible under each restructuring scenario.

In short: the parties need to compare **specific restructuring packages**, not just repeat red lines.
