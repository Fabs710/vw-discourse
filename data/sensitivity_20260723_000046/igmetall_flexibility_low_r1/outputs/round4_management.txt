## To the works council
You are still reframing the issue as if the only question were whether management has “proven” closures unavoidable. That is not the full question.

The real question is: **can the German cost base be brought down to a level that is structurally competitive, durable, and financeable to 2030?**  
That is the test. Not sentiment. Not precedent. Not whether closure is politically unpleasant.

You are right that the latest expert assessment says a no-closure package is **operationally possible in principle**. I accept that. But you are ignoring the second half of the answer: the savings are likely **modest to moderate unless the company accepts deeper specialization and product reallocation**. That is exactly my point.

So where I agree:
- a cosmetic deal is worthless;
- the bridge must be quantified;
- management, ownership, and labour all need to contribute.

Where I disagree:
- your insistence that “no closures” can be treated as a default red line **before** the economics are solved;
- your assumption that permanent overcapacity can be removed without any deeper footprint change;
- your reading of the expert as if “possible in principle” means “sufficient at the required scale.”

It does not.

If you want to rule out closures, then show me the **full, verified arithmetic** and the **operational plan** that actually gets us to target margins. Right now you keep offering protections, but protections are not a restructuring.
