## Neutral moderator summary — after Round 4

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad, though conditional, agreement on several points:

- A **cosmetic** deal would not be acceptable to any side.
- The restructuring must be **durable** and **quantified**.
- The company faces a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- **Temporary measures alone** are unlikely to solve it.
- A **serious no-closure test** should be examined before any final decision.
- All sides accept that the answer depends on the **residual gap** after non-closure measures are fully counted.
- There is shared recognition that a viable package would likely need some combination of:
  - product reallocation,
  - plant specialization,
  - capacity rationalization,
  - overhead reduction,
  - labour contribution,
  - management restraint,
  - and ownership/investor contribution.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main disputes are still unresolved:

- **Whether plant closures are necessary**
  - Works council, union, and state government continue to reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, controlling family, and investors keep closures and equivalent footprint reduction on the table.

- **What the expert assessments imply**
  - Employee-side actors read the experts as supporting the feasibility of preserving all German plants.
  - Management-side actors read the same experts as showing that a no-closure package may be insufficient unless the restructuring is very deep.

- **How to interpret “possible in principle”**
  - One side treats this as evidence that closures are not needed.
  - The other treats it as a conditional statement that still requires proof of economic equivalence.

- **The burden of proof**
  - Employee-side actors insist management has not yet proven that closures are unavoidable.
  - Management-side actors insist the no-closure camp has not yet proven that its alternative is sufficient.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several central trade-offs remain open:

- **Employment/site security vs. cost reset**
  - Preserving all sites may protect jobs and legitimacy, but may limit the depth of fixed-cost reduction.

- **Speed vs. durability**
  - Non-closure solutions may avoid shutdown delays, but could take time to implement and may not be permanent unless structurally embedded.

- **Burden-sharing vs. competitiveness**
  - All parties accept burden-sharing in principle, but disagree on how much is required and from whom.

- **Operational redesign vs. structural reduction**
  - The debate has not yet resolved how far specialization and load balancing can go before footprint reduction becomes unavoidable.

- **Political legitimacy vs. financial sufficiency**
  - The state and labour side prioritize no closures and social stability; management and investors prioritize achieving target margins and investment headroom.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from position statements to **a common quantified test**.

A useful next step would be:

- a **single agreed bridge** from current cost base to target cost base,
- listing each lever separately:
  - management savings,
  - overhead/procurement cuts,
  - product reallocation,
  - specialization,
  - shift or line reductions,
  - voluntary exits,
  - labour contributions,
  - ownership contributions,
- and then showing the **exact residual gap**.

The next discussion should then answer only one question:

> After all non-closure measures are fully and independently quantified, is the remaining gap small enough to sustain all German plants without undermining competitiveness?

That is the key unresolved issue going into the next round.
