I will be very clear.

I agree with **all parties** on one point: this company needs a **durable restructuring** that restores competitiveness and creates room for future investment. I also agree that a cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.

But I **do not** accept that German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or “realistic” answer. From the regional state’s perspective, that would destroy jobs, weaken the industrial base, and damage the company’s role as a regional anchor. That is not an acceptable starting point.

## To management
You have still not proven that closures are unavoidable. You repeatedly assert that the footprint must shrink “materially,” but you have not yet shown the full arithmetic in a way that justifies irreversible plant shutdowns.

If you want credibility, bring the numbers:
- exact cost gap,
- management overhead reductions,
- executive restraint,
- procurement and process savings,
- productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits and redeployment,
- and only then the residual gap.

Until that is on the table, closure is not a necessity; it is a preferred instrument. I reject that logic.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree that a fake compromise would be value-destructive. But your version of “economically equivalent” cannot mean “the workforce carries the burden while ownership and capital simply hold the line.”

A fair and durable package must include:
- management contribution,
- owner acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- real employee contribution,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

If you want burden-sharing, then share it. Do not call it stewardship if it is just shifting adjustment onto workers.

## To the works council and union
I support your core red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no gutting of the job-security framework.

That said, if we want a no-closure solution to stand up, it must be **quantified** and **durable**. Temporary flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time adjustments can be part of that. But they must be paired with:
- management restraint,
- ownership contribution,
- and binding site and investment commitments.

## On the moderator’s bridging options
### 1) Conditional no-closure period
This is only acceptable if it is a real protection, not a disguised countdown to shutdown. A “trial” that simply postpones the same closure decision is not acceptable.

### 2) Capacity reduction without closure
This is the direction I would examine most seriously, provided it is genuine:
- lower output,
- product reallocation,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary instruments.

But if “capacity reduction” is just closure by another name, I will oppose it.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with quantified burden caps
Yes — but the caps must cover management and ownership as well, not only labour. The burden cannot sit on the workforce alone.

## What I can support
I can support a package that includes:
- capacity reduction **without plant closures**,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- a temporary employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- and binding investment commitments.

I will also support a serious review mechanism if it is tied to clear performance targets and does **not** become a pretext for later closure.

## What I will not accept
I will not support:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced redundancies,
- partial closures disguised as restructuring,
- a deal that breaks the company’s social foundation,
- or a settlement that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are not bargaining chips. They are essential to the company’s long-term legitimacy and regional function.

## Bottom line
If management, owners, and investors want a deal, then stop treating the workforce as the only adjustable variable. A durable solution must protect German sites and jobs while still restoring competitiveness.

So my position is unchanged:
- **We are open to restructuring.**
- **We are not open to closures or forced layoffs in Germany.**
- **We will back a quantified, shared-sacrifice, no-closure solution if it is genuinely economically credible.**
- **We will oppose any outcome that guts regional employment or sidelines our veto and co-determination role.**

If needed, I am prepared to use the full leverage available under the special statute to block a bad deal.
