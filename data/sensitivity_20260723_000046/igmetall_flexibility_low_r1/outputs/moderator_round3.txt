### Core impasse
The central deadlock is now clear:

1. **Employee representatives and the state reject plant closures and compulsory redundancies as a starting point.**
2. **Management, controlling ownership, and capital-market investors say no-closure is acceptable only if it delivers a quantitatively equivalent and durable cost reset.**
3. **The dispute is not over whether restructuring is needed, but over whether the non-closure package can be made large and permanent enough to substitute for footprint reduction.**

So the impasse is **not trust alone**; it is a **verification gap**:
- management has not yet provided a fully itemized economic bridge proving why closures are necessary;
- employee-side proposals have not yet demonstrated that they can close the gap at the scale and durability required.

---

### Concrete compromise positions to bridge the gap

#### 1) Independent quantified bridge with a time-bound no-closure window
**Proposal:**  
Create a jointly mandated, independently audited cost-and-capacity bridge covering:
- baseline cost gap,
- savings from management cuts,
- executive restraint,
- procurement/overhead,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity measures,
- and residual gap.

**Features:**
- fixed timeline for verification,
- no closure decision during the audit window,
- binding disclosure of assumptions,
- independent validation of whether the residual gap is small enough to absorb without closures.

**Why it bridges conflict:**  
It addresses the employee-side demand for proof and the management/owner demand for economically credible evidence before any final commitment.

---

#### 2) No-closure package with escalation triggers and burden-sharing caps
**Proposal:**  
Agree a temporary no-closure settlement with:
- employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- ownership return restraint,
- and quantified savings targets.

Then attach **predefined escalation triggers**:
- if agreed savings are not achieved by a specified date, parties reopen talks on footprint adjustment;
- if targets are met, the no-closure commitment extends automatically.

**Features:**
- burden caps apply to all sides,
- triggers are mechanical, not discretionary,
- no compulsory redundancies during the initial phase,
- a joint review board monitors delivery.

**Why it bridges conflict:**  
It preserves the employee red line for the first phase while giving management and investors a credible fallback if the package underperforms.

---

#### 3) Capacity reduction without formal closures, paired with site guarantees
**Proposal:**  
Replace “closure” with a structured capacity-consolidation plan that includes:
- product-line reallocation,
- lower output at selected sites,
- internal transfer of volume,
- specialization of sites,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- and targeted investment in the retained footprint.

**Features:**
- no formal plant shutdowns in the agreement period,
- binding site guarantees to a defined date,
- measurable capacity and fixed-cost reduction,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- periodic review of utilization and competitiveness.

**Why it bridges conflict:**  
It gives management and investors meaningful footprint reduction while preserving the employee-side demand for no closures and no forced layoffs during the agreed horizon.

### One pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact residual gap remains after management cuts, executive restraint, procurement savings, flexibility measures, voluntary exits, and redeployment are fully quantified — and what evidence shows that this residual gap cannot be closed without plant closures?**

#### To the works council
**If closure is ruled out, what permanent and quantified savings can you commit to that are large enough to close the competitiveness gap, not just delay it?**

#### To the industrial union
**What specific temporary employee contributions are you prepared to put on the table, and what binding triggers would you accept to ensure they remain temporary and do not become a one-way concession?**

#### To the controlling family holding
**What ownership-side contribution are you prepared to make in measurable terms — for example lower near-term returns, capital restraint, or investment commitments — before expecting footprint change from the workforce?**

#### To the regional state government
**What concrete leverage will you use to enforce a full no-closure test, and under what conditions would you accept a negotiated capacity reduction that stops short of formal closures?**

#### To institutional investors
**What minimum combination of permanent cost reduction, capacity adjustment, and cash improvement would you consider sufficient to support a no-closure settlement?**
