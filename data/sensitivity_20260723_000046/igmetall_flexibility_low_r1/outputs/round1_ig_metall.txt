We agree on the essentials, and I want to make that very clear: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no demolition of the collective wage framework**.

The workforce is not prepared to carry a restructuring that shifts all pain downward while management, owners, and capital markets remain sheltered. That would not be a turnaround; it would be a transfer of risk onto employees.

What is on the table from our side is a **serious shared-sacrifice solution**:

- **Capacity reduction without closures**
- **Working-time flexibility and voluntary instruments**
- **A temporary employee contribution, if and only if it is matched by real management and owner contributions**
- **Protection of all sites and a binding commitment to future investment and products in Germany**
- **No forced layoffs**

If management insists that competitiveness requires action, then the action must be **balanced**. That means:
- Management must contribute through its own cost reductions and restraint.
- Owners must accept lower short-term returns.
- The company must give up the logic of treating German plants as disposable.

We will support a restructuring that secures the company’s future, but not one that uses crisis as a pretext for breaking protections built over decades. If plant closures or compulsory redundancies remain on the table, then we are not in a negotiation about renewal — we are in a conflict about whether workers or owners bear the burden.

So our position is firm:
**protect jobs, protect sites, protect the collective agreement, and negotiate a fair contribution from all sides.**
