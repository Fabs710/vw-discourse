We have heard enough generalities. Now we need clarity.

### To management
Your argument that there is a “structural cost crisis” does not give you a blank cheque to break the industrial and social foundations of this company. You keep repeating that the footprint is “too large,” but you still have not shown why the only answer must be **German plant closures** and **forced job cuts**.

That is exactly where we disagree.

- We accept that the company needs a durable solution.
- We accept that capacity and costs must be addressed.
- We do **not** accept that this automatically means closing plants, threatening livelihoods, and dismantling the job-security framework.

If management wants credibility, it must put numbers on the table:
- How large is the cost gap?
- What exact savings come from which measure?
- What savings come from management cuts, procurement, overhead, executive compensation, and investment discipline?
- What savings come from working-time flexibility, voluntary instruments, and temporary adjustments?
- What savings are still missing after that?

Until you show that breakdown, “closures may be necessary” is just a pressure tactic.

### To the family holding and investors
You keep saying the alternative must be “economically equivalent.” Fine. Then let us be precise.

An “economically equivalent” alternative means:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site guarantees to 2030,
- and real burden-sharing from ownership and management.

If you demand a workforce contribution, then it must be matched by:
- management restraint,
- owner acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- and a genuine investment commitment to German plants and future products.

What we will not accept is this one-sided logic:
workers sacrifice security, while owners and capital markets define “feasible” as whatever protects their short-term expectations.

That is not balance. That is shifting the crisis onto employees.

### To the state government
You are right to insist on regional industrial stability and co-determination. But your role cannot be reduced to a mediator who accepts “some kind of compromise” if management keeps pushing closures.

Your leverage matters. Use it.  
A company with this ownership and governance structure does **not** get to ignore the employee side and then claim there is no alternative.

### What we can accept
We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time measures,
- temporary flexibility,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- targeted productivity measures,
- and a negotiated, temporary employee contribution.

But only under clear conditions:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site guarantees through 2030**
4. **A real investment and product commitment for every German site**
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework**

### What we reject
We reject:
- any “partial closure” or “site consolidation” that is really closure by another name,
- any restructuring that depends on fear to force people out,
- any plan that treats German plants as disposable,
- any settlement that weakens the protections workers have relied on for decades.

### On the claim that our position is “too absolute”
Yes — because the stakes are absolute.

When you threaten sites, you threaten communities, skills, supply chains, apprenticeships, and the company’s long-term industrial base. That is not a technical adjustment. It is a fundamental break with the social contract that made this company successful.

### Bottom line
Management must stop presenting closures and layoffs as the only serious option. They are not.  
If you want a real restructuring, bring forward a **quantified alternative** that preserves every German plant and protects every worker from compulsory redundancy.

If you cannot do that, then do not pretend the works council is the obstacle. We are not the obstacle. We are the line that prevents this company from destroying its own future in the name of short-term restructuring.

If needed, I am prepared to intensify resistance.
