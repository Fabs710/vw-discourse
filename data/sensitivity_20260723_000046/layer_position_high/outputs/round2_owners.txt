I will be direct.

The expert’s analysis confirms what we already know: **there is no free lunch here**. A serious competitiveness problem exists, and a purely cosmetic solution will not do. On that point, I agree with management, investors, and the expert.

But I do **not** agree that plant closures and forced redundancies should remain the default framing. In this governance structure, with co-determination, state influence, and real veto points, a solution that starts from “closures are on the table” is not just provocative — it is likely to be value-destructive if it triggers prolonged conflict. That is not stewardship.

## Where I agree

### With management
- The problem is structural, not temporary.
- The cost base must come down durably.
- Investment capacity for future products matters.
- A deal that merely postpones adjustment is not acceptable.

### With the expert
- A pure non-closure package is the hardest to make credible if the gap is large.
- A hybrid package is the most realistic negotiation form.
- The key question is whether the savings are large enough and durable enough.

### With the union, works council, and state
- Prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- Trust and legitimacy matter.
- Any workforce contribution must be tied to real guarantees and investment commitments.

## Where I disagree

### On management’s approach
Management is still speaking as if structural necessity automatically justifies maximal leverage. I do not accept that.

If management cannot yet demonstrate, with hard numbers, that non-closure and hybrid options are insufficient, then it should stop treating closures as the implicit baseline. The burden is on management to prove necessity, not on employees to prove innocence.

The expert made that clear: **the answer depends on the size of the gap**. If the gap is moderate, a non-closure solution may work. If it is mid-sized, hybrid is likely needed. Only if the gap is large do closures become increasingly likely. That is exactly why I do not accept premature closure talk.

### On labour’s position
I also do **not** accept the employee side’s claim that all German sites can simply be guaranteed through 2030 regardless of economics.

That is not a serious governance position if the underlying economics cannot support it.

A guarantee can be valuable only if it is paired with real, measurable contributions that actually change the cost base. Otherwise it becomes a delay mechanism, not a solution. And I will not support a structure that locks in value erosion.

## My position

The controlling family holding will support a **hard, quantified, durable restructuring**. Not a symbolic compromise, not open-ended postponement, and not a conflict spiral.

That means:

1. **We need the cost gap quantified by scenario.**
   - What is the savings target?
   - What comes from labour-cost measures?
   - What comes from overhead reduction?
   - What comes from capacity rationalization?
   - What is the one-off cost of each option?
   - What is the labour-disruption risk?

2. **The first serious preferred route is a hybrid package.**
   - capacity reduction,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - voluntary departures,
   - redeployment,
   - qualification,
   - temporary employee contribution,
   - management restraint,
   - and binding investment commitments.

3. **Plant closures are not my preferred starting point, but they cannot be ruled out in advance.**
   - If the numbers show that only closures or equivalent footprint reductions can close the gap, then I will not pretend otherwise.
   - But that conclusion must be demonstrated, not asserted.

4. **Forced redundancies are a last-resort outcome, not a first resort.**
   - Because the value destruction from a prolonged fight can exceed the savings from brute force.
   - Because co-determination reality matters.
   - Because the company’s social fabric is an asset, not a nuisance.

## What I would support now

I would support an immediate mandate for a **numbers-based negotiation** on a hybrid restructuring, with the following requirements:

- a transparent comparison of closure, hybrid, and non-closure options,
- annual run-rate savings by measure,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- employment impact,
- operational risk,
- strike/veto risk,
- and the investment package tied to each scenario.

I would also support exploring:
- voluntary exit schemes,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- temporary bonus restraint,
- productivity and overhead simplification,
- and selective footprint adjustments short of blanket closures.

## What I will not support

I will not support:
- a rushed closure strategy before alternatives are tested,
- a deal that protects all current structures while leaving the margin gap open,
- or a settlement that shifts the burden almost entirely onto labour while management and owners stay vague.

## Bottom line

The expert’s conclusion is the right one: **the central question is whether a non-closure or hybrid package can produce the same durable savings as closures, after fixed costs and execution risk are considered**.

Until that is answered with real numbers, I will not endorse closures as inevitable.

So my message to management is simple:
**Bring the quantified scenario comparison.**
And to labour:
**Bring a real structural contribution, not just red lines.**

If needed to produce a board-ready comparison, I would support bringing in a finance-and-operations scenario team to model the options in detail.
