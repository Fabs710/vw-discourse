I will respond directly.

To management: you are still asking the workforce to accept the deepest pain while refusing to give the only assurances that would make any contribution politically or socially defensible. You say the problem is structural; I do not dispute that. But a structural problem does **not** justify structural damage to livelihoods, sites, and co-determination.

Let me be unambiguous:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the job-security framework**
- **No deal that turns workers into the adjustment buffer while management keeps strategic freedom**

You keep saying “capacity must come out of the system.” Fine. Then show a plan that removes capacity **without closing plants and without forced layoffs**. That is the only serious starting point. If you cannot do that, then your plan is not a restructuring proposal; it is a threat dressed up as arithmetic.

To the investors: you are right about one thing — cosmetic fixes are useless. But you are wrong if you believe the only “credible” path is to sacrifice sites and jobs. A company like this does not restore value by destroying its industrial base and provoking a labour conflict across Germany. That is not discipline; that is self-harm.

To the family owners and the state: I hear your concern for long-term value and regional stability. On this we are aligned. But value and stability are not preserved by tearing up trust and breaking the historical social contract. If you want a durable solution, then you must use your influence to force management back to a negotiated path that protects all German sites.

Here is the framework we will accept for serious talks:
1. **Binding guarantees for all German sites through 2030**
2. **No forced redundancies**
3. **Real co-determination over any restructuring**
4. **A quantified alternative package** based on:
   - working-time reductions,
   - voluntary instruments,
   - redeployment,
   - qualification,
   - temporary and strictly limited workforce contributions,
   - management restraint,
   - and owner-side responsibility
5. **Clear investment commitments** in future products, technology, and plant modernization

What we will **not** accept:
- closures as a “last resort” that everyone knows are being prepared now,
- forced job cuts “if needed,”
- one-sided wage or benefit degradation,
- or any approach that makes permanent insecurity the new normal.

Management is still failing to answer the central question: **How much savings come from non-destructive measures, and what contribution are management and owners prepared to make before demanding sacrifices from the workforce?** Until that is answered, your insistence on closures is just a demand, not a justification.

If you want a credible alternative, then bring us:
- the exact cost gap,
- the savings from capacity reallocation without closures,
- the savings from reduced working time,
- the investment and margin effects,
- and the management and owner contributions.

Until then, the position of the works council remains firm: **no closures, no forced layoffs, no weakening of co-determination**. If management keeps pushing unilateral cuts, then it will continue to face mass resistance, including warning strikes and escalation across the German sites.
