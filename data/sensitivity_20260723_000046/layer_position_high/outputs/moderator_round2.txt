## 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **structural competitiveness problem** and that a purely cosmetic solution is insufficient.
- There is broad agreement that any settlement must be **durable**, **quantified**, and linked to **future investment**.
- There is shared recognition that a prolonged conflict would be **value-destructive**.
- Several parties accept that a **scenario comparison** is needed, including:
  - annual savings by option,
  - one-off restructuring costs,
  - employment impact,
  - disruption / veto / strike risk,
  - and effects on margins and returns.
- There is some common ground that a **hybrid restructuring** is the most plausible negotiation form if savings are large enough.

## 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**
  - Management and investors keep them as a possible necessary instrument.
  - Works council, union, and state reject closures as the starting point and want them ruled out.
  - Family holding prefers not to start there, but will not rule them out in advance.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour side rejects them outright.
  - Management insists they remain possible if no agreement delivers sufficient savings.
- **Site guarantees**
  - Labour and state want binding guarantees for German sites.
  - Management says open-ended guarantees would prevent needed restructuring.
- **Burden sharing**
  - Labour insists management and owners must contribute materially.
  - Management says workforce contributions must be substantial enough to close the gap.
  - Investors want credible savings, regardless of source, but do not accept a weak compromise.
- **Governance and co-determination**
  - Labour and state insist co-determination cannot be hollowed out.
  - Management warns against governance becoming a veto on restructuring.

## 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The **size of the actual cost gap** is still not specified.
- There is no agreed answer yet on how much can be saved through:
  - working-time reductions,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - wage restraint,
  - overhead simplification,
  - and capacity reallocation.
- The parties have not yet compared:
  - **closure-led**, **hybrid**, and **non-closure** options on a common numeric basis.
- The time dimension remains unresolved:
  - short-term labour peace versus long-term competitiveness,
  - temporary concessions versus durable structural savings.
- The discussion has not yet settled whether **non-closure measures can realistically match the savings from footprint reduction**.

## 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **one shared scenario table**.
- Require a side-by-side comparison of:
  - savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - job/site impact,
  - implementation risk,
  - and expected return improvement.
- Clarify the **minimum savings target** management says is required.
- Ask labour to specify which **quantified concessions** it could consider under binding guarantees.
- Ask owners and the state what **constraints or support** they are prepared to provide to make a negotiated hybrid viable.
