1) **What has been agreed**
- All sides acknowledge that the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a solution must be **durable**, not a short-term patch.
- Most parties accept that **purely cosmetic measures are insufficient**.
- There is shared interest in avoiding a **prolonged conflict** that would damage value, operations, and trust.
- Several parties are open to discussing a package that may include:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - voluntary departures,
  - temporary or conditional workforce contributions,
  - and investment commitments.

2) **Where the core disagreements remain**
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works Council, union, state government, and owners reject closures as a first or preferred response; management and investors keep them on the table as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies / forced layoffs**: Employee-side actors reject them categorically; management has not withdrawn them as an option.
- **Scope of workforce contribution**: Labour side is willing to consider contributions only with binding guarantees; management and investors want a larger, more durable cost reduction than temporary concessions alone.
- **How much burden employees should bear**: Labour and political actors oppose one-sided adjustment; management and investors insist the adjustment must be materially significant and structurally sufficient.
- **Whether all sites can be preserved**: Employee-side and state actors want site protection; management and some investor voices say this may not be compatible with competitiveness.

3) **What key trade-offs are still unaddressed**
- The parties have **not yet quantified**:
  - the actual cost gap,
  - savings from closures vs. non-closure alternatives,
  - the size and duration of any employee contribution,
  - management/owner-side contributions,
  - and the investment commitments tied to each scenario.
- There is no shared answer yet on the central trade-off between:
  - **job and site protection**, and
  - **a restructuring large enough to restore margins**.
- It remains unresolved whether a package of **voluntary, flexible, and temporary measures** can close the gap sufficiently, or whether **structural site reductions** are unavoidable.
- No side has yet presented a fully worked alternative that simultaneously satisfies:
  - competitiveness,
  - employment protection,
  - governance legitimacy,
  - and long-term investment capacity.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from positions to **numbers and scenarios**.
- Ask management to present a **clear comparison** of options: savings, risks, and operational impact.
- Ask labour, state, and owners to test whether a **credible non-closure package** can close the gap.
- Focus on **what each side would materially contribute**, and what binding guarantees would be exchanged in return.
- Keep the discussion centered on a **quantified, durable settlement**, rather than repeating red lines alone.
