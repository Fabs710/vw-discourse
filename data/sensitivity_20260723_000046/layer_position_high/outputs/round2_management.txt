I’ll respond plainly.

## To the Works Council
I understand your red lines, but I do **not** accept the proposition that we can secure the company’s future while preserving all current German sites and all current cost structures.

You say: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of co-determination.  
I say: if we lock those outcomes in now, then we also lock in an **uncompetitive cost base**. That is not responsible management, and it is not a durable solution.

You keep asking for a plan that removes capacity without closures and without forced layoffs. That is exactly the kind of formulation that sounds protective but can easily become a **semantic escape route**. If the output capacity comes down, if fixed costs must come down, and if the business needs a structural reset, then at some point you cannot rule out site changes in advance and still claim seriousness.

What I can accept:
- capacity reduction through negotiated instruments,
- redeployment,
- voluntary departures,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary contributions,
- qualification measures,
- and a binding investment package.

What I cannot accept:
- a guarantee framework that makes structural adjustment impossible,
- a deal that preserves every site regardless of economics,
- or a “socially acceptable” package that leaves the company’s cost base fundamentally unchanged.

If you want to avoid closures, then you must bring something **quantitatively equivalent**. Not slogans. Numbers. If you can close the cost gap with durable labour-cost relief, capacity reduction, and operational flexibility, then I am prepared to engage. If not, then we are just delaying the inevitable.

## To the Trade Union
You are right that cosmetic fixes are useless. On that, we agree.

But you are wrong to frame management’s position as if we were asking labour to “finance” the crisis alone. We are not. We are asking for a **real structural contribution** because the current structure is not competitive.

I also reject the claim that closures and compulsory redundancies are just a negotiating threat. No — they are a real consequence if the company cannot get the savings it needs through agreement. I would prefer a negotiated path. But I will not pretend the alternative is acceptable if it leaves the business structurally weak.

You say:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no open-ended insecurity,
- no one-sided wage cuts.

I say:
- no one-sided workforce protection that ignores the economic reality,
- no deal that protects today’s expectations while starving the future of investment,
- no “balanced package” that is too small to matter.

If you want a durable settlement, then the workforce contribution must be **material, sustained, and enforceable**. Short-term bonus restraint alone is not enough. Working-time gimmicks alone are not enough. Voluntary departures alone are not enough.

## To the State Government
I appreciate your concern for regional employment and industrial stability. But stability without competitiveness is not a solution; it is a slow decline.

You say German sites should not be treated as disposable. I agree. But sites also cannot be treated as untouchable if they are structurally too expensive. That is precisely the point of restructuring.

I do take your point that a destructive confrontation would damage value. That is why I am here, and why I am still pushing for a negotiated settlement. But I need you to understand this clearly: **if the political response is to block all real adjustment, then you are not protecting value — you are postponing its destruction.**

If you want to help, then use your influence to support:
- a serious workforce contribution,
- faster capacity reduction,
- binding productivity improvement,
- and a cost base that actually allows future investment in Germany.

## To the Controlling Family Holding
I agree with you on one key point: a prolonged conflict would destroy value. I do **not** want that either.

Where I disagree is your suggestion that we should first exhaust every alternative before taking closures seriously. We have already exhausted years of delay, underinvestment in competitiveness, and the assumption that the old model could somehow carry on. That is over.

You say you prefer a quantified alternative package. Fine — so do I. But that package has to be credible in the **real world**, not just acceptable in a boardroom. If the package does not produce enough savings, then it is not an alternative.

You also say you do not want a process that destabilizes governance. Neither do I. But governance also cannot become a veto over necessary restructuring. A company that cannot adjust its footprint will not preserve value for long.

## To the Institutional Investors
You are right to demand numbers. I support that.

Let me be equally clear: investor patience does **not** create economic reality. You can say “show us the path back to target returns” all you want — that is exactly what management is trying to do. But the path cannot be built on denial.

You do not get to insist on target returns and simultaneously reject the structural measures that make them possible.

So here is the position:
- yes, we need a quantified path back to target margins,
- yes, cosmetic compromise is unacceptable,
- yes, the burden must be materially shared,
- but no, the answer is not a permanent guarantee regime that prevents real restructuring.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am prepared to consider a serious negotiated package if it delivers all of the following:
1. **Durable reduction in labour cost**
2. **Meaningful reduction in overcapacity**
3. **A structurally lower cost base, not just a temporary bridge**
4. **Binding contribution from the workforce**
5. **Investment focus through 2030**
6. **A credible path back to return-on-sales target**

That package may include:
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary concessions,
- site-specific transformations,
- and, if necessary, site rationalization.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a deal that rules out closures in advance,
- a deal that preserves full employment guarantees regardless of economics,
- a deal that substitutes symbolism for structural savings,
- or a deal that leaves us exposed to the same cost crisis in 18 months.

## Bottom line
We need a settlement that is **hard enough to matter**.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it must bring a package that closes the gap in a durable and measurable way. If it cannot, then management will have no choice but to continue pursuing more forceful restructuring measures.

I am not moving off the core objective:
**restore competitiveness, cut overcapacity and labour costs, and free up funds for future products and technology to 2030.**

If you want to talk seriously, bring a numbers-based alternative that actually achieves that.
