I hear the red lines being stated, but I need to be equally clear: **a solution that leaves our German cost base structurally uncompetitive is not a solution**.

We are not in a theoretical debate. We are facing a shrinking European market, brutal price pressure, and a return-on-sales gap that is already undermining our ability to invest in future products, software, and battery technology. If we do not make a **durable** adjustment now, we will lose far more jobs later because the business will continue to erode.

So let me respond directly:

- **I agree** that trust matters, and that any change has to be negotiated responsibly.
- **I disagree** that “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” can be fixed in advance as untouchable absolutes. If we are serious about competitiveness, then **capacity must come out of the system**. That may include site changes, not just temporary instruments.
- **I also disagree** that the burden can be met through a cosmetic package of working-time tweaks, voluntary exits, or short-term wage restraint alone. That would not correct the structural cost problem.

What management needs is not symbolic participation — it is a **material, durable labour-cost contribution and a real reduction in overcapacity**. That means:

1. **A meaningful reduction in German production capacity**
2. **Lower labour costs on a sustained basis**
3. **No deal that simply postpones the problem to 2028 or 2030**
4. **A settlement that restores margins and frees investment capacity**

If the employee side is prepared to discuss a package that genuinely delivers those outcomes, then I am prepared to consider the path by which that happens. That could include redeployment, working-time flexibility, transformation measures, and voluntary instruments — but only if they are part of a **credible structural package**, not a substitute for one.

To be explicit:  
- I will not accept a paper agreement that protects every existing structure and leaves us with the same cost base.  
- I will not accept a compromise that preserves all sites while asking management to absorb the competitiveness gap indefinitely.  
- I will not ask our investors, owners, and customers to finance an uncompetitive domestic footprint.

We are open on **how** the savings are achieved. We are not open on **whether** they must be achieved.

If the workforce wants to avoid closures, then it needs to bring forward an alternative that is **quantitatively credible**, **durable**, and **large enough** to close the gap. Otherwise, the logic of the situation points toward deeper intervention, including plant-level measures.
