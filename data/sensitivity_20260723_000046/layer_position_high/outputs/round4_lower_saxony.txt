## To management
I agree with you on one point: this company needs a **durable structural reset**, not a cosmetic truce. I also accept that the cost base must come down and that temporary gestures alone will not restore competitiveness.

Where I disagree is on your continued insistence that **plant rationalization must remain on the table as a standing option** before you have shown that a no-closure or deep hybrid package cannot close the gap.

The scenario work does **not** prove that closures are necessary. It says:
- a **no-closure** package can work if the gap is moderate,
- a **deep hybrid** is the most plausible negotiated route,
- and closure-led restructuring carries the highest implementation and conflict risk.

That means your closure argument is still a claim, not a proof.

What I will not accept:
- closures as the default benchmark,
- forced redundancies as leverage,
- or a restructuring that guts regional employment in order to preserve management freedom.

If the economics truly require footprint reduction, then show it in plant-level numbers. Until then, you should stop treating irreversible damage as inevitable.

## To the works council and trade union
I agree with you that:
- no cosmetic compromise is acceptable,
- no one-sided burden shifting onto labour is acceptable,
- co-determination must not be hollowed out,
- and forced redundancies are the wrong instrument.

I also agree that the burden must be **shared** and that temporary workforce contributions can only be discussed with binding guarantees.

Where I do not fully follow you is on making **“no closures” an absolute starting condition without testing the full economics**. I understand the political and social logic, but I cannot support a position that closes off every footprint adjustment regardless of what the numbers show.

That said, the current scenario work does **not** justify management’s closure-first posture. It actually strengthens the case for a **deep negotiated hybrid** before any talk of shutdowns.

So my position is:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no sidelining of co-determination**
- **no one-sided labour bill**
- **serious negotiation on a deep hybrid package**
- **closures only if, and only if, the plant-level model proves there is no viable alternative**

That is a much higher bar than management is currently trying to set.

## To the controlling family holding

Where I disagree is with any assumption that the “harshest” plan is automatically the best value plan. In a company like this, conflict risk, veto risk, and strike risk are not side issues; they can destroy the financial case.

So yes:
- demand hard numbers,
- demand durable savings,
- demand real management and owner contributions.

But do **not** push the company into a closure-led solution before it is proven necessary. That would be reckless in a co-determined governance system with real blocking minorities.

## My position
The state will support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:

1. **It is quantitatively credible**
   - We need a plant-level comparison of:
     - no-closure,
     - deep hybrid,
     - closure-led.
   - Including annual savings, one-off costs, employment impact, implementation risk, and return-on-sales effect.

2. **It protects core employment and sites in the state**
   - I am not prepared to endorse regional industrial hollowing-out.
   - The company must remain a regional anchor, not a shell.

3. **It preserves co-determination and our veto role**
   - Any attempt to bypass or weaken those rights is unacceptable.

4. **It shares the burden fairly**
   - Management, owners, and labour must all contribute.
   - The workforce is not the only adjustment buffer.

5. **It is durable**
   - Temporary concessions that merely postpone the problem are not enough.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to support a **deep hybrid restructuring** if it is genuinely deep enough to restore competitiveness. That can include:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- temporary and strictly limited employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- overhead simplification,
- productivity improvements,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is the zone where a negotiated settlement may still protect the company’s industrial base.

## What I am prepared to block
I will block any outcome that:
- starts from plant closures,
- relies on forced redundancies,
- or treats the state as a bystander while regional employment is permanently damaged.
