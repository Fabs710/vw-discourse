As dispersed institutional shareholders, our position is straightforward: **we support a restructuring only if it is credible, durable, and sufficient to restore competitive margins**. That is the only basis on which we can underwrite the business through this cycle.

Where we agree:
- We agree with management that the current German cost base is a real problem, not a rhetorical one.
- We agree that a cosmetic fix would be value-destructive.
- We agree with the family holding that prolonged conflict and strikes would destroy value.
- We agree with the union, works council, and state government that a settlement needs legitimacy and cannot simply be a one-sided demand on labour.

Where we disagree:
- We do **not** accept an outcome that leaves the company with a structurally uncompetitive cost base.
- We do **not** support a deal that preserves every site and every legacy protection if that means the margin gap remains open.
- We do **not** support indefinite wage restraint or temporary measures that merely delay the adjustment.
- We do **not** support a political or symbolic compromise that is too weak to change operating performance.

Our benchmark is simple: **show us a quantified path back to target returns on sales and competitive margins**. If the alternative package from labour and ownership can close the cost gap materially and durably, then we will support it. If it cannot, then resisting plant rationalization is not a sustainable position.

To management:
You need to present hard numbers, not just warnings. How much savings come from closures, how much from working-time changes, how much from productivity, and what the earnings impact is under each scenario. Without that, the market will discount the plan as bluff or hesitation.

To labour:
Your refusal of closures and forced redundancies may be politically understandable, but from our perspective it is not sufficient on its own. If you want to avoid structural cuts, then you need to put forward an alternative that is **equally durable, equally large, and enforceable**. Temporary contributions, voluntary departures, and capacity flexibility may help, but only if they add up to a real reset.

To the state and family owners:
Your insistence on social stability is sensible, but stability without competitiveness is not value-preserving. If governance blocks necessary restructuring, capital will reprice the company accordingly. We will not provide patient capital for a business model that cannot earn its cost of capital.

So our message is:
- **No to cosmetic compromise**
- **No to open-ended margin erosion**
- **Yes to a credible, quantified, durable restructuring**
- **Yes to a negotiated solution if it actually closes the competitiveness gap**

If the next proposal does not materially improve the numbers, then from our side the market will increasingly conclude that the company is avoiding the hard choices rather than making them.
