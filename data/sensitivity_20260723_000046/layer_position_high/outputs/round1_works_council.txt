As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the red line.

If management wants a serious solution, then it must be based on:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- binding job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- real co-determination over any restructuring,
- and a fair burden-sharing approach that does not place the entire adjustment on the workforce.

We understand the competitive pressure and the need to secure the company’s future. But the answer cannot be to sacrifice workers and destroy industrial capacity in Germany while management preserves strategic freedom at our expense. A company that breaks trust, tears up long-standing job-security commitments, and announces closures as a first move should not expect cooperation.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary measures,
- redeployment,
- qualification and transformation agreements,
- and, if necessary, a temporary and strictly limited employee contribution tied to hard guarantees on jobs and investment.

But we will oppose any plan that includes:
- shutting German sites,
- forced job cuts,
- or dismantling co-determination and established protection frameworks.

If management wants peace, it needs to come to the table with a credible plan that protects all German sites and secures employment for the long term. Otherwise, it should expect strong resistance from the workforce.
