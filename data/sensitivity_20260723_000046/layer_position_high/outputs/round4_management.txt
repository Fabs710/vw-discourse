## To the works council and union
I hear the repeated line: “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of co-determination.” I understand the red lines. I do **not** accept them as a prior condition for restructuring.

The scenario work does **not** prove that a no-closure solution is sufficient. It says it **may** work if the gap is moderate and if the package is deep enough. That is not the same as proving that we can rule out footprint reduction.

You are making a category error: you are treating “possible in principle” as “sufficient in practice.”

What I agree with:
- a cosmetic deal is useless,
- temporary concessions alone are not enough,
- management and owners must contribute,
- and a destructive conflict would be bad for everyone.

What I reject:
- a blanket no-closure commitment up front,
- open-ended job and site guarantees through 2030,
- and the attempt to turn co-determination into a veto over necessary structural adjustment.

If we preserve all current German sites regardless of economics, then we do **not** solve the crisis. We postpone it and weaken the company further.

## To the state government
You are right that governance legitimacy matters and that one-sided burden shifting is unacceptable. But you are wrong if you believe the state should use its blocking power to pre-empt all footprint reduction before the economics are tested.

Your role should be to insist on a credible, quantified restructuring — not to freeze the cost base in place.

I agree with you on:
- the need for a quantified comparison,
- the need for burden-sharing,
- and the need to avoid a value-destructive strike spiral.

I disagree with you on the implication that “no closures” should be the default state position. That is a political preference, not an industrial solution.

## To the family holding
You are closer to my position than some others, but not close enough.

You say closures are not preferred and that you will not rule them out if numbers justify them. Fine. But “not ruling out” is not enough if management is then forced to negotiate under permanent ambiguity and labour is given the impression that nothing structurally painful will ever happen.

I need the family to back the hard part: a real restructuring with teeth.

If the model shows that a hybrid package closes the gap, I will take it. If it does not, then we cannot keep all sites open simply because that is the easier political outcome.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to reject cosmetic compromise. You are also right that the company needs a lower cost base, credible run-rate savings, and a real return path.

Where I disagree with you is your tendency to treat the harshest option as the serious one. It is not automatically the best value option if it triggers strikes, veto risk, implementation delay, and reputational damage.

That said, I do not accept the opposite extreme either: a weak no-closure compromise that leaves the structural gap open.

So the test is simple: **does the package actually restore competitiveness at scale?**

If yes, I support it. If no, then we need footprint reduction.

## The core point you are all still avoiding
Nobody has yet shown me a quantified, durable, industrially credible alternative that:
- preserves all current sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- materially reduces the fixed-cost base,
- and closes the return-on-sales gap through 2030.

That is the burden the employee side is trying to shift away from.

The expert numbers are useful, but they also show the limits:
- no-closure can work only in the moderate-gap case,
- hybrid is the most plausible negotiated path,
- closures become more likely if the gap is large.

That is exactly why I will **not** withdraw plant rationalization from the table.

- productivity improvements,
- overhead simplification,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,

But I need this stated clearly: those measures must add up to a **structural** cost reset, not a temporary truce.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a blanket no-closure guarantee,
- permanent preservation of all German sites regardless of utilization,
- a deal that leaves us uncompetitive into 2030,
- or an agreement that turns short-term labor peace into long-term margin erosion.
