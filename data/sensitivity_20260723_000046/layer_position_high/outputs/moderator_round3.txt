## Core impasse

The main deadlock is between:

1. **Employment-security red lines**  
   Works council and union require:
   - no plant closures
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - no weakening of co-determination

2. **Structural-cost-reset necessity**  
   Management and investors require:
   - a materially lower cost base
   - credible annual run-rate savings
   - some form of capacity reduction if the gap is too large

3. **The missing quantified bridge**  
   All sides now agree the key unknown is:
   - whether management-side, owner-side, and workforce-side measures can close the gap **without** footprint reduction

So the impasse is not about whether change is needed; it is about **what level of structural adjustment is required, and who bears it**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Phased restructuring with a decision gate
A time-limited package is agreed first, built around:
- working-time changes
- voluntary departures
- redeployment
- qualification
- management restraint
- owner-side support

Then a **binding review gate** is set after a fixed period, using pre-agreed metrics:
- savings delivered
- utilization
- margin effect
- employment impact
- implementation risk

If targets are met, no further measures are triggered.  
If targets are missed, the parties move to a second-stage negotiation on deeper capacity measures.

**Purpose:**  
This bridges the gap between “no closures now” and “management needs proof of durability.”

---

### Proposal 2: No compulsory redundancies, but limited footprint adjustment only by negotiated attrition and specialization
This keeps:
- no forced layoffs
- no unilateral closures

But allows:
- site specialization
- shift elimination
- line transfers
- natural attrition
- voluntary exit packages
- partial site downsizing

In other words, capacity can come out of the system without an immediate closure decision.

**Purpose:**  
This offers management a real cost-base reduction while preserving the works council and union’s core red lines.

---

### Proposal 3: Binding burden-sharing corridor
A structured burden-sharing formula is set in advance:
- management contribution
- owner contribution
- workforce contribution

Each side commits to a defined range, and the company’s response is linked to the amount of verified gap closed inside that corridor.

If the quantified gap remains outside the corridor after those contributions, the parties reopen discussion on footprint measures.

**Purpose:**  
This moves the dispute from abstract principle to measurable distribution of effort.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact annual run-rate savings do you expect from non-closure measures alone, after management-side and owner-side contributions, and at what point do you say those measures are no longer sufficient?**

### To the works council
**If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are guaranteed, what specific time-bounded workforce contributions would you accept to close the gap, and under what measurable conditions would you allow the negotiation to move to a second-stage review?**

### To the trade union
**What is the maximum temporary burden-sharing package you are prepared to support, in quantified terms, before you would consider that the non-closure path has been exhausted?**

### To the state government
**What concrete use of your blocking power are you prepared to tie to a quantified no-closure or hybrid scenario, and what minimum employment and site-protection conditions must be met for you not to intervene?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Will you commit to forcing management to present a fully quantified no-closure and hybrid package first, before any closure option is treated as actionable?**

### To the institutional investors
**What minimum savings and margin-improvement thresholds would make you support a no-closure or hybrid deal, and would you accept a phased approach if it proves the economics before any footprint decision?**

### To management-side finance and operations modeler
**What is the best estimate of the gap after all non-closure levers, and how sensitive is that estimate to implementation slippage, labor peace, and owner-side support?**
