I will respond as clearly as possible.

## To management

You are right about one thing: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic patch. But you are still trying to define “durable” in a way that shifts the structural burden almost entirely onto the workforce.

That is not acceptable.

Let me be explicit:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No open-ended insecurity for the workforce**
- **No one-sided adjustment through wage cuts and job losses**

If management wants employees to contribute, then it has to be in a **balanced package** that includes real contributions from management, owners, and the company’s strategic decision-makers. You cannot ask the workforce to finance the adjustment while preserving all management options and leaving ownership contributions vague.

You keep saying capacity must come out of the system. Fine. Then present a plan that achieves that **without closures and without forced layoffs**. If you cannot do that, then your proposal is not yet a restructuring plan — it is a demand for the workforce to absorb the pain.

## To the works council

I agree with your core line: **there will be no acceptance of closures or compulsory redundancies**. That is non-negotiable from our side as well.

I also agree that co-determination cannot be hollowed out. A restructuring that treats the workforce as a passive adjustment variable would destroy trust and provoke prolonged conflict. We will not accept that.

Where I want to sharpen the point is this: we do need to be prepared for a **serious, quantified alternative package**. That means:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- voluntary instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- capacity adjustments,
- and possibly a **strictly limited employee contribution**.

But this only becomes negotiable if it is tied to:
- binding site guarantees,
- binding employment security,
- and real investment commitments.

Without that, there is no basis for workforce sacrifice.

## To the family owners and the state government

You are both right to reject a destructive “cut first, negotiate later” approach. I welcome that.

But I also need to say this clearly: if you want the union to engage constructively, then your influence must be used to force management into a **fair burden-sharing model**. It is not enough to say you prefer social stability. Stability must be backed by concrete commitments.

That means:
- no closures as the default,
- no forced redundancies,
- management restraint,
- owner-side responsibility,
- and a serious industrial plan for future products and investment.

If you want labour peace, then you need to back a deal that workers can actually defend in front of their members.

## To the investors

You are correct that cosmetic compromise is not enough. We are not offering one.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible answer is deep structural cuts through closures and permanent labour degradation. That is short-sighted. A company of this size does not regain value by detonating trust, provoking industrial action, and destroying industrial capacity in Germany.

What you should support is a package that is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **fairly shared**
- and **credible on competitiveness**

That may include labour contributions, but not at the expense of basic job security and wage standards.

## What we are prepared to discuss

We remain open to a real settlement based on shared sacrifice, including:

- temporary wage restraint or the forgoing of bonuses,
- working-time reductions,
- flexibility in capacity utilization,
- redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- voluntary departure options,
- and a time-limited employee contribution,

**but only if** there are binding guarantees on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- collective wage framework protection,
- job security,
- and investment in future products and sites.

## What we reject

We reject:
- closures “as an option” that are obviously being kept ready as leverage,
- forced layoffs,
- permanent wage degradation,
- breaking the collective framework,
- and making workers bear the full cost of management’s strategic mistakes.

## Bottom line

We are not blocking a solution. We are blocking a **one-sided solution**.

If management wants a deal, it must bring:
1. the exact quantified cost gap,
2. the savings from non-destructive measures,
3. the concrete management and owner contribution,
4. and binding guarantees that any workforce contribution will protect jobs and sites.

Until then, we remain in opposition and will use the instruments at our disposal. If necessary, that includes further industrial action.
