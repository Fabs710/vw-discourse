We welcome the more quantified discussion, and we agree with the expert and the family holding on the central point: **there is no credible value case for a cosmetic settlement**.

Our position remains straightforward:

- **We support a restructuring only if it is large, durable, and financially credible.**

## Where we agree

### With management
You are right that the problem is structural, not temporary. If the company cannot restore margins and free up investment capacity, the equity story deteriorates further. We also agree that short-term strikes and prolonged conflict would destroy value.

### With the expert
The scenario analysis is helpful, and the core conclusion is the right one:  
- a pure non-closure solution is the hardest to make credible if the gap is large,
- a hybrid package is the most realistic settlement form,
- closures become more likely as the required savings target rises.

### With the family holding
We agree that conflict is value-destructive and that a negotiated solution is preferable if it can actually close the gap.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
Your insistence on “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as fixed red lines may be politically understandable, but it is **not sufficient** from a shareholder perspective.

If those red lines prevent the company from reaching a competitive cost base, then they are not protecting value — they are impairing it.

We are not asking labour to “sacrifice for its own sake.” We are saying that if the business case requires meaningful footprint reduction, then that has to remain possible. A package that merely preserves all sites and all legacy protections while the margin gap remains open is not acceptable to us.

### With management
At the same time, management has not yet earned a blank cheque for closures. The expert is right that the key question is the size of the gap. So we support the demand for a **scenario-based comparison** before any final commitment.

## Our benchmark

We need to see, in numbers:

1. the annual cost gap,
2. the run-rate savings from each option,
3. the one-off restructuring costs,
4. the employment impact,
5. the strike/veto risk,
6. the expected margin and return-on-sales outcome.

Without that, no one can honestly claim the plan is optimal.

## What we would support now

We would support a **hard, board-level scenario process** with three options:

- **Closure-led restructuring** if that is the only route to sufficiently large durable savings.
- **Hybrid restructuring** if it achieves most of the required savings with lower conflict and acceptable execution risk.
- **Non-closure restructuring** only if it can be shown to close the gap materially and durably, not merely defer the problem.

We are not attached to one mechanism. We are attached to the outcome: **competitive margins, disciplined cost reduction, and a credible return on capital**.

## What we would not support

We would not support:

- a settlement that preserves every site by default,
- a package of temporary concessions that expires before the economics improve,
- a political compromise that looks stable but leaves the business underperforming,
- or an outcome that keeps the market guessing and depresses the share price further.

The standard is not comfort. The standard is **durable competitiveness and shareholder value**.
