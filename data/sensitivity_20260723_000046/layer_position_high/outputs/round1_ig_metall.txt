I agree with the Works Council on the core point: **there will be no acceptance of plant closures or compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiating detail; it is the line that defines whether there is a deal at all.

Management has a legitimate problem with costs and competitiveness, but **it cannot solve that problem by putting the entire burden on the workforce**. If the company wants employees to contribute, then that contribution must be part of a **balanced package** with real sacrifice from management, the company, and the owners as well.

Our position is clear:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided wage cuts without binding guarantees**
- **No “restructuring” that creates open-ended insecurity for the workforce**

We are prepared to discuss a credible alternative that protects jobs and sites, including:
- temporary wage restraint or the forgoing of bonuses,
- working-time reductions,
- flexibility in capacity,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary departure options,
- and a transformation contribution from the workforce — **but only if jobs, sites, and investment commitments are secured in return**.

If management’s only answer is closures, forced layoffs, and permanent wage degradation, then there is nothing to negotiate from our side. In that case, we will use the instruments available to us, including industrial action. The workforce will not pay for a crisis it did not create while shareholders and management escape responsibility.

What we need is a **durable settlement based on shared sacrifice and job security**. Anything else is unacceptable.
