We agree on the diagnosis: this company has a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive. We also agree that a prolonged conflict would hurt margins, cash flow, and valuation.

Where we differ is on what counts as a credible fix.

## To management
You are still too willing to keep closures and forced redundancies as a standing threat. From a capital-markets perspective, that is only acceptable if you can show that the alternatives fail on hard numbers.

Right now, the scenario work suggests:
- **no-closure** may work if the gap is moderate,
- **deep hybrid** is the most plausible negotiated route,
- **closure-led** carries the highest execution and conflict risk.

That matters. Because what destroys shareholder value here is not just high labour cost — it is **value leakage from delay, strikes, veto conflict, and reputational damage**.

So I do not accept a closure-first posture without proof. You need to show, plant by plant, that the savings gap cannot be closed by:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity/overhead simplification,
- management restraint,
- and owner-side contribution.

If you cannot show that, then closures are not “necessary”; they are just the harshest available option.

## To the works council and union
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. We would not support that either. A weak compromise that leaves the cost base uncompetitive would be unacceptable.

But I need to be equally clear: a blanket “no closures, no forced redundancies” position is not enough on its own. We do not invest in protected cost structures; we invest in **returns**. If the company cannot restore margins, the share price and capital allocation suffer.

So your position is only credible if the alternative package is genuinely deep and durable. That means:
- real savings,
- not symbolic savings,
- not temporary relief that fades in 18–36 months,
- and not a deal that preserves all legacy costs while calling itself restructuring.

We are open to a non-closure or hybrid deal, but only if it materially restores competitiveness.

## To the state government
We agree with your insistence on co-determination, burden-sharing, and regional responsibility. But the state should not use its blocking role to freeze industrial adjustment if the economics require more.

From a shareholder perspective, the state’s role should be to ensure:
- the numbers are real,
- the burden is shared,
- and the outcome is durable.

It should not be to guarantee every site regardless of economics.

## To the family holding
You are closer to the right stance than management is. You are right not to prefer closures, and right that value destruction from conflict can outweigh headline savings.

But “not preferred” is not enough. The family should use its governance power to force management to produce a credible comparison:
1. no-closure,
2. deep hybrid,
3. closure-led.

Only then can investors judge whether footprint reduction is truly required. Until then, closure talk is premature.

## Our position
We will support the path that most credibly restores **risk-adjusted equity value**.

That means:
- we prefer a **deep hybrid** if it closes the gap durably,
- we can accept a no-closure package only if the savings are real and lasting,
- and we will not support a settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

What we will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- open-ended concessions with no structural effect,
- or a settlement that avoids conflict today but destroys returns tomorrow.

- the minimum savings needed to restore target margins,

- the residual gap after management and owner contributions,
- and the risk-adjusted value impact of each path.

Our line remains unchanged:
- **no cosmetic deal**
- **no structurally impaired margin outcome**
- **no support for closure-first logic without proof**
- **support for a deep, durable restructuring that restores competitiveness**

If the numbers show that a non-closure or deep hybrid package can do that, we will back it. If not, management must not pretend the problem can be wished away.
