# 1) Scenario comparison: illustrative board view

## A. No-closure package
Assumption: all German sites remain open; savings come from working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, wage restraint, overhead cuts, and limited productivity gains.

| Metric | Range |
|---|---:|
| Annual run-rate savings | €300m–€700m |
| One-off restructuring cost | €200m–€700m |
| Workforce reduction | 5%–10% |
| Closure / shutdown risk | None |
| Strike / conflict risk | Medium |
| Implementation risk | Medium |
| Net value effect | Positive if gap is moderate; weak if gap is large |

### Typical composition of savings
- Voluntary exits / attrition: €100m–€250m
- Working-time flexibility / shift reductions: €50m–€150m
- Wage restraint / temporary contribution: €50m–€150m
- Overhead simplification: €50m–€150m
- Productivity / logistics / procurement effects: €50m–€100m

### Main weakness
If the structural gap is above roughly **€700m–€800m per year**, this path often underdelivers unless labour concessions are unusually deep and sustained.

---

## B. Deep hybrid package
Assumption: no immediate full plant closure, but selective downsizing, specialization, shift elimination, partial footprint consolidation, and larger labour-cost restructuring.

| Metric | Range |
|---|---:|
| Annual run-rate savings | €700m–€1.5bn |
| One-off restructuring cost | €500m–€1.5bn |
| Workforce reduction | 10%–20% |
| Closure / shutdown risk | Low to moderate |
| Strike / conflict risk | Medium to high |
| Implementation risk | Medium |
| Net value effect | Usually best risk-adjusted outcome |

### Typical composition of savings
- Headcount reduction via attrition/voluntary exits: €150m–€350m
- Shift elimination / line specialization: €150m–€400m
- Working-time / wage restraint: €100m–€250m
- Overhead simplification / management layers: €100m–€250m
- Product / capacity reallocation: €150m–€400m

### Main strength
This is usually the **best negotiated compromise** because it creates real structural savings without the full operational and political shock of closure-led restructuring.

---

## C. Closure-led package
Assumption: one or more sites are closed or materially relocated, with full footprint reduction and deeper capacity reset.

| Metric | Range |
|---|---:|
| Annual run-rate savings | €1.0bn–€2.0bn+ gross |
| One-off restructuring cost | €1.0bn–€3.0bn+ |
| Workforce reduction | 20%+ in affected footprint |
| Closure / shutdown risk | High |
| Strike / conflict risk | High |
| Implementation risk | High |
| Net value effect | Uncertain; can be worse than headline savings suggest |

### Why gross savings are not the whole story
Closure-led plans often incur:
- severance
- decommissioning
- write-downs / impairments
- relocation costs
- parallel-run costs
- supplier disruption
- legal delay
- industrial action
- reputational damage

So the **realized net benefit** can be materially below headline gross savings.

# 2) Minimum savings target: what does management likely need?

Without company-specific numbers, a useful board test is:

- If the structural gap is **€300m–€700m/year**, a no-closure package may work.
- If the gap is **€700m–€1.5bn/year**, a deep hybrid is usually the most plausible route.
- If the gap is **above €1.5bn/year**, footprint reduction becomes much harder to avoid.

For a large German passenger-car business under pressure, the “minimum durable improvement” often needs to be enough to lift:
- operating margin,
- cash conversion,
- and investment capacity through 2030.

A practical test is whether the deal closes the gap to within roughly **€300m–€500m/year residual shortfall**. Above that, the company may still be structurally underpowered.

# 3) What changes the answer most?

## Biggest upside to a no-closure path
- Very high voluntary exit uptake
- credible wage restraint
- meaningful management overhead cuts
- sustained productivity gains
- no industrial action

## Biggest downside to a no-closure path
- strike days
- weak uptake of voluntary exits
- slippage in shift changes
- political or co-determination resistance
- owner-side support smaller than assumed

A **20%–30% implementation slippage** can erase most of the advantage of a soft compromise.

# 4) Operational risk ranking

From lowest to highest risk:

1. **No-closure**
   - Lowest legal/social disruption
   - But may be economically insufficient

2. **Deep hybrid**
   - Best balance of savings and feasibility
   - Still politically difficult

3. **Closure-led**
   - Highest gross savings potential
   - Highest conflict, delay, and execution risk

---

# 5) Board-level conclusion

## Most likely economically credible settlement
A **deep hybrid** is usually the most realistic negotiated outcome if the company’s gap is genuinely structural and material.

## When no-closure works
Only if:
- the gap is moderate,
- labour contributions are real and durable,
- and management/owners also contribute materially.

## When closure becomes likely
If, after management-side and owner-side contributions, the **residual annual gap remains too large** to close through non-closure levers alone.
