## Core impasse

The blockage is no longer over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **the threshold for footprint reduction**.

In procedural terms, the parties are split on three linked questions:

1. **Whether closures are a necessary instrument or only a last resort**
2. **What level of quantified savings makes a no-closure or hybrid deal credible**
3. **Who must carry the burden of proof and initial contribution**

The employee side and the state/family side are insisting on:  
- non-closure first,  
- quantified proof before any plant rationalization,  
- and binding owner/management contributions.

Management and investors are insisting on:  
- a durable structural reset,  
- no pre-commitment to all sites staying open,  
- and a package that truly closes the cost gap rather than postponing it.

So the current impasse is not about the existence of restructuring, but about **the sequencing, safeguards, and evidentiary standard for irreversible site decisions**.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Quantified “non-closure first” test with a hard decision gate
**Proposal:**  
Agree to a time-bounded first phase in which the joint modeler must quantify:
- the maximum achievable savings from no-closure measures,
- the owner/management contribution,
- and the remaining annual gap.

Then set a pre-agreed decision gate:  
- if the residual gap is below a jointly defined threshold, no closure proceeds;  
- if above it, footprint reduction moves to a second-stage review with stricter safeguards.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It gives management and investors the proof standard they want, while giving labour and state actors the assurance that closures are not immediate or open-ended.

---

### 2) Deep hybrid package with site guarantees for a limited period
**Proposal:**  
Negotiate a deep hybrid package that includes:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- management restraint,
- and owner-side support,

in exchange for a **time-limited site guarantee** for German plants during the implementation window.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It avoids an immediate closure fight, gives the workforce a real protection period, and gives management a structural savings path without having to pre-commit to permanent preservation beyond the agreed horizon.

---

### 3) Burden-sharing ladder with symmetry
**Proposal:**  
Structure contributions in layers:
- first, management and owners deliver defined savings and capital support;
- second, workforce measures activate only to the extent needed;
- third, footprint reduction is considered only if the residual gap remains above an agreed band.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It directly addresses the dispute about who should move first, and it prevents either side from carrying the full burden alone.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**Question:**  
What exact residual annual savings gap would you accept as the maximum that still permits all German sites to stay open, and under what verified conditions?

### Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What specific workforce contributions are you prepared to put on the table, and what binding limits would you require so they remain temporary and non-prejudicial to jobs?

### Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
What is your quantified minimum annual savings requirement after management-side measures, and at what residual gap would you formally rule out no-closure as insufficient?

### Regional State Government
**Question:**  
What objective plant-level test would you require before allowing any footprint reduction to proceed, and what would constitute sufficient proof for you?

### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Will you instruct management to negotiate a deep no-closure or hybrid package first, and only treat closures as reviewable if that package fails the quantified test?

### Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What level of implementation risk, strike exposure, and residual margin shortfall would you still accept in a negotiated non-closure or hybrid deal before insisting on footprint reduction?

### Expert
**Question:**  
What residual annual gap, in your view, would be the practical tipping point at which a deep hybrid ceases to be credible and footprint reduction becomes economically likely?
