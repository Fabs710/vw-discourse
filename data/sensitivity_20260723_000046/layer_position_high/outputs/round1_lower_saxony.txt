As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be very clear: **we recognize the seriousness of the competitiveness problem, but we will not support a restructuring that destroys regional employment or treats German sites as disposable.**

There is one point on which I agree with management: **the company does need a durable solution**, not a short-lived patch. If the cost base is structurally out of line, then we cannot simply paper over that and hope the market improves. But I disagree with the implied conclusion that the only credible answer is closures, forced redundancy, and a permanent weakening of the German industrial footprint.

That is not an acceptable starting point for a company that is a central economic anchor in several regions and that has long benefited from public legitimacy, co-determination, and social partnership.

To the works council and union: I support your core principle that **no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures should be the first response**. That is the right political and social line. At the same time, I would urge you not to reduce the discussion to red lines alone. If we want to protect sites and jobs, we must also be ready to discuss a **serious, durable restructuring package** that can actually close the competitiveness gap. That may include:

- capacity adjustments short of closures,
- flexible working-time solutions,
- qualified voluntary departures,
- redeployment across the group,
- training and transformation measures,
- and a temporary employee contribution if, and only if, it is tied to binding investment, job, and site commitments.

To management: if you want the state and the workforce to engage constructively, then you need to come forward with a plan that is **credible without being destructive**. A strategy that begins with plant closures and large-scale job cuts may satisfy a narrow cost logic, but it will fail the broader test of industrial responsibility, social stability, and long-term resilience. In particular, I will not accept a process where the state’s co-determination role is sidelined or where decisions are effectively forced through by threatening irreversible regional damage.

So my position is this:

- **Yes** to a restructuring that restores competitiveness.
- **No** to closures and forced layoffs as the default solution.
- **No** to shifting the full burden onto employees or regions.
- **Yes** to a negotiated package that protects German sites, preserves as many jobs as possible, and secures future investment.
- **Yes** to seriousness, but **no** to a “cut first, negotiate later” approach.

The company is large enough, and the governance structure is strong enough, that a more balanced settlement is still possible. But it must be a genuine social-partnership solution, not a unilateral retrenchment dressed up as necessity.

I would therefore urge management to table an alternative that quantifies:
1. the actual cost gap,
2. the savings achievable without closures,
3. the required contribution from owners and management,
4. and the investment commitments that would accompany any workforce contribution.

Without that, there is no basis for asking the workforce or the state to accept sacrifice.
