We agree on the main diagnosis: this company has a **structural competitiveness problem**, and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value. That is the right starting point.

Where we differ is on what counts as a credible remedy.

## Where we agree
- A prolonged conflict would be **value-destructive**.
- The solution must be **durable**, not temporary optics.
- The discussion needs to move from slogans to a **quantified scenario comparison**.
- A **hybrid package** is, in principle, the most plausible negotiated route if it truly closes most of the gap.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A no-closure package is not credible unless the numbers prove it
The works council and union keep repeating “no closures, no forced redundancies” as if that were itself a solution. It is not. From a shareholder perspective, if the company’s fixed-cost base remains uncompetitive, then preserving every site by principle is just a slower way to destroy value.

We do **not** support an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired.

### 2) Management has not yet justified closures either
At the same time, management has also not yet earned support for closures as an assumed necessity. The expert’s point is decisive: it depends on the **size of the cost gap** and whether non-closure or hybrid measures can close it durably.

So we are not giving management a blank cheque for plant closures. We want proof, not threats.

### 3) The key test is not political acceptability; it is savings durability
We will back the path that delivers:
- a materially lower cost base,
- credible annual run-rate savings,
- enforceable implementation,
- and a realistic return to target margins.

If a no-closure package can do that, fine. If not, it is not acceptable just because it avoids conflict.

## Our position
We are prepared to support:
- a **deep hybrid restructuring**,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- temporary and time-bounded workforce contributions,
- management restraint,
- and binding investment commitments.

But we are **not** prepared to support:
- a symbolic compromise,
- open-ended concessions without structural savings,
- or a settlement that preserves all legacy cost structures while asking capital markets to wait.

## On the remaining open question
The real unresolved issue is simple:

**Can a no-closure or hybrid package close the gap after management-side and owner-side contributions, or does the company still need footprint reduction?**

Until that is answered with hard numbers, no one should pretend the answer is known.

## What we want next
We support the call for a board-grade scenario table, but it needs to be rigorous and decision-useful.

[SUMMON: board-level finance and operations scenario modeler to quantify annual run-rate savings, one-off restructuring costs, employment impact, strike/veto risk, implementation timeline, and return-on-sales effects across no-closure, hybrid, and closure-led restructuring scenarios, including management-side and owner-side contributions]

## Bottom line
- We will support the restructuring path that **most credibly restores margins**.
- We will not support a settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.
- We will not support cosmetic peace at the expense of long-term equity value.

If the hybrid package is deep enough, we will prefer it over disruptive closures. If it is not, then the company must consider more forceful structural measures.
