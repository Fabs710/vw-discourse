I hear the red lines you have set, but I need to be equally clear from management’s side: **the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**.

We are operating in a shrinking European market, against intense low-cost and Chinese competition, with German production costs materially above target. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target, and without a **structural** correction we will not have the cash to invest in the next generation of products, software, battery technology, and industrial capability through 2030.

So let me respond directly:

- **No plant closures and no forced redundancies** are not credible as absolute, untouchable principles if they leave us with the same overcapacity and cost base.
- **Binding guarantees through 2030** are only meaningful if the business is still competitive in 2030.
- **Temporary measures** like working-time adjustments or bonuses cuts may help bridge a quarter or two, but they do not solve a structural cost problem.

I do agree on one point: **we should avoid a cosmetic deal**. I do not want a short-term compromise that preserves every site on paper while locking in an uncompetitive structure. That would simply postpone the crisis and make the eventual adjustment harsher.

What I am prepared to discuss is a package that achieves **durable savings** without making closure the only instrument:
- significant labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- internal reallocation and retraining,
- and, where necessary, site adjustments that reflect the real economic situation.

But the core test is simple: **does the package deliver enough cost reduction and capacity realignment to restore competitiveness?** If not, it is not enough.

To the union and works council: if you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a credible alternative that delivers the same structural effect. Not just delays, not just slogans, but numbers that materially change the cost base. If the answer is only “no closures, no layoffs, no reductions,” then you are asking management to accept decline.

We are open to a negotiated solution, but only a solution that is **durable, structural, and economically sufficient**. Anything else is not a restructuring. It is denial.
