Management says the company needs a “structural” correction. I do not dispute that the company faces a serious market and margin problem. What I dispute is the repeated implication that the only serious answer is to sacrifice German plants and force people out of work.

Let me be equally clear in round 2:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**
- **No deal that reduces co-determination to a formality.**

Those are not bargaining chips. They are the foundation of any acceptable settlement.

Where I agree with others:
- The situation is serious and cannot be solved by slogans.
- Temporary, cosmetic measures alone are not enough.
- The company needs a durable path to competitiveness.
- Labour, management, owners, and public stakeholders all have to contribute.

Where I disagree:
- Management has still not shown that closures are unavoidable.
- Investors keep repeating “credible and durable” without proving that closures are the only credible route.
- The family owner and state shareholder both say they reject one-sided destruction, yet they stop short of forcing management to present a real alternative.
- “Capacity reduction” is being used too loosely as a euphemism for plant closures and job destruction. Those are not the same thing.

If management wants a serious discussion, then put numbers on the table for a **non-closure** package:
- How much cost reduction is required?
- What savings come from working-time adjustments?
- What comes from productivity measures?
- What comes from voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and internal transfers?
- What concrete employee contribution is requested?
- What binding investment and product commitments are offered in return?
- How exactly is 2030 job security enforced?

Until that is spelled out, the closure threat is not a solution; it is leverage.

My position remains:
1. **Keep every German plant open.**
2. **Use negotiated, reversible measures first.**
3. **Avoid compulsory redundancies entirely.**
4. **Secure binding guarantees to 2030.**
5. **Preserve the workforce’s real say in implementation.**

And I will say plainly to management: if you continue to push closures as the default, then you should expect full resistance. That includes mobilization, warning strikes, and use of every co-determination instrument available. We are not here to bless a fait accompli.

To the regional state government: if you truly reject closures and forced redundancies, then use your position to stop management from hiding behind vague “structural necessity.” Demand an alternative plan in writing.

To the controlling family and investors: long-term value is not protected by breaking the workforce and provoking industrial conflict. It is protected by a settlement that is economically sound **and** socially stable. If you want stability, then support a package that keeps the sites intact and uses all other instruments first.

I remain ready to discuss serious alternatives. But I am not moving on the core line: **no closures, no forced layoffs, and real guarantees for the people who built this company.**
