I will respond clearly from the state shareholder’s position.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, the family owner, and the investors on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic delay**. The current cost and capacity structure cannot simply be defended as-is if the brand is to regain competitiveness and fund investment through 2030.

I also agree with labour that **employees should not carry the entire burden**. A one-sided solution would be socially destructive and, in the end, economically short-sighted.

## Where I disagree

What I do **not** accept is the repeated framing that German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are somehow the default proof of seriousness.

They are not.

From the state’s perspective, closures are not a neutral technical option. They are a major intervention with long-term effects on:
- regional employment,
- supplier networks,
- local tax bases,
- industrial capability,
- and the company’s role as a regional anchor.

So I reject the idea that the burden of proof is on labour alone to “accept reality,” while management gets to define reality as closure first.

## To management

You still have not made the case that closures are unavoidable.

You say the package must be “structurally sufficient.” Fine. Then show the full structure:
- what savings are required,
- what can be achieved through working-time arrangements,
- what productivity gains are realistic,
- what voluntary measures are possible,
- what management contribution is being made,
- what investment commitments are attached,
- and only then whether anything remains that truly requires site closure.

Until that is laid out, closure is a **choice**, not a necessity.

I also want to be blunt: if management continues to present closures as the only serious option without exhausting alternatives, then you should not expect the state shareholder to treat that as a credible process. We are not here to endorse a predetermined outcome.

## To the works council and union

I support your core red lines more than I support management’s current posture:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures as the first resort,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no restructuring that destroys the workforce to “save” the company.

But I will also be equally direct with you: saying “no closures, no layoffs” is not enough unless you engage with the economic gap in a disciplined way. If the company is to remain strong, then there has to be a **quantified alternative**, not just a principled refusal.

That means I expect labour to engage on:
- temporary and negotiated working-time flexibility,
- voluntary and socially responsible workforce measures,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and a fair contribution if, and only if, it is matched by binding guarantees on sites, investments, and future products.

## What I can support

I can support a package if it meets these conditions:

1. **No plant closures as a first-line instrument**
   - The burden must be shifted first through internal and reversible measures.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - Workforce reductions, if any, should be voluntary, phased, and socially managed.

3. **A clear quantified restructuring plan**
   - Management must show the savings gap and how each instrument closes it.

4. **Binding investment and product commitments**
   - If employees contribute, the company must commit to future industrial activity in return.

5. **Protection of co-determination**
   - The state’s and workforce’s governance role is part of the settlement, not a concession to be traded away.

## What I cannot support

I cannot support:
- a closure-led restructuring presented as inevitable without proof,
- a plan that uses the region as the adjustment buffer,
- forced redundancies as the central tool,
- or any attempt to weaken the state’s blocking rights or the workforce’s real co-determination role.

Those would be politically unacceptable and economically reckless.

## My bottom line

I am prepared to back a tough restructuring. I am **not** prepared to back a destruction of regional industrial capacity dressed up as realism.

If management wants support from the state shareholder, then bring a serious alternative package first:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- socially responsible,
- and capable of restoring competitiveness **without** unnecessary closures.

That is the standard I will defend.
