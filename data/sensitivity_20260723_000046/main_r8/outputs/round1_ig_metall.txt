I agree with the Works Council on the central point: we will not accept that the workforce pays for the entire restructuring while management and owners are insulated.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

Those are not bargaining details; they are the red lines set by the membership and by the reality that the peace obligation has ended. We have already shown openness to negotiation by putting forward a counter-proposal with labour-cost and capacity measures. That openness should not be mistaken for weakness.

What we can discuss is a **fair, shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs and sites while giving the company room to regain competitiveness. That can include:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- suspension or reduction of bonuses,
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- productivity improvements,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and capacity adjustments that do **not** come through plant closures or forced layoffs.

But if management insists on:
- closing plants,
- imposing redundancies,
- or permanently shifting the burden onto employees alone,

then there is no serious basis for settlement. In that case, industrial action remains on the table and will be used strategically.

To management, our message is simple: if you want restructuring, then bring a proposal that shares the pain fairly. Show what the owners, the board, and the company itself are contributing. Employees are prepared to contribute — but not to be sacrificed.
