We agree on the diagnosis, and that matters: the company has a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic deal would destroy value. We also agree that employees should not be asked to carry the whole burden. Where we part ways is on what “acceptable” means if it does **not** actually restore margins.

From the capital markets’ side, the test is still very simple:

- Does the package produce **durable, quantified cost reduction**?
- Does it **realign capacity** enough to stop margin leakage?
- Does it do so with **deal certainty** and manageable industrial risk?
- And does it avoid locking in an uncompetitive structure under the cover of “peace”?

If the answer is no, then we do **not** support it.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current cost base is not sustainable.
- **Labour is right** that a one-sided solution built on forced redundancies and endless conflict is value-destructive.
- **The state and family** are right that a rushed, socially explosive solution can damage the franchise and the region.
- Everyone is right that temporary gestures alone are insufficient.

### Where we disagree
#### To the Works Council and Union:
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” line is understandable, but it is **not sufficient** if it leaves the company structurally overcosted. Investors cannot back a settlement that preserves every site, protects every legacy arrangement, and still expects the equity story to improve on faith.

I do **not** need closures as an ideology. I do need a package that is **economically equivalent** if you want to avoid them. So far, I have not heard such a package in quantified form. “Working-time flexibility,” “productivity measures,” and “voluntary exits” are all useful, but unless they add up to real annual savings and capacity rationalisation, they are not a solution.

#### To Management:
You are right to insist on numbers, but you are still leaning too hard on the implication that closures are the only serious answer. If you want shareholder backing, don’t present this as a binary choice between plant shutdowns and denial. Present the **full bridge**:
- savings from labour cost measures,
- savings from footprint optimisation,
- savings from internal redeployment and automation,
- and the residual gap.

Until that is clear, the market cannot assess whether you are pushing a necessary restructuring or just the most disruptive one.

#### To the State and Family:
We hear the concern about social stability and governance constraints. But constraints do not create value. If governance blocks a credible restructuring, the market will mark the company down. That may be politically uncomfortable, but it is economically real.

### What would move us
We would support a package if it is:
1. **Quantified** — with a clear savings target and line-by-line contribution by measure.
2. **Durable** — not a temporary bridge to the next crisis.
3. **Enforceable** — not dependent on goodwill alone.
4. **Competitiveness-focused** — restoring returns on sales, not preserving legacy structures.
5. **Implementation-safe** — avoiding prolonged strikes that burn cash and customer trust.

### Our bottom line
We are open to alternatives to closures. We are **not** open to alternatives to competitiveness.

So:
- If a non-closure package truly delivers the same structural savings, we can support it.
- If it does not, then management should stop pretending that symbolic concessions will fix the business.
- And if the final deal protects sites but leaves margins structurally impaired, we will not endorse it.

Our support depends on one thing only: **credible return to competitive margins through disciplined, durable restructuring.**
