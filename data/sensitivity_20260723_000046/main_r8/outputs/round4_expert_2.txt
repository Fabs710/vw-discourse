## Short answer
A **non-closure restructuring could be sufficient in principle**, but **it is not yet proven** that it can close the savings gap **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

At this stage, the decisive issue is not ideology; it is whether the **verified recurring savings** from non-closure measures are large enough relative to the company’s structural cost gap and overcapacity.

## What can be said with confidence
From an automotive footprint and cost-structure perspective, the following is clear:

- **Working-time changes, productivity gains, redeployment, voluntary exits, procurement savings, and overhead reductions can all produce material savings.**
- However, these measures usually **do not fully substitute for major fixed-cost reductions** if a company has:
  - persistent overcapacity,
  - underutilized plants,
  - duplicate production structures,
  - or product lines concentrated in high-cost sites.
- The closer the company is to a **true fixed-footprint problem**, the less likely a purely non-closure package is to fully close a large structural gap.
- If the gap is mainly in **labour cost, shift pattern inefficiency, and overhead**, then a non-closure package has a much better chance.

To judge feasibility rigorously, I would need at minimum:

1. **Plant-by-plant capacity and utilization**

2. **Plant-by-plant cost base**

   - fixed overhead
   - maintenance
   - logistics/internal transport
   - energy
   - depreciation where relevant

3. **The exact savings target**
   - annual recurring savings required
   - one-time restructuring costs
   - required timing

4. **Measure-by-measure savings assumptions**
   - working-time reduction or flexibility
   - wage/benefit moderation
   - management overhead cuts
   - procurement savings
   - voluntary exit rates
   - redeployment feasibility
   - retraining costs
   - any owner/group contribution

5. **Volume and mix assumptions**
   - expected European demand
   - product allocation by plant
   - EV/ICE mix
   - sensitivity to further market decline
