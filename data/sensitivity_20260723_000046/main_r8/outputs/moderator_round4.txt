Facilitator summary: the discourse has plateaued because the parties are no longer disputing the need for restructuring in principle; they are disputing the **sequence, proof standard, and red lines** around it.

## Core impasse
The main deadlock is between two frameworks:

1. **Labour / state shareholder / controlling family in part**
   - no-closure first
   - no compulsory redundancies
   - independent verification before any footprint decision
   - burden-sharing must include management and owners

2. **Management / investors**
   - a non-closure option may be acceptable only if it is proven to close the gap
   - they want a quantified options paper that also includes a residual closure case
   - they do not accept absolute no-closure commitments in advance

So the real impasse is not “restructuring vs no restructuring,” but:
- **whether closures may be treated as an open option before the savings bridge is proven**, and
- **whether the verification process is meant to test non-closure viability or to stage a later closure decision**.

---

## Possible compromise positions
These are facilitator proposals for bridging the most conflicting positions:

### 1) Sequenced option hierarchy with a procedural standstill
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a formal sequence:
- Step 1: independently audited non-closure package
- Step 2: independently audited mixed package
- Step 3: residual-closure case only if Steps 1 and 2 fail to close the verified gap

During Step 1 and Step 2:
- no closure decision
- no compulsory redundancies
- no public closure announcements

This could give labour and the state shareholder procedural protection while still preserving management’s ability to test economic necessity.

### 2) Conditional no-closure commitment with a defined review trigger
**Proposal:**  
Agree that German plant closures are excluded **unless and until** an independent auditor confirms that:
- non-closure measures cannot close the verified annual gap, and
- the residual gap remains above a pre-agreed threshold after management/owner contributions

This would not be a permanent veto, but a conditional no-closure default tied to measurable proof standards.

### 3) Burden-sharing package with a joint downside protection clause
**Proposal:**  
Combine:
- employee flexibility, productivity, redeployment, voluntary exits
- management overhead cuts
- owner contribution
- binding investment and product commitments

In exchange:
- job-security framework to 2030
- no compulsory redundancies as the first resort
- closure only if a jointly reviewed trigger is met after the verified savings bridge

This could reduce distrust by making every side contribute and by defining the fallback path in advance.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific quantified proof would you accept as sufficient to move from “non-closure first” to discussing a residual closure case, without abandoning your no-compulsory-redundancy line?**

### Trade Union
**Would you accept a time-limited process in which no closure decisions are allowed until an independent auditor has tested a fully populated savings bridge, including management and owner contributions?**

### Regional State Government
**Will you commit to supporting an independent verification process even if it may ultimately confirm that some footprint reduction is needed, provided the process first tests a genuine non-closure alternative?**

### Controlling Family
**Will you pre-commit to backing the verified economically sufficient option, even if it requires meaningful owner contribution and a negotiated non-closure package that is less convenient than closures?**

### Management
**Will you disclose plant-by-plant cost and capacity data on a timetable that allows the auditor to test a non-closure package before any closure path is presented as necessary?**

### Institutional Investors
**If an independent review shows that a non-closure package materially narrows the gap and preserves competitiveness with lower industrial risk, would you support it even if the footprint remains largely intact?**

### Independent Expert
**What minimum threshold of residual gap, after non-closure measures and owner/management contributions, would you regard as a credible trigger to consider footprint reduction?**
