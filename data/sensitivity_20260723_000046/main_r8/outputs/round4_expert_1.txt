Yes. From an industrial restructuring perspective, the minimum data set needed to determine whether a **non-closure package** closes the gap is fairly specific.

## Minimum data set required

### 1) Plant-by-plant operating base
For each German plant and major function:
- headcount by function and shift
- annual output and utilization
- direct labour cost
- indirect labour / support cost
- fixed manufacturing overhead
- energy, maintenance, logistics, and internal transport costs
- depreciation / asset base where relevant
- current unit cost at realistic volume

### 2) Capacity and footprint data
- rated capacity vs. actual volume
- structural spare capacity by line and plant
- product allocation by model, platform, and powertrain
- overlap between plants in the same product families
- constraints on redeployment between sites

### 3) Savings bridge by measure
For each candidate measure, with annual recurring effect and one-time cost:
- working-time changes
- wage/bonus moderation or allowance changes
- removal/modification of legacy guarantees
- productivity gains
- internal redeployment
- voluntary exits / natural attrition
- qualification / reskilling cost
- procurement and overhead reduction
- management overhead contribution
- owner or group-level contribution if any

### 4) Implementation assumptions
- timing of each measure
- uptake rates for voluntary exits and redeployment
- feasibility limits by site
- strike / disruption sensitivity
- any legal or contractual dependence affecting timing
- one-time restructuring cash cost and payback period

### 5) Financial outcome measures
Under each scenario:
- recurring annual savings
- EBITDA / operating profit impact
- margin impact
- cash impact
- break-even volume change
- return on sales effect
- sensitivity to market volume decline

## What must be compared
At minimum, three scenarios:
1. **Non-closure package**
2. **Mixed package**: non-closure measures plus limited footprint change
3. **Residual-closure case**: closure / deeper footprint correction if needed

Each scenario should show:
- recurring savings
- one-time costs
- capacity removed or preserved
- headcount effect
- residual cost gap vs. target

## When verification can be completed
If management provides the plant-level data promptly, a **first-pass independent verification** can usually be done in **2–4 weeks** for a desk-based review.

If it requires:
- plant visits,
- line-level validation,
- and reconciliation with HR / controlling / operations data,

then a more robust assessment is typically **4–8 weeks**.

## Practical limitation
The key issue is not the arithmetic itself; it is whether the company will disclose enough detail to make the bridge credible. Without plant-level cost and capacity data, any “no-closure” claim is not verifiable.

## Bottom line
A credible assessment is possible only if the auditor gets:
- plant-by-plant costs,
- capacity/utilization,
- measure-by-measure savings,
- one-time costs,
- and timing assumptions.

Without that, nobody can reliably say whether non-closure alternatives fully close the gap or only reduce it.
