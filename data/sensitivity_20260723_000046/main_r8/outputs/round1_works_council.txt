As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. The workforce has already delivered flexibility, productivity and sacrifices over many years. What we cannot accept now is that management tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, undermining co-determination, and breaking the historic job-security framework.

Our priorities are:

1. Keep every German plant open.
2. Exclude forced layoffs.
3. Secure binding site and job guarantees through 2030.
4. Preserve co-determination and a real say for employee representatives in any restructuring.
5. Achieve competitiveness through negotiated, reversible measures rather than irreversible cuts.

We are prepared to discuss alternative ways to reduce costs and adapt capacity:
- temporary and negotiated working-time adjustments,
- internal reallocation,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary rather than compulsory reductions,
- productivity measures,
- and a fair employee contribution if it is matched by binding guarantees on sites, investments, and future products.

But let me be equally clear: if the proposal still includes plant closures, forced layoffs, or the unilateral dismantling of job-security commitments, then it is unacceptable. We have the power and the duty to defend the workforce, and we will use every means available — including industrial action and our blocking rights — if management tries to force through a fait accompli.

If management wants a serious settlement, it must come with binding guarantees, shared sacrifice, and a restructuring that protects the company without sacrificing the people who built it.
