I will be direct.

## To management
I agree with your central diagnosis: the company has a structural competitiveness problem, and cosmetic measures will not fix it. I also agree that the cost base must come down materially if we want to protect long-term value and preserve the investment capacity for future products.

Where I disagree is your tendency to talk as if closures and compulsory redundancies are already the default answer before the non-closure case has been properly proven.

That is not how I will govern this.

You still have not produced a sufficiently clear, plant-by-plant savings bridge that shows:
- the annual recurring gap,
- what each non-closure measure contributes,
- what management and ownership contribute,
- and what residual gap remains after all internal measures.

Until that is on the table, I will not back a closure-led line. Not because closures are impossible in principle, but because they are value-destructive unless and until they are shown to be truly necessary.

## To the Works Council
I agree with you that the workforce should not be asked to absorb all the pain, and I agree that forced redundancies are highly undesirable. I also agree that co-determination is not decorative.

Where I disagree is your absolute framing that no plant closures can be discussed at all, regardless of the numbers. That is too rigid for a company facing a genuine structural problem.

If a non-closure package can really close the gap, I will prefer it. But I will not pretend in advance that this is proven. You need to engage with the economics, not only with the red lines. A veto posture without quantified sufficiency is not a restructuring answer.

## To the union
You are right to insist on shared sacrifice and to reject a one-sided burden shift. I can support meaningful employee contributions if they are part of a durable settlement.

But I will not support a package that relies on indefinite goodwill, temporary concessions, or slogans about flexibility while preserving structural overcapacity. The question is whether the recurring savings are enough to restore competitiveness. If not, the business will just be back here later, weaker and more unstable.

So yes to a serious negotiated contribution. No to a weak compromise that postpones the reckoning.

## To the regional state government
I respect your role and your concern for regional employment. I also share your opposition to unnecessary instability.

But I disagree with the way you treat closures as something to be ruled out politically before the facts are established. If you want to use your influence responsibly, then support an independent verification process and insist on a quantified comparison of:
1. non-closure package,
2. mixed package,
3. residual-closure case.

If the numbers show that a non-closure solution is sufficient, I will support it. If they do not, then I will not support a political veto that protects the footprint at the expense of the company’s future.

## To institutional investors
I agree with your insistence on durability, execution certainty, and margin recovery. You are right that a cosmetic deal would destroy value.

But I do not accept the implication that the most severe restructuring is automatically the best one. A closure-led outcome that triggers prolonged conflict, production disruption, and permanent distrust can also destroy value.

I will support the economically sufficient outcome, not the most dramatic one. That distinction matters.

## On the expert input
The expert did not prove that closures are necessary. The expert said the answer depends on the actual data, especially whether the problem is mainly labor inefficiency or excess fixed footprint.

That is exactly why I insist on a hard, audited savings bridge before anyone asks the owners to endorse irreversible decisions.

## My position
I remain committed to the following:
- restore competitiveness and margins,
- protect long-term enterprise value,
- avoid value-destructive instability,
- preserve governance control,
- and support the scenario that is economically sufficient.

That means:

### I will support
- serious cost reduction,
- productivity measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- and a fair employee contribution,

if they are part of a verified, durable package.

### I will not support
- a closure-first approach without proof,
- compulsory redundancies as a reflex,
- or a settlement that preserves every structure while leaving the company uncompetitive.

## Bottom line
I am not here to protect every legacy arrangement. I am here to protect the company’s long-term value.

That requires a real restructuring, not a symbolic one. But it also requires discipline: no irreversible decisions before the economics are verified, and no reckless destruction of industrial peace.

That is my line.
