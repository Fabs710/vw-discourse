I will respond directly.

First, I agree with the Works Council on the central principle: **the workforce will not accept a restructuring that solves management’s problem by destroying jobs, breaking the collective framework, and shifting all the pain onto employees**. That is not a durable solution; it is a one-sided transfer of risk and cost onto the people who already carried this company through difficult years.

At the same time, I want to be precise about the union’s position, because “agreement in principle” is not enough.

## Where we agree
We agree that:
- the company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**;
- a **cosmetic deal** is useless;
- the solution must be **durable**, not just a delay;
- employees are prepared to contribute if the burden is **shared fairly**;
- any settlement must protect the **collective bargaining framework** and avoid forced social damage.

## Where we disagree
Management is still speaking as if **plant closures and forced redundancies are the default benchmark of seriousness**. That is not an argument; it is a threat. And it is not yet proven.

You keep saying “structural” and “durable,” but you have not shown that the only way to achieve those goals is to close plants in Germany or impose redundancies. Before you demand irreversible destruction, you must prove why a package of:
- working-time adjustments,
- productivity improvements,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary reductions,
- capacity balancing,
- and a negotiated labour-cost contribution

cannot deliver the required effect.

## My line remains clear
The union will not accept:
- **compulsory redundancies**,
- **German plant closures**,
- **dismantling of the collective wage framework**,
- or a deal where employees contribute while management and owners contribute little or nothing.

That is not negotiable by repetition or pressure.

## What we can discuss
We are still prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package** if it is real. That means:
- a measurable employee contribution,
- temporary or structural labour-cost relief,
- capacity reduction **without** closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal mobility,
- retraining and qualification,
- and binding commitments on future investment and product allocation.

But this only works if the other side also contributes. If management wants sacrifices from the workforce, then management must also put something on the table:
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- investment commitments,
- and real guarantees on employment and sites.

## On the question of numbers
The moderator is right: we need quantified options. So here is the union’s position in practical terms:

- Show us the savings gap.
- Show us what you already assume from productivity and efficiency.
- Show us what employee contribution you want.
- Show us what contribution comes from management, owners, and internal restructuring.
- Show us why this cannot be achieved without closures.

Until that is done, closures remain a choice, not a necessity.

## To management
If your answer is still “accept closures or accept decline,” then you are not bargaining; you are escalating. If that is your position, then you should expect escalation in return. We have already ended the peace obligation’s effect in practice, and the workforce will not stand aside while jobs and standards are dismantled.

## To the state shareholder and the family owner
If you genuinely oppose one-sided destruction, then do not just say so rhetorically. Use your governance influence to stop management from turning restructuring into a social and industrial break with the workforce.

## Bottom line
The union is ready for a **tough but fair settlement**.  
What we are **not** ready for is:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or a restructuring where employees bear the entire burden.

If management wants a deal, bring forward a concrete package that preserves sites, protects collective bargaining, and distributes the sacrifice fairly. Otherwise, we will continue to use our bargaining power, including industrial action, to defend the workforce.
