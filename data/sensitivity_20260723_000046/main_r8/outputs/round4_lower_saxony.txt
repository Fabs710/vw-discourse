I’ll be direct.

I agree with management, investors, and the family on one essential point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic patch**. I also agree that the German cost base has to come down and that the burden cannot fall on employees alone.

But I **do not** agree that plant closures and compulsory redundancies should be treated as a normal or default answer. From my perspective as regional state shareholder, that is still **unproven** and **unacceptable as a starting point**.

## Where I agree
- The company has a real competitiveness problem.
- A temporary truce without structural change would fail.
- Employees should contribute through flexibility, productivity, redeployment, qualification, and voluntary measures.
- Management, owners, and capital must also contribute.
- We need hard numbers, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You keep saying that closures may be necessary, but you still have not shown a **plant-by-plant savings bridge** proving that the non-closure options are insufficient.

Until that is shown, closures are not a necessity; they are a choice.  
And I will not accept German plant closures as the first-line instrument.

### To investors
I understand your insistence on credible margins and implementation certainty. But I reject the idea that the only credible path is a severe footprint cut. A socially explosive restructuring that destroys trust, triggers disruption, and weakens the company’s regional base is not automatically value-creating.

### To the controlling family
You say you will back the economically sufficient scenario. Then back the scenario that is **actually verified** to be sufficient. Do not pre-commit to footprint cuts before the data is in.

## My position
I will support a tough settlement if it contains:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no automatic German plant closures**
- **real employee contribution**
- **management and owner contribution**
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **preservation of co-determination and the state’s blocking role**
- **independent verification of the savings bridge**

I will not support a deal that:
- guts regional employment,
- sidelines the state’s veto role,
- or treats German sites as expendable by default.

## What I need next
We need a **fully quantified options paper** comparing:
1. a non-closure package,
2. a mixed package,
3. and only then a residual-closure case if the first two truly fail.

That paper must include:
- plant-by-plant capacity and utilization,
- direct and indirect cost by site,
- savings from working-time and productivity measures,
- savings from voluntary exits and redeployment,
- management and owner contribution,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and the residual gap under each scenario.

## Final word
I am not here to block restructuring. I am here to block a restructuring that destroys regional jobs and weakens the company’s social compact without proof that such destruction is unavoidable.

So my answer remains:

**Non-closure first. No compulsory redundancies. No erosion of co-determination. No decision on closures until the numbers have been independently verified.**
