**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem** and that a **durable—not cosmetic—solution** is needed.
- There is broad openness to **negotiated restructuring** rather than unilateral action.
- Several potential instruments were named as possible components of a package:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - productivity measures,
  - internal redeployment,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - voluntary workforce measures,
  - labour-cost contributions,
  - capacity adjustments.
- There is general recognition that any agreement must balance **economic viability**, **employee protection**, and **investment capacity**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**: Works Council, union, and state shareholder reject them; management and capital-side actors say they cannot rule them out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**: Rejected by labour and the state shareholder; management and investors treat them as possible if alternatives are insufficient.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees**: Labour wants binding guarantees through 2030; management and shareholders say guarantees must depend on economic viability and cannot be absolute.
- **Extent of employee sacrifice vs. corporate/governance contribution**: Labour wants shared sacrifice matched by binding commitments from management/owners; shareholders want clear, measurable cost and capacity reduction before committing support.
- **Role of co-determination and shareholder veto powers**: Labour and the state want these protected; management wants sufficient freedom to execute restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** to restore competitiveness, and what exact combination of measures delivers it.
- Whether **equivalent savings** can be achieved **without closures or forced layoffs**, and if so, with what binding commitments.
- What **state shareholder, family owner, and management** are prepared to contribute in exchange for employee concessions.
- How to reconcile **short-term industrial peace** with **long-term structural change**.
- What a **credible enforcement mechanism** would look like for any guarantees, voluntary measures, or investment commitments.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: each side should specify what it can give, what it needs, and what numerical effect it expects.
- Test alternative packages against the same question: **Do they deliver enough durable savings without unnecessary destruction?**
- Clarify which elements are **non-negotiable**, which are **conditional**, and which can be traded.
- Focus on a structured exchange around:
  1. required savings and capacity changes,
  2. possible non-closure alternatives,
  3. employee contribution in return for binding guarantees,
  4. governance and implementation safeguards.
