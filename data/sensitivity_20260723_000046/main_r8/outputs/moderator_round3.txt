## Core impasse

The central blockage is:

**No shared, verified savings bridge.**

Management, investors, the family, and the state shareholder are treating **footprint correction/closures as potentially necessary**, while the works council and union are insisting **closures must not be the starting assumption** and that **non-closure measures may be sufficient**.

The expert input did not settle this because it confirmed only that:
- non-closure measures can be meaningful,
- but sufficiency depends on the actual numbers and site-level overcapacity.

So the dispute is no longer mainly about values; it is about **proof, sequencing, and acceptable risk allocation**:
1. **Who must produce the numbers first**
2. **Whether closures are treated as a default option or residual option**
3. **Whether labor must concede before management proves insufficiency of alternatives**

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Sequenced “non-closure first” test with a binding review gate
**Facilitator framing:**  
A time-limited process where all parties agree to test a quantified non-closure package first, before any closure decision is tabled.

**Elements:**
- Management provides a plant-by-plant savings bridge.
- A neutral verifier audits the assumptions.
- Parties test:
  1. non-closure package,
  2. mixed package,
  3. residual-closure case.
- A closure discussion is allowed only if the verified non-closure and mixed packages fail to close the gap.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Addresses works council/union demand that closures not be the starting point.
- Gives management/investors a structured path to test whether no-closure is actually viable.
- Gives state shareholder and family a governance-based process rather than an open-ended veto.

---

### Proposal 2: Conditional no-closure commitment tied to quantified employee and management contributions
**Facilitator framing:**  
A conditional commitment to keep German plants open, but only if a jointly defined savings target is met through negotiated measures.

**Elements:**
- Binding no-closure intent for a defined period.
- Employee-side measures are paired with explicit management/owner contributions.
- If the verified savings target is not met by a certain date, the parties reopen the footprint question.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Gives the workforce a meaningful protection window.
- Gives management and investors a measurable test rather than an indefinite promise.
- Preserves leverage for all sides while avoiding immediate closure escalation.

---

### Proposal 3: Mixed package with site-specific protections and fallback options
**Facilitator framing:**  
A differentiated settlement where some sites are protected, some functions are consolidated internally, and only residual structural gaps remain open for later review.

**Elements:**
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, productivity, and working-time changes first.
- Site-specific guarantees for strategically important plants.
- A fallback mechanism for residual undercapacity, reviewed only after implementation results are known.

**Why this may bridge stakeholders:**
- Gives labor a stronger job-security framework.
- Gives management a path to structural savings.
- Gives investors a credible implementation route without immediate full closure decisions.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact annual savings gap must be closed, and how much of it is attributable to each German plant and each non-closure measure?**

### To the Works Council
**If a verified non-closure package still leaves a material residual gap, what specific additional internal measures would you accept before any discussion of footprint change?**

### To the Union
**Would you support a time-limited, quantified non-closure test if it included binding employee protections and a neutral verification mechanism?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Will you commit to backing a structured verification process that could, if necessary, distinguish between “not yet proven” and “economically insufficient” without pre-empting the result?**

### To the Controlling Family
**Will you support the outcome that the independent verification shows to be economically sufficient, even if it is not management’s preferred initial option?**

### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you accept a delayed closure decision if an independent audit first tested whether a non-closure or mixed package can deliver equivalent recurring savings and implementation certainty?**
