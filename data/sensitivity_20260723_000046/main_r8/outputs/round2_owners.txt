I will be direct.

I agree with all parties on one central point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. The current cost base and capacity structure cannot remain unchanged if we want to protect long-term value, fund future investment, and restore margins.

Where I **agree**:
- Management is right that the business cannot be carried indefinitely with structurally uncompetitive costs.
- Labour is right that a one-sided settlement imposed on employees would be unstable and ultimately value-destructive.
- The state shareholder is right that social and regional consequences matter, and that a rushed, uncontrolled break would be costly.
- Investors are right that any outcome must be **credible, quantified, and durable**.

Where I **disagree**:
- I do **not** accept that plant closures and forced redundancies are the default or only serious answer.
- I do **not** accept a closure-led solution unless management proves, with numbers, that every serious alternative has been exhausted and still falls short.
- I do **not** accept “no closures, no layoffs” as an absolute principle if it simply preserves overcapacity and guarantees future value erosion.

That is the balance. Long-term ownership means I will not back denial, but I will also not back destruction for its own sake.

## My position on the current discussion

### To management
You still have not demonstrated that closures are unavoidable. You have stated the problem clearly, but you have not yet shown a complete, quantified bridge from the current cost base to sustainable profitability **without** first exhausting other instruments.

If you want governance support, bring a package that separates:
- savings from working-time measures,
- productivity gains,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- labour-cost contribution,
- footprint optimisation,
- and only then any residual need for site-specific adjustment.

That is the standard.

If the numbers show that a residual closure is truly required, then we will judge that case on its merits. But I will not endorse closures as a starting assumption.

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on no compulsory redundancies and no automatic closures is understandable. But I need to be equally clear: if your position means the business remains structurally overmanned and overcosted, then it is not a viable long-term answer.

What I need from you is not slogans, but a **credible alternative package** with real magnitude:
- what annual savings are you willing to accept?
- what working-time or pay elements can be adjusted?
- what voluntary measures are realistic?
- what productivity commitments can be enforced?
- what job and site guarantees are truly compatible with competitiveness?

If you cannot answer that, then your red lines become a refusal to solve the problem, not a bargaining position.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect your role and your concern for social stability. But I would caution against turning “no closures” into a political veto over all meaningful restructuring. That would not preserve the company; it would merely defer the reckoning.

At the same time, I do hear your point that closures are not a neutral technical tool. They require a very high justification threshold. I accept that. But that threshold must be economic, not political theatre.

### To Institutional Investors
You are right to push for measurable outcomes. However, capital value is not protected by insisting on the most visibly drastic solution. It is protected by the solution that actually restores returns on a sustained basis.

So I would say this plainly: if a non-closure package can close the gap credibly, that is preferable. If not, then the governance bodies must be honest about the need for harder choices.

## What I can support
I can support a restructuring package if it meets these conditions:
1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity adjustment**
3. **Strong employee contribution, but not one-sided**
4. **Protection of investment capacity for future products and technology**
5. **Industrial peace and implementation stability**
6. **No weakening of control or governance rights**

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a fake compromise that preserves every structure and erodes margins,
- a settlement that damages long-term value for a short-term headline,
- any permanent impairment of the enterprise’s competitiveness,
- or any outcome that undermines owners’ governance control.

## Practical next step
Before anyone talks about final red lines, I want management to table a **quantified options paper** with at least three scenarios:
- a non-closure restructuring,
- a mixed package,
- and a residual-closure case with explicit justification.

Each scenario should show:
- cost savings,
- capacity changes,
- employment impact,
- implementation timing,
- industrial risk,
- and effect on return on sales.

If that analysis shows a credible alternative to closures, I will support that route. If it does not, then we will have to face the harder conclusion.

I am open to a tough settlement. I am **not** open to a settlement that protects appearances while destroying value.
