## Trade Union Position – Round 4

I will speak plainly.

### To management
You are still trying to move the discussion onto the workforce’s shoulders before you have shown, in hard numbers, that your preferred measures are unavoidable.

I agree with one thing: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic patch.  
I also agree that employees should contribute. We already signaled that with our counter-proposal: working-time flexibility, productivity measures, redeployment, qualification, voluntary measures, and a fair labour-cost contribution.

But I **do not** accept your repeated implication that closures are simply the “serious” option while a no-closure solution is somehow unserious. That is not a fact; it is a framing choice.

What you still have not provided is:
- the exact annual savings gap,
- the plant-by-plant cost burden,
- the share of savings from each non-closure measure,
- the contribution expected from management and owners,
- and the residual gap after internal measures.

Until that exists, **German plant closures are not justified**. They are only one possible outcome, and not one I will treat as the starting point.

Let me be equally clear:  
**No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures in Germany. No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

If management wants a serious settlement, it must first put all internal levers on the table and show that they are insufficient. Not assume it.

---

### To the Works Council
I largely agree with your core line:
- no forced layoffs,
- no plant closures,
- no hollowing out of co-determination,
- no one-sided burden shifting.

You are right to reject the attempt to hide closures under phrases like “capacity reduction.” If that is what management means, it should say so openly.

Where I want to sharpen the union position is this: we should engage on a **real quantified alternative**, not because we are softening our red lines, but because we need to show that there is a credible route to savings without breaking the company’s social compact.

That means we should insist on:
- a complete savings bridge,
- verified assumptions,
- and a serious test of internal measures first.

But I will not join any process that legitimizes closures as the default. The burden is on management to prove the need, not on labour to prove the absence of it.

---

### To the Regional State Government
I support your rejection of socially destructive restructuring. Your blocking position matters, and it should be used now, not after a closure logic has already been normalized.

However, I also need you to be clear: a verification process is only useful if it is used to test whether **non-closure measures are sufficient**, not to create a procedural corridor toward closure decisions that management already wants.

So yes, I support independent verification.  
No, I do **not** support any process that treats closures as the normal fallback if labour does not concede enough quickly enough.

If you want to protect jobs and regional industry, then use your influence to insist on:
- a non-closure-first approach,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding investment,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

---

### To the controlling family
You say you will back whatever is economically sufficient. Fine. Then back the scenario that is **actually proven sufficient**, not the one that simply preserves owner freedom to cut deeper.

What I will not accept is the idea that “economic sufficiency” automatically means plant closures or forced layoffs. It does not.

If a non-closure package can close the gap, then support it.  
If it cannot, then show the evidence. But do not ask the workforce to accept irreversible damage before that evidence exists.

And let me be blunt: a durable company is not built by making the workforce bear all the pain while ownership protects itself from any real sacrifice.

---

### To investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal would destroy value.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible way to restore value is to threaten closures and forced layoffs up front. That creates industrial conflict, long-term distrust, and a poisoned bargaining climate. Those are real costs.

A “credible” deal must be:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- durable,
- and not dependent on permanent goodwill.

We are prepared to support a hard but fair package if it includes:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no German plant closures,
- binding job security,
- and real shared sacrifice from management and owners.

What we will not support is a restructuring that shifts the pain almost entirely onto employees and regions.

---

## Where I stand on the expert input
The expert did **not** say closures are required. The expert said the answer depends on the actual numbers and whether the problem is mainly labor/overhead inefficiency or excess fixed footprint.

That matters. It means:
- closures are not proven,
- closures are not inevitable,
- and management still has not met the burden of proof.

So yes, I support the call for proper data and independent verification.  
No, I do **not** accept the hidden assumption that the verification will justify closures. It may. It may not. We are not conceding that point in advance.

---

## What could move us
Only one thing: a **fair, shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs and sites, with management and owners also contributing materially.

That means:
- employees contribute through flexibility, productivity, and limited labour-cost moderation,
- management and owners contribute through overhead cuts, investment commitments, and a real acceptance of burden-sharing,
- and the result is a durable settlement with no forced redundancies and no plant closures.

If that is not on the table, then there is no basis for further movement.

---

## My current position remains
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No German plant closures**
- **No weakening of the collective wage agreement**
- **No reduction of co-determination to a decorative role**

We are open to serious internal measures and a fair employee contribution, but only in exchange for:
- site security,
- job security,
- shared sacrifice,
- and a verifiable savings bridge that does **not** rely on closures as the default.

If management refuses that framework, then this dispute will be decided by leverage, not by persuasion.
