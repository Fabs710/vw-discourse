I will be direct.

I agree with management on the **diagnosis**: the company cannot carry a structurally uncompetitive cost base forever. If margins stay weak, investment capacity will erode, and that is ultimately the worst outcome for everyone — especially for long-term owners.

But I do **not** accept the implication that the only credible answer is plant closures and forced redundancies in Germany. That would be an unnecessarily destructive path if there are other ways to achieve the required structural improvement.

To the works council and the union: I understand why you are defending jobs, sites, and co-determination. Those institutions are part of how this company has maintained social stability and industrial strength. But “no closures, no layoffs” cannot be an absolute if the alternative is permanent value erosion. If the company is left with the same overcapacity and cost structure, then we are merely postponing a larger crisis. That would not protect the workforce in the long run.

To management: if you want support for a major restructuring, you need to show that you have genuinely explored and quantified alternatives that preserve value without destroying industrial peace. I am open to a tough package, but only if it is **credible, durable, and economically sufficient**. A superficial compromise that protects optics but not competitiveness is not acceptable.

To the regional state government: I appreciate your insistence on social responsibility and your recognition that the company needs a durable solution. But the political reality cannot be allowed to harden into a veto on all meaningful change. If the company’s economics require capacity reduction, then that reality has to be addressed. The key question is not whether there is adjustment, but whether it is structured in a way that preserves the enterprise’s long-term viability.

My position is therefore straightforward:

- I support a restructuring that **restores competitiveness and margins**.
- I do **not** support a solution that permanently weakens the enterprise.
- I am open to alternatives to closures and forced layoffs, but only if they deliver **real, measurable, lasting savings**.
- I will not back a deal that trades short-term peace for long-term decline.

If there is a package with meaningful labour-cost contribution, genuine capacity reduction, and a path to sustainable profitability — while preserving the company’s control and future investment capacity — then I can support it through governance.

If there is not, then I will not pretend that an inadequate compromise is a solution.
