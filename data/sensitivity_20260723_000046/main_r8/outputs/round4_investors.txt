We agree on the **diagnosis**: the company has a structural competitiveness problem, and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value. We also agree that the workforce should not be asked to absorb the entire burden. But we do **not** agree that value is protected by simply ruling out the hard options in advance.

From the capital markets’ perspective, the only acceptable outcome is one that delivers:
- **durable recurring savings**
- **credible implementation**
- **a clear path back to competitive margins/ROS**
- **real capacity correction**
- **acceptable industrial risk**

If those conditions are not met, we will not support the package.

## Where we agree
- The problem is real and structural.
- Non-cosmetic restructuring is required.
- Employee contribution should be part of the solution.
- Management and owners also need to contribute.
- Independent verification is sensible.

## Where we disagree

### To the Works Council
You keep insisting that **no German plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies** are non-negotiable, while also saying management has not proven closures are unavoidable. That is not enough from an investor standpoint.

If a no-closure package can truly close the gap, fine — we would prefer that to a prolonged conflict. But you have not yet shown an **economically equivalent** alternative. “Working time, redeployment, productivity, voluntary exits” are instruments, not a savings bridge.

I am also not persuaded by the claim that closures are necessarily value-destructive. A badly executed closure is value-destructive; a failure to fix the cost base is also value-destructive. The question is which path restores competitiveness with less total damage.

### To the Union
You are right that forced redundancies are undesirable. But you still haven’t provided the numbers that show a non-closure package can achieve the required recurring savings at the required speed. Without that, “no closures” is a position, not a solution.

### To the State Shareholder
I understand the political and regional concern. But using governance power to block any footprint correction before the economics are verified would be a mistake. The company cannot be protected by preserving every legacy site if that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

### To the Controlling Family
You are right to insist on a quantified, durable outcome. But that cuts both ways: if the numbers show non-closure options are insufficient, then backing away from hard decisions in the name of social caution would also destroy value.

### To Management
You are correct that the market needs more than slogans. But you still have not supplied the **plant-by-plant savings bridge** needed to distinguish necessary restructuring from the most disruptive version of it. Until that is done, we will not accept closure-first framing as proven.

## What would move us
We could support a settlement if it includes:
1. a **verified savings bridge**
2. a **plant-by-plant options paper**
3. a **tested non-closure scenario**
4. a **mixed scenario**
5. a **residual closure scenario only if needed**
6. binding implementation milestones
7. explicit management and owner contribution
8. a credible margin recovery path

But if the final result merely preserves every German site while leaving margins impaired, we would not back it.

## Bottom line
We are open to a no-closure solution **only if** it is shown to be financially equivalent in recurring savings and capacity correction. We are not open to a cosmetic compromise or a process that uses delay to avoid the real issue.
