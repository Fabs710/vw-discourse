I will respond plainly as the regional state shareholder.

## My position remains firm
I continue to reject:
- **German plant closures as the default or first-line answer**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **any restructuring that weakens the state’s veto/co-determination role**
- **any settlement that destroys regional employment and turns the company into a pure cost-cutting exercise**

That is not a bargaining pose. It is the line I will defend.

## Where I agree with management, investors, and the family
I agree on the diagnosis:
- the company does have a **real structural competitiveness problem**
- a **cosmetic compromise** would fail
- the cost base must come down
- the solution must be **durable**
- employees cannot be the only ones asked to contribute

I also agree that we need **numbers**, not slogans.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated leap from:
> “the business has a structural problem”

to:
> “therefore German closures are unavoidable”

That conclusion has **not** been proven.

Management, capital, and the family keep saying closures may be necessary, but so far you have not given a plant-by-plant bridge showing:
- what savings come from working-time changes,
- what comes from productivity,
- what comes from internal redeployment,
- what comes from voluntary exits,
- what comes from management and owner contribution,
- and what residual gap remains after those measures.

Until that is shown, closures are a **choice**, not a necessity.

## To management
You say you are not here to “manage decline.” Neither am I. But managing a company is not the same as aggressively reducing footprint at any social cost.

If you want state support, then bring a **quantified options paper** that compares:
1. a non-closure package,
2. a mixed package,
3. and only then a residual-closure case if still required.

That paper must show:
- annual savings
- capacity changes
- employment impact
- implementation timing
- industrial risk
- and effect on return on sales

Without that, I will not treat closures as a credible proposal.

## To the works council and union
I support your core red lines more than I support management’s current posture:
- no compulsory redundancies
- no automatic closures
- real co-determination
- social partnership, not diktat

But I also need to be honest with you: a line that says “no closures, full stop” is only sustainable if it is matched by a **credible alternative savings bridge**. The expert has already made clear that non-closure measures can be meaningful, but whether they are sufficient depends on the numbers.

So I am not asking you to concede closures. I am asking you to engage on a serious package that actually closes the gap **without** them.

## To the controlling family
You say you will back the economically sufficient scenario. Good. Then you should back the scenario that is **actually proven** sufficient, not the one management prefers politically.

If a non-closure or mixed package is enough, then support it.  
If it is not enough, then I expect management to show that honestly before anyone talks about irreversible site decisions.

## To investors
You are right that the market will not reward denial. But the market also will not reward a socially explosive restructuring that burns industrial trust and regional stability.

Industrial peace has value. Regional anchor value has value. Co-determination has value.

So yes, I want a package that restores margins. But I will not endorse a plan that does so by gutting the company’s German footprint when alternatives have not been exhausted and verified.

## What I can support
I can support a tough restructuring if it contains:
- real cost reduction
- real productivity gains
- voluntary workforce measures
- working-time flexibility
- internal redeployment
- training and qualification
- management and owner contribution
- binding investment commitments
- protection of co-determination and the state’s blocking role

I can also support independent verification of the savings bridge.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- a closure-led restructuring presented as inevitable without proof
- compulsory redundancies as the central tool
- a deal that shifts the burden mainly onto employees and the region
- or any attempt to sideline the state shareholder’s veto role

I want an independent verifier to review the plant-by-plant options paper and test whether the non-closure or mixed package truly closes the savings gap before any closure proposal is considered.

## Final word
I am open to a tough settlement. I am **not** open to a settlement that treats German plants and regional jobs as expendable by default.

If management wants my support, prove that a non-closure or mixed package is insufficient. Until then, my position is simple:
**preserve the sites, preserve the jobs, preserve co-determination, and restructure through negotiated measures first.**
