I will be direct.

## My overall position
I support a **durable restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins**. I do **not** support a symbolic deal that preserves today’s cost base and postpones the problem. I also do **not** support a reckless closure-led confrontation that destroys labour peace, damages the brand, and permanently weakens the enterprise.

The decision criterion is simple:
- **Does it materially and durably restore value?**
- **Does it avoid avoidable instability?**
- **Does it preserve governance control and the long-term industrial base?**

If the answer is no, I will not back it.

---

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis:
- the company has a structural competitiveness problem,
- the German cost base is too high,
- and we cannot live on hope or cosmetic measures.

Where I disagree is your current tendency to imply that **closures are already the default answer** before the non-closure options have been fully quantified.

That is not acceptable governance from my perspective.

You have **not yet provided the full plant-by-plant savings bridge** that would justify claiming closures are unavoidable. Until you do, I will not endorse a closure-first frame. I want:
- savings by measure,
- savings by site,
- fixed-cost impact,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and the residual gap after all non-closure tools.

If the residual gap remains material, then we can discuss footprint changes on facts, not on assertion. But do not ask the owners to bless irreversible destruction before the business case is proven.

---

## To the Works Council
I agree that a one-sided solution imposed on the workforce would be value-destructive. I also agree that **co-determination is real, not decorative**.

Where I disagree is your attempt to convert **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies”** into an absolute principle without showing how the economics close.

That position is understandable politically, but it is not yet a complete restructuring answer.

You repeatedly say management must prove closures are indispensable. Fair enough. But you also need to engage with the numbers, not only the red lines. If a non-closure package can truly deliver the required durable savings, I will support it. If it cannot, then refusing to acknowledge that fact would be protecting a structure that erodes the company’s future.

So I need a **credible quantified alternative**, not just a veto.

---

## To the Union
I share your view that employees should not bear the entire burden, and I agree that forced redundancies and endless conflict are bad for value.

But I am not going to endorse a settlement that leaves the company structurally overmanned and under-margin simply because it is socially easier in the short term.

If you want to avoid closures, then present the numbers:
- what working-time adjustments save,
- what wage moderation or benefit changes save,
- what voluntary exits save,
- what productivity gains save,
- what internal redeployment actually removes from the cost base,
- and what the total is versus the required target.

The expert has already said the obvious: non-closure measures can be meaningful, but whether they are sufficient depends on the scale of the overcapacity and fixed-cost burden. Exactly. So prove sufficiency. If you can, I will prefer that route. If you cannot, then insisting on no closures becomes a refusal to solve the problem.

---

## To the Regional State Government
I respect your role and I understand why closures are politically difficult. I also agree that closures are not a neutral technical tool.

But I will be equally blunt: the state cannot both demand competitiveness and at the same time pre-emptively rule out the instruments that may be needed to achieve it.

What I will support is:
- a structured verification process,
- a serious attempt at a non-closure package,
- and, only if needed, a residual discussion of footprint changes based on the numbers.

What I will not support is turning the state’s position into a permanent veto over necessary restructuring. That would protect appearances, not value.

---

## To Institutional Investors
You are right to insist on durability and quantified savings. I agree with that.

Where I will caution you is this: do not confuse **maximum visible severity** with **maximum value creation**. A closure-led outcome that triggers prolonged conflict, production disruption, and lasting distrust can destroy more value than it creates.

You should support the package that:
- closes the structural gap,
- restores margin,
- and remains executable.

If that can be done without closures, that is preferable. If it cannot, then we have to face that honestly. But I will not let this be driven by headline optics.

---

## On the expert input
The expert assessment was useful, but it did **not** prove that closures are required. It said:
- non-closure measures can close part of the gap,
- mixed packages may be sufficient in some cases,
- closures are the strongest lever if overcapacity and fixed-site costs are large.

That is precisely why I insist on the quantified bridge. The expert confirmed the decision must be based on data, not slogans.

So yes: I support the independent review. But I do **not** accept anyone smuggling in the conclusion that because closures are powerful, they must therefore be the answer.

---

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to back a settlement if it has all of the following:
1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity adjustment**
3. **Meaningful employee contribution**
4. **No destructive instability**
5. **Protection of future investment capacity**
6. **Preservation of governance control**

That can include:
- working-time changes,
- voluntary workforce measures,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- labour-cost contributions,
- and possibly footprint changes if proven necessary.

What I will not support is a fake compromise that preserves every structure and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

---

## What I need next
Before any final discussion of closures, I want:
- a **plant-by-plant options paper**,
- a **full savings bridge**,
- and **independent verification** of whether the non-closure or mixed package closes the gap.

That analysis must compare:
1. non-closure package,
2. mixed package,
3. residual-closure case.

If the numbers show that a non-closure package is sufficient, I will support that route. If they show that it is not, then the company must be honest about the residual need for harder choices.

---

## My bottom line
I am not here to preserve every legacy arrangement.  
I am also not here to approve avoidable destruction.

I will back the scenario that is **economically sufficient, durable, and stable**.  
I will not back a scenario that merely postpones the reckoning, and I will not back a closure-first approach without proof.
