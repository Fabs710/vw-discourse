As the regional state shareholder, I need to be very clear: **I do accept that the company needs restructuring**, and I do not dismiss the seriousness of the cost and competitiveness problem. But I **do not accept** a restructuring that is achieved by **plant closures in Germany, compulsory redundancies, or a weakening of the state’s and workforce’s co-determination rights**.

Management is right about one thing: a cosmetic solution will not work. But the conclusion cannot be “therefore close plants and cut jobs first.” That would be economically and politically reckless, and it would damage the company’s role as a regional anchor far beyond the immediate balance-sheet effect.

Where I agree:
- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a temporary patch.
- There must be **real productivity and cost improvements**.
- Labour and management both need to contribute.
- The future of the group depends on restoring competitiveness and funding investment.

Where I disagree strongly:
- **German plant closures are not an acceptable default instrument.**
- **Forced redundancies are not a socially responsible first resort.**
- A restructuring that ignores the regional economic consequences is not a serious long-term strategy.
- Any attempt to sideline the state shareholder or weaken our veto/co-determination role will face resistance.

My position is straightforward:  
We are prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring package** if it combines:
1. **capacity adjustments without closures wherever possible**,  
2. **voluntary and socially responsible workforce measures**,  
3. **working-time and productivity arrangements**,  
4. **a fair employee contribution matched by binding investment commitments**, and  
5. **clear site and job guarantees for a defined period**.

That is the path I can defend to workers, the region, and the public.

To management: if you claim closures are unavoidable, then you must prove why all alternatives have failed and why the burden cannot be shared differently. Right now I have not heard a convincing case that the only way forward is to sacrifice plants and large numbers of jobs in Germany.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that the workforce must not be made to carry the whole burden. But I also need you to engage with the reality that the company must achieve material savings. “No closures, no layoffs” is a political and social red line for me; however, if we want to preserve the company’s long-term health, we will need a package that goes beyond slogans and includes measurable structural change.

So my message is this:  
**I am open to a tough negotiated settlement, but not to a one-sided cut-and-close strategy.** The company’s future must be secured **with** the workforce and the region, not against them.
