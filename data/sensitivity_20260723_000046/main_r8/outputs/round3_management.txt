I’ll be direct.

## To the Works Council and Union

I agree with you on one point: this must be **durable**, not cosmetic. A temporary patch that leaves the cost base intact would be a failure.

But I **do not** accept your core line that Germany can be restructured to competitiveness **without** any plant closures and **without** compulsory redundancies, simply by repeating “working time, productivity, redeployment, voluntary exits” as if those words by themselves add up to the required savings.

That is not a restructuring plan. It is a request to preserve the current footprint and hope the numbers work out.

You keep demanding that management prove closures are indispensable. Fair enough. But the burden of proof cuts both ways: if you want a no-closure solution, then **show me the quantified savings bridge** that closes a structural cost gap in a mature, overcapacity market. Not slogans. Numbers.

What I agree to:
- voluntary measures first where they are meaningful,
- redeployment where it genuinely reduces total cost,
- qualification where it improves deployability,
- productivity improvements,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

What I do **not** accept:
- a package that leaves the company structurally overmanned,
- a deal that preserves every site regardless of economics,
- or a 2030 guarantee that is detached from the business model and therefore just delays the next crisis.

You say “no closures” is a red line. Then you need to explain how that red line is compatible with:
- shrinking European demand,
- intense low-cost competition,
- a German factory cost base above target,
- and a brand missing its return-on-sales target.

If the answer is “with enough goodwill and flexibility,” that is not enough.

## To the Regional State Government

I respect your concern about regional impact. But you are still trying to turn a difficult industrial reality into a political request to avoid the hard decision.

I agree that closures are not neutral. I also agree they should not be used casually. But neither can they be ruled out in advance by political preference if they are required to restore competitiveness.

What I **do not** accept is the idea that the state shareholder can support the company while simultaneously rejecting the very footprint correction that may be necessary to save its future. That is inconsistent.

If you want to prevent closures, then support a package that actually closes the cost gap. If you cannot do that, then you are not providing a solution — only a constraint.

## To the Controlling Family

You are right to demand a quantified, durable result. But I need to be equally clear: “support the scenario that is economically sufficient” does not mean we can wait indefinitely while every party insists the burden should fall somewhere else.

The family holding must decide whether it wants:
- a real competitiveness reset,
- or a prolonged erosion of value under the cover of social caution.

I am not asking for destruction. I am asking for structural correction. Those are not the same.

If a non-closure package can demonstrably deliver equivalent durable savings, I will consider it. But that proof has not been supplied. Until then, I will not pretend that “avoid closures at all costs” is a credible ownership strategy.

## To Institutional Investors

You are right to insist on a credible return to margins. You are also right that industrial peace has value. But let’s be precise: industrial peace that protects an uncompetitive structure is not value creation.

I agree with your insistence on:
- quantified savings,
- durable restructuring,
- and implementation certainty.

Where I disagree is the implication that the company should wait until someone produces a perfect non-closure bridge while the cost base remains uncorrected. Time is not on our side.

If labour wants to avoid closures, then the package must be economically equivalent in **recurring annual savings**, not just symbolically equivalent in headlines. Otherwise, it does not solve the problem.

## To the Expert Input

The expert assessment was useful, but it did **not** change the core reality.

It confirmed three things:
1. non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings,
2. a mixed package may be sufficient in some cases,
3. but if overcapacity and fixed-site costs are large, closures remain the strongest tool.

That supports my position: **we need the actual numbers**, and we need them fast. Not because closures are automatically preferred, but because they may be required if the gap is large.

So yes: I support a plant-by-plant savings bridge and an independent verification process. But I do **not** accept the conclusion some of you are trying to smuggle in — namely, that because non-closure tools exist, closures can be ruled out. They cannot.

## Where I Stand Now

Let me be unambiguous.

### I agree:
- The solution must be durable.
- Employees should contribute.
- Alternatives to closures should be tested seriously.
- A cosmetic compromise would be unacceptable.
- The package must be quantified and enforceable.

### I disagree:
- I do not accept “no closures” as an absolute principle.
- I do not accept “no compulsory redundancies” as a stand-alone solution if voluntary measures fail to close the gap.
- I do not accept site guarantees that are detached from economic reality.
- I do not accept that management should be forced to keep an uncompetitive footprint in Germany.

### What I need next:
A **fully quantified options paper**, plant by plant, showing:
- current capacity and utilization,
- direct and indirect cost by site,
- savings from working-time changes,
- savings from productivity and redeployment,
- savings from voluntary exits,
- savings from labour-cost measures,
- management and owner contribution,
- and the residual gap under each scenario.

That analysis must compare at least:
1. non-closure package,
2. mixed package,
3. residual-closure package.

If the non-closure package closes the gap, fine — I will support that route.  
If it does not, then we need the courage to say so and act accordingly.

## Final word

I am not here to negotiate decline. I am here to restore competitiveness.

That means:
- real cost reduction,
- real capacity correction,
- real labour-cost contribution,
- and real investment headroom for 2030.

If the only way to achieve that is through difficult site decisions, I will not rule them out.  
If someone can prove a no-closure package achieves the same durable outcome, I am willing to look at it — but not to assume it.

The next round should be about numbers, not slogans.
