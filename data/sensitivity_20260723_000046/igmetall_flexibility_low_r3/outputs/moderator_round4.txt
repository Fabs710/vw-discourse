### Core impasse

The main deadlock is between:

- **Labour-side actors** insisting that restructuring must be **closure-free, no compulsory redundancies, and co-determination-safe**, and
- **Management / capital-side actors** insisting that the plan must deliver **material, durable cost and capacity reduction**, while keeping **plant closures available** if the numbers require them.

In practical terms, the conflict is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which instruments may be used before the savings gap is fully quantified**.

A second, related impasse is sequencing:
- labour wants **binding security first**, then contributions;
- management wants **credible economics first**, then security commitments.

The state government is caught between those positions, seeking a site-preserving solution but not wanting to ignore economic reality.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions

#### 1) Quantified no-closure test phase
**Proposal:** A time-limited restructuring phase with a binding no-closure commitment for the duration of the test, paired with hard monthly or quarterly savings milestones.

**Core elements:**
- no German plant closures during the test window,
- no compulsory redundancies during that window,
- quantified contributions from management, owners, and labour,
- transparent reporting on savings achieved,
- pre-agreed review gate based on audited numbers,
- if the gap remains unclosed at the end of the period, parties re-enter negotiations on next-step options.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It gives labour and the state a real protection period, while giving management and investors a structured way to test whether the closure-free route is sufficient.

---

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring with targeted footprint action only as last resort
**Proposal:** A package combining:
- management and overhead cuts,
- owner contribution,
- voluntary workforce measures,
- working-time moderation,
- portfolio simplification,
- and only limited, pre-defined footprint adjustments if the quantified savings gap still remains.

**Core elements:**
- closures are not the starting tool,
- they are only triggered if a jointly reviewed numerical threshold is not met,
- all other levers must be activated first,
- co-determination stays involved in implementation,
- the plan is explicitly framed as “reduce capacity where necessary, preserve sites where possible.”

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It avoids making closures the default, while preserving management’s claim that structural action remains available if needed.

---

#### 3) Security-for-contribution exchange with enforceable safeguards
**Proposal:** A formal exchange package in which labour accepts bounded, temporary contributions only in return for hard site and job safeguards.

**Core elements:**
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- no closures during the agreement period,
- temporary and capped labour contribution,
- visible management restraint,
- visible owner contribution,
- binding investment commitments for remaining German sites,
- enforcement clauses and transparency obligations.

**Why this bridges the gap:**  
It makes contribution and security reciprocal rather than sequential, which may reduce distrust and improve implementability.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
**What exact savings target remains after management cuts, owner contribution, overhead reduction, portfolio simplification, and voluntary workforce measures are fully quantified, and what portion of that gap can be closed without any plant closures?**

#### To the Works Council
**If a binding no-closure test phase were offered with audited milestones and enforceable security clauses, what specific temporary contributions would you be prepared to discuss, and under what conditions?**

#### To the Union
**What is the maximum bounded labour contribution you would consider if it were matched by binding site security, management restraint, and visible owner participation?**

#### To the State Government
**Will you support a formal no-closure test phase with hard numbers and a review gate, using your blocking position to prevent premature irreversible action during that period?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a hybrid package that preserves sites unless the quantified gap remains materially open after all non-closure measures have been exhausted and independently verified?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified improvement in cost, capacity, and return on sales would make you regard a closure-free or hybrid package as value-preserving rather than merely delayed restructuring?**

#### To the Expert
**Can you define the specific numerical threshold at which a no-closure package becomes unlikely to close the gap sufficiently, so the parties know when the discussion has moved from feasible to implausible?**
