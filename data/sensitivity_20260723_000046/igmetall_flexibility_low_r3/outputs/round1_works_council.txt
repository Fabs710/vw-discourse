As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for a restructuring that management failed to prepare responsibly.

If the company wants a viable future, then it must come with a credible alternative that protects the German sites, protects the workforce, and gives real security through 2030. That means:

- binding guarantees for every German plant,
- no forced layoffs,
- negotiated, socially acceptable adjustments rather than unilateral cuts,
- a genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring,
- and a fair contribution from all sides, including management and capital.

We are not blind to the competitive pressure. We know the market is difficult. But burdening the workforce alone, closing plants, and tearing up protections is the wrong answer. It destroys trust, destroys skills, and weakens the company for the long term.

If management is serious about the future, then it must negotiate a solution that secures sites, jobs, and investment. If not, they should expect fierce resistance from the workforce, from the works council, and from the entire co-determination side.
