I will speak plainly, because the company does not need more generalities.

The family holding’s position remains this: **we support a credible restructuring that restores competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity. We do not support a solution that preserves every structure, pushes the problem into the future, and leaves the company permanently uncompetitive.**

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one point: **the burden cannot fall on labour alone**.  
If employees are asked to contribute, then management, the ownership layer, and capital must also contribute visibly. That is not only fair; it is necessary for labour peace and execution.

I also agree that a cosmetic deal would be dangerous. A temporary patch that leaves the German cost base structurally too high would destroy value later.

And I agree that Germany remains strategically important. The goal is not to hollow out the core asset, but to make it viable.

## Where I disagree
### 1) To the Works Council
I do **not** accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute starting point.

You are asking for binding guarantees through 2030, but you have not shown how those guarantees can coexist with the level of structural change management says is needed. If the footprint is too large and costs are too high, then simply ruling out closures or workforce reduction does not solve the math.

I hear your point about trust, skills, and regional ecosystems. That matters. But **trust is not preserved by pretending the economics do not exist**. If the alternative is permanent margin erosion and a weaker company, then that also destroys jobs — just more slowly.

### 2) To the Union
Your willingness to discuss bounded concessions is constructive, but I cannot accept the sequence “site security first, economics later.”

The company needs a package that is economically sound at the outset. If labour makes concessions, those concessions must contribute to a real structural fix, not merely delay a more painful adjustment. A restructuring fund, wage moderation, working-time flexibility — these can be part of the answer, but only if they are sufficient and durable.

### 3) To the State Government
I respect your role and the regional responsibility you carry. But the blocking powers built into the governance structure are not there to prevent all difficult change. They are there to prevent reckless change.

A site-preserving solution is desirable. A site-preserving solution that fails to restore competitiveness is not.

### 4) To Investors
You are right that the market will not reward a cosmetic compromise. But the market will also not reward a socially explosive package that triggers prolonged strikes, operational disruption, and reputational damage. So the test is not “hardest possible cuts”; it is **credible and executable structural improvement**.

### 5) To Management
Management is correct that the status quo is not acceptable. But it must also be disciplined: no threat-based negotiation, no treating labour as the only adjustable variable, and no overclaiming that closures are automatically the first and only answer.

## What would move us
The family holding could support a settlement if it contains all of the following:

- **real structural cost reduction**,
- **a durable reduction in capacity**,
- **meaningful management restraint and overhead cuts**,
- **a visible contribution from ownership and capital**,
- **labour flexibility and moderated compensation**,
- **investment commitments for the sites that remain**,
- and **labour peace sufficient to execute the plan**.

That package may include closures or deep site restructuring if, and only if, equivalent savings cannot be achieved through other measures with equal reliability and speed. I am not saying closures must happen. I am saying they cannot be excluded by slogan.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that keeps all sites open regardless of economics,
- unconditional guarantees that block necessary restructuring,
- or a compromise that preserves the old cost structure while claiming victory.

That would be a managed decline, and that is fundamentally unacceptable.

## My position on the current trade-offs
The clearest path to agreement is not to insist that one side “wins.” It is to establish whether the proposed non-closure alternatives truly deliver the same savings, on the same timeline, with the same certainty.

If the answer is yes, I will listen.  
If the answer is no, then selective closures or deeper restructuring remain on the table.

So my request is simple: bring a **quantified package**.  
Show:
- the savings,
- the timing,
- the labour contribution,
- the management contribution,
- and the investment commitments.

Without that, the argument for unconditional protection is not convincing enough to justify the value risk.
