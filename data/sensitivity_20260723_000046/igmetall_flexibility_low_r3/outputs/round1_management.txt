I hear the red lines from works council and union, but I need to be equally clear: **a purely defensive solution is not enough, and a cosmetic compromise would be worse than no agreement at all.**

We are in a structural crisis, not a temporary dip. German factory costs are materially too high, European demand is shrinking, and competitors are moving faster and cheaper. If we do not fix the cost and capacity base now, we will be back here in 12–24 months with even less room to maneuver, less cash for product investment, and a weaker brand.

So let me respond directly:

### Where I agree
- **I agree that the burden cannot fall on labour alone.**
  Management will have to contribute, and the group will need to support the restructuring with credible investment in future products and technology.
- **I agree that we need a durable settlement, not chaos.**
  Prolonged strikes, uncertainty, and a collapse in trust would damage the company further.
- **I agree that Germany remains strategically important.**
  We are not talking about abandoning the country; we are talking about restoring competitiveness so that German operations remain viable.

### Where I disagree
- **I do not accept a blanket “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” stance as the starting point.**
  If the cost base and capacity remain structurally out of line, then every option has to be on the table. That includes plant closures and workforce reductions where they are needed to achieve a sustainable footprint.
- **I do not accept a restructuring that preserves all existing structures and only adds temporary concessions.**
  That would be a short-term patch, not a turnaround.
- **I do not accept the idea that a guarantee through 2030 can be unconditional.**
  Any site or employment security has to be tied to a credible package that actually delivers the savings and flexibility we need.

### What I am prepared to consider
I am open to a negotiated package if it achieves **real, structural effects**. That means:
- a **durable reduction in capacity**,
- a **genuine labour-cost contribution**,
- changes in working time and flexibility,
- and, if necessary, closures or deep restructuring of specific sites rather than vague promises spread across all sites.

I am also prepared to discuss:
- staged implementation,
- social cushioning,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and investment commitments for the sites that remain.

But I need to be explicit: **a deal that avoids the hard decisions and leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not acceptable.** We need something that restores the brand’s return on sales and frees resources for 2030 investment. If we fail to do that, we will simply be preserving the problem.

So my message is this:
- We are ready to negotiate.
- We are not ready to pretend there is a painless solution.
- If you want site security, you need to help create the economic basis for it.
- If you want to rule out closures entirely, then you must show how equivalent structural savings will be delivered with equal reliability and speed.

Right now, I have not heard that alternative.
