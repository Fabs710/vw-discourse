From the perspective of dispersed institutional investors, the central question is very simple:

**Will this restructuring credibly restore competitive margins and protect the long-term value of the equity, or will it preserve an uncompetitive cost base through a politically convenient but financially weak compromise?**

Right now, we are not prepared to back a cosmetic settlement.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and even parts of the labour side on one important point: **the burden cannot fall on employees alone**. A restructuring that relies only on wage restraint while leaving capacity, complexity, and management costs untouched would not be credible.

We also agree that **prolonged conflict is value-destructive**. Strikes, uncertainty, and delay damage margins, cash flow, and execution. If this drags on, all shareholders lose.

And we agree that **the company needs a durable solution, not a short-term patch**. Investors have already seen too many “bridge” measures that postpone the problem and then come back later with worse economics.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** share the position that plant closures are automatically unacceptable, nor do we accept a restructuring framework that effectively rules out the tools needed to fix the cost base.

If the German footprint is structurally too large and costs are materially above peers, then **the company must be able to take decisive action**. That may include closures, deep site restructuring, capacity reduction, and workforce reduction. We are not interested in preserving every site if the result is structurally impaired margins and weaker returns on capital.

We also do **not** accept unconditional job or site guarantees through 2030 if they prevent a real turnaround. Guarantees are only valuable if they are consistent with an economically viable plan. Otherwise they simply lock in underperformance and shift the problem into the future.

## What matters to us
We would support whatever package credibly delivers:

- **durable cost reduction**,
- **capacity aligned to market reality**,
- **a clear improvement in return on sales**,
- **execution certainty**,
- and **enough flexibility to compete against lower-cost rivals**.

That means any agreement must be judged by outcomes, not rhetoric. We will look for:
- measurable and front-loaded savings,
- a credible reduction in structural overheads,
- a meaningful cut in excess capacity,
- and evidence that labour-cost concessions are real, lasting, and not easily reversed.

## On the other stakeholders
### To the Works Council
We respect co-determination, but co-determination does not change the arithmetic. If the cost base remains uncompetitive, employment is not protected by blocking change; it is endangered by it. “No closures, no redundancies” is not a financial strategy.

### To the Union
We recognize the value of industrial peace, but strikes cannot be used to force the company into an uneconomic compromise. If labour wants security, it must be matched by economic realism. Shared sacrifice is acceptable; open-ended resistance is not.

### To Management
We support decisive restructuring, but not vague threat-based management. You need to present a package that is:
- specific,
- measurable,
- and credible on implementation.

If you ask investors to tolerate disruption, then we need assurance that the pain is leading somewhere. Management restraint and operational simplification have to be part of the plan, not just labour concessions.

### To the State Government
We understand the political sensitivity, but public ownership and regional interests cannot become a veto over necessary economic repair. Protecting jobs by preventing restructuring is not a sustainable policy if it destroys value and ultimately weakens the company’s future.

### To the Family Holding
We agree that the company cannot afford a managed decline. But stewardship also means backing the hard decisions, not only the language of long-term value. If you want market confidence, you need to support a package that actually fixes the economics.

## Our bottom line
We are **open to a negotiated restructuring**, but only if it is genuinely structural and clearly restores competitiveness.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a prolonged conflict that erodes margins,
- or a deal that preserves an uncompetitive German cost base.

If the final package does not deliver credible, durable savings and a believable path back to target profitability, then investors should expect continued pressure on the share price and growing capital discipline from the market.
