I largely agree with the Works Council on the core red lines: no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no tearing up of hard-won collective protections as if the workforce alone must pay for management’s strategic failures.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: the collective bargaining agreement has lapsed, the peace obligation has ended, and our members are prepared to act. Warning strikes have already shown that this is not an empty threat. If management insists on shifting the full burden onto workers through closures, layoffs, and unilateral wage destruction, then we will escalate.

Where I differ slightly is on how a durable solution can still be built. We are not refusing contribution. We have already signalled openness to a shared-sacrifice package:
- labour-cost moderation,
- capacity reduction,
- temporary concessions,
- possibly foregoing bonuses or contributing to a restructuring fund,
- and negotiated working-time measures.

But that only makes sense if it is tied to real security:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- and a credible investment and product commitment for the German sites.

What is unacceptable is a restructuring where employees give concessions while management and owners keep their hands clean. That is not a turnaround; that is a transfer of crisis costs onto the workforce. If the company wants credibility, then the contribution must be shared:
- management must also cut costs and accept restraint,
- owners and capital must make a visible contribution,
- and there must be a clear commitment to future industrial employment here in Germany.

So my position is straightforward:
- We are open to negotiations on a fair, reversible, socially responsible restructuring.
- We are not open to compulsory redundancies or plant closures.
- We will not accept open-ended job insecurity dressed up as competitiveness.
- And if management comes back with a package that still treats labour as the only adjustable variable, we will fight it.

If the other stakeholders want a solution, then the next proposal must prove three things: job security, site security, and shared pain. Without that, there is no basis for agreement.
