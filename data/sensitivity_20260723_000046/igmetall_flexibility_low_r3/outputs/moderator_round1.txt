### Moderator Summary — Round 1

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that the situation requires a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement that **the burden cannot fall on labour alone**.
- Several stakeholders accept that **management, owners, and capital must also contribute**.
- There is shared recognition that **Germany remains strategically important** and that prolonged conflict would damage the company.
- Most parties are open to a **negotiated solution**, provided it is credible and sustainable.

#### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works Council, union, and state government reject them as a starting point; management, family holding, and investors say they cannot be ruled out.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour and public stakeholders reject them; management and capital say workforce reductions may be necessary if structural savings are required.
- **Site/job guarantees through 2030:**  
  Labour and state side want binding protection; management and capital argue guarantees must be conditional on economic viability.
- **Extent of restructuring:**  
  Labour side wants socially negotiated adjustments and redeployment; management and investors want deeper, structural capacity and cost reductions.
- **Role of co-determination and public stakeholder influence:**  
  Labour and state emphasize strong involvement and protection; capital side emphasizes economic necessity and implementation speed.

#### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to reconcile **site and job security** with **credible structural cost reduction**.
- What level of **labour contribution** is acceptable if there are also **management and owner contributions**.
- Whether savings can be achieved through **redeployment, working-time changes, voluntary exits, and flexibility** alone, or whether **closures/reductions** are unavoidable.
- What concrete **investment and product commitments** can be tied to concessions.
- How to define a package that is both **socially acceptable** and **financially sufficient**.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **specific package options**:
- identify which measures are on the table,
- quantify what each side can contribute,
- specify what protections or guarantees would be exchanged,
- and clarify which combinations could produce both **competitiveness** and **job/site security**.

The next round should focus on **tested trade-offs**, not general red lines.
