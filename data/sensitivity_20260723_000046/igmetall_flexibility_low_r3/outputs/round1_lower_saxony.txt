I will be equally direct.

We, as the regional state and anchor shareholder, **do not accept a restructuring that solves the cost problem by gutting employment in our state or by sidelining co-determination**. That is not a sustainable turnaround; it is an economically and politically destructive shortcut.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one point: **the company cannot simply drift**. A durable response is needed, and competitiveness matters. If the group is structurally losing money, then inaction would eventually threaten even more jobs and sites.

I also agree with the union and works council that **the burden cannot fall on the workforce alone**. If employees are asked for concessions, then management, the group, and capital must all contribute visibly.

### Where I disagree
I clearly disagree with the idea that **plant closures in Germany are the default solution**. For a company of this importance, with strong social partnership traditions and with stakeholders that intentionally hold veto rights, “close first, negotiate later” is not an acceptable starting point.

I also disagree with any framing that treats the regional state’s role as merely symbolic. We are not a passive investor. We are a legitimate public stakeholder with a blocking minority and a responsibility to protect:
- industrial employment,
- regional stability,
- and the long-term viability of the company as a regional anchor.

A restructuring that destroys that role or forces us into accepting closures as a fait accompli will meet resistance.

### What I can support
I am open to a **real restructuring package** if it satisfies three conditions:

1. **Jobs and sites are protected as far as economically possible**
   - no blanket plant closures as the first resort,
   - no compulsory redundancies if alternatives exist,
   - maximum use of internal redeployment, working-time measures, voluntary exits, and phased adjustments.

2. **The savings are structural and credible**
   - not cosmetic measures,
   - not just temporary wage concessions,
   - but a package that genuinely improves productivity, simplifies operations, and strengthens the product roadmap.

3. **The burden is shared fairly**
   - management must accept restraint,
   - the owners and capital must contribute,
   - and the workforce must be part of the solution, not the sole target.

### My position on the current proposals
At this point, I cannot support a proposal that includes German plant closures without first exhausting alternatives that preserve sites and jobs. I am not saying “no change”; I am saying **no one-sided closure strategy**.

If management wants our support, then it needs to come back with something materially better than:
- closures,
- mass layoffs,
- and the erosion of long-standing protections.

What would be meaningful to us is a negotiated package with:
- binding site commitments,
- a credible employment bridge,
- investments tied to future products,
- and a restructuring burden distributed across management, owners, and labour.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to be constructive, but not to rubber-stamp industrial retreat. If there is a path to competitiveness that keeps the company rooted in Germany and preserves its role as a regional economic anchor, we are ready to work on it. If the proposal remains a plan for closures and social damage dressed up as necessity, then we will not support it.
