## Core impasse

The central impasse is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All parties accept that some form of restructuring is necessary.

The impasse is **how the savings gap is closed** and **who bears the irreversible risk**:

- **Management / family holding / investors** want a package that is fast, durable, and large enough to reset competitiveness; they insist closures must remain available if non-closure measures fall short.
- **Works council / union** insist that closures and compulsory redundancies must be ruled out, and that labour should not concede first without binding security.
- **State government** wants a site-preserving solution, but not an economically empty one.
- The **expert** has narrowed the dispute: a closure-free package is possible in principle, but only if the savings gap is manageable and all sides make material, quantified contributions.

So the true blockage is this:

> **Can the parties agree on a quantified, enforceable non-closure package that closes the savings gap fast enough, without making closures the fallback threat or labour the first mover?**

---

## Facilitator proposals: concrete compromise positions

### 1) Time-bound no-closure package with hard milestones and a review gate
A possible bridge is a **time-bound no-closure restructuring phase** with:
- explicit savings targets by quarter,
- quantified contributions from management, owners, and labour,
- a binding prohibition on plant closures during the test phase,
- and a jointly agreed review gate at the end of the period.

At the review gate, parties assess whether the non-closure measures delivered the required savings. If not, the discussion moves to additional measures, but only after the non-closure package has been fully implemented and audited.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It gives the works council and union genuine security in the near term, while giving management and capital a way to test whether the economics actually work.

---

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” plus capacity reduction through voluntary and internal tools only
A second compromise is a package that explicitly rules out:
- compulsory redundancies,
- immediate plant closures,
- and unilateral site shutdowns,

but still allows:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- internal redeployment,
- reduced shifts,
- line consolidation within sites,
- and temporary working-time adjustments.

This would be paired with:
- management overhead cuts,
- owner support,
- and product/portfolio simplification.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It preserves the works council’s core red lines while still allowing real capacity reduction and cost improvement.

---

### 3) Security-for-contribution exchange with enforceable guarantees
A third compromise is an exchange structure:

- Labour provides a **bounded, temporary, measurable contribution**.
- In return, management and owners provide **binding site guarantees**, investment commitments, and co-determined implementation rules.
- The guarantees are enforceable through agreed governance mechanisms, not just political statements.

This can include:
- capped wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- flexibility measures,
- and voluntary workforce adjustment,
in exchange for:
- no closures during the agreed period,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and protected co-determination in implementation.

**Why this bridges conflict:**  
It turns the dispute from “trust us first” into a reciprocal, legally and institutionally anchored swap.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What exact savings gap remains after management cuts, owner contribution, portfolio simplification, voluntary exits, and working-time measures are fully quantified — and how large is that remaining gap in euros and timing?**

### To the Works Council
**If plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what specific combination of labor measures are you prepared to quantify and commit to so the restructuring gap is still closed on time?**

### To the Union
**What is the maximum bounded, temporary labour contribution you are prepared to put on the table, and what enforceable security do you require in return?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete blocking or oversight mechanism are you prepared to use to ensure a site-preserving deal is actually binding, rather than merely rhetorical?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What minimum financial and governance conditions would make you accept a no-closure package as credible, even if it delivers savings more slowly than a footprint reset?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What quantified savings, by when, and at what execution risk would make a closure-free package acceptable to you as value-preserving rather than value-dilutive?**
