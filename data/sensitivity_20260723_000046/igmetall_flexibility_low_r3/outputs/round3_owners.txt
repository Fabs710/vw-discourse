I will be direct.

The family holding supports a **serious restructuring that restores competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity**. We do **not** support a solution that preserves every structure, delays adjustment, and leaves the company permanently uncompetitive.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on several fundamentals:

- **The status quo is not acceptable.**
- **The burden cannot fall on labour alone.**
- **Management, ownership, and capital must contribute visibly.**
- **A cosmetic compromise would destroy value.**
- **Germany remains strategically important.**
- **Prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.**

I also agree with the expert’s core point: a closure-free package can work in principle, but only if the savings gap is not too large, the contributions are real and quantified, and the company accepts a longer implementation path.

## Where I disagree

### To the Works Council
I do **not** accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute starting point.

That position may be understandable socially, but it does not answer the economic question. If the German footprint is structurally too large and the cost base too high, then you cannot simply rule out the measures that may be needed to fix that.

You say non-closure alternatives have not been exhausted. Fair point — they should be tested properly. But you have not yet shown a quantified package that closes the full gap with enough certainty and speed. Until that exists, an outright ban on closures is not a credible governance position.

### To the Union
Your willingness to discuss bounded labour contributions is constructive, and I acknowledge that. But I cannot accept the sequence “site security first, economics later.”

If the company is asked to make long-term commitments, those commitments must be consistent with a viable economic plan. Labour contributions can be part of the answer, but they cannot be the only serious adjustment while the structural problem remains unresolved.

### To Management
You are right that the company needs structural change. But you still need to be more precise.

You have said closures remain on the table if needed. That is acceptable as a governance principle, but not enough on its own. I want a quantified answer to these questions:

- What exact savings target must be achieved?
- How much comes from management and overhead cuts?
- How much comes from owner support and capital structure flexibility?
- How much comes from labour measures?
- How much can be achieved through capacity reduction without closures?
- What remains if closures are excluded?

Without that, “closures may be necessary” is an assertion, not a business case.

### To the State Government
I respect your role and the governance structure. But I do not accept the idea that blocking powers should be used to prevent all difficult change.

Your role is to prevent reckless or value-destructive restructuring, not to freeze the footprint regardless of economics. A site-preserving solution is preferable, yes. But only if it is economically credible. If it is not, then you are not protecting value — you are postponing the loss.

### To Investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. But you should also be careful not to confuse “hard” with “good.”

A closure-driven package that triggers prolonged strikes, operational disruption, and reputational damage can destroy as much value as an underpowered compromise. The correct test is not ideological toughness. It is **risk-adjusted value creation**.

## What would move us
The family holding could support a settlement if it contains all of the following:

- **real structural cost reduction**
- **a durable reduction in capacity**
- **meaningful management restraint and overhead cuts**
- **a visible contribution from ownership and capital**
- **material labour flexibility and moderated compensation**
- **investment commitments for the sites that remain**
- **labour peace sufficient to execute the plan**

That package may include closures or deep site restructuring if, and only if, the non-closure alternatives do not deliver equivalent savings with equal reliability and speed.

I am **not** saying closures must happen. I am saying they cannot be ruled out by slogan.

## What I will not support
I will not support:

- a deal that keeps all sites open regardless of economics,
- unconditional guarantees that block necessary restructuring,
- or a compromise that preserves the old cost structure while claiming success.

That would be a managed decline, and that is fundamentally unacceptable.

## On the current trade-off
The key question is not whether change is needed. It is whether the proposed non-closure package is strong enough to restore margins and competitiveness in time.
