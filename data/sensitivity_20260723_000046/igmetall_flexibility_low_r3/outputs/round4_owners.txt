I’ll be direct.

The family holding remains aligned with the basic diagnosis from management, investors, and the expert: **the current structure is not competitive enough, and a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive**. But I also agree with labour and the state on one crucial point: **a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value too**. So the question is not whether to restructure; it is whether the package is **credible, durable, and executable**.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that the company needs:
- a durable reduction in cost,
- a lower fixed-cost base,
- better capacity utilization,
- and a path back to competitive margins by 2030.

You are also right that the burden cannot fall on labour alone. Management, owners, and capital all have to contribute.

### With the expert
The expert’s core point is correct: a **closure-free restructuring is possible in principle**, but only if:
- the savings gap is manageable,
- all sides make material quantified contributions,
- and the company accepts a slower implementation path.

That is the right framework. It is also the right warning: no-closure is not automatically enough.

### With the state government
I agree that co-determination and the special governance structure are not inconveniences to be bypassed. They are part of the company’s institutional reality. Any settlement that ignores that reality will fail in implementation.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
I do **not** accept an absolute ban on closures and compulsory redundancies as a starting principle.

That position is understandable politically, but it is not yet a complete economic answer. If the German footprint is structurally too large and the cost base materially above target, then you cannot preclude the tools that may be needed to fix that. If you want us to support a no-closure path, then bring the numbers that prove it closes the gap with enough certainty and speed.

So far, I have heard a lot of insistence on red lines, but not a full quantified alternative.

### With management
I also do **not** accept loose talk that “closures may be necessary” as a substitute for a proper business case.

You need to be more precise. The family holding expects a quantified comparison of:
- the actual savings target,
- management cuts,
- owner/holding contribution,
- labour contribution,
- savings from working-time, redeployment, and overhead reduction,
- and the residual gap if closures are excluded.

Without that, closure talk becomes a negotiating posture rather than a governance decision.

### With investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. But don’t reduce the decision to “hardest lever = best lever.” A closure-driven package can destroy value if it triggers prolonged strikes, reputational damage, and loss of capability. Risk-adjusted value matters more than brute-force savings on paper.

## My position

The family holding is prepared to support a settlement if it contains all of the following:
- **real structural cost reduction**
- **meaningful capacity reduction**
- **visible management restraint and overhead cuts**
- **a material owner contribution**
- **a bounded, measurable labour contribution**
- **investment commitments for the remaining German sites**
- **a labour peace outcome that allows implementation**

What we are **not** prepared to support is:
- a deal that preserves the full footprint regardless of economics,
- unconditional guarantees that block necessary restructuring,
- or a “site-preserving” compromise that merely postpones the loss.

## On closures

I am not saying closures must happen. I am saying they **cannot be ruled out in advance by slogan**.

If non-closure alternatives can truly close the gap, then show it. If they cannot, then the company must retain the option of deeper footprint action. That is not ideological. That is stewardship.

## What I want next

Before taking a firmer governance position, I want a **quantified comparison** of three options:
1. closure-based restructuring,
2. closure-free restructuring with quantified management/owner/labour contributions,
3. hybrid restructuring,

including:
- annual savings,
- one-time costs,
- timing to realization,
- execution risk,
- and impact on return on sales through 2030.

## Bottom line

The family holding supports a serious restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins.

We will support a negotiated, site-preserving solution **if it is hard enough to work**.

We will not support a solution that:
- keeps every structure intact,
- avoids the hard economic questions,
- and leaves the company permanently uncompetitive.

If the numbers justify a closure-free route, we can consider it. If they do not, then closures or deeper restructuring remain on the table.

That is the line.
