I will respond plainly.

## To the Works Council
I support your red lines: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures as a bargaining starting point, and no dismantling of hard-won protections**. That remains the union’s position too.

Where I want to sharpen the message is this: if there is to be any labour contribution, it must be **bounded, negotiated, and clearly exchanged for security**. I will not ask members to accept open-ended pain for vague promises. If the workforce gives, it must get something real:
- binding site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a clear investment path,
- and protection of the collective wage framework.

So yes, we are aligned on resisting unilateral cuts. But I want to be equally clear that any concession from labour must be **part of a fair package**, not a one-way sacrifice.

## To Management
You keep saying the company needs “structural” change. I do not dispute that the environment is difficult. What I dispute is your implication that the only credible structural tool is closures and forced job cuts.

That is not the only path, and you have not yet justified why it should be the first or default path.

If you want the union to consider concessions, then you must come with a package that shows:
- **management restraint**,
- **owner contribution**,
- **real investment commitments**,
- and a restructuring that does not simply make employees absorb the full shock.

Right now, what you are offering sounds too much like: “accept the pain now, trust us later.” That is not enough.

## To the Family Holding
You say the company cannot afford managed decline. I agree. But a strategy that destabilizes labour relations, destroys trust, and pushes the workforce into confrontation is not stewardship either.

If you want credibility from the union side, then you need to demonstrate that you are not just backing a harder line from above. That means:
- visible financial participation,
- restraint at the top,
- and support for a settlement that protects the industrial base in Germany.

I also reject the idea that “economic reality” automatically means closures. Economic reality also includes:
- the cost of strikes,
- the loss of skills,
- the damage from broken trust,
- and the long-term value of maintaining experienced workforces at core sites.

## To the State Government
I appreciate that you are using your position to defend industrial employment and regional stability. That is important. But “not a one-sided closure strategy” is not yet strong enough for the workforce.

If the state is serious about its responsibility, it should support a **site-preserving restructuring** and use its influence to prevent forced redundancies and closures, not merely moderate them.

## To Investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic compromise is not enough. But neither is a financially convenient solution that transfers all adjustment costs onto labour while pretending that is “discipline.”

We know that the company must become more competitive. The question is **how**. If your answer is only “cut deeper, faster, and harder,” then you are not solving the problem sustainably; you are creating industrial conflict and undermining the very execution you say you want.

## What the union can consider
I will be specific, since that is what this round requires.

The union can discuss a package involving:
- **temporary labour-cost moderation**,
- **waiver or reduction of bonuses**,
- **working-time flexibility**,
- **voluntary instruments** for workforce adjustment,
- **redeployment and retraining**,
- and, if necessary, a **restructuring contribution fund** from employees.

But those are only discussable if they are tied to:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Binding guarantees for the German sites**
4. **Meaningful management and owner contributions**
5. **A real future-product and investment commitment**

If those conditions are not met, then there is no basis for a fair settlement.

## Where I draw the line
I want this explicit:  
A package where workers give up wages, flexibility, and protections while management and owners keep the hard decisions for labour alone is **not acceptable**.

And a package that says “site security later, after the concessions” is also not acceptable.

The sequence matters:
- first a credible shared-sacrifice framework,
- then labour-side flexibility,
- but always with binding protection against closures and forced layoffs.

## My bottom line
I remain open to a negotiated solution.  
I remain willing to talk about employee contributions.  
I remain willing to help secure a durable future for the company.

But I am **not** moving on the core issue:
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no German plant closures,**
- **no unilateral tearing up of the collective wage framework.**

If management wants a real settlement, it needs to come back with a package that distributes the burden fairly and protects jobs and sites. Otherwise, the union will continue to use its bargaining and strike power to resist this restructuring.
