I will be equally direct.

The state government is **not** prepared to accept a restructuring logic that starts from **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the default answer**. That may be convenient for execution, but it is not acceptable as a governing principle for a company that is a regional economic anchor and a major employer in our state.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, the family holding, investors, the works council, the union, and the expert on the fundamentals:

- the status quo is not sustainable,
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**,
- the burden cannot fall on labour alone,
- management, owners, and capital must contribute visibly,
- and a purely cosmetic deal would be value-destructive.

I also agree with the expert’s central point: a **closure-free restructuring is possible in principle**, but only if the savings gap is realistically bridgeable and all sides make **material, quantified, enforceable** contributions.

That is the right standard.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You keep speaking as if closures are the “realistic” benchmark. That is not yet proven. It is an **assertion**, not a business case.

You have not shown:
- the exact savings target,
- what management can cut,
- what owners can contribute,
- what labour can contribute,
- what can be achieved through working-time, redeployment, portfolio simplification, and overhead cuts,
- and what residual gap remains **before** closures are considered.

Until that analysis is complete, closures are not “necessary”; they are merely the hardest available lever.

I also reject the implied sequence that labour must first concede security before the company proves the economics. That is the wrong order if you want trust and implementability.

### To the family holding and investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. But you are too quick to treat a closure-led reset as automatically superior.

It is not enough to say “harder is better.” A closure package can create:
- strike risk,
- operational disruption,
- loss of know-how,
- supplier spillovers,
- reputational damage,
- and long-term damage to the company’s regional legitimacy.

Those are real costs. A financially disciplined view must price them in.

### To the works council and union
I understand and support your resistance to plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That resistance is legitimate.

But I also need to be clear: saying “no closures” is not enough unless you can help produce a package with **hard numbers**. The state will stand against unjustified destruction, but we will not ignore arithmetic.

So the strongest position is not only rejection; it is a credible alternative. That means:
- quantified non-closure savings,
- clear labour contribution,
- management restraint,
- owner support,
- and enforceable investment commitments.

### To the expert
Your assessment is helpful and balanced. I want to sharpen one point: the comparison must include **conflict costs and execution failure risk**.

A closure package on paper is not the same thing as a closure package in reality. If it triggers prolonged industrial action and weak implementation, the “faster” route may not be faster at all.

That is why I support a **quantified comparison** of the three packages.

## What would move the state

The state can support a restructuring package if it contains all of the following:

- clear quantified savings,
- meaningful management and overhead cuts,
- visible owner and capital contribution,
- a **material but bounded** labour contribution,
- capacity reduction **without plant closures as the first instrument**,
- binding investment commitments for German sites,
- a credible product and platform roadmap,
- and full involvement of co-determination in implementation.

That is a package I can defend politically and economically.

## What I cannot support

I cannot support:
- a plan centered on German plant closures,
- mass job losses,
- forced redundancies as the first resort,
- or a restructuring that weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor.

And I will be explicit: I am not prepared to endorse a “site-preserving” label if, in practice, it means hollowing out the region through attrition or shifting production away from our state.

## Bottom line

The state will support a **site-preserving restructuring** that is:
- economically credible,
- socially negotiated,
- and consistent with the company’s long-term role as a regional economic anchor.

We will **not** support a plan whose logic is:
1. closures first,
2. layoffs next,
3. and only then asking whether the company can survive the damage.

If management believes closures are unavoidable, then prove it **after** the non-closure alternatives have been fully quantified and tested — not before.

Until then, the burden of proof remains on those proposing irreversible damage.
