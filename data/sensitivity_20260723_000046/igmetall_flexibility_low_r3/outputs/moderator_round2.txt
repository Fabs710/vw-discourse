Below is a procedural readout, not a substantive position.

## Core impasse
The plateau is centered on **one unresolved trade-off**:

- **Works Council / Union / State Government:** insist on a **site-preserving restructuring** with **no compulsory redundancies** and strong protection against plant closures.
- **Management / Family Holding / Investors:** insist on **credible structural savings**, and do **not** accept an advance ban on closures or deeper footprint reductions.
- **Expert:** indicates that a **no-closure path is possible in some cases**, but usually with **slower implementation, higher execution risk, and potentially lower total savings** than a hard footprint reset.

So the real impasse is not “whether change is needed,” but:

> **Can the required savings be achieved with a closure-free package that is both large enough and fast enough, while still preserving job and site security?**

That remains unanswered.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Time-bound no-closure test phase
A **conditional site-preserving package** could be negotiated for a fixed period, with:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures during the period,
- voluntary workforce adjustment only,
- management and owner contributions up front,
- quantified savings milestones,
- and a hard review point after a defined interval.

**Purpose:**  
This may bridge the workforce side’s demand for immediate protection and the ownership side’s demand for proof. If milestones are missed, the parties would revisit structural options.

**Why it may help:**  
It converts the closure dispute into a **performance-based test** rather than an all-or-nothing commitment.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with site-preserving default, closure fallback
A second option is a **hybrid package**:
- priority use of redeployment, working-time moderation, voluntary exits, overhead cuts, and product rationalization,
- **site-preserving capacity reductions first**,
- and a **pre-agreed fallback mechanism** if quantified savings are not reached.

That fallback could be limited to:
- specific underutilized functions,
- partial line transfers,
- or narrowly defined site adjustments,
rather than immediate broad closures.

**Purpose:**  
This may bridge management/investor insistence on structural credibility with the unions’ resistance to blanket closure logic.

**Why it may help:**  
It avoids a hard commitment to “no closures ever” while still making closures a **last-resort instrument**, not the starting point.

---

### Proposal 3: Exchange package linking labour contribution to binding security
A third compromise frame would be:
- bounded labour concessions,
- explicit management and owner contributions,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a legally/politically anchored security package for German sites.

A possible sequence:
1. labour contribution is capped in time and scope,
2. management and ownership contribute visibly and immediately,
3. sites receive binding investment and future-product commitments,
4. workforce adjustment occurs only through voluntary measures,
5. disputed structural questions are resolved by joint review against agreed metrics.

**Purpose:**  
This may address the union’s and works council’s concern that labour should not pay first and trust later.

**Why it may help:**  
It creates a clearer exchange: **concessions for enforceable security**, rather than concessions for assurances.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific quantified savings and timeline would make a closure-free package acceptable enough to suspend your red line on closures for the duration of negotiations?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What capped labour contribution, expressed in measurable terms, would you be prepared to recommend to members if it came with binding site security and owner contribution?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum savings target that you believe cannot be achieved without closures, and what exact non-closure alternatives have you already ruled out on operational grounds?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete instruments are you prepared to use to support a site-preserving solution, and under what measurable conditions would you stop short of opposing a closure-inclusive plan?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What level of verified non-closure savings would be sufficient for you to support a settlement that excludes closures for a defined period?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What evidence would convince you that a longer, closure-free implementation path still delivers acceptable risk-adjusted value rather than merely postponing restructuring?**

### To the Expert
**What minimum conditions must be met for a no-closure package to be considered structurally equivalent enough to closures for this company’s situation?**

---

## Facilitator next-step suggestion
A productive next round would likely require each stakeholder to answer in one of two formats:

1. **Non-negotiables**, and  
2. **Quantified concessions or thresholds** they can accept.

That would reveal whether there is a genuine bridge, or whether the current positions remain structurally incompatible.
