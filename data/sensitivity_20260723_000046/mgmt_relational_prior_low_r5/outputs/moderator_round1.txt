### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.**
- There is broad support for a **credible restructuring/industrial plan through 2030**.
- All sides accept the need for some mix of:
  - productivity improvements,
  - qualification/redeployment,
  - voluntary instruments,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - and some form of **management/owner contribution**.
- There is also broad recognition that any solution should aim to be **durable**, not just a short-term fix.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and regional state government reject them.
  - Management, family holding, and institutional investors keep them **on the table** if economics require it.

- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Works Council and union reject them categorically.
  - Management, family holding, and investors do not accept an absolute exclusion.

- **Scope of protection**
  - Employee side wants binding site guarantees and job security through 2030.
  - Capital-side actors argue that any binding guarantee must still allow structural adjustment if the business case requires it.

- **Meaning of “fair contribution”**
  - Employee side expects shared sacrifice including management/owners.
  - Capital-side actors agree on contribution in principle but say it must be **material enough** to restore competitiveness.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much capacity reduction is needed**, and through which instruments.
- **Whether savings can be achieved without closures**, and if so whether they are large and durable enough.
- **What specific employee concessions** would be considered acceptable, and what they would be matched with from management/owners.
- **What binding protections** can be offered to workers and regions without preventing restructuring.
- **How to balance social peace and investor confidence** if the measures trigger industrial conflict or governance deadlock.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principle statements to **quantified options**:
  - required savings,
  - number of sites/sites affected,
  - job impacts,
  - timeline,
  - and each side’s concrete contribution.
- Test **alternative packages** side by side:
  - no-closure package,
  - limited-closure package,
  - and a mixed package with stronger employee/management concessions.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - what it can **definitely accept**,
  - what it can **conditionally accept**,
  - and what is **non-negotiable**.
- Keep the discussion focused on a **single negotiable package** that can be judged against the same economic target.
