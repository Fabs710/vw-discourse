I hear the line you are drawing, but I need to be equally clear: **a cosmetic solution that preserves every line on paper while leaving our German cost base structurally uncompetitive is not an option**.

We are facing a hard reality:
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our factory costs in Germany are too high,
- and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we do not fix this now, we will be forced into a much worse outcome later — weaker investment capacity, lower margins, and ultimately a smaller industrial footprint anyway.

So let me respond directly:

### Where I agree
I agree that we need:
- a credible industrial plan through 2030,
- qualification and redeployment where possible,
- voluntary instruments before blunt forced measures where they can genuinely work,
- and investment commitments that secure the future product pipeline.

I also agree that management cannot ask the workforce to carry everything alone. A durable solution has to be negotiated.

### Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the premise that:
- all German plants must be guaranteed open regardless of economics,
- compulsory redundancies are automatically off the table,
- or that existing protections can remain untouched if the cost structure stays uncompetitive.

That would not be restructuring. It would be delay.

And I am not prepared to pretend that capacity can remain unchanged when the market no longer supports it. If we preserve overcapacity, we simply preserve the problem.

### On closures
Let me be frank: **plant closures are on the table** because the situation is serious enough. I am not saying closures are the preferred route, but I am saying they are a real option if we cannot achieve equivalent structural savings another way.

What I need from the employee side is not a refusal in principle, but a **credible alternative package** that delivers the same structural effect:
- durable capacity reduction,
- materially lower labour costs,
- and a lasting improvement in returns.

If you can deliver that, I will consider alternatives seriously. If you cannot, then you are asking management to accept uncompetitive economics indefinitely — and I will not do that.

### On labour contribution
A negotiated employee contribution is necessary. That can include:
- wage moderation,
- temporary or structural concessions,
- working-time adjustments,
- reduced bonus burden,
- and other cost measures that actually move the P&L.

But it has to be **material**. Symbolic sacrifices will not close the gap.

### On management and owners
You are right that all sides must contribute, including management. We will take that seriously. But let’s be precise: management and owners cannot substitute for a sustainable operating model. Cost discipline at the top is necessary, not sufficient.

### Bottom line
I am prepared to negotiate a serious settlement, but the settlement must meet three conditions:
1. **Durable cost reduction**
2. **Structural capacity adjustment**
3. **A real labour-cost contribution**

If your position remains “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no breaking of protections” regardless of the economics, then you are not offering a solution to the crisis — you are defending the status quo that created it.

I am ready to discuss a package. But it must restore competitiveness, not merely postpone the reckoning.
