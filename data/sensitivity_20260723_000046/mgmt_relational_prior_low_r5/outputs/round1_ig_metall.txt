I agree with the Works Council on the core line: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. Those are not acceptable ways to solve management’s cost problem, and we will not support a restructuring that makes employees carry all the pain.

At the same time, we are not coming here to refuse every change. The workforce has already shown willingness to contribute if the package is fair and if there is real security in return. That means we are prepared to discuss:

- a negotiated labour-cost contribution,
- temporary or reversible capacity reductions,
- working-time solutions,
- waiver or reduction of bonuses,
- voluntary instruments and internal redeployment,
- qualification and training for the transition,
- and a binding industrial plan with investment commitments.

What we will not accept is management presenting layoffs and closures as the only serious option while shareholders, owners, and top management remain untouched. If the company needs savings, then **management must also contribute**, and the owners must accept that a sustainable solution is not built on destroying the workforce’s future.

So my position is clear:

1. **No closures.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**
4. **Any contribution from employees must be matched by contributions from management and owners.**
5. **Any restructuring must protect jobs, sites, and future production in Germany.**

If management wants peace, it needs to come back with a real shared-sacrifice package, not a one-sided austerity plan. Until then, the union will continue to mobilise — including warning strikes if necessary.
