Management, the family holding, and the capital markets are trying to recast this as a simple economics problem. It is not. It is a question of whether the company is prepared to solve its structural issues **without breaking the social contract that made this enterprise strong in the first place**.

Let me be equally direct in round 2:

### What I agree with
I agree that doing nothing is not an option.  
I agree that the company needs a credible industrial plan through 2030.  
I agree that productivity, qualification, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and management contribution all belong on the table.  
I also agree that any real solution must be durable.

### What I do not accept
I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as a starting point or as a bargaining chip.  
I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies.  
I do **not** accept that the burden of adjustment is pushed onto workers while owners and management present themselves as neutral referees.

If the company can only “restore competitiveness” by destroying German sites and forcing people out, then the model is failing in a way that leadership has to answer for — not the workforce.

### To management
You keep saying the numbers require closures. Then show us the numbers transparently, site by site, and show us why alternatives have allegedly been exhausted. Right now, what we see is a management attempt to create a fait accompli and force the workforce to absorb the strategic mistakes.

If you want a serious negotiation, bring:
- a quantified savings target,
- a full breakdown of cost drivers,
- a binding investment and product plan for each German site,
- and a concrete no-forced-layoff path.

Until then, “closures are on the table” is not a solution. It is pressure.

### To the family holding
You talk about value discipline. Fine. Then understand this: **value is also destroyed when you break trust, trigger mass industrial conflict, and hollow out the industrial base in Germany**. A company that wins a temporary cost cut by detonating its labor relations has not solved its problem — it has delayed the next one.

If you want to protect long-term enterprise value, then you should support:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- management sacrifice,
- and a negotiated contribution framework that avoids plant closures.

### To the institutional investors
You are focused on the market story and returns. We are focused on the workers, the sites, and the company’s long-term industrial reality. Those are not in conflict unless you force them to be.

You say “equally effective” savings are needed. Then let’s be precise: show a package that achieves the target **without closures and without forced layoffs**. If you cannot demonstrate that closures are economically unavoidable — not merely convenient — then your argument is not a justification, it is a preference for shifting pain onto the workforce.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your support for German sites, but I want to be clear: we do not need polite concern, we need **veto-backed action**. If management comes with closures or compulsory redundancies, then the state shareholder and co-determination stakeholders must use every lever available to stop it.

### On the actual negotiation line
Our position remains unchanged and non-negotiable on the essentials:

1. **Every German plant stays open.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Binding job guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework.**
5. **A negotiated transformation package instead of unilateral cuts.**

What we are prepared to discuss:
- temporary and reversible capacity adjustments,
- voluntary exit instruments only,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- fair wage moderation if and only if it is matched by management and owner contribution,
- and site-specific industrial investment commitments.

What we will not discuss as acceptable solutions:
- plant closures,
- mass dismissals,
- breaking existing protections,
- or using “competitiveness” as the excuse to dismantle the workforce.

### Final point
If management insists on closure as the price of restructuring, then let me be clear: you are not asking for negotiation, you are asking for surrender. That will not happen.

We are ready to shape a real alternative. But it must be one that protects jobs, protects sites, and protects the future of manufacturing in Germany.
