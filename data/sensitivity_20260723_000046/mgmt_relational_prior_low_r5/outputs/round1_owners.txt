I will be direct.

We agree on one point only: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a credible path back to competitiveness, and it needs it fast enough to stop further value erosion. That is not in dispute.

Where I disagree is with the emerging attempt to make **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful structural change”** the starting and ending point. That is not a strategy; it is a veto on reality.

### To the Works Council
I respect the insistence on preserving jobs and social stability. But a blanket rejection of plant closures and compulsory redundancies, irrespective of economics, is not credible as a governing position. If the German footprint remains structurally too large for the market, then some form of capacity adjustment is unavoidable. You can debate the instruments, but you cannot wish away the underlying mismatch.

What I can support:
- voluntary instruments first,
- redeployment and qualification where realistic,
- working-time flexibility,
- and a negotiated package that avoids unnecessary social damage.

What I cannot support:
- a permanent guarantee that every German site stays open regardless of performance,
- or protections that lock in uncompetitive cost structures and leave the family holding to absorb the consequences.

### To the Union
You are right that the burden cannot fall only on employees. Management must contribute, and top-layer costs should be part of the discussion. But I will not accept the premise that management and owner-side restraint can substitute for operational restructuring. Governance discipline is necessary; it is not sufficient.

A material labour contribution will be needed if the company is to recover margins. That does not mean humiliation or one-sided austerity. It does mean concessions that actually change the economics.

### To the Regional State Government
I appreciate the concern for the industrial base and regional employment. But the state’s role is not to freeze the company’s structure in place. If we protect every location regardless of competitiveness, we end up preserving decline and weakening the very base we want to defend.

I agree that any solution must preserve long-term industrial value in Germany. I do **not** agree that this automatically means no closures under any circumstances. If a site is structurally non-viable, refusing to address that only stores up a bigger crisis.

### Management
I support the direction of a serious restructuring, but it must be executed with discipline and credibility. “Cuts” for their own sake would be irresponsible. We need:
- a clear business case for each structural measure,
- a defensible plan for future products and investment,
- and a negotiated process that minimises reputational damage and industrial conflict.

### My position
The family holding will support a solution that:
1. restores competitiveness and margins,
2. reduces structural overcapacity,
3. secures the long-term value of the enterprise,
4. and does so through the governance process, not through public brinkmanship.

We will **not** support an outcome that permanently preserves an uncompetitive structure just to avoid hard decisions. That would destroy value, weaken the company’s strategic position, and ultimately harm everyone, including the workforce and the region.

So the real question is not whether change happens. It is whether the parties are prepared to negotiate a **credible structural package** that includes capacity reduction, cost improvement, and a fair social settlement. If not, then conflict will only make the necessary adjustment more painful later.
