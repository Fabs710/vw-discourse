Core impasse:

1. **Instrument conflict**
   - Management, investors, and the controlling family see **plant closures / footprint reduction** as the most credible way to remove permanent fixed cost.
   - Works council and union reject closures as a starting point and categorically reject **compulsory redundancies**.

2. **Evidence conflict**
   - Employee side says the company has not proven that non-destructive alternatives fail.
   - Management and capital side say no-closure proposals have not yet been shown to deliver **equivalent structural savings**.

3. **Definition conflict**
   - The sides disagree on what counts as “equally effective”:
     - labor side includes industrial continuity, supplier effects, conflict cost, legitimacy, and site viability;
     - capital/management side prioritizes permanent margin recovery and fixed-cost reduction.

4. **Governance conflict**
   - The state shareholder wants restructuring, but not at the cost of German site closures or loss of co-determination credibility.
   - The holding and investors want decisive restructuring, but not value-destroying conflict.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “No forced redundancies, but site-by-site mandatory restructuring”
A package could be structured around:
- no compulsory redundancies for an agreed period,
- voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and working-time adjustments as first-line tools,
- plus site-specific cost and capacity targets that each plant must meet.

If a site misses the target, the parties would pre-agree a second-stage review with independent verification before any closure step is considered.

**Bridge value:** preserves the employee red line on forced layoffs while giving management a pathway to achieve measurable structural correction.

---

### Proposal 2: “Mixed package with one constrained footprint action plus broader protection elsewhere”
A compromise could combine:
- one limited closure or line consolidation only where the site-by-site audit shows no credible future product or competitiveness case,
- with binding guarantees that the remaining German sites stay open through 2030,
- and with management/owner contributions, investment commitments, and voluntary reductions across the rest of the network.

**Bridge value:** gives the economic side a real capacity reduction, while limiting the number of sites affected and preserving most employment and legitimacy.

---

### Proposal 3: “Conditional no-closure package with automatic fallback”
The parties could test a no-closure option first, but only under strict conditions:
- quantified savings target,
- time-limited implementation window,
- mandatory management and owner contribution,
- explicit redeployment and productivity milestones,
- and an independent audit after a defined period.

If the package misses the milestones, a pre-negotiated fallback mechanism activates: additional footprint reduction or site consolidation becomes subject to a fast-track decision process.

**Bridge value:** allows the no-closure route to be tried seriously without making it open-ended.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
What exact combination of voluntary exits, overhead cuts, product transfers, working-time measures, and owner contribution would you accept as a **fully quantified no-closure alternative**, and what savings gap would still force you to reopen closure discussions?

### To the Works Council
What specific burden-sharing package would make you accept a **time-limited, verified restructuring** if it achieved the required savings without compulsory redundancies, even if it included one site consolidation?

### To the Industrial Union
What minimum guarantees on wages, jobs, and redeployment would be sufficient for you to negotiate a **temporary labor contribution** tied to measurable management and owner concessions?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you commit to supporting a compromise only if it includes **binding site guarantees, no forced layoffs, and independent verification**, or are there additional governance conditions you require to use your blocking power?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Would you accept a package that preserves most German sites but includes **one limited footprint reduction** if an independent audit shows it is the least value-destructive way to close the cost gap?

### To the Institutional Investors
Would you support a restructuring that delivers **slightly slower margin recovery** if it materially reduces strike risk, protects operational continuity, and avoids broader social disruption?

### To the Expert
Can you define a threshold at which a no-closure package stops being industrially credible—specifically, what level of permanent cost reduction, capacity reduction, and headcount change would you regard as the minimum for structural viability?
