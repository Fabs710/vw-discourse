Let me be equally clear from the regional state shareholder perspective:

We recognise the economic pressure. We do not deny that the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness. But **a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany and deep job destruction is not a solution we can support**. That would damage not only workers, but the company’s long-term industrial base and the region’s economy.

### Where we agree
I agree with management on one point: doing nothing is not an option.  
I also agree with the union and works council that a purely one-sided austerity plan is unacceptable.

We can support:
- productivity improvements,
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- qualification and redeployment,
- investment commitments in new products and technologies,
- and a negotiated employee contribution, **if it is fair and shared**.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that closures are the natural or necessary first response.

Closing German plants may look like the easiest accounting solution, but it is a blunt instrument with lasting damage:
- it destroys regional employment,
- weakens supplier networks,
- undermines social partnership,
- and risks hollowing out the company’s industrial credibility in Germany.

And I want to be explicit: **we will not accept an outcome that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role, or one that gut’s regional employment to solve a short-term cost problem**. That is not a serious long-term strategy.

### To management
If you need structural savings, then bring a package that is actually negotiable and socially sustainable:
- no forced redundancies,
- no plant closure as the default path,
- a measurable contribution from management overhead and executive structures,
- a fair employee contribution,
- and binding commitments on investment and future production in Germany.

If you want the region and the workforce to carry sacrifices, then the company must also commit to keeping industrial value here.

### To the works council and union
I support your core rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. But I will also say this: if the company can be saved with a serious package that preserves sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, then we should be prepared to negotiate responsibly. That means not just saying “no,” but shaping an alternative that is durable and credible.

### Our position
The state shareholder is prepared to back a restructuring **only if** it:
1. preserves German sites,
2. avoids mass layoffs and compulsory redundancies,
3. protects long-term industrial capacity in the region,
4. and distributes the burden fairly across labor, management, and owners.

If management insists that closures are unavoidable, then we are not moving toward agreement — we are moving toward confrontation, and we have the leverage to make that path very expensive.
