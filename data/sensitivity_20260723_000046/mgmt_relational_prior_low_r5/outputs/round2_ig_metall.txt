I will respond clearly to each side.

### To management
You are right about one thing: **doing nothing is not an option**.  
But you are still using “competitiveness” as a blanket justification for shifting the whole burden onto the workforce. That is not acceptable.

What I **agree** with:
- the company needs a durable industrial plan through 2030,
- there must be real savings,
- voluntary instruments, qualification, redeployment, and working-time flexibility belong on the table,
- management and owners must contribute.

What I **do not accept**:
- plant closures as the default answer,
- compulsory redundancies,
- a restructuring that destroys collective protections,
- or a situation where employees pay for everything while capital and top management stay largely untouched.

If you want a serious negotiation, bring **transparent numbers**:
- site-by-site cost drivers,
- quantified savings targets,
- and a comparison of alternatives that avoid closures and forced layoffs.

Until then, “closures are on the table” is not a solution. It is leverage.

### To the Works Council
I support your core line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That remains the correct boundary.  
I also support binding site and job security through 2030, because workers cannot be asked to accept open-ended insecurity in the name of transformation.

Where I would sharpen the position is this: if the company needs a contribution, then it must be a **shared-sacrifice package**:
- employee contribution,
- management contribution,
- owner contribution,
- and binding investment commitments.

That is the only kind of settlement we can take to our members with credibility.

### To the Regional State Government
Your position on preserving German industrial capacity is important, and your blocking role matters here.  
I agree that closures would do lasting damage to the region, suppliers, and trust in social partnership.

But support for the workforce must not remain only political language. If management brings closures or forced layoffs, then the state shareholder must be prepared to use its leverage decisively.

### To the controlling family holding
You speak about value discipline. Fine. Then understand this: **value is also destroyed by breaking labor relations, provoking strikes, and hollowing out the industrial base**.  
A hard cut that detonates social peace may look efficient on a spreadsheet and disastrous in reality.

I agree that the company needs structural savings. I do **not** agree that the only credible path is plant closures and job destruction. A fair settlement must prove that competitiveness can be restored **without making employees the sole adjustment variable**.

### To institutional investors
You keep saying the package must be “equally effective.” That is the right test in principle. But it cannot become a code word for “we prefer layoffs because they are cleaner financially.”

If you want support, then accept this:
- savings must be real,
- but they must come from **a balanced package**, not from one-sided labor cuts.

And let me be explicit: if your benchmark is a settlement that preserves returns by abandoning job security and collective standards, then we will resist that with industrial means.

### Our position in round 2
We remain open to a negotiated solution, but only on these terms:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
4. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
5. **A fair employee contribution only if matched by management and owner contribution**
6. **A credible investment and product plan securing future production in Germany**

### What we can discuss
- temporary wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit instruments only,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- capacity reductions that do not mean closures,
- and management-side savings.

### What we will not discuss as acceptable
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- dismantling the wage agreement,
- or “competitiveness” as a pretext for social dumping.

If management refuses to move away from closures and compulsory redundancies, then this stops being a negotiation over restructuring and becomes a confrontation over who pays for the crisis. In that case, the union will stand with the workforce.
