### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.**
- The company needs a **durable industrial/restructuring plan through 2030**.
- There is broad agreement that the solution must include some combination of:
  - productivity improvements,
  - qualification/redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary instruments,
  - management and overhead contribution,
  - and binding investment/product planning.
- There is also shared acceptance that any package must be **real, quantified, and durable**, not cosmetic.
- All parties now accept that **some structural correction is needed**; the dispute is about the form it should take.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state shareholder reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, family holding, and investors see closures or equivalent capacity reduction as potentially necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee-side and state-side actors reject them.
  - Management, family holding, and investors do not accept an absolute ban if economics require adjustment.
- **How to achieve savings**
  - Employee-side actors want a **no-closure, no-forced-layoff package**.
  - Capital and management sides demand **evidence that such a package can match the economics** of closure-based restructuring.
- **Role of governance/public stakeholders**
  - The state shareholder and co-determination actors want to use leverage to protect sites/jobs.
  - Management, family holding, and investors argue governance should not freeze an uncompetitive structure.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Quantified comparison of alternatives**
  - No side has yet produced a full side-by-side model showing:
    - no-closure package vs. closure/deeper-cut package,
    - exact savings,
    - capacity reduction,
    - job impact,
    - timing,
    - and margin effect.
- **How much structural change is enough**
  - The parties agree cost cuts are needed, but not on the minimum level of **permanent capacity/fixed-cost reduction** required.
- **Burden-sharing balance**
  - It remains unresolved how contributions will be split among:
    - employees,
    - management,
    - owners,
    - and potentially the state/governance side.
- **Durability vs. social stability**
  - The central tension remains whether a socially preferred package can also be economically durable.
- **Treatment of site guarantees**
  - Employee-side demands for binding site guarantees conflict with the need for flexibility in restructuring.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **one common factual basis**:
  1. site-by-site cost and capacity data,
  2. quantified savings target,
  3. at least two fully costed restructuring scenarios,
  4. explicit burden-sharing assumptions,
  5. and clear criteria for what counts as “equally effective.”
- Ask each side to respond to the same question:  
  **What package restores competitiveness through 2030 with the least total value destruction?**
- Keep the next round focused on **numbers, timing, and enforceability**, not repeat positions on red lines.
