We agree on the diagnosis that **doing nothing is not an option**. That is the only point in this exchange that is already investable.

But I need to be equally clear: **I will not support a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base, protects every legacy structure, and calls that “responsible restructuring.”** If the package does not credibly restore margins, capital will keep pricing the company as a structurally weaker business.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that:
- German costs are too high,
- capacity is above what current demand can sustain,
- and the brand needs a durable return to target margins.

I also agree that:
- voluntary instruments should be tried first where they are real,
- management and overhead costs must be part of the burden-sharing,
- and any package has to be durable, not cosmetic.

### With the family holding
I agree that governance should not protect decline. A settlement that simply freezes the structure and postpones adjustment would destroy long-term enterprise value. I also agree that any alternative to closures must be **quantified, enforceable, and large enough** to matter.

### With the state shareholder
I agree that a purely one-sided austerity plan would be politically and industrially unstable. A value-preserving restructuring should not blow up social peace for no savings.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and union
Your position is too absolute.

I understand the political and social reasons for rejecting closures and compulsory redundancies. But from a shareholder standpoint, **blanket “no closures, no forced redundancies” lines are only acceptable if you can produce a package that achieves the same economics**.

So far, I have not seen that. I have seen:
- strong objections,
- demands for guarantees,
- and a preference for voluntary and flexible measures.

That is not enough if the company still ends up with:
- structurally high fixed costs,
- overcapacity,
- and margins below the cost of capital.

A no-closure package is only credible if it delivers:
1. measurable capacity reduction,
2. durable labour-cost reduction,
3. and a clear path back to competitive returns.

If it cannot do that, then it is not a solution; it is delay.

### With the state government
I respect the state’s co-determination role and its concern for the region. But I do **not** support using that leverage to freeze the industrial footprint regardless of economics.

Protecting every site may feel responsible in the near term, but if it leaves the business structurally weaker, it eventually harms:
- the company,
- the workforce,
- the region,
- and shareholders.

The state should be pushing for a durable industrial plan, not vetoing structural change in principle.

## What I need to see

The discussion is still too abstract. I want a **quantified comparison** between at least two packages:

### Package A: no closures
This must show:
- exact cost savings,
- exact capacity reductions,
- labour-cost contribution,
- management/owner contribution,
- timing,
- and how margins return to target.

### Package B: limited structural closures or deeper capacity cuts
This must show:
- which sites are affected,
- what savings are achieved,
- what the job impact is,
- and how much faster and more durable the margin recovery is.

If Package A really gets to the same economic result, I could support it.  
If it does not, then insisting on it becomes financially irresponsible.

## My current red lines

I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the cost base uncompetitive,
- a restructuring that relies on optimism instead of numbers,
- or prolonged conflict that destroys value through delay.

I am willing to support:
- a serious negotiated package,
- material employee contributions,
- management overhead cuts,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and, if proven necessary, targeted structural capacity reduction.

## Final position
I am not taking sides in the social conflict. I am taking the side of **risk-adjusted value creation**.

So my message to all parties is simple:
- Bring me a package that **quantifiably** restores competitiveness.
- If you can do that without closures, I will listen.
- If you cannot, then you should stop pretending that preserving everything is compatible with shareholder value.
