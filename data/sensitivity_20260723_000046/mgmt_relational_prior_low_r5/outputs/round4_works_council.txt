## First: what I agree with

I agree with the expert and the moderator on one point: **doing nothing is not an option**.  
I also agree that the company needs a **durable plan through 2030**, not a short-term patch.

And yes, I accept that any real package must include:
- productivity improvements,
- qualification and redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary instruments,
- management and overhead contribution,
- and binding investment/product commitments.

So let nobody claim the works council is refusing restructuring. We are refusing **destructive restructuring**.

## What I reject, again and without ambiguity

### 1) I reject plant closures as the default answer
Management, investors, and the family holding keep trying to normalize closures as “realism.”  
No. Closures are not realism. They are the **most damaging instrument** available.

A closure does not just remove cost. It destroys:
- jobs,
- skills,
- supplier networks,
- regional trust,
- production continuity,
- and the basis for future cooperation.

If the company chooses closures, it is not proving strength. It is proving that it failed to solve the problem industrially.

### 2) I reject compulsory redundancies categorically
This is not moving.  
It will not move.

No operational layoffs. No forced redundancies. Not as a “last resort,” not as a “fallback,” not as an “efficiency measure.”  
That line is fixed.

### 3) I reject the attempt to define “equally effective” in narrow financial terms
Capital keeps saying: match the savings, match the margin, match the fixed-cost reduction.  
That is not the whole test.

A valid comparison must also count:
- conflict cost,
- strike risk,
- supplier damage,
- implementation risk,
- long-term legitimacy,
- and the destruction of co-determination credibility.

If you exclude those, you are not comparing total value. You are only comparing what is convenient for balance sheets.

What I want from you is simple:
- a genuine no-closure scenario,

Structural adjustment can also mean:
- removing shifts,
- reallocating volume,
- reducing overhead,
- changing working time,
- using voluntary departures,

That is structural too.  
That is durable too.

So if your conclusion is that a no-closure package is “execution-sensitive,” then fine — that is an argument for better planning, not for closure-first thinking.
