As moderator, the core impasse is now clear:

## Core impasse
The parties are not primarily disagreeing on **whether restructuring is needed**. Most accept that it is.

They are blocked on **what counts as a credible equivalent alternative** to plant closures and forced redundancies.

- **Management, the family holding, and investors** are saying: a no-closure route is only acceptable if it delivers permanent, quantified, structural savings and capacity reduction comparable to closure-based restructuring.
- **Works council, union, and regional government** are saying: closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points, and the comparison must include conflict, legitimacy, supplier, and implementation costs.

So the deadlock is between:
1. **economic equivalence** as defined by capital/management, and
2. **social/industrial legitimacy** as defined by labor and the state.

The expert input sharpened the issue: a non-closure package is only credible if it permanently removes enough fixed cost and capacity to restore viability through 2030.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) A “no-closure first” package with pre-agreed trigger review
**Proposal:**  
Parties negotiate a package that assumes no closures initially, with voluntary exits, redeployment, shift/line consolidation, overhead cuts, management contribution, and investment commitments. A jointly agreed review point is set, based on audited milestones.

**Bridge logic:**  
- Gives labor and state a real no-closure path to test.
- Gives management and capital a hard review if the package fails to deliver quantified milestones.
- Avoids immediate closure decisions while not ruling them out forever.

**Possible safeguard:**  
If agreed savings, utilization, or volume-transfer targets are missed by a defined date, the parties re-open site-level options under a structured process.

---

### 2) A mixed package with “site protection in exchange for stronger structural measures elsewhere”
**Proposal:**  
Keep more sites open, but pair that with deeper permanent cuts in non-site areas:
- stronger management/overhead reduction,
- product concentration,
- shift elimination,
- internal volume transfer,
- supplier renegotiation,
- and larger voluntary exit volumes.

**Bridge logic:**  
- Gives the works council and state a path away from immediate closures.
- Gives capital a package with more than temporary concessions.
- Makes the burden-sharing visibly broader than labor alone.

**Possible safeguard:**  
Savings and capacity correction are audited plant by plant, and the package must still meet a minimum 2030 margin target.

---

### 3) A conditional “site-by-site protection plus fallback options” arrangement
**Proposal:**  
Parties identify which sites are protected under a guaranteed transformation plan, and which sites enter a structured fallback review if they cannot meet defined productivity, product, or utilization thresholds.

**Bridge logic:**  
- Reduces the fear of blanket closures.
- Preserves management’s insistence that not every site is automatically protected.
- Creates a disciplined process rather than a political stalemate.

**Possible safeguard:**  
Fallback review does not mean automatic closure; it means a jointly governed assessment of all alternatives before any final decision.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**Question:**  
What minimum, quantified permanent savings and capacity reduction would you accept from a no-closure package in order to treat it as a serious alternative to closures?

### To the Industrial Union
**Question:**  
Which specific combination of voluntary exits, working-time changes, productivity measures, and wage/bonus contributions would the union be prepared to table as a binding package through 2030?

### To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Can you present a plant-by-plant model showing the exact gap between the no-closure package and the closure-based scenario, including conflict and implementation risk, so the comparison can be judged on the full economics?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
If a no-closure package falls short of the target economics by a modest margin, are you prepared to support a conditional fallback mechanism, or is your position an absolute no-closure line regardless of quantified results?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Would you accept a negotiated plan that preserves more German sites if it demonstrably restores long-term value, even if it requires accepting a slower or more complex adjustment path than closures?

### To the Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What level of permanent savings and capacity reduction would you regard as sufficient to support a non-closure package, even if the path is more labor-intensive and politically complex?
