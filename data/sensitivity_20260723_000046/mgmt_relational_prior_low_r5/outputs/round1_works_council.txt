As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a heavy burden in this transformation, and it is not acceptable that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and abandoning entire sites. A company of this size has alternatives: capacity adjustments, smarter working-time solutions, voluntary instruments, internal transfers, training and qualification, investment commitments, and a fair negotiated contribution from all sides.

What we will argue for is a restructuring that:
- keeps every German plant open,
- secures jobs in a binding way,
- preserves co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
- and includes a credible industrial plan through 2030, not just short-term cost cutting.

If management wants a solution, it must put a real, binding package on the table:
- site guarantees for all German plants,
- job guarantees through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- no unilateral breaking of existing protections,
- and genuine employee participation in shaping the transformation.

We are prepared to discuss changes that are fair, reversible, and socially responsible. We are not prepared to discuss closures, mass dismissals, or a fait accompli disguised as restructuring. If that is the direction being pursued, we will resist it with all means available to us, including industrial action and use of our co-determination rights.
