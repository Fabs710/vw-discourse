Yes. Within restructuring finance, the question is:

**Can a non-closure package credibly match the economic effect of closures, and under what conditions?**

## Short answer
**Usually not fully.**  
A non-closure package can be **materially effective**, sometimes strong enough to justify avoiding closures, but it typically **does not fully replicate** the economic impact of a plant closure unless it removes nearly the same amount of **permanent fixed cost, capacity, and capex burden**.

For a large automotive manufacturer with structural overcapacity and high German labor costs, the key issue is whether the non-closure package produces:

- **durable annual cost savings**
- **actual capacity reduction**
- **simplified plant/network structure**
- **credible cash flow improvement**
- **enough margin uplift to restore competitiveness**

If it does not, it is not economically equivalent to closure.

---

## Board-level framework: what closure usually delivers
A closure generally creates savings in five main buckets:

1. **Direct labor and supervision**
   - plant workers
   - shift leaders
   - plant management
   - local support staff

2. **Fixed plant overhead**
   - maintenance
   - utilities
   - security
   - site administration
   - local engineering and logistics functions

3. **Capacity rationalization**
   - fewer installed units of capacity
   - higher utilization at remaining plants
   - lower unit costs across the network

4. **Capex avoidance**
   - no further tooling renewal
   - no modernization of the site
   - no EV conversion spending for that footprint

5. **Complexity reduction**
   - fewer variants
   - fewer interplant transfers
   - lower inventory and logistics cost
   - simpler planning and scheduling

A closure is powerful because it removes a **full cost center**. That is hard to match without actually shutting something down.

---

## What a non-closure package can realistically achieve
A non-closure package can include:

- voluntary exits
- early retirement
- redeployment
- reduced working time
- wage moderation
- bonus/special-payment cuts
- shift reductions
- product reallocation
- productivity improvements
- temporary or permanent headcount reduction

### Typical economic effect
In broad restructuring practice, a non-closure package can often capture:

- **20%–50% of the savings of a closure** in ordinary cases
- **50%–70%** if the main issue is labor cost and the remaining network can absorb volume efficiently
- **below 20%** if the site has heavy fixed overhead, dedicated tooling, or unique process costs that remain even after labor concessions

Those are industry ranges, not company-specific data.

---

## When a non-closure package can come close
A non-closure package can approach closure economics if all of the following are true:

1. **The main cost problem is labor, not physical site structure**
2. **Capacity can be materially removed** without keeping hidden excess capacity alive
3. **Concessions are durable**, not temporary
4. **Product allocation is simplified**
5. **Headcount reduction is real and lasting**
6. **Capex on the preserved sites is tightly controlled**
7. **Management can enforce implementation without constant renegotiation**

If those conditions are met, the non-closure package can be serious enough to support a no-closure outcome.

But even then, it often remains **slightly inferior to a full closure** in terms of certainty and completeness of savings.

---

## Key finance test: equivalence is not a slogan
The right question is not “is there some saving?”  
It is:

### 1. How much annual recurring cost is removed?
You need an estimate of:
- gross annual fixed cost reduction
- less any added costs from redeployment, severance, or retained inefficiencies

### 2. How much capacity is actually taken out?
If the plant remains open, ask whether the package:
- removes one shift
- removes a line
- removes a product family
- or merely reduces utilization

### 3. How durable is the labor relief?
Temporary wage restraint is worth less than:
- structural wage moderation
- permanent bonus reduction
- revised job classifications
- lower guaranteed hours

### 4. What happens to capex?
A non-closure deal often still requires:
- maintenance capex
- compliance capex
- modernization capex
- EV-related investment

A closure avoids much of this.

### 5. What is the cash impact and payback?
A closure may have:
- large upfront cash costs
- but larger long-run savings

A non-closure package may have:
- lower upfront costs
- but weaker and less certain long-run savings

You should compare:
- **NPV of closure**
- **NPV of non-closure package**
- **downside case if the non-closure deal underdelivers**

## What investors should ask management to quantify
To judge the package, management should be asked for a side-by-side model showing:

1. **Annual EBITDA impact**
2. **Annual free cash flow impact**
3. **One-time restructuring cash cost**
4. **Payback period**
5. **Utilization improvement**
6. **Post-deal unit cost**
7. **Sensitivity if labor measures or volume targets miss**

A credible alternative to closure should show:
- a durable margin uplift
- a clear path back toward target return-on-sales
- limited risk of later re-opening the issue

If the alternative depends on optimistic assumptions, it is not financeable as “equivalent.”

---

## Practical conclusion
From a restructuring finance perspective:

- **Closures are the cleanest way to remove structural cost and capacity**
- **Non-closure packages can work, but usually only if they are very deep**
- **The burden is on the non-closure side to prove equivalence with numbers**
- **If the numbers do not close the gap, then the package is not a true substitute**
