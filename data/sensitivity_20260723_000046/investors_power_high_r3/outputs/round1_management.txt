Let me be clear: I hear the rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. I also acknowledge that the workforce has already absorbed change and that a one-sided solution will not hold.

But I need to state the management position just as clearly:

### What we agree on
- We do need a negotiated solution.
- We do need a future industrial strategy and product commitment.
- We do need shared contributions, not just employee pain.
- We do need to preserve the company’s ability to invest through 2030.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that we can protect every site and every job as a starting point while leaving the cost base untouched.

The hard reality is this:
- German factory costs are materially above target.
- The European market is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying, including from lower-cost producers.
- The brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

Under those conditions, a cosmetic package, or a deal that preserves all capacity and simply pushes adjustment into temporary measures, is not enough. That would postpone the crisis, not solve it.

### What must be on the table
If we are serious about competitiveness, then the package has to deliver:
- a **structural and durable reduction in capacity**,
- a **genuine reduction in labour costs**,
- and a **clear path to restoring margins**.

That can be through plant closures, or through other measures that achieve the same economic effect. I am open on the mechanism. I am **not** open on the outcome being weak.

### On closures and job guarantees
You say “no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies.” I understand the political and social message, but I cannot accept that as an absolute line if there is no equivalent structural saving elsewhere.

If you want to avoid closures, then you need to bring me something that is genuinely equivalent:
- larger and durable labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reduction of sufficient scale,
- productivity and flexibility gains that actually change the cost base,
- and a settlement that is credible through 2030.

Not symbolic concessions. Not temporary accounting relief. Real savings.

### On strikes and confrontation
I would prefer a negotiated settlement, but I will not pretend that prolonged conflict changes the economics. If there is no serious offer that fixes the cost base, then confrontation becomes part of the process. What is unacceptable to management is a deal that looks cooperative but leaves us uncompetitive.

### Bottom line
Management is prepared to negotiate, but not to sign up to an arrangement that preserves the status quo in substance. The company needs:
- fewer costs,
- less overcapacity,
- and a stronger foundation for investment.

If labour wants security, it has to help create the conditions for it. Security cannot be built on an uncompetitive structure.
