## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All stakeholders now accept that it is.

The impasse is the **sequencing and credibility of sacrifice**:

- **Labour-side stakeholders** insist on binding **no-closure / no-forced-redundancy guarantees first**, with co-determination preserved, and only then any employee contribution.
- **Management and capital-side stakeholders** insist that any non-closure solution must be **economically equivalent** to closures in durable cost and capacity reduction, and they want the option of closures left open if it is not.

So the deadlock is:  
**security first** vs **economic equivalence first**.

A second, related impasse is the meaning of “equivalence”:
- labour hears it as a disguised closure threat,
- management/investors hear it as the minimum test for a serious deal.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Time-bound non-closure pact with hard triggers
**Proposed structure:**  
A binding moratorium on German plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period, paired with a jointly agreed trigger mechanism.

**Elements:**
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies for a fixed period, e.g. to end-2027 or end-2028;
- joint review milestones every 6 or 12 months;
- pre-agreed economic triggers that would reopen negotiations if savings/capacity targets are missed;
- if triggers are hit, the parties must enter accelerated bargaining on additional measures before any unilateral step.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
It gives labour real near-term security while giving management and capital a structured way to test whether the alternative package truly works.

---

### 2) Sequenced package: security in exchange for measurable savings
**Proposed structure:**  
A phased agreement where labour-side contributions are activated only after management/ownership commit to defined site and job protections, but the whole package is conditioned on verified savings.

**Elements:**
- Phase 1: binding site/job protection and no forced redundancies;
- Phase 2: employee contributions such as working-time flexibility, bonus moderation, voluntary exits, and redeployment;
- Phase 3: management/owner commitments on investment, product allocation, and capacity rationalization;
- all phases monitored against a jointly audited savings corridor.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
It addresses labour’s demand for upfront security while giving management measurable cost relief and a verified implementation path.

---

### 3) Capacity reduction without shutdowns, plus a fallback review
**Proposed structure:**  
A non-closure capacity program that seeks all savings through shift patterns, product allocation, productivity, and voluntary instruments first, while reserving a later review only if quantified targets remain unmet.

**Elements:**
- reduced shifts and overtime;
- internal transfer and qualification programs;
- voluntary departure and early-retirement windows;
- site-specific product reallocation;
- joint productivity initiatives;
- a later “last resort” review only if agreed thresholds are not achieved.

**Why this may bridge parties:**  
It allows management to pursue real capacity reduction while keeping the explicit closure decision off the table unless the joint plan fails.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific, measurable cost and capacity measures would you accept as credible proof that closure is not necessary, and under what independent verification standard?**

### Industrial Union
**Which employee contributions are you prepared to put on the table immediately if a binding no-closure, no-forced-redundancy framework is agreed?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings and capacity-reduction target must a non-closure package meet before you would say closures are not needed?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete governance or political support will you provide to help make a non-closure compromise enforceable, rather than only expressing opposition to closures?**

### Institutional Investors
**Would you support a time-bound no-closure pact if it included audited savings triggers and an automatic renegotiation mechanism on missed targets?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Will you commit to backing a negotiated package that preserves sites and avoids forced redundancies if it demonstrably meets the required return and margin thresholds?**
