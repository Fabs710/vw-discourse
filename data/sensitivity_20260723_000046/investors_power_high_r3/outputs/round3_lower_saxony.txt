I will speak plainly.

## My position
The regional state government recognizes that this company needs restructuring. I do **not** deny the cost problem, the overcapacity issue, or the need for a durable solution. What I will not accept is the idea that the only “serious” route is to start with **German plant closures** and **forced redundancies**.

That is not a neutral economic choice for us. It would damage:
- regional employment,
- industrial capability in the state,
- social stability,
- and the long-term role of this company as an economic anchor.

And it would do so while undermining trust in the very governance structure that is supposed to balance interests here.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on several points:
- the current structure is too expensive,
- the status quo is not sustainable,
- a cosmetic compromise would fail,
- and any settlement must be durable through 2030.

I also agree with labour that employees cannot be asked to carry the entire burden alone. A one-sided solution would be politically and economically reckless.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not the default
Management keeps saying closures may be necessary if no equivalent savings are found. That is a legitimate test **in principle**. But I have not yet seen the kind of transparent comparison that would justify making closures the benchmark.

Before any closure is put on the table, I expect a serious, quantified comparison of:
- capacity reductions without site shutdowns,
- shift and working-time changes,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity and flexibility agreements,
- and contributions from management and owners.

If those can produce the required effect, then closures are not justified. If they cannot, then the burden is on management to show that clearly, not rhetorically.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
I reject forced layoffs. Not because job adjustment is never necessary, but because in this company and this region, forced redundancies would trigger exactly the damage we are trying to avoid:
- labour conflict,
- political backlash,
- loss of trust,
- and harm to the regional economy.

If the company needs headcount adjustment, then it must be through voluntary, negotiated, and socially responsible instruments.

### 3) “No closures, no redundancies” is not a slogan; it is a hard boundary
I want to be equally clear with the works council and the union: I understand why you are holding this line, and I support the underlying principle. But I also need a solution that is economically credible.

So yes, I support avoiding closures and compulsory redundancies — **if** the alternative package is real, measurable, and durable. A vague promise of “flexibility” or “temporary moderation” is not enough.

## What I can support
I can support a restructuring package built around:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding employment and site protection through 2030**
- **voluntary exits, redeployment, and early retirement where applicable**
- **working-time and productivity agreements**
- **temporary, negotiated labour contributions**
- **clear investment commitments for future products and technologies**
- **preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is the kind of social-partnership solution I can defend.

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- closures as the first answer,
- forced layoffs,
- a one-sided burden shift onto employees,
- or any restructuring that permanently weakens the company’s industrial base in Germany.

I also cannot support any attempt to sideline the state’s role in governance. That role exists for a reason: to protect long-term regional and public interests, not to be bypassed when decisions become politically difficult.

## To management
If you want support for a serious restructuring, bring:
- the exact cost gap,
- the savings target,
- what has been tested already,
- and why a non-closure package is or is not sufficient.

Do not ask for a blank cheque to close sites and call it responsibility.

## To the works council and union
I support your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. But I also need you to move from principle to constructively negotiated alternatives.

If you want my active backing, then show me what additional flexibility you can accept on:
- working time,
- redeployment,
- voluntary departure schemes,
- temporary wage moderation,
- and productivity measures,

without crossing the red line into closures or forced dismissals.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right that the company must become competitive. I do not dispute that.

But competitiveness is not only a matter of cutting cost. It also depends on:
- industrial stability,
- workforce commitment,
- implementation capacity,
- and legitimacy.

A settlement that looks efficient on paper but triggers prolonged industrial conflict is not a durable value solution.

## Bottom line
My position is clear:
- **yes** to restructuring,
- **yes** to shared sacrifice,
- **yes** to durable competitiveness,
- **no** to German plant closures as the default,
- **no** to compulsory redundancies,
- **no** to any outcome that guts regional employment or removes the state’s co-determination role.

If there is a credible alternative that preserves sites and jobs while restoring competitiveness, I will work for it. If not, I will resist a destructive solution.

If needed, I can next help test the concrete numbers of a non-closure package against the savings target.
