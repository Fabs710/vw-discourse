## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **what must be irrevocably excluded up front** versus **what must remain available as a fallback if the economics are not met**.

More specifically, the conflict concentrates on four linked points:

1. **Plant closures**
   - Works council, union, and regional state government want them excluded.
   - Management, investors, and the family holding want them retained as a legitimate restructuring tool.

2. **Compulsory redundancies**
   - Employee-side and state actors want them excluded.
   - Management and capital-side actors want them retained as a fallback if voluntary measures fall short.

3. **Binding security vs. economic flexibility**
   - Labour wants binding site/job security through 2030 as the precondition for negotiation.
   - Management and owners want economic reforms first or at least simultaneously, not after security is locked in.

4. **Meaning of “equivalence”**
   - Labour says closure is not socially equivalent to any cost-saving alternative.
   - Management and investors say any alternative must be economically equivalent to closure on fixed costs, capacity, and durability.

So the core impasse is: **Can a non-closure package be made deep and durable enough to replace closure-based restructuring, while still giving labour and the state a binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff assurance?**

---

## Facilitator compromise positions

### 1) Deep non-closure package with independent equivalence test
A compromise position is to create a **time-limited restructuring test phase** in which:
- closures and compulsory redundancies are **not used during the test period**,
- labour contributes through voluntary exits, working-time changes, wage moderation, redeployment, and productivity measures,
- management commits to transparent target figures for fixed-cost reduction and capacity reduction,
- an **independent advisor** verifies whether the non-closure package closes a specified percentage of the economic gap versus closure,
- a pre-agreed threshold determines whether the parties move to a second phase.

This bridges the gap by replacing “trust us” with **measurable deliverables**.

---

### 2) No-closure / no-forced-layoff pact with conditional escalation only after failure of agreed milestones
A second compromise is a **binding no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy pact** through a defined period, but only if:
- milestones are met on cost, capacity, and productivity,
- both sides accept an automatic review by an independent third party,
- any escalation option is limited to **renegotiation**, not immediate closure,
- state participation is used to reinforce compliance and mediation.

This preserves the workforce’s red lines while giving management a structured route to revisit the deal **only if the agreed plan demonstrably fails**.

---

### 3) Site-by-site differentiated settlement
A third compromise is to avoid an all-or-nothing company-wide rule and instead negotiate:
- **site-specific agreements** based on actual capacity and product allocation,
- stronger guarantees for sites that can absorb volume and modernization,
- deeper concessions where overcapacity is highest,
- a common framework of no compulsory redundancies plus voluntary instruments,
- and a shared commitment that no site is closed before alternatives at that site are exhausted and independently reviewed.

This can reduce deadlock by shifting the debate from abstract principles to **plant-level economics**.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific, quantified contribution package are you prepared to support that preserves all German sites through 2030 while still delivering a fixed annual cost-out large enough to satisfy management’s competitiveness target?**

### Industrial Union
**What minimum level of permanent labor-cost, working-time, and capacity reduction would you regard as sufficient to make a no-closure settlement defensible to your membership?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings gap remains after a deep non-closure package, and what independent evidence would you accept before concluding that closures are genuinely necessary rather than merely preferred?**

### Regional State Government
**Are you prepared to back a binding no-closure framework if independent verification shows that it achieves most, but not all, of the savings of closure, and if not, what threshold would trigger your support?**

### Institutional Investors
**What measurable operating margin, EBITDA, or return-on-sales improvement would make a non-closure package acceptable to you even if it does not fully replicate closure economics?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Will you commit to backing a deep non-closure solution if it is independently verified to close the majority of the economic gap, even where it falls short of one-for-one equivalence with closure?**

### Independent Expert / Advisors
**Can you define a precise benchmark for “economically close enough” so the parties can test the non-closure option against the closure alternative without arguing about terminology?**
