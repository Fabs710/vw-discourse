I agree with the Works Council on the red lines: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**. That is not a maximalist posture; it is the minimum basis for any serious negotiation.

Let me be equally clear where I stand as the union representing the workforce in collective bargaining:

### What we support
We are prepared to discuss a **shared-sacrifice package** if it genuinely protects jobs and sites. That can include, for example:
- temporary wage moderation or deferred increases,
- foregoing bonuses or special payments,
- working-time reductions,
- flexibility instruments,
- voluntary departures only,
- and a jointly agreed capacity adjustment.

We are not refusing every contribution from labour. We have already signaled openness to contribute if that contribution is **fairly shared** and tied to **binding security for jobs and plants**.

### What we reject
We do **not** accept a restructuring where:
- employees carry the whole burden,
- management preserves its own options while the workforce loses everything,
- closures are used as a threat to force concessions,
- or the company destroys industrial capacity and then calls that a turnaround.

That is neither fair nor sustainable. It would deepen mistrust, damage morale, and make any agreement unstable.

### On the management position
If management’s claim is simply “costs are too high, so we need plant closures and layoffs,” then that is not a solution — it is a transfer of the crisis onto the workforce. A serious restructuring must also include:
- management and owners taking responsibility,
- a credible industrial strategy,
- investment commitments,
- and a future product plan.

If the company wants labour to contribute, then capital, management, and the owners must contribute as well. Otherwise, there is no legitimacy to the demands being made.

### Our bargaining line
We are open to negotiating a package that combines:
- reduced labour cost,
- moderated capacity,
- and productivity gains,

but only in exchange for:
- **binding site guarantees,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **no closures,**
- and a durable collective agreement.

Without that, we will defend our position with all available means, including industrial action. The workforce is already mobilised, and there is no appetite for being forced to pay for a restructuring that others design and employees alone finance.

So: yes to a fair settlement, no to one-sided cuts. If the company wants peace, it must offer security, not threats.
