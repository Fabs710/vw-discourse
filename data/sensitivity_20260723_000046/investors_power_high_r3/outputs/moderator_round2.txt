### Core impasse
The central deadlock is **not** over whether the company needs restructuring; nearly everyone accepts that it does. The impasse is over **the means of achieving it and who bears the risk**:

1. **Employee-side red lines:** Works Council and Union reject **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies**.
2. **Management / capital-side requirement:** Management, investors, and the family holding insist on **durable, structural cost reduction and capacity rationalization** that is economically equivalent to closures if closures are avoided.
3. **State’s position:** The government supports restructuring but wants to **avoid social and regional damage**, especially closures and forced layoffs.
4. **Unresolved issue:** No side has yet tabled a package that is both:
   - **economically credible enough** for capital and management, and
   - **socially acceptable enough** for labour and the state.

So the plateau is at the point where each side is asking the others to move first on the most sensitive issue: **job/site security versus structural cost cuts**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions to bridge the gap

#### Proposal 1: “No forced layoffs, conditional site review”
A compromise structure could be:
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **A temporary moratorium on German plant closures**
- A **joint audit period** (e.g., 6–9 months) to test alternatives
- Clear, pre-agreed **trigger criteria** for any site adjustments if targets are missed

This would give labour protection from immediate closures while giving management a defined path if alternatives fail.

---

#### Proposal 2: “Employment security package with measurable offsetting savings”
A negotiated package could combine:
- **Binding employment protection through 2030**
- **Voluntary exit programs, early retirement, redeployment, and reduced working time**
- **Temporary labour-cost concessions**
- In return, management/owners commit to:
  - **investment in future products/technology**
  - **clear efficiency measures**
  - **quantified cost savings benchmarks**
  - **no closures unless the agreed savings corridor is missed**

This bridges the gap by tying worker concessions to enforceable management commitments.

---

#### Proposal 3: “Capacity reduction without closure as first resort”
If plant closure is the central red line, a possible bridge is:
- **Capacity consolidation across sites**
- **Partial line transfers**
- **Shift reductions**
- **Flexible work-time arrangements**
- **Voluntary workforce reductions**
- **Targeted product allocation by site**

This seeks to achieve the economics of closure **without immediate site shutdown**, while still delivering real structural savings.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
If a package delivers **binding no-compulsory-redundancy protection**, significant voluntary reductions, and measurable cost savings, **what specific additional flexibility on site utilization or working time would you be willing to accept to avoid closures?**

#### Industrial Union
What is the **minimum enforceable package of labor contributions**—in wages, hours, flexibility, or voluntary exits—that you would consider sufficient to justify a **no-closure, no-forced-layoff** settlement?

#### Group & Brand Management
What exact **cost gap, timeline, and savings threshold** would lead you to conclude that an alternative to closures is genuinely sufficient, and can you publish those criteria for negotiation?

#### Regional State Government
If no-closure protection is maintained, what **specific state-backed instruments**—for example mediation, investment facilitation, training support, or settlement guarantees—are you prepared to use to make the package economically viable?

#### Institutional Investors
What level of **durable margin improvement and cash generation** would you accept from a non-closure package, and over what time horizon, before you would support it as equivalent to plant rationalization?

#### Controlling Family Holding
Would you be prepared to endorse a **time-limited no-closure framework** if it is paired with enforceable productivity, capacity, and investment commitments that preserve long-term value even if short-term restructuring is slower?
