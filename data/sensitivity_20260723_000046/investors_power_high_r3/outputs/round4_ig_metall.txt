I will respond directly.

## To the works council
I agree with you on the **substance**: no compulsory redundancies, no German plant closures, and no weakening of co-determination. Those are not bargaining chips for me either.

Where I differ is mainly on **tone and sequencing**, not the core line. You are absolutely right that “temporary moratorium” language can be abused. A review clause by itself is not a guarantee. So I do **not** support any package that leaves the door open to forced layoffs as a routine fallback.

That said, we should be careful not to turn “binding through 2030” into a slogan without showing how the company actually gets there. If we want to preserve sites and jobs, then we need a package that is not only politically defensible but economically credible enough to hold.

That means:
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary wage moderation,
- reduced bonuses/special payments,
- productivity measures,
- and investment commitments.

Those are real instruments. A guarantee without a credible operating model behind it will not survive pressure from management and capital.

## To management
I reject your attempt to make closures the benchmark for seriousness.

You keep saying “economic equivalence,” but from our side there is **no social equivalence** to closing a German plant or forcing people out. That is exactly why we refuse to accept closures and compulsory redundancies as the organizing principle of the deal.

If you want labour to contribute, then present your numbers honestly:
- what savings are required,
- over what timeframe,
- and which measures have genuinely been tested before you invoke closures.

Do not ask us to treat closures as the default proving ground. That is not negotiation; that is pressure.

Also, let me be blunt: if your only credible restructuring logic depends on threatening closure and compulsory redundancies, then management has not yet earned the right to demand sacrifice from the workforce.

## To the family holding and investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic compromise is worthless. I agree.

But you are wrong if you think the only value-preserving route is one that permanently destabilizes the workforce. Industrial conflict, strike escalation, skill loss, and trust destruction also destroy value.

So yes, labour must contribute. But the contribution must be part of a **shared-sacrifice package**, not a one-sided transfer of risk to employees.

That means:
- management and owners also contribute,
- labour cost relief is negotiated,
- investment is secured,
- and site/job security is binding.

If you want long-term value, back a durable social settlement instead of treating workers as the adjustable part.

## To the state government
I appreciate your support for rejecting closures and forced redundancies.

But I need to be equally clear: support in principle is not enough if management can still use governance as a threat. If you are serious, then use your position to help lock in:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- and a real framework for job security through 2030.

At the same time, I also expect you to help with the constructive side:
- mediation,
- training support,
- qualification,
- and pressure on management and ownership to contribute fairly.

## Where I agree with the overall discussion
I agree that:
- the company needs a real restructuring,
- the current cost base is too high,
- the solution must be durable,
- and labour must contribute.

I also agree that a settlement which just postpones the problem is unacceptable.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- closures as the first serious option,
- compulsory redundancies as an acceptable fallback,
- or “conditional” guarantees that keep the workforce under permanent threat.

And I do not accept the idea that the workforce must first prove compliance before it receives security. For us, security is the condition that makes serious bargaining possible.

## What I am prepared to put on the table
If the company commits clearly to:
- **no German plant closures,**
- **no compulsory redundancies,**
- **binding site and job security through 2030,**
- **preserved collective bargaining structures,**
- and **real investment commitments,**

then the union is prepared to negotiate a serious contribution package, for example:
- temporary wage moderation,
- reduced special payments,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- and productivity/flexibility measures.

That is a fair trade.

What I will not accept is a deal where workers give concessions and management retains the right to later say the guarantees were only provisional.

## On the mediator’s bridge ideas
### Time-bound non-closure pact with triggers
Not as framed. If it keeps closures alive as a later weapon, it is not sufficient.

### Sequenced package
Sequencing is secondary. The key question is whether the end state is real:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- and binding security.

If yes, we can discuss the package. If not, sequencing is just a way to delay confrontation.

### Capacity reduction without shutdowns
This is the only direction that is even worth exploring seriously. But it must be genuine:
- shift changes,
- overtime reduction,
- voluntary departures,
- internal transfers,
- working-time solutions,
- and product allocation changes.

Not hidden closures, not site hollowing, not “review later” language.

## My bottom line
The union’s position remains:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Preserve the collective wage framework**
- **Employees contribute fairly, but not alone**
- **Management, owners, and shareholders must also take responsibility**
- **Any agreement must be durable and enforceable**

If the others want a settlement, they need to stop treating our red lines as bargaining noise.

Bring a package that respects them and is economically credible, and we will negotiate seriously.

If not, then we are back to open conflict, and we will use the instruments available to us.
