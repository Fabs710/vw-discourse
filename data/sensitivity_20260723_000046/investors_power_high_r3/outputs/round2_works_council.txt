I will respond very clearly: **the workforce side will not trade away plant security and job security just to make management’s restructuring easier to execute.**

I hear the arguments from management, investors, and the family holding about competitiveness, cost base, and overcapacity. Those concerns are real. But your conclusion is still the wrong one if it ends in **German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

### Where I agree
- Yes, the company needs a credible path back to competitiveness.
- Yes, a cosmetic solution is not acceptable.
- Yes, labour can contribute if the settlement is fair and balanced.
- Yes, the future product and investment plan matters.

### Where I strongly disagree
#### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable “economic necessity” by default
Management says closures may be necessary. I say: **prove that every alternative has been exhausted first**.

This company has options that do not destroy sites:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary departures,
- temporary wage moderation,
- productivity agreements,
- phased redeployment,
- and jointly designed efficiency measures.

You keep saying “equivalent savings.” Fine — then show them. But you do **not** get to start from closures as the presumed answer. For us, closures are not a technical instrument. They are irreversible destruction of livelihoods, regional economies, and industrial capability.

#### 2) Compulsory redundancies are a red line
The workforce will not accept forced layoffs as the price of management’s strategic correction. If the company needs headcount adjustment, then it must be through:
- voluntary programs,
- early retirement where applicable,
- internal transfers,
- training and redeployment,
- and negotiated working-time instruments.

If you choose compulsory redundancies, you are choosing confrontation. That is not a side issue; it is the line between negotiation and aggression.

#### 3) “No closures, no redundancies” is not a slogan — it is the minimum condition for a fair settlement
To management and shareholders: you keep asking for durability through 2030, but you want that durability built on the back of broken trust. That will not work.

A deal can be durable only if employees know:
- their sites remain open,
- their jobs are not being sacrificed through forced dismissals,
- and the collective bargaining framework remains intact.

Without that, any supposed peace is temporary and unstable.

### On the numbers and “credible savings”
You ask for concrete proposals. Here is the concrete answer from our side:

A credible package must include:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding employment and site guarantees through 2030**
- **a negotiated labour contribution only if it is matched by commitments from management and owners**
- **investment commitments for future models and technologies**
- **a co-determination role in shaping the restructuring, not just endorsing it**

If management needs cost relief, then it must be earned through negotiated instruments, not imposed through site destruction.

### To the institutional investors
You say governance constraints are the problem. No — the problem is believing that value can only be restored by threatening the workforce. That is short-term thinking.

A company with strong employee commitment, protected industrial capacity, and social stability is not a weakness. It is a competitive asset. A restructuring that tears up social trust may improve a spreadsheet in the short term, but it creates long-term instability, industrial conflict, and reputational damage.

### To the family holding
You say you want long-term value. Then stop supporting measures that would permanently damage the company’s industrial base and social legitimacy. Long-term value is not served by treating German sites as disposable.

### To management
If you want a real negotiation, then stop presenting closures as the benchmark and job guarantees as the concession. It is the other way around: **site security and employment security are the condition for any workforce contribution**.

If you are prepared to negotiate within those boundaries, then we can talk about:
- flexibility,
- time-limited employee contributions,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary instruments,
- productivity improvements,
- and productivity-linked investment commitments.

If not, then you are asking the workforce to finance a restructuring that strips them of their future. That is unacceptable.

### My line remains unchanged
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of co-determination or job-security protections**

If those are the demands, then we are still in direct conflict. If you want a solution, bring a plan that protects the sites and the people first, and we can discuss contributions after that.
