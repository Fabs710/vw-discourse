I will be direct.

We are aligned on the diagnosis: the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic settlement. The cost base is too high, overcapacity is real, and the brand must get back to acceptable margins. On that, I agree with management and the investors.

Where I do **not** agree is with any solution that turns into a prolonged industrial conflict or permanently weakens the enterprise through blunt force.

## Where I agree
- The current structure is not competitive enough.
- Labour must contribute.
- Management must bring a credible industrial plan, not just demands.
- Any settlement must be durable through 2030.
- Value destruction through endless confrontation is unacceptable.

## Where I disagree
### 1) No “default closure” logic
I do **not** accept plant closures as the starting point. They may be a last resort if, and only if, equivalent savings cannot be achieved otherwise. But the burden of proof is on management to show that all serious alternatives have been exhausted.

That means a real comparison against:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary departures,
- productivity and flexibility gains,
- negotiated labour-cost relief,
- and management/owner contributions.

If those alternatives can deliver comparable durability and economics, then closures are unnecessary and value-destructive.

### 2) No purely symbolic employee contribution
I also do not accept the opposite extreme: a deal that avoids closures in name but leaves the economics unchanged. A weak compromise that only postpones the problem would erode long-term value just as surely as an unnecessary closure.

So if labour wants site and job security, the contribution must be:
- substantial,
- durable,
- and measurable.

Not temporary optics. Real structural effect.

### 3) No value-destructive instability
The family holding will not back a settlement that turns into a public showdown, a brand-damaging strike war, or a governance crisis. We are long-term owners. We can accept short-term pain for durable competitiveness, but not an outcome that damages the asset itself.

## What I can support
I can support a package if it does all of the following:
- materially reduces costs,
- meaningfully cuts excess capacity,
- restores the margin trajectory,
- includes a fair labour contribution,
- preserves labour peace for a meaningful period,
- and protects the long-term industrial base where economically feasible.

That can be done through a mix of:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- phased labor-cost moderation,
- targeted productivity agreements,
- and investment commitments for future products.

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- a weak compromise that leaves the company structurally impaired,
- a one-sided burden shift onto employees,
- a settlement that destroys trust and triggers prolonged conflict,
- or permanent impairment of the company’s long-term earnings power.

## To the Works Council and Union
I hear your insistence on no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies. I understand the importance of stability, but I will be frank: those lines cannot be absolute if they prevent a real restructuring.

If you want our support for avoiding closures, then you need to bring a package that is genuinely equivalent in economic effect. We will not protect every current structure simply because it is politically easier.

## To Management
You need to be more explicit and more credible on the numbers. “Closures may be necessary” is not enough. Show:
- the size of the gap,
- the cost reduction required,
- what alternatives have been tested,
- and why a non-closure package would or would not work.

That is the level of seriousness required if you want governance backing.

## To the State Government
I understand your concern for regional stability. But protecting the regional base cannot mean freezing the company in an uncompetitive structure. If we can preserve sites and jobs through a serious alternative, I support that. If we cannot, then sentiment alone cannot decide.

## To the Investors
You are right that the economics must work. I agree. But “hard enough” is not the same as “maximum cuts.” A settlement that is economically credible and socially stable can protect value better than a blunt restructuring that triggers prolonged conflict.

## Bottom line
The controlling family holding will support a **credible, durable, and economically real restructuring**. We will **not** support:
- a hollow compromise,
- a destructive stand-off,
- or a settlement that permanently impairs the enterprise.

If the parties can produce an alternative to closures that is truly equivalent in savings and certainty, we will consider it. If not, then management must be prepared to present the hard case plainly, with evidence.
