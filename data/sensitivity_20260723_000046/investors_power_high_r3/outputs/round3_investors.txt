We appreciate that everyone now agrees the current cost base is unsustainable and that a cosmetic settlement would destroy value. That is the starting point, and it is correct.

But we need to be very clear: **from a capital-markets perspective, “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” is not a viable absolute if it prevents a durable return to competitive margins.** If the alternative package is not economically equivalent in scale, durability, and certainty, then it is simply not enough.

## Where we agree
- **Management** is right that the company needs structural, not temporary, cost reduction.
- **The family holding** is right that a weak compromise would just defer the problem.
- **The state government** is right that a deal must avoid needless damage to industrial stability.
- **The union and works council** are right that labour cannot be asked to carry the entire burden alone.
- Everyone is right that prolonged conflict is value-destructive.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be treated as a veto on economics
The works council and union keep saying closures are unacceptable in principle. We understand the concern, but principle does not pay for structural overcapacity.

If the business case shows that Germany has excess capacity and uncompetitive fixed costs, then those costs must come out somewhere. If they cannot come out through closures, then the alternative must be **genuinely equivalent**:
- durable capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- measurable productivity gains,
- and enforceable implementation.

So far, we have not seen that equivalence demonstrated. We have seen assertions, not numbers.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies is acceptable only if the savings are real
We are prepared to support a no-forced-layoff solution **if** it delivers the same economic effect. But if “voluntary” measures merely spread pain over time while preserving the same fixed-cost structure, then it does not solve the margin problem.

A non-closure package is only acceptable if it:
- materially improves return-on-sales,
- reduces structural cost, not just one-off expense,
- and is durable enough to survive beyond the next bargaining round.

### 3) The state’s role cannot become a shield against competitiveness
We respect the political sensitivity and the state’s co-determination role. But a blocking position cannot become a guarantee that the company remains overstaffed and under-margined.

A governance structure that prevents hard choices may protect jobs in the short term, but it can also destroy enterprise value. That would be a failure for all shareholders, including the state and the family.

## On the mediator’s bridge proposals
### Proposal 1: “No forced layoffs, conditional site review”
This is too soft unless the review is tied to hard triggers and real consequences. A moratorium without enforceable economics is just delay.

### Proposal 2: “Employment security package with measurable offsetting savings”
This is the only direction that sounds credible — but only if the offsetting savings are quantified, audited, and large enough.

That means:
- clear savings corridors,
- measurable headcount/capacity effects,
- binding labour contributions,
- and a firm timeline.

Without that, it is a cosmetic compromise.

### Proposal 3: “Capacity reduction without closure as first resort”
We are open to that **if it really reduces the cost base**. But if it is just internal reshuffling while German fixed costs remain too high, then it is not a solution.

## What would move us
We would support a non-closure deal if it came with:
- quantified, durable margin improvement,
- meaningful and enforceable capacity rationalisation,
- substantial labour contributions,
- explicit cost-out milestones,
- and deal certainty that avoids prolonged strike risk.

We would not need plant closures if those conditions are met. But if they are not met, then capital will rightly conclude the business remains structurally impaired.

## Bottom line
Our position remains unchanged:
- **We support a durable restructuring.**
- **We do not support a weak compromise.**
- **We will support no closures only if the alternative is economically equivalent.**
- **If not, closures remain a legitimate tool.**

The burden is on management and labour to produce a package that fixes the economics, not just the optics. If that package exists, we can support it. If it does not, then we will not back a settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive.
