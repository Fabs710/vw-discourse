### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that a restructuring is necessary and that the current cost base is unsustainable.
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be durable, not merely cosmetic.
- There is shared interest in a negotiated settlement rather than open-ended confrontation.
- Most parties accept that labour, management, and owners/shareholders all need to contribute in some form.
- There is general support for preserving the company’s long-term viability and future investment capacity.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and state government reject closures; management, institutional investors, and family holding say closures may be necessary if no equivalent savings are found.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour side and state government reject them; management and capital holders want no absolute exclusion if needed for competitiveness.
- **Binding job and site guarantees:** Labour side wants strong guarantees through 2030; management and shareholders will only accept them if the economic savings are sufficient and credible.
- **Scope of labour contribution:** All agree labour should contribute, but there is no agreement on how large, how durable, or in what form.
- **Role of governance/state veto:** State side insists it cannot support a destructive solution; investors see governance constraints as part of the problem if they block competitiveness.
- **Definition of “acceptable restructuring”:** Labour emphasizes social balance and reversibility; management and shareholders emphasize structural cost reduction and capacity rationalization.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- The size and durability of savings required versus the extent of job and site protection.
- Whether capacity reductions can be achieved without closures, and if so, whether they will be economically equivalent.
- How to divide sacrifices among employees, management, owners, and capital providers.
- What specific future-product and investment commitments would justify labour concessions.
- Whether any employment guarantees can be made binding without limiting the company’s ability to adjust through 2030.
- How to balance immediate conflict avoidance with the need for credible long-term competitiveness.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to numbers: identify the minimum savings, capacity changes, and labour contributions each side would consider credible.
- Test alternative packages side by side: closures vs. non-closure equivalents, with explicit economic outcomes.
- Clarify which guarantees are essential, which are conditional, and which can be time-limited.
- Ask each side to propose one concrete compromise package that preserves its core red lines while addressing the others’ main concerns.
