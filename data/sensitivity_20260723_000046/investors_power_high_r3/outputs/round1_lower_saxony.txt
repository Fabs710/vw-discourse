I will be equally direct.

We **do** share one key assessment with management: this company cannot simply coast on the old structure. The cost base, market pressure, and competitive environment are real. No one here is denying that.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only credible answer is plant closure, mass job cuts, or permanently weakening the company’s German industrial base.

### Where I agree
- A restructuring is necessary.
- It must be durable, not cosmetic.
- The company needs a credible path back to competitiveness.
- Labour, owners, and management all have to contribute.

### Where I disagree sharply
I reject any approach that starts from the premise that German sites must be closed to make the numbers work.

That is not a neutral “economic” decision in our case. It would mean:
- destroying regional employment,
- undermining a major industrial anchor,
- and using irreversible cuts when negotiated alternatives have not been exhausted.

As a shareholder and political actor, I cannot support a solution that guts the regional economy in the name of efficiency. And I will be clear: if a proposal relies on closures or on removing the state’s co-determination and veto role, it will not have my support.

### To management
If you want our backing, bring us a restructuring that is:
- **socially responsible,**
- **credible economically,**
- and **reversible where possible**.

That means serious alternatives must be examined first:
- capacity reduction without closures,
- working-time solutions,
- voluntary measures,
- productivity improvements,
- management and shareholder contributions,
- and a negotiated employee contribution tied to binding safeguards.

I am not asking for the status quo. I am asking for a restructuring that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor instead of hollowing it out.

### To the works council and union
I support your insistence that there must be no forced redundancies and no one-sided burden shifting. That is not just a labour issue; it is an issue of economic stewardship and political responsibility.

At the same time, I will be frank: if we want to protect sites and jobs, then the package must still produce real savings and a real competitiveness gain. A refusal to move at all would also be irresponsible. The state is prepared to support a negotiated solution, but not a block on every adjustment.

### What I can support
I can support a settlement if it includes:
- binding site and employment protection,
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a fair labour contribution,
- investment commitments for future products,
- and a restructuring that actually improves the company’s prospects.

### What I cannot support
- closures presented as the default solution,
- permanent loss of jobs in the region,
- forcing the workforce to pay while capital is protected,
- or any attempt to sideline the state shareholder and its veto rights.

So my position is: **yes to restructuring, no to social and industrial destruction**. If management wants to test whether there is a responsible alternative to closures, I am ready to work on that. If not, then you should expect resistance — not acquiescence.
