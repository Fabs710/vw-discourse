## To management, investors, and the family holding
You keep repeating “economic equivalence” as if that settles the matter. It does not.

From the workforce side, **there is no equivalence to closing a German plant or forcing people out of their jobs**. That is precisely why we reject the framing. It turns social destruction into a technical accounting exercise.

You say the company needs durable cost reduction. I do not dispute the need for restructuring. What I dispute is your assumption that the only credible route is to keep closures and forced redundancies on the table as a pressure instrument. That is not a neutral test. It is a threat.

If you want credibility, then show us a restructuring package that:
- keeps **every German plant open**,
- rules out **compulsory redundancies**,
- preserves **co-determination**,
- and delivers savings through **voluntary, negotiated, reversible measures**.

Until then, I see no reason to move.

## On the moderator’s “time-bound non-closure pact”
I reject the idea in its current form.

A “fixed period” moratorium with trigger mechanisms is not a guarantee. It is a postponed closure decision dressed up as stability. The workforce has seen this pattern before: first a temporary pause, then “objective review,” then the same pressure returns with more leverage over employees.

If a closure threat remains embedded in the design, then the so-called pact is not a pact. It is conditional surrender.

We need **binding site security through 2030**, not a provisional ceasefire.

## On “sequenced package” proposals
Again, the order matters less than the substance.

You can sequence it however you like, but if the package contains an open door to closures or forced redundancies, then the core problem remains unresolved. We will not trade away job security in exchange for promises that can be reinterpreted later.

And let me be clear: **job security is not a reward for good behavior; it is the precondition for serious bargaining**.

## On “capacity reduction without shutdowns”
This is the only direction that is even discussable, but only if it is genuine.

That means:
- shift reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- early retirement,
- training and qualification,
- product allocation changes,
- and productivity measures.

It does **not** mean:
- one site is hollowed out so another can survive,
- hidden closure through underutilization,
- or “review later” language that preserves the closure option.

If the economics require closing a plant, then your model is wrong, your investment allocation is wrong, or your management priorities are wrong. The answer is not to push the cost onto workers.

## To the regional state government
You say you oppose closures and forced redundancies. That is correct and necessary.

But opposition is not enough. Use your role to make it impossible to weaponize the board structure against the workforce. If you are serious, then back a solution that:
- blocks closures,
- blocks compulsory redundancies,
- and protects the job-security framework.

Do not tell us that “if the package fails, we must reopen the issue.” That is precisely how management keeps pressure alive.

## To the union
We stand together on the core issue: no closures, no forced redundancies. I appreciate your support.

But I also want to be clear that we must not let the discussion drift into a trade where the workforce gives up more and more flexibility while management keeps the nuclear option in reserve. That is not shared sacrifice. That is asymmetric risk shifting.

Employee contributions are only acceptable if they are tied to **binding guarantees**, not conditional promises.

I do agree on a few points:
- The company needs a real restructuring, not cosmetics.
- Labour will have to contribute.
- The solution must be durable.
- Industrial conflict would be damaging.

But none of that changes the basic fact: **there is no legitimate restructuring path that starts from plant closures and forced layoffs in Germany**.

- reduced overtime,

- productivity improvements,

We cannot discuss:
- closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
