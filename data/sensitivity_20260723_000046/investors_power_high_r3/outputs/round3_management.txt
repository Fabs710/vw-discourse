I will be blunt, because the company cannot afford slogans.

## Where I agree
I agree with the union, the works council, the state government, the family holding, and investors on one fundamental point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic truce**.

I also agree that:
- labour cannot carry the entire burden,
- the solution has to work through 2030,
- and future investment in products and technology is essential.

So yes: we are aligned on the need for a real solution.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute if the economics do not work
To the works council and union: I hear your red line. I understand why you are holding it. But I cannot accept a position that rules out plant closures under any circumstances if that means the company keeps an uncompetitive cost and capacity structure.

You keep saying “prove the alternatives.” Fine. But alternatives must be more than names on a list. They must produce:
- durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- and a real improvement in margins.

If they do not, then “no closures” is not a protection of the company. It is a guarantee that the problem survives.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not enough on its own
I am prepared to work with voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and negotiated labour-cost measures. But if you reject closures and compulsory redundancies, then you must offer something that is **economically equivalent**.

And I mean equivalent in substance, not in rhetoric:
- not temporary optics,
- not accounting moves,
- not a delay of the same problem,
- but structural savings that change the cost base.

If the alternative cannot do that, then it is not an alternative.

### 3) The state government’s position is understandable, but it does not solve the business problem
Regional stability matters. I accept that.

But saying “no closures, no forced layoffs” does not magically make the company competitive. It only shifts the burden of proof onto management to show whether a different package can deliver the same effect.

If it can, I will support it. If it cannot, then we cannot pretend the numbers have changed because the politics are difficult.

### 4) The family holding and investors are right to demand credibility
I agree with them that a weak compromise would be a mistake. A settlement that preserves too much capacity and too much cost would simply postpone the next crisis.

Where I differ from the idea of “protect every current structure” is simple: **our obligation is not to preserve every site as it is today. Our obligation is to restore the company’s competitiveness.**

That requires hard choices.

---

## What management needs
Let me state the management position clearly, because this is where we stand:

We need to:
1. **reduce overcapacity**
2. **lower labour costs structurally**
3. **restore the brand’s return-on-sales path**
4. **free funds for product and technology investment**
5. **do all of this durably, not temporarily**

That is the only responsible course if we want this company to remain strong in 2030 and beyond.

## What I am prepared to accept
I am not insisting that closures are the only route.

If the employee side can deliver a package that is truly equivalent, I am open to it. That means:
- substantial and durable labour-cost relief,
- real capacity reduction,
- measurable productivity gains,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- and enforceable commitments.

If that package is serious enough, then yes, closures may not be necessary.

## What I am not prepared to accept
I will not accept:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves every site regardless of economics,
- a one-sided burden shift onto management or shareholders,
- or a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

And I will say this plainly: **a “no closures, no redundancies” line is not enough unless it is matched by equally hard economics.**

---

## On the mediator’s bridge proposals
### Proposal 1: “No forced layoffs, conditional site review”
This is not good enough as framed.

A moratorium with a later “review” can easily become a delay tactic. I am not interested in an arrangement that simply postpones the same fight. If there is to be a review, it must be tied to clear, measurable economic thresholds and a credible restructuring package.

### Proposal 2: “Employment security package with measurable offsetting savings”
This is closer to serious. But the order matters less than the result.

I can accept binding employment security only if it is paired with:
- real labour-cost relief,
- capacity adjustment,
- productivity and flexibility gains,
- and investment commitments that improve the business case.

What I cannot accept is site security with no economic effect.

### Proposal 3: “Capacity reduction without closure as first resort”
This is the direction I am prepared to explore.

But let us be honest: if “capacity reduction” is only a euphemism for preserving the same fixed cost base while moving work around, it will not solve the issue. If it is real, measurable, and durable, then it may.

---

## My direct response to labour
You say employees should not finance their own insecurity. I agree with that principle.

But employees also cannot insist on full protection while the company absorbs the full adjustment burden. That would be equally one-sided.

A fair settlement must include:
- a meaningful employee contribution,
- lower structural costs,
- and a real path back to competitiveness.

Anything less is not fair to the company, the owners, or the long-term future of the workforce.

## My direct response to the state government
If you want to avoid closures, help produce a package that is genuinely equivalent in economics.

What I need from you is not just political resistance. I need support for:
- a credible voluntary adjustment framework,
- faster qualification and redeployment,
- and a negotiated package that actually reduces the cost base.

If the outcome does not materially improve competitiveness, then political comfort will not save the company.

## My direct response to the investors and family holding
You are right to demand a durable, economically real solution. I share that standard.

Where I push back is on the idea that governance constraints make hard decisions impossible. They do not. They mean hard decisions have to be negotiated into a package that can pass the board and survive implementation.

That is exactly what management is trying to do.

---

## Bottom line
My position remains unchanged:

- **We need a structural, durable cost and capacity reduction.**
- **Temporary or cosmetic concessions are not enough.**
- **I am open to avoiding closures if — and only if — the alternative is economically equivalent.**
- **If it is not, then closures and stronger restructuring measures remain on the table.**

So let us stop pretending that “no closures” by itself is a solution. It is not. The solution is a package that restores competitiveness. If the employee side can help produce that, I am ready to negotiate. If not, I will not pretend the numbers work when they do not.
