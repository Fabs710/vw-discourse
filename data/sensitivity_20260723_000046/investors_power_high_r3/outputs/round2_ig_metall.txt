I will respond bluntly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state government, and even parts of management and the owners on one point: **a cosmetic deal is not enough**. This company needs a durable solution, not a temporary truce.

I also agree that labour cannot simply say “no” to everything. We have already signaled openness to:
- temporary wage moderation,
- foregoing bonuses or special payments,
- working-time reductions,
- voluntary instruments,
- and capacity adjustments,

**if** those measures are part of a fair, negotiated package that protects jobs and sites.

## Where I disagree
### 1) No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies are not negotiable starting points
I do **not** accept closures as the default answer to a cost problem. And I do **not** accept forced layoffs as a legitimate restructuring tool when other instruments have not been exhausted.

The works council is right on this: you do not start by destroying capacity and then call that responsibility.

To management, investors, and the family holding: if your answer to every problem is “close plants” or “cut jobs,” then you are not presenting a restructuring plan — you are transferring the burden onto the workforce.

### 2) The burden must be shared
What is still missing from your proposals is a fair distribution of sacrifice.

If employees are expected to accept wage restraint, flexibility, and capacity reductions, then I expect:
- management to contribute visibly,
- owners and shareholders to absorb part of the pain,
- and the company to commit to investment, future products, and employment security.

I will not endorse a package where workers pay twice:
- once through lower incomes,
- and again through job insecurity and plant closures.

### 3) “Equivalent savings” must be real, not rhetorical
Management keeps saying closures may be necessary unless we produce equivalent savings. Fine. Then let’s be concrete.

Equivalent savings cannot mean:
- vague promises,
- one-off accounting effects,
- or shifting risk into the workforce through endless insecurity.

If closures are being used as leverage, then the alternative must be a **measurable, durable, and enforceable** package of:
- capacity reduction,
- productivity improvements,
- working-time flexibility,
- and labour-cost relief,

combined with **binding site and employment guarantees**.

## What I can support
I can support a package with:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures**
- **binding employment and site protection through 2030**
- **a negotiated employee contribution**
- **capacity reductions through voluntary and flexible means**
- **investment commitments for future models and technologies**
- **a durable collective agreement**

That is not “soft.” It is the minimum basis for social peace and industrial stability.

## What I cannot support
I will not accept:
- closures presented as unavoidable before alternatives are exhausted,
- compulsory redundancies,
- open-ended insecurity,
- a one-sided cost-cutting program,
- or any attempt to weaken collective bargaining protections.

If management insists that restructuring must mean plants shut and workers forced out, then we are not close to a deal. We are in conflict.

## To management
If you want a serious bargaining process, then bring a proposal that shows:
- how much cost must be removed,
- by which instruments,
- over what time horizon,
- and how jobs, sites, and the collective agreement will be protected.

Do not ask for blank-check concessions. We are ready to contribute, but only under conditions of fairness and reciprocity.

## To the investors and family holding
You want durability and value. So do we. But durability does **not** come from breaking trust with the workforce. It comes from a settlement that employees can live with and defend.

A company that wins cost cuts by provoking a protracted labour conflict may please the spreadsheet for a quarter, but it damages productivity, morale, and long-term stability.

## Bottom line
The union’s position remains:
- **fair shared sacrifice, yes**
- **forced layoffs, no**
- **plant closures in Germany, no**
- **collective bargaining preserved, yes**

If the others want a real agreement, they need to stop treating workforce security as a concession and start treating it as the precondition for any contribution from labour.

If that is not understood, then I will not soften my line.
