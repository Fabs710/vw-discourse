I will be direct.

I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one point: **this situation requires a real restructuring, not a cosmetic fix**. The cost base must come down, and the company must regain competitiveness if it is to invest through 2030.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion that the only credible path is German plant closures and forced redundancies. That would be a grave mistake economically, politically, and socially.

## Where I agree
- The current structure is too expensive.
- Overcapacity is a real issue.
- Labour must contribute if there is to be a durable settlement.
- A temporary truce without structural improvement would fail.
- The company needs room to invest in future products and technology.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not the starting point
To management: you keep saying closures may be necessary if no equivalent savings are found. That is a legitimate test, but you are not yet showing that all alternatives have been exhausted.

Before anyone talks about shutting German sites, I expect a serious comparison against:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary departures,
- phased redeployment,
- productivity and flexibility agreements,
- and management/owner contributions.

If those alternatives can deliver the needed economics, then closures are not justified. And if they cannot, then the numbers must be shown transparently, not asserted.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
To management and the investors: forced layoffs are not a neutral instrument in this company. They would trigger political backlash, industrial conflict, and damage to the brand and regional economy that will not show up neatly in a quarterly model.

To the union and works council: I support your rejection of compulsory redundancies. That is not just a labour demand; it is a legitimate concern for industrial stability and regional responsibility.

### 3) No one gets to shift the burden onto the state and workforce alone
I also agree with the union that labour cannot be the only side making sacrifices. If employees are expected to accept wage restraint, flexibility, or temporary concessions, then management, owners, and capital must contribute visibly as well.

A one-sided burden shift would be unacceptable.

## What I can support
I can support a negotiated restructuring if it includes all of the following:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding employment and site protection through 2030**
- **a meaningful, negotiated labour contribution**
- **capacity adjustment through reversible and socially responsible measures**
- **investment commitments for future models and technologies**
- **a credible path to restore competitiveness**

That is not a refusal to restructure. It is a demand that restructuring be done responsibly.

## What I cannot support
I cannot support:
- closures presented as the default answer,
- a deal that guts regional employment,
- a restructuring that weakens the company’s industrial base in Germany,
- or any attempt to sideline the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are core to our responsibility as an anchor shareholder and political actor.

## To management
If you want my support, bring a package that shows:
- how much cost must be removed,
- by which instruments,
- over what time horizon,
- and how jobs and sites are protected while that happens.

If the only answer is “close plants or accept mass layoffs,” then you are asking for support for a solution that I cannot back.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence on no closures and no forced redundancies. But I also need to be clear: a settlement must still produce real savings and real competitiveness gains. A refusal to move at all will not be enough either.

We should be looking for a package built around:
- voluntary instruments,
- working-time solutions,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary and fair labour contributions,
- and binding future-product commitments.

That is the serious middle ground.

## Bottom line
My position remains:
- **yes** to restructuring,
- **yes** to shared sacrifice,
- **no** to plant closures as the default solution,
- **no** to compulsory redundancies,
- **no** to any outcome that damages the regional economic base or removes the state’s co-determination role.

If there is a durable alternative that protects jobs and sites while restoring competitiveness, I am prepared to work for it. If not, then I will resist a destructive solution.
