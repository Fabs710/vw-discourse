I will be equally clear: **a restructuring that keeps “closures and forced reductions” on the table as a real option is not acceptable from the workforce side.** We have already seen what that language means in practice: pressure on employees, broken trust, and a management attempt to turn a strategic failure into a jobs problem.

I do agree on one point raised by several of you: **the company needs a durable solution, not cosmetic measures.** If the business is structurally under pressure, then yes, we need a serious package. But the burden of proof is on management and the owners to show that they are not simply reaching for the easiest and most destructive instrument.

### Where I agree
- **Competitiveness matters.** We are not denying the market reality.
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.** We know what a prolonged confrontation means for everyone.
- **Labour can contribute** through working-time flexibility, temporary moderation, and productivity improvements.
- **Investment and future products matter** if you want a German industrial base in 2030 and beyond.

### Where I disagree
#### 1. Plant closures are not a neutral “instrument”
A plant closure is not just another restructuring tool. It is a permanent destruction of jobs, skills, and regional industrial capacity. Once you close a site, you do not “optimize” it later. You break a workforce, damage supplier networks, and weaken the company’s long-term capability.

So no — I will not accept the framing that closures simply “must remain on the table” as an abstract possibility. For us, that means you are already negotiating under threat.

#### 2. Forced redundancies are a red line
If the company can only deliver savings by imposing compulsory layoffs, then management has failed to build a credible industrial plan. We are prepared to discuss:
- voluntary retirement schemes,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time reductions,
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- capacity adjustments through negotiation,

but **not forced redundancies**.

#### 3. “Shared sacrifice” must be real, not one-sided
I hear the calls for employee contribution. Fine. But then let’s be honest:
- management must reduce overhead and take accountability,
- owners must accept their role and not demand one-sided labour concessions,
- and the company must commit to German sites with binding guarantees.

Otherwise “shared sacrifice” is just code for workers paying for strategic misjudgments they did not make.

### What would move us
You all keep talking about durability. Then make it durable. The conditions are straightforward:
1. **Binding site guarantees for all German plants to 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No plant closures**
4. **A genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
5. **Binding investment commitments and product allocation for the German footprint**
6. **A fair management and owner contribution**

If those conditions are met, then we can talk about meaningful employee contributions. If not, then there is nothing to negotiate in the workforce’s interest.

### To management
You say you want a structural reset. I say: **show us a structural plan that does not destroy the industrial base.** If there are savings to be made, start at the top, in procurement, administration, executive cost, and efficiency. Show me that every non-labour lever has been exhausted before you ask for sacrifices from the people actually building the cars.

### To the controlling shareholder and capital markets
You cannot demand competitiveness while treating jobs and sites as disposable. Long-term value is not created by breaking the workforce’s trust. If you push for closures, you will face resistance with all means available to us, including industrial action and the use of our co-determination rights.

### To the state government
Your role is not to mediate a managed decline. If regional employment and the industrial base matter, then you must use your blocking position to prevent a closure-led solution and support a settlement that preserves German capacity.

### Bottom line
We are ready for a tough negotiation. We are not ready to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a restructuring that shifts the crisis onto the workforce.

Bring forward a package that protects sites and jobs through 2030, includes real investment, and distributes the burden fairly. Without that, you will not get agreement from the works council, and you should be under no illusion about the level of resistance that will follow.
