We agree on the diagnosis: the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic peace deal. We also agree that labour, management, and owners must all contribute.

But I need to draw a hard line on the method.

### Where I agree
- The cost base has to come down.
- The company must regain competitiveness.
- Employee contributions can be part of the solution.
- Investment and future product commitments are necessary.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to escalation.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the starting framework. In a company like this, in a region like ours, those are not neutral instruments. They mean irreversible damage to jobs, skills, supplier networks, and regional stability.

To the controlling shareholder and management:  
You keep saying closures must remain available if the numbers require them. I understand the logic, but I do **not** accept closures being treated as the default threat around which everyone must negotiate. That is not a balanced restructuring; it is pressure politics. Before any closure is even contemplated, you must demonstrate that all other instruments have been exhausted and that the savings cannot be achieved otherwise.

To the union and works council:  
I support your insistence on no forced redundancies and protection of sites. That is not sentimentalism; it is a legitimate defence of an industrial base that took decades to build. But I also expect the workforce side to engage seriously on measurable contributions, not just principles. If you want us to defend this company politically and through governance, then we need something concrete we can justify as a shared sacrifice package.

### What I can support
A package along these lines is still conceivable and, in my view, the right direction to test:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures in Germany as the first resort**
- **Binding site guarantees for the core operations**
- **Temporary, negotiated labour contributions**
  - working-time flexibility
  - bonus moderation
  - limited wage restraint
  - voluntary exit windows
- **Management and owner contribution**
  - overhead reduction
  - executive restraint
  - dividend discipline where appropriate
- **Targeted capacity adjustment through internal means**
  - shifts, utilisation, product allocation, redeployment
- **Binding investment and product commitments** for German sites
- **A joint monitoring mechanism** with supervisory-board oversight, including the state’s role

That is a real restructuring. It is not “status quo with nicer language.” But it also does not accept the idea that German plants are disposable.

### What I cannot accept
- A closure-led strategy presented as inevitable from the outset.
- A restructuring that pushes the main burden onto employees.
- Any outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.
- Any settlement that treats regional employment as an afterthought.

### To management
If you want my support, bring a quantified scenario that shows:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. how much can be closed without closures,
3. what employee contribution is requested,
4. what management and owner contribution is real and verifiable,
5. and what binding investment follows from that package.

Do **not** come back with vague claims that “structural change” requires closures unless you can prove that alternatives fail.

### To the controlling shareholder
I recognise your duty to preserve enterprise value. But value is not preserved by detonating the company’s regional legitimacy. If you force through a closure-led approach against the state, workforce, and co-determination structure, you may win a narrow cost argument and lose the company’s social and political foundation.

### To the union and works council
I want to be equally frank: if you insist on absolute red lines without being prepared to discuss concrete contribution instruments, you risk leaving the initiative entirely to management. We should not do that. We should force management to choose a genuinely balanced package by putting forward a credible counterproposal ourselves.

### My bottom line
I will support a **negotiated restructuring that protects jobs and sites in Germany, preserves co-determination, and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice**.

I will **not** support a closure-led restructuring or any settlement that guts regional employment. If a package cannot be built without that, then the package is not good enough and should be sent back.

If management wants progress, I expect the next round to include:
- quantified alternatives to closure,
- a concrete employee contribution proposal,
- a management/owner contribution,
- and binding guarantees for the German footprint.

That is the standard I will defend.
