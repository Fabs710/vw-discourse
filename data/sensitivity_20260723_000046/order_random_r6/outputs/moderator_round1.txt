### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a serious competitiveness and cost problem.
- All parties accept that a cosmetic or short-term fix is insufficient.
- There is broad support for a negotiated settlement rather than unmanaged conflict.
- There is common ground on the need for:
  - some form of employee contribution,
  - management/owner contribution,
  - investment and product commitments,
  - a durable, medium-term solution.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Union, works council, and state government reject closures as a default or acceptable outcome.  
  - Management, controlling family, and investors insist closures must remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Union and works council reject forced layoffs.  
  - Management and shareholder side will not rule them out if necessary.
- **Scope of restructuring:**  
  - Labour and state want restructuring without dismantling sites or industrial capacity.  
  - Management and investors want a structural reset, including real capacity reduction if required.
- **Role of guarantees:**  
  - Labour and state want binding employment/site/investment guarantees up front.  
  - Management and investors want guarantees tied to measurable cost and capacity reductions first.
- **State involvement:**  
  - The state insists on a strong co-determination/blocking role and opposes a closure-led solution.  
  - Other parties see that as potentially limiting necessary restructuring.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How much capacity reduction is actually required to restore competitiveness.
- Whether equivalent savings can be achieved through:
  - working-time changes,
  - wage moderation,
  - bonus restraint,
  - productivity measures,
  - internal redeployment,
  - or other negotiated instruments,
  instead of closures or layoffs.
- What level of employee contribution is acceptable, and what concrete counter-guarantees it should trigger.
- How to balance short-term labour peace against long-term structural change.
- Which commitments on jobs, sites, and investment can be made credibly before the final restructuring design is settled.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and options**: identify the exact gap to be closed and compare specific restructuring scenarios.
- Ask each side to table:
  - minimum acceptable employee contribution,
  - acceptable limits on capacity reduction,
  - proposed guarantees on employment/sites/investment,
  - and any management/owner contributions.
- Focus discussion on **equivalent alternatives** to closures and compulsory redundancies, and on what binding safeguards would make a negotiated package acceptable.
