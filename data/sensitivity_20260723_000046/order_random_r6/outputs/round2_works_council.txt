Let me be absolutely clear: **the workforce will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of this restructuring.** Not as a “fallback,” not as a “structural tool,” and not as a bargaining threat. We have heard that language before, and it always means the same thing: workers are supposed to absorb management’s failure while others preserve their options.

### Where I agree
I agree that the company needs a **durable** solution. I agree that a cosmetic deal would be useless. I agree that labour can and will contribute if the outcome protects jobs, sites, and industrial capacity. And I agree that management and owners must also contribute. On that, there is no dispute.

### Where I fundamentally disagree
#### 1) “Closures must remain on the table” is not a neutral statement
That is not just an instrument; it is a threat to tens of thousands of families, to supplier networks, and to the long-term industrial base in Germany. A plant closure is irreversible. Once you close a site, you do not “restructure” it later. You destroy capability.

So no, I do **not** accept management’s framing that closures are simply a business option that must stay available until the workforce proves otherwise. That is not partnership. That is coercion.

#### 2) Forced redundancies are not acceptable
If the only way management can “close the gap” is by imposing compulsory layoffs, then management has not presented a credible industrial plan. We are prepared to discuss voluntary exits, retirement windows, redeployment, working-time flexibility, temporary wage moderation, and bonus restraint. But **not forced redundancies**.

#### 3) The burden cannot be pushed onto labour while guarantees are postponed
Management and the shareholders keep saying guarantees can come later, after the numbers are proven. That is backwards. Workers are being asked to make sacrifices now, while the other side refuses to commit now.

If you want a serious settlement, then the conditions are clear:
- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding site guarantees for all German plants to 2030**
- **Binding investment and product commitments**
- **A genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
- **A fair contribution from management and owners**

Without those, there is no basis for agreement from the works council.

### To management
You say you need a structural reset. Fine. Then show us a structural plan that does **not** destroy the industrial base. You have not done that yet.

What have you actually put on the table?  
- Pressure.  
- Threats.  
- Closure language.  
- And a demand that workers trust you after breaking the historic job-security framework.

That is why we do not trust this process.

If there are savings to be found, start where the margins are easiest:
- top management,
- overhead,
- procurement,
- executive pay,
- internal inefficiencies,
- product mix,
- and management discipline.

Do **not** come to the workers first with closures and layoffs.

### To the controlling family
You speak of enterprise value. So do we. But long-term value is not created by destroying trust, detonating industrial relations, and turning a skilled workforce into a cost-cutting target.

If you force a closure-led strategy, you may get a headline and a temporary cost reduction. What you will also get is a damaged company, resistance across the sites, and a breakdown of the social foundation that has made this group strong.

### To the regional state government
You are right to reject closure-led restructuring. But let us be honest: that stance only matters if you use your veto power decisively. The works council will not help decorate a deal that keeps the plants open in name but empties them out in practice.

What we need is a **binding settlement**, not a managed decline dressed up as compromise.

### To the investors
You keep saying capital needs hard numbers. Fine — so do we. But your logic cannot be: “prove competitiveness first, then maybe we’ll protect jobs.” That flips the burden onto the workforce while giving management a free hand.

If you want a deal that is durable, then it must be built on:
- shared sacrifice,
- binding safeguards,
- and a real industrial future in Germany.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a serious employee contribution, but only within the red lines I have stated. That could include:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity adjustments through utilisation and shift changes,
- voluntary exit programs,
- and productivity measures agreed with the workforce.

But the trade-off must be real and enforceable:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- binding investment,
- and site guarantees through 2030.

### Bottom line
If the other side wants a settlement, it needs to stop treating the workforce as the adjustment buffer. We are not blocking a solution; **we are blocking a destructive solution**.

Bring a package that protects every German plant, protects jobs, preserves co-determination, and shares the burden fairly. Then we can talk.  
If not, then you should expect the works council to oppose this with everything we have, including industrial action and the full use of our co-determination rights.
