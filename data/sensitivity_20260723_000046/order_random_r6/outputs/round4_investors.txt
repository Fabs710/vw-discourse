We agree with management, the family, and the experts on the central financial point: **the business must get back to competitive margins, and a cosmetic settlement would destroy value**.

That is our priority. Not preserving every site, not avoiding discomfort, but **restoring risk-adjusted returns** through durable cost reduction.

## Where we agree

We agree that:
- the company has a structural cost problem,
- labour must contribute,
- management and owners must contribute,
- the solution must be quantified, not rhetorical,
- and any deal has to be durable enough to support investment and the return to target margins.

We also agree with the independent assessments that:
- internal measures can produce meaningful savings,
- a no-closure package may work **if the gap is moderate**,
- but if the overcapacity and fixed-cost burden are large, then internal measures alone may be insufficient.

That is the key economic test.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The union and works council are still overreaching
We understand why they want no closures and no compulsory redundancies. But from an investor perspective, those cannot be treated as absolute red lines if the numbers do not work.

A no-closure stance is acceptable **only if it is proven to close the gap in a durable way**. If it does not, then insisting on it becomes a demand to preserve excess cost and weak margins. We will not support that.

### 2) The state government is right to demand sequencing, but not to freeze economics
We support a time-limited protected negotiation phase. That is sensible. But it must not become a de facto permanent shield against necessary restructuring.

Regional stability matters, but so does solvency, capital discipline, and future product investment. If the structure stays uncompetitive, the longer-term employment outcome gets worse, not better.

### 3) Management is right on optionality, but must now quantify it properly
We support keeping closures and compulsory redundancies available as fallback tools if the internal package falls short. But management now needs to stop speaking in generalities.

We want:
- a no-closure scenario,
- a hybrid scenario,
- a fallback structural scenario,

with:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- savings by measure,
- timing,
- execution risk,
- and impact on the margin target.

Without that, “structural necessity” is too vague to justify anything.

## Our position on the protected negotiation phase
We are open to a **time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies during negotiations**, provided it is paired with:

1. full disclosure of the financial and plant-level data,
2. quantified commitments from labour, management, and owners,
3. clear milestones,
4. and an agreed trigger for re-opening structural options if the gap is not closed.

That is a reasonable bridge between protection and flexibility.

But we will not support a moratorium that turns into a permanent veto on footprint adjustment.

## What would move us
A no-closure deal could earn our support if it delivers:
- a credible return to target margins,
- verifiable cost reduction,
- durable savings, not just temporary concessions,
- and no hidden continuation of overcapacity.

If that can be shown, we do **not** need closures for ideological reasons.

If it cannot be shown, then closure and capacity rationalization must remain available. That is capital discipline.

## What we would support
We would support a package with:
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary exits,
- retirement windows,
- redeployment,
- management overhead cuts,
- executive restraint,
- owner contribution,
- and investment commitments tied to delivery.

We would also support a structured review point after the measures have been tested.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic peace deal,
- a package that preserves too much excess capacity,
- or a solution that trades short-term labor peace for long-term margin erosion.

## Bottom line
We are **open to a no-closure outcome only if the numbers prove it works**.

If the company can restore competitive margins without closures, and can do so durably, then that is fine.

If it cannot, then management must retain the ability to act structurally. The company cannot be asked to carry uncompetitive capacity forever.
