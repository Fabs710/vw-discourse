### Core impasse
The central deadlock is **sequence and scope**:

- **Union / works council / regional state** want **binding protection first**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, site guarantees, then negotiated sacrifices.
- **Management / controlling family / investors** want **structural flexibility first**: closures and redundancies must remain available if quantified savings are otherwise insufficient.
- The result is a trust impasse: one side sees the other’s “necessary flexibility” as coercive leverage; the other side sees absolute red lines as precluding a viable restructuring.

A second, related impasse is **what counts as proof**:
- Worker-side actors want the company to prove that all non-destructive alternatives are exhausted before any footprint change.
- Capital-side actors want alternatives to be quantified and benchmarked against the savings required, without prior exclusion of closures.

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete compromise pathways that could bridge the strongest conflict lines:

#### 1) Sequenced restructuring with a conditional closure freeze
- Agree to a **time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies** for an initial negotiation phase.
- In that period, management tables **fully quantified scenarios**:
  1. no-closure package,
  2. hybrid package,
  3. fallback structural package.
- If the no-closure package reaches an agreed savings threshold, closures are removed from the deal.
- If it does not, any closure proposal becomes subject to **strict procedural safeguards**: independent business-case review, alternative-search proof, and joint governance approval.

**Bridge function:** gives labour protection during negotiations while preserving management’s claim that closures remain a last-resort tool.

#### 2) “No forced layoffs” with controlled voluntary footprint adjustment
- Commit to **no compulsory redundancies**.
- Permit **voluntary exits, retirement windows, redeployment, working-time reductions, shift consolidation, and temporary wage/bonus restraint**.
- If capacity must fall beyond what these measures can achieve, establish a **jointly governed adjustment pool**: employees affected by site changes receive priority placement, compensation, retraining, and transfer rights.
- Closures are not excluded in principle, but can only occur if the joint body certifies that the same capacity reduction cannot be achieved by internal means.

**Bridge function:** preserves the union’s red line on forced layoffs while offering capital a credible route to structural change.

#### 3) Conditional site guarantees tied to measured contribution
- Provide **binding site guarantees for core operations** through a defined period, but not across all plants indiscriminately.
- In exchange, labour accepts a **measurable contribution package**:
  - wage moderation,
  - bonus restraint,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - productivity measures,
  - utilization changes.
- Management and owners accept **parallel, verifiable contributions**:
  - overhead reduction,
  - executive restraint,
  - capital discipline,
  - investment commitments.
- Review points are scheduled at fixed milestones, with pre-agreed triggers if savings or investment commitments are missed.

**Bridge function:** converts abstract trust claims into a monitored exchange of obligations.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Industrial Union
If closures and compulsory redundancies are ruled out absolutely, **what specific combination of wage, working-time, productivity, and utilization measures would you accept as sufficient to close the quantified gap, and over what time period?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
If you insist closures remain available as a tool, **what independently verifiable test would you accept before a closure can be pursued, and what binding non-layoff alternatives must be exhausted first?**

#### To the Regional State Government
If you reject closures as a first resort, **what concrete governance lever will you use to ensure the company still achieves the required savings and does not simply defer the problem?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
Before reopening the footprint question, **will you table a fully quantified comparison of the savings from the no-closure, hybrid, and closure scenarios, including the effect on jobs, capacity, and investment timing?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
If a negotiated package without closures produces the required margin improvement, **will you support it even if it preserves more sites than you would otherwise prefer, and what metrics will you use to decide?**

#### To the Works Council
If management provides a quantified plan showing that site protection through 2030 cannot be financed without stronger internal adjustments, **what additional flexibility, if any, are you prepared to discuss short of closures and compulsory redundancies?**
