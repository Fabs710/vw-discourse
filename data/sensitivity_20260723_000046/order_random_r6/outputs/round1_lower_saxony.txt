We agree on the diagnosis that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem and that a durable solution must restore margins and investment capacity. We also agree that a negotiated employee contribution is preferable to a prolonged confrontation.

But I need to be very clear: **I do not accept plant closures in Germany as the default instrument, and I will not support a package that shifts the main burden onto the workforce or sidelines co-determination.** Our blocking-minority position and board seats are there precisely to prevent that kind of one-sided outcome.

Where I see common ground:
- A restructuring is necessary.
- Labour, management, and owners all need to contribute.
- The solution must be durable, not a short-term patch.
- Investment commitments and product security matter.

Where I disagree strongly:
- **“Structural measures must remain available” cannot mean closures and mass layoffs are simply held in reserve until the workforce capitulates.** That is not a balanced negotiation; it is coercion.
- German sites are not abstract cost factors. They are regional economic anchors, with skilled labour, supplier networks, and long-term value.
- Any plan that reduces capacity should first exhaust alternatives: working-time instruments, temporary wage moderation, internal flexibility, productivity gains, and a fair management/owner contribution.

From the state’s perspective, the non-negotiables are clear:
1. **No outcome that guts regional employment.**
2. **No removal of the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
3. **No restructuring that treats German plants as disposable.**

What I could support is a negotiated package with these elements:
- site guarantees for the core German operations,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- temporary and proportionate employee contributions,
- management contribution and cost discipline at the top,
- targeted capacity adjustments only where they can be done without plant closure,
- binding investment and product commitments for the German footprint,
- a joint monitoring mechanism with the workforce and anchor shareholders.

If management truly needs more room, then it should come with **credible, enforceable commitments** to future production and employment in Germany—not with demands for closures presented as inevitabilities.

So my position is: **yes to restructuring, yes to shared sacrifice, no to closure-led restructuring and no to a settlement that undermines the long-term industrial base of the region.** I expect the other parties to bring forward a package that meets that standard.
