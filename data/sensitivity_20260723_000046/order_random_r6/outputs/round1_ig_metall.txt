We recognise that the company is under real pressure, and we are prepared to contribute to a viable solution. But let me be equally clear from the start: **we will not accept compulsory redundancies, plant closures, or a restructuring that shifts the full burden onto the workforce**.

Our priorities are straightforward:

1. **Protect jobs and sites**
   - No forced layoffs.
   - No closure of German plants.
   - No erosion of the collective wage framework as the price of management failure.

2. **Fair sharing of sacrifice**
   - If cost reduction is necessary, then management, owners, and employees must all contribute.
   - Workers can discuss measures such as temporary wage moderation, bonus waiver, working-time adjustments, capacity reductions, or other negotiated contributions.
   - But these must come with binding guarantees on employment, investment, and plant security.

3. **A durable industrial settlement**
   - We want a solution that stabilises the company for the medium term, not a short-term cut that destroys trust and capability.
   - That means clear commitments on future products, investment in German sites, and a restructuring path that preserves industrial know-how.

We are open to negotiation, including a serious employee contribution, if it is part of a balanced package. But if management insists on closures, forced redundancies, or unilateral wage dumping, then you should expect strong resistance, including industrial action.  

Our position is simple: **shared sacrifice, yes; workers paying alone, no.**
