### Core impasse
The central blockage is this:

- **Management / family / investors** want **closure and redundancy options to remain available** as credible restructuring tools.
- **Union / works council** want a **binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff framework** during negotiation, with site guarantees first.
- **State government** is aligned against closure-led restructuring, but wants evidence-based sequencing rather than a hard blanket prohibition.
- The experts have said **internal measures may be enough if the gap is moderate, but may be insufficient if the structural gap is large**.

So the impasse is not whether restructuring is needed. It is **which instruments are allowed before the numbers are proven**, and **who carries adjustment risk if internal measures fall short**.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### 1) Time-limited protected negotiation phase
A **fixed-duration negotiation window** during which:
- no plant closures are initiated,
- no compulsory redundancies are executed,
- all parties table quantified proposals,
- management retains the right to re-open structural options only if agreed milestones are not met.

This bridges:
- the union/state demand for protection during talks,
- and management/investor demand for eventual option value.

#### 2) Conditional no-closure package with trigger-based review
A **conditional no-closure agreement** built around measurable targets:
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- overhead cuts,
- management/owner contribution,
- and product/investment commitments.

If the agreed savings and utilization targets are met, the no-closure framework continues.  
If they are not met, parties move to a predefined second-stage review of footprint options.

This bridges:
- labour’s demand for a genuine no-closure effort,
- and management’s insistence on a verified fallback.

#### 3) Phased capacity adjustment without immediate closure/forced layoffs
A package that prioritizes:
- shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- internal redeployment,
- attrition-led headcount reduction,
- voluntary exit programs,
- and site-role changes.

Only if these measures fail to close the residual gap would deeper structural measures be reviewed.

This bridges:
- the union’s resistance to destructive measures,
- with management’s need for real capacity reduction pathways.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Industrial Union
What **specific quantified employee contribution** would you accept, and on what **measurable outcome** would you agree that a later structural review may begin?

#### Controlling Family
Are you prepared to support a **time-limited closure freeze** during negotiations if the company receives a binding, quantified labour and management contribution package?

#### Regional State Government
What exact **governance mechanism** will you use to make a negotiation-phase protection framework enforceable, rather than symbolic?

#### Group & Brand Management
What is the **minimum residual cost gap** you still consider unacceptable after internal measures, and what metric will determine that threshold?

#### Institutional Investors
Would you support a **no-closure package** if it delivered the target margin through verified, durable savings even if it preserved more capacity than a closure-based plan?

#### Works Council
If a quantified no-closure package shows a credible path to target margins, what concrete labour-side commitments are you prepared to lock in up front?
