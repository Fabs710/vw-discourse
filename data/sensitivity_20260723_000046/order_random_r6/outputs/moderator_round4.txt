### Core impasse
The impasse sits in **sequencing and conditionality**:

- **Labour / state / works council** want a **binding no-closure, no-forced-redundancy phase first**, with structural options excluded during negotiations.
- **Management / family / investors** want **closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available as fallback tools**, arguing that economics may require them if the residual gap stays large.

So the dispute is not just over outcomes; it is over **whether the negotiation starts under a closure moratorium or under retained restructuring optionality**.

A second, related impasse is over **the threshold for “enough” savings**:
- one side wants the gap fixed before any structural option is revived,
- the other wants structural options kept live unless the no-closure package clearly closes the gap.

---

### Facilitator proposals for possible compromise
Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled without endorsing any side.

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited binding moratorium with trigger-based review
A **protected negotiation phase** of fixed duration, during which:
- no closures occur,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- full plant-by-plant data is shared,
- labour, management, and owners each table quantified contributions,
- and an independent mediator verifies progress.

At the end of the phase, structural options are **not decided in advance**, but become reviewable only if agreed milestones are missed.

**Purpose:** gives labour and the state the protection they want, while preserving a defined fallback path for management and capital.

---

#### Proposal 2: Conditional no-closure package with a narrow exception clause
A **no-closure default** for all sites through the negotiation horizon, but with a narrow, pre-agreed exception:
- a site can only be revisited if independent analysis shows it remains unviable **after** all internal measures,
- the review standard is plant-specific,
- and the burden of proof sits with management.

This would be paired with:
- quantified employee concessions,
- management/owner contributions,
- and investment commitments.

**Purpose:** addresses labour’s concern that closure is being used as leverage, while not fully removing economics-based reassessment.

---

#### Proposal 3: Staged restructuring package with two decision gates
A two-stage deal:
1. **Stage 1:** internal measures only, under a closure-free window.
2. **Stage 2:** if the residual gap remains above a defined threshold, a jointly supervised review opens on footprint adjustments.

The parties would agree **in advance** on:
- the threshold definition,
- the data set,
- who audits it,
- and what measures count toward the internal package.

**Purpose:** converts the argument from “trust us” into a measurable process with explicit decision rules.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Industrial Union
If a protected negotiation phase is established with full disclosure and independent audit, **what quantified labour contribution is the union prepared to commit to immediately, and under what precise conditions would it accept a review of structural options?**

#### To Works Council
If plant-by-plant data shows one or more sites remain structurally uncompetitive after internal measures, **what governance mechanism would the works council accept to prevent endless deadlock while still protecting employment where viable?**

#### To Regional State Government
If you insist on a binding moratorium during negotiations, **what enforcement mechanism will the state actually use to ensure both sides disclose complete figures and meet milestones, rather than allowing the moratorium to become a delay tactic?**

#### To Controlling Family
If a no-closure package is presented that is independently verified to close most of the gap, **will you commit in advance not to reopen closure demands unless the agreed trigger is objectively missed?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
If labour accepts quantified concessions in a protected phase, **what specific management and overhead cuts will you commit to first, before invoking any footprint or redundancy option?**

#### To Institutional Investors
If the no-closure package produces a credible path to target margins but with slower savings realization, **what minimum financial metrics would still make you support it instead of insisting on structural cuts?**
