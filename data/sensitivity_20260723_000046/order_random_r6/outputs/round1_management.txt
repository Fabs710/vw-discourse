I appreciate that both of you recognise the gravity of the situation. On one point we are aligned: this company cannot continue with a cost base that leaves it uncompetitive, and a cosmetic deal will not be enough.

But I need to be equally direct: **a package that rules out closures, forced reductions, and structural change in advance does not solve the problem we are facing.** It may sound balanced politically, but it does not restore competitiveness.

To the union side:
- I hear your willingness to discuss wage moderation, working-time adjustments, and other contributions.
- That is welcome, but it is **not sufficient on its own** if the underlying capacity problem remains.
- If we simply spread pain without actually reducing the structural burden, we will be back here in a year or two with even weaker margins and less room to invest.
- “No closures” as an absolute principle is not compatible with the scale of the crisis.

To the state government:
- I understand your concern for regional employment and the industrial base.
- But saying “no closures” and “no outcome that guts regional employment” does not answer how the company is supposed to close the gap in factory costs and capacity.
- Co-determination and the state’s role are respected, but they cannot become a mechanism to freeze an uncompetitive footprint indefinitely.
- If we fail here, the long-term damage to the region and its jobs will be worse.

My position is this:

1. **We need a durable reduction in cost and capacity.**
   - That can include plant closures if necessary.
   - It can also include other structural measures if they deliver the same effect.
   - But the result must be real, not symbolic.

2. **Employee contribution is necessary.**
   - That means labour-cost moderation, working-time flexibility, and productivity gains.
   - I am not talking about a token gesture.

3. **Investment commitments must follow, not substitute for, restructuring.**
   - We will commit to future products and technology only if the company is put on a sustainable footing.

4. **We are prepared to negotiate the form, not the need, for change.**
   - If there is a credible package that achieves equivalent savings without closures, I will engage seriously.
   - But I will not accept a settlement that preserves every site, preserves current labour costs, and leaves the core problem untouched.

So let me be very clear: **I am open on the instrument, but not on the outcome.** The outcome has to be a structural reset of the German cost base. If that requires closures, then closures must remain on the table. If there are alternatives, they must be real and quantifiable, not political placeholders.
