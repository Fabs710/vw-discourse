### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs **real restructuring**, not cosmetic delay.
- There is broad agreement that the current **cost base is too high** and competitiveness must be restored.
- Most actors accept that **capacity reduction** and some form of **employee contribution** may be part of a solution.
- There is shared opposition to a settlement that leaves the company **structurally uncompetitive**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Management keeps them on the table as potentially necessary; the state government, union, and works council oppose closures as a default or acceptable starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** The union and works council reject them; management has not ruled them out.
- **Extent and form of labour contribution:** Investors and management want savings that are large, binding, and durable; labour representatives want contributions only in a package with strong job and site protections.
- **Job and site guarantees:** Workers want binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors have not accepted that as a prerequisite.
- **Role of the state/shareholder governance:** The regional government insists on its veto role and on protecting the regional industrial base, which may constrain closure options.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. job security:** No agreed formula shows how to achieve the needed cost reduction without closures or layoffs.
- **Speed vs. social acceptability:** Management wants rapid, structural action; labour and the state want alternatives tested first.
- **Durability vs. flexibility:** Investors want enforceable, lasting measures; labour wants protections that limit management’s ability to revisit sites later.
- **Economic sufficiency vs. regional legitimacy:** No common basis yet for what level of savings is “enough” if achieved through non-closure measures.
- **Distribution of burden:** There is no agreement on how management, owners, workers, and the state should each contribute.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle to **numbers**: specify the cost gap, target savings, and the quantitative effect of each proposed measure.
- Compare two or more **fully costed packages**: closure-based vs. non-closure alternatives.
- Clarify which measures are **binding, durable, and enforceable**.
- Identify the minimum acceptable combination of **capacity cuts, labour concessions, investment commitments, and job/site guarantees** that could still restore competitiveness.
