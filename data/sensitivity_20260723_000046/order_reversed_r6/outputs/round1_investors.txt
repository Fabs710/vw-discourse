Our position is straightforward: we support a restructuring only if it delivers a **credible, durable, and enforceable return to competitive margins**.

What we care about most is:
- **a clear reduction in the cost base**
- **a realistic path back to target returns on sales**
- **execution certainty**
- **no cosmetic compromise that delays the problem**

What we are **not** prepared to support:
- a settlement that preserves structurally uncompetitive German costs
- half-measures that protect every legacy arrangement but leave margins impaired
- prolonged conflict that destroys value without producing real savings

On the means, we are **not wedded to plant closures as an end in themselves**. If equivalent savings can be locked in through a combination of capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, lower labour costs, and binding operational changes, that can be acceptable. But the burden is on management and the other stakeholders to show that the package is **quantitatively sufficient, durable, and credible**.

So our basic stance is:
- **support decisive restructuring**
- **support alternative solutions if they genuinely match the economics**
- **oppose anything that leaves the company cost-inefficient or margin-dilutive**

We are watching for one thing above all: **does this actually restore competitiveness, or just postpone pain?**
