## Where I disagree

### 1) To the works council and union
You keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, site guarantees through 2030” as if those conditions can be treated as preconditions. They cannot.

That may feel protective in the short term, but it makes future investment impossible and pushes the burden onto the whole company.

I am prepared to negotiate on the means. I am **not** prepared to accept a framework that forbids the very tools needed to restore competitiveness.

### 2) To the regional state government
You say closures should not be the default. I can accept that. But you are still treating closures as something that must be politically disqualified in advance. That is not a credible governance position when factory costs are materially above target and the market is shrinking.

If a site cannot be made viable through other measures, then protecting it indefinitely is not responsible regional stewardship — it is deferred decline. I will not agree to a settlement that substitutes political comfort for economic reality.

### 3) To investors and owners
You are right to demand the bridge. But I also need to be explicit: **a non-closure option only works if it is genuinely deep**. If the alternative package is shallow, then all the NPV modeling in the world won’t change the fact that it leaves us with too much cost and too much capacity.

So I accept your standard, but I want to be clear about the conclusion it may lead to:  
if the non-closure package does not close the gap, then **stronger structural action becomes necessary**.

## On the economics
The specialist’s framework is the right one. And under that framework, the burden is on the alternative proposal to prove three things:

1. **Savings are large enough**
2. **Savings are durable enough**
3. **Savings are enforceable enough**

That means a real package has to include:
- hard capacity reduction,
- permanent labor-cost reset,
- tangible headcount reduction,
- and operational flexibility that lasts beyond the next bargaining round.

Anything less is not equivalent to closure. It is just a slower path to the same problem.

## My position
Management is not saying “closure first” as ideology. We are saying:

- **we need a structural cut in overcapacity**
- **we need a meaningful labour-cost contribution**
- **we need a durable improvement in unit costs**
- **we need a path back to target return on sales**
- **we need funds freed for future products and technology through 2030**

If a non-closure package can truly do that, then it deserves consideration. But it must be **arithmetically and operationally equivalent**, not symbolically preferable.

- a deal that preserves uncompetitive German costs,
- a package that delays adjustment,
- guarantees that eliminate future flexibility,
