### Core impasse

The discussion is stalled around **one central conflict**:

- **Capital-side stakeholders** want a restructuring that is **economically sufficient, durable, and flexible enough** to restore competitiveness, including the option of plant closures if needed.
- **Labour-side and state stakeholders** want restructuring **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, while also preserving **site and job security through 2030**.
- **Management** is being asked to prove, quantitatively, whether a non-closure package can close the cost gap; until then, the parties are arguing over the instrument rather than the data.

In procedural terms, the impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed, but over **which constraints are negotiable**:
1. whether closures may be used at all,
2. whether binding job/site guarantees can coexist with competitiveness,
3. and whether the non-closure package can deliver equivalent, enforceable savings.

---

### Facilitator proposals for bridging positions

#### Proposal 1: Conditional non-closure package with a trigger mechanism
A compromise could be structured as:

- **No immediate closures**
- A **binding non-closure restructuring package** for a defined period
- **Quantified savings targets** by site and group level
- A **pre-agreed trigger**: if the package misses agreed cost/margin milestones by a specific date, the parties reopen the question of deeper structural measures, including closures or transfers

This gives labour and the state a chance to avoid closures now, while giving investors and owners a credible backstop.

#### Proposal 2: Site protection with partial flexibility
A second possible bridge is:

- **Preserve all sites for now**
- Allow **capacity reduction, shift cuts, line consolidation, and voluntary departures**
- Replace absolute job guarantees with a **mix of employment protection and adjustment tools**, such as:
  - no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
  - capped headcount reduction through voluntary programs,
  - limited redeployment across sites,
  - and periodic review of utilization and cost performance

This reduces the gap between “full protection” and “closure-first” by making the adjustment real but not immediate or absolute.

#### Proposal 3: Tripartite burden-sharing package
A third compromise would require each side to contribute explicitly:

- **Workforce**: working-time flexibility, wage restraint, bonus sacrifice, voluntary exits
- **Management**: overhead reduction, executive cost cuts, capital discipline, operational simplification
- **Owners/investors**: acceptance of a staged path, longer payback horizon, and continued funding of the transition
- **State**: support for training, redeployment, and investment incentives, but only in exchange for measurable restructuring steps

This reframes the dispute away from “who bears all the pain” toward “who contributes what, and in what order.”

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Institutional Investors
**What specific non-closure savings threshold, by year and by NPV, would be sufficient for you to accept a no-closure settlement as economically equivalent to closures?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a staged restructuring that preserves all sites initially, if it included a hard trigger for deeper measures should the agreed savings targets not be met?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**What level of job reduction, if any, would you consider acceptable in a non-closure package if it is the minimum required to secure the company’s competitiveness and future investment?**

#### To Management
**Can you present, within a single model, the cost gap, the savings from each measure, and the point at which the non-closure package would fail to meet the required return-on-sales target?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**Which concrete labour contributions are you prepared to put on the table now—working time, wages, bonuses, redeployment, voluntary exits—and what scale of savings do they have to achieve to be considered sufficient?**

#### To the Works Council
**If full site guarantees cannot be maintained everywhere, what alternative employment-security package would you accept that still protects the bulk of jobs and prevents compulsory redundancies?**
