We are aligned on one point: **the company needs a credible restructuring that restores competitiveness**. I do not dispute that the cost base is too high and that the current path is not sustainable.

Where I need to draw a clear line is this: **“deep restructuring” cannot mean plant closures and mass layoffs as the default first choice** if there are viable alternatives that achieve comparable savings. As regional shareholder, I cannot support a solution that **guts jobs in the state**, damages the industrial base, and weakens the company’s role as a regional anchor when other tools may still be available.

So my position is:

- **Yes** to a durable cost reduction
- **Yes** to verifiable, enforceable savings
- **Yes** to capacity adjustment where necessary
- **No** to closures that destroy viable sites without exhausting alternatives
- **No** to a settlement that shifts the burden almost entirely onto workers and the region

To the capital-market side: I agree that cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. But the same standard applies to closure-first logic. A closure may look decisive on paper, yet it can create long-term political resistance, social conflict, and capacity loss that undermine the very competitiveness you want. If savings can be delivered through working-time flexibility, labor-cost contributions, product allocation, and targeted capacity reductions, that is the more responsible route.

To the controlling family: I recognize the long-term horizon, and on that basis I would expect support for a solution that preserves the company **and** its regional legitimacy. A restructuring that burns through trust, employment, and co-determination would not be a durable value strategy. The state will not consent to being sidelined or to any outcome that removes our veto role on major plant decisions.

So let me be explicit about the condition under which I can move:
- **a negotiated restructuring that protects core sites and the bulk of jobs**
- **real but socially balanced employee contributions**
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **no unilateral closure path while alternatives remain untested**

If those conditions are not met, then I will resist a closure-driven solution. We are prepared to accept adjustment. We are not prepared to accept regional industrial decline dressed up as restructuring.
