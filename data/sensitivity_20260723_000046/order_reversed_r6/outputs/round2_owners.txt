We welcome the shift toward a quantified comparison. That is the right basis for a governance decision.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a **durable and enforceable** cost reset.
- We should **not** preserve a structurally uncompetitive cost base just to avoid difficult choices.
- A **non-closure solution** can be acceptable, but only if it is economically equivalent, not merely politically easier.
- The right test is **net present value** and implementation certainty, not slogans.

### Where we disagree
To the works council and union: I do **not** accept a framing in which binding job and site guarantees through 2030 are a prerequisite, regardless of economics. If those guarantees eliminate the company’s ability to restore competitiveness, they destroy value and simply defer a harder adjustment. Long-term ownership does not mean permanent protection of every legacy arrangement.

To the regional state government: I understand the political and regional logic, but I cannot support a position that effectively rules out closures before the economic case is made. A site is not automatically entitled to survive if it is structurally uncompetitive. If closures are avoided, that must be because the alternative package truly matches the economics — not because closure is politically difficult.

To management: I agree you need stronger measures, but you still owe us the actual bridge. We need the plant-level and group-level numbers, with:
- current cost gap,
- annual savings by measure,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- timing,
- and sensitivity cases.

Without that, “deep restructuring” is still only a claim.

### My governance position
The family holding will support a solution that:
- restores competitive margins,
- is durable over the cycle,
- preserves the company’s ability to invest,
- and avoids value-destructive, prolonged conflict.

We will **not** support:
- cosmetic compromises,
- temporary concessions disguised as structural reform,
- or any settlement that leaves German operations permanently above target cost.

### On the two options
At this stage, I am **not** in favour of declaring plant closures the default outcome. But I am equally **not** prepared to rule them out in advance. If the non-closure package does not close the gap in a credible way, then stronger structural action becomes unavoidable.

So my position is simple:
- show us the **numbers**
- prove the **durability**
- demonstrate the **execution path**
- and then we can judge whether closures are necessary or whether a non-closure package is genuinely equivalent

If the alternative is only “protect everything and hope,” then that is not acceptable. If the alternative is a real, enforceable cost reset, then it deserves serious consideration.
