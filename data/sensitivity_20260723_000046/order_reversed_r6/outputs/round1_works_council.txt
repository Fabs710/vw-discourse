I will be very direct.

We agree on one point only: **this company needs a real restructuring, not a fake one**. But I will not accept a “real restructuring” that is paid for by **closing German plants, breaking the historic job-security promise, and forcing workers out**. That is not restructuring; that is dismantling the workforce that built this company.

### Where I agree
- The cost base is too high.
- The company needs a durable solution, not postponement.
- Capacity will have to be adjusted.
- Labour can contribute if the package is fair and balanced.
- Half measures and cosmetic language are useless.

### Where I clearly disagree
To management: you keep framing closures as a possible necessity. From our side, **plant closures are not an acceptable instrument** and **compulsory redundancies are a red line**. You are asking workers to absorb the consequences of strategic mistakes while keeping the right to destroy sites that are still viable if properly managed. I do not accept that logic.

To investors and owners: you say the package must be economically sufficient. Fine. But do not pretend that the only path to sufficiency is closures and layoffs. If you want durable savings, then accept **binding job guarantees, binding site guarantees, and genuine operational changes**. Otherwise you are demanding one-sided sacrifice from labour while preserving ownership flexibility.

To the state government: I welcome your rejection of closure-first thinking, but I need more than political sympathy. We need **clear, binding support for sites and jobs through 2030**. If you are serious about preserving the industrial base, then you must back a settlement that rules out closures and forced layoffs.

### What would be required to move us
There is only one basis on which we can talk seriously:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site guarantees through 2030**
- **binding job-security framework through 2030**
- **a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring**
- **fair employee contributions matched by commitments from management and owners**
- **investment and product allocation for the German plants**

If those conditions are not on the table, then there is no settlement from our side. We are prepared to negotiate a contribution from the workforce. We are **not** prepared to negotiate away the existence of our sites or the security of our members’ jobs.

### On the current course
Management should understand this clearly: if you continue to push closures and forced layoffs, you will face full resistance. We have already shown that the workforce is prepared to mobilize. That is not a threat for effect; it is the reality of this situation.

So my message is simple:
- **open the books on the real economics**
- **bring forward an alternative that preserves every German plant**
- **guarantee jobs and sites to 2030**
- **or expect this to be fought hard, in the works council, in the supervisory board, and on the shop floor**

I am not here to bargain away our red lines. I am here to defend them.
