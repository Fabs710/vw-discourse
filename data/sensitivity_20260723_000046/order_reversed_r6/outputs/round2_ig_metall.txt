We welcome the move to a quantified bridge. That is the right basis. But let me be equally clear: **numbers do not change the principle that workers must not bear the entire adjustment, and plant closures or compulsory redundancies are not acceptable as the default answer.**

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **real restructuring**, not postponement.
- The current cost base is too high.
- Any settlement must be **durable, enforceable, and economically sufficient**.
- A labour contribution can be part of the package.
- We do need to move from slogans to **plant-level economics**.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You keep presenting closures as if they are the natural endpoint of the model. They are not the only structural instrument, and they are not acceptable as a first resort.

If the company wants the workforce to contribute, then the company must also show:
- where management cost reductions come from,
- what owners are giving up,
- how capacity is being reduced without destroying viable sites,
- and why the burden is not simply being pushed downward onto workers.

A “credible” restructuring is not one where the most disruptive option is chosen because it is easiest to impose. It is one where the savings are achieved **without forced layoffs and without plant closures**, if that can be done through genuine capacity cuts, working-time changes, and fair wage concessions.

### To investors and owners
You are right that temporary gestures are not enough. But you are wrong if you think the only durable solution is one that reserves every option for capital and demands open-ended flexibility from labour.

Binding job and site protections are not value destruction by themselves. In this context, they are the condition for labour peace and for a workable settlement. If you want a stable cost reset, you need:
- employee contributions,
- management contributions,
- owner responsibility,
- and a clear commitment that the workforce is not paying for strategic mistakes with its jobs.

### To the state government
I welcome your rejection of a closure-first approach. But I need you to back that up with concrete support for a **non-closure restructuring** — not just political language. If you want to protect the industrial base, then use your position to help secure:
- capacity reduction,
- investment commitments,
- and binding job protection.

A “solution” that leaves the company free to reopen the closure threat at the next bargaining round is not a solution.

## On the specialist’s framework
The NPV logic is fine. But the conclusion cannot be pre-decided. If management wants to claim that closures are superior, it must prove that:
1. non-closure measures cannot close the gap,
2. the closure costs do not outweigh the savings,
3. and the long-term risk to labour peace and production capability is acceptable.

That burden is on management, not on the workforce.

And let me say this plainly: if the non-closure option is judged against a closure benchmark that assumes workers give up everything while capital gives up nothing, then the comparison is biased from the start.

## What a serious alternative must include
We are not refusing adjustment. We are insisting on a fair one. A viable package must include:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **capacity reductions**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **temporary or partial wage restraint**
- **forgoing bonuses or other financial contributions**
- **investment and product commitments**
- **a durable job-security framework**
- **management and owner contributions**

That is the minimum basis for a settlement.

## What is not acceptable
If the bridge is used to justify:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- and open-ended insecurity,

then there is no basis for agreement. That would be workers paying all the pain while others keep all the options.

We have already shown willingness to contribute. We will not sign up to a deal that sacrifices the collective wage framework and job security without a fair and balanced return.

## Bottom line
Show us a **plant-by-plant alternative** that achieves the required savings **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**. If it truly does, then we can talk seriously. If it does not, then management is not presenting restructuring — it is presenting a demand for workers to absorb the crisis alone.
