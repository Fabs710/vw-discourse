We agree on one thing: **this must be a real restructuring, not a cosmetic delay**. If the company stays structurally uncompetitive, that is bad for everyone, including our members. But I need to be equally clear: **the burden cannot be loaded onto workers alone**, and **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points**.

### Where I agree
- **A durable cost reduction is necessary.**
- **Capacity must be adjusted.**
- **An employee contribution can be part of a package.**
- **Temporary peace bought by postponing the problem is not acceptable.**

### Where I disagree
To management: you are presenting closures and job cuts as if they were the natural first answer. They are not. They are the most destructive answer. Before anyone talks about shutting plants or forcing layoffs, you must show that you have exhausted alternatives that preserve jobs and sites.

To the investors and owners: I hear your demand for enforceable savings. Fine. But “enforceable” must apply to management and ownership too. We will not accept a deal where workers surrender wages, flexibility, and guarantees while capital keeps all its claims intact and management avoids any real responsibility.

To the regional state government: I welcome your rejection of a closure-first approach. But that position must be backed by concrete commitments, not just political language. If you want sites and jobs protected, then you need to support a package that gives the company a viable cost structure without destroying the regional industrial base.

### What a serious settlement would require
We are prepared to discuss:
- **capacity reductions**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **temporary or partial wage restraint**
- **forgoing bonuses or other financial contributions**
- **product and investment commitments tied to job security**

But only if the package includes:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **binding guarantees for core sites**
- **a fair distribution of sacrifice**
- **clear contributions from management and owners**

### What is unacceptable
If the proposal is essentially:
- workers pay,
- workers accept insecurity,
- workers lose guarantees,
- and the company still reserves the right to close plants anyway,

then there is no basis for a settlement. That would mean workers bear all the pain while management and ownership shift the risk downward. We will not accept that, and members will not accept that.

### Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated contribution from labour. We are not open to a restructuring that turns into **forced layoffs, plant closures, and permanent insecurity**. If the company wants durability, it needs a package built on **shared sacrifice and site protection**, not on unilateral sacrifice by the workforce.

If management is serious, bring forward numbers showing how the savings can be achieved **without closures and without compulsory redundancies**. Then we can talk. If not, you should expect resistance, including industrial action.
