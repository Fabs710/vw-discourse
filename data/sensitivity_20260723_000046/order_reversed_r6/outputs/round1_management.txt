I agree on one essential point: **this must produce a real, durable improvement in competitiveness**. Anything that leaves us with a structurally uncompetitive cost base is not a solution.

But I need to be equally clear on the rest:

### Where I agree
- **Cosmetic compromise is unacceptable.**
- **We need quantified, enforceable savings.**
- **Capacity must come down.**
- **The package must restore margin performance, not just buy time.**

### Where I disagree
To the regional state government: I cannot accept the idea that **plant closures are off the table as a default principle**. If a site is structurally uncompetitive and cannot be made viable through other measures, then protecting it at all costs simply shifts the burden onto the rest of the company. That is not responsible shareholder behavior. It also ignores the fact that we are in a **time-critical cost crisis**.

To the capital markets and the controlling family: I hear the call for durability and credibility, and that is exactly why I am pressing for stronger measures. But I want to be explicit: **“alternative measures” only work if they are large enough and binding enough**. Voluntary gestures, temporary concessions, or soft capacity changes will not move the needle enough. We need a package that actually changes the cost curve through 2030.

### My position
Management is prepared to accept a negotiated solution **if it delivers all of the following**:
- **structural capacity reduction**
- **material labour-cost contribution**
- **binding operational flexibility**
- **credible, permanent improvement in unit costs**
- **a path back to our return-on-sales target**

That means:
- no cosmetic preservation of every existing site and arrangement
- no settlement that leaves German production permanently above target cost
- no agreement that funds today’s overcapacity by sacrificing future investment

### On closures
I am not starting from the premise that closures are desirable. I am starting from the premise that **they may be necessary** if the economics demand it. If the other stakeholders want to avoid closures, then they need to bring forward a package that is **arithmetically equivalent** in savings, enforceability, and speed.

### Bottom line
We need a settlement that is:
- **structural**
- **durable**
- **cost-defensive enough to restore competitiveness**
- **credible to owners and investors**

If the answer is only “protect sites and jobs,” then we will still have the same crisis six months from now, only worse. If the answer is a real contribution from labour and a real reduction in overcapacity, then we have a basis to move.
