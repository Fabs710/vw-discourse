### Core impasse
The main blockage is **not** a lack of willingness to restructure. It is a disagreement over **what counts as an acceptable restructuring instrument**.

More specifically, the impasse sits on three linked points:

1. **Economic equivalence**
   - Investors, management, and the controlling family require proof that a non-closure package delivers savings and durability comparable to closures.
   - Labour, the works council, and the state want job and site protection to remain central, not conditional.

2. **Hardness of the trigger**
   - Some actors see a trigger as a credible safeguard only if it can lead to deeper structural measures.
   - Others will only accept a trigger if it cannot become a backdoor to closures.

3. **Distribution of burden**
   - Management/investors want cost reduction to show up clearly in the P&L.
   - Labour and the state want burden-sharing that does not fall primarily on employees.
   - The state also wants regional legitimacy and industrial continuity preserved.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options that could move the process forward. These are proposals for consideration, not endorsements.

#### 1) Staged non-closure package with a hard review gate
- Implement a **first-stage non-closure package** with measurable savings from working-time changes, wage restraint, overhead cuts, and voluntary exits.
- Set a **near-term review gate** using objective metrics such as EBIT, utilization, and realized savings.
- If targets are missed, the parties must immediately negotiate **additional non-closure measures first** before any closure decision is revisited.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It gives management and investors a testable path, while giving labour and the state a chance to prove that closures are not needed immediately.

---

#### 2) Site-specific protection with differentiated flexibility
- Keep **core sites protected**, but allow **limited, site-specific flexibility** where the cost gap is proven largest.
- Pair that with binding commitments on:
  - redeployment,
  - retraining,
  - voluntary departure programs,
  - and investment in the retained footprint.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It avoids a blanket closure/no-closure binary and creates room for the state and works council to protect the regional base while allowing management to cut costs where the numbers are strongest.

---

#### 3) Tripartite burden-sharing package with quantified contributions
- Require each side to make a **quantified contribution**:
  - workforce: labor-cost moderation, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits,
  - management: overhead and structural simplification,
  - owners: longer payback tolerance and staged returns,
  - state: transition support, retraining, and regional stabilization measures.

- Use one shared model showing:
  - current cost gap,
  - savings by measure,
  - residual gap,
  - and the point where non-closure measures no longer suffice.

**Why it may bridge the gap:**  
It shifts the discussion from “who gives up what” to “what combination closes the gap,” which is the common factual basis all sides have requested.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of recurring annual savings, NPV, and payback would make you willing to support a non-closure package even if it preserves site guarantees through 2030?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**What is the maximum duration of ownership tolerance for a staged non-closure solution before you would require escalation to structural measures?**

#### Regional State Government
**What concrete level of cost reduction and employment protection would make you willing to support a package that includes partial capacity reduction but no plant closures?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What is the exact quantified gap between the proposed non-closure measures and the closure-based alternative, and at what threshold do you judge the non-closure path to have failed?**

#### Industrial Union
**Which specific concessions on working time, pay, or voluntary exits would you accept in exchange for binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitments?**

#### Works Council
**What operational changes, productivity measures, or capacity reductions would you accept as substitutes for closures if they are backed by verifiable plant-level savings?**

#### Restructuring Finance/Costing Specialist
**Can you produce one unified plant-by-plant model that shows the minimum additional savings required for the non-closure package to match the closure scenario on a risk-adjusted NPV basis?**
