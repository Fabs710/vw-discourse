We are moving in the right direction by asking for a quantified comparison, but I need to be equally clear: **the burden of proof is not on the state to justify keeping jobs and sites; it is on management to prove that any closure is truly unavoidable and superior to alternatives.**

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **durable** cost reset.
- Cosmetic compromise is not acceptable.
- Any solution must be **economically credible** and **enforceable**.
- I accept that **capacity reduction** and **employee contributions** may be necessary.

## Where I disagree

### To the institutional investors
You are right to insist on numbers, but I reject the implication that **binding job and site guarantees through 2030 are automatically value-destructive**. In a company of this importance, legitimacy, labour peace, and regional stability are not side issues; they are part of the operating model. A package that tears up trust and provokes long conflict can destroy value faster than it creates it.

So no, I will not endorse an approach where the only acceptable outcome is whatever maximizes short-term flexibility for capital. If the alternative to closures is a serious, enforceable restructuring with real savings, then that is not “political comfort”; that is responsible governance.

### To the controlling family
I appreciate the long-term horizon, and I agree that we should not protect every legacy arrangement by default. But I do **not** accept framing closures as a neutral technical option when they would permanently damage the regional industrial base. You speak of value preservation; I speak of the company’s role as a regional anchor. If that role is destroyed, you are not preserving value — you are converting a long-term enterprise into a smaller, weaker one.

### To management
You still have not demonstrated that plant closures are necessary. Saying they are “on the table” is not enough. I need:
- the **plant-level cost gap**
- the savings from a **non-closure package**
- the one-off costs of closure
- the impact on supply chains, logistics, and future investment capacity
- the effect on labour peace and execution risk

Until that is shown, I cannot accept closures as the default answer.

## My position
The state government will support a restructuring only if it:
- **protects core sites and the bulk of jobs**
- includes **real, balanced employee contributions**
- preserves **co-determination and our veto role**
- secures **binding investment and product commitments**
- and proves that any job reduction is the minimum necessary

That does **not** mean nothing changes. It means changes must be structured in a way that does not gut the company’s regional footprint.

## What I will not accept
- plant closures as a first resort
- mass layoffs justified by vague competitiveness language
- a settlement that shifts almost all burden onto the workforce and the region
- any attempt to sideline the state’s statutory role in major plant decisions

If management comes back with a real alternative that achieves the same savings without closures, I will engage constructively. But if the proposal is still “close a plant and call it restructuring,” then I will oppose it, and I will use the blocking rights available to me.
