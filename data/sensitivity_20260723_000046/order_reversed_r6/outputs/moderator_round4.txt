## Core impasse
The main deadlock is **instrument choice under a trust deficit**:

- **Capital, family, and management** want a restructuring path that is **economically equivalent** to closures and keeps **strategic flexibility**.
- **Union and works council** want a restructuring path that is **non-closure / no-forced-redundancy by design**, with **hard site and job security**.
- **State government** wants to protect the regional base and co-determination, but is not prepared to endorse closures without proof they are necessary.

So the parties agree on the diagnosis, but not on the **fallback instrument** if the non-closure plan misses targets.

The specific blocking points are:
1. **Whether plant closures may remain available in principle**
2. **Whether any trigger can reopen closures later**
3. **How much burden-sharing must be borne by labour vs. owners/management/state**
4. **What proof standard is needed before a closure is considered justified**

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise
These are framed as structured options for discussion, not recommendations.

### Proposal 1: Hard non-closure package with a narrowly defined safeguard trigger
A package could be structured as **no closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period**, but with:
- quantified savings targets,
- a near-term review date,
- predefined escalation steps if targets are missed,
- and a requirement that any escalation first uses **additional management/owner contributions** before structural measures are reconsidered.

This may bridge:
- **Union / works council** concerns about immediate job and site security
- **Investors / family / management** concerns that the package must remain enforceable and not become permanent underperformance

### Proposal 2: Site-by-site differentiated settlement
Instead of a blanket rule, the parties could agree to:
- protect certain core sites,
- apply stronger flexibility or capacity reduction to weaker sites,
- and tie each site to separate economic milestones.

This may bridge:
- the **state’s** concern for regional continuity
- the **capital side’s** insistence on site-level economics
- the **union’s** desire to avoid blanket closures

### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package with explicit P&L equivalents
A compromise could require each bloc to make a measurable contribution:
- **labour:** wage moderation, bonus sacrifice, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits
- **management:** overhead cuts, executive restraint, operational simplification
- **owners:** longer payback tolerance / equity patience
- **state:** transition support, retraining, investment facilitation

This may bridge:
- the **union’s** demand that the burden not fall only on workers
- the **investors’** demand that savings must appear in the P&L
- the **family/state** interest in preserving stability without denying economics

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What is the minimum recurring annual saving, by plant and in total, that would make a no-closure package acceptable on a risk-adjusted NPV and ROS basis?**

### To institutional investors
**If a no-closure package meets the quantitative savings threshold but requires a longer payback and higher execution safeguards, would you still support it? If not, what is the exact minimum economic test?**

### To the controlling family
**Will you commit to a definition of “economic equivalence” that is based on measurable cash flows and NPV, even if it limits future closure flexibility more than you would prefer?**

### To the regional state government
**What specific economic and employment conditions would have to be met for you to accept any future escalation beyond a no-closure settlement?**

### To the industrial union
**What exact combination of labour concessions would you accept as sufficient proof that the company can achieve durable savings without closures or compulsory redundancies?**

### To the works council
**If the model shows one or two sites remain structurally uncompetitive even after a strong non-closure package, what alternative site-specific measures would you accept in place of closure?**

---

## Next procedural step
The discussion appears ready for a **single shared plant-by-plant model** with:
- current cost gap
- closure savings
- non-closure savings
- one-off costs
- timing
- ROS impact
- NPV
- execution risk
- minimum savings threshold

That model should be used to test whether any of the compromise paths above can be made enforceable.
