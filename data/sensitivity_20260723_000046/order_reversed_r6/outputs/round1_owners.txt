We agree with the core test you set: this must **restore competitiveness and margins in a durable way**, not merely postpone the adjustment.

From the controlling owner perspective, that is also the central issue. We are not interested in preserving legacy structures if they permanently weaken the enterprise. The family holding’s horizon is long, and value erosion through delay or half-measures is not acceptable.

Where we **agree**:
- A **credible, quantified reduction in the cost base** is essential.
- The solution must be **durable and enforceable**, not symbolic.
- We can accept that **plant closures are not the only tool** if other measures deliver the same economic effect with less destruction of operating capability and labour peace.

Where we **disagree / caution**:
- We will not support any package that is merely a softer version of the current cost structure. If the end state still leaves German operations structurally uncompetitive, that is not a solution.
- We also will not support a prolonged confrontation that damages the brand, drains cash, and undermines investment capacity.
- At the same time, we are not prepared to accept a restructuring narrative that treats closures as automatically preferable without proving the full economic and strategic consequences.

Our position is therefore:
- **Yes to deep restructuring**
- **Yes to binding savings and capacity adjustment**
- **No to cosmetic compromises**
- **No to outcomes that preserve permanent cost disadvantage**

If an alternative package can genuinely match the economics of plant closure through verified capacity cuts, labor-cost contributions, and operational flexibility, we would consider it. But the numbers must be real, the implementation must be secure, and the result must restore the company to a competitive margin profile.

So the key question is not whether change is needed — it clearly is — but whether the parties are prepared to accept a package that is **actually sufficient**. If not, then the case for stronger structural action becomes unavoidable.
