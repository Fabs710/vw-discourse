I agree with the central premise from management, investors, and the family: **this company needs real restructuring now**. I also agree that we cannot rely on cosmetic delay or vague promises. But I do **not** accept a framing in which plant closures are treated as the default serious option before the alternatives have been fully proven.

## Where I agree
- The cost base is too high.
- The restructuring must be durable, not temporary.
- The board needs a **plant-by-plant quantified bridge**.
- A non-closure package is only credible if it delivers **real, recurring savings**, not symbolic concessions.
- Burden-sharing must be shared across workforce, management, owners, and public stakeholders.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You still have not shown that closures are **necessary** rather than merely available. Saying closures are “on the table” is not the same as proving they are required.

What I need is not another general warning, but a **hard comparison**:
- current cost gap,
- savings from each non-closure measure,
- one-off costs,
- timing of savings,
- execution risk,
- and the resulting ROS under each scenario.

If the non-closure package genuinely fails on those numbers, then we can discuss deeper measures. But you have not earned a closure-first posture in advance.

### To the institutional investors
Your demand for economic equivalence is legitimate. I support that principle. But you are too quick to treat job and site guarantees as automatically value-destructive.

In a German industrial company like this, **labour peace, know-how retention, and regional stability are part of value creation**, not soft extras. A settlement that tears up trust may look efficient on paper and still destroy more value in execution than it creates.

So yes: savings must be durable and quantified. But no: that does not mean every closure option is economically superior from the outset.

### To the controlling family
I recognize the long-term horizon, and I share the view that this cannot be solved by preserving every legacy arrangement. But long-term stewardship also means protecting the industrial base that made this company strong.

I do **not** accept the idea that the family’s role is simply to preserve strategic flexibility at any cost. If the German footprint is permanently weakened, the company loses more than a site — it loses regional legitimacy, industrial depth, and social stability.

### To the union and works council
I hear the insistence on no closures and no forced redundancies. I understand why. But I also need to be honest: if you insist on **full guarantees through 2030** without showing how the savings gap is closed, then you are setting a very high bar.

I am prepared to support strong employment protection **if** it is backed by:
- working-time flexibility,
- wage restraint,
- bonus sacrifice,
- voluntary exits,
- capacity reduction,
- and measurable productivity gains.

But a package that simply says “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” without delivering the required savings will not be enough.

## On the trigger idea
A trigger mechanism can only work if it is **hard, objective, and genuinely binding**. I do **not** support a backdoor closure clause dressed up as a review process.

If there is a trigger, it must first activate:
- additional management contribution,
- additional owner contribution,
- additional non-closure measures,

and only if all of that still fails should deeper structural options be reconsidered. I will not endorse a setup where the workforce gives concessions now and closures remain the hidden default later.

## My position
The regional state government can support a restructuring only if it:
- **protects the core sites and the bulk of jobs**
- preserves our **co-determination and veto role**
- includes **real and measurable employee contributions**
- binds management and owners to meaningful contributions as well
- and proves that any job reduction is the minimum necessary

What I will not support is:
- plant closures as the starting instrument,
- mass layoffs disguised as competitiveness,
- or a settlement that sidelines the state and turns the regional economy into collateral damage.

## What I need next
Before anyone pushes for a final decision, management should present the promised **plant-by-plant bridge** with:
1. current cost gap,
2. savings from non-closure measures,
3. savings from closure options,
4. one-off costs,
5. timing,
6. ROS impact,
7. and the minimum savings threshold at which the non-closure package still fails.

[SUMMON: concise plant-by-plant decision matrix with NPV, payback, ROS impact, execution risk, and the minimum non-closure savings threshold that would let the state support a no-closure package]

## Bottom line
I am open to a serious non-closure restructuring. I am **not** open to a settlement that sacrifices regional employment and industrial continuity before the economics have been fully demonstrated. If the numbers show that a non-closure path can close the gap, preserve the core footprint, and protect the company’s long-term health, I will engage constructively. If not, the burden remains on management to explain why it should be allowed to damage the regional base.
