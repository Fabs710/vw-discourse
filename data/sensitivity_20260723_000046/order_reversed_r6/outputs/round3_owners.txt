We agree on the **objective**: the company needs a **durable cost reset** and must not remain structurally uncompetitive. We also agree that temporary gestures, vague promises, or endless process are not acceptable.

Where I differ from the labour side and, to some extent, the state is on the **constraint set**. A package that insists on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and full guarantees through 2030** is not a serious restructuring test unless it can be shown — quantitatively — to deliver the same savings and the same durability as a stronger structural package. If it cannot, then it is not a protection of value; it is a postponement of adjustment.

### To the Institutional Investors
Your insistence on an economic comparison is correct. I support that. But I do not want this discussion to become a purely theoretical search for a politically painless outcome. The benchmark is simple: **does the package restore competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity over the cycle?** If yes, we can consider non-closure. If not, then we must not tie the company’s hands.

### To Management
You still owe the board a proper bridge:
- current cost gap by site and group,
- savings by measure,
- timing of savings,
- one-off restructuring costs,
- and the effect on return on sales under each scenario.

That is the minimum needed for governance. If the non-closure package closes the gap, it deserves support. If it does not, then we should stop pretending that avoidance of closures is an economic solution.

### To the Industrial Union and Works Council
I hear the concern about labour peace, know-how, and regional stability. Those are real. But stability cannot mean preserving every legacy arrangement regardless of cost. If the workforce wants a non-closure solution, it has to be **material**, not symbolic:
- hard capacity reduction,
- lasting labour-cost reduction,
- credible headcount adjustment,
- and enforceable productivity gains.

What I cannot accept is a package where the company gives up flexibility, but the savings are too small or too temporary to repair competitiveness. That would protect the form of employment while eroding the substance of the business.

### To the Regional State Government
I understand the statutory role and the importance of the regional industrial base. But closures cannot be ruled out in advance if they are economically necessary. The state’s role should be to ensure the decision is disciplined and well evidenced, not to pre-empt it for political reasons.

At the same time, I agree that a solution which ignores labour peace and the regional footprint can destroy value. That is precisely why the alternative package must be real, not merely comforting.

## My governance position
I am open to a **staged non-closure package** only if it includes:
- hard, measurable savings,
- a clear failure trigger,
- and a path to deeper measures if targets are missed.

But I will not support a deal that permanently blocks the company from taking necessary structural action. The family holding’s duty is long-term stewardship, not preservation of every site at any price.

## Bottom line
- **Yes** to real restructuring.
- **Yes** to a non-closure path **if** it is economically equivalent and enforceable.
- **No** to blanket guarantees that freeze the cost base.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the company above target cost and dependent on hope.

If management can bring a quantified package that truly matches the economics, we should evaluate it seriously. If not, then stronger structural measures remain on the table.
