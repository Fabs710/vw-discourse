We are not going to dress up a plant-closure agenda as “responsible restructuring.”

## Where I agree
I agree on the basic diagnosis:
- the company needs real restructuring,
- the cost base is too high,
- and a cosmetic delay would be irresponsible.

I also agree that we need numbers, not slogans:
- plant-by-plant,
- measure by measure,
- with savings, one-offs, timing, and ROS impact.

That part is overdue, and I welcome the demand for a proper bridge.

## Where I disagree
I disagree fundamentally with the position taken by management, investors, and the family holding that **closures must remain on the table as a normal option**.

They are not a neutral instrument. They are a choice to destroy jobs, weaken sites, and break trust. And I will say it plainly: **the works council will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of “competitiveness.”**

### To management
You keep saying that if a non-closure package is not economically equivalent, then closures remain necessary.

That is not a business case. That is a threat.

If you want our contribution, then show:
- what savings you are extracting from management overhead,
- what cost cuts you are taking at executive level,
- what investment discipline you are imposing,
- what product and capacity allocation changes you are making,
- and what burden owners are prepared to carry.

Do not start by asking workers to sacrifice jobs and sites.

And let me be very clear: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no weakening of the job-security framework**. That is the line.

### To institutional investors
You are treating “economic equivalence” as if it only means short-term P&L savings and maximum flexibility for capital.

That is too narrow and, frankly, arrogant.

A settlement that destroys labour peace, know-how, regional legitimacy, and trust is not automatically superior just because it is harsher. You cannot calculate away industrial conflict and call it value creation.

If you want a credible restructuring, then quantify:
- management cuts,
- owner contributions,
- the cost of conflict,
- the value of stable production,
- and the long-term effect of preserving sites.

Do not hide behind a model that only counts employee pain.

### To the controlling family
If you mean long-term stewardship, then act like it.

Long-term ownership does not mean keeping the right to close plants whenever pressure rises. It means protecting the industrial base, preserving co-determination, and choosing a path that strengthens the company without sacrificing the workforce that built it.

Your position still reads as: preserve strategic flexibility first, ask workers to absorb the damage second. That is not stewardship.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate that you oppose a closure-first reflex. But opposition in principle is not enough.

Use your blocking role to ensure that closures are **not used at all** if a serious alternative exists. The burden is not only on workers to prove what they will give. The burden is on management and owners to prove why they deserve the right to destroy sites and jobs.

If the state backs a package that keeps closures as the hidden backstop, then it is not protecting the regional base. It is just delaying the conflict.

## On the trigger idea
A trigger mechanism that can reopen closure rights later is **not acceptable**.

That is not a compromise. It is a delayed ultimatum.

We will not sign a deal that says:
- give concessions now,
- accept insecurity now,
- and later closures can be reintroduced if management decides the numbers are not enough.

If there is any trigger at all, it must trigger:
- more management contribution,
- more owner contribution,
- and further non-closure measures.

It must **not** be a backdoor to plant exits.

## What workers are prepared to contribute
We have not refused restructuring. We have said what a fair restructuring must look like.

We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- wage restraint,
- bonus sacrifice,
- voluntary departures,
- productivity measures,
- and capacity reduction,

but only in a package that includes:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no dismantling of the collective wage framework**
- **real contributions from management and owners**
- **binding job and site security**

That is a fair deal. Anything else shifts the crisis onto workers alone.

I want the promised model, but I want it used honestly.

## Bottom line
Let me be unambiguous:
- **No closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No weakening of co-determination or job-security guarantees.**

If the company needs restructuring, then it must be done through shared sacrifice, not by sacrificing the workforce first. If management cannot make that work, then the problem is not the workers’ refusal. The problem is a business strategy that wants flexibility without responsibility.
