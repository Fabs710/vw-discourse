We appreciate that the discussion is now moving from principles to the actual economics. That is the right direction.

Our position remains unchanged in substance:

## What we agree with
- The company needs **real restructuring**, not delay.
- The current **cost base is too high**.
- Any solution must be **durable, enforceable, and large enough** to restore margins.
- A package can be acceptable **without plant closures** if — and only if — it delivers the same economic result.

## Where we disagree
### To the works council and union
We do **not** accept a solution whose main feature is **binding job and site guarantees through 2030** if those guarantees prevent the company from reaching a competitive cost structure. From an investor standpoint, that is not protection of value; it is locking in underperformance.

We also do **not** accept a package that relies on:
- temporary concessions,
- soft promises,
- delayed savings,
- or protections that make future adjustment impossible.

If the numbers do not work, then the guarantees are not value-preserving — they are value-destructive.

### To the regional state government
We understand the political concern about closures, but we cannot support a position that effectively rules them out **before** the economics are proven. A closure is not desirable in itself, but neither is preserving every site regardless of cost.

What matters is this:
- does the final package restore competitive unit costs?
- does it do so quickly enough?
- is it credible enough to be believed by the market?

If the answer is no, then the company is just buying time.

### To management
We support structural action, but we need the **numbers**. So far, “deep restructuring” remains a direction, not a plan. You need to show:
- the current cost gap,
- the savings contribution from each measure,
- the impact on return on sales,
- and why the proposed package is durable.

Without that, none of the stakeholder positions can be properly evaluated.

## Our bottom line
We will back:
- a **closure-based package** if it is the only way to restore competitiveness, or
- a **non-closure package** if it achieves the same savings with equal durability and enforceability.

What we will **not** back is a settlement that preserves political comfort but leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## What we need next
Before we can move, management should put forward a **side-by-side comparison** of:
1. a closure-based restructuring, and  
2. a non-closure alternative,

with quantified savings, timing, implementation risk, and effect on margins.

If a credible alternative exists, we are open to it. But if the alternative is just a politically convenient compromise with insufficient savings, then we will not support it.
