We welcome the expert input because it moves this discussion from ideology to economics. From a capital-markets perspective, that is exactly where it needs to be.

## Our reading of the expert assessment
The key point for us is simple:

- **non-closure measures can close part of the gap, sometimes materially**
- but **if the structural gap is large, they may not close it fully**
- therefore, the right question is not “closures or no closures” in the abstract, but **which package gets the company back to competitive margins with the best risk-adjusted certainty**

That is the standard we care about.

## Where we agree
### With management
We agree that:
- cosmetic measures are worthless,
- the cost base is too high,
- the footprint has to rationalize,
- and legacy arrangements cannot remain untouchable if they prevent competitiveness.

We also agree that a deal which looks socially neat but leaves the business structurally impaired would be a bad outcome for shareholders.

### With the family
We agree that:
- prolonged conflict destroys value,
- a hollow compromise is unacceptable,
- and restructuring has to be real, quantified, and durable.

### With the state
We agree that:
- a socially explosive solution is bad for execution,
- and industrial peace has value.

### With labour
We agree that:
- the company cannot just push through a destructive conflict and expect value to survive,
- and any employee contribution should be matched by real commitments from management and owners.

## Where we disagree
### On “no closures”
This is where we part company with the works council and union.

An absolute no-closure rule is **not** acceptable from our side. If a plant is structurally uneconomic and alternatives cannot deliver equivalent economics, then closure or deep conversion with real capacity removal has to remain available.

If the company is forced to keep every site open regardless of economics, then the cost base stays uncompetitive and the pain is simply deferred. That is not preservation of value; it is erosion of value.

### On “no compulsory redundancies”
We prefer voluntary solutions. But a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies cannot become an effective veto if voluntary measures fail to close the gap.

If the only way to restore margins is to keep every adjustment voluntary, then the company may end up with no credible restructuring at all. We cannot support that.

### On sequencing
We do **not** accept the idea that binding site and job guarantees must come first, with economics to be proven later.

The company needs:
1. quantified savings,
2. clear capacity reduction,
3. enforceable implementation,
4. and then binding commitments calibrated to that package.

Security without economics is not enough. Economics without execution certainty is also not enough. But the sequencing must be grounded in the numbers.

## What the expert report changes
It changes one thing for us: it confirms that **management must prove the gap**, not simply assert that closures are unavoidable.

That matters.

So we do **not** support pre-emptive closure as a slogan.
We do support closure or deep conversion **if** the quantified comparison shows alternatives cannot deliver equivalent run-rate savings and capacity reduction.

In other words:
- **alternatives must be seriously tested**
- but **they are not automatically sufficient**
- and **they do not get a free pass just because they are less painful**

## What we want to see next
We would like the management team to publish a side-by-side matrix with:
- required annual savings,
- contribution of each lever,
- one-time restructuring cost,
- payback period,
- employment effect,
- and implementation risk.

The matrix should compare at least:
1. closure scenario,
2. deep conversion scenario,
3. negotiated no-closure scenario,
4. hybrid scenario.

That is the minimum needed for investors to assess whether the proposed path is genuinely value-creating.

## Our bottom line
We will back the option that delivers:
- **credible and durable margin recovery**
- **meaningful capacity reduction**
- **disciplined cost reduction**
- **enforceable implementation**
- **and the best risk-adjusted value outcome**

We will **not** back:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that leaves structural overcapacity in place,
- or a compromise that protects every legacy entitlement while impairing the business.

If a no-closure package can truly close the gap, we are open to it.
If it cannot, then harder measures remain justified.

That is our position.
