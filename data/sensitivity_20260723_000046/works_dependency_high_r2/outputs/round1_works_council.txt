We will not accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies in Germany.

The works council’s position is clear:

- Every German plant must remain open.
- No operational layoffs.
- No unilateral breaking of the historic job-security commitments.
- Any restructuring must be negotiated, binding, and socially responsible.
- The workforce must have a genuine co-determination role in shaping the future path, not just be informed after the fact.

We recognize that the company needs a viable future and that competitiveness matters. But we will not let management solve its structural problems by shifting the burden onto employees through closures, mass job cuts, and the destruction of trust. That is neither fair nor sustainable.

If there is a serious alternative, we are prepared to discuss it. That means:
- capacity adjustments without site closures,
- working-time and flexibility solutions,
- training and requalification,
- investment commitments for the German sites,
- and binding guarantees for jobs and locations through 2030.

But let me be equally clear: if the proposal still contains plant closures, forced layoffs, or a one-sided demolition of labour standards, then we reject it. We are prepared to resist that with all available means, including industrial action and full use of our co-determination rights.
