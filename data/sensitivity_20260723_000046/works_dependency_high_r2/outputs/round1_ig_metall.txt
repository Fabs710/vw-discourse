The trade union shares the core concern expressed by the works council: **no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral destruction of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the outset: if management’s restructuring plan relies on making the workforce carry the full burden through closures, forced layoffs, pay cuts, and the removal of hard-won protections, then we will oppose it decisively. That is not a fair restructuring; it is a transfer of risk from owners and management to employees.

Where we **agree**:
- The company needs a viable future.
- The cost base must be addressed.
- Capacity reductions may be part of the answer.
- A negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.
- Investment in German sites, training, and future products is essential.

Where we **disagree**:
- We reject any model that uses plant closures as the starting point.
- We reject compulsory redundancies.
- We reject a one-sided approach where wages, working conditions, and job security are cut while owners and management remain insulated.
- We reject “competitiveness” arguments if they are simply a pretext for hollowing out the German industrial base.

We have already signaled that we are prepared to discuss **shared sacrifice**:
- temporary or negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- working-time and flexibility adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- requalification and deployment measures,
- and possibly foregoing certain bonuses or introducing a workforce contribution, **if** that is matched by firm commitments on jobs, sites, and investment.

But the sequence matters. Before we accept any contribution from employees, we need clear commitments that:
1. German sites remain open,
2. no compulsory redundancies are imposed,
3. the collective-bargaining framework is preserved,
4. management and owners also contribute materially,
5. and the restructuring is tied to a credible long-term industrial plan.

If those conditions are not met, then the union will not stand still. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect us to use the tools available to defend their interests. We are prepared to escalate industrial action strategically if management insists on making workers bear all the pain.

So my message to all sides is this: we are ready for a durable settlement, but only a **fair** one. Shared sacrifice is possible. Socially destructive restructuring is not.
