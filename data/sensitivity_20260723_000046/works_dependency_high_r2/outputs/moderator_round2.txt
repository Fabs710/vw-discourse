## Neutral moderator summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a **real, durable restructuring**, not cosmetic measures;
- the current **cost base and capacity structure are too high**;
- the transition to **e-mobility** and weak market conditions require adjustment;
- **investment in future products, technology, and German sites** is necessary;
- prolonged conflict would be **value-destructive** for everyone;
- any employee contribution should be matched by **management and owner contributions**;
- **negotiation remains preferable** to open-ended industrial conflict.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved disputes are:
- **Plant closures:** works council, union, and state reject closures as a starting point; management, family, and investors keep them as a real fallback or necessary option.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** labour and the state oppose them; management, family, and investors do not accept a blanket ban.
- **Historic guarantees / collective protections:** labour and state want them preserved; management and owners want them revised where they constrain competitiveness.
- **Sequence of concessions:** labour wants binding job/site security first; management and capital want quantified cost relief and capacity reduction first or at least in parallel.
- **Definition of “equivalent” savings:** management and capital insist on economically comparable savings; labour insists those savings must be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Still unresolved are the practical trade-offs between:
- **job and site security vs. capacity reduction**;
- **voluntary measures/work-sharing vs. enforceable workforce adjustment**;
- **preserving historic guarantees vs. restoring competitiveness**;
- **short-term industrial peace vs. rapid cost reduction**;
- **regional industrial base protection vs. footprint rationalization**;
- **employee concessions vs. binding commitments from management, owners, and state**.

No party has yet presented a fully quantified package showing how the required savings and capacity cuts can be achieved **without** closures or compulsory redundancies.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Next round should move from principle to **numbers and sequencing**:
- quantify the savings gap and which measures close it;
- specify what counts as a genuinely **economically equivalent alternative**;
- identify which commitments can be made **binding**, and on what timeline;
- clarify what concessions each side is prepared to make **first**, and what triggers escalation if the gap remains open.

A useful next step would be a side-by-side proposal matrix: **measure / expected savings / impact on jobs and sites / timing / conditions**.
