I hear the red lines, but I need to be equally clear about mine.

We are in a structural crisis, not a temporary downturn. German factory costs are far above target, capacity is too high for the market we face, and the brand is missing its return-on-sales objective. If we respond with language about “shared sacrifice” but leave the cost base fundamentally unchanged, then we have not solved the problem — we have only postponed it.

So let me respond directly:

- **I agree** that the company needs a viable future, investment in German sites, and a negotiated solution rather than chaos.
- **I agree** that the workforce should not be asked to carry the burden alone.
- **I do not agree** that “no plant closures” can be an absolute starting point.
- **I do not agree** that compulsory redundancies are off the table if we cannot achieve a durable alternative.
- **I do not agree** that preserving every historic guarantee is compatible with restoring competitiveness.

What matters is not whether we call it a closure, a conversion, or a deep capacity reduction. What matters is whether the final package delivers a **structural, sustainable reduction in cost and capacity**. Anything less is cosmetic.

To be very explicit: I am prepared to negotiate on the form of the restructuring. That means I am open to alternatives to outright closures if — and only if — they produce comparable, lasting savings. Those alternatives would have to include some combination of:

- genuine capacity cuts,
- meaningful labour-cost reductions,
- changes in working time and flexibility,
- removal or redesign of legacy guarantees,
- and a credible contribution from the workforce.

But I will not sign up to a package that protects every site and every existing entitlement while asking management to fund the future elsewhere. That does not restore competitiveness. It does not protect the investment case for 2030. And it does not give us the margin we need to fund future products and technology.

On the specific points raised:

1. **Plant closures**  
   I am not threatening closures for effect. I am saying they are on the table because the current footprint is too expensive and too large. If the employee side can bring forward a package that achieves the same structural relief without closures, I will consider it. If not, then closures remain a necessary option.

2. **Compulsory redundancies**  
   Nobody wants them. But I will not accept a regime where employment adjustment is blocked in all forms regardless of economic reality. If there is a serious alternative path, we should pursue it. If there is not, we cannot rule out workforce reduction measures that create the required savings.

3. **Collective-bargaining framework**  
   We are not trying to abolish social partnership. We are saying the framework must be fit for the company’s future, not frozen in the past. Legacy arrangements cannot become a veto on survival.

4. **Investment and future products**  
   Yes, there must be a credible industrial plan. But that plan only works if the underlying cost base is competitive. Investment without restructuring is just delay.

So my position is this: I am ready to discuss a durable settlement, but it must deliver real cost and capacity relief. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it needs to bring forward something that is economically equivalent in substance, not just in rhetoric. And it must be on the scale this crisis requires.
