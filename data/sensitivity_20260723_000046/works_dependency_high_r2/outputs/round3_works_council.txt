Let me respond plainly, because the stakes are too high for euphemisms.

## To management
You still have not answered the central point: **why should the workforce accept closures or forced redundancies when you have not shown a full, quantified alternative search?**

You keep speaking about “structural overcapacity” and “competitiveness,” but you still present the hardest measures as if they were the most natural ones. They are not. They are the most destructive ones.

If you want us to discuss real restructuring, then put numbers on the table:
- How much savings are actually required?
- What does each measure contribute?
- What comes from working time, redeployment, internal efficiency, investment shifts, and product allocation?
- What part of the gap is truly impossible to close without destroying sites and jobs?

Until then, “closures remain on the table” is not a strategy. It is a threat.

And I will say it again: **German plants are not a menu of disposable assets.**

## To the controlling family
You say you want a serious restructuring, not a symbolic one. Fine. But your idea of seriousness seems to begin with making the workforce absorb the most irreversible damage first.

That is not stewardship. Stewardship means protecting the industrial base, not sacrificing it for quick margin repair.

If you are genuinely serious about long-term value, then stop treating the historic protections as an obstacle and start treating them as the basis for a durable settlement. A deal built on fear and broken promises will not create trust, only resistance.

## To investors
You are repeatedly demanding “economic equivalence” while ignoring that **social destruction itself creates economic damage**.

You talk about margins, but what about:
- strike risk,
- lost output,
- reputational harm,
- loss of know-how,
- damaged labour relations,
- and the cost of turning this company into a permanent conflict zone?

A restructuring that is “efficient” only on paper and unworkable in practice is not value creation. It is false accounting.

If you want durability, you need to accept that **keeping every German site open and avoiding compulsory redundancies is not a soft preference; it is the precondition for industrial peace and execution certainty**.

## To the regional state government
You are closer to our position than management’s, but let me be very clear: “no unnecessary closures” is not enough.

If the state truly wants to defend the industrial base, it must do more than caution. It must use its governance power to insist on:
- every German plant remaining open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job-security through 2030,
- and a transformation plan built on redeployment, qualification, and investment.

Anything weaker leaves management free to come back with closure blackmail later.

## To the union
We are aligned on the essentials, and I appreciate that. But I want to sharpen one point: any talk of “labour contributions” must not become a gateway to cutting jobs indirectly.

We can discuss:
- temporary moderation,
- flexibility,
- redeployment,
- training,
- and negotiated contributions.

But only inside a framework where:
- all plants stay open,
- no one is forced out,
- and the collective-bargaining framework is not dismantled.

That is the line. If that line is crossed, then it is not a contribution — it is coercion.

## What we insist on
Let me be explicit, because ambiguity is being used against the workforce:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job-security guarantees through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination rights**
- **Protection of the collective wage framework**
- **Capacity adjustment through work-sharing, redeployment, qualification, and product allocation**
- **Investment commitments for German sites**

This is not maximalism. This is the minimum for a legitimate restructuring.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary labor-cost relief,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departure tools only,
- retraining and qualification,
- internal redeployment,
- and site-specific efficiency measures.

But those are not concessions in exchange for closures. They are part of a negotiated transformation **only if the company commits first to keeping every German plant open and ruling out compulsory layoffs**.

## What we reject
We reject:
- “closure” disguised as “conversion,”
- “adjustment” disguised as layoffs,
- “legacy reform” disguised as the destruction of trust,
- and any attempt to turn the workforce’s own security framework into the bargaining chip for management’s mistakes.

## Bottom line
Management still wants flexibility for the company, but none for the workforce. That is not a balanced restructuring. It is one-sided power play.

If you want our support, bring a plan that:
1. keeps every German plant open,
2. rules out compulsory redundancies,
3. protects co-determination and job security,
4. and shows the actual savings path in hard numbers.

Until then, our position does not move.

If the company insists on closures or forced layoffs, then we will resist with everything available to us — in the supervisory board, in collective bargaining, and through industrial action.
