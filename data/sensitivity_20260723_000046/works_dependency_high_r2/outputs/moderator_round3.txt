### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company needs a **real restructuring**, not cosmetic measures.
- There is broad agreement that **financial credibility, durability, and execution certainty** matter.
- Most parties acknowledge that **employee contributions may be part of a package**, but not in isolation.
- There is general support for a **quantified comparison of options** before final decisions.
- The expert input was accepted as useful for framing the issue as a **gap-closure and capacity-adjustment problem**.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:** Management, some investors, and the family insist closures must remain available; works council and union reject closures as a starting point and seek to rule them out in Germany.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Management and capital-side actors want to retain them as a last resort; labour representatives reject them as a bargaining instrument.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour and the state want binding job/site security; management and investors argue such guarantees cannot come before economic proof and may be too rigid.
- **Legacy protections:** Labour sees them as part of the trust architecture; management and investors see them as potentially blocking competitiveness if unchanged.
- **Sequencing and burden of proof:** Labour wants management to prove that destructive measures are unavoidable after alternatives are fully tested; management wants labour to put forward economically equivalent alternatives without preconditions.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are actually required** and from which levers.
- **How much of the gap can be closed** through wages, working time, redeployment, allowances, voluntary exits, and product allocation.
- **Whether non-closure measures can achieve equivalent economics** to closures or deep conversion.
- **The balance between social peace and structural speed**: preserving trust versus acting quickly on overcapacity.
- **The cost of transition** versus the cost of prolonged conflict or delayed restructuring.
- **What counts as an acceptable “conversion”**: a genuine capacity reduction versus a nominal change with limited economic effect.
- **What binding commitments** are feasible on jobs, sites, investment, and co-determination.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to a **shared scenario matrix**. Each side should respond to the same set of questions:
1. What is the **required annual savings gap**?
2. What is the expected contribution of each measure?
3. What are the **one-time costs, payback, and execution risks**?
4. Which measures are **conditional**, and which are **binding**?
5. What level of **job/site security** is compatible with the quantified economics?

The next round should aim to compare **closure, deep conversion, negotiated no-closure, and hybrid options** on the same financial and employment basis.
