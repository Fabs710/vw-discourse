Management, the family, and investors keep repeating “structural savings” as if that alone justifies breaking the social contract. It does not.

Let me be equally direct in Round 2:

## What I agree with
I agree that the company needs a viable future. I agree that the cost base must be addressed. I agree that the transition to e-mobility and the weak European market create pressure. And I agree that cosmetic measures are not enough.

But none of that changes the fact that **the workforce must not be made to pay for management’s strategic errors through plant closures and forced layoffs**.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Plant closures are not an acceptable “option on the table”
Management, the family, and investors keep saying closures are only a fallback. That is not reassuring. Once you put closures on the table, you are already threatening the workforce with irreversible damage. For us, that is not a bargaining chip. It is a red line.

If the company needs structural savings, then achieve them **without closing German plants**. Period.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
I also reject the idea that forced layoffs become legitimate if alternatives are hard. “Economic necessity” is often the language used to normalize what is, in practice, an attack on jobs and livelihoods.

We will not accept a restructuring that treats people as disposable adjustment material.

### 3) Historic guarantees are not a nuisance to be removed
The guarantees were not a decorative promise. They were part of the company’s social model and part of the trust that made long-term cooperation possible. Breaking them now would destroy credibility for years.

If you want workforce contributions, you have to start by respecting the basis of that trust.

## On the arguments from management
Management says it needs “comparable, lasting savings.” Fine. Then show us a package that actually does that **without closures and without forced redundancies**.

That means:
- capacity reduction through reorganization and working-time tools,
- redeployment instead of dismissal,
- qualification and reskilling,
- voluntary instruments only,
- temporary and negotiated labour contributions,
- and real investment commitments for German plants.

What you do **not** get is a blank cheque to shut sites and cut staff simply because it is the fastest route on a spreadsheet.

## On the family and investors
You both talk about long-term value and competitiveness. Then stop treating the German workforce as the main cost problem.

If you want durable savings, contribute through:
- moderated return expectations,
- reinvestment into transformation,
- support for investment in new products and plant modernization,
- and a settlement that preserves industrial peace instead of provoking strikes.

A deal that may look efficient to analysts but tears apart the workforce is not a strong deal. It is short-sighted and unstable.

## On the state
I appreciate that the regional government rejects closures as a starting point. That is the correct stance. But I want to be very clear: “no unnecessary plant closures” is still too weak if it leaves the door open to closures simply because management says so.

The state should use its position to defend:
- every German site,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job-security guarantees,
- and a restructuring that preserves the industrial base.

## What we will support
We are prepared to discuss a real alternative path, but only if it includes:
- **all German plants remaining open**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding guarantees through 2030**
- **capacity adjustments through working time, flexibility, and redeployment**
- **training and qualification**
- **investment commitments**
- **genuine co-determination in shaping the transformation**

That is the minimum basis for a serious discussion.

## What we reject
We reject:
- closures dressed up as “repurposing,”
- layoffs dressed up as “adjustment,”
- attacks on collective protections dressed up as “modernization,”
- and a process where management demands employee concessions before offering any binding security.

## Bottom line
If the company wants a future, it must build that future with its workforce, not against it.

Bring us a restructuring that protects jobs and sites, and we will engage. Bring us a plan built around closures and forced redundancies, and we will resist it with every means available — including industrial action and our full co-determination leverage.
