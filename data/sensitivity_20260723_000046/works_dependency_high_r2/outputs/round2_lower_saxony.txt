I want to be very clear: I understand the pressure management is under, and I do not deny that the company needs a real competitiveness reset. But I do **not** accept a restructuring logic that starts from plant closures and forced redundancies as the default answer.

## To management
I agree that cosmetic measures are not enough. I agree that the current cost base and capacity structure are too high. And I agree that the company needs a durable plan, not a temporary ceasefire.

Where I disagree is your implied sequence: **first threaten closures and layoffs, then ask for trust**. That is the wrong order. If you want support from the state as anchor shareholder, you need to show that you have genuinely exhausted the non-destructive alternatives.

That means a package built around:
- capacity reduction without plant closure wherever possible,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and qualification,
- targeted labour-cost contributions,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

If you can demonstrate that those measures do not close the gap, then we can discuss more difficult options. But you have not earned an automatic license to close German plants.

## To the works council and union
I support your central red line: **German sites are not bargaining chips**. I also support your insistence that the workforce cannot be the only side paying for the transition.

Where I would sharpen the position is this: we should not just say “no” to closures; we should force the discussion onto a concrete alternative that preserves sites and jobs **and** delivers real savings. That is the only way to avoid management claiming there is no alternative.

So I support:
- no compulsory redundancies as a primary instrument,
- no unilateral breaking of job-security commitments,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- training and redeployment,
- and employee contributions only in a negotiated, time-limited, fair framework.

But I will not support a position that simply freezes the structure as it is. We do need adjustment. The question is whether it is done with social responsibility or imposed brutally later.

## To the controlling family
You are right that the company cannot be allowed to drift. But “value protection” cannot mean treating the regional industrial base as expendable. A deal that weakens the company’s roots in Germany and destabilizes the social partnership may look efficient in the short run, but it risks long-term political and industrial damage.

So I agree on the need for hard numbers and discipline. I do **not** agree with any reading that makes closures and workforce cuts the easiest path just because they are the fastest to model.

## To the institutional investors
You are right that the final result must be economically credible. What you understate is that a socially explosive outcome can also destroy value: strikes, political backlash, loss of trust, and long-term damage to execution all have a cost.

A deal that preserves industrial peace, keeps the company anchored in Germany, and still achieves meaningful cost relief is not “soft.” It is strategically superior if it is durable.

## My position
I am prepared to support a negotiated restructuring if it meets these conditions:
1. **No unnecessary plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the first or preferred tool**
3. **Binding protection for regional employment and co-determination**
4. **Real workforce contribution, but matched by management and owners**
5. **A credible industrial plan for future products and investment**
6. **A cost-reduction path that is serious enough to restore competitiveness**

What I will not support:
- a settlement that guts regional employment,
- a package that sidelines the state’s board-level role,
- or a “solution” whose main effect is to shift the burden onto workers and the state while leaving management and owners insulated.

If management wants our support, bring forward a concrete alternative package with numbers, sequencing, and guarantees. Until then, closures remain something I will actively resist, not accommodate.
