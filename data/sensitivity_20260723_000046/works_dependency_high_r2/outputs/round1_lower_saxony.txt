I am listening carefully, and I will be equally direct.

We all agree on one thing: the company needs a viable future. The German sites cannot simply be preserved by wishful thinking, and competitiveness matters. I also recognize that management cannot be expected to solve a structural cost problem with symbolic measures only.

But I must be very clear where I stand.

## Where I agree
- I agree that the restructuring must be **credible, durable, and economically real**.
- I agree that **the workforce cannot be the only side contributing**.
- I agree that the company needs room to invest in future products and technology.
- I agree that a negotiated solution is preferable to a destructive open confrontation.

## Where I disagree
### 1. Plant closures in Germany are not my starting point
As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I cannot support a course that treats German site closures as an ordinary restructuring tool. We are not talking about an abstract portfolio adjustment. We are talking about industrial sites that anchor jobs, suppliers, skills, and regional stability.

If closures are used too readily, the company may gain short-term savings but lose long-term industrial capability, trust, and social legitimacy. That is a cost management often underestimates.

So: I do **not** accept closures as the default answer.

### 2. No compulsory redundancies as a first-line instrument
I also do not accept compulsory redundancies as the easy fallback. Once you cross that line, you damage the social partnership that has long been part of this company’s strength. That may create more conflict than savings.

That said, I am not pretending that adjustment can happen without any workforce contribution. It can. But it must be negotiated, fair, and socially contained.

### 3. “Comparable savings” must be proven, not asserted
Management keeps saying that closures or deep cuts are necessary. I understand the pressure. But if you want the state to accept painful measures, you must demonstrate that every less destructive alternative has been seriously exhausted.

That means:
- capacity reductions short of site closure,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary or negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation programs,
- and a credible investment path for the German plants.

If those measures can achieve the necessary effect, then that is the better route. If not, management must explain the numbers transparently, not simply insist on the harshest option.

## My red lines
Let me state my limits plainly:
- I will not support a restructuring that **guts regional employment**.
- I will not support a settlement that **removes the state’s co-determination and veto role**.
- I will not support a package that sacrifices German sites while leaving strategic commitments vague.
- I will not support a one-sided restructuring where owners and management shift the burden onto the workforce alone.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** if it meets these conditions:
1. **No unnecessary plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the primary instrument**
3. **Binding investment commitments for German sites**
4. **A real workforce contribution, but balanced by contributions from management and owners**
5. **Protection of co-determination and the state’s board-level rights**
6. **A long-term plan that preserves the company as a regional industrial anchor**

That is the direction I can work with. Anything that tries to force through closures first and negotiations later is the wrong sequence.

## To management
If you want support, bring forward a restructuring that is economically serious **and** socially responsible. Show us:
- which savings are needed,
- what alternatives were tested,
- what the workforce contribution would look like,
- what you and the owners are contributing,
- and how jobs, sites, and future investment are secured.

Then we can discuss a real settlement.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that there must be a fair deal, not a unilateral demolition of labour standards. But we also have to be prepared for a solution that includes some burden-sharing if it preserves sites and long-term industrial strength. A total refusal to adjust anything would weaken our position if management can demonstrate hard economic necessity.

So my message is: **I will not help force through closures or mass layoffs, but I am open to a negotiated restructuring that protects the regional industrial base and keeps the company viable.**
