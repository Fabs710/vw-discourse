## Moderator Summary — Round 1

### 1) What has been agreed
Across all parties, there is broad agreement that:
- the company needs a viable long-term future,
- the current cost base and capacity structure are not sustainable,
- cosmetic measures alone are insufficient,
- a negotiated settlement is preferable to open conflict,
- investment in future products, technology, and German sites is important,
- and any restructuring should be durable rather than purely temporary.

There is also broad openness to some combination of:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- labour-cost contributions,
- training/requalification,
- and a defined industrial plan with implementation accountability.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issues are:
- **Plant closures in Germany:** employee representatives and the state reject closures as a starting point, while management, the family, and investors keep them as an option if alternatives do not deliver equivalent savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** unions, works council, and the state oppose them as a primary tool; management, the family, and investors do not rule them out if necessary.
- **Scope of job and site guarantees:** labour and the state seek binding protections, while management and capital providers say historic guarantees cannot remain untouchable if they block restructuring.
- **Burden-sharing:** all sides say contributions should not fall only on employees, but they differ on what counts as a fair and sufficient contribution from each side.
- **Acceptable form of restructuring:** labour wants socially responsible adjustment without closures or forced layoffs; management and shareholders want a package that produces clear, lasting cost and capacity relief.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several trade-offs remain unresolved:
- **How much savings are required** to restore competitiveness, and how this is quantified.
- **Which measures can deliver equivalent durable savings** without closures or forced redundancies.
- **How to balance job and site security against structural cost reduction.**
- **What each stakeholder group is prepared to contribute** in concrete terms.
- **Whether investment commitments can be linked to workforce concessions** in a binding package.
- **What the fallback is** if negotiations fail to close the cost gap.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to specifics:
- Ask management to present the minimum viable restructuring package with quantified savings.
- Ask labour and the state to specify what non-closure alternatives they would accept and under what conditions.
- Identify which elements are negotiable, which are time-limited, and which require binding guarantees.
- Test whether there is a package that achieves durable competitiveness **without** plant closures or compulsory redundancies, or whether that gap remains too large.

The discussion is now at the point where concrete numbers, sequencing, and enforceable commitments are needed.
