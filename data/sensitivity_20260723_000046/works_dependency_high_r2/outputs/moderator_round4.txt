## Core impasse

The plateau is centered on **whether the restructuring can be completed without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies**.

That disagreement has three layers:

1. **Economic layer**  
   Management and investors/family/state want proof of a durable cost and capacity reduction. Labour wants proof that non-destructive measures are not enough before any irreversible steps are discussed.

2. **Sequencing layer**  
   Labour wants: quantify the gap first, then test alternatives, then discuss residuals.  
   Management wants: retain closure and forced-redundancy options as live fallback tools while the comparison is being built.

3. **Governance layer**  
   The state, family, and investors differ on how far they are willing to pre-commit the company. Labour wants binding protection through 2030; management and capital want flexibility if the gap remains large.

In short: **the impasse is not “restructuring vs no restructuring,” but “binding protection first vs optionality first.”**

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### 1) “Binding protection with hardship trigger”
A compromise package could include:
- **no compulsory redundancies through 2030**
- **no German plant closures through 2030**
- full use of voluntary tools, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and productivity measures
- a narrowly defined **hardship trigger** requiring:
  - independent verification of a residual gap,
  - a second round of negotiation,
  - supervisory-board review,
  - and enhanced employee protections before any exceptional deviation

This bridges labour’s need for binding security with management’s concern that it cannot be locked into absolute rigidity if the business case changes materially.

### 2) “Phased restructuring with decision gates”
Instead of deciding closure vs no closure now, the parties could agree to:
- Phase 1: quantified no-closure package
- Phase 2: implementation and quarterly review of savings
- Phase 3: only if targets are missed, a jointly defined escalation process for deep conversion measures

This allows management to test whether the non-destructive package closes enough of the gap, while labour gets the assurance that closures are not the default first move.

### 3) “Shared-sacrifice package tied to enforceable investment and product commitments”
A third bridge would combine:
- temporary wage moderation / flexibility / allowances reform from labour
- binding investment, product allocation, and training commitments from owners and management
- explicit no-forced-redundancy and site-protection commitments through 2030

This reframes the dispute from “who gives up jobs?” to “what does each side put into a durable industrial plan?”

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council / labour
**What exact measurable savings threshold would make you accept a jointly verified hardship review in 2027 or 2028 without triggering an automatic closure discussion?**

### Industrial Union
**Are you prepared to trade temporary wage and working-time concessions for a legally binding no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy package through 2030, and if so, what are your minimum enforceable safeguards?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to a fully quantified no-closure scenario first, with closures and compulsory redundancies excluded from the initial comparison matrix, if that matrix includes a defined escalation point for unmet targets?**

### Regional State Government
**Will you condition any political or governance support on a binding site- and job-protection package, or do you reserve the right to support a plan that includes closures if the numbers warrant it?**

### Controlling Family
**Are you prepared to instruct the board to prioritize a no-closure settlement through 2030, provided the savings gap is independently verified and the package contains review and hardship mechanisms?**

### Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of annual run-rate savings and execution certainty would make you accept a no-closure package even if it leaves some residual capacity in Germany?**

### Expert in restructuring finance/operations
**What verified residual gap percentage would, in your view, make a no-closure package structurally insufficient even after full use of voluntary exits, flexibility, and product shifts?**
