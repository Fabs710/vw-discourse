As the regional state shareholder, I will be equally clear:

I **do** agree that the company needs a durable restructuring. I **do** agree that the current cost base, overcapacity, and competitive pressure cannot simply be ignored. And I **do** agree that a credible solution must be structural, not cosmetic.

But I **strongly disagree** with any route that starts from plant closures in Germany as the default answer. For us, closures are not a neutral technical instrument; they are a decision with lasting consequences for employment, suppliers, regional stability, and the company’s identity as an industrial anchor. That is exactly why we hold a blocking minority and why co-determination exists.

### Where I align with the other sides
- **With the workforce:** job security, site security, and a fair burden-sharing approach are legitimate demands.
- **With management:** the company needs real savings, not postponement.
- **With both:** the restructuring must be durable and linked to investment in future products and technology.

### Where I differ sharply
Management says closures are “on the table” because they are among the few instruments that can achieve savings. I do not accept that as a starting principle. Before any closure is discussed, the company must exhaust and credibly demonstrate alternatives that preserve industrial capacity in Germany:
- capacity reduction through coordinated production shifts,
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- and management and owner-side contributions as well.

If the answer is simply “the workforce must pay, sites must close, and guarantees must disappear,” then that is not a restructuring I can support. It would shift the entire adjustment burden onto employees and the region while weakening the company’s long-term social license.

### My line
I am **open to a negotiated restructuring** if it:
- preserves the core German sites,
- avoids compulsory redundancies,
- provides binding employment and investment commitments,
- and shares the burden fairly across management, owners, and workforce.

I am **not** open to a package that:
- guts regional employment,
- removes the state’s co-determination and veto role,
- or treats plant closures as the easy path to competitiveness.

### What I expect next
If management wants my support, I expect a concrete alternative package, not just a demand for acceptance:
- measurable savings,
- site and job protection,
- a credible competitiveness plan,
- and a social-partnership framework that keeps the company viable in the long run.

So my position is straightforward:  
**Yes to restructuring. No to a closure-led strategy unless every serious alternative has been shown to fail.**  
And even then, any move in that direction would have to be exceptionally justified, socially balanced, and politically defensible.
