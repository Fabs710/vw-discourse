### Core impasse
The main deadlock is over **whether structural savings can be achieved without plant closures and compulsory redundancies**.

More precisely, the parties are split on four linked issues:

1. **Red lines vs. flexibility**
   - Works council and union set “no closures / no forced redundancies / job guarantees” as non-negotiable.
   - Management, family, and investors insist those instruments must remain available if savings are not otherwise sufficient.

2. **Burden-sharing**
   - Employee side wants visible management/owner contributions and legal/site guarantees.
   - Ownership and management argue employee concessions must be materially larger and more durable.

3. **Sequence**
   - Employee side wants alternatives proven first, before closures are used as leverage.
   - Management/owners want hard measures kept on the table from the outset.

4. **Definition of “equivalent”**
   - Investors/family ask for “economic equivalence” to closures/redundancies.
   - Employee side sees temporary concessions plus guarantees as sufficient only if they truly preserve jobs and sites.

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options to test whether a settlement is possible:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a “closure last-resort” trigger
A package could be structured in two phases:
- **Phase 1:** capacity reductions, working-time measures, redeployment, qualification, voluntary tools, and temporary employee contributions.
- **Phase 2:** a narrowly defined review point with objective triggers only if savings and capacity targets are not met.

Key feature:
- Closures and compulsory redundancies are **not removed forever**, but they are **deferred and constrained by measurable failure criteria**.
- This may allow the employee side to avoid immediate closure pressure while giving management an enforcement mechanism.

#### 2) No compulsory redundancies, but stronger voluntary exit and redeployment architecture
A compromise could combine:
- a formal commitment to **no compulsory redundancies**,
- with a stronger package of **voluntary exits**, early retirement bridges, internal transfer guarantees, retraining, and cross-plant redeployment,
- plus a quantified capacity reduction plan.

Key feature:
- This shifts adjustment away from forced layoffs while still producing headcount reduction and cost savings.
- It would need binding targets and a credible financing framework from management/ownership.

#### 3) Site-preservation pact tied to measurable labor and owner contributions
A settlement could include:
- **site guarantees for defined German plants**,
- **time-limited employee contributions**,
- **management compensation restraint and owner-side distribution restraint**,
- and **binding investment commitments** for product and technology programs through 2030.

Key feature:
- This makes preservation of sites conditional on a shared-sacrifice package.
- It may bridge the works council/state concern for industrial continuity and the capital side’s demand for real savings.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
What exact savings gap must be closed, by when, and what is the minimum package that would let you rule out plant closures as economically unnecessary rather than merely politically undesirable?

#### To the Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies remain absolute red lines, what specific, quantified employee-side contribution would you accept that still delivers the cost reduction management says it needs?

#### To the Industrial Union
Would you accept a binding voluntary-redeployment and early-exit framework with measurable headcount reduction, even if it preserves sites but does not preserve every current job in its exact form?

#### To the Regional State Government
What concrete leverage will the state use to keep negotiations focused on industrial continuity, and what objective conditions would lead you to support a tougher restructuring step if alternatives fail?

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to commit visible owner-side sacrifices—such as limits on distributions or additional capital commitments—in exchange for a no-closure settlement that delivers verified, durable savings?

#### To Institutional Investors
What financial metrics and governance safeguards would be sufficient for you to support a settlement that avoids closures but relies on substantial redeployment, voluntary exits, and time-limited concessions?
