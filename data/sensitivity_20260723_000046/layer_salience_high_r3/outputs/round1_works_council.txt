As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of the burden, and management cannot now solve strategic mistakes by destroying jobs, breaking trust, and hollowing out the company’s German industrial base. A restructuring that is built on closure threats and forced layoffs is not a viable path to a sustainable future.

What we are prepared to discuss is a serious alternative package that protects sites and employment:
- binding guarantees for all German plants,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a credible plan to improve competitiveness through capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, training, and negotiated employee contributions,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the transformation.

If management wants support for a restructuring, it must come with security: job security, site security, and investment security through 2030. Anything less is a one-sided demand that shifts the entire crisis onto employees.

We are ready to negotiate hard on how to secure the company’s future. We are not ready to negotiate away people’s livelihoods.
