I will respond directly and without softening the core issue.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding job and site security,
- and a restructuring that does not turn the workforce into the shock absorber for management mistakes.

I also agree with you that “closure as last resort” is not neutral. If it stays on the table as a standing threat, it poisons trust and undermines any serious willingness to contribute. That part of your argument is right.

Where I would sharpen our common position is this: we should not allow “capacity reduction” or “voluntary tools” to become a backdoor for forced downsizing. If we discuss headcount reduction, it must be through voluntary, internal, and protected instruments only, with enforceable safeguards.

## To management
I agree with you on one point: the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic delay. You are also right that the current cost base and overcapacity cannot simply remain unchanged.

But I do **not** accept your conclusion that this requires keeping closures and compulsory redundancies as ordinary bargaining instruments. You present that as realism; from our side it looks like a demand that workers pre-pay the restructuring while management retains the right to escalate to plant destruction if we do not concede enough.

That is not a fair starting point.

You say time-limited concessions are insufficient if the structural gap remains. Fair enough. Then bring a package that closes the gap with:
- working-time measures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and visible management/owner-side burden-sharing.

If that is not enough, then you need to show why in numbers, not by threat.

## To the regional state
I appreciate your effort to keep the discussion focused on industrial continuity. That matters. But “only if alternatives fail” is still too weak if management is using closures as leverage from the outset.

If the state wants to preserve the regional industrial base, it should not normalize plant closures as a standard fallback. It should use its influence to force a serious alternative package first, with binding safeguards, not open-ended pressure.

## To the controlling family
You say you want a deal that is durable and value-preserving. Then you must accept that value is not only return on capital. Value also includes:
- stable industrial capability,
- skilled workforces,
- reliable production networks,
- and social peace.

If you insist that workers carry the burden while ownership contributes only rhetoric, then you are not asking for shared sacrifice. You are asking for unilateral sacrifice.

That will not get our support.

## To the investors
You want economic equivalence. We understand that. But equivalence cannot mean “same savings, different wording” if the result is still insecurity for the workforce and no visible contribution from management and owners.

A deal is only credible if it is:
- materially fair,
- durable,
- and balanced across all stakeholder groups.

## Our position in this round
Let me restate it clearly:

We are prepared to discuss a settlement that includes:
- a quantified employee contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage moderation or bonus sacrifice,
- redeployment,
- training,
- and capacity adjustments,

**if and only if** it is built on:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **Visible management and owner contributions**
5. **Real investment commitments for future products and technology**

That is not an opening offer to be chipped away. It is the minimum basis for industrial peace.

## What we reject
We reject:
- closures as a standing option,
- forced layoffs,
- hidden downsizing through “voluntary” mechanisms,
- and a package where employees pay most of the price while the top merely approves it.

If the company is serious about a negotiated settlement, it must stop treating workforce insecurity as an acceptable tool of governance.

## A concrete path we can discuss
If management wants a real alternative, then the discussion should move to:
- a fixed capacity-reduction target through product and shift realignment,
- a voluntary exit and retirement bridge program,
- redeployment across the German network,
- a time-limited wage contribution and bonus restraint,
- management-side compensation restraint,
- and a binding investment and site-security agreement through 2030.

That is the kind of shared-sacrifice package we can bargain on.

## Bottom line
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking restructuring by closure threat and forced layoffs.

If the company wants our cooperation, it must come with a fair, durable, and balanced package that protects jobs and sites and shares the burden across management, owners, and workforce.

If not, then do not expect us to help normalize job destruction in the name of competitiveness.
