I will engage directly and plainly.

### To management
I agree with one part of your diagnosis: the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a postponement. I also agree that we need real savings, not symbolism.

But I do **not** accept your framing that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain normal tools on the table as if the burden of proving otherwise lies only with the workforce. In Germany, that is not just a labor issue; it is a governance issue, a co-determination issue, and a question of whether management is serious about a negotiated industrial future.

What I reject is this implicit logic:
- employees are asked for concessions,
- employees are told to accept pain up front,
- and only then might the company consider alternatives.

That is the wrong sequence.

If you want the union to consider a fair contribution, then show us a package that delivers the needed structural effect **without** forcing layoffs and **without** closures. Not a vague intention. Not “we’ll see.” A concrete plan.

What we can discuss seriously:
- temporary and time-limited wage contributions,
- bonus waivers or deferrals,
- working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- capacity adjustments through shift, volume, and product allocation,
- and productivity measures that are not just code for job destruction.

What we cannot accept:
- compulsory redundancies,
- German plant closures,
- using the collective wage framework as the adjustment shock absorber,
- or open-ended insecurity dressed up as competitiveness.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not just a slogan; it is the minimum condition for industrial peace.

Where I would sharpen the point is this: we should not let “capacity reduction” become a euphemism for headcount destruction by another route. If we discuss capacity adjustments, they must be tied to:
- clear workload planning,
- protected employment,
- transparent redeployment,
- and enforceable guarantees.

I support the works council’s demand for binding site and job security through 2030, but I want to be equally clear: any employee contribution must be **balanced, time-limited, and matched by management and owner-side concessions**. The burden cannot land only on the workforce.

### To the regional state
I appreciate your rejection of closures as a default. That matters. But “only after alternatives fail” is not enough comfort for the workforce when management is already signaling closures as leverage. We need the public stakeholder side to use its influence to force real alternative planning now, not after industrial conflict has escalated.

If the state wants to help stabilize the company, then it should push for:
- job preservation,
- investment commitments,
- training and transformation funding,
- and a negotiated solution that avoids social ruin.

### To the controlling family and investors
You keep saying the alternative must be “economically equivalent.” Fine. But equivalence cannot mean that workers absorb all the pain while owners and management contribute only rhetoric.

A fair package must include visible contributions from the top:
- restraint on dividends or capital distributions if needed,
- management-side cost discipline,
- investment commitments to the German sites,
- and a genuine willingness to preserve industrial capability rather than treat it as a line item to cut.

If your only acceptable path is one that preserves returns by cutting jobs and shutting plants, then you are telling us that employees are the adjustment variable of last resort. That is precisely what we will resist.

### Our bargaining position
We are prepared to negotiate a **shared-sacrifice package** if it includes all four of these elements:

1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures in Germany**
3. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**
4. **Binding guarantees for jobs, sites, and future investment**

If management brings forward a credible package with real savings and fair burden-sharing, we can discuss it. If not, then industrial action remains on the table and members will not accept being the only side that pays.

### Bottom line
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing a restructuring that is financed by insecurity, closures, and forced job losses.

Show us a serious alternative that protects employment and shares the burden fairly, and we are ready to bargain.  
If not, then there will be no stable settlement from our side.
