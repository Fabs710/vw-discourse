I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me be equally direct.

As the controlling shareholder, my overriding concern is not preserving every existing structure at any cost, but protecting the long-term value, competitiveness, and governance stability of the enterprise. A company that cannot restore margins will erode value for everyone — owners, employees, region, and capital markets alike.

### Where I agree
- **Management is right** that the company needs a durable structural solution, not a delay tactic.
- **The workforce and state are right** that a purely punitive approach would be destabilizing and would damage the company’s social license.
- **All sides are right** that this must be more than a cosmetic reshuffle; it has to change the economics of the business.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the position that “no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies” can be a hard starting line if it prevents a real competitiveness reset. If that line means the company is locked into an uncompetitive cost base, then it is not stewardship — it is drift.

At the same time, I also do **not** support a blunt closure-first strategy if it creates prolonged industrial conflict, reputational damage, or irreversible damage to the enterprise. Destroying the company’s labor peace to force a point would also be value-destructive.

### My view on what is required
Any acceptable settlement must satisfy three conditions:

1. **Material and durable cost improvement**  
   The package must close the structural gap, not just postpone it.

2. **Labor peace and execution certainty**  
   A deal that triggers repeated strikes, legal blockage, or ongoing confrontation is not a solution.

3. **Protection of core long-term value**  
   The company’s industrial base, technology path, and governance stability must remain intact.

### What I am prepared to support
I am open to a negotiated package that includes:
- capacity reduction,
- workforce flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- a meaningful but balanced employee contribution,
- and targeted site adjustments if they are unavoidable and clearly justified.

I am **not** prepared to endorse a package that merely preserves all structures and shifts the burden into temporary measures while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

### What I need to see
If the employee side wants to avoid harsher measures, it must help produce a package that is genuinely equivalent in economic effect. That means:
- measurable labor cost relief,
- credible capacity reduction,
- binding execution,
- and a settlement durable enough to survive beyond the next bargaining round.

If management wants my backing, it must show that it is pursuing restructuring in a way that is economically real, operationally executable, and politically survivable. I will not support a plan that endangers ownership value through a prolonged showdown, but I also will not support an arrangement that leaves the company stuck.

So my position is this:  
**I will back a tough restructuring if it is credible, durable, and value-preserving. I will not back denial of the problem, and I will not back a conflict spiral.**  

Bring forward a package that actually restores competitiveness. If it is strong enough, I will support it through governance. If it is not, then we are simply delaying the inevitable.
