## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is this:

- **Employee side / state:** wants restructuring **only on a no-closure, no-forced-redundancy basis**, with binding guarantees through 2030.
- **Management / shareholders:** want to keep **closures and compulsory redundancies as credible fallback options** unless alternatives prove economically equivalent.
- **Specialist:** says a no-closure package can work only if it delivers roughly **70–80% of closure-case recurring savings** and real capacity reduction; otherwise it is likely not durable.

So the conflict is over **the acceptable failure mechanism** and **the threshold of economic equivalence**.

---

## Three possible compromise positions

### 1) Time-limited no-closure pact with quantified savings test
A structured compromise could be:

- **No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a fixed period**  
- In return, the employee side delivers a package of:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - working-time reductions,
  - redeployment,
  - bonus restraint,
  - and productivity measures.
- A **jointly agreed savings target** is set plant by plant.
- At the review point, if the package reaches a defined threshold, the no-closure pact continues; if not, parties re-enter talks with escalation rules.

This bridges:
- employee demand for immediate protection,
- management demand for measurable performance,
- state demand for industrial continuity.

---

### 2) No closures, but durable capacity reduction and a conditional review clause
A second compromise could be:

- **All German sites stay open**, but
- the company gets permission to implement:
  - shift reductions,
  - product consolidation,
  - indirect-function cuts,
  - and internal footprint shrinking.
- The employee side accepts **stronger operating flexibility** in exchange for:
  - no closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - and binding investment commitments.
- A **conditional review clause** activates only if volume or margin deterioration reaches predefined external thresholds.

This bridges:
- labor’s no-closure line,
- management’s need for real capacity reduction,
- investors’ demand for durability.

---

### 3) Hybrid settlement: no closures now, but a narrowly defined hard-measures trigger
A third option is:

- **Immediate moratorium on closures and forced layoffs**
- A fixed transition period for restructuring through voluntary tools only
- A **strict trigger mechanism** based on pre-agreed metrics, such as:
  - annual savings shortfall,
  - utilization collapse,
  - or a verified market shock
- If triggered, the parties must first exhaust further negotiated measures before any hard action is considered.

This bridges:
- the workforce’s need to remove the immediate threat,
- management’s insistence that hard measures cannot be abolished forever,
- the state’s interest in avoiding premature site destruction.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are non-negotiable, **what minimum plant-by-plant savings and capacity reductions would you accept as sufficient to justify a durable 2030 pact?**

### To the Industrial Union
Would you support a **time-limited no-closure agreement** if it is paired with measurable savings, investment commitments, and a jointly monitored capacity-reduction plan?

### To Group & Brand Management
Will you place on the table a **full residual-gap calculation** showing exactly how much remains after voluntary tools, and agree that hard measures are only revisited if that gap is still material?

### To the Regional State Government
Are you prepared to use your influence to back a **binding industrial continuity package** if it includes measurable savings and transparency, even if it restricts management’s fallback options for a defined period?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Will you commit to **visible owner-side burden-sharing**—for example on compensation, distributions, or transition capital—if that is the price of a no-closure settlement?

### To Institutional Investors
What specific **return-on-sales or recurring-savings threshold** would make you view a no-closure package as economically sufficient rather than merely politically preferable?

### To the Expert / Restructuring Economist
What exact combination of measures would get the package closest to the **70–80% equivalence band** you described, without relying on closures or compulsory redundancies?
