The union shares the core line of the Works Council: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. That is non-negotiable for us as well.

I also want to be very clear: we do **not** deny the economic pressure. We know the company faces a tough market, higher cost base, and the need to adapt. But restructuring cannot mean that the workforce carries all the pain while management, owners, and the capital side remain untouched. That is not a fair transformation; it is a transfer of risk onto employees.

Where we agree:
- The company needs a durable competitiveness solution.
- Capacity adjustments may be necessary.
- We are prepared to discuss an employee contribution.
- Working-time measures, internal redeployment, qualification, and flexibility instruments are all on the table.

Where we do **not** agree:
- Plant closures as a restructuring tool.
- Forced layoffs.
- Any erosion of the collective wage framework as a one-sided fix.
- Open-ended insecurity for the workforce.

If management wants a settlement, it must be based on **shared sacrifice** and not just workforce sacrifice. That means:
- binding site and job security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a negotiated contribution from employees that is time-limited and balanced,
- real concessions from management and owners as well,
- and investment commitments for the German sites and future products.

We have already shown willingness to negotiate constructively. But if the response is simply “accept closures and job cuts,” then we will resist that with all available means. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect action, not lectures about competitiveness.

So my message is simple: we are ready for a serious deal, but it must protect jobs, protect sites, and distribute the burden fairly. Without that, there will be no stable settlement.
