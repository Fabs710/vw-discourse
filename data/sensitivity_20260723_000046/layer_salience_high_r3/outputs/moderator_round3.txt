## Core impasse

The plateau is centered on one unresolved question:

**Can the company achieve a sufficiently durable cost reset without treating plant closures and compulsory redundancies as standing options during the negotiation?**

That splits the stakeholders into two camps:

- **Employee-side actors (Works Council, Union, State in part)**: insist that closures and forced layoffs must be ruled out as bargaining tools, and that any restructuring must be bounded by job/site guarantees and burden-sharing from owners and management.
- **Management, controlling family, investors**: insist that they cannot pre-commit to “no closures / no compulsory redundancies” unless an alternative package produces **economically equivalent** savings, speed, and durability.

The specialist input sharpened the impasse:
- alternatives can likely replace **a substantial share** of savings,
- but not necessarily the **full fixed-cost reset** of closures if overcapacity is structural.

So the negotiation is stuck between:
1. **employment-security guarantees**, and
2. **management’s need for credible structural flexibility**.

---

## Concrete compromise positions

### 1) Quantified no-closure test phase with a fail-safe review
**Proposal:**  
Adopt a time-limited restructuring phase in which:
- closures and compulsory redundancies are **not used during the phase**,
- the company implements voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, and investment-linked consolidation,
- a jointly agreed KPI set tracks savings, utilization, and execution,
- and a **joint review date** is set to assess whether the alternative package closes the gap.

**Bridge effect:**  
This gives the employee side a real no-closure period and gives management a defined escape hatch only after quantified evidence, not as an open threat.

---

### 2) Site-security pact with conditional capacity realignment
**Proposal:**  
Negotiate a pact that includes:
- **no German plant closures** for a defined period,
- **no compulsory redundancies** except under a narrowly defined failure mechanism,
- binding investment commitments,
- workforce contributions via temporary wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexible working time,
- and a jointly agreed capacity-reduction plan achieved through voluntary exits, attrition, and internal redeployment.

**Bridge effect:**  
This preserves the employee-side red lines while still acknowledging that some capacity reduction is necessary.

---

### 3) Burden-sharing package with escrowed commitments
**Proposal:**  
Set up a package where:
- employee contributions are time-limited and quantified,
- management/owner contributions are equally quantified,
- investment commitments and site-security commitments are placed into a **binding implementation framework**,
- and any move toward hard measures requires a supermajority decision or an external arbitration trigger.

**Bridge effect:**  
This addresses the recurring complaint that the burden falls mainly on labor and creates symmetry between concessions and commitments.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**If a no-closure package met the quantified savings target and included enforceable investment, redeployment, and employment safeguards, what specific mechanism would you accept for verifying that the company is not silently eroding site capacity?**

### Industrial Union
**Would you support a time-limited phase in which closures and compulsory redundancies are paused, provided the company agrees to binding savings milestones and an external review trigger if those milestones are missed?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings gap remains after voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, and owner/management contributions are applied, and at what threshold would you conclude that hard measures are truly unavoidable?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete governance lever are you prepared to use to keep the company on a no-closure track while still ensuring the restructuring remains economically credible to the board?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What visible owner-side contribution are you prepared to commit so that “economic equivalence” is not perceived as labor bearing the entire burden?**

### Institutional Investors
**What minimum combination of savings, timing, and execution certainty would make a no-closure restructuring acceptable to you as a value-preserving outcome?**

### Industrial restructuring specialist / workforce-transition economist
**What threshold of labor-cost reduction and fixed-cost reduction would, in your view, make a no-closure package materially comparable to a closure-led scenario?**
