Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

I acknowledge one point from both of you: this company does need a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one. We are not looking for a short-lived patch. The cost base in Germany is too high, the market is shrinking, and the brand is not where it needs to be. If we do not structurally restore competitiveness now, we will damage the company far more severely later.

But I have to reject the central premise of your position: **“no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no structural break.”** That is not a credible answer to the scale of the problem.

### Where I agree
- We need a negotiated settlement, not endless confrontation.
- We need investment in future products, technology, and transformation through 2030.
- We need measures that preserve operational capability and avoid unnecessary destruction of know-how.
- Capacity adjustments, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and training can and should be part of the package.

### Where I disagree
What I cannot accept is a package that preserves every site, preserves every guarantee, and merely shifts the burden into temporary measures while the cost problem remains unresolved. That would leave us uncompetitive and would fail the fiduciary duty to the company, the owners, and the long-term future of the workforce itself.

So let me state the management position plainly:

- **German overcapacity must be reduced.**
- **Labour costs must come down structurally.**
- **We need a meaningful workforce contribution.**
- **We need flexibility on site structure and headcount.**

I am not saying closures are a political slogan. I am saying they are on the table because they are one of the few instruments that can deliver real, durable savings at the scale required. If you want me to consider alternatives, they must be **equivalent in effect**, not just softer in language.

That means:
- no model that keeps all plants open and simply spreads pain more slowly;
- no deal that avoids compulsory redundancies in name but leaves the company carrying the same excess cost;
- no arrangement that protects guarantees without delivering margin improvement.

### What could move us
If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring something that is genuinely structural:
- a substantial and durable labour-cost contribution,
- capacity reduction through working-time or production realignment that actually lowers fixed costs,
- binding flexibility for workforce deployment,
- and an agreement that restores the brand to a viable return-on-sales path.

We also need clarity on timing. We are under acute pressure now. Investor confidence, owner confidence, and operational credibility cannot wait for a vague process that postpones decisions for years.

So my message is simple as well:  
**I am open on the method, but not on the necessity of deep structural savings.** If the only answer is “no closures, no layoffs, no cost reset,” then that is not a solution. It is a refusal to confront reality.

If you want a negotiated path, bring a package that closes the gap in a measurable, durable way. Otherwise, management will have to keep all options open.
