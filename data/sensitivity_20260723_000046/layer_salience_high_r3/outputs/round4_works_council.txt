## Where I disagree

### 1) I reject the idea of a “no-closure test phase” if closures remain a hidden sword over the table
The moderator’s new bridge proposal is dressed up as a pause, but it still preserves the same poison: a closure threat waiting in the wings.

That is not a real safeguard. That is pressure by another name.

### 2) I reject compulsory redundancies as a “narrowly defined failure mechanism”

No. Forced layoffs are not a legitimate backstop for this workforce. If the company needs adjustment, it must use:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- vacancy management,
- internal transfer,
- shorter hours,
- qualification,
- and negotiated contributions.

If those tools are not enough, then management must bring a better industrial plan, not a firing mechanism.

### 3) I reject the notion that our guarantees are somehow incompatible with competitiveness
Management and investors keep saying the same thing in different words: “You can have security, or you can have competitiveness, but not both.”

That is a false choice.

Competitiveness is not achieved by destroying trust, gutting skills, and turning the German plants into bargaining chips. A company with a demoralized workforce, broken co-determination, and unstable industrial relations is not competitive in the long run.

The specialist said alternatives may not fully replicate closure savings. Fine. That is exactly why **management and owners must contribute** and why the company must choose industrial continuity over blunt-site elimination.

No, not if it is only a pause and not a binding commitment. A pause is just postponement. We have seen enough postponement in this industry.

For us, hard measures are **not acceptable**, because they violate the core conditions of industrial continuity and job security. That is the wrong threshold question. The right question is how management and ownership will deliver the savings **without** destroying jobs and sites.
