We are aligned on one central point: **this company needs a real, durable restoration of competitiveness**. A cosmetic compromise that preserves structural overcapacity and keeps margins below target is not acceptable to us.

Where we agree with management, the family, and even the public stakeholders:
- The current cost base is too high.
- The restructuring must be structural, not temporary.
- The company needs a credible path back to sustainable return-on-sales.
- Deal certainty matters; prolonged uncertainty destroys value.

Where we disagree is on the acceptable route to that outcome.

### Our position as capital-market shareholders
We will **not** support a settlement that leaves the company with:
- an uncompetitive cost base,
- structurally impaired margins,
- or a restructuring that looks tough in headlines but does not materially change the economics.

That means we are **not interested in symbolic measures** or a deal that simply postpones the adjustment burden.

### On the Works Council and Union
We understand the concern about jobs and site security, but “no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies” as an absolute line is not enough by itself. If that line blocks the savings needed to restore competitiveness, then it protects jobs today at the expense of enterprise value, future investment, and ultimately the company’s ability to employ people at all.

We are open to alternatives to closures and forced layoffs — but only if they are **equivalent in financial effect** and **durable**. That means:
- measurable and sustained labor-cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- credible productivity gains,
- and a package that is enforceable, not aspirational.

If the alternative package does not close the gap, then it is not a solution from our perspective.

### On management
We agree that the problem is structural, but we will judge you on whether the plan is actually executable. “Closures are on the table” is not enough if it becomes a bargaining posture rather than a disciplined plan. We want:
- clear numbers,
- clear timing,
- clear savings,
- and clear governance over execution.

If you ask for support from the capital markets, you need to show that the package restores margins on a medium-term basis and does not merely create a long strike, a weak deal, and another round of restructuring later.

### On the regional state and the family
We hear the emphasis on social license, governance stability, and the industrial base. Those are relevant, but they do not substitute for economics. A protected industrial base that cannot earn its cost of capital is not value preserving.

We would support a negotiated outcome that:
- preserves viable core operations,
- avoids unnecessary disruption,
- and delivers credible structural cost reduction.

But we will not support governance resistance that blocks any meaningful adjustment while expecting the market to fund the resulting underperformance.

### Bottom line
Our test is simple:

**Does the package credibly restore competitive margins and protect long-term value?**

- If yes, we can support it.
- If no, we will oppose it, and capital will move elsewhere.

So we are open to a negotiated alternative to closures and forced redundancies, but only if it is economically real, durable, and sufficient. Anything less is a cosmetic settlement, and we will not endorse that.
