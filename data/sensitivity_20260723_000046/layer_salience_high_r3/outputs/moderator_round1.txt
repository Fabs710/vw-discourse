### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term patch.
- All sides accept that the current situation involves **high cost pressure, overcapacity, and competitiveness challenges**.
- There is broad willingness to discuss **capacity adjustments, redeployment, training, working-time solutions, and some form of employee contribution**.
- There is general support for **investment in future products, technology, and transformation through 2030**.
- All sides say they prefer a **negotiated settlement** over open-ended conflict.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works Council, union, and regional state reject closures as a starting point.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors insist closures may be necessary if no equivalent savings are found.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Workforce side rejects them outright.  
  - Management and capital-side actors do not accept a hard ban if it prevents structural savings.
- **Extent of guarantees**:  
  - Employee and state side want binding job, site, and investment security.  
  - Management and investors say guarantees must not prevent meaningful restructuring.
- **Burden-sharing**:  
  - Employees and state want management and owners to contribute visibly.  
  - Management and investors focus on the need for workforce-side cost reduction and structural change.
- **Definition of an acceptable alternative**:  
  - Workforce side wants alternatives that protect sites and jobs.  
  - Capital side says alternatives must be economically equivalent to closures/layoffs in effect.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **material cost reduction** without plant closures or compulsory redundancies.
- How to combine **job and site security** with **credible overcapacity reduction**.
- What specific **management/owner contributions** would accompany employee concessions.
- Whether there is a **measurable, enforceable alternative package** that can satisfy both labor peace and financial targets.
- How timing will work: management wants speed and certainty, while workforce and public stakeholders want stronger safeguards and consultation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Ask each side to move from principles to **specific, quantified proposals**.
- Identify which measures could deliver **equivalent savings** to closures or redundancies.
- Clarify the **minimum acceptable package** for each side on:
  - jobs,
  - sites,
  - costs,
  - investment,
  - and governance.
- Separate issues into two tracks:
  1. **Economic measures**: savings, capacity, productivity, labor costs.
  2. **Protection measures**: guarantees, redeployment, training, and transition support.
- Require each side to state what it is **prepared to concede** as well as what it will **not accept**.
