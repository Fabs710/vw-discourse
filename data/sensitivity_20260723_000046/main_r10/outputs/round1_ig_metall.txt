The trade union shares the core line of the works council on the essentials: **no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**. If management thinks the workforce will simply absorb all the pain of a strategy failure, that will not happen.

We are willing to talk about a **fair shared-sacrifice package**. That can include:
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- temporary or targeted labour-cost relief,
- capacity reductions through working-time tools,
- redeployment and qualification,
- moderation on bonuses or other non-core elements,
- and, if needed, a time-limited adjustment path.

But this only works if management, owners, and the company itself also contribute materially. We will not accept a model where workers pay through lower wages, higher insecurity, and job losses while the ownership side remains untouched.

Where I agree with the works council:
- protecting German sites is essential,
- forced redundancies are unacceptable,
- and restructuring must be negotiated, not dictated.

Where I need to be clear:
- if management’s “restructuring” secretly means closures first and bargaining later, then there is no basis for trust,
- and if the company wants labour to contribute, it must provide **binding guarantees on jobs and sites** in return.

So my position is simple:
**Talk yes. Sacrifice yes, if shared. Plant closures and compulsory redundancies no.**
If management wants a durable settlement, it should put a serious alternative on the table now: capacity adjustment, investment commitments, and a wage/benefit contribution that is temporary, balanced, and tied to guaranteed employment security.
