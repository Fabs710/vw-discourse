### Neutral moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.** All parties accept that the company needs restructuring.
- **A purely cosmetic compromise is not acceptable.** There is broad agreement that any solution must deliver real, durable savings.
- **Open industrial conflict would be harmful.** Management, owners, state, and investors all acknowledge the value damage from prolonged escalation.
- **A no-closure alternative is at least plausible in principle.** The expert assessments indicate it can generate meaningful savings if it is sufficiently deep and structured.
- **Quantification is now necessary.** All sides agree the next step must be a side-by-side comparison of closure-based and no-closure scenarios.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Employee side and state oppose closures as the preferred route; management, investors, and owners keep them on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject them as a starting tool; management and investors do not rule them out if economics require it.
- **Job-security guarantees:**  
  Employee side wants binding protections through 2030; management says such guarantees must follow viability, not precede it.
- **Scope of labor contribution:**  
  Employees accept temporary, negotiated contributions only with guarantees; capital and management seek more substantial and durable labor-side adjustment.
- **Whether a no-closure package can be economically equivalent:**  
  This remains the central unresolved question.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. employment protection:** how much cost reduction can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.
- **Speed vs. stability:** closures may cut fixed costs faster, but a negotiated no-closure package may reduce conflict and preserve trust.
- **Durability vs. flexibility:** temporary labor concessions may bridge the gap, but may not solve structural overcapacity.
- **Capex vs. restructuring severity:** no-closure options may require investment and operational reconfiguration rather than site exits.
- **Governance vs. bargaining leverage:** ownership/state influence and management leverage have not yet been translated into a shared package design.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single quantified comparison table**:
  1. closure-based scenario,  
  2. no-closure scenario.
- For each, specify:
  - annual savings,
  - one-time costs,
  - capacity reduction,
  - headcount impact,
  - timeline,
  - required investment,
  - and which guarantees/concessions are attached.
- The next round should focus on **which package closes the cost gap most credibly while minimizing conflict and preserving long-term viability**.
