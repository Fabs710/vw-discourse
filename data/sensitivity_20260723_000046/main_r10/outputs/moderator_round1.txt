### 1) What has been agreed
- **Doing nothing is not an option.** All sides accept that the company needs restructuring and improved competitiveness.
- **Negotiation is preferred over unilateral action.** There is broad agreement that a solution should be bargained, not imposed.
- **The company must remain investable and competitive.** Everyone recognizes the need to preserve long-term viability and investment capacity.
- **A shared contribution is conceivable.** There is general openness to some mix of working-time tools, redeployment, qualification, flexibility, and temporary labour-cost relief.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works council, union, and state reject closures as a solution; management does not rule them out; owners and investors leave them conditionally open.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject them as a starting point; management will not pre-commit against them.
- **Job-security and site guarantees:**  
  Employee side wants binding protection through 2030; management and investors say that cannot be promised if it prevents necessary restructuring.
- **Extent of labour contribution:**  
  Management, owners, and investors want material, durable cost relief; employee side says any contribution must be balanced and matched by guarantees.
- **Whether non-closure alternatives can deliver equivalent savings:**  
  This remains the central unresolved question.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. job/site security:** how much cost reduction is required, and what level of employment protection can realistically be attached to it.
- **Speed vs. social stability:** whether a faster restructuring with harder measures is preferable to a slower negotiated adjustment.
- **Capacity reduction vs. closures:** whether demand/cost gaps can be closed through working-time and redeployment measures alone.
- **Shared sacrifice vs. asymmetry:** who contributes, by how much, and whether management/owners will also accept meaningful burdens.
- **Credibility of alternatives:** no side has yet presented a fully quantified alternative package that replaces closures with comparable economic effect.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **specific package design**:
- define the **target savings and capacity reduction** required,
- identify which **non-closure measures** could deliver them,
- specify what **binding guarantees** could be offered in return,
- and clarify which elements are **non-negotiable** versus **open to trade**.

A useful next step would be a side-by-side comparison of:
1. closure-based restructuring, and  
2. a no-closure alternative with quantified savings, timeline, and guarantees.
