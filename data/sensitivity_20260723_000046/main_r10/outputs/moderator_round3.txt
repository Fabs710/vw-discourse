**Facilitator assessment of the impasse**

The core impasse is now clear:  
**management, owners, and investors require durable structural savings and capacity reduction; the works council and union require no closures and no compulsory redundancies; the state government and family holding want restructuring without a destructive conflict.**

The disagreement is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. It is about **which instruments are permitted to achieve it**.

### Where the deadlock sits
1. **Scope of permissible capacity reduction**
   - Employee side: no closures, no forced layoffs.
   - Management / capital side: closures remain available if needed.

2. **Definition of “credible” savings**
   - Employee side: working-time, redeployment, voluntary exits, and labor-cost relief can be enough.
   - Management / capital side: those measures may be insufficient unless they materially reduce fixed costs and overcapacity.

3. **Allocation of burden**
   - Employee side: shared sacrifice, but with binding site and job protection.
   - Capital side: contribution expected from labor, but not at the expense of competitiveness or governance flexibility.

4. **Trust and bargaining leverage**
   - The employee side sees closure threats as a trust breaker.
   - Management and investors see red lines as risking a compromise that does not solve the economics.

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### 1) **Phased no-closure restructuring with trigger-based review**
A negotiated package could be built around:
- no immediate closures,
- a defined initial savings program using shift reductions, redeployment, voluntary exits, and labor-cost relief,
- and a **pre-agreed review point** after verified implementation.

If the first phase meets quantified milestones, closures stay off the table.  
If it fails, the parties enter a second-stage negotiation on stronger capacity measures.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Gives the workforce a no-closure starting point.  
- Gives management and capital a fallback if savings do not materialize.  
- Reduces the sense of an all-or-nothing fight.

### 2) **No formal closures, but “capacity consolidation” inside the German network**
Instead of plant shutdowns, parties could negotiate:
- removal of shifts,
- line transfers,
- production concentration in fewer halls or units,
- and internal site specialization.

This would preserve sites legally and symbolically, while still delivering meaningful capacity reduction.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Addresses the employee red line against closures.  
- Gives management actual fixed-cost relief if the consolidation is deep enough.  
- Offers the state and family holding a less disruptive path.

### 3) **Higher labor contribution in exchange for binding protection and investment**
A balanced package could combine:
- time-limited wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- voluntary exits and early retirement,
- strong reskilling and redeployment guarantees,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

In exchange, the company commits to:
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- no closures during that period,
- and a verified savings target.

**Why it may bridge sides:**  
- Creates measurable employee contribution.  
- Gives management and investors a cost-reduction path.  
- Gives the state and union tangible protections and future-oriented commitments.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What quantified savings threshold would you accept as sufficient to keep a no-closure package credible, and what specific reductions in shifts, indirect labor, or site overhead are you prepared to trade for binding job guarantees?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited labor contribution package if it were tied to independently verified savings, no compulsory redundancies, and a binding no-closure period? If yes, under what exact conditions?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What site-by-site evidence can you provide that a no-closure package would fail, and what minimum savings, headcount reduction, and capacity removal would satisfy your economic test without closures?**

### To the Regional State Government
**Would you be prepared to support a phased compromise that preserves sites now but permits a later review if savings targets are missed, and what governance conditions would you require to make that acceptable?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What level of durability and conflict risk would make you prefer a negotiated no-closure settlement over a closure-led solution, and are you willing to back a binding no-closure period in exchange for verified performance milestones?**

### To Institutional Investors
**If a no-closure package delivered, say, 70–80% of the closure scenario’s recurring savings with materially lower conflict risk, would you support it, and what additional conditions would you require for approval?**
