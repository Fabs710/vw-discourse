I will respond plainly.

## My position

I agree with everyone on one fundamental point: **doing nothing is not an option**. The company needs a durable restructuring and a credible path back to competitiveness.

But I do **not** accept the management framing that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are simply normal tools to be deployed if the workforce does not concede enough. That is too one-sided, too risky for the regional economy, and too damaging to the company’s social partnership model.

## Where I agree

### With management
- The cost problem is real.
- The company must restore margins and investment capacity.
- A cosmetic compromise would be irresponsible.

### With the works council and union
- Restructuring must be negotiated, not imposed.
- Forced redundancies should be avoided as far as possible.
- A serious alternative to closures must be tested.

### With the family holding and investors
- The company cannot remain structurally uncompetitive.
- A prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- Any solution must be economically real, not symbolic.

## Where I disagree

### On plant closures
I do **not** support closures in Germany as the default route, and I will not treat them as an ordinary bargaining option from the start.

Why? Because a closure is not just a cost line. It destroys:
- jobs,
- supplier networks,
- regional skills,
- trust in future bargaining,
- and the company’s role as an industrial anchor.

That damage is lasting. It cannot be casually traded away for short-term savings.

### On compulsory redundancies
I do not accept making forced job cuts the starting point of restructuring. If management insists on that as its first move, it is choosing confrontation over negotiation.

### On the “closures or nothing” logic
I reject the idea that the only serious restructuring is one that starts with plant exits. The expert input was useful here: yes, closures reduce fixed costs more decisively, but a no-closure package can still deliver meaningful savings if it is properly designed and shared.

## What I consider acceptable

I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring package** if it has these features:

1. **No plant closures as the chosen path**
   - Capacity reduction can be discussed.
   - Site consolidation inside the network may be discussed.
   - But closure should not be the first or preferred answer.

2. **No compulsory redundancies as the primary tool**
   - Headcount reduction should come, if necessary, through attrition, redeployment, qualification, early retirement, and voluntary instruments.

3. **Binding protection for jobs and sites**
   - If workers are asked for concessions, they must receive credible guarantees in return.
   - A time horizon through 2030 is not unreasonable if the package is balanced and real.

4. **Shared burden**
   - Labour can contribute.
   - But management and ownership must also contribute materially.
   - A one-sided adjustment is not acceptable.

## What I need to see next

The discussion is now at the point where slogans are not enough. I want a **side-by-side package comparison** with real numbers:

- What exact savings would the closure-based plan deliver?
- What exact savings would the no-closure alternative deliver?
- How much capacity is reduced in each case?
- What happens to employment in each case?
- What investment commitments are tied to the German sites?
- What concessions would labour make, and for how long?
- What contribution will management and owners make?

Without that, we are still debating principles, not deciding responsibly.

## My bottom line

I am **open to restructuring**.  
I am **not open to deindustrialisation disguised as restructuring**.

If there is a credible no-closure package that delivers durable savings and protects the state’s industrial base, I will support it.

If management comes back insisting that closures and forced layoffs are unavoidable without first proving that no-closure alternatives fail, then I will oppose that course and use the state’s rights accordingly.
