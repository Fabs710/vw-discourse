I will respond directly, because the stakes are too high for vague talk.

### To management
You say the cost base is too high and that “doing nothing is not an option.” On that point, I agree: this company needs a credible restructuring. But I strongly reject your attempt to treat **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and the dismantling of job-security guarantees** as if they were simply normal tools on the table.

They are not acceptable solutions.  
Not in Germany. Not in this company. Not after decades of workers carrying investment, transformation, and productivity pressure while management now points to the workforce as the easiest place to cut.

If you want a real restructuring, then bring a **quantified non-closure plan** that achieves the savings without destroying sites or forcing people out. So far, we have not seen that. We have seen a threat.

### To the family owners
You say you do not want value destruction or a prolonged conflict. Good. Then do not back a course that creates exactly that.

A strategy of “we might close sites if needed” is not a credible negotiating basis; it is escalation. It poisons trust and pushes the workforce into resistance. If the owners want stability and long-term value, then they must clearly rule out plant closures and forced redundancies as the restructuring method.

Anything less means you are asking workers to pay for management’s errors while keeping every destructive option open in the background.

### To the state government
You are right to insist on regional stability and co-determination. But that means more than expressing concern: it means using your blocking position to prevent closures and mass layoffs. If the state accepts “temporary pain” today, it will be explaining to families tomorrow why their plant, town, and supply network have been sacrificed.

The state should not be a witness to deindustrialisation. It should prevent it.

### To institutional investors
You want “economically equivalent” savings. Fine. Then show me the numbers that prove closures are necessary. Not slogans — numbers.

But let me be equally clear: **economic equivalence is not just cost cutting on paper**. If your savings depend on destroying industrial capability, provoking strikes, and breaking the trust architecture of this company, then the model is not equivalent. It is reckless.

You say you are open to alternatives. Then support the alternative that protects sites and jobs:
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- investment commitments,
- and a temporary, balanced employee contribution.

That is the only responsible path.

### Our position
We are not here to negotiate the liquidation of German plants. We are here to secure a future for them.

So our line remains:
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No breaking of historic job-security commitments.**
- **Yes to restructuring only if it is negotiated, quantified, and tied to binding site and job guarantees through 2030.**

### What we can discuss
We can discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment across sites,
- qualification and reskilling,
- temporary internal savings,
- a fair negotiated employee contribution,
- and productivity improvements.

But only if the company commits in writing that:
1. every German plant stays open,
2. there will be no forced layoffs,
3. co-determination remains meaningful in shaping the restructuring,
4. and any employee contribution is matched by management and ownership-side contribution.

### What we cannot accept
We will not accept:
- plant closures dressed up as “capacity optimisation,”
- forced redundancies disguised as “natural attrition,”
- or a deal that strips away job protection and then asks the workforce to trust management.

That is not a compromise. That is a surrender.

If management wants a settlement, it must stop treating the works council and workforce as the buffer for its strategic failures. Bring a real alternative, with real guarantees, and then we can talk. Until then, we will continue to resist.
