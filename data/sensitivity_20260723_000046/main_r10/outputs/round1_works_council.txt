As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for a restructuring that management created through its own strategic mistakes.

Our priority is to secure every German plant, protect every site, and preserve co-determination and the job-security framework that has given this company stability for decades. If management wants a future for this company, it must pursue restructuring through alternatives that avoid closures and forced layoffs: capacity adjustments, internal redeployment, working-time solutions, qualification, and a fair negotiated employee contribution — but only on the basis of binding guarantees for sites and jobs through 2030.

We are prepared to discuss change. We are not prepared to discuss liquidation of sites or throwing workers out onto the street. Any plan must be credible, reversible where possible, and jointly shaped with employee representation. If management comes with a fait accompli, it will face resistance from us, from the workforce, and from the works council’s full co-determination rights.
