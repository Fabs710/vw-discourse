## Facilitator assessment: core impasse

The central impasse is **not** whether restructuring is needed; all parties now accept that it is. The impasse is:

**Whether the restructuring must include plant closures as a necessary instrument, or whether a no-closure package can be made economically equivalent in substance with binding guarantees.**

That splits the room into two blocks:

- **Employee side / state shareholder:** insists on **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, protected co-determination**, and asks management to prove that a no-closure path cannot work.
- **Management / family / investors:** insist on **durable fixed-cost and capacity reduction**, and argue that a no-closure path must be proven **site by site** to be financially equivalent.

A secondary impasse is **trust and sequencing**:
- the workforce fears a “temporary” deal will become a **delayed closure threat** later;
- capital fears a no-closure deal will become a **symbolic compromise** that leaves the company uncompetitive.

## Concrete compromise positions to test

### 1) Binding no-closure phase with verified milestones
A first-phase agreement could include:
- **no plant closures during the phase**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **shift reductions, voluntary exits, redeployment, qualification, and temporary labor-cost relief**
- a **site-by-site savings target**
- **independent verification**
- a defined review date

**Key safeguard:** the review must not automatically reopen closure threats; it should only trigger a jointly defined escalation path if verified savings targets are missed.

### 2) “No closure of core sites” plus network consolidation
A second option is a narrower protection model:
- all **core German sites remain open**
- some **non-core functions, lines, or capacity** may be consolidated within the network
- any adjustment is paired with **job guarantees, investment commitments, and co-determination protections**

This is designed to allow **capacity reduction without full plant closure**.

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with reciprocal guarantees
A third compromise frame:
- labor accepts **temporary moderation**, flexibility, and redeployment
- management and owners provide **written contributions**, investment commitments, and governance assurances
- the state shareholder uses its position to enforce **non-closure preference** for a defined period

This only works if the package is **mutual, time-limited, and quantified**.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What specific cost- and capacity-reduction measures, quantified site by site, would you accept as sufficient to deliver a credible no-closure package through 2030?**

### To the Trade Union
**What temporary labor concessions are you prepared to recommend if they are matched by binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff, and investment guarantees?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings gap remains after applying a deep no-closure scenario, and which site-by-site figures prove that closures are still necessary?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete conditions would you attach to your blocking position so that it forces a quantified no-closure alternative rather than simply delaying a decision?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Would you support a binding no-closure settlement if it meets a specified recurring-savings threshold and includes measurable owner-side contributions?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum recurring-savings and implementation-certainty threshold would make you accept a no-closure package instead of a closure-led one?**
