I agree with management on one point: **doing nothing durable is not an option**. The competitive pressure is real, and the company must restore its earnings power and preserve investment capacity.

But I must be equally clear: **I do not accept plant closures in Germany as the default solution**, and I do not accept a restructuring that shifts the burden primarily onto workers and regional communities while sidelining the state’s co-determination role.

### Where I agree
- The company needs a **credible restructuring**, not symbolism.
- There must be a **material cost adjustment**.
- The solution has to protect the company’s **long-term competitiveness** and ability to invest.
- Negotiation is the right path; a pure diktat would be politically and governance-wise unacceptable.

### Where I disagree
- **Closing plants first and bargaining later is not acceptable.**
  Once a site is closed, jobs, supplier networks, skills, and regional value chains are gone. That is not a reversible adjustment; it is a strategic cut with lasting damage.
- **Compulsory redundancies should remain the last resort, not the starting point.**
  We will not support a restructuring that treats people as the easiest variable.
- **The state’s co-determination and veto rights are not ornamental.**
  Any attempt to bypass them would create a governance conflict and a political crisis the company does not need.

### What would be acceptable from my side
I am open to a negotiated package if it meets three conditions:

1. **No plant closures as the chosen path**
   - Capacity reductions can be discussed.
   - Working-time solutions, redeployment, and qualification can be discussed.
   - But the burden must not be resolved by shutting down sites in Germany.

2. **Binding protection for jobs and sites**
   - If labour is asked to contribute, there must be credible guarantees on employment and investment.
   - Any sacrifice must be tied to a durable commitment to the German industrial base.

3. **A fair sharing of the adjustment**
   - Management, owners, and labour all have to contribute.
   - That may include temporary labour-cost relief, internal flexibility, and changes to non-core cost items.
   - But I will not support a solution that simply compresses wages and destroys jobs while leaving strategic errors uncorrected.

### My bottom line
I am prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring that preserves sites, protects most jobs, and restores competitiveness**. I am **not** prepared to support plant closures or a one-sided cutback strategy that damages regional economic stability and undermines the company’s social partnership model.

If management wants my support, it should come forward with:
- a serious alternative to closures,
- a verified capacity and productivity plan,
- investment commitments,
- and a binding employment framework that respects co-determination.

That is the only kind of restructuring that is both economically credible and politically legitimate.
