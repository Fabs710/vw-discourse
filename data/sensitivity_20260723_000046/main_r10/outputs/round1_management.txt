I’ll be equally clear: I do **not** accept a cosmetic solution that preserves today’s cost base while pretending the crisis is solved.

I hear two points I can agree with:
- Restructuring must be **negotiated**, not simply imposed.
- Any solution should preserve the company’s long-term ability to invest in products, technology, and the 2030 portfolio.

But I strongly disagree with the idea that we can rule out plant closures, structural capacity cuts, and meaningful labour-cost changes **before** we have a viable alternative that closes the competitiveness gap.

### The reality
Our German cost base is too high. European demand is shrinking. Competition is brutal. If we do nothing durable, we will lose market share, margins, and ultimately more jobs than any restructuring would remove. That is not a scare tactic; that is the arithmetic of this business.

### On your red lines
- **No closures at all**: I cannot accept that as a starting point. If a site is structurally uncompetitive and overcapacity persists, we must be able to address it.
- **No compulsory redundancies**: I am prepared to discuss mechanisms to minimize forced layoffs, but I will not pre-commit to protecting every role regardless of business need.
- **No job-security changes**: again, not acceptable if the result is that we preserve an uncompetitive structure until the next crisis.

### On “shared sacrifice”
I am open to a negotiated contribution from the workforce, but it must be **material and durable**, not symbolic. That means:
- real labour-cost relief,
- capacity adjustment that matches demand,
- flexibility in working time and deployment,
- and structural savings that actually move the return-on-sales line.

If we cannot achieve that, then we are not restructuring — we are postponing the problem.

### What I can consider
I am not fixated on closures as a political goal. What I need is the outcome:
- a **sustainable reduction in cost and capacity**,
- protection of the company’s investment capacity,
- and a credible path back to competitiveness.

If the employee side can bring forward a package that genuinely delivers comparable structural savings without closures, I will engage seriously. But I will not sign up to a deal that leaves the cost base fundamentally untouched and calls that a compromise.

So my position is straightforward:
**We need deep, durable restructuring. If you want to avoid closures, bring an equally durable alternative that produces the same economic effect.**
