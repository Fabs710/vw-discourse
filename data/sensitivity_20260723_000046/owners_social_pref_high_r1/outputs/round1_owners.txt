I appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me respond as the controlling shareholder, and I will be equally clear: **we will support a restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins, but not one that merely preserves the status quo under a different name.**

Where I agree:
- The company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one.
- Labour peace matters. A prolonged conflict would erode value for everyone.
- Any package must be tied to **future products, investment, and site utilization**.
- A one-sided burden on employees would be neither fair nor sustainable.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** accept that every German plant must remain open regardless of economics.
- I do **not** accept a framework that rules out compulsory redundancies in all circumstances.
- I do **not** accept guarantees that freeze the structure through 2030 if they prevent the necessary reset.
- I also do **not** accept a settlement that protects legacy arrangements while leaving the company uncompetitive.

From an ownership perspective, the core issue is simple: **the enterprise must regain competitiveness, or the long-term value of the family holding is damaged.** That is not in anyone’s interest. The company cannot continue to carry an overcapacity and cost structure that is structurally above target while assuming the market will absorb it.

That said, I am not demanding closures as a principle. I am demanding **results**.

So my position is:
1. **A credible, measurable cost and capacity reduction is required.**
2. **If comparable savings can be achieved without closures, that is preferable.**
3. **If they cannot, then more difficult measures must remain available.**
4. **Any settlement must be durable and must not undermine governance control or the company’s ability to act later.**

To management: you need to show that the plan is not just severe, but effective. If you want owner support for a difficult package, bring a case that proves the long-term economics, not just the short-term pressure.

To the works council, union, and state government: I hear the social and political concerns. But we cannot equate “protecting the German footprint” with “protecting every existing structure.” If the savings can be delivered through capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, and negotiated labour-cost measures, then that path should be explored seriously. If not, then you are asking owners to underwrite structural weakness indefinitely.

What I would look for in a settlement:
- **A genuine and quantified savings package**
- **Shared contribution from workforce, management, and capital**
- **A credible plan for future products and investment**
- **Durable labour peace**
- **No outcome that permanently impairs the company’s value or the owners’ ability to govern**

So I am not lining up behind any automatic closure program, but neither am I prepared to bless a deal that simply preserves all plants, all guarantees, and all legacy costs. **The burden has to be allocated in a way that restores competitiveness, or there is no settlement worth having.**

If management can bring forward a package that achieves the necessary savings without destroying value, we will consider it seriously. If not, then the difficult measures remain on the table.
