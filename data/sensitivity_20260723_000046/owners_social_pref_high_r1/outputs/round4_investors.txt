We agree with the core diagnosis: **this company needs a durable restructuring that restores competitive margins**. A cosmetic settlement or a prolonged conflict would destroy value. We also agree that the next step must be **numbers, not rhetoric**.

That said, we are **not** prepared to endorse a no-closure position just because it is politically preferred. Nor are we prepared to endorse closures just because they are management’s instinct. Our standard is simple: **what delivers the best risk-adjusted, durable improvement in cash earnings and margins?**

## Where we agree

### With management
- The current cost base is too high.
- Overcapacity is real.
- Temporary concessions alone are not enough if the gap is structural.
- The package must be durable and implementable.

### With the expert
- A no-closure solution may work **if** the savings gap is moderate and the measures are permanent.
- If the gap is large and the plants are structurally underutilized, closures become economically more compelling.
- The right test is a **side-by-side, plant-by-plant comparison** over several years.

### With the family and state
- A weak compromise that preserves an uncompetitive structure would destroy enterprise value.
- Prolonged conflict would also destroy value.
- We need a negotiated outcome if it can be made economically credible.

## Where we disagree

### With the works council and union
Your position is not a restructuring proposal; it is a **constraint set**:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030.

That may be understandable from a labour perspective, but it is not enough for shareholders. We cannot support a deal that leaves the company with:
- structurally high fixed costs,
- excess capacity,
- and insufficient flexibility to adapt.

You keep saying the burden of proof is on management. Fair enough. But the burden of proof also applies to you: **show that a no-closure package can actually close the gap, not merely shift it around.**

### With management
At the same time, management has not yet earned the right to say closures are unavoidable. It has asserted the logic, but not yet published the comparison.

So we reject two bad shortcuts:
1. “Closures first, details later.”
2. “No closures, no matter the economics.”

Both are wrong.

## What we need next
We want a **single standardized comparison** for each plant and for the network as a whole:

### For the closure scenario
- annual savings
- one-off restructuring cash costs
- impairment / write-downs
- transfer and ramp-up costs
- capacity removed
- job impact
- effect on 2026–2030 margins and cash flow

### For the no-closure scenario
- permanent labor-cost savings
- headcount reduction achievable voluntarily
- working-time and shift effects
- overhead and procurement savings
- residual capacity imbalance
- durability of concessions
- effect on 2026–2030 margins and cash flow

Without that, this is not a decision; it is a debate about preferences.

## Our position
We are not ideologically attached to closures. We are attached to:
1. **durable cost reduction**
2. **credible margin restoration**
3. **implementation certainty**
4. **preservation of long-term value**

That means:
- If the no-closure package truly delivers equivalent durable savings, we can support it.
- If it does not, then more difficult measures, including closures and permanent job reductions, remain justified.

## What would move us
A no-closure deal would need to show:
- real and permanent capacity reduction,
- meaningful and durable labor-cost relief,
- clear implementation timing,
- and no hidden return to the old cost base after a short transition.

Temporary wage moderation, voluntary exits, or vague productivity promises are not enough if the structural gap remains.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the company structurally impaired,
- or a settlement that preserves every site regardless of economics.

If the no-closure route can close the gap, prove it.  
If it cannot, then the harder measures should not be ruled out by politics alone.

## Bottom line
We support a **durable restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins**.

We do **not** support a deal that merely buys peace while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.

So we align with the call for quantified scenarios, but we want the comparison to be honest, plant-specific, and cash-based. If management brings that, we will judge it on merit. If the no-closure option is genuinely equivalent, fine. If not, then management should stop pretending closures are off the table forever.
