I will speak plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and the investors on one central point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. The cost base and capacity issue are real, and pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

I also agree that any settlement must:
- restore competitiveness,
- be financially credible,
- include future products and investment,
- and be more than a temporary truce.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or starting solution.

That is not a neutral economic necessity; it is a choice that would shift the burden disproportionately onto the workforce and the regional economy. For the state government, that is not acceptable if there is still a serious alternative route to the same economic result.

I also **do not** accept any outcome that weakens or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role. That role is part of the governance balance here, and it is not negotiable away.

## My position on the current debate
The burden of proof remains with management and the owners if they want closures.

So far, I hear a lot of assertion:
- “The cost base is too high.”
- “Reversible measures won’t be enough.”
- “Closures remain necessary unless proven otherwise.”

That is not yet a sufficient case for plant closures in Germany.

If management believes closures are unavoidable, then it must show:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. why that gap cannot be closed by a negotiated no-closure package,
3. which sites are allegedly dispensable,
4. and what the regional and employment consequences would be.

Until then, closure talk remains a threat, not a demonstrated necessity.

## What I could support
I am prepared to support a **serious negotiated restructuring** if it is built on these principles:

- **No plant closures in Germany unless absolutely unavoidable and proven so.**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred instrument.**
- **Capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, redeployment, voluntary exits, and productivity improvements first.**
- **A fair employee contribution only if management and owners also contribute.**
- **Binding commitments on investment, product allocation, and site utilisation.**
- **Durable protection for the regional industrial base.**

That is the only kind of package that would be politically and economically defensible from my side.

## To management
You say you need implementation certainty and permanent savings. Understood.

But you have not shown that the only way to achieve that is by cutting into the company’s German employment base. If you want the state to accept sacrifice from the workforce, then you need to bring a package that is actually balanced:
- management discipline,
- owner commitment,
- investment guarantees,
- and social responsibility.

A plan that starts with closures and layoffs is not balanced. It is confrontation.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the German plants and jobs cannot simply be treated as disposable. That is the right line.

At the same time, I would urge both of you to recognize that the company must remain competitively viable. So a package that protects sites and jobs **while still delivering measurable structural savings** is the target we should keep working toward.

But let me be equally clear: any “solution” that merely delays the cut and leaves the company uncompetitive later would also be unacceptable. Protecting jobs in the short term only to lose them later through erosion is not a win.

## To the controlling family
You are right that value creation matters. But value is not created by sacrificing the regional anchor function that has made this company strong over decades.

If the company’s social legitimacy in Germany is damaged, that has consequences for industrial relations, production stability, political support, and ultimately competitiveness. So yes, margins matter — but so does trust.

## To investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. I agree with that.

But you should not assume that only plant closures can produce credibility. A quantified, enforceable, negotiated package with real capacity reductions, working-time changes, and labour-cost contributions can also be credible if it is properly designed and backed by binding commitments.

## My bottom line
The regional state government will support a **durable, negotiated restructuring** that:
- preserves the company as a regional economic anchor,
- protects as many German jobs and sites as possible,
- restores competitiveness,
- and respects co-determination.

We will **not** support an outcome that:
- guts regional employment,
- uses closures as the easy first answer,
- or strips the state of its governance role.

So my message is simple:

**Bring forward a real no-closure alternative with numbers, binding commitments, and fair burden-sharing.**  
If it is credible, we can work with it.  
If not, we will oppose any closure-driven plan that destroys jobs and weakens the company’s regional foundation.
