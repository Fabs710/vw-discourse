**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) Points of agreement
- All parties accept that the company needs a **durable restructuring / turnaround**.
- There is broad agreement that a solution should, where possible, be **negotiated rather than imposed**.
- Most parties see value in a package that includes some combination of:
  - **investment and future product allocation**,
  - **capacity adjustments**,
  - **working-time flexibility**,
  - **voluntary exit instruments**,
  - **productivity / process improvements**,
  - and some form of **burden-sharing**.
- There is shared recognition that a purely cosmetic or short-term deal would not be acceptable.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures as a starting point.
  - Management, controlling family, and institutional investors keep closures on the table if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Labour-side actors and the state government reject them.
  - Management and owners do not rule them out if no equivalent savings can be achieved otherwise.
- **Binding site / job guarantees through 2030**
  - Labour-side actors want strong guarantees.
  - Management, owners, and investors view long guarantees as potentially incompatible with flexibility and competitiveness.
- **How to achieve savings**
  - Labour-side actors prefer savings through non-closure measures first.
  - Management, owners, and investors want **quantified, lasting cost and capacity reductions** and question whether non-closure measures alone will be sufficient.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unresolved
- **Social protection vs. economic restructuring**
  - How much job and site protection can be preserved without leaving the company structurally uncompetitive?
- **Voluntary measures vs. mandatory measures**
  - Whether voluntary exits, working-time changes, and temporary concessions can produce enough savings, or whether stronger measures are unavoidable.
- **Short-term labour peace vs. long-term flexibility**
  - Whether stronger guarantees can be given now without limiting future management action.
- **Burden-sharing**
  - What share of adjustment should fall on labour, management, owners, and capital.
- **Credibility of alternatives**
  - Whether a no-closure package can be demonstrated as delivering the same economic result as closures.

### 4) Refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **specific package design**:
- quantify the savings needed,
- identify which measures would deliver them,
- specify what protections or guarantees would be exchanged for employee contributions,
- and distinguish clearly between **necessary outcomes** and **preferred means**.

A useful next step would be for management to present **one concrete closure-based option and one concrete no-closure alternative**, each with numbers, timing, and implications for jobs, sites, and investment.
