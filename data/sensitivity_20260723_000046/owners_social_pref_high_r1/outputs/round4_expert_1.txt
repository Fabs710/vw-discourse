## Economic realism test
A no-closure option closes the gap only if the package can replace the **permanent fixed-cost removal** that closure provides. In automotive restructuring, that is often hard because:
- plants carry substantial fixed overhead,
- underutilization drives unit costs up,
- temporary wage measures expire,
- and keeping all sites open can preserve too much excess capacity.

So the key question is not whether the company can get “some savings,” but whether it can get **enough durable savings** to restore competitiveness.

## Practical conclusion
If the company’s required savings are **moderate**, a negotiated no-closure package may be realistic.  
If the required savings are **large and structural**, then **a no-closure package becomes much harder to justify economically** unless it includes very deep permanent labor and network changes.
