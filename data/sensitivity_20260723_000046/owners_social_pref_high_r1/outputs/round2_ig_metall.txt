I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the Works Council, the state government, and even parts of what management, owners, and investors are saying: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. We are not denying the cost pressure, the market pressure, or the need to restore competitiveness.

I also agree that a settlement must include:
- a credible industrial plan,
- future product and investment commitments,
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- and some form of employee contribution.

So yes: **we are prepared to contribute**. But contribution is not the same as capitulation.

## Where I disagree
I disagree strongly with the repeated implication that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default “credible” answer**.

That is not a neutral economic conclusion. It is a choice about who bears the burden.

If management, owners, and investors insist on starting from closures and forced layoffs, then the message to the workforce is:
- employees pay,
- management keeps maneuvering room,
- capital preserves control,
- and the social cost is outsourced to the plants.

That is exactly what we reject.

## To management
You have not yet shown that closures are unavoidable.

Saying “the cost base is too high” is not enough to justify:
- closing German plants,
- forcing redundancies,
- and breaking long-standing collective protections.

If you want us to consider deeper concessions, then you must present a **real no-closure pathway** with numbers that stand up:
- how much savings are needed,
- how capacity will be adjusted,
- what labour-cost relief you are asking for,
- and why those savings cannot be achieved through negotiated measures first.

Right now, your position is still a demand for structural pain without sufficient proof that there is no alternative.

## To the controlling family
You say you want value creation, not preservation for its own sake. I accept that. But value is not created by tearing up trust and turning restructuring into a unilateral transfer of risk onto labour.

A company with stable labour relations, secure sites, and committed workers is also value. A short-term spreadsheet improvement that destroys that trust can be very expensive later.

So if you want a settlement that is durable, you need to back a package that protects the industrial base rather than treating it as disposable.

## To the institutional investors
You are right about one thing: a cosmetic deal is unacceptable.

But you are wrong if you think the only credible route to margins is closures and forced layoffs. There are other credible routes if management is serious:
- capacity reduction without plant closure,
- redeployment and working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- limited temporary labour-cost contributions,
- bonus sacrifices,
- productivity agreements,
- and targeted investment to stabilize the future model mix.

If those measures are quantified properly, they can be credible. What is not credible is demanding workforce sacrifice first and proving necessity later.

## To the regional state government
We welcome your clear stance against closures and forced redundancies. But that stance needs to translate into real leverage, not just political language.

If the state is serious about protecting its industrial base, then it should back a binding package that makes closures unnecessary:
- site security,
- future allocation,
- investment commitments,
- and protection against unilateral cuts.

## What the union can accept
Let me be specific so there is no misunderstanding:

We can negotiate:
- temporary or limited labour-cost contributions,
- flexibility in working time,
- productivity improvements,
- capacity reductions,
- voluntary instruments,
- and a fair burden-sharing package,

**if and only if** those measures are tied to:
1. **no plant closures in Germany**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **a binding collective framework**
4. **durable site and employment security**
5. **real commitments on future products and investment**

We are open to employees contributing fairly. We are **not** open to employees paying all the pain while management and owners keep all the options.

## What we will not accept
Let me be absolutely explicit:
- No closure package disguised as “capacity optimization.”
- No forced layoffs presented as “necessary flexibility.”
- No weakening of the collective wage framework without a real countercommitment.
- No temporary concessions that merely buy time for a second round of cuts.
- No restructuring that leaves the German sites permanently insecure.

## Bottom line
If management wants labour peace, it must come back with a **credible no-closure alternative**.

If it cannot do that, then the confrontation will continue, and the workforce will use the instruments available to it. We are not here to be persuaded by pressure alone.

We are here to protect jobs, protect the collective agreement, and secure a settlement where the burden is shared fairly — **not shifted onto the people who built this company’s value**.
