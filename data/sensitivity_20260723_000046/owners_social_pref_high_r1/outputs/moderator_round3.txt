### 1) What has been agreed
- **A durable restructuring is necessary.** No party supports a purely cosmetic or temporary pause.
- **The current situation requires real cost and capacity adjustment.**
- **Numbers are essential.** All sides accepted the need for a quantified, side-by-side comparison of options.
- **A package may include multiple levers**, such as:
  - productivity improvements,
  - capacity reduction,
  - working-time changes,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - overhead/procurement cuts,
  - and some employee contribution.
- **Future investment and product commitments to 2030 matter** and must be part of any settlement.
- **A no-closure option has not been ruled out in principle** if it can deliver equivalent durable savings.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works council and union reject them; management, investors, and owners keep them on the table if needed; state government opposes them as a starting point.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour parties reject them outright; management and capital side view them as possible if the gap cannot be closed otherwise.
- **Burden of proof:**  
  Labour and state say management has not yet shown closures are necessary; management says labour has not yet shown a no-closure package can fully close the gap.
- **Job/site security through 2030:**  
  Labour wants binding guarantees; management and capital side say such guarantees are only acceptable if they do not freeze needed flexibility.
- **What counts as a real alternative:**  
  Labour sees non-closure restructuring as viable; management/investors argue it must still prove durable and sufficient at the required scale.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Scale of the savings gap vs. feasibility of non-closure measures:** no agreed figure yet.
- **Durability of savings:** temporary concessions versus permanent structural cost reduction remains unresolved.
- **Capacity reduction without closures:** unclear whether this can materially solve overcapacity.
- **Voluntary exits vs. compulsory job cuts:** how much headcount reduction can realistically be achieved voluntarily is unknown.
- **Regional/social impact vs. competitiveness:** the cost of preserving sites/jobs versus the cost of closures has not been quantified.
- **Investment capacity vs. labor protection:** how much future investment is preserved under each scenario is still open.
- **Implementation certainty:** no agreement yet on which package can be executed reliably and fast enough.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from position statements to **one standardized comparison**:
  1. **Closure-based scenario**
  2. **No-closure scenario**
- For both, require the same metrics:
  - annual savings,
  - one-off costs,
  - timing,
  - site-specific impact,
  - job impact,
  - durability of savings,
  - and effect on 2030 investment capacity.
- Ask management to present **plant-by-plant and measure-by-measure numbers**.
- Ask labour to specify **what exact cost/capacity reduction a no-closure deal would deliver** and by when.
- Keep the next round focused on **quantified feasibility**, not on repeating red lines.
