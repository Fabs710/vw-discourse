## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. The current cost base, capacity situation, and investment needs cannot be ignored. I also agree that:
- future products and investment to 2030 matter,
- the numbers matter,
- and a side-by-side comparison is now necessary.

The expert’s contribution is useful because it confirms something important: **a no-closure solution is not ruled out in principle**. It can work if the gap is moderate and the measures are durable. That matters.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the attempt to make **plant closures and compulsory redundancies the default starting point**. That is not a neutral “economic necessity.” It is a choice about who carries the burden.

And from our perspective as the regional state and anchor shareholder, that burden cannot first and foremost fall on:
- German jobs,
- German sites,
- and the company’s social legitimacy in the region.

### To management
You are right that a cosmetic deal is unacceptable. But you are **not yet justified** in treating closures as the baseline.

The expert did not say closures are required. The expert said:
- the answer depends on the **size of the gap**,
- the **permanence** of the measures,
- and the **capacity effect**.

That means the burden is still on management to show, with plant-by-plant figures, that no-closure measures cannot do the job.

Until that is shown, closures remain an **assertion**, not a proven necessity.

### To the works council and union
I support your rejection of automatic closures and compulsory redundancies. That is legitimate, and I will not treat the workforce as disposable.

But I also need to be clear: a no-closure package must be more than a slogan. It must be **real and durable**:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- overhead and procurement cuts,
- and a fair employee contribution.

If you want the no-closure route to hold, it must be financially credible. What I will not support is a deal that simply preserves everything unchanged and calls that restructuring.

### To the controlling family
You are right that long-term value matters. But long-term value is not protected by destroying trust with the workforce and hollowing out the regional anchor role.

A company cannot remain strong in Germany if it treats regional sites as expendable and co-determination as an obstacle. That may look efficient on a spreadsheet for a moment; it is not sustainable governance.

So I welcome your openness to a no-closure route **if the economics work**. But I reject the tone that makes closures sound like the unavoidable first answer before the alternative has been seriously tested.

### To the institutional investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive. I agree.

But you should not overstate the case for closures before the numbers are in. The expert did not say the no-closure path fails; the expert said it may fail if the gap is large and the measures are not durable. That is not a blank cheque for closures.

### To management and capital
I also want to be very clear on one point: **the state’s blocking role is real**. It is there to protect the company as a regional economic anchor, not to bless a plan that guts the domestic base.

So if the next round of figures shows that a serious no-closure package can bridge the gap, then that route deserves preference. If not, then we will assess what additional measures are truly unavoidable. But we are not there yet.

## What I want next
I support the call for a **plant-by-plant, side-by-side model**, but it must be honest and complete:
- annual savings,
- one-off costs,
- timing,
- site impact,
- job impact,
- durability of savings,
- and effect on 2030 investment capacity.

And I want the same standard applied to both scenarios:
1. closure-based scenario
2. no-closure scenario

## What is not acceptable
What I will not support is:
- an outcome that guts regional employment,
- a deal that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role,
- or a “restructuring” that is really just closure by another name.

To be explicit: **no automatic plant closures in Germany, and no solution that makes the state a passive bystander while the regional industrial base is dismantled.**

## Bottom line
My position remains:
- durable restructuring is necessary,
- a no-closure option remains possible and should be tested seriously,
- closures are not proven necessary at this stage,
- and any acceptable settlement must protect jobs, sites, and the company’s role as a regional anchor while restoring competitiveness.

If the next round of numbers shows the no-closure route is insufficient, we will confront that honestly. But until then, I will not accept closures as the default answer.
