Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

I acknowledge the concern about historic guarantees and the social impact. But I do **not** accept the premise that Germany’s current cost base can be protected and that competitiveness will somehow recover on its own. It will not.

We are facing:
- a shrinking European market,
- fierce price pressure,
- a structural transition in EV and China that has not been managed well enough,
- and German operating costs that are materially above target.

If we respond with **no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and only reversible measures**, then we are not restructuring the company — we are postponing the problem. That is not acceptable. It would leave us with the same uncompetitive cost base, less room to invest, and a weaker future.

Where I agree:
- I agree that we need a **durable, negotiated solution**.
- I agree that employee contribution must be part of a broader package, not an isolated demand.
- I agree that future products, investment allocation, and site utilization matter.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** accept “every German plant must remain open” as a starting point.
- I do **not** accept a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies if we cannot achieve the necessary structural savings otherwise.
- I do **not** accept binding site guarantees through 2030 if they simply freeze an uncompetitive structure in place.

If you want me to be specific: management needs a package that produces **real, measurable, lasting cost and capacity reduction**. That means one or more of the following must be on the table:
- plant closures or equivalent capacity exits,
- material headcount reduction,
- permanent labour-cost reductions,
- and changes to work rules that materially lower the cost base.

Temporary wage restraint, voluntary schemes alone, or pure redeployment will **not** be sufficient if they do not close the structural gap.

I am open on the **means**, but not on the **result**:
- If you can show me a package with comparable savings through capacity reduction, work-time changes, and a genuine labour-cost contribution, then we can discuss avoiding closures.
- If you cannot, then closures remain on the table.

And I need to stress one point: prolonged strikes will damage output, customers, and confidence, but they will not change the underlying economics. Neither will a cosmetic deal. Investors, owners, and the board will expect a solution that restores the brand to its return-on-sales target and frees funds for future products and technology to 2030.

So my position is this:
**I am prepared to negotiate, but only on a restructuring that actually reduces cost and capacity in a durable way.**
Anything less is not a solution.
