As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will **not accept plant closures in Germany** and we will **not accept compulsory redundancies**. That is our red line.

The workforce has already carried enough uncertainty. Management cannot expect us to approve a restructuring that destroys jobs, breaks historic guarantees, and turns employees into the sole shock absorber for strategic failures. If the company needs a turnaround, then it must come through **fair, negotiated, and reversible measures** that protect the German sites and preserve long-term industrial strength.

Our priorities are:

1. **Every German plant must remain open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A genuine co-determination role for labour in shaping any restructuring.**
5. **Any contribution from employees must be limited, fair, and matched by real commitments from management and owners.**

We are prepared to discuss alternatives to closures, including:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary retirement or voluntary severance only where absolutely necessary,
- productivity and process improvements,
- and a negotiated employee contribution, but only if it is tied to **clear investment commitments, future product allocation, and legally or contractually binding site protection**.

What we will not do is sign off on a plan that uses closures and mass layoffs as the starting point. That would be a breach of trust and a direct attack on the workforce, and it would meet fierce resistance.

So my position is simple: **secure the future of the German plants, secure the jobs, and negotiate a restructuring that does not sacrifice people to balance the books.** If management wants our cooperation, it must come back with a credible alternative that respects these conditions.
