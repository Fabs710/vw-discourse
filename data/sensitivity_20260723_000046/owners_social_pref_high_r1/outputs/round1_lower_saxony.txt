I will be direct.

I agree with the **Works Council and the union** on the central point: **Germany’s plants and jobs cannot be treated as disposable**. A restructuring that starts from plant closures and compulsory redundancies is not acceptable to the regional state government. It would damage the industrial base, trigger major social and political fallout, and undermine the company’s role as a regional anchor.

At the same time, I do **not** dismiss management’s cost problem. The company clearly needs a credible turnaround and a durable improvement in competitiveness. But there is a hard boundary here: **the solution cannot be to hollow out the German footprint and shift the burden one-sidedly onto the workforce**.

So my position is:

- **No closure strategy as the default answer.**
- **No compulsory redundancies as the first or preferred instrument.**
- **No outcome that strips the state of its co-determination and veto role.**

Where I am constructive is on a **negotiated restructuring with social partnership**. I can support:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time measures,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary and socially responsible departures,
- temporary or limited employee contributions,
- and a genuine industrial strategy tied to future products and investment.

But any employee contribution must be matched by real commitments from management and the owners. That means:
1. **Binding protection for key German sites.**
2. **A clear employment perspective, not just vague promises.**
3. **Investment and product allocation for the affected plants.**
4. **A burden-sharing approach across capital, management, and labour.**

To management specifically: I hear the argument that the cost base must come down. But I would caution strongly against believing that closures are the only credible lever. There is room for a negotiated package that preserves sites and still delivers structural savings — if management is prepared to negotiate seriously rather than present plant closure as a fait accompli.

To the works council and union: I support your insistence that there must be no one-sided sacrifice by the workforce. But I also urge that we keep the door open to a serious settlement that includes measurable adjustments, provided they are fair, limited, and tied to durable guarantees.

My message is simple: **the regional state will not back a restructuring that destroys jobs and industrial capacity in Germany.** We are prepared to support a tougher, negotiated path that restores competitiveness **without abandoning the company’s social responsibility or its regional anchor function**.

If management wants progress, it should come back with a package that protects sites, preserves employment as far as possible, and secures the company’s long-term health. Without that, I cannot support it.
