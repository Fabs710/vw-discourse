I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one essential point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**. The cost base, capacity situation, and investment needs are real. A purely symbolic compromise would fail the company, the workforce, and the region.

I also agree that:
- future products and investment to 2030 matter,
- quantified scenarios are necessary,
- and a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the repeated assumption that **plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the default or starting point**.

That is not an economic law. It is a choice about who carries the burden. And for us, as the regional state and anchor shareholder, that burden cannot be shifted onto German jobs and sites as the first response.

### To management
You say the numbers will decide. Fine. But you still have not shown that the gap cannot be closed **without** closures or forced layoffs. Saying “the alternative must match the economics” is correct, but it cuts both ways: you have not yet demonstrated that closures are the only route to those economics.

What I want from you is not another general warning, but a **side-by-side, site-specific comparison**:
- exact savings needed,
- exact savings from each measure,
- which savings are permanent,
- what capacity reduction is actually required,
- and what a no-closure package would deliver over the same horizon.

Until that is on the table, closure remains an assertion, not a proven necessity.

### To the union and works council
I support your insistence that the workforce should not be forced to pay first. That is legitimate, and I will not treat German plants as disposable. At the same time, I also need to be clear: **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no flexibility” as an absolute framework is not enough unless it can be translated into a credible industrial plan**.

So I am not asking you to surrender. I am asking you to show that a no-closure package can be made durable and measurable:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- and a fair, limited employee contribution.

That path is legitimate if it is real. What is not legitimate is a disguised dismantling plan, or a deal that preserves social peace today only to weaken the company tomorrow.

### To the controlling family
You are right that long-term value matters. But long-term value is not protected by damaging the company’s social legitimacy in Germany. If the state’s industrial base is hollowed out, trust, stability, and political support erode with it.

So I welcome your openness to a no-closure option **if the economics work**. But I would also caution against treating closures as the easy fallback before the alternative has been properly tested. That may save time, but it can destroy the very anchor function that has made this company strong.

### To the institutional investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. I agree with that. But you should not overstate the case for closures before the numbers are in. If a negotiated no-closure restructuring can deliver credible, durable savings and restore margins, then that is preferable to a destructive conflict.

What I will not support is a settlement that is financially tidy on paper but socially and politically reckless in the region.

## What I can support
I can support a **serious negotiated restructuring** if it includes:
- measurable and durable cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- future product and investment commitments,
- a fair employee contribution,
- and protection of as many German jobs and sites as possible.

That is not a refusal to restructure. It is a refusal to accept closure as the first answer.

## What I cannot support
I will not support:
- a closure-driven plan that starts by gutting regional employment,
- compulsory redundancies as the default instrument,
- or any outcome that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are core to our role as an anchor shareholder and regional government.

## My bottom line
Bring me the numbers — but bring them honestly:
- closure-based scenario,
- no-closure scenario,
- same accounting horizon,
- same durability test,
- and clear site-by-site implications.

If the no-closure route can genuinely close the gap, I will work for it.  
If it cannot, then we will need to assess what further measures are unavoidable — but only then.

Until that point, my position remains:

**No automatic closures. No automatic forced layoffs.  
Protect the company’s long-term health by protecting its regional base and co-determination role.**
