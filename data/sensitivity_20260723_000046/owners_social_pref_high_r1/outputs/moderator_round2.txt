## Moderator Summary — Round 2

### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement across all sides that:
- the company needs a **durable restructuring**, not a short-term pause;
- **future products and investment** are necessary;
- the current **cost base and capacity situation require change**;
- a **cosmetic compromise** would not be acceptable;
- any settlement should ideally be **negotiated** rather than driven by prolonged conflict;
- employee contributions, productivity measures, and capacity adjustments are legitimate elements of discussion.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The central dispute is still whether **plant closures and compulsory redundancies** are:
- a necessary part of the restructuring, or
- avoidable if a serious no-closure package is designed.

Key divides remain on:
- **burden-sharing**: labour representatives reject workforce-led sacrifice as the default, while management, investors, and the family emphasize structural savings;
- **job and site guarantees**: works council, union, and state push for strong or binding protections, while management and owners resist commitments that could limit flexibility;
- **proof standard**: labour and the state demand concrete evidence that closures are unavoidable; management and capital argue that any alternative must prove it can deliver equivalent savings;
- **scope of acceptable measures**: labour accepts only voluntary and reversible instruments with safeguards, while management insists that temporary or reversible measures alone are likely insufficient.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several important trade-offs remain unresolved:
- **how to reconcile competitiveness with job security**;
- **whether non-closure measures can achieve the same durability and scale of savings as closures**;
- **what level of employee contribution is acceptable if management and owners also contribute**;
- **how to balance site protection against the need for operational flexibility**;
- **how investment commitments and future product allocation will be tied to any restructuring package**;
- **how to make any agreement enforceable without simply postponing the same conflict**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from position statements to **side-by-side scenarios**. To narrow the gap, the discussion should focus on:
- a **quantified closure-based scenario** and a **quantified no-closure scenario**;
- site-by-site impacts, timing, and permanence of savings;
- what employee, management, owner, and state contributions each side is prepared to make;
- which commitments would be **binding**, and which would remain conditional;
- whether a package can deliver both **durable competitiveness** and **socially acceptable burden-sharing**.

In short: the next round should test, in numbers, whether a credible no-closure package can close the gap—or whether the parties are still too far apart for agreement.
