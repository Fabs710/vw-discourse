## Core impasse

The plateau is now centered on **one unresolved question**:

> **Can the company achieve the required durable cost reduction without German plant closures and compulsory redundancies?**

Everything else flows from that.

### Where the conflict sits
- **Works council + union**: accept restructuring only inside a hard red line of no closures / no forced layoffs / strong job security.
- **Management + investors**: require a restructuring package that is economically equivalent to closures if it is to avoid them.
- **State + family**: open to a no-closure route, but only if it is financially credible and not merely a delay tactic.
- **Expert**: says both routes are possible in principle, but the decisive test is plant-specific numbers, durability of savings, and multi-year NPV.

### Procedural diagnosis
So the impasse is not about whether restructuring is needed. It is about:
1. **the size of the savings gap**,
2. **whether no-closure measures can permanently close it**, and
3. **what burden-sharing is acceptable if they cannot**.

---

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the conflict

### Proposal 1: Dual-track restructuring package with a trigger
A negotiated package could be built in **two layers**:

- **Layer A now**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, immediate cost measures, voluntary exits, working-time changes, management/overhead cuts, procurement savings, and capacity rationalization inside the network.
- **Layer B trigger**: if the independently verified numbers show that Layer A misses a defined savings target by a set date, then a pre-agreed escalation mechanism opens for specific additional measures.

This gives labour the no-closure test it demands, while giving management and capital a structured fallback if the package underperforms.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-by-site “equivalence test” before any closure decision
Before any closure is even tabled as a live option, an independent modeller would compare each site on the same basis:
- annual savings,
- one-off costs,
- capacity impact,
- stranded costs,
- and five-year NPV.

The parties would agree in advance on:
- the accounting horizon,
- the cost assumptions,
- the minimum required savings,
- and the threshold for declaring a no-closure option economically sufficient or insufficient.

This does not decide the outcome in advance; it creates a shared test so the dispute shifts from slogans to verified figures.

---

### Proposal 3: No-closure settlement with stronger binding guarantees, but narrower scope
A middle position could pair:
- **binding no-closure and no-forced-redundancy commitments for a defined period**, with
- **substantial, measurable employee and management contributions**,
- **and tightly defined productivity and capacity milestones**.

In return, labour would accept:
- more extensive working-time flexibility,
- voluntary departures,
- redeployment obligations,
- and a limited, time-bound employee contribution.

This would only work if management and capital also accept hard commitments on investment, product allocation, and management savings.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**If the independent model shows that the no-closure package misses the required savings by a material margin, what specific additional measures would you permit without crossing your red lines?**

### 2) Industrial Union
**What exact permanent measures, by category and magnitude, would you accept to prove that a no-closure package is economically equivalent to closures?**

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to publishing plant-by-plant numbers and accepting an independent equivalence test before insisting that closures are necessary?**

### 4) Regional State Government
**Will you support a time-limited no-closure test period with clear performance triggers, even if that requires you to refrain from using your blocking power immediately?**

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to back a no-closure package if the independent model shows it meets the same multi-year NPV target as a closure-based plan?**

### 6) Institutional Investors
**What minimum level of permanent cost reduction and implementation certainty would make a no-closure package acceptable to you despite the absence of closures?**

### 7) Independent Expert / Financial Modeller
**What single threshold or decision rule should the parties adopt to determine, objectively, whether the no-closure path is economically sufficient?**
