I agree with the Works Council on the core point: **closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable starting points**. If management’s “solution” is to offload the crisis onto the workforce while protecting profits, dividends, and strategic flexibility for everyone else, then that is not a restructuring plan we can support.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework.**

Those are not bargaining chips to us; they are the basis for any serious settlement.

That said, I am not here to pretend there is no cost pressure. We have already signaled willingness to discuss **shared-sacrifice alternatives**. The union can talk about:
- temporary or limited labour-cost contributions,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions,
- flexibility measures,
- forgoing bonuses or other earnings components,
- voluntary instruments if truly necessary,
- and productivity measures,

but only if these are embedded in a package that **protects jobs, protects sites, and gives the workforce something durable in return**.

What we will not accept is a one-sided model where employees pay through wage restraint, longer hours, or concessions while management retains full freedom to close plants later anyway. That would be unfair and would simply buy time for a second round of cuts.

So if management wants a deal, it needs to put something real on the table:
1. **Binding guarantees against plant closures and forced redundancies.**
2. **A credible employment and investment commitment.**
3. **A fair distribution of the burden across management, owners, and workforce.**
4. **A negotiated industrial strategy for future products and site utilisation.**

If those conditions are met, then we can negotiate seriously about a contribution from employees. If they are not met, then the workforce should expect us to use every legitimate instrument available, including industrial action.

In short: **we are open to contribution, not capitulation.**
