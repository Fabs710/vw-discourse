Management is still not answering the central question: **why should the workforce pay for a restructuring that begins with closures and forced layoffs when there are still alternative routes on the table?**

Let me be precise.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic pause. We also need investment, future products, and a credible plan for competitiveness. I am not denying the economic pressure.

## Where I disagree
I strongly reject the framing that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the “default credible” answer.

That is exactly the kind of one-sided logic we cannot accept:
- management keeps flexibility,
- owners protect control and value,
- investors want savings,
- and the workforce is told to absorb the social damage.

No. If the company wants labour’s cooperation, it must first prove that it has exhausted the **non-closure** path.

## What management still has to show
You have not yet presented a convincing case that:
1. **every German plant must close or be reduced permanently**, or
2. **forced layoffs are unavoidable**, or
3. **the savings gap cannot be closed through a negotiated package**

Without that proof, talk of closures is just pressure politics.

## Our position remains clear
We will only discuss restructuring on the basis of:
- **no German plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and employment guarantees through 2030**
- **real co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
- **fair burden-sharing, not workforce sacrifice**

## On the arguments from the other stakeholders

### To management
You say temporary measures are insufficient. Then bring numbers, not threats. Show us the exact gap, the exact capacity need, and the exact savings that allegedly cannot be achieved otherwise. Until then, closures remain an assertion, not a necessity.

### To the controlling family
You talk about value creation, but destroying trust and tearing up job-security commitments also destroys value. A company that forces through closures against the workforce in Germany may “improve” a spreadsheet, but it will damage industrial relations, production stability, and the long-term base of the enterprise.

### To the investors
You want a credible route back to margins. Fine. But credibility is not the same as mass displacement. A negotiated no-closure package with capacity adjustments, working-time measures, productivity gains, and limited employee contribution can also be credible — if management commits to the plants and future allocation of products.

### To the regional state government
Your support for protecting sites is right. But that protection must be real, not rhetorical. If the state is serious about its blocking role, it should not allow management to use closure threats as leverage while still expecting social peace. We need you to stand behind a binding protection package.

## What we can accept
We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time changes,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary instruments only,
- productivity and process improvements,
- a temporary, limited employee contribution,
- and a negotiated industrial plan for future products and investment.

But only if these come with:
- **binding guarantees against closures**
- **no forced redundancies**
- **site security through 2030**
- **enforceable commitments on investment and workload**

## What we will not accept
Let me say it plainly:  
If the “package” still contains plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or vague promises that can be withdrawn later, then it is not a package — it is a disguised dismantling plan.

And if management comes back with the same message dressed in softer language, the workforce will not be fooled.

## Bottom line
The burden of proof is on management and the owners.

If they want our cooperation, they must come back with a **concrete no-closure plan**:
- quantified savings,
- verified by site-by-site numbers,
- delivered through reversible and socially responsible measures,
- with binding job and plant guarantees.

Until then, our answer is unchanged:  
**No closures. No forced layoffs. No surrender of co-determination.**
