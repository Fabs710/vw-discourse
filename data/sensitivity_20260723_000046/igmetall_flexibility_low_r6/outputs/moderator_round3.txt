## Core impasse

The central blockage is **not** the general recognition that restructuring is needed. The impasse is over **the instrument and proof standard**:

1. **Labour/Works Council/Union** want a restructuring **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies** as the starting point.
2. **Management / investors / controlling owner** want **credible, durable cost and capacity reductions**, and view closures and deeper headcount cuts as potentially necessary.
3. **The state shareholder** and other stakeholders want to protect the German industrial base, but also want a plan that is economically believable.

So the deadlock is:

> **Can the required savings and capacity reduction be achieved without closures and forced layoffs, and if so, who has to prove it with plant-level data?**

At present, each side is asking the other to move first:
- labour asks management to prove necessity of closures;
- management asks labour to prove equivalence of closure-free measures.

That is why the discussion has plateaued.

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited closure moratorium + binding verification phase
A compromise could be:

- **All sides agree to a temporary moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period**
- In return, **labour accepts immediate participation in a plant-by-plant verification process**
- An independent expert is given access to site-level cost, volume, utilization, and headcount data
- The mandate is explicitly to test:
  - closure-based scenario,
  - closure-free scenario,
  - and a mixed scenario
- A clear decision gate is set at the end of the review period

**Purpose:** This bridges the works council’s red line with management’s demand for evidence.

---

### Proposal 2: Mixed restructuring package with site protection for core sites and conditional flexibility elsewhere
A second compromise could be:

- **Core German sites are protected from closure in the first phase**
- The company instead pursues:
  - attrition,
  - redeployment,
  - working-time measures,
  - temporary wage/bonus moderation,
  - productivity commitments,
  - investment upgrades
- In parallel, **a limited number of sites or functions may be subject to conditional review**, but only if predefined savings targets are missed

This avoids an immediate all-or-nothing clash and creates a staged path:
- protected core footprint,
- measurable labour contribution,
- and an objective trigger mechanism if the savings gap remains.

**Purpose:** This may bridge labour’s “no closures now” stance with management’s insistence that structural adjustment must remain possible.

---

### Proposal 3: Job-security package through 2030 with quantified shared sacrifice
A third compromise could be:

- **No compulsory redundancies for a defined period**
- **No unilateral closure decisions during that period**
- Employees agree to a **temporary, quantified contribution**:
  - bonus restraint,
  - limited working-time moderation,
  - deferred payments,
  - targeted flexibility
- Management, owners, and shareholders commit to matching contributions:
  - reduced distributions,
  - investment commitments,
  - executive restraint,
  - operational efficiency program
- The package includes a **binding savings target** and a **joint monitoring committee**

If the target is met, the no-forced-layoff regime continues. If it is not met, the parties return to negotiations under pre-agreed rules.

**Purpose:** This creates symmetry: labour contribution is matched by capital-side contribution, reducing the sense of one-sided sacrifice.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact plant-by-plant savings gap remains after you have applied every closure-free measure you are willing to consider, and can you show that gap in numbers rather than in principle?**

### To the Works Council
**If closures and forced layoffs are off the table, what specific and quantified capacity and cost reductions are you prepared to deliver by site so the company still becomes competitive?**

### To the Industrial Union
**What temporary worker contribution is the union willing to put on the table that is large enough to matter, but still consistent with your red lines on job security?**

### To the regional state government
**What concrete leverage will you use to ensure the company actually tests closure-free options before closures are treated as acceptable?**

### To the controlling family holding
**Will you support a restructuring that preserves trust and jobs if it requires lower short-term returns, provided the long-term competitiveness case is credible?**

### To the institutional investors
**What level of verified savings and capacity reduction would make a closure-free package acceptable to you, and are you prepared to back a staged solution if those thresholds are met?**
