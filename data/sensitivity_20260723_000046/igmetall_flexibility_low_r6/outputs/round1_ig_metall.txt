I agree with the Works Council on the core point: this cannot be a one-sided restructuring where the workforce absorbs all the pain while management and the owners protect themselves.

Let me be equally clear from the union side:

- We do not accept plant closures.
- We do not accept compulsory redundancies.
- We do not accept the dismantling of the collective wage framework as a “solution.”
- We do not accept open-ended insecurity for the workforce.

Management says the cost base must come down. We understand that the company is under pressure. But if the answer is simply “cut jobs, close plants, and push wage losses onto employees,” then that is not a restructuring we can support. That would be social and industrial devastation, not a fair transformation.

What we are prepared to discuss is a genuine shared-sacrifice package, but only under clear conditions:
- site security and no closure of German plants,
- no forced layoffs,
- capacity reductions and operational flexibility instead of wholesale destruction,
- a temporary, negotiated employee contribution if, and only if, management, the owners, and the company also contribute materially,
- guarantees on future investment, new products, and the long-term role of German sites,
- and respect for collective bargaining standards.

If management wants labour peace, it needs to present a serious alternative that preserves jobs and sites. For example, there may be room for:
- reduced working time,
- temporary forgone bonuses or special payments,
- a solidarity contribution by employees,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- and a negotiated reduction in capacity.

But that is only acceptable if the sacrifice is truly shared. We will not allow the workforce to be told that they must pay for every mistake, every market failure, and every strategic misstep while others are shielded.

So my message is simple: we are ready to bargain, but not to surrender. If the proposal still contains plant closures or compulsory redundancies, then we are not at a compromise point — we are at a conflict point.
