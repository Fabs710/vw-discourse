## Where I agree
I agree with one point from almost everyone: the company needs a real restructuring, not theatre. Capacity, productivity, qualification, and investment all matter. I also agree that a negotiated process is preferable to open conflict.

And I agree with the moderator that this is now a question of proof, sequencing, and concrete packages.

## Where I disagree
I **do not** accept the framing that the only credible restructuring is one that keeps plant closures and compulsory redundancies on the table.

That framing is precisely the problem.

Management says: “Show us numbers first.”  
We say: “Show us why you think destroying sites and livelihoods is the right answer before you demand surrender.”

You do not get to use closures as a threat and then call it evidence-based management.

## To management
You have still not proven that German plant closures are necessary.

Not “desirable.”
Not “helpful.”
Not “maybe unavoidable if all else fails.”

Necessary.

That is a much higher bar, and you have not met it. What you have offered is a preference for the harshest instrument because it is the fastest way to shift the burden onto workers. That is not a restructuring plan; it is a power play.

You say a blanket ban on closures and compulsory redundancies leaves only symbolic measures. That is false. There are real operational tools that do not destroy sites:
- internal redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- temporary moderation,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- capacity rationalisation through shifts and product allocation,
- investment to secure future volumes,
- and management/owner contributions.

If those are not enough, then prove it with hard, plant-by-plant numbers — not with broad claims about “market reality.”

### What I will not accept from management
- no unilateral closure decisions,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of the historic job-security framework,
- no “last resort” rhetoric while closures are already being used as leverage.

If management wants a serious discussion, then the starting assumption must be: **all German plants stay open, and nobody is forced out**.

## To the trade union
I stand with you on the core red lines:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided labour sacrifice.

Your point that any worker contribution must be matched by management, owners, and shareholders is absolutely right.

Where I would sharpen the line is this: we should not allow the language of “shared sacrifice” to become a euphemism for structural job loss. A temporary contribution is one thing. A permanent weakening of job security is another.

So yes, we can discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- bonus deferrals,
- limited, negotiated concessions,
- training and redeployment.

But only under binding guarantees on:
- all German sites,
- no forced layoffs,
- and a genuine co-determination role in implementation.
