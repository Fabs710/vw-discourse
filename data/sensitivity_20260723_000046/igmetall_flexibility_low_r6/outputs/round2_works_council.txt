Management, the family, and the capital market investors keep repeating the same thing: “show us hard numbers, show us durable savings, show us why closures may be necessary.”

Let me respond just as clearly:

**You have not shown that plant closures are necessary.**  
You have shown only that management prefers the fastest and harshest route to shift the burden onto the workforce. That is not the same thing.

We do **not** accept closures as a starting point, and we do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as an acceptable “fallback.” Those are not bargaining chips to us; they are red lines. If you want a serious discussion, then build it on the premise that **all German plants remain open** and **no worker is forced out**.

### Where I agree
I agree with the moderator on the need to move from principles to concrete package design. Fine. Then let us be concrete.

I also agree that the company needs:
- capacity adjustment,
- productivity improvements,
- investment in future products,
- qualification,
- and internal redeployment.

We are prepared to discuss all of that.

### Where I disagree
I reject the idea that the only “serious” package is one that includes closures or forced job cuts.

I also reject the attempt to make the workforce carry the main cost while management and owners present themselves as mere observers of “market realities.” This company’s crisis was not created by shop-floor workers, and they will not be the ones to pay for it alone.

### What a real alternative must include
If management wants a closure-free solution, then here is what must be on the table:

- **Binding site guarantees for all German plants through 2030**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Internal redeployment instead of dismissals**
- **Working-time flexibility and temporary reductions where needed**
- **A negotiated employee contribution, but only if it is limited, temporary, and matched by real contributions from management, owners, and shareholders**
- **A clear investment commitment for products, technology, and qualification at every German site**
- **A genuine co-determination role in designing and monitoring the restructuring**

That is the minimum basis for serious talks.

### What is not acceptable
Not acceptable are:
- plant closures “as a last resort” that are already being used as a threat to extract concessions,
- compulsory redundancies dressed up as restructuring,
- the removal of established job-security protections,
- and demands for durable labour-cost cuts without binding guarantees on sites and jobs.

If you want workers to contribute, then the company must also contribute — through ownership, investment, management restraint, and a credible long-term industrial plan. A one-sided wage sacrifice is not a solution. It is exploitation under a different name.

### To management
Stop speaking as if closures are inevitable. They are not inevitable unless you choose them.  
Bring a genuine alternative that keeps the German footprint intact and proves, in operational terms, how you will adjust capacity without destroying sites and livelihoods.

### To the family and investors
If you care about long-term enterprise value, then you should not support a restructuring model that destroys social trust, triggers conflict, and damages the company’s industrial base in Germany. Short-term “discipline” at the expense of future stability is not stewardship.

### To the regional state government
You are right to reject closures and mass layoffs. But that has to mean more than political statements. Use your influence to block any plan that sacrifices German plants and demands workers accept the losses alone.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a **real** restructuring:
- with capacity changes,
- with productivity improvements,
- with working-time solutions,
- with qualification and investment,
- and with a fair, temporary employee contribution.

But we are **not** negotiating away:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the job-security framework.

If the other side cannot present a closure-free package that preserves all sites and jobs, then the problem is not that labour is inflexible. The problem is that management is asking workers to solve a strategic failure by surrendering their livelihoods. That will meet resistance, not consent.
