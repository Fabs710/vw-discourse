From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, I would frame this very simply: **we need a credible, durable return to competitive margins, not a managed drift into permanent underperformance.**

### Where we agree
I agree with management, and with the family to a degree, on the core diagnosis:
- the current cost base is not competitive,
- capacity is too high for the market environment,
- and the brand is missing its return-on-sales target.

I also agree that a purely symbolic settlement would be value-destructive. If the outcome only preserves the status quo while postponing the adjustment, that will hurt earnings, valuation, and access to future capital.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** support an approach that treats plant closures as the only serious option from the outset if there is a credible alternative that achieves the same savings and capacity correction. We care about economics, not ideology. If closures are necessary, show why. If they are not necessary, then do not use them as leverage to force a weak deal.

At the same time, I also do **not** support the employee side’s attempt to preclude closures and compulsory redundancies categorically without demonstrating a replacement package of equal scale and durability. A blanket “no” is not a restructuring solution. It is a demand that shareholders absorb the downside while the structural problem remains.

### What would move us
We would support the path that credibly does all of the following:
- restores margins on a durable basis,
- reduces capacity to match demand,
- lowers labour costs meaningfully and for the medium term,
- avoids prolonged strikes and operational disruption,
- and gives investors confidence that this is not a temporary truce.

That means:
- if closures are on the table, they must be justified by hard numbers and executed cleanly;
- if closures are avoided, the substitute must be **equally real**: not one-off concessions, but durable reductions in cost and capacity;
- any employee contribution should be substantive, not cosmetic;
- and management must commit to measurable milestones and transparency so markets can judge whether the restructuring is working.

### Our red line
What we will not back is an outcome that leaves the company with:
- structurally uncompetitive labour and capacity costs,
- margins still below target,
- and a settlement that simply defers conflict until the next earnings disappointment.

That would be value-destructive, and capital will respond accordingly.

### Bottom line
We are open to either a closure-based restructuring or a closure-free alternative. But the standard is the same: **show us the hard savings, the durable capacity adjustment, and the path back to competitive returns.** Without that, there is no shareholder support for the deal.
