I’ll be direct.

I agree with the Works Council on the core point: **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are not acceptable as a starting point, and they are not acceptable as the “inevitable” outcome either.** The burden of restructuring cannot be dumped onto the workforce while management, owners, and capital markets keep their protections intact.

I also agree with the moderator that we need to move from abstract positions to a concrete package. Fine. Then let’s be concrete.

## What I agree with
- The company needs a **real restructuring**.
- We need **capacity adjustment**, **productivity improvements**, **qualification**, and **investment**.
- A **negotiated solution** is preferable to escalation.
- Any employee contribution has to be **balanced** by contributions from management and owners.

## What I reject
I reject three things completely:

1. **Plant closures in Germany**  
   You have still not demonstrated that closures are necessary rather than chosen for speed and leverage.

2. **Compulsory redundancies**  
   These are not an acceptable “fallback” if management cannot force through its preferred model.

3. **A one-sided labour sacrifice**  
   We will not sign up to durable wage, staffing, or security cuts while management and owners present this as unavoidable and employees carry the full pain.

## On management’s argument
Management keeps saying the numbers require closures. Then show the numbers in a way that proves:
- closures are truly unavoidable,
- closure-free measures cannot reach the same savings,
- and the burden is shared fairly.

So far, what I hear is not proof. It is a preference for the most brutal instrument.

If management wants us to discuss a credible alternative, then the package must be built on:
- **no plant closures**,
- **no compulsory redundancies**,
- **internal redeployment**,
- **working-time solutions**,
- **capacity reduction through operations, not site destruction**,
- and a **temporary, negotiated employee contribution** only if it is matched by real management and owner contributions.

## On the family and investors
You both talk about long-term value, and I take that seriously. Then long-term value means:
- no industrial war,
- no destruction of trust,
- no permanent damage to the workforce and the German industrial base,
- and no restructuring that turns the company into a low-trust employer.

If you want stability and returns, then support a settlement that preserves jobs and sites while making adjustment possible. A deal that triggers escalation may look “disciplined” on a spreadsheet, but it is bad stewardship.

## On the state government
Your position against closures and mass layoffs is correct. But influence has to be used to block a bad deal, not just to express concern. If the company insists on plant closures as its opening move, then there should be no political blessing for that path.

## What we can discuss
We are prepared to discuss a serious package along these lines:
- temporary employee contribution,
- reduced working time where needed,
- elimination or deferral of bonuses/special payments,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and capacity reductions that do **not** rely on plant closures or forced layoffs.

But this only works if the other side accepts:
- binding site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and respect for the collective wage framework.

## What I need to hear next
If management, the family, and investors want an actual settlement, then come with:
- a closure-free capacity and cost plan,
- a quantified employee contribution,
- quantified management and owner contributions,
- and a binding job and site-security framework.

If that cannot be done, then let’s be honest: the obstacle is not labour’s unwillingness to compromise. The obstacle is management’s refusal to search for a solution that does not sacrifice workers first.

We are ready to bargain seriously. We are **not** ready to sign off on closures, forced redundancies, or the dismantling of collective protections.
