From an industrial restructuring standpoint, the current discussion has clarified the **positions**, but not yet the **operational arithmetic**.

## What the data and statements support
1. **The company does need restructuring.**  
   That is consistent across nearly all parties.

2. **There is likely structural overcapacity in Germany.**  
   That is plausible given the described market decline, cost gap, and margin pressure.

3. **A closure-free package has not yet been demonstrated as equivalent.**  
   The labour side has asserted feasibility, but no plant-by-plant numbers have been presented here showing:
   - fixed-cost savings,
   - utilization changes,
   - headcount effects,
   - and durability through 2030.

4. **A closure-based package also has not yet been proven necessary.**  
   Management and capital-side actors have argued necessity, but in this record they have not yet produced the comparative plant-level case that shows closures outperform a closure-free or mixed alternative after accounting for conflict risk and transition cost.

5. **The key missing item is a common scenario model.**  
   Without common assumptions, this is still a dispute about narratives, not verified plant economics.

## What remains uncertain
- Which plants are structurally non-competitive versus temporarily underloaded.
- How much capacity can be removed through:
  - shift changes,
  - line rationalization,
  - product allocation,
  - redeployment,
  - and attrition,
  without closures.
- The real fixed-cost savings from closing any specific plant.
- The one-time costs of closure:
  - severance,
  - transfer costs,
  - tooling relocation,
  - supply-chain disruption,
  - write-downs,
  - and labor conflict.
- Whether a closure-free plan can reach the required return-on-sales improvement fast enough.

## My judgment
At this stage, **neither side has met the burden of proof**:
- labour has not quantified an equivalent closure-free restructuring,
- management has not quantified why closures are unavoidable.

So the only evidence-based next step is a **plant-by-plant comparison under one shared model**.

## What that review must include
To be decision-useful, the expert review should compare:
- current and projected utilization by plant,
- fixed and variable cost per unit,
- labor cost per vehicle,
- transfer and closure costs,
- capex required to keep each site competitive,
- savings from working-time, wage, redeployment, and attrition measures,
- and NPV/margin impact over at least 3–5 years.

## Bottom line
- **Supported by the record:** restructuring is necessary; plant-level evidence is missing.
- **Not yet supported:** that closures are either indispensable or avoidable.
- **Best next move:** an independent, plant-by-plant industrial and financial verification with common assumptions and a binding timetable.
