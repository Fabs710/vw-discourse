### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the current situation is not sustainable and that some form of restructuring is necessary.
- There is broad agreement on the need for:
  - investment in future products, technology, and qualification,
  - internal redeployment and workforce upskilling,
  - some form of capacity adjustment,
  - and a negotiated, rather than unilateral, process.
- Several parties also accept that any employee contribution must be balanced by contributions from management, owners, and/or shareholders.
- There is shared concern about avoiding prolonged industrial conflict and preserving the long-term viability of the enterprise.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:** Labour, the Works Council, and the state government reject them; management, family holding, and investors keep them on the table if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour-side actors reject them; management and capital-side actors say they may be unavoidable unless an equivalent alternative exists.
- **Scope and durability of employee concessions:** Labour is open to temporary and negotiated sacrifices, but only with binding guarantees; capital-side actors want meaningful, durable cost reductions.
- **Level of guarantees:** Labour and the state want binding site and job-security commitments through 2030; management and investors argue such guarantees are only acceptable if the structure becomes competitive.
- **Assessment of “shared sacrifice”:** Labour argues current proposals risk shifting too much burden onto workers; management and shareholders argue symbolic concessions are insufficient.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The parties have not yet reconciled **job and site protection** with **durable cost reduction**.
- There is no agreed formula showing how a closure-free plan could deliver savings equal to those from closures and redundancies.
- It remains unresolved how much of the adjustment should come from:
  - labour cost,
  - working time,
  - capacity reduction,
  - productivity gains,
  - investment,
  - or ownership/shareholder contributions.
- There is still no shared definition of what would count as a sufficiently “serious” alternative to closures.
- The distribution of risk is unresolved: who bears the cost if the restructuring fails to restore competitiveness?

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **concrete package design**.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - what exact concessions it can make,
  - what it requires in return,
  - and what numerical savings or capacity effects those measures would deliver.
- The next round should focus on:
  - a closure-free alternative, if one exists,
  - or the conditions under which closures would be treated as last resort.
- The key task is to test whether a negotiated package can simultaneously achieve:
  - competitiveness,
  - job and site protection,
  - and long-term investment credibility.
