I’ll respond directly.

I agree with one part of what both labour-side stakeholders are saying: this cannot be a cosmetic exercise. We do need a serious, negotiated restructuring, and we do need investment in future products, technology, and qualification. On that, we are aligned.

But I fundamentally disagree with the central premise that seems to be driving your position: **that German plants must remain open, jobs must be protected to 2030, and management should simply find savings somewhere else.** That is not a restructuring plan. That is a wish list.

The business reality has not changed:
- our European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our German cost base is too high,
- and the brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we lock in all sites, lock in all jobs, and rule out compulsory redundancies in advance, then we are left with symbolic measures and deferred pain. That would preserve the problem, not solve it.

### Where I agree
I do agree that:
- internal redeployment should be used where possible,
- qualification and future investment are essential,
- productivity improvements matter,
- and any settlement must be durable, not a one-year patch.

I also agree that a one-sided approach would be destabilizing. The company needs a negotiated outcome, not a unilateral diktat.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket ban on plant closures,
- a blanket ban on compulsory redundancies,
- or a demand for binding site guarantees through 2030 regardless of the economics.

Those positions may be understandable politically, but they do not answer the cost crisis. If you want to rule out closures, then you must show a package that delivers equivalent, durable capacity and cost reduction. So far, I have not heard that package.

### On your “closure-free alternative”
You keep saying a closure-free plan should be possible. I am open to alternatives — but they must meet the standard of hard numbers.

That means:
- real, lasting reduction in labour costs,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- operational flexibility,
- and a structural improvement in margins.

Temporary concessions, bonus deferrals, or short-term working-time adjustments alone do not get us there. They may buy time, but they do not fix the cost base.

### On the claim that closures are just management’s preference
That is simply not credible. Closures are not a preference; they are one possible consequence of an uncompetitive structure. If the alternative package achieves the same savings, then closures may not be needed. But you have not shown that alternative yet.

### To the Works Council and union
You are asking for binding site and job guarantees first, and restructuring second. I cannot accept that sequence.

If the company commits first and the savings never materialize, then we have merely postponed the crisis. We will not do that again. The agreement must be built around:
- measurable cost reduction,
- durable capacity adjustment,
- and a labour contribution that is real, not temporary theatre.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your concern for the region and for social stability. But if you want to help protect the German industrial base, then you cannot simply veto every hard decision. Protecting the region long term requires making the company competitive now. Otherwise you protect jobs for a short period and lose far more later.

### To the family and investors
I agree with your concern about long-term value. But long-term value will not be protected by vague pressure alone. We need a settlement that materially improves margins and frees resources for future investment.

### My position, clearly stated
We remain prepared to negotiate a closure-free solution **only if** it delivers the same structural effect as closures and redundancies would deliver. That means:
- substantial and durable labour-cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- no cosmetic guarantees,
- and a settlement that restores competitiveness through 2030.

If that cannot be achieved, then I will not pretend that preserving every site and every job is compatible with the company’s survival.

So the next step is not more general declarations. It is simple:
**Present a quantified alternative that closes the cost gap.**
If you cannot, then you are asking management — and ultimately owners and investors — to accept structural weakness in exchange for temporary peace. That is not acceptable.
