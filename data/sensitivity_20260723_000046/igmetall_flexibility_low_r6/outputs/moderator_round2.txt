**Facilitator assessment of the impasse**

The core impasse is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. Most parties agree that capacity reduction, productivity improvement, investment, and qualification are necessary.

The impasse is over **the sequencing and proof standard**:

- **Labour-side stakeholders** want **binding site security, no compulsory redundancies, and no closures as the starting premise**.
- **Management and capital-side stakeholders** want **hard, quantified evidence that a closure-free package can deliver durable savings and capacity reduction at scale**.
- The **state** and **family holding** sit in the middle but have not resolved the central question: **what concrete package can reduce costs enough without destroying sites and jobs?**

So the stalemate is a **credibility gap**:
1. Labour says: “Show the case for closures; we need guarantees first.”
2. Management says: “Show the alternative; we need savings first.”
3. Each side is demanding the other side move first.

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### 1) Two-track package: temporary site protection plus hard triggers
A possible bridge is a **time-limited protection agreement** for German plants, paired with **explicit performance triggers**.

**Structure:**
- No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period, e.g. **24–36 months**.
- In return, labour accepts:
  - temporary working-time reductions,
  - bonus deferrals,
  - targeted pay moderation,
  - and accelerated redeployment/qualification.
- Management commits to:
  - specific investment,
  - productivity measures,
  - and a quantified milestone plan.
- If milestones are missed, the parties reopen the question of deeper structural steps under a defined process.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives labour a real safeguard now, while giving management a credible path to argue for tougher measures later if the savings do not materialize.

---

### 2) Closure-free first phase with an independent verification mandate
A second compromise is to agree on a **closure-free first phase**, but only if an **independent industrial and financial review** verifies whether it closes the gap.

**Structure:**
- Commission an agreed third-party reviewer.
- The reviewer tests:
  - closure-free savings,
  - capacity reduction options,
  - cost impact,
  - and job effects.
- During the review period:
  - no closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - limited interim measures only.
- The parties pre-agree that the review will produce one of two outcomes:
  - a closure-free settlement if the numbers work, or
  - a narrower set of fallback measures if they do not.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It addresses management’s demand for hard numbers and labour’s objection to closures being used as a threat before alternatives are tested.

---

### 3) Shared-sacrifice package with asymmetric protections
A third option is a **shared-sacrifice model** that reduces the burden asymmetry.

**Structure:**
- Labour contribution:
  - temporary moderation,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - bonus reductions or deferrals.
- Management contribution:
  - executive restraint,
  - overhead reductions,
  - governance commitments,
  - and no dividend or payout escalation during restructuring.
- Owner/investor contribution:
  - patience on returns,
  - explicit restructuring support,
  - possible capital support tied to investment and transformation.
- State contribution:
  - facilitation, training support, and regional adjustment measures.

**Core trade-off:**  
Labour gets stronger job and site protections than management currently accepts, but not absolute permanence; management gets real cost relief and operational flexibility, but not unrestricted closure rights.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are non-negotiable, what specific, quantified measures can you accept that deliver the same durable cost and capacity reduction management is demanding?**

### Industrial Union
**What is the maximum temporary contribution you would accept from workers if it were paired with binding investment, redeployment guarantees, and an independently verified closure-free savings plan?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Can you present, within this process, a plant-by-plant quantified comparison showing why closure-free measures are insufficient before any closure decision is treated as necessary?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete leverage will the state use to ensure the company actually tests closure-free options before any closure is considered, rather than merely urging restraint?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Will you support a time-limited no-closure framework if it is tied to measurable milestones and an independent review, or do you require closure authority to remain fully open from the start?**

### Institutional Investors
**What level of temporary compromise would you accept if it is backed by verifiable milestones, and what evidence would you require to conclude that a closure-free deal is credible?**
