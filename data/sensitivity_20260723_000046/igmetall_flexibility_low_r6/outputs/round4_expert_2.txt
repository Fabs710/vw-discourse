From an industrial and financial verification standpoint, the record still supports only one firm conclusion: **restructuring is necessary, but neither a closure-free plan nor a closure-based plan has yet been proven on the evidence presented here.**

## What can be verified now
- There is broad agreement that the current German footprint is too costly and overcapacity exists.
- There is also broad agreement that any solution must be durable, not symbolic.
- But there is **no plant-by-plant quantified comparison** yet showing:
  - which sites are structurally uneconomic,
  - what savings closures would actually deliver net of one-time costs,
  - and whether closure-free measures can match those savings.

## What is still missing
A decision-grade comparison needs, for **each German plant**:
- current and projected utilization,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- labor cost per unit,
- variable cost per unit,
- capex required to remain competitive,
- logistics/supply-chain effects,
- one-time closure or transfer costs,
- headcount impact,
- and a 3–5 year NPV / margin impact comparison.

## What the current statements do and do not prove
### Management / capital side
They have asserted that closures may be necessary, but in this record they have **not yet demonstrated necessity quantitatively**.

### Labour / works council / union
They have asserted that closures are avoidable, but in this record they have **not yet demonstrated equivalence quantitatively**.

## Industrial judgment
So the evidence supports **an independent plant-by-plant verification first**, using common assumptions and a binding timetable. That is the only defensible next step if the parties want to know whether a closure-free package is realistic or whether deeper structural measures are unavoidable.
