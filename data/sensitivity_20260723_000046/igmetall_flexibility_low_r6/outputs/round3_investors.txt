We agree with the diagnosis, and we are still not persuaded on the remedy.

## Where we agree
- The current structure is not sustainable.
- The company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a symbolic one.
- Capacity has to come down.
- Labour costs have to become competitive.
- Investment in future products, technology, and qualification is essential.
- A prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.

That is the right diagnosis, and we support the objective of restoring margins and capital discipline.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A categorical “no closures” line is not credible without a quantified alternative
To the Works Council and union: your insistence on protecting jobs and sites is understandable, but from a shareholder perspective it is **not sufficient**. You keep asserting that all German plants can remain open if management, owners, and shareholders contribute enough. Maybe. But so far there is **no plant-by-plant evidence** in the record showing that a closure-free package can deliver the same durable cost and capacity reduction.

Temporary concessions, working-time measures, redeployment, and bonus restraint may help. They do **not yet prove** that the cost base becomes structurally competitive.

### 2) Management has not yet proven that closures are necessary
To management: we are not signing up to closures as a reflex. You have argued forcefully that the cost base is too high, but you have **not yet shown necessity**, only plausibility. If closures are on the table, then you need to demonstrate:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- the savings from closure scenarios,
- why closure-free measures are insufficient,
- and how the restructuring restores return on sales on a durable basis.

“Market reality” is not enough. We want a quantified case.

### 3) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable unless they are truly unavoidable
We dislike forced layoffs because they are disruptive and can trigger prolonged conflict, which is value-destructive. But we also will not support a structure that preserves excess capacity and leaves the company uncompetitive. If labour can’t deliver a serious substitute, then insisting that no one ever leaves is not a solution either.

## What would move us
We would support a deal if it credibly delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a clear path back to competitive margins,
- no prolonged strike damage,
- transparent milestones and accountability,
- and investment linked to the restructuring.

That can be achieved with or without closures. The outcome matters more than the instrument.

## On “shared sacrifice”
We support shared sacrifice, but only if it is real and balanced:
- employee contribution should be substantive, not symbolic;
- management should commit to real operational discipline;
- owners should accept that value preservation may require patience and governance restraint;
- and the state should facilitate a workable solution rather than freeze the problem.

But “shared” does **not** mean shareholders underwrite a deal that leaves the company structurally weak. That would be a bad compromise dressed up as balance.

## On the expert assessments
The experts have essentially confirmed the key point: a closure-free plan is **possible in theory**, but only if it is backed by hard numbers showing equivalence. We do not have that evidence yet.

So we are not prepared to endorse either extreme:
- not a closure-at-all-costs strategy,
- and not a closure-free strategy that cannot demonstrate comparable savings.

## What we want next
We want the promised **plant-by-plant verification** and a side-by-side comparison of:
1. closure-based savings,
2. closure-free savings,
3. headcount and utilization effects,
4. investment requirements,
5. and long-term value impact.

If labour can produce a closure-free package that genuinely closes the gap, we would support it. If not, management should not be prevented from pursuing tougher measures. But no one should expect shareholders to back an arrangement that leaves the business uncompetitive and calls that compromise.

### Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution. We are **not** open to:
- cosmetic concessions,
- prolonged conflict,
- or a settlement that preserves the current cost base.

Bring the numbers. Without them, neither side has made the case.
