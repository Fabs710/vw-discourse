As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for management’s restructuring mistakes.

If the company wants a viable future, then it must be built on:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- job security through 2030,
- no forced layoffs,
- real co-determination in shaping the transformation,
- and a fair, negotiated contribution from all sides, not a one-sided sacrifice by the workforce.

We understand the pressure on the business. We know the market is difficult. But “competitiveness” cannot mean that workers pay the full bill while plants are closed and livelihoods destroyed. That is not restructuring; that is social demolition.

We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and future-proof investment,
- productivity measures,
- and a fair, temporary employee contribution if it is balanced by binding guarantees from management and the owners.

What we are not prepared to discuss is:
- shutting German sites,
- firing people against their will,
- or treating the workforce as the easiest variable in the equation.

If management wants our support, it must come with a serious plan that protects jobs, protects sites, and protects the future of the German plants to 2030 and beyond. Without that, it will face determined resistance from the workforce and from the co-determination side.
