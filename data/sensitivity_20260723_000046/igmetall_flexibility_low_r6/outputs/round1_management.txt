Let me be equally direct.

I hear the clear rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies. I understand the political and social sensitivity. But I cannot accept a position that simply declares German plants untouchable while ignoring the reality of the business.

The facts are not negotiable:
- our European market is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- our German cost base is far above target,
- and the brand is not meeting its return-on-sales objective.

If we respond with slogans about “shared sacrifice” but leave the structural cost problem intact, we will all be back here in a worse crisis within a short time. That is not responsible restructuring.

Where I agree:
- We need a negotiated solution, not unilateral imposition.
- We need a credible future for German sites.
- We need investment in new products, technology, and qualification.
- We need some form of internal redeployment and capacity adjustment rather than simple destruction for its own sake.

Where I disagree:
- A blanket “no closures” line is not a viable starting point if the numbers do not work.
- “No compulsory redundancies” is only possible if there is a real, durable alternative that delivers the same cost reduction.
- A temporary or symbolic employee contribution is not enough. We need a structural labour-cost contribution and durable capacity reduction.

So here is my position:
We are prepared to discuss alternatives to closures, but only if they achieve the same scale and permanence of savings. That means:
- meaningful reduction in excess capacity,
- lower labour costs on a lasting basis,
- operational flexibility,
- and a settlement that restores competitiveness through 2030.

If the employee side wants to rule out closures completely, then it must put forward a package that replaces those savings in full, not partially. Otherwise, we are just postponing the inevitable.

And let me be clear: prolonged strikes will not improve the company’s position. They will damage output, reputation, and confidence further. I do not want confrontation for its own sake, but I will not pretend that a cosmetic deal is acceptable.

So yes, I am open to a negotiated path. But it must be a serious one:
- no cosmetic guarantees,
- no short-term compromise that preserves an uncompetitive structure,
- and no outcome that fails to deliver durable savings.

If you want us to rule out plant closures, then show me a package that truly fixes the cost base. If not, then all options remain on the table.
