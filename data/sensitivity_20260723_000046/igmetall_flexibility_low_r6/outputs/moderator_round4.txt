Here is a facilitator-style synthesis of the plateau and the next-step options.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is about **the permissible form of restructuring**:

- **Management, the family holding, and investors** want the option of closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available if the numbers justify them.
- **Works Council and union** want a binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy settlement, with worker contributions only if temporary and matched by others.
- **State government** wants to protect sites and jobs, but also needs a plan that is operationally credible.
- **Expert review** has not yet resolved the issue because there is still no shared plant-by-plant scenario model with agreed assumptions.

So the core impasse is:
**Who bears the burden of structural adjustment, and whether plant closures may remain an available fallback before a quantified comparison is completed.**

---

## Three concrete compromise positions for consideration

### 1) Sequenced decision model with a no-closure first test
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Adopt a binding two-stage process:

- **Stage 1:** independent plant-by-plant verification of a closure-free restructuring package, with full access to site-level data and a fixed deadline.
- **Stage 2:** only if Stage 1 fails to close the cost/capacity gap, compare against a limited closure-based scenario under the same assumptions.

**Bridge value:**  
This addresses labour’s demand that closures not be the default, while preserving management’s claim that closures cannot be ruled out forever without evidence.

---

### 2) Temporary no-closure moratorium with explicit review triggers
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Agree on a time-limited no-closure period during which:
- no site is closed,
- no compulsory redundancies are implemented,
- worker contributions are limited and temporary,
- and all parties participate in a quantified restructuring review.

At the end of the period, any proposal for closure must meet predefined triggers, such as:
- verified utilization thresholds,
- fixed-cost gap targets,
- and a documented failure of redeployment/capacity-balancing measures.

**Bridge value:**  
This gives works council and union immediate protection, while giving management and capital-side actors a path to revisit deeper measures if the verified data require it.

---

### 3) Mixed restructuring package with site-specific preservation and site-specific adjustment
**Facilitator proposal:**  
Instead of an all-or-nothing rule, classify plants into categories:
- **core sites**: preserved with investment and productivity measures,
- **adjustment sites**: continue operating but with capacity, shift, or product-mix changes,
- **review sites**: subject to a later, evidence-based decision if they remain uncompetitive after a defined improvement period.

This would be paired with:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time moderation,
- limited wage/bonus restraint,
- and binding no-forced-dismissal rules during the adjustment phase.

**Bridge value:**  
This creates differentiated treatment rather than a blanket closure fight, which may be more acceptable to the state, family, and labour than immediate shutdown logic.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**Question:**  
What specific plant-by-plant indicators, if met by closure-free measures, would lead you to rule out closures for the current restructuring cycle?

### To Works Council
**Question:**  
What quantified savings, by plant and by measure, would you accept as sufficient proof that a closure-free restructuring can fully close the competitiveness gap?

### To the Trade Union
**Question:**  
What temporary worker contributions are you prepared to accept, and what exact binding safeguards would you require to ensure they do not become permanent insecurity?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Will you commit to supporting a verification process whose findings may be uncomfortable politically, provided the process is genuinely independent and plant-specific?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
Are you prepared to back a temporary no-closure sequencing if it is the only way to secure trust and obtain credible operational numbers?

### To the Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
If a verified closure-free plan delivers the same NPV and margin improvement as a closure-based plan, would you support it even if the footprint remains larger than you prefer?

### To the Expert
**Question:**  
What minimum data package and timeline are necessary for a decision-grade comparison that both sides would have to recognize as fair?
