As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: I share management’s concern about competitiveness, but I do **not** accept plant closures as the default answer, and I do **not** accept a restructuring that shifts the full burden onto workers and the German regions.

I agree with management on one point: the status quo is not sustainable. The company does need a durable solution on costs, capacity, and future investment. But I strongly disagree with the implication that the only credible path is closures and compulsory redundancies. That would be economically damaging, politically reckless, and in conflict with the role we are supposed to play as a long-term shareholder and regional stabilizer.

To the Works Council and the union: I support your core red lines against closures and forced layoffs. Those are not just slogans; they reflect the company’s social responsibility and the region’s legitimate interest. At the same time, if we want to protect sites and jobs, we cannot ignore the need for real adjustment. A purely defensive “no” will not secure the company’s future. I expect you to engage seriously on productivity, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, qualification, and a fair but temporary employee contribution if it is genuinely balanced.

To management: if you want political and shareholder support, you need to come forward with a restructuring that is compatible with the company’s role as a regional industrial anchor. That means:
- no plant closures in Germany as the first resort,
- no mass compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of established co-determination and job-security protections,
- and a credible investment and product commitment for the German sites.

I am prepared to consider a negotiated package that includes:
- capacity reductions,
- temporary working-time measures,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation investment,
- and a balanced contribution from labour and capital alike.

But I am **not** prepared to support a plan that relies on closing plants and cutting deep into the domestic workforce while preserving the rest of the structure untouched. That would be socially destructive and, frankly, short-sighted.

So my position is this: bring me a restructuring that protects the company’s future **and** preserves the region’s industrial base. If the proposal still depends on closures and sweeping job destruction, then it will meet resistance from me.
