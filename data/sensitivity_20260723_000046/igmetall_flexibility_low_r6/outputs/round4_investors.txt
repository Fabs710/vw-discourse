We are still in the same place on substance: **we need durable cost reduction and a credible path back to competitive margins**. On that criterion, I am not persuaded by either extreme.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the status quo is not sustainable.
- **The Works Council and union are right** that symbolic concessions are not enough and that any employee contribution must be matched by others.
- **The state and family are right** that prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- **The experts are right** that this must be tested with plant-level numbers, not slogans.

## Where we disagree
### 1) With the Works Council and union
Your position is not acceptable to us as stated.

A flat **“all German plants stay open, no compulsory redundancies, no closures as a bargaining instrument”** line is **not yet credible** from a shareholder perspective. You keep asking management to prove necessity, but you have not shown a closure-free package that closes the cost and capacity gap at the required scale.

Temporary moderation, redeployment, and working-time changes may help. They do **not** by themselves prove structural competitiveness. We will not back a deal that preserves the current cost base and calls that a solution.

### 2) With management
Management still has **not** proven that closures are necessary. “Plausible” is not enough. If closures remain on the table, we need:
- plant-by-plant economics,
- utilization and fixed-cost data,
- quantified savings from closure scenarios,
- and a clean comparison against non-closure measures.

Without that, closure talk is leverage, not evidence-based restructuring.

### 3) With the family and state
We understand the political and governance constraints, but we will not support a settlement that simply delays the adjustment or protects every site regardless of economics. That would be a value-destructive cosmetic compromise.

## Our position
We support a **serious, quantified restructuring** that:
- reduces capacity materially,
- lowers the cost base durably,
- avoids prolonged strikes,
- and restores return on sales.

That can be achieved with or without closures — but only if the numbers prove it. Right now, they do not.

We want the promised verification process to proceed immediately and transparently.

## Bottom line
- **No support for a cosmetic no-closure deal**
- **No support for closures without proof of necessity**
- **Support only for a quantified package that credibly restores competitiveness**

If the next round produces hard numbers showing a closure-free plan can truly deliver durable savings, we will consider it. If not, management must be free to pursue deeper structural measures.
