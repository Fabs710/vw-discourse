### 1) What has been agreed
- **All sides accept that the company faces a real competitiveness and cost problem.**
- There is broad agreement that any solution must be **more than a short-term cut** and should include a **credible industrial/future-product plan**.
- Several parties accept that a settlement should, in principle, combine:
  - **capacity reduction or restructuring,**
  - **working-time and redeployment measures,**
  - **qualification/investment commitments,**
  - and some form of **workforce contribution**.
- There is shared concern that a drawn-out, value-destructive confrontation would be harmful.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Works council, union, and regional state government reject closures in Germany.  
  - Management, family holding, and investors keep closures as a possible tool if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works council, union, and state government reject them.  
  - Management will not rule them out if required for structural adjustment.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030:**  
  - Labour side wants binding guarantees.  
  - Management and shareholders say blanket guarantees are too rigid and conditional security is the most they can discuss.
- **Scope of employee contribution:**  
  - Labour side will only contribute as part of a balanced deal with strong protections.  
  - Management and shareholders want a more substantial and measurable contribution.
- **Governance and political constraints:**  
  - State government insists on preserving co-determination and blocking rights.  
  - Management and capital-side actors want enough flexibility to implement restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost and capacity reduction is necessary** to restore competitiveness.
- **Whether equivalent savings can be achieved without closures** and, if so, what measures would actually deliver them.
- **What level of workforce contribution is acceptable** in pay, working time, flexibility, or exits.
- **What binding investment commitments management/shareholders are prepared to make** in exchange for labour concessions.
- **How to balance social stability and regional impact** against financial and operational requirements.
- **What “conditional security” would mean in practice**: duration, triggers, exceptions, and enforcement.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **package design**. The next round should identify:
- a **quantified target** for cost and capacity reduction,
- the **specific instruments** to achieve it,
- the **minimum acceptable protections** for jobs/sites,
- and the **investment and governance commitments** required in return.

A useful next step would be for each side to state:
1. the **maximum acceptable workforce contribution**,  
2. the **minimum acceptable job/site protection**, and  
3. the **measures they are prepared to put on the table immediately**.
