I will be direct.

I agree with the works council on the core facts: this is a real competitiveness problem, and a durable settlement has to be more than a temporary patch. I also agree with the state government that a one-sided shock strategy would be socially and politically destructive. And I agree with the moderator that the discussion now has to move from slogans to a package.

But I do **not** agree with management, the family holding, or investors on one crucial point: **you are still treating closure and forced job cuts as acceptable fallback options while asking the workforce to pre-commit to sacrifice.** That is not a fair basis for bargaining.

## What I support
The union is still prepared to negotiate a serious transformation package that includes:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary departure schemes,
- temporary and limited labour-cost contributions,
- and productivity and flexibility measures.

But this only works if there is a **balanced deal** with real commitments from management and owners.

## What is not acceptable
Let me be clear:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework as the price of entry.**
- **No open-ended insecurity disguised as “conditionality.”**

If the company insists on keeping closures and forced layoffs on the table, then it is not negotiating a solution; it is trying to bargain under threat. That will not get the workforce’s consent.

## On the arguments from management
Management says the cost base is too high. That may be true. But a cost problem does not justify shifting all adjustment onto workers while preserving management’s freedom to use the harshest instruments.

If you want an employee contribution, then show the same seriousness on your side:
- binding investment commitments,
- future product allocation,
- measurable management and owner contributions,
- and a credible industrial plan that protects the German footprint.

Without that, demands for wage moderation or flexibility are one-sided.

## On the arguments from the family holding and investors
You both say you want long-term enterprise value. Then listen carefully: **industrial peace and workforce legitimacy are part of long-term value**.

A strategy that provokes prolonged strikes, destroys trust, and weakens the company’s social architecture is not “discipline”; it is a value risk. A durable solution is one that is economically credible **and** socially sustainable.

## On what could move us
We are prepared to discuss a package if it contains all of the following:

1. **Binding no-closure commitment for German plants**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Employment security through 2030**
4. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**
5. **Meaningful investment in future products and technologies**
6. **Capacity adjustment through negotiated instruments, not coercion**
7. **Preservation of collective bargaining standards and co-determination**

That is the minimum for a credible settlement.

## On the next step
If management wants to be taken seriously, bring forward:
- a quantified savings target,
- a specific non-closure restructuring plan,
- the exact volume of employee contribution you seek,
- and the investment package you are prepared to lock in.

Then we can judge whether it is fair and workable.

If not, then the union will continue to mobilise. We do not want escalation for its own sake, but we will not accept a restructuring where the workforce bears all the pain while others keep all the options.
