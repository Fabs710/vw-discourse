I agree with the works council on the core principle: this crisis cannot be solved by forcing the workforce to pay alone.

Let me be equally clear from the trade union side:

- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No dismantling of the collective wage framework as a one-way street.**

Management talks about competitiveness, but competitiveness achieved by tearing up employment security, hollowing out standards, and threatening sites is not a durable solution. It shifts the risk entirely onto employees while protecting capital and managerial discretion. That is not acceptable.

We have already shown that we are willing to negotiate responsibly. We have signaled openness to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time solutions,
- temporary or partial labour-cost contributions,
- and employee concessions,

**but only** if these are part of a balanced package with real commitments from management and owners. That means:
- binding job and site security,
- credible investment in future products and technologies,
- fair burden-sharing,
- and no attempt to use the restructuring to break collective bargaining standards.

If management insists on closures or forced layoffs, then it is choosing confrontation. The union has the mandate and the means to respond, including industrial action. We do not want escalation for its own sake, but we will not be pressured into accepting a restructuring that makes workers absorb all the pain.

So our position is straightforward:  
**A negotiated transformation, yes. A one-sided cutback program at the expense of jobs, wages, and sites, no.**
