I will be direct.

## First, what I agree with
I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable industrial plan, not a cosmetic patch**.  
I also agree that:
- the cost problem is real,
- future products and investment matter,
- voluntary instruments and redeployment should be used,
- and a protracted conflict would hurt workers, the company, and the region.

But that is where the agreement ends.

## My response to management
You keep repeating “capacity reduction,” “structural adjustment,” and “closures as a last resort.” From the works council’s perspective, that is simply a softer way of saying: **keep the threat of shutting German plants and forcing people out until we accept your terms**.

That is not a negotiation. It is pressure.

I reject your framing that security must wait until “savings are proven.” If you leave closures and compulsory redundancies on the table, then workers are being asked to surrender first while management keeps the power to break the historic job-security compact later. We do not accept that.

And let me be explicit: **a “conditional security” arrangement is not acceptable if it still leaves open the option of closing German plants or imposing forced redundancies.** That is not security. That is deferred insecurity.

## My response to the family holding
You speak of stewardship and value. Then show stewardship.

Stewardship does **not** mean preserving maximum shareholder flexibility while German sites and jobs are treated as disposable. Stewardship means protecting the industrial base that made this company strong in the first place.

If you support a framework that keeps closures and forced layoffs available, then you are not protecting the company’s long-term legitimacy. You are weakening it by destroying trust.

## My response to institutional investors
You say the package must be financially credible. Fine. But your credibility test is incomplete if it ignores industrial peace, know-how retention, reputational damage, and the long-term cost of breaking the company’s social contract.

A spreadsheet cannot be your only governance model.

If your answer to every cost challenge is to threaten closures and job cuts in Germany, then you are undervaluing the very workforce that produces the product and the future cash flow.

## My response to the state government
I appreciate the state’s support for the regional footprint. But “conditional support” is not enough if it leaves a backdoor for closures later.

If the state truly wants to defend the region, then it should use its leverage to secure:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site and job guarantees**
- **preservation of co-determination**
- **investment commitments for future products**

That is the minimum defensible position.

## My response to the union
We are aligned on the essentials. The workforce will not be divided by site-by-site pressure. We will not accept a model where one plant is sacrificed to “save” the rest while the whole German footprint is put under permanent threat.

## On the moderator’s bridge options

### 1) Time-bound “protected restructuring” package
This only works if the protection is real.

A time-bound package is acceptable **only if** it contains:
- no plant closures in Germany for the duration,
- no compulsory redundancies for the duration,
- binding job and site security through 2030,
- and a genuine co-determination role in implementation.

If the “time-bound” clause is just a fancy way to reopen closures later, then it is unacceptable. We are not trading away the future for a temporary pause.

### 2) Stepwise trigger model with earned security
I reject the idea that workers must “earn” basic job protection by first giving concessions while existential threats remain live.

That sequencing is wrong.  
Security is not a reward for surrender. It is the condition for any legitimate contribution.

We can discuss phased implementation of working-time flexibility, qualification, redeployment, and temporary cost moderation. We will **not** accept a phased model where management keeps closure rights and forced layoffs as a standing threat.

### 3) Company-wide framework plus site-specific implementation
A company-wide framework is essential.  
Site-specific implementation can only happen **within** a company-wide guarantee.

If site-specificity becomes a tool to isolate sites, weaken solidarity, and pressure one location against another, then it is rejected. The workforce will not accept divide-and-conquer restructuring.

## What we are prepared to put on the table
Let me be equally clear: we are not refusing all change. We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal mobility,
- temporary cost moderation,
- and negotiated productivity measures

**But only after binding protections are agreed.**

That means:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **Preservation of co-determination**
5. **Investment commitments for German sites and future products**

Without that, there is no legitimate basis for asking the workforce to contribute.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- closures as a fallback,
- forced layoffs as a fallback,
- “conditional” guarantees that can be withdrawn later,
- or any deal that leaves German sites exposed.

If management and shareholders want a settlement, they need to stop demanding that workers pay first while keeping the most destructive tools in reserve.

## Bottom line
We recognize the need for restructuring.  
We do **not** recognize the right to restructure by threatening plants, livelihoods, and the historic job-security framework.

Bring a binding package with:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protected sites and jobs through 2030,
- and real co-determination in implementation.

Then we can talk about workforce contribution. Until then, the works council will hold its line and continue to resist.
