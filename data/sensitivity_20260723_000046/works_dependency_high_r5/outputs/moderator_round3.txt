### Core impasse
The deadlock is centered on **sequencing and enforceability**:

- **Labour side** (works council and union) wants **binding security first**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and a job/site guarantee through 2030 before any sacrifice.
- **Management, family holding, and investors** want **economic adjustment first**: they will not remove closures and involuntary redundancies from the toolset until savings are proven.
- **State government** is trying to preserve the regional footprint, but only conditionally.
- The disagreement is not about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **which risks are removed up front, which remain as fallback tools, and how to verify that concessions are reciprocal rather than one-sided**.

In practical terms, the impasse is:  
**labour will not concede while existential threats remain live; owners/management will not remove those threats without guaranteed savings.**

---

### Possible compromise positions

#### 1) Time-bound “protected restructuring” package
A package could be negotiated that includes:
- **no plant closures in Germany for a defined period**,
- **no compulsory redundancies for that same period**,
- a **quantified employee contribution**,
- and a **jointly monitored savings and transformation plan**.

To address management’s concern, the no-closure/no-forced-layoff commitment would be **time-limited and performance-linked**, with objective review points.  
To address labour’s concern, the commitments would be **written and binding**, not merely aspirational.

---

#### 2) Stepwise trigger model with earned security
A second bridge would be a phased arrangement:

- **Phase 1:** labour provides initial concessions on working time, flexibility, qualification, or temporary cost moderation.
- **Phase 2:** once agreed savings and investment milestones are independently verified, the company grants stronger security commitments, such as site protection or a no-forced-layoff clause.
- **Phase 3:** if targets are missed, a defined escalation process applies, but only after joint review.

This would allow management to avoid a blank upfront guarantee while giving labour **a clear path to earning security**, not just hoping for it.

---

#### 3) Company-wide framework plus site-specific implementation
A third compromise would combine:
- a **company-wide framework** on employment security, co-determination, and no forced redundancies as first resort,
- with **site-specific agreements** on investment, product allocation, and capacity changes.

This would prevent isolated site-by-site pressure while still giving management room to tailor operational changes.  
It also allows the state to defend the regional footprint without requiring every site to be identical.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council (Betriebsrat)
**What specific, measurable workforce contribution can the works council commit to immediately if management agrees to a time-bound no-closure / no-forced-layoff framework?**

#### Industrial Union
**Which concessions can the union credibly mobilize among members now, and what verification mechanism would make those concessions acceptable to your side?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target, timeline, and capacity reduction are you prepared to put on the table so others can judge whether a non-closure alternative is financially credible?**

#### Regional State Government
**What concrete leverage are you prepared to use to convert your political support for the regional footprint into enforceable commitments from the company?**

#### Controlling Family Holding
**Under what precise economic conditions would you accept a binding no-closure / no-forced-layoff period, and who would determine whether those conditions are met?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What level of quantified improvement in margin and cash generation would make you support a deal that limits restructuring tools for a defined period?**
