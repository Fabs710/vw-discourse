I will be equally direct.

I agree with management on the diagnosis: the company cannot carry a permanently uncompetitive cost base, and pretending otherwise would be irresponsible. The market is changing fast, margins matter, and the family holding will not back a strategy that simply postpones the adjustment and erodes enterprise value.

That said, I do **not** accept a restructuring that destroys value through prolonged conflict, reputational damage, or an uncontrolled breakdown of labour relations. Nor do I accept a plan that treats plant closures as a default instrument before every serious alternative has been exhausted.

### To management
Your point on competitiveness is valid. But you need to be clearer that you are not just pursuing a cost-cutting exercise; you need a **durable industrial logic** that restores profitability and keeps the company investable. If the package is seen as purely punitive, it will fail politically and operationally.

What I can support is:
- a credible reduction in structural overcapacity,
- measurable labour-cost improvement,
- higher flexibility,
- and a real transformation plan tied to future products and investment.

What I cannot support is:
- open-ended confrontation,
- a strategy that risks major industrial unrest without a realistic path to resolution,
- or anything that weakens governance control or turns this into a permanent public crisis.

### To the works council and union
I understand your insistence on social responsibility and stability. That is not unreasonable. But your absolute “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to guarantees” position goes too far if the company’s economics do not work.

If you block every meaningful structural adjustment up front, you are not protecting the company; you are protecting an unsustainable status quo. That would eventually harm jobs, investment, and the long-term position of the German sites even more.

I am prepared to support:
- negotiated workforce reductions through voluntary mechanisms,
- redeployment and qualification,
- working-time solutions,
- and a limited, fair contribution from labour,

**if and only if** these are part of a package that genuinely restores competitiveness.

But I will not endorse a deal where the owners and management commit to investment while the workforce contributes little or nothing to the necessary adjustment. That would not be fair, and it would not be economically credible.

### To the regional state government
I appreciate your concern for regional stability, and I agree that a destructive closure strategy would be costly in many ways. But the state cannot make the company’s structural problems disappear by vetoing hard decisions indefinitely.

If you want to exercise blocking power responsibly, then you also have to accept that the company may need deeper operational change than you would prefer. Otherwise, you are simply shifting the burden into the future and onto the balance sheet.

### Where I stand
The family holding’s priority is long-term enterprise value, not symbolic wins in any one round of bargaining. That means we support:
- a **substantial and durable competitiveness program**,
- **labour peace**,
- and **investment in the future**.

We do **not** support:
- a no-change outcome,
- indefinite protection of all sites regardless of economics,
- or a solution that makes the company ungovernable.

So the condition for our support is clear:  
we need a package that materially improves the cost base and restores margins, while avoiding unnecessary destabilisation. If that requires difficult compromises from all sides, so be it. If it requires only management to retreat and the workforce to contribute nothing, that will not get our backing.

In short: **yes to a serious restructuring; no to a value-destroying standoff; and no to preserving every current structure if the business case no longer supports it.**
