## Core impasse
There are two competing logics that have not been reconciled:

1. **Labour-side security logic**
   - No plant closures
   - No compulsory redundancies
   - Binding job/site guarantees through 2030
   - Co-determination preserved
   - Employee contribution only if security is first guaranteed

2. **Management/shareholder flexibility logic**
   - Structural capacity reduction is necessary
   - Closures must remain available if savings are insufficient
   - Compulsory redundancies cannot be ruled out absolutely
   - Guarantees cannot be unconditional or freeze restructuring

In short:  
**The labour side wants security before sacrifice; management and shareholders want sacrifice before security.**  
That sequencing dispute is blocking progress.

A second layer of impasse is that **each side is asking the other to move first on the most sensitive point**:
- labour asks for closure and layoff tools to be removed before concessions,
- management asks for concessions before committing to no closures or no forced redundancies.

---

## Possible compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridge options that could narrow the gap.

### 1) Conditional no-closure / no-forced-layoff package with triggers
A negotiated agreement could include:
- **No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined initial period**
- In return, the workforce commits to quantified cost reductions and flexibility measures
- If savings targets are not met by agreed milestones, parties reopen the package through a formal review mechanism

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives labour immediate security, while giving management a structured escape valve tied to objective performance, rather than an open-ended threat.

---

### 2) Site-specific agreements instead of a blanket 2030 guarantee
Rather than one company-wide absolute guarantee, the parties could negotiate:
- **site-by-site commitments**
- investment and product allocation for each German location
- employment/security terms linked to specific business plans
- redeployment and qualification commitments for sites under pressure

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It preserves the principle of security, but allows differentiated treatment where economics differ, which may be more acceptable to management and investors.

---

### 3) “No involuntary redundancies first” plus a jointly monitored adjustment corridor
A package could establish:
- a strong preference for voluntary exits, retirement, redeployment, qualification, and working-time measures
- a jointly monitored headcount reduction corridor
- compulsory redundancies excluded **unless** jointly verified alternatives are exhausted and targets remain unmet

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It does not require management to abandon structural adjustment, but it makes involuntary layoffs the last resort rather than a standing option.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific quantified workforce contribution are you prepared to make if management agrees to a time-bound no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy framework with review milestones?**

### Industrial Union
**What level of cost and capacity reduction would the union regard as sufficient to justify a limited, time-bound employee contribution and conditional security?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target and timeline do you require before you would convert “closures and compulsory redundancies remain available” into a conditional-last-resort clause rather than a standing option?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete form of conditional support or political backing would the state offer if the company accepted a binding no-closure framework paired with measurable savings and investment commitments?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Would the family support a package that excludes closures and forced layoffs for a defined period if it contains verified savings, capacity reduction, and credible margin recovery triggers?**

### Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified improvement in margin, capacity, and labour cost would make you comfortable backing a settlement that prioritizes negotiated workforce adjustments over closures?**
