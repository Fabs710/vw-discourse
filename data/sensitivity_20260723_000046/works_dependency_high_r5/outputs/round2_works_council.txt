Management is still asking the workforce to absorb the main pain while leaving the most destructive options on the table. That is not a package; that is pressure.

Let me respond point by point.

### On the diagnosis
Yes, we accept that the company faces a serious competitiveness problem. We are not denying the market reality. But diagnosing a problem does **not** justify making German plants and German jobs the first sacrifice.

### On closures
We reject closures in Germany. Full stop.

The fact that management wants to keep closures “available” as a tool is exactly what creates mistrust and escalation. You cannot demand labour peace while holding plant closures over people’s heads. That is not negotiation; that is coercion.

If you want a serious discussion, then remove closures from the table and prove that you are prepared to solve the cost problem through alternatives that preserve the industrial footprint.

### On compulsory redundancies
We reject compulsory redundancies as well. There is no credible future for this company if restructuring means forcing thousands of employees out against their will.

If headcount has to change, then the only acceptable path is through:
- voluntary exit schemes,
- retirement transitions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and working-time solutions.

### On job and site guarantees
A binding guarantee through 2030 is not a side issue. It is the minimum basis for any workable compromise.

Without that, any “contribution” from the workforce is just a blank cheque. We will not sign up to a deal where we give up pay, flexibility, or protections today only to face the next round of cuts tomorrow.

### On employee contribution
We are prepared to discuss a workforce contribution, but only under three conditions:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding security for jobs and sites through 2030**

That contribution can include:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary cost moderation,
- internal redeployment,
- training and qualification,
- and limited, time-bound financial concessions if necessary.

But let me be clear: we will not accept an open-ended wage sacrifice while management keeps the right to close plants later. That is not balanced burden-sharing.

### On investment and future products
Here I agree with the state government and, in principle, with the need for a real industrial plan. If you want the workforce to help stabilise the company, then the company and owners must commit to:
- future product allocation for German sites,
- investment in new technologies,
- and concrete commitments that preserve long-term employment here.

But these commitments cannot be vague promises. We need binding terms, not press releases.

### On governance
We also reject any attempt to weaken co-determination or sideline the works council in the name of “flexibility.” The co-determination framework is not an obstacle; it is the mechanism that makes this company stable in the long run. If management wants our trust, it must respect that.

### Where we stand
So the position remains unchanged:

- **No plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination and job-security rules**
- **A negotiated, fair employee contribution only as part of a secure package**

If management and shareholders want a solution, they need to come back with a concrete plan that meets those conditions. Until then, we will continue to resist, including industrial mobilisation if necessary.
