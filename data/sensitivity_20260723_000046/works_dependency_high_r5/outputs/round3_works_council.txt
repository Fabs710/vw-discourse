I will respond directly.

## First: the sequencing problem
I hear the moderator’s framing clearly: labour wants security before sacrifice; management and shareholders want sacrifice before security. That is exactly why this is stalled.

From the works council’s perspective, the answer is straightforward: **security must come first in order for any sacrifice to be legitimate**. We are not going to hand over cost concessions while closures and forced layoffs remain live threats. That is not a balanced package; it is extortion by another name.

## Where I agree with others
I agree with all parties on one point: this company needs a real industrial plan, not a PR exercise.

I also agree that:
- the cost problem is real,
- future products and investment matter,
- a drawn-out conflict would hurt everyone,
- and voluntary, negotiated instruments are far preferable to confrontation.

But agreement on the diagnosis does **not** mean agreement on the remedy.

## Where I disagree sharply

### To management
You keep saying closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available until savings are proven. That is precisely the problem.

If you keep the most destructive tools on the table, then every “negotiation” is conducted under threat. Workers are being asked to contribute while management preserves the option to break the historic job-security compact. We do not accept that.

And let’s be clear: **“conditional security” is too vague if it still leaves open the possibility of plant closures in Germany.** A condition that can be used to justify shutdown later is not security. It is deferred insecurity.

If you want a contribution from the workforce, then you must first commit to:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **preservation of co-determination and job-security rules**

Without those, there is nothing to bargain over.

### To the family holding
You talk about stewardship and value, but stewardship does not mean preserving shareholder flexibility at the expense of workers’ livelihoods. It means protecting the company’s industrial base.

You say closures must remain available “if necessary.” Necessary according to whom? Management has already chosen to define the workforce as the primary adjustment variable. That is not stewardship; that is pressure politics.

If you want legitimacy from the workforce, then support a package that proves you are not willing to destroy German sites as an easy escape route.

### To institutional investors
You keep talking about margins and modeling. Fine. But if your model requires threatening plant closures and forced redundancies in Germany, then your model is incomplete.

A value-maximizing solution cannot ignore industrial peace, skilled labour retention, reputational damage, and the long-term cost of breaking trust. The cheapest line item is not always the best strategy. The workers are not a disposable variable in your spreadsheet.

### To the state government
I appreciate your defence of regional stability and co-determination. But I need to be equally direct: **conditional support is not enough if it leaves the door open to closures later.** The state has leverage here. Use it to defend the industrial footprint, not to administer a managed retreat.

If you truly stand for the region, then back a framework with:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- site protection,
- and future investment commitments.

### To the union
We are aligned on the essentials. The workforce will continue to stand behind a hard line on closures and compulsory redundancies. Industrial mobilisation remains on the table because management has not offered a credible security framework.

## On the moderator’s bridge options

### 1) “Conditional no-closure / no-forced-layoff package with triggers”
This is only acceptable if the first condition is real and binding.

If “no plant closures” means no closures for the duration of the agreement, with no hidden escape hatch, then we can talk. But if the trigger mechanism allows management to reopen closures simply because they decide targets were missed, then that is not a guarantee. That is a trap.

So I reject any trigger structure that weakens the no-closure commitment into a suggestion.

### 2) Site-specific agreements instead of a blanket 2030 guarantee
We are open to site-specific operational planning, but not to a system where each site is isolated and exposed to separate pressure.

A company-wide job and site guarantee through 2030 is still the minimum basis for fairness and equal treatment. Site-by-site deals can be discussed only if they sit **inside** that overarching guarantee, not instead of it.

### 3) “No involuntary redundancies first” plus jointly monitored corridor
This is the closest to a workable operational principle, but only if “last resort” never becomes a back door to forced layoffs.

If the company wants headcount adjustment, it must be through:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement transitions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- and negotiated productivity measures.

Compulsory redundancies remain unacceptable. “Exhausted alternatives” cannot be interpreted by management alone.

## What we are prepared to put on the table
Let me be very clear: the works council is not refusing all change. We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary cost moderation,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal mobility,
- and limited, time-bound workforce contributions

**But only after binding guarantees are agreed.**

That is the order. Not because we want to be difficult, but because workers will not pay for a restructuring that leaves their plants exposed and their jobs insecure.

## What is non-negotiable
Our red lines remain unchanged:
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**

If these are not accepted, then there is no basis for a settlement from our side.

## What would actually move us
Only one thing: a written, binding package that:
1. excludes closures,
2. excludes forced layoffs,
3. guarantees jobs and sites through 2030,
4. locks in investment and future product allocation for German plants,
5. and gives the works council a real co-determination role in implementation.

Without that, there is no reason to soften. The workforce has already shown it is prepared to resist, and we will continue to do so.

If management and shareholders want a solution, stop asking the workforce to trust threats. Put the guarantees on the table first.
