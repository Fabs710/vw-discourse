I will be equally direct.

We are not making progress if management and shareholders continue to ask for **pre-commitment from the workforce** while keeping **closures and forced layoffs** as standing threats. That is exactly the sequencing problem the moderator identified — and from the union’s side, I will not pretend it is acceptable.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on the diagnosis:
- the company has a real competitiveness problem,
- a durable solution must be structural,
- future products and investment matter,
- and a prolonged conflict would damage the company, workers, and the region.

I also agree that a package can include:
- capacity reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- and a limited workforce contribution.

So yes, we are prepared to bargain seriously.

## Where I disagree
### To management
You say closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available until savings are proven. That means the workforce is asked to fund the restructuring while the company keeps a gun on the table.

That is not a balanced negotiation. It is pressure bargaining.

And let me be clear: **we do not accept open-ended “conditional security” if it still leaves plant closures in Germany as a live option.** If the company wants a contribution from employees, it needs to show the same seriousness by removing the most destructive options from the table.

### To the family holding and investors
You keep saying this is about value and competitiveness. It is also about legitimacy, industrial peace, and the long-term cost of destroying trust.

A deal that looks good in a spreadsheet but triggers prolonged strikes, destabilises the workforce, and weakens the company’s industrial base is not a good-value deal. It is a false economy.

### To the state government
I appreciate your effort to protect the regional anchor function. But “conditional support” is not enough if it still leaves the workforce exposed to closures later. The state’s leverage should be used to secure **real site protection**, not just managed ambiguity.

## On the moderator’s bridge options

### 1) Conditional no-closure / no-forced-layoff package with triggers
This only becomes acceptable if the no-closure and no-forced-layoff commitments are **real and binding for the agreement period**.

What we could discuss is:
- no plant closures in Germany for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- a jointly monitored savings and transformation package,
- and a review mechanism that addresses execution problems **without** turning the agreement into a closure trap.

But if “triggers” mean management can reopen closures whenever it wants, then that is not security. We reject that.

### 2) Site-specific agreements
We are open to site-specific industrial planning, but not as a substitute for a company-wide protection framework.

Site-by-site differentiation can make sense for investment and product allocation. It cannot become a tool to isolate sites and weaken collective protection. The overall framework still has to protect jobs, sites, and co-determination across the company.

### 3) “No involuntary redundancies first”
This is the only principle that is operationally realistic.

We can work with:
- voluntary exit schemes,
- early retirement transitions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- reduced working time,
- productivity measures,
- and temporary cost moderation.

But let me state the boundary clearly: **compulsory redundancies are not acceptable as a normal restructuring tool.** If management insists on keeping them as a standing option, then it is refusing to share risk fairly.

## What would move us
We can discuss a workforce contribution if — and only if — the package includes all of the following:

1. **Binding no plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Employment security through 2030**
4. **A fair, time-limited employee contribution**
5. **Binding investment in future products and technology**
6. **Capacity reduction through negotiated instruments, not coercion**
7. **Preservation of collective bargaining and co-determination**

That is not an excessive list. That is the minimum for a credible settlement.

## What we are prepared to put on the table now
The union is still prepared to negotiate:
- temporary wage moderation or forfeiture of specific bonuses,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal mobility,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and voluntary exit support.

But we will only put that on the table **as part of a secure, balanced package**. We will not ask members to carry the pain while management keeps the harshest tools in reserve.

## Bottom line
If management wants a deal, then stop demanding that workers sacrifice first while leaving closures and layoffs alive.

Bring forward:
- a quantified savings target,
- a non-closure restructuring plan,
- a concrete investment roadmap,
- and binding employment security.

Then we can talk about the workforce contribution in a serious way.

If that is not possible, then the union will continue to mobilise. We do not want escalation for its own sake, but we will not accept a restructuring where the burden falls mainly on workers and the company keeps the right to dismantle the industrial footprint later.
