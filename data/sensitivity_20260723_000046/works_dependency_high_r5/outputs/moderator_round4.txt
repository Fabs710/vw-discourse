Core impasse
- **Security vs. flexibility**: labor-side stakeholders want binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy guarantees first; management, family holding, and investors want to preserve restructuring tools until the economics are fixed.
- **Sequencing dispute**: labor wants protection before concessions; management wants measurable concessions before stronger protection.
- **Credibility gap**: all sides agree the problem is real, but they dispute whether a non-closure package can deliver savings and capacity reduction comparable to closures.

Facilitator compromise proposals
1. **Time-bound protected restructuring with hard metrics**
   - A fixed-term agreement through 2030, with:
     - no plant closures during the term,
     - no compulsory redundancies during the term,
     - defined capacity-reduction and savings targets,
     - binding employee contributions,
     - binding investment commitments,
     - and jointly monitored milestones.
   - If milestones are missed, the parties return to bargaining on additional measures before any escalation.

2. **Phased security-for-delivery model**
   - Phase 1: immediate labor contribution and operational changes begin at once.
   - Phase 2: once pre-agreed savings and utilization thresholds are verified, security commitments strengthen.
   - Phase 3: only if the entire package underperforms do the parties reopen the hard-tools discussion.
   - This would avoid a blanket upfront veto while still giving labor a path to stronger protection.

3. **Company-wide framework with site-level safeguards**
   - A single company-wide restructuring agreement sets:
     - common rules for cost reduction,
     - protections against divide-and-conquer bargaining,
     - and criteria for allocation of products, investment, and capacity.
   - Site-specific measures are permitted only if they do not violate the company-wide safeguards and are reviewed jointly.

Pointed question to each stakeholder

- **Works Council (Betriebsrat):**
  - If closures and compulsory redundancies are removed as tools, what specific, quantified operational changes will you accept to prove the non-closure package closes the gap?

- **Industrial Union:**
  - What minimum measurable savings and capacity reduction would make a non-closure settlement defensible to your members and credible to the other stakeholders?

- **Group & Brand Management:**
  - What exact numeric target would you accept as evidence that a labor-based alternative is equivalent enough to justify temporary no-closure and no-forced-layoff protection?

- **Regional State Government:**
  - What enforceable conditions would justify the state using its leverage to support a time-bound no-closure package without weakening its credibility on restructuring?

- **Controlling Family Holding:**
  - Under what verified financial and operational milestones would you accept a binding protection period through 2030?

- **Institutional Investors:**
  - What concrete evidence would convince you that a non-closure package delivers durable margin recovery rather than merely delaying the problem?

- **Restructuring finance / industrial economics expert:**
  - What data points are still missing to determine whether a non-closure package can realistically match the savings of closure-led restructuring?
