I will be very direct as well.

I agree with management on one point: **the company cannot ignore structural overcapacity and a weak cost position**. If nothing changes, the long-term risk to the company, and therefore to jobs, is real. So I am not here to defend the status quo at any price.

But I clearly disagree with the idea that **plant closures in Germany must remain on the table as a normal restructuring instrument**. For this company, in this region, closures are not a technical detail. They are a political and economic shock with lasting consequences for employment, suppliers, skills, and regional stability. They would also undermine trust in the whole co-determination and social-partnership model that has supported this company for decades.

### To management
A solution that is built around threatening closures and forcing the workforce into submission will not get my support.

I can support a **serious restructuring** only if it is:
- socially balanced,
- economically credible,
- and designed to preserve the company as a regional anchor.

That means you need to come forward with a package that demonstrably closes the cost gap **without gutting German industrial capacity**. If you want flexibility from the state and the workforce, then you must also provide:
- binding investment commitments,
- clear product allocation for German sites,
- realistic capacity adjustments,
- and a negotiated burden-sharing model.

What I cannot accept is a scheme that shifts all adjustment onto employees and regions while treating plant closure as the default fallback.

### To the works council and union
I support your core red lines in principle: **no compulsory redundancies and no one-sided sacrifice by the workforce**. I also share the view that a blanket destruction of guarantees would be unacceptable.

At the same time, I will be honest: a complete refusal of any meaningful structural change would also be irresponsible. If the company is to remain competitive, there must be room for:
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- moderate and negotiated employee contributions,
- and capacity reduction where it can be done without closures.

So I am **not** endorsing inaction. I am endorsing a restructuring path that protects sites and jobs as far as possible while addressing the real cost problem.

### What I will support
I am prepared to support a negotiated package if it contains:
1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A credible cost and capacity reduction through alternatives to closure**
4. **Binding investment and future-product commitments**
5. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**
6. **A fair, limited workforce contribution as part of a balanced deal**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- closures as a first resort,
- mass layoffs,
- or a restructuring that damages the regional economy beyond repair.

And I want to be explicit on the governance point: **the state will not be sidelined**, and we will not accept any arrangement that weakens our co-determination or blocking rights. Those are part of the institutional balance that must be respected.

So my message is this:  
**We need a negotiated restructuring, not a demolition.**  
If management wants support for change, it must prove that change can be achieved while preserving the company’s industrial footprint and social responsibility.
