Let me be equally direct.

I acknowledge one point from both the works council and the union: **a durable solution cannot be built on short-term cosmetics**. We do need an industrial plan, not just a headline cost cut. And yes, there must be investment in future products and technology if this company is to compete beyond 2030.

But I do **not** accept your red lines as they stand.

### On plant closures
We have said clearly that the German cost base is structurally too high and that we cannot carry overcapacity indefinitely. If every site is declared untouchable in advance, then we are not restructuring — we are postponing the crisis. That is not acceptable to management, to owners, or to investors, and it would be irresponsible in the current market environment.

I am not coming to this table to preserve the status quo. If the numbers do not work, then **site rationalisation remains on the table**, including closures if necessary. I will not rule that out before we have a credible package that closes the cost gap.

### On compulsory redundancies
I understand this is a hard line for you, but I also have to be clear: if we are serious about reducing structural overcapacity, the workforce footprint must come down materially. I would prefer to achieve that through negotiated instruments — voluntary exits, early retirement, redeployment, working-time adjustments, and targeted reductions — but I will not commit upfront to a solution that makes the necessary adjustment impossible.

### On job and site guarantees through 2030
A blanket guarantee through 2030 is not compatible with the scale of the problem as I see it. I am prepared to discuss **conditional security**, but only if it is tied to a package that delivers real, durable savings and restores margin. Guarantees cannot become a shield against needed structural change.

### On employee contribution
I do agree there must be a negotiated workforce contribution. That is unavoidable. But it must be meaningful, measurable, and sufficient to matter — not symbolic. If labour costs remain where they are, the company will not regain competitiveness. We need a real contribution on pay, working time, and flexibility.

### On what I am prepared to consider
I am open to:
- capacity reductions short of full closures where they actually remove cost,
- redeployment and qualification,
- voluntary departure programs,
- working-time solutions,
- investment commitments where they are economically justified,
- and a negotiated labour-cost package.

But I need to say this clearly: **a deal that preserves all sites, preserves all guarantees, and only asks management to “wait and see” is not a restructuring.** It fails the company, and I will not support it.

So my position is this:  
We need a **substantial, structural reduction in cost and capacity**, including the possibility of closures if no equivalent savings can be secured otherwise. If the employee side wants to avoid that outcome, then it must come forward with a package that delivers comparable and durable relief. Without that, we will remain in confrontation.
