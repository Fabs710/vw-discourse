The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines.

Management cannot simply present a cost-cutting plan that sacrifices jobs, sites, and the company’s social obligations while asking the workforce to carry the full burden. We know the company faces real pressure, but the answer cannot be a one-sided restructuring at the expense of thousands of employees and entire regions.

What we will argue for is a negotiated solution that protects all German plants, secures employment through 2030, and gives the workforce a genuine role in shaping the transformation. If savings are needed, they must come from alternatives such as:
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- working-time solutions,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- investment commitments for future products,
- and a fair employee contribution only as part of a binding package with site and job guarantees.

We are prepared to negotiate seriously, but only on the basis of:
1. no plant closures,
2. no forced layoffs,
3. binding job and site guarantees through 2030,
4. preservation of co-determination and the existing job-security framework,
5. and a credible industrial plan for the future, not just a short-term cut programme.

If management comes with a fait accompli, we will resist it. The workforce has already shown it is prepared to mobilise, and we will use every legitimate means available to defend jobs and sites.
