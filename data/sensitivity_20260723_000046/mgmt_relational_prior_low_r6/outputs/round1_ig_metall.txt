I agree with the core line from the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no unilateral tearing up of the collective framework**.

Let me be equally clear from the union side: the workforce is prepared to contribute, but **not as the sole payer of management’s restructuring failure**. If the company wants a durable settlement, it must be a **shared-sacrifice package** with binding guarantees.

### Where we align
- **German sites must remain open.**
- **Forced layoffs are unacceptable.**
- **Any restructuring must be negotiated, not imposed.**
- **Savings can and should come from alternatives**: capacity adjustment, working-time measures, internal redeployment, qualification, and temporary wage concessions or relinquished bonuses if they are matched by real security.

### Where I disagree with management’s logic
If the proposal is built around:
- plant closures,
- mass compulsory redundancies,
- permanent deterioration of pay and conditions,
- and job insecurity shifted entirely onto employees,

then it is not a restructuring plan, it is a **cost-cutting transfer of risk onto labour**. That will not be accepted.

### What would be a viable basis for talks
We have already shown openness to difficult contributions. That openness remains, but only under clear conditions:
- **binding site and job security**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **credible investment commitments for the future**
- **management and owners also taking a measurable hit**
- **fair contributions from the workforce only as part of a balanced package**

### Bottom line
If the other side wants an agreement, they need to come back with a proposal that preserves the industrial base and shares the burden fairly. Otherwise, they should expect coordinated resistance from the union and the workforce. We are not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that sacrifices jobs to protect margins.
