## Core impasse

The central deadlock is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **which restructuring instruments are permissible** and **who bears the burden of proof/risk**:

1. **Employee side / state**:  
   No closures, no compulsory redundancies, all German sites preserved, with burden-sharing across management, owners, and labour.

2. **Management / owners / investors**:  
   A credible solution must produce a **durable structural cost and capacity reset**; they are not accepting an open-ended commitment to preserve every site if the numbers do not work.

3. **Expert input**:  
   Non-closure measures can reduce costs meaningfully, but likely **do not fully substitute** for closure or deep downsizing if overcapacity is structurally large.

So the real clash is between:
- **maximum site/job protection**, and
- **a plant-network reset large enough to satisfy competitiveness requirements**.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### Compromise 1: “No closures now” with a verified review trigger
A time-limited package could be adopted that:
- keeps all sites open initially,
- uses working-time reductions, redeployment, voluntary exits, qualification, and temporary management/owner concessions,
- and sets a **jointly agreed review point** after verified plant-by-plant cost testing.

**Trigger condition:**  
If agreed savings and utilization targets are not met by a fixed date, parties reopen discussion on more structural capacity measures.

This does not resolve the full disagreement, but it can bridge the immediate gap by replacing a closure decision now with a **data-gated decision later**.

---

### Compromise 2: “Protected footprint reduction” instead of outright closure
If full site preservation is not feasible, a compromise could be:
- no complete site closure in the first phase,
- but one or more plants are **downsized, retooled, or partially repurposed**,
- with guaranteed future product allocation where viable,
- and strict limits on compulsory redundancies through redeployment and voluntary exit measures.

This shifts the discussion from “closure vs. no closure” to **what minimum efficient footprint each site can carry**.

---

### Compromise 3: Binding social contract for restructuring
A package could combine:
- temporary labour concessions,
- management compensation restraint,
- owner-funded investment and transition support,
- job security guarantees for core operations,
- and a defined ceiling on compulsory measures, with only a narrowly defined fallback if targets are missed.

This gives each side something measurable:
- labour gets protection and co-determination,
- management gets a credible cost path,
- owners/investors get enforceability,
- the state gets preservation of industrial presence.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**If plant-by-plant data show that one site cannot remain economically viable without a major footprint reduction, would you accept a downsizing/repurposing model that avoids full closure but still reduces capacity materially? If not, what exact economic test would make your “all sites open” position workable?**

### 2) Industrial Union
**What specific combination of concessions would you authorize in bargaining if management and owners provide binding site guarantees and investment commitments—particularly, would you accept temporary wage moderation, longer working-time flexibility, or bonus restraint in exchange for no compulsory redundancies?**

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to a quantified option set showing, for each plant, the savings from non-closure measures before any closure decision is tabled—and if so, what minimum savings gap would still justify closing a site?**

### 4) Regional State Government
**If the data show that one plant cannot be kept open in its current form, would the state support a partial conversion/downsizing plan as a condition for preserving the broader German network, rather than insisting on unchanged site structures?**

### 5) Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to guarantee medium-term investment and transition funding in exchange for a restructuring package that preserves most sites but requires substantial, measurable contributions from ownership and management?**

### 6) Institutional Investors
**Would you support a package that preserves all sites for now but includes a hard review trigger, enforceable performance targets, and a pre-agreed fallback if cost and utilization targets are missed?**
