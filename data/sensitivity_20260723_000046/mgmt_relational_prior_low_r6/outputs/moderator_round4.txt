Here is the procedural readout of the impasse and a set of facilitator proposals.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the sequence and proof standard** for restructuring:
- **Employee side / state:** no closures or compulsory redundancies unless every non-closure option has been proven insufficient, plant by plant.
- **Management / capital side:** a credible 2030 cost reset may require footprint reduction, and they do not accept a blanket rule out of closures or forced reductions before the numbers are complete.

So the dispute is really:
1. **What must be proven first?**
2. **How much burden must be shared by each side?**
3. **Whether “protected downsizing” is a genuine compromise or a disguised closure path.**

## Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridging options for consideration.

### 1) Time-limited non-closure test period
**Proposal:** Establish a fixed audit-and-bargaining window during which all parties commit to a plant-by-plant test of non-closure measures before any closure decision is activated.

**Core elements:**
- independent auditor
- plant-by-plant financial model
- quantified savings target
- defined deadline
- temporary freeze on closures and compulsory redundancies during the test period
- automatic review trigger if savings gap remains

**Why it bridges:**  
It addresses the employee/state demand for proof first, while giving management/capital a bounded process rather than an open-ended veto.

---

### 2) Conditional no-forced-redundancy package with fallback triggers
**Proposal:** Agree now on a package built around voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, wage moderation, management restraint, and owner contributions, with explicit fallback triggers if the agreed savings gap is not closed.

**Core elements:**
- no compulsory redundancies during Phase 1
- voluntary measures first
- management/owner contribution floor
- plant-level performance milestones
- pre-agreed fallback options only if milestones fail
- no automatic closure language before the fallback review

**Why it bridges:**  
It gives labour and the state immediate protection while acknowledging management/capital’s claim that the final gap must still be closed somehow if targets are missed.

---

### 3) Site-by-site differentiated settlement
**Proposal:** Replace the all-sites-open versus closure binary with a site-specific settlement:
- some plants receive full preservation and product commitments,
- some receive repurposing with guaranteed industrial activity,
- only sites shown by the audit to be structurally non-viable enter a separate negotiation track.

**Core elements:**
- classify sites by viability bands
- preserve industrial use where possible
- prohibit hollowing-out without a plan
- separate treatment for marginal versus structurally weak sites
- worker protections tied to each site category

**Why it bridges:**  
It may reduce the absolute conflict by avoiding a single uniform rule for all plants.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**Question:**  
If an independent plant-by-plant audit shows that one or more sites remain structurally underutilized even after the full non-closure package, what specific outcome would you accept short of closure to make those sites viable?

### Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What exact temporary labour concessions, and for how long, would you authorize if they are matched by binding owner and management contributions and a verified no-closure test period?

### Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a time-limited, independent non-closure test with a closure moratorium during the audit period, and state the precise savings gap that must still remain before you seek stronger measures?

### Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Would you support a conditional restructuring framework that permits site-specific adjustment only after an independent audit has exhausted all non-closure options, including a clearly defined veto review point?

### Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What specific capital, governance, or product-allocation commitments are you prepared to make to reduce the savings gap before any discussion of site reduction begins?

### Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
Would you support a temporary no-closure phase if it is tied to hard milestones, independent verification, and a credible path to 2030 margins, even if it delays the final decision?
