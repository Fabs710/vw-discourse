We agree with the broad diagnosis: this is a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip, and doing nothing is not an option. We also agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.

Where we part ways is on the conclusion the employee side keeps trying to hard-code into the outcome: **all sites open, no compulsory redundancies, and job security through 2030**. That is not a credible restructuring plan unless it is proven plant by plant to restore margins. So far, it has not been.

To the Works Council and union: we understand the concern about hollowing out sites. But you are asking capital to accept a package that may preserve employment headlines while leaving the cost base structurally impaired. That is not acceptable for shareholders. If the company still carries subscale plants, excess fixed cost, and weak utilization, then margins will remain under pressure and the share price will continue to reflect that. Temporary measures alone do not solve that.

To management: we support a real reset, not a cosmetic one. But “closures remain on the table” is not enough. You need to show the numbers clearly:
- plant-by-plant fixed-cost absorption,
- utilization,
- capex needs,
- logistics effects,
- and 2030 margin impact under each scenario.

Without that, you cannot demonstrate that the proposed restructuring is the best risk-adjusted route to competitiveness.

To the state and family holding: we respect your governance role and understand the political sensitivity. But long-term value does not come from preserving every legacy structure. It comes from restoring a cost base that can compete in a shrinking European market against lower-cost rivals. If certain sites cannot be made viable, then avoiding hard decisions only delays the problem and increases execution risk.

Our position is straightforward:

- **We will support a package that delivers durable cost reduction and a credible path back to competitive margins.**
- **We will not support a settlement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.**
- **We will not support a purely cosmetic compromise that defers the real adjustment.**

That said, we are open to a middle path **only if** it is genuinely credible:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- temporary employee concessions,
- and targeted owner/management contributions,

but only if these measures close the savings gap with measurable confidence. If they do not, then protected downsizing or closures remain rational instruments.
