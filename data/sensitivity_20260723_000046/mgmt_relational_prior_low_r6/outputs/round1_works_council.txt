As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for management’s restructuring mistakes.

The workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. If the company needs savings, then we are prepared to discuss alternatives — but only those that preserve every German site, protect jobs, and secure the future of the plants through 2030.

What we will argue for:
- Binding guarantees that all German plants remain open
- No forced layoffs
- A genuine employment guarantee through 2030
- Alternative savings through capacity adjustment, working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification, and negotiated contribution from management and shareholders
- Real co-determination in shaping any restructuring plan, not a fait accompli

What we will argue against:
- Any closure of a German plant
- Any relocation that amounts to site hollowing-out
- Any unilateral cost-cutting imposed on the workforce
- Any attempt to tear up the historic social compact

If management wants our agreement, it must come with credible, binding commitments for jobs, sites, and investment. Otherwise, it will face full resistance from the workforce and from the Works Council.
