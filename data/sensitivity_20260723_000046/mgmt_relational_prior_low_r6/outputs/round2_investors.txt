We agree with several parts of the current discussion, but our support is conditional on one thing: **a package that actually restores competitive margins, not one that merely postpones the adjustment**.

## Where we agree
- **Doing nothing is not an option.**
- **A prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.**
- **Any settlement must be durable, not cosmetic.**
- **Working-time measures, redeployment, qualification, and voluntary exits can be useful tools** if they deliver real savings.
- The expert input is right that **non-closure measures can reduce costs meaningfully**.

## Where we disagree
### To the Works Council and union
We respect the social and industrial logic of your position, but we cannot accept an absolute line of:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- all sites open,
- employment security through 2030,

**if that makes the company structurally uncompetitive**.

The expert has already made the key point: alternatives may help, but **they usually do not fully replace the economics of a targeted closure or deep footprint reduction** when overcapacity is structural. If your package only protects sites and jobs while leaving the cost base above peer levels, then it is not a solution from a shareholder perspective.

### To the state government
We understand the regional role and the governance constraints, but we do **not** accept the idea that preserving the regional anchor role means freezing the current industrial footprint. A company can remain an anchor while still making hard structural adjustments. If the result is merely political comfort and weaker margins, capital will leave and the problem gets worse.

### To management
We agree that the company needs a real restructuring, but management still has to prove that the proposed savings are **quantified, durable, and executable**. “Structural change” is the right direction, but it cannot be just a threat designed to force concessions. Investors need a credible implementation path, not a confrontation strategy.

### To the family holding
We agree with your insistence on real change, and we share your rejection of a symbolic deal. But we also need clarity that the final package will be judged by **value creation**, not by how hard it looks. A harsher package is not automatically a better one if it triggers long conflict, execution risk, or brand damage.

## What would move us
We would support a settlement if it includes:
- **material, lasting cost reduction**
- **real capacity correction**
- **measurable improvement in return-on-sales**
- **no cosmetic substitution of temporary measures for structural action**
- **investment commitments only if tied to credible savings and execution**

## What would not move us
We would not support:
- a deal that preserves all legacy structures and calls it restructuring
- a package that leaves the company’s cost base uncompetitive
- a settlement that avoids hard choices and simply defers them
- prolonged industrial action that destroys cash flow and credibility

## Our position on the expert assessment
The expert has not proven that closures are necessary in every case, but the assessment does show that **“keep every site open” is not yet operationally demonstrated as compatible with the required savings**. That matters. If management and labour want to avoid closures, they must show plant-by-plant how the same savings are achieved with equivalent durability.

## What we need next
Before we can support any final outcome, we need:
1. **plant-by-plant savings and utilization data**
2. **a clear comparison of closure vs. downsizing vs. negotiated labor-cost measures**
3. **the expected margin impact under each scenario**
4. **proof that the chosen package is enforceable and durable**

## Bottom line
We are prepared to back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**. But we will not support a settlement that leaves the cost base uncompetitive, the margins structurally impaired, or the restructuring non-credible.
