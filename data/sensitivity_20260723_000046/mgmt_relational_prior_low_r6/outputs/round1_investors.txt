We are aligned on the **diagnosis**: the cost base cannot stay where it is if this brand is to restore margins, protect investment capacity, and remain competitive. I also agree that a purely cosmetic settlement would be unacceptable. The market will not reward a deal that preserves structural overcapacity and calls it restructuring.

But I need to be equally clear: **I will not support an outcome that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.** That is the line that matters to us as capital providers.

### Where I agree
- Management is right that **doing nothing is not an option**.
- The family holding is right that a **ritual or symbolic restructuring** would destroy value.
- The union, works council, and state are right that a **prolonged conflict is value-destructive**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that “no plant closures” or “no compulsory redundancies” can be absolute starting points if they prevent the company from reaching a viable cost structure.

If the only acceptable outcome is to preserve every site, every job guarantee, and every legacy structure intact, then the burden simply gets shifted into:
- permanently weaker margins,
- lower returns on sales,
- reduced investment capacity,
- and ultimately a weaker share price.

That is not a protected compromise; it is deferred destruction of value.

### What I need to see
For us, the key test is not the political optics of the package. It is whether it produces a **credible, durable path back to competitive margins**. That means:

- a **material and lasting reduction in costs**
- **real capacity correction**, not just temporary measures
- labour-cost savings that are not merely one-off
- a settlement that can actually be executed without endless reversal risk
- investment commitments only if they are matched by real structural improvement

### On alternatives to closures
We are open to alternatives **if** they genuinely deliver equivalent savings and durability. But the standard is high. “Capacity adjustment,” “working-time measures,” and “redeployment” are fine as instruments only if they produce the same economic effect as hard restructuring.

If they do not, then they are not solutions; they are postponements.

### On labour contributions
Yes, the workforce can be part of the solution. But it must be part of a package that is economically sufficient. We would not support a deal where employees make concessions while management preserves an uncompetitive footprint and calls that shared sacrifice.

### On strikes and prolonged conflict
We do not want industrial escalation. A long strike would damage earnings, cash flow, and credibility. But equally, a weak settlement that locks in margin pressure would be worse over time than a short, hard negotiation.

### Bottom line
Our support depends on one thing: **a credible, enforceable restructuring that restores competitiveness at scale**.

- If the final package delivers that, we can support it.
- If it merely protects existing structures and delays the adjustment, we will oppose it and continue to pressure management through capital-market channels.

We are not here to defend any one stakeholder’s comfort. We are here to protect long-term value.
