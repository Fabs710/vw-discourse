I will be direct.

## To management
I agree with you on the diagnosis: this is a structural crisis, and doing nothing is not an option. I also agree that the company needs a durable cost and capacity correction by 2030.

Where I disagree is your conclusion that closures, forced redundancies, or a “blanket no-redundancy cannot be accepted” stance are therefore justified. That is **not yet proven**. You keep turning a conditional expert statement into a mandate. It is not.

You still have not shown, plant by plant, that:
- non-closure measures fail,
- downsizing/repurposing cannot work,
- and the residual savings gap truly requires plant closures.

Until that evidence exists, I will not accept closures as the default instrument.

## To the controlling family and investors
I hear your concern for competitiveness and value. I share the concern for value destruction. But I reject the idea that value protection must come through site closures and forced job cuts.

A company like this does not remain a regional anchor by hollowing out its German base. It remains an anchor through:
- viable sites,
- skilled jobs,
- investment,
- and social stability.

If you want long-term value, then support a package that preserves the German industrial footprint as far as economically possible, rather than treating closures as the benchmark of seriousness.

## To the works council and union
I agree with your central red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided sacrifice,
- no erosion of co-determination,
- and no hollowing out of sites disguised as compromise.

Where I would add a caution is this: “all sites open” must remain tied to economic reality, not just political assertion. If a site needs adjustment, that adjustment should first be through:
- working-time tools,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- product allocation,
- and capacity correction,
**not** closure.

I can support protected downsizing or repurposing only if it genuinely preserves the site as an industrial location with a future — not as a staged exit. But I will not accept closures as the starting point.

## To the state government
I support your line that the state’s co-determination and veto role cannot be sidelined, and that regional responsibility matters. That is exactly right.

I also support your insistence that the company must remain an industrial anchor in the region. A settlement that damages regional employment on a lasting basis would be politically and economically unacceptable.

Where I would push slightly further is this: the state should use its leverage to insist on a **serious non-closure restructuring test** before any closure is even considered. That means a real comparison of:
- status quo,
- non-closure savings,
- protected downsizing/repurposing,
- and closure.

Not slogans. Numbers.

## My position
I remain where I have been from the start:
- **No outcome that guts regional employment.**
- **No outcome that sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
- **No closure-led solution unless every serious alternative has been quantified and shown to fail.**

That is not obstruction. That is responsible ownership.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a hard but fair restructuring package if it includes:
- real capacity correction,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- product allocation changes,
- management restraint,
- owner contributions,
- and binding investment commitments.

If the savings gap can be closed that way, then that is the right path.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- plant closures as the first resort,
- compulsory redundancies as the fallback,
- or any package that weakens the regional economic anchor.

## On the expert question
The experts have already told us what we needed to hear: non-closure measures can help materially, but they may not be enough if overcapacity is truly structural. Fine. Then the next step is not to jump to closures. The next step is to quantify what remains after the full non-closure package.

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant industrial and financial auditor to test whether a non-closure package, protected downsizing, and management/owner contributions can close the 2030 savings gap without plant closures or compulsory redundancies]

I want that audit to answer one question clearly:
**What residual gap remains after every non-closure option is exhausted, and can that gap be closed without sacrificing sites or forcing people out?**

Until that answer exists, I will not move toward closures.

## Bottom line
I am open to a real restructuring. I am not open to a restructuring that makes workers, sites, and the regional economy pay for strategic failure.

If management wants my support, it must come back with a package that:
1. preserves the company as a regional anchor,
2. protects jobs and sites as far as economically possible,
3. keeps co-determination intact,
4. and demonstrates with hard numbers why any harsher measure would be unavoidable.

Anything else is unacceptable.
