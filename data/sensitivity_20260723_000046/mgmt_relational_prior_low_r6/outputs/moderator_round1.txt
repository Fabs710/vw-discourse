### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree that the company faces a **serious structural crisis** and that **doing nothing is not an option**.
- There is broad agreement that any solution should be **negotiated rather than imposed**.
- Most parties accept that a viable package will likely need a mix of measures, including:
  - **investment commitments**
  - **qualification / retraining**
  - **redeployment**
  - **working-time adjustments**
  - **capacity correction**
- There is also shared recognition that a **prolonged conflict would be damaging** for the company and stakeholders.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Employee side and state oppose closures; management, family holding, and investors keep closures on the table as a possible instrument.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee side and state reject them; management and capital-side actors do not rule out job reductions if needed for competitiveness.
- **Scope of job security:**  
  Works Council and union seek binding security through 2030; management and investors say that is only possible if the cost base is structurally corrected.
- **Nature of restructuring:**  
  Employee/state side want socially managed adjustment with site preservation; management/ownership/capital side want a more substantial and durable cost and capacity reduction.
- **Role of the state and governance constraints:**  
  The state insists its co-determination/veto role cannot be sidelined; management and owners want governance to enable decisive restructuring.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus how much site and employment protection can be preserved.
- Whether **alternatives to closures** can generate savings that are truly equivalent in scale and durability.
- What mix of **temporary workforce concessions** and **management/owner contributions** would be considered balanced.
- How to reconcile **binding security commitments** with the need for **structural flexibility**.
- Which sites, if any, could be adjusted without being seen as unacceptable hollowing-out or de facto closure.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **package design**: identify which measures are essential, which are negotiable, and what specific combination could deliver both **competitiveness** and **job/site protection**. A useful next step would be to quantify:
- required savings,
- acceptable instruments,
- non-negotiable protections,
- and the conditions under which each side could support a settlement.
