As the regional state shareholder, I will be equally direct.

I **agree** with the central diagnosis: the company faces a structural crisis, and doing nothing is not an option. I also agree that a settlement must be **credible**, not cosmetic, and that short-term peace without a viable industrial basis would be irresponsible.

But I **do not accept** the repeated implication that the only serious answer is plant closures, forced redundancies, and a one-sided transfer of the adjustment burden onto labour and the region.

## Where I agree
- There must be a **real restructuring**.
- The company needs a **durable cost correction**.
- A prolonged conflict would damage the company, jobs, and investment capacity.
- Alternatives such as working-time solutions, redeployment, qualification, and capacity adjustment must be part of the package.
- Owners, management, and labour all have to contribute.

## Where I disagree
I disagree with management, the family holding, and investors on one crucial point:  
**“Structural change” is not the same as “closing German plants.”**

You keep treating closures as if they are the natural proof of seriousness. From the state’s perspective, that is not acceptable as a starting point. It may be the easiest line on a spreadsheet, but it is not automatically the right answer for an industrial anchor company that carries major regional responsibility and is governed under co-determination and special statutory protections.

The expert input was useful, but it does **not** settle the matter in management’s favour. It says non-closure options can be insufficient **if** the overcapacity is large and persistent. That is a conditional statement, not a mandate for closures. It means the burden of proof is still on those calling for plant cuts.

## What I expect before any talk of closures
If management insists that alternatives cannot work, then I want the numbers on the table, plant by plant:
- current and projected utilization
- fixed and variable cost by site
- savings from working-time measures
- savings from redeployment and qualification
- cost of voluntary programs
- capex and logistics implications of any footprint change

Until that is shown convincingly, I will not treat closures as justified.

## My non-negotiables
I will not support:
- a package that **guts regional employment**
- any outcome that **removes the state’s co-determination or veto role**
- mass compulsory redundancies as the default instrument
- a restructuring that permanently weakens the company’s role as a regional economic anchor

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are core conditions of responsible ownership.

## What I could support
I could support a package that includes:
- binding site guarantees for the German network
- no compulsory redundancies
- substantial use of natural attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time instruments
- targeted, temporary workforce contributions if and only if they are matched by contributions from management and owners
- real investment commitments for future products and technology
- a restructuring designed to restore competitiveness **without dismantling the company’s regional base**

If one plant truly cannot be kept viable, then I would still require a last-resort solution that avoids a destructive closure logic and protects the surrounding employment ecosystem as far as possible. But I am not endorsing closures as the default.

## To management
If you want my consent, come back with a plan that proves:
1. the cost gap can be closed without plant closures, or
2. if not, why every non-closure alternative has been exhausted and why the remaining impact can still be limited in a socially and regionally responsible way.

Right now, that case has **not** been made.

## To the workforce side
I support your insistence that the workforce cannot pay alone. That is right. But I also need honesty from all sides: if the company’s overcapacity is real, then some contribution may be needed. My support is for a **negotiated restructuring that protects sites and jobs to the maximum extent possible**, not for a frozen status quo.

## Bottom line
The state shareholder will back a restructuring only if it preserves:
- jobs in the state,
- the German site network as far as economically possible,
- and the company’s role as a regional anchor.

I am open to a hard but fair settlement. I am **not** open to a closure-driven solution that treats the region as expendable.
