I will be direct.

I agree with management on the central point: **this company cannot preserve its current German cost structure and remain competitively viable**. If we pretend otherwise, we are not protecting jobs — we are postponing a larger erosion of value, investment capacity, and ultimately employment.

At the same time, I agree with the state and the employee side on one important issue: **a destructive, open-ended conflict is not acceptable**. The family holding is a long-term owner. We do not want a headline-driven confrontation that damages the brand, weakens labour peace, or turns this into a governance crisis.

Where I disagree with the employee side and the state is on the implication that the current site structure is somehow sacrosanct. If the economic case requires structural change, then **structural change must be on the table**. “No closures” as an absolute red line is not a serious answer if it prevents the company from restoring competitiveness.

That said, I am also not endorsing a simplistic closure-and-layoff program. From an ownership perspective, that would be too blunt and potentially value-destructive if it triggers prolonged strikes, political escalation, or irreversible damage to industrial capability. We need a solution that is **durable, credible, and enforceable**.

So my position is this:

- **I support a restructuring that materially improves margins and cost competitiveness.**
- **I do not support cosmetic measures that preserve every cost and every structure while calling it reform.**
- **I do not support a conflict that permanently damages the enterprise or its governance.**
- **I am open to negotiated alternatives to closures, but only if they deliver real savings at scale.**

What would be credible in my view?
- A substantial reduction in overcapacity.
- A meaningful labour-cost contribution.
- Capacity and deployment adjustments that are real, not symbolic.
- Strong investment commitments tied to future products and technology.
- Socially managed transitions where possible, but not at the expense of economic reality.

What would not be credible?
- An unconditional guarantee that all sites stay open regardless of economics.
- Refusing every form of structural adjustment and then expecting the owners to absorb the loss.
- A settlement that buys short-term peace while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

So I am not here to block talks. I am here to say that the owners will support a **real restructuring**, not a ritual one. If the other stakeholders want our backing, they will need to engage with the scale of the problem, not just the politics of it.
