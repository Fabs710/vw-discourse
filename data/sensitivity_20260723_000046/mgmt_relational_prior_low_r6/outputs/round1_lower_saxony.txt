As the regional state government and a long-standing anchor shareholder, my position is clear:

We **do** recognize the seriousness of the structural crisis. No one in this room should pretend that the company can ignore overcapacity, cost pressure, or the global shift in the automotive market. A negotiated restructuring is necessary.

But I must be equally clear about the limits:

- **I do not accept plant closures in Germany as the starting point.**
- **I do not accept mass compulsory redundancies.**
- **I do not accept a restructuring that breaks the regional economic anchor role of this company.**
- And I will not allow the state’s co-determination and veto role to be sidelined as if it were an obstacle rather than part of the governance framework.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: doing nothing is not an option. But your framing is too narrow and too one-sided. If you make closures and layoffs the default answer, you are not presenting a strategy, you are shifting the burden onto workers and regions that have already carried this company for decades.

A sustainable restructuring must start from a different premise:
- preserve industrial capacity in Germany,
- avoid irreversible site closures where at all possible,
- use working-time instruments, redeployment, qualification, product mix changes, and phased capacity adjustment first,
- and require a fair contribution from owners and management as well, not only from labour.

If you argue that competitiveness requires a material cost correction, I am prepared to engage with that. But **the form of the adjustment matters**. We can discuss a negotiated package. We cannot accept a bulldozer approach.

### To the Works Council and the union
I support your insistence that the workforce cannot be made to pay alone. You are right to reject unilateral cuts and empty promises. The state will back a settlement that protects jobs and sites and gives the plants a future.

At the same time, I need to be honest: if the company’s economic pressure is as severe as management says, then some contribution from labour may be unavoidable — but only **within a package that preserves sites, avoids forced redundancies, and secures long-term investment**. That is the difference between responsible adjustment and social destruction.

### What I can support
I could support a package built around:
- binding guarantees for all German sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- substantial use of natural attrition, redeployment, and working-time solutions,
- targeted capacity reductions without plant closures,
- investment commitments for future products and technology,
- and a fair, temporary, negotiated contribution from the workforce if it is matched by contributions from management and the owners.

### What I cannot support
I will oppose any package that:
- closes a German plant,
- permanently hollow-outs a site,
- cuts jobs on a scale that devastates regional employment,
- or turns the state’s role into a symbolic afterthought while decisions are effectively imposed elsewhere.

### Bottom line
I am not here to block restructuring. I am here to ensure it is **socially responsible, regionally sustainable, and economically credible**. If management wants support from the state shareholder, then it needs to come back with a plan that protects the company **and** the communities around it. Without that, it will not have my consent.
