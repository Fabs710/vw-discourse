Let me be equally clear: I hear the demand for security, but I do **not** accept the premise that the current cost base can simply be defended intact and still leave this company competitive.

We are not in a normal bargaining round. We are in a **structural crisis**. The European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our German cost base is materially above target. If we leave the structure unchanged, we will lose margins, lose investment capacity, and ultimately lose jobs on a much larger scale later.

### Where I agree
I agree that we need a **negotiated** solution. I am not pretending management can impose a durable restructuring unilaterally. And I agree that the company needs a **future-facing package** that includes investment, qualification, and a clear industrial roadmap to 2030.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept:
- a blanket refusal of any plant closure as a starting point,
- a total exclusion of compulsory job reductions,
- or a settlement that preserves every existing site and every existing cost structure regardless of competitiveness.

Those positions may be understandable politically, but they do not solve the economic problem. If the answer is only “preserve everything,” then we are simply postponing the reckoning.

### What is non-negotiable from management’s side
We need a package that delivers:
- a **durable reduction in overcapacity**,
- a **substantial and lasting labour-cost contribution**,
- and a **structural improvement in plant economics**.

That can be achieved through a mix of measures — including capacity reductions, redeployment, working-time changes, and yes, if necessary, **site-specific adjustments**. I am not fixing the instrument today, but I am fixing the outcome: the cost base must come down materially and sustainably.

### On your proposed red lines
You say “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no site changes.” If those are absolute, then the burden shifts entirely onto internal savings that are unlikely to be sufficient at the scale required. I am not willing to sign up to a cosmetic deal that leaves the company uncompetitive.

### On guarantees and contributions
If the workforce expects security through 2030, then there must be a credible economic basis for it. That means:
- meaningful labour-cost moderation,
- flexibility on capacity and deployment,
- and acceptance that not every current structure can survive.

In return, management can discuss:
- investment commitments,
- qualification and transition support,
- and a clear roadmap for future products and technology.

But I need to be direct: **job security without structural correction is not a viable offer**.

### Bottom line
I am ready for a negotiated solution, but only one that **changes the company’s cost structure in a durable way**. If the employee side rules out closures, rule out job reductions, and rule out real cost correction, then we are not negotiating a solution — we are managing decline. And that is not acceptable.
