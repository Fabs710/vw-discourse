I’ll be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, and the expert that this is a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary dip. The company cannot be left with a cost base that stays above peers while the European market shrinks and competition intensifies.

I also agree with the Works Council, the union, and the state on one point: **a prolonged conflict would destroy value**. That would hurt the company, the workforce, and the owners. A negotiated settlement is clearly preferable.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to turn
- “no plant closures,”
- “no compulsory redundancies,”
- “all sites open,”
- “job security through 2030”

into hard preconditions.

That is not a credible response to a structural overcapacity problem. It is a demand to preserve the current footprint regardless of economics. As a controlling owner, I cannot support a solution that locks in an uncompetitive structure and erodes margins for years.

I also do **not** accept the state’s position that preserving the regional anchor role means effectively freezing the industrial footprint. Regional responsibility matters, but it does not override the need for viability.

## On the experts
The experts were useful, and their message is important:

- non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings,
- but they usually do **not** fully substitute for closure or deep downsizing when overcapacity is structural,
- and “all plants open” has not been demonstrated as compatible with the required savings.

That is the key point. The burden of proof has **not** shifted to the owners or management to prove that closures are necessary in every case. The burden remains on those claiming that all sites can stay open **and** the company can still restore competitiveness at the required scale.

## What I would support
I am prepared to back a negotiated package if it delivers all three of these:
1. **durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity correction**
3. **no value-destructive instability**

That can include:
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- voluntary exit programs,
- temporary labour contributions,
- targeted investment commitments,
- and, where justified by the numbers, site-specific restructuring.

I am not fixated on one instrument. I am fixated on the outcome.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a cosmetic deal that preserves every legacy structure and calls it restructuring,
- a package that rules out structural measures in principle,
- a settlement that leaves the company uncompetitive,
- or a long conflict that destroys cash flow, brand strength, and governance credibility.

I also will not support any arrangement that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance control.

## To the Works Council
You say the company should close the gap through management, owners, and alternative measures first. I am open to that **if it is credible and quantified**. But “all German plants remain open” is not a plan; it is a red line.

If you want the family holding to support a non-closure package, bring me plant-by-plant evidence that it can:
- remove enough fixed cost,
- correct overcapacity durably,
- and restore the return-on-sales path.

Until then, I will not commit to ruling out closures.

## To the union
I hear your refusal of compulsory redundancies. I am not seeking layoffs for their own sake. But I will not rule out forced reductions if they are the only way to achieve a sustainable solution.

If labour wants job security, it must be anchored in a viable business model. Security cannot be declared into existence while the company remains structurally uncompetitive.

## To the state government
Your regional stewardship matters, and I respect the co-determination framework. But stewardship also means accepting that some industrial adjustment may be necessary. Preserving every site regardless of economics would be a short-term political comfort and a long-term value problem.

## To management
You need to be equally disciplined. Do not use closure talk as a bluff, and do not come back with a package that is merely symbolic. If you want owner support, present a **quantified option set**:
- closure case,
- downsizing case,
- non-closure concession case,
- with plant-level savings, utilization, and 2030 margin impact.

That is the only way to test whether a non-closure path is actually credible.

## Bottom line
The family holding is prepared to support a real restructuring. We are **not** prepared to support:
- a frozen footprint,
- a symbolic compromise,
- or a settlement that sacrifices long-term value for short-term peace.

If the employee side and the state can demonstrate, with hard numbers, that the required savings can be achieved without closures and without permanent damage to competitiveness, we will examine that seriously. If not, stronger structural measures remain on the table.
