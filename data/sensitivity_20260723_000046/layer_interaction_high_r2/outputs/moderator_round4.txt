### Facilitator summary: where the impasse now sits

The discourse has converged on the same diagnosis, but not on the same remedy.

## Core impasse
The central conflict is:

**Employee side:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, binding site/job security through 2030, co-determination respected.  
**Management / owners / investors:** a materially lower recurring cost base is required, and closures or footprint reduction must remain available if non-closure measures do not close the gap.

So the deadlock is not about whether there is a problem. It is about **whether the restructuring can be completed without irreversible footprint reduction**, and if so, **under what binding guarantees and what cost savings**.

A second, linked impasse is procedural:

- Employee side wants **security first, contribution second**
- Management side wants **flexibility first, guarantees conditional on performance**

# Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

Below are three concrete bridge options that could be tabled for testing. These are not endorsements; they are process proposals.

## Compromise position 1: Time-limited no-closure implementation pact
**Structure**
- Immediate non-closure restructuring package
- Binding milestones on savings delivery
- Joint monitoring body with labor, management, and shareholder/state observers
- Defined review point after 12–18 months

**Safeguard**
- No plant closures or compulsory redundancies during the implementation period

**Trigger**
- If milestones are missed, a second-stage discussion opens on deeper structural measures

**Why it may bridge**
- Gives labor immediate protection
- Gives management a serious test of alternatives
- Gives investors a measurable path rather than a permanent veto

---

## Compromise position 2: No compulsory redundancies, but limited footprint adjustment
**Structure**
- No forced layoffs
- Attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, voluntary exits, and labor-cost restraint
- Possible consolidation of sub-activities, lines, or functions
- Closure of specific activities only if site-level closure is not required

**Safeguard**
- Binding co-determination on implementation
- A social plan and qualification package
- Explicit commitment that any footprint reduction must be the least invasive option available

**Why it may bridge**
- Preserves the strongest labor red line on forced redundancies
- Gives management some structural relief if the cost gap remains material
- May be more politically feasible than full plant closure

---

## Compromise position 3: Shared-sacrifice package with asymmetric protections
**Structure**
- Temporary wage restraint, bonus restraint, deferred payments, productivity measures
- Executive and overhead restraint visible and quantified
- Company commits to investment in German sites and product allocation
- Strong employment protection clause for a defined period

**Safeguard**
- No closures and no compulsory redundancies for a fixed term
- If savings targets are not met, an agreed escalation process is triggered before any unilateral action

**Why it may bridge**
- Addresses fairness concerns directly
- Makes labor contributions contingent on real reciprocal commitments
- Preserves a future debate without forcing an immediate closure decision

# One pointed question to each stakeholder

## To the Works Council
**Question:**  
If a jointly audited non-closure package reaches the agreed savings target, would you accept a time-limited review mechanism that preserves no closures during implementation but allows a second-stage discussion if the target is missed?

## To the Industrial Union
**Question:**  
Would you be prepared to translate your “no compulsory redundancies” position into a negotiated package that permits attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and temporary labor-cost restraint, provided the company gives binding job and site guarantees?

## To Group & Brand Management
**Question:**  
Can you specify the exact annual savings target and the residual gap after non-closure measures, and state clearly whether a no-closure package would be accepted if that gap is closed by negotiated labor and management contributions?

## To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Will you define the precise conditions under which you would support a no-closure settlement, and will you state whether you would oppose footprint reduction if a jointly verified alternative closes the gap?

## To the Controlling Family Holding
**Question:**  
What level of residual gap would you consider sufficient to justify deeper footprint reduction, and would you support a time-limited no-closure pact if it is independently verified to meet the required savings?

## To the Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
Would you accept a no-closure solution if it is independently audited, time-bound, and demonstrably sufficient to restore the target margin, even if it preserves the current site footprint?

## To the Expert
**Question:**  
Can you translate the current disagreement into a decision rule: what residual annual gap, after non-closure measures, would make footprint reduction economically dominant rather than merely optional?
