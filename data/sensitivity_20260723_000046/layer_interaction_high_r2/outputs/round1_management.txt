We hear the clear red lines from both the works council and the union. Let me respond just as clearly.

I agree with one point: **this must be a long-term solution, not a cosmetic cut that leaves the company uncompetitive**. If we do not reduce structural costs and capacity, we will not restore the brand to a sustainable return and we will not have the funds to invest in future products and technology.

Where I disagree is on the premise that **no plant closure and no compulsory redundancies** can be fixed in advance as untouchable constraints. Given the current cost base, shrinking European demand, and fierce competition, I do not see how we can simply preserve every site and every structure and still get to a viable 2030 position. That would be pretending the problem is smaller than it is.

So let me be direct about management’s view:

- **We need a durable reduction in capacity.**
- **We need a genuine labour-cost contribution.**
- **We need a restructuring that is large enough to matter.**

If the employee side offers only attrition, internal transfers, and working-time measures that do not materially change the cost base, then that is not enough. It may slow the deterioration, but it will not restore competitiveness.

At the same time, I am not here to make confrontation the goal. I understand that forced closures and layoffs are highly disruptive and would only be justified if there is no other way to achieve the required structural effect. So I am open to discussing **alternative mechanisms** — but only if they deliver equivalent, measurable savings.

For example, I would be prepared to consider a package built around:
- capacity reductions through reallocation and consolidation,
- meaningful wage and bonus restraint,
- a temporary or staged labour-cost contribution,
- revised working-time arrangements,
- and a binding productivity and efficiency program,
**provided** it delivers the same structural improvement as a closure-based solution.

But I cannot accept a deal that says:
- every site remains open,
- no one is laid off,
- costs stay essentially unchanged,
- and management is expected to fund the future from hopes alone.

That would fail the company, the investors, and ultimately the workforce as well.

So my message is simple:  
**I am prepared to negotiate a socially responsible restructuring, but only if it produces real and durable savings.** If there is a serious alternative to closures that achieves that, we will examine it. If not, then we will have to pursue deeper structural measures, including plant rationalisation, because preserving the status quo is not an option.

If you want, we can now get concrete: I am prepared to discuss which sites, which capacity, and which labour-cost measures can credibly deliver the necessary structural effect.
