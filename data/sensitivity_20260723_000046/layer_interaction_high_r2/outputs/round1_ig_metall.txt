We share the central point that this cannot be solved by putting the whole burden on the workforce.

The union is **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the dismantling of the collective wage framework as the price for management’s strategic mistakes.

That said, we are not here to deny the reality of the situation. If the company needs structural relief, then we are prepared to negotiate **shared sacrifices** — but only under clear conditions.

Our position is this:

1. **No closures, no forced layoffs.**  
   Those are red lines. A restructuring that destroys sites and throws people out of work is not a socially responsible transformation; it is a one-sided cost cut.

2. **A fair contribution from labour is possible, but only as part of a balanced package.**  
   We have already signalled openness to measures such as:
   - temporary wage concessions,
   - foregoing bonuses or special payments,
   - working-time reductions,
   - capacity adjustments through attrition and internal redeployment,
   - and, if necessary, an employee contribution into a restructuring fund.  
   But these can only be considered if management, owners, and other stakeholders also contribute materially.

3. **Management and shareholders must bear part of the pain.**  
   If the company wants labour to accept a contribution, then it must also commit to:
   - site and job security,
   - investment in German plants,
   - a credible product pipeline,
   - and a restructuring that does not hollow out the industrial base.

4. **We need durable guarantees, not vague promises.**  
   Short-term cuts with open-ended uncertainty afterward are unacceptable. If employees are to contribute now, they need a binding agreement that protects jobs and gives the sites a future.

So to be explicit: we are **open to negotiation**, but not to coercion. If the proposal remains plant closures, forced redundancies, and permanent insecurity, then we will oppose it with all available means, including industrial action. If, however, management comes with a genuine shared-sacrifice package that secures jobs and sites, then we are willing to talk seriously.
