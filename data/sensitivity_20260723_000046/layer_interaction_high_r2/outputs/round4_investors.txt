We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the discipline required to solve it.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural cost problem**.
- A cosmetic truce would be value-destructive.
- The solution must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.
- Non-closure measures should be used if they genuinely close the gap.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** accept a settlement that leaves:
- the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- margins permanently impaired,
- or restructuring so watered down that the share price and cash generation remain under pressure.

That is the limit for us. If the package does not restore competitive margins, we will not support it.

## On the employee-side red lines
The works council and union keep repeating:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030.

We understand why they want that. But from a shareholder perspective, those are acceptable only if they still allow the company to reach a credible cost position. So far, nobody has shown that they do.

A blanket no-closure position is not a solution in itself. It is only acceptable if the numbers prove that it does not block competitiveness.

## On management’s position
Management is right about one thing: non-closure measures may not fully replace footprint reduction if overcapacity is structural.

But management still has not provided a clean decision bridge. We need the residual-gap analysis, not just assertions.

## What we need now
Please put the case in a form investors can actually underwrite:

1. **Exact annual savings target**
2. **Savings from each non-closure measure**
   - attrition
   - redeployment
   - working-time changes
   - bonus restraint
   - productivity / overhead cuts
   - executive restraint
3. **Residual gap**
4. **Time to realization**
5. **Durability through 2030**

If the residual gap is small enough, a no-closure package may be acceptable. If the gap remains material, then management must retain the right to pursue footprint reduction.

## Our position on the bridge proposals
### Proposal 1: conditional no-closure package
Acceptable only if:
- it is genuinely time-limited,
- the savings are audited,
- and it does not become a permanent veto on necessary restructuring.

### Proposal 2: no forced redundancies plus voluntary footprint reduction
Potentially workable, but only if “voluntary” is not just a delayed closure in disguise.

### Proposal 3: shared sacrifice with investment commitments
This is the most credible direction, but only if the sacrifice is real and the outcome is economically sufficient.

## To the works council and union
If you want shareholder support, the package must do more than preserve headcount on paper. It has to:
- lower the fixed cost base materially,
- improve operating margin,
- and free capital for product investment.

If that cannot be achieved without some irreversible structural action, then that action must remain available.

## To management, the family, and the state
We are not asking for closures as a first instinct. We are asking for a settlement that is **economically sufficient**.

That means:
- no cosmetic deal,
- no prolonged conflict,
- no structural underperformance.

## Bottom line
We can back a negotiated restructuring if it credibly restores competitiveness and margin discipline.

We will **not** back an outcome that preserves the current cost problem under a more comfortable political label.

If the numbers show the non-closure package is enough, fine.  
If they do not, then deeper footprint reduction must stay on the table.
