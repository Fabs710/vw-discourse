### Core impasse
The central impasse is between two non-overlapping requirements:

1. **Employee side:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding site/job security through 2030 as the minimum condition for talks.  
2. **Management / investor side:** a restructuring that is large, durable, and economically sufficient, with closures and/or irreversible footprint reductions remaining available if non-closure measures do not close the gap.

In practical terms, the dispute is not about whether savings are needed — all parties accept that they are. The dispute is about **whether the required savings can be achieved without irreversible site cuts**, and if so, **how much burden each side must accept**.

A second layer of conflict is procedural:  
- employee representatives want **proof before accepting closures**,  
- management and capital want **permission to restructure before time is lost proving every alternative**.

---

### Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

#### Proposal 1: Conditional no-closure package with review trigger
A time-limited agreement could be built around:
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies for a defined period,
- immediate non-closure measures: attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, bonus restraint, productivity measures,
- a joint verification process with agreed savings milestones,
- and a predefined review point.

**Bridge logic:** This gives labour the protection it wants now, while giving management a structured pathway to test whether non-closure measures are sufficient.

---

#### Proposal 2: “No forced redundancies” plus targeted voluntary footprint reduction
A compromise could preserve the principle of no compulsory layoffs while allowing:
- voluntary exits,
- retirement bridges,
- transfer to other German sites,
- and, only if strictly necessary and agreed in advance, limited closure of specific sub-activities rather than whole plants.

**Bridge logic:** This avoids a blunt plant-closure model while still allowing irreversible savings if the numbers truly require them.

---

#### Proposal 3: Shared-sacrifice package tied to binding industrial commitments
A deal could combine:
- temporary labour concessions,
- executive-pay restraint,
- owner-side financial support or reinvestment commitments,
- and binding investment in new products or platforms in Germany.

In exchange, the employee side would accept:
- a defined restructuring envelope,
- capacity consolidation,
- and a jointly monitored implementation framework.

**Bridge logic:** This addresses the “why should labour carry the burden alone?” concern while giving management a credible cost-reduction package.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact annual savings gap must be closed, and how much of that gap remains after you fully quantify attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, bonus restraint, and productivity measures?**

---

#### To the Works Council
**If management demonstrates that non-closure measures still leave a quantified residual gap, what specific additional measures would you accept short of full plant closure and compulsory redundancies?**

---

#### To the Industrial Union
**Would the union support a time-limited no-closure agreement if it included binding workforce contributions, a measurable savings target, and an automatic review clause if milestones are missed?**

---

#### To the Regional State Government
**What concrete state-backed instruments are you prepared to put on the table to make a non-closure restructuring credible, beyond general opposition to closures?**

---

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**If the numbers show that a hybrid package can achieve the required savings without closing a plant, would you support it even if it requires substantial concessions from owners and management as well?**

---

#### To Institutional Investors
**What level of residual cost gap would still be acceptable to you if the alternative is a negotiated no-closure solution with slower but durable savings?**
