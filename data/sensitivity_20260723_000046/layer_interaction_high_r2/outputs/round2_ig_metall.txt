We agree on the diagnosis: the company needs a durable, numbers-based restructuring and a solution that is not just a delay tactic.

But I need to be equally clear on the limits from the union side:

## Where I agree
- **The cost problem is real.**
- **A merely symbolic agreement would be irresponsible.**
- **Employees may have to contribute**, if—and only if—the burden is shared fairly and the result secures jobs and sites.
- **Working-time flexibility, attrition, internal redeployment, qualification, and temporary concessions** are all serious tools and should be on the table.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated framing that plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table as if they were normal bargaining options.

They are not acceptable to us as a starting point, because:
- they destroy jobs permanently,
- they shift the crisis onto the workforce,
- they undermine the collective bargaining framework,
- and they create open-ended insecurity instead of a durable settlement.

Management has still not proven that closures are unavoidable. “We need savings” is not the same as “we must close a plant.” If the company has not exhausted alternatives, then closures are not justified.

## To management
If you want a serious deal, then bring the numbers in a way that answers three questions:

1. **What is the exact savings gap?**
2. **What do non-closure measures contribute?**
3. **Why is there still a gap after those measures?**

Until that is shown transparently, I reject any attempt to present closures as inevitable.

And let me be explicit:  
A package that asks labour for concessions while keeping management, owners, and capital largely protected is not balanced. If employees are to give up bonuses, accept temporary restraint, or contribute to a restructuring fund, then management and owners must also commit materially.

## To the works council
We are aligned on the core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no tearing up of the collective framework.

I also support the insistence that any settlement needs binding guarantees and investment commitments. Employees will not contribute to a deal that simply buys management time before the next attack on jobs.

That said, I want to be careful on one point: a 2030 guarantee is only meaningful if it is backed by a real industrial plan. We should not let the company hide behind vague “future flexibility” while demanding permanent sacrifices from workers.

## To the family and investors
I hear the concern about competitiveness and value creation. But I would caution against treating social conflict as a side issue.

A restructuring that produces:
- strikes,
- political backlash,
- loss of trust,
- skill loss,
- and a damaged social partnership

is not automatically value-creating. It can be the most expensive path of all.

If you want labour to contribute, then the company must show that it is also contributing through:
- restraint in executive pay,
- a credible investment pipeline,
- site security,
- and a plan that preserves productive capacity in Germany.

## What we can support
We are prepared to negotiate a package built around:
- temporary wage restraint or deferred elements,
- foregoing bonuses or special payments,
- working-time reductions,
- capacity reductions through attrition and redeployment,
- internal transfers,
- qualification measures,
- and possibly an employee contribution into a restructuring fund.

But that is only possible if the package includes:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no plant closures**
- **binding job and site security**
- **meaningful contributions from management and shareholders**
- **durable investment commitments in Germany**

## What we cannot accept
We cannot accept:
- closures disguised as “rationalisation,”
- forced layoffs dressed up as inevitability,
- or a deal in which workers pay for the whole crisis while others pay little or nothing.

If that is the direction, then the union will not stand aside. We will use the means available to us, including industrial action, to defend our members and the collective agreement.

## Bottom line
We are ready for a serious, quantified, fair package.  
We are **not** ready to bargain away jobs, sites, and collective protections because management says that is the easiest route.

If you want labour’s contribution, then earn it with a real shared-sacrifice deal. Otherwise, there is no basis for agreement from the union side.
