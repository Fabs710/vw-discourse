## Where I agree
I agree with the analysis that the company has a **serious structural competitiveness problem** and that a cosmetic deal would be irresponsible. I also agree that we need a **quantified savings bridge**, not more abstractions.

And yes: the expert input matters. It confirms that non-closure measures can create **real** savings, but that they may not fully substitute for footprint reduction if the overcapacity is truly structural.

That part is credible.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated attempt to convert that credible warning into a political default for plant closures.

We are not at “closures are the only responsible option.”  
We are still at: **show that a serious non-closure package cannot work first**.

That distinction matters, and I will not let it be blurred.

## To management
You keep saying you need “structural” savings. Fine. Then quantify them properly and fully:
- exact annual savings target,
- savings from attrition,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- bonus restraint,
- executive restraint,
- procurement and overhead reductions,
- and the residual gap.

Until that bridge is on the table, closures are not a necessity. They are your preferred instrument.

And let me be clear: I do not accept a situation where the state is asked to bless irreversible site cuts before alternatives have been fully tested.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that jobs and sites are not just bargaining chips. That is legitimate, and it reflects the company’s role in the region.

But I also need to be frank: if the non-closure package is to be credible, it must be **more than a symbolic promise of flexibility**. It has to deliver hard savings. That means the workforce will need to contribute in measurable ways:
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus restraint or deferral,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment,
- attrition,
- productivity gains,
- and possibly an employee contribution fund.

That is acceptable to me **if** it is matched by:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- real investment commitments in Germany,
- and binding respect for co-determination.

That is the deal space.

## To the family and investors
I understand the concern about delay and value destruction. I share the concern about drift.

But I reject the notion that protecting the regional industrial base is somehow a sentimental add-on to the economic calculation. It is part of the economic calculation. A closure-led confrontation can damage:
- trust,
- skills,
- supplier networks,
- labor peace,
- and the company’s legitimacy in its home region.

Those are not side effects. They are real costs.

So if you want my support for deeper restructuring, you must show that the company cannot get there by negotiated means first.

## On the expert assessment
The expert’s main value here is to show the **range** of what non-closure measures can do. And that range is not trivial.

What the expert did **not** establish is that a German plant must be closed.  
That conclusion is being smuggled in too early.

The real question is:
**Can a negotiated package close enough of the gap to avoid irreversible site cuts while still restoring competitiveness?**

That is still open. Therefore closures cannot be treated as the starting assumption.

## What I can support
I can support a serious restructuring package that combines:
- attrition,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary labour-cost concessions,
- productivity and overhead reductions,
- executive restraint,
- and binding investment in Germany.

I can also support a **time-limited no-closure implementation phase** if:
- milestones are audited,
- the savings are real,
- and the package is reviewed jointly and transparently.

What I cannot support is:
- a blanket exclusion of non-closure testing,
- a closure-first mindset,
- or a demand that the state surrender its role before the numbers justify it.

## What would move me
What could move me is a package that:
- preserves German sites,
- protects employment,
- restores competitiveness,
- and gives the company room to invest through 2030.

That is not a soft position. That is the economically and politically responsible one for a regional anchor shareholder.

## What I will not accept
Let me be explicit:
- no plant closures as the default option,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no sidelining of co-determination,
- no stripping the region of its industrial base to solve a short-term cost problem.

If management insists that closures remain the starting lever, then we are not close to agreement.
