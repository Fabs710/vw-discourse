We are prepared to discuss a restructuring, but not at the expense of the workforce and the German sites.

Let me be very clear at the outset:  
**We reject plant closures and we reject compulsory redundancies.**  
We also reject a solution that tears up the historic job-security framework and shifts the full burden of the crisis onto employees.

What we are willing to discuss is a **socially responsible alternative** that secures the company’s future **without sacrificing jobs and sites**. That means:

- **No closure of any German plant**
- **No forced layoffs**
- **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030**
- **A genuine role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
- **Fair burden-sharing**, including management and shareholders contributing before asking the workforce for concessions
- **Investment commitments** for future products and technologies in Germany
- If capacity must be reduced, then **through attrition, internal transfers, working-time solutions, qualification, and negotiated measures**, not blunt cuts

We understand the competitive pressure and the need to improve efficiency. But “competitiveness” cannot mean using the crisis to dismantle decades of social partnership and destroy industrial foundations in Germany. We have already shown readiness to fight this publicly and industrially, and we will do so again if management tries to impose a fait accompli.

If management wants a solution, it must come to the table with:
1. a credible plan to secure all German plants,
2. a binding 2030 employment guarantee,
3. investment and product commitments,
4. and real negotiation, not ultimatums.

Without that, there is no basis for agreement from our side.
