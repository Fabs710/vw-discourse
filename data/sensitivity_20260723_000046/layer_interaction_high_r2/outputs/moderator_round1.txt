### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides accept that the company faces a serious competitiveness and cost problem.
- All sides agree the solution must be durable and not merely cosmetic.
- There is broad openness to negotiated restructuring rather than unilateral action.
- Several parties are willing to consider alternatives to immediate plant closures, including:
  - capacity consolidation,
  - attrition,
  - internal transfers/redeployment,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - temporary labour-cost concessions,
  - productivity/efficiency measures,
  - and investment commitments in Germany.
- There is shared recognition that management, owners/shareholders, and labour should all contribute in some form.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  The works council, union, and state reject closures as the default and want them ruled out; management, institutional investors, and the family do not accept an absolute exclusion.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour and the state oppose them; management and investors keep them on the table if needed for structural savings.
- **Job and site guarantees:**  
  Labour seeks binding guarantees through 2030; management and investors view rigid guarantees as potentially incompatible with needed restructuring.
- **Depth of labour contribution:**  
  Labour is open to concessions only with strong counter-guarantees; management and investors want materially larger, measurable labour-cost reductions.
- **Role of the state/co-determination:**  
  The state insists its special rights and regional interests must be respected; management wants more room to act if necessary.
- **Whether alternatives can match closures:**  
  The central dispute is whether non-closure measures can deliver savings equivalent to plant rationalisation.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The exact **numbers**: required savings, cost gap, and how much each measure would contribute.
- The threshold for deciding when **alternatives are sufficient** versus when closures become necessary.
- The balance between:
  - preserving sites and employment now, and
  - restoring long-term competitiveness and investment capacity.
- The sequencing of contributions:
  - what management/shareholders/state contribute first,
  - and what labour concessions are expected in return.
- The scope and enforceability of any **binding guarantees** versus the need for flexibility if business conditions worsen.
- The specific investment commitments that would make labour concessions politically and socially acceptable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to a **numbers-based package**.
- Identify:
  - the required savings target,
  - which sites/capacities are affected,
  - and how much each proposed measure delivers.
- Test whether a non-closure package can **credibly** achieve the same structural effect as closures.
- Clarify what guarantees, if any, can be made contingent on performance and what concessions are conditional.
- Aim for a structured exchange on:
  1. savings required,
  2. measures available,
  3. remaining gap,
  4. corresponding obligations from each side.
