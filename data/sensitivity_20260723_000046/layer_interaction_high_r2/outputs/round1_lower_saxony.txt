Thank you. I want to be equally clear from the state’s side.

We recognize the company’s cost problem and the need for a credible restructuring. We are not defending a status quo that simply ignores market reality. But we **do not accept the framing that plant closures and mass job losses are the default answer**.

Our position is straightforward:

- **No weakening of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **No outcome that guts regional employment**
- **No closure of German plants as the first or easy option**
- **Yes to a restructuring that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor**

I agree with the union and works council on the core point: if employees are asked to contribute, it must be part of a **balanced, negotiated package**. The burden cannot fall on the workforce alone while capital is insulated. The company has obligations to its people, its sites, and the regions that have supported it for decades.

Where I disagree with management is the assumption that “durable savings” necessarily require closures. That is too narrow and too risky. Once a plant is closed, the damage to the region is often irreversible. That is not a reversible adjustment; it is a permanent loss of industrial capacity, supplier networks, and trust. From a long-term public-interest perspective, that should be the last resort, not the starting point.

That said, I am not dismissing the need for hard measures. A viable alternative could include:

- capacity reductions through internal consolidation,
- working-time flexibility,
- targeted voluntary exits and attrition,
- temporary labour-cost measures,
- management restraint,
- and a real investment package for future products and technologies in Germany.

But this only becomes acceptable if it is tied to:
1. **binding site security**
2. **job security for the German core operations**
3. **clear investment commitments**
4. **a credible industrial strategy for the affected regions**
5. **respect for co-determination and the special shareholder rights that exist for a reason**

So my message to management is: if you want our support, bring us a restructuring that restores competitiveness **without hollowing out the company’s domestic base**. We will not sign off on a solution that treats German plants as disposable assets.

At the same time, I would ask the employee side to stay open to **meaningful, temporary contributions** if they genuinely help secure the long term. The state can support a social-partnership solution, but only if it is credible and balanced.

In short:  
**We are open to restructuring. We are not open to dismantlement.**  
If management wants a durable settlement, it needs to come back with numbers and measures that prove closures are not necessary. If it cannot do that, then it should not be surprised by resistance from the state, from the workforce, and from the broader regional public interest.
