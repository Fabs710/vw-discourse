As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family, and even the investors on one point: **this cannot be a cosmetic restructuring**. The company needs a durable solution that restores competitiveness and secures the future of the brand.

I also agree that the restructuring must be **numbers-based**. If the alternatives cannot materially close the cost gap, then we are wasting time.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated implication that plant closures and compulsory redundancies are simply the “credible” or default answer.

That is too narrow, too risky, and too destructive for the regional economy. A closure is not just another efficiency measure. It is a permanent loss of industrial capacity, supplier networks, skills, and trust. Once that step is taken, it cannot be undone. For a company that is central to the region, that must remain a last resort, not the starting position.

I also do not accept any approach that uses the crisis to weaken the state’s co-determination and veto role. That role exists for a reason, and it cannot be treated as an obstacle to be bypassed.

## To management
You say you need “durable savings.” I accept that. But you have not yet demonstrated that closures are unavoidable rather than merely administratively convenient.

Before asking the workforce or the state to accept irreversible cuts, management must show:
- the exact savings target,
- the contribution of non-closure measures,
- the remaining gap,
- and why that gap cannot be closed through a balanced restructuring.

Non-closure measures are not a sideshow. They are the proper first test in a company of this significance. That includes:
- capacity consolidation without plant shutdowns where possible,
- attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary labour-cost measures,
- productivity improvements,
- and a real investment plan for Germany.

If management cannot prove these are insufficient, then it should not demand plant closures as if they were already justified.

## To the works council and union
I support your insistence that the workforce cannot be made to carry the full burden.

But I also want to be constructive on the substance: I am prepared to support a negotiated social-partnership package if it is credible and balanced. That means:
- protecting German plants wherever possible,
- avoiding compulsory redundancies,
- using attrition and redeployment first,
- temporary and proportionate labour contributions,
- and binding investment and product commitments in Germany.

I do not want an empty promise of “no pain.” There will have to be contributions from all sides. But those contributions must not become a cover for dismantling the domestic base.

## To the family and investors
I understand the concern for value creation and margin restoration. But long-term value is not served by treating the German industrial base as expendable.

A settlement that triggers strikes, political backlash, loss of skills, and damage to the company’s regional legitimacy can be more expensive than a negotiated solution that shares the burden fairly.

So yes, the economics matter. But so does social stability. In this company, the two are linked.

## My bottom line
The state’s position is this:

- **We support restructuring.**
- **We do not support closures as the default answer.**
- **We do not support mass layoffs as a first resort.**
- **We do not accept any outcome that guts regional employment.**
- **We do support a negotiated package that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor.**

If management wants our backing, it must come forward with a package that proves:
1. the company can regain competitiveness **without hollowing out its German base**, or
2. if no such package exists, exactly why not.

Until then, I will not endorse plant closures as inevitable. And I will use the state’s rights and influence to ensure that the regional interest, co-determination, and social partnership are not pushed aside.
