We are prepared to discuss **numbers**, but not to negotiate away the workforce’s core protections under the guise of “realism.”

Let me be equally direct in Round 2:

## What I agree with
I agree with management, the family, and the investors on one point:  
**the company needs durable savings and a credible path back to competitiveness.**

I also agree that a purely cosmetic solution would be irresponsible. If we were simply trying to postpone the problem, that would not protect jobs in the medium term.

## What I do not accept
What I do **not** accept is the repeated implication that the only “credible” restructuring is one that includes:

- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- and the dismantling of the historic job-security framework.

That is not a neutral economic conclusion. It is a **choice** — and it is a choice we reject.

Management still has not shown that closures are unavoidable. Saying “capacity must come down” is not the same as proving that a German plant must be shut. And saying “labour must contribute” is not the same as proving that employees must accept forced layoffs or the loss of site guarantees.

## To management
If you want our support, bring the hard numbers in a way that proves:

1. **what savings are actually required**,  
2. **what each alternative measure contributes**, and  
3. **why a non-closure package cannot close the gap**.

Until then, closures remain a proposal — not a necessity.

Also, let me be clear: **a binding 2030 site and job guarantee is not a decorative demand.** It is the minimum basis for any serious negotiation. If management wants labour to contribute, then it must first commit to preserving the German industrial base.

## To the family and investors
I hear the concern about value creation and return on sales. But you are ignoring the value destruction caused by a closure-based strategy:

- loss of trust,
- loss of skills,
- destabilization of supplier networks,
- labour conflict,
- political backlash,
- and damage to the brand and the company’s social license to operate.

A “numbers-based” package that destroys long-term industrial peace is not automatically rational. Repeated restructuring and permanent conflict are also expensive.

## What a serious alternative must include
We are not refusing adjustment. We are saying the adjustment must be **socially responsible and reversible where possible**. A credible package can include:

- capacity consolidation **without closure**,
- internal redeployment across German sites,
- attrition and voluntary exits,
- working-time reductions,
- temporary wage restraint,
- bonus deferrals,
- productivity improvements,
- training and qualification,
- and investment commitments for new products in Germany.

If management believes these measures are insufficient, then show us the math. But do not present closures as a foregone conclusion before that proof exists.

## Our red lines remain unchanged
To avoid any misunderstanding:

- **No closure of any German plant**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No tearing up of the job-security framework**
- **No fait accompli**

If those are crossed, then there is no agreement from the works council side, and you can expect full resistance — industrially, publicly, and through co-determination.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:

- how much capacity can be reduced through **attrition and redeployment**,
- what **temporary employee contribution** could look like if it is truly matched by commitments from management and owners,
- what **investment guarantees** are being offered,
- and what **co-determination role** the workforce will have in implementing any plan.

But we will not be pushed into accepting plant closures or forced layoffs because management prefers the apparent simplicity of a blunt cut.

## Bottom line
If you want a settlement, then bring a package that:

- protects every German plant,
- guarantees jobs through 2030,
- and shares the burden fairly across management, owners, and labour.

If you cannot do that, then there is no basis for expecting the works council to stand down.
