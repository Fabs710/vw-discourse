We agree on the diagnosis, but not on an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally impaired.

## Where we agree
- The company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**.
- A cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.
- The answer must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.
- Non-closure measures are worth pursuing if they materially reduce the cost base.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** accept the employee-side attempt to make:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and a 2030 guarantee for every site

the default minimum for talks.

That position may be understandable politically, but from a shareholder perspective it is only acceptable if it still delivers **competitive margins and a materially lower fixed-cost base**. So far, no one has shown that it does.

## What the expert assessments tell us
The experts were helpful, and their message is not ambiguous:
- non-closure measures can deliver real savings,
- but they often capture only a portion of the savings from footprint reduction,
- and if the overcapacity is structural, some irreversible action may still be necessary.

That is exactly our concern. The question is not whether alternatives have some value. They do. The question is whether they are **sufficient**.

## What we need to see
Before we back any settlement, we need a hard bridge:
1. exact annual savings target,
2. quantified savings from attrition, redeployment, working-time changes, bonus restraint, productivity, and executive restraint,
3. the residual gap,
4. the time to achieve those savings,
5. and the durability of the package.

If a non-closure package closes the gap credibly, fine. If it does not, then management should not be prevented from using stronger restructuring tools.

## Our position on the current red lines
We will **not** support an arrangement that:
- protects every site regardless of economics,
- preserves an above-peer cost base,
- or turns the restructuring into a temporary truce that postpones the same crisis.

We are also not endorsing closures as a first instinct. But closures or equivalent footprint reduction must remain on the table if the numbers require them.

## To management
You are right to insist that temporary concessions alone are not enough. But you still need to present the residual-gap analysis clearly.

## To the works council and union
You are right that trust and social peace matter. But investor support depends on **results**, not on whether the deal is politically comfortable. If the package leaves the company expensive and weak, that is not a compromise we can endorse.

## To the state and the family
We respect the need for social stability and long-term stewardship. But stewardship means protecting the company’s future competitiveness, not freezing the current footprint.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution only if it produces:
- materially lower structural costs,
- a credible return to target margins,
- and enough flexibility to adjust if non-closure measures fall short.

Without that, the outcome is not a restructuring. It is delay.
