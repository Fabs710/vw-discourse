Here is a neutral facilitator readout of the plateau and possible ways forward.

## Core impasse
The discourse is blocked on one central issue:

**Whether a credible restructuring can be achieved with all German plants kept open, or whether management/investors require at least some closures to reach the 2030 economics.**

That single disagreement is driving several linked deadlocks:
- **Scope of restructuring:** no-closure vs. closure-led
- **Burden-sharing:** how much employees contribute, and under what guarantees
- **Credibility:** whether non-closure savings are sufficiently quantified and binding
- **Governance:** whether co-determination and the state’s role remain unchanged
- **Trust:** whether any “review” or “moratorium” is a genuine safeguard or a deferred closure path

In procedural terms, the conflict is not yet about details of measures. It is about **which restructuring architecture is admissible at all**.

---

## Possible compromise positions
Below are three facilitator-style bridging options that could narrow the gap. These are not endorsements; they are candidate negotiation frames.

### 1) Time-bound no-closure restructuring with hard triggers
A compromise where:
- all German plants remain open for a defined period,
- workforce contribution measures are agreed immediately,
- management commits to a plant-by-plant savings bridge,
- and any future reconsideration is allowed only if predefined financial triggers are missed.

**Why it may bridge parties:**  
- Satisfies labor and state red lines in the near term  
- Gives management/investors a performance-based exit if targets are missed  
- Converts the closure dispute into a measurable delivery test

**Key requirement:** triggers must be objective, audited, and jointly monitored.

---

### 2) Hybrid footprint protection with product/volume concentration
A compromise where:
- plants remain legally open,
- but specific products, lines, or shifts are removed from weaker sites,
- volume is concentrated in fewer production lanes,
- and the workforce accepts limited, time-bound concessions in exchange for binding investment and site guarantees.

**Why it may bridge parties:**  
- Delivers some of the fixed-cost relief management wants  
- Avoids the symbolic and political break of formal closures  
- Offers labor and the state a way to claim site protection with real internal correction

**Key requirement:** the package must produce measurable recurring savings, not just employment smoothing.

---

### 3) Two-step settlement: immediate burden-sharing, deferred structural decision
A compromise where:
- both sides agree now on voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, wage restraint, and productivity measures,
- while the company receives a limited period to prove that these measures close the gap,
- and only then is the question of closures revisited.

**Why it may bridge parties:**  
- Reduces immediate conflict and buys time  
- Lets the parties test the non-closure hypothesis with actual numbers  
- Avoids a premature commitment to either full closure or full preservation

**Key requirement:** the deferred decision must be tied to a jointly agreed evidence base, not unilateral management review.

---

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific internal capacity reductions, voluntary exits, and productivity measures would you accept as sufficient evidence that a no-closure plan is real rather than symbolic?**

### Industrial Union
**What quantified workforce contribution, including duration and scope, are you prepared to table if all German plants are guaranteed to remain open for a defined horizon?**

### Group & Brand Management
**Which plant-by-plant numbers would you accept as the minimum proof that a no-closure route cannot meet the 2030 target before asking for closure authority?**

### Regional State Government
**What binding monitoring or trigger mechanism would you support to distinguish a genuine no-closure restructuring from a deferred closure process?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**What evidence threshold would you require before treating plant closures as unavoidable rather than simply preferable?**

### Institutional Investors
**Would you support a no-closure package if it delivered verified recurring savings and a credible 2030 margin path, even if the one-time savings are lower than in a closure scenario?**

### Independent Expert
**What single metric would you prioritize to determine whether the no-closure route is genuinely credible: utilization, recurring EBIT savings, or headcount reduction?**
