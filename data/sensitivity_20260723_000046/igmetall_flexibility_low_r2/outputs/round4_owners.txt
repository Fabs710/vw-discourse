## My position
I support a **serious restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins by 2030**.  
I do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves a structurally uncompetitive cost base.  
I also do **not** accept that plant closures are automatically the starting point before a credible no-closure or hybrid alternative has been proven insufficient.

That is the standard I will apply.

---

## To management
I agree with your core diagnosis:
- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and “business as usual” is not an option.

Where I disagree is with the way you are leaning on closures before you have fully established why non-closure measures cannot get the job done.

The expert inputs made one thing clear: **closures usually deliver stronger recurring savings**, but **that is not the same as proving necessity**. If you want governance support for closures, bring a **plant-by-plant business case** that shows:
- current utilization,
- transferable volume,
- recurring savings by measure,
- one-time costs,
- and the 2030 margin bridge under each scenario.

Until then, I will not treat closures as justified merely because they are cleaner on paper.

What I will support is:
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- capacity concentration,
- and limited labor-cost contributions.

But only if the result is **durable recurring savings** and not a delay tactic.

---

## To the works council
I respect that you are defending jobs and sites. That is your role.  
But I do **not** agree that “no closures” by itself is a restructuring plan.

You say a serious no-closure path is possible. Fine. Then prove it in numbers.

I agree with you that:
- a weak or symbolic compromise is unacceptable,
- and disguised shutdowns through vague reviews or deferred triggers are not acceptable either.

But I do **not** accept your attempt to make all closures illegitimate in advance, regardless of the economics. If the no-closure route cannot remove enough fixed cost and capacity, then it is not a solution; it is a postponement.

So yes, I am open to a no-closure package — but only if it includes:
- real capacity reduction,
- real headcount reduction through voluntary means,
- real labor-cost relief,
- and a credible 2030 margin path.

If that package does not exist, then I will not support a structure that locks in underperformance.

## To the industrial union
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss real concessions:
- wage restraint,
- reduced bonuses,
- flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- and productivity measures.

That is constructive.

But let’s be clear: those measures only matter if they produce **material recurring savings**. If the package is too small, too temporary, or too soft on capacity, then it will not change the company’s trajectory.

I agree with you that workers should not carry the full burden.  
I disagree with you if “no closures” becomes an absolute principle that prevents the company from reaching competitiveness.

If you want my support for a no-closure solution, it must be:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- durable,
- and strong enough to close the gap.

## To the regional state government
I agree with you on the importance of regional stability and industrial legitimacy. Those matter.

But I do **not** accept a position that rules out closures in principle while also demanding full competitiveness. If a no-closure route can genuinely work, it is the better route. If it cannot, then we cannot preserve every site merely for symbolism.

Your role should be to help produce a serious solution, not to freeze the structure.

I would support a time-bound no-closure framework **only if** it is tied to:
- verified savings,
- real capacity concentration,
- workforce contribution,
- and a hard monitoring regime.

What I will not support is a framework that simply defers the same decision while leaving the cost base untouched.

---

## To institutional investors
I agree with your standard:
- durable savings,
- credible margin restoration,
- and no cosmetic compromise.

That is the correct lens.

Where I differ from a closure-first mindset is this: closures are a tool, not the goal. If a no-closure or hybrid package achieves the same recurring economics with less instability, that better protects long-term value. If it does not, then closures remain on the table.

So your support should be conditioned on one thing: **show me the recurring economics**.  
Not slogans. Not red lines. Not general claims.
