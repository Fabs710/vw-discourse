We agree on the core point: **there will be no settlement that is built on plant closures or compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable detail; it is the red line for the workforce, and the union will defend it with all means available.

Management is trying to present a one-sided “competitiveness” story where employees are asked to absorb the pain first and most. That is not acceptable. If the company wants a durable restructuring, then the burden must be **shared fairly**:

- management and owners must contribute,
- capital and overhead must be examined seriously,
- capacity can be adjusted,
- working time, internal redeployment, and qualification must be used,
- and the workforce can discuss a **limited, negotiated labour-cost contribution**.

But that only makes sense if it is tied to **binding guarantees**:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of the collective wage framework,
- and a credible commitment to future investment and product allocation in Germany.

We also want to be clear on what we will not accept:
- “temporary” cuts that become permanent,
- wage concessions with no site security,
- vague promises instead of binding commitments,
- or restructuring that simply shifts the cost onto employees while management keeps full flexibility.

So yes, we are prepared to talk about a fair contribution from the workforce. But that is not a blank cheque. If the company wants our cooperation, it must show that it is prepared to protect jobs and sites first, not after the damage is done.

If management continues to push closures or forced layoffs, then you should assume escalating resistance, including industrial action. We have already shown that the peace obligation is over. The workforce is ready to defend its interests.
