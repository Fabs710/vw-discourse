I appreciate the clarity from all sides. From the controlling family’s perspective, I will be equally clear: **we need a restructuring that restores competitiveness in a durable way, and we cannot accept a solution that merely defers the problem or destroys the company’s long-term value.**

### Where I agree
- I agree with management that the company’s German cost base is not competitive enough and that structural action is necessary.
- I agree with the works council, the union, and the state government that a settlement must preserve industrial peace and avoid unnecessary instability.
- I also agree that a negotiated path is preferable to a prolonged confrontation that damages output, reputation, and value.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that the only way to secure competitiveness is automatically through plant closures and compulsory redundancies. That may be one possible instrument, but it is not a satisfactory answer unless it is truly unavoidable and demonstrably better than alternatives.

At the same time, I also do **not** accept the position that no material structural change is possible if closures are off the table. A simple “no closures, no layoffs, no meaningful cost adjustment” would not be a viable outcome either. It would protect headlines in the short term and erode the enterprise over time.

### What matters to us
The family holding’s priority is not short-term conflict avoidance at any price. It is:
- a competitive cost base,
- a credible profitability path,
- stable labour relations,
- and preservation of the enterprise’s long-term value and governance integrity.

That means we will support a package only if it is **economically credible** and **durable**.

### What I would consider workable
A serious package could include:
- capacity reductions where they are operationally justified,
- working-time adjustments and flexibility,
- internal redeployment and qualification,
- a limited and clearly defined employee contribution,
- investment commitments tied to the restructuring,
- and a binding mechanism to review progress against competitiveness targets.

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- a settlement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- a cosmetic deal that avoids hard decisions while margins continue to deteriorate,
- or a prolonged standoff that inflicts value destruction and weakens the company’s future.

### To management
If you want support from the controlling shareholder, you need to present a plan that is not just decisive, but credible. If closures are part of that plan, you must show clearly why less destructive alternatives are insufficient and how value is protected. Do not assume support for a maximalist approach by default.

### To the works council and union
If you want to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then you must engage on the substance of competitiveness, not just on red lines. The question is not whether employees contribute at all, but whether the proposed contribution is sufficient to restore the business. If it is not, then you are asking the owners to accept a continuing deterioration.

### To the state government
Your concern for the regional footprint is understandable and important. But a regional footprint is only sustainable if the business is competitive. A commitment to no closures cannot become a substitute for economic reality. I would support a solution that preserves sites **if** it also delivers the needed structural improvement.

### Bottom line
We are **not** here to approve a value-destroying compromise, and we are **not** here to endorse closure as a reflex. The right outcome is a restructuring package that is tough enough to restore competitiveness, but disciplined enough to preserve labour peace and the long-term strength of the company.

If the parties want our support, they should bring forward a quantified package that shows:
1. where the savings come from,
2. why they are sufficient,
3. how labour peace is secured,
4. and how the company’s long-term value is protected.

Without that, I will not treat any proposal as acceptable.
