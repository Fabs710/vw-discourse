I do **not** support compulsory redundancies.  
I do **not** support any solution that weakens co-determination or the state’s veto role.

## To management

Where I disagree is your leap from “we need serious restructuring” to “therefore closures are necessary.”

That has **not** been proven.

What the expert summaries show is:
- a no-closure path is **possible**,
- but only if it is deep and quantified,
- with real capacity concentration, voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, productivity gains, and labor-cost contribution.

That is the path I want you to pursue first, because it protects the company’s role in the region and avoids unnecessary destruction of jobs and trust.

If you want to argue closures are unavoidable, then bring:
- a plant-by-plant business case,
- verified utilization data,
- the fixed-cost stack by site,
- and a credible comparison showing why non-closure measures cannot close the gap.

Until then, closures remain a proposal, not a necessity.

- no plant closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- no weakening of the employment framework.

- every German plant remains open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- co-determination preserved,
- investment commitments secured.

- measurable capacity reduction inside the footprint,
- real labor-cost relief,
