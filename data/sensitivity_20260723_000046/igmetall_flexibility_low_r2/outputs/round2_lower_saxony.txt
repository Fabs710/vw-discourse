As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I’ll speak plainly.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on one point: **the company needs real structural improvement, not a symbolic compromise.**  
I also agree with the union and works council that **industrial peace, regional stability, and long-term employment security** matter. A restructuring that destroys trust, sidelines co-determination, and leaves the state with a hollowed-out industrial base is not acceptable.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the current framing from management that plant closures are the default or that job and site protection is somehow incompatible with competitiveness. The expert made clear that a **no-closure path can be viable** if it is serious: volume concentration, voluntary exits, labor-cost contribution, working-time flexibility, and product/investment commitments. That is not a fantasy; it is a real restructuring route.

Likewise, I do **not** accept the idea that the workforce must first surrender closures and redundancy protection before management shows its hand. If you want employee contributions, then you need to prove that you are using **all non-destructive options first** and that the savings are truly sufficient.

### To management
Your argument still sounds like: “accept closures unless the workforce magically solves the problem.” That is too one-sided.

If you want support from this shareholder, then bring a **quantified no-closure package**:
- where exactly the savings come from,
- how much of the cost gap is closed by voluntary measures,
- what capacity is being removed without shutting plants,
- what investment goes to German sites,
- and how you preserve the company as a regional anchor.

Right now, you have not shown that closures are unavoidable. You have shown only that they are the easiest instrument for management.

### To the works council and union
I support your rejection of plant closures and compulsory redundancies as a matter of principle. But I will also be direct with you: a simple “everything stays, nothing changes” is not a workable answer either.

If we want to defend sites and jobs, then the workforce side must be prepared for:
- a limited and time-bound contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- and measurable productivity gains.

That is the price of preserving the footprint. I am prepared to back that. I am **not** prepared to back mass layoffs or site closure as the price of management flexibility.

### To the controlling family
You speak of long-term value, and I agree with that. But long-term value is not just a margin chart; it is also the company’s standing in its home region, its labour relations, and its legitimacy as a major employer.

A package that preserves value but strips out the company’s regional role and co-determination culture is not a victory. It is a self-inflicted strategic loss.

### To institutional investors
You are right that capital cannot back a permanently uncompetitive structure. But you should also recognize that **a well-designed no-closure restructuring can deliver substantial recurring savings** without tearing up the social foundation of the business.

Your test should not be “does it close a plant?” but “does it deliver recurring savings, lower utilization waste, and a credible margin path by 2030?” If yes, then closures are not the only serious option.

---

## My position
I will support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:

1. **No plant closures in Germany**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Preservation of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
4. **Binding site and investment commitments**
5. **A real, limited workforce contribution**
6. **Measurable capacity reduction through non-closure means**
7. **A credible path to competitiveness by 2030**

What I will **not** support:
- a closure-led plan as the starting point,
- forced layoffs dressed up as restructuring,
- or any deal that weakens our co-determination role or guts regional employment.

So my message is simple:  
**Bring a real no-closure restructuring package, and we can support it. Bring a closure package, and you will face my veto.**
