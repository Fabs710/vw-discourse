### Core impasse

The central blockage is between **workforce/state-side red lines** and **management/investor demands for structural savings**:

- **Works Council, union, and state government:** no plant closures in Germany, no compulsory redundancies, and no weakening of existing job-security guarantees.
- **Management, family holding, and institutional investors:** the company needs durable, quantified cost and capacity reductions; they will not accept a deal that preserves the current cost base.

In short: **one side is asking for binding job and site protection; the other is asking for binding structural relief.** Neither side has yet specified a package that delivers both at once.

---

### Possible compromise positions to bridge the gap

#### 1) “No closures now” with a binding review trigger
A compromise could include:
- a **moratorium on plant closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined period**,
- paired with a **binding restructuring review** against agreed financial and operational targets,
- and a **pre-agreed escalation mechanism** if targets are missed.

This gives workforce-side actors immediate protection while giving management/investors a route to reopen structural options later if the economics do not improve.

#### 2) Capacity reduction through non-layoff instruments first
A second compromise could prioritize:
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification/retraining,
- transfer of production volumes within the German footprint,
- and limited, negotiated labor-cost contributions.

This would aim to generate part of the required savings **without immediate closures or compulsory redundancies**, while still producing measurable cost relief.

#### 3) Site guarantees tied to explicit workforce contributions and investment commitments
A third path could be a **package deal**:
- binding site and employment protections through a defined horizon,
- in exchange for a **time-limited employee contribution**,
- plus management commitments on investment, product allocation, and productivity measures,
- with independent monitoring of whether the package is actually closing the competitiveness gap.

This would create reciprocity: workforce concessions would only apply alongside verifiable company commitments.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Works Council
If plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain excluded, **what specific mix of capacity reduction, working-time change, redeployment, and labor-cost contribution would you accept that still produces a measurable competitiveness gain by 2030?**

#### To Industrial Union
What is the **maximum temporary contribution from wages, working time, or flexibility** you would consider, and under what **binding guarantees** on jobs, sites, and investment would that contribution be acceptable?

#### To Group & Brand Management
Can you specify **which savings must come from closures versus which can come from non-closure measures**, and what quantified evidence shows that the non-closure path cannot reach the same target?

#### To Regional State Government
Would you support a **moratorium plus review model** if it includes binding employment protection, monitored targets, and a credible fallback plan if competitiveness goals are missed?

#### To Controlling Family Holding
What level of **verified recurring savings** would be sufficient for you to back a no-closure package, and are you prepared to accept a time-limited governance arrangement to monitor delivery?

#### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a restructuring package that avoids closures **if and only if** it delivers the same recurring margin improvement through capacity, labor-cost, and productivity measures, and what proof would you require upfront?
