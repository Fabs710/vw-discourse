We have now heard the numbers, and they confirm two things at once:

1. **A restructuring is necessary.**
2. **Management still has not proved that plant closures or compulsory redundancies are the only way to achieve it.**

That matters. Because “necessary restructuring” is not the same as “employees must pay with jobs.”

### To management
Your argument is still too blunt: you say the company needs a lower cost base, therefore closures and layoffs are on the table. The expert just showed that this is an **option**, not a proof of inevitability.

What I take from the assessment is:
- non-closure measures can deliver **material savings**,
- voluntary exits, capacity consolidation, working-time tools, and labor-cost contributions can be substantial,
- and the real question is whether management is prepared to do the hard work of designing a **serious no-closure package**.

Right now, you have not earned the right to demand plant closures. You have only shown that they are the **easiest** instrument for management. That is not enough.

### To the controlling family and institutional investors
The expert also makes clear that a no-closure path must be **large and disciplined** to be credible. Fine. We do not dispute that.

But your side keeps framing the issue as if the only economically serious answer is closure. That is overstated. The report explicitly says a non-closure path can work if there is:
- hard volume concentration,
- meaningful voluntary headcount reduction,
- multi-year labor-cost relief,
- product and investment commitments,
- and governance/monitoring.

That is a **real restructuring**. It is not a cosmetic delay.

What we will not accept is that shareholders define “credible” as “whatever shifts the most pain onto the workforce fastest.”

### To the state government
You are right to insist that the regional footprint and co-determination matter. But I want to be careful here: a “moratorium plus review” only works if it is a genuine no-closure framework, not a hidden countdown to closures later.

We can discuss a binding review mechanism only if:
- the plants remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies are triggered,
- the review is about delivery of non-closure measures,
- and any further step requires fresh negotiation, not automatic execution.

### What the expert report means for our position
The expert did **not** say: “close a plant or the company fails.”
The expert said:
- a no-closure route can be viable,
- but it must include real savings,
- and it must not simply preserve the status quo.

That is exactly why the union has already signaled openness to:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a **limited employee contribution**.

So let me be very explicit about what we are prepared to consider:

#### We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary restraint on bonuses and special payments,
- limited wage contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- internal redeployment,
- capacity concentration within the German footprint,
- and binding productivity measures.

#### We are **not** prepared to discuss:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- tearing up the collective wage framework,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or “temporary” concessions without binding guarantees.

### What would make a deal possible
If management wants our cooperation, then bring a package that has all three of these elements:

1. **Job and site security**
   - no German plant closures,
   - no compulsory redundancies,
   - clear protection of the collective bargaining framework.

2. **Real savings**
   - quantified headcount reduction through voluntary means,
   - measurable capacity consolidation,
   - labor-cost contribution,
   - productivity and overhead action.

3. **Reciprocity**
   - binding investment commitments,
   - product allocation for German sites,
   - a time-limited and limited workforce contribution,
   - and joint monitoring with a real review of whether targets are being met.

If those conditions are met, then we can negotiate seriously.

### Where I draw the line
I want to say this plainly because the pressure in this room is growing: **if the demand remains “accept closures or accept compulsory redundancies,” then there is no basis for agreement.** We will not be pushed into that by repeated assertions of competitiveness.

The workforce has already contributed through flexibility, productivity pressure, and uncertainty. It will not now be told that the next contribution must be the destruction of secure jobs while management and owners preserve full freedom of action.

### Bottom line
We are ready for a tough bargain.
We are not ready for a surrender.

If management wants a durable settlement, it must come back with a **quantified no-closure restructuring package** that proves it can close the gap without forced layoffs. Otherwise, expect resistance — including industrial action — because our members did not end the peace obligation to be told that the only solution is to sacrifice their jobs.
