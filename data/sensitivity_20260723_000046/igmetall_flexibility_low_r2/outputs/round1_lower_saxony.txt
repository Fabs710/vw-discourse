As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I need to be very clear: **we are not prepared to support a restructuring that is built on plant closures in Germany or on a one-sided shift of risk onto the workforce.** That is not compatible with our responsibility for regional employment, industrial stability, or the long-term legitimacy of this company in its home region.

I do agree with management on one point: **the company does need a credible competitiveness path.** Pretending that the cost and capacity problem does not exist would be irresponsible. But the answer cannot be “close sites first and ask questions later.” A solution that tears out jobs, weakens the regional industrial base, and sidelines co-determination would create damage that is not easily reversible — economically or politically.

Where I stand:

- **To the works council and union:** your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable and, in principle, I share the core concern. But the company also needs a solution that is more than a simple freeze. If we want to preserve sites, then we need a negotiated restructuring that actually changes the operating model: capacity adjustment, internal redeployment, qualifications, working-time tools, and a fair but limited workforce contribution. I am prepared to back that. I am not prepared to back empty guarantees with no economic substance behind them.

- **To management:** if you want support from this shareholder, you need to stop treating closures as the default answer. You are asking for structural change, but you have not yet shown that the same effect cannot be achieved through a combination of measures that preserve the regional footprint. You need to put a serious alternative on the table: quantified savings, investment commitments, product allocation, and a path to competitiveness that does not rely on gutting German sites.

What would be acceptable from our point of view is a package along these lines:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- substantial use of working-time, redeployment, and qualification measures,
- a transparent and limited employee contribution,
- binding investment and product commitments for German sites,
- and a restructuring path that is reviewed jointly and adjusted if targets are missed.

What is **not** acceptable:
- closures as a starting point,
- forced layoffs,
- removal of the state’s co-determination and veto role through pressure tactics,
- or a “restructure now, promises later” approach.

So my position is firm: **we will not endorse a plan that sacrifices sites and jobs in the state.** If management wants our support, it must present a credible restructuring that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor. If it cannot do that, then it should not expect a straightforward approval from this shareholder.
