We appreciate the clearer positions, and we will be equally clear.

## Our position
As dispersed capital-market shareholders, our priority is **risk-adjusted value creation**. That means:

- a **credible return to competitive margins**,
- **durable structural cost reduction**,
- and **deal certainty** that is not likely to unravel in another round of conflict.

We are **not** attached to closures as an ideology. But we are also **not** prepared to support a settlement that leaves the company’s cost base structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family holding, and the experts on the core diagnosis:

- the German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity and underutilization are real problems,
- and incremental tweaks will not be enough.

We also agree with the workforce and the state on one important point:

- a prolonged strike, open-ended confrontation, or cosmetic settlement would destroy value.

So the real question is not whether restructuring is needed. It is **which restructuring actually restores competitiveness**.

## Where we disagree

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a principle
The works council and union are trying to turn plant closures into a taboo. We do **not** accept that.

If a no-closure package can truly deliver the same recurring savings and margin improvement, fine. But if it cannot, then “no closures” is just a demand to preserve an uncompetitive structure.

That is unacceptable to us.

### 2) But closures also are not a free pass
At the same time, management does **not** get a blank cheque to close plants simply because closures are administratively cleaner.

The expert reports were helpful here: they show that closures usually offer stronger recurring savings, but also that non-closure restructuring can work **if it is real**:
- volume concentration,
- meaningful voluntary exits,
- labor-cost contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- and disciplined product allocation.

So our standard is not “close plants at any cost.”  
Our standard is: **choose the path that credibly restores margins and value.**

### 3) A weak compromise is the worst outcome
The one outcome we would actively oppose is a cosmetic settlement:
- no closures,
- no real headcount reduction,
- no meaningful labor-cost change,
- and then a claim of victory.

That would preserve conflict and still leave the company structurally weak. We would not support it.

## What the expert reports mean to us
The reports make the economics plain.

### What a closure-led path offers
- stronger recurring EBIT improvement,
- better utilization at surviving sites,
- higher probability of hitting target margins by 2030,
- but with higher one-time cost and execution risk.

### What a no-closure path requires
A no-closure package only becomes credible if it includes:
- hard capacity reduction inside the footprint,
- significant voluntary workforce reduction,
- real labor-cost relief,
- productivity improvements,
- and firm investment/product commitments.

In other words: **keeping every plant open is not a restructuring unless the economics are actually fixed.**

## Our view on each stakeholder

### To management
You have not yet shown that closures are absolutely necessary. But you have also not been given a credible no-closure alternative with numbers.

That is the missing piece. Bring us:
- plant-by-plant utilization,
- recurring savings by measure,
- closure versus retention economics,
- and a bridge to 2030 margins.

Without that, we cannot endorse any final package.

### To the works council and union
Your red lines are understandable, but not sufficient. We will not support a deal that protects every site and every guarantee while leaving the company uncompetitive.

If you want a no-closure outcome, it must include:
- real internal consolidation,
- voluntary exits,
- labor-cost contribution,
- and measurable productivity gains.

If you cannot produce those savings, then your position is effectively a blockade.

### To the state government
Your concern for jobs and regional stability is legitimate. But protection without competitiveness is just deferred decline.

We could support a no-closure path if it is financially real. We would not support a political freeze that preserves the footprint and postpones the correction.

### To the controlling family
You are right that cosmetic compromise is dangerous. We agree. But that also means we will not support a compromise that preserves all legacy structures and claims success without real cost reduction.

The family should back whichever option restores recurring value most credibly.

## What we would support
We would support a package that meets all of these conditions:

1. **Durable recurring savings**
2. **Measurable capacity reduction**
3. **Material workforce contribution**
4. **A credible 2030 margin path**
5. **Deal certainty and low execution risk**
6. **No cosmetic compromise**

## What we would not support
We would not support:
- a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- a no-closure deal that is only symbolic,
- or a strike-prone stalemate dressed up as reform.

## Bottom line
We are open to a no-closure solution **only if** it is economically equivalent in substance, not just politically easier.

If management can demonstrate that the non-closure path delivers the required recurring savings and margin recovery, we can support it.

If not, then deeper restructuring, including closures, remains on the table because preserving every site at the expense of competitiveness is not protecting shareholder value — it is eroding it.
