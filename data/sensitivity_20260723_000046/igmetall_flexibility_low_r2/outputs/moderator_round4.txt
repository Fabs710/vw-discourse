As facilitator, I see the impasse as having narrowed to one central fault line:

## Core impasse
The parties agree on the need for **real restructuring** and reject a **cosmetic compromise**, but they do **not** agree on the means:

- **Works Council / Union / State / Family / Investors**:  
  accept restructuring only if it does **not** include plant closures or hidden shutdown mechanisms, and if job/security/co-determination protections remain intact.

- **Management**:  
  will not accept a “no-closure” package unless it is **quantified, durable, and economically equivalent** to closure-led savings.

- **Experts**:  
  indicate that both paths may be possible, but only if the no-closure route removes enough **recurring fixed cost and capacity** to be credible through 2030.

So the impasse is not “restructuring vs no restructuring.”  
It is **whether a no-closure solution can be made sufficiently binding and economically credible without creating future closure rights**.

---

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### 1) Binding no-closure pact with independently verified savings milestones
**Structure**
- All German plants remain open for a defined period.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Job-security framework and co-determination preserved.
- In exchange, the employee side accepts a limited, temporary contribution package.

**Key safeguard**
- No trigger-based closure rights.
- Instead, an **independent expert** verifies whether the agreed savings and capacity transfer milestones are met.

**Why this may bridge**
- Addresses the works council/union/state red line on hidden shutdown mechanisms.
- Gives management/investors a measurable, enforceable savings path.

---

### 2) Hybrid footprint adjustment without formal closures
**Structure**
- No formal plant closures in Germany.
- But product lines, shifts, and volumes are concentrated into fewer sites.
- Voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, and working-time flexibility are used to reduce excess capacity.

**Key safeguard**
- Any site that is reduced must retain an operating role rather than becoming a dormant shell.
- The agreement includes a plant-by-plant capacity map and a joint monitoring committee.

**Why this may bridge**
- May satisfy management’s need for real capacity reduction.
- May be more acceptable to the works council and state than outright closures.
- Gives investors a clearer economic logic than a freeze.

---

### 3) Staged settlement with a hard evidentiary review before any footprint decision
**Structure**
- Immediate agreement on labor-cost, productivity, and voluntary-exit measures.
- No closure decisions now.
- A joint, independent plant-by-plant bridge is produced within a fixed timeframe.

**Key safeguard**
- The review is limited to economics and capacity data.
- It does **not** create automatic closure authority.
- Any later footprint change would require a fresh bargaining round.

**Why this may bridge**
- Reduces immediate conflict.
- Gives management and investors the data they insist on.
- Gives labor and the state assurance that no hidden shutdown path is being embedded.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Management
**Question:**  
What exact recurring annual savings, by site and by measure, would you accept as sufficient to rule out closures for the 2030 target?

### To the Works Council
**Question:**  
What specific quantified capacity and cost reductions are you prepared to accept within a no-closure framework so that the package is not merely a freeze?

### To the Industrial Union
**Question:**  
What temporary and limited workforce contribution package would you consider legitimate if — and only if — every German plant remains open and no compulsory redundancies are introduced?

### To the Regional State Government
**Question:**  
Would you support a binding no-closure settlement if it includes independently verified milestones and a plant-by-plant capacity bridge, even if that requires temporary labor concessions?

### To the Controlling Family
**Question:**  
Will you explicitly support a no-closure restructuring if it is independently shown to deliver durable recurring savings comparable to the closure alternative?

### To Institutional Investors
**Question:**  
What minimum recurring EBIT improvement and utilization threshold would make a no-closure or hybrid settlement acceptable to you as value-protective?

### To the Independent Expert
**Question:**  
Can you specify the point at which a no-closure plan stops being economically credible and becomes only a delay mechanism, using plant-by-plant utilization and fixed-cost data?
