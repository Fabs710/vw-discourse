### To management
I agree with your diagnosis that the company needs a durable cost reset and that a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive. I do **not** agree that you have yet justified plant closures as the necessary starting point.

The expert views cut both ways:
- yes, closures generally deliver stronger recurring savings,
- but a no-closure route can still be credible if it is large, quantified, and disciplined.

What is still missing from management is the **plant-by-plant case**:
- current utilization,
- transferable volume,
- recurring savings by measure,
- closure cost versus retention cost,
- and the margin bridge to 2030.

Until that is on the table, I will not treat closures as proven or inevitable. I will not support a move that permanently impairs value if a serious non-closure package can do the job.

### To the works council and union
I respect the force of your red lines, but I do **not** accept your attempt to turn “no closures” into a complete solution by itself.

A protected footprint is only defensible if it is paired with real internal correction:
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- labor-cost contributions,
- capacity concentration,
- and measurable productivity gains.

If you want my support for a no-closure outcome, it must be a **real restructuring**, not a freeze. I am open to a limited and time-bound workforce contribution, but only if it produces tangible recurring savings and not just deferred pain.

I will say this plainly: if the package keeps every plant open, preserves all protections unchanged, and does not materially lower the cost base, then it is not acceptable.

### To the regional state government
I agree with you on the importance of regional stability and the company’s legitimacy in its home market. I also agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.

But I do **not** accept a position that makes plant closures politically impossible regardless of economics. If the company can be restored more effectively without closures, that is the better route. If not, sentiment alone cannot overrule the need for a durable reset.

Your support is valuable if it helps produce a serious no-closure package with binding volume concentration, productivity improvements, and real savings. It is not valuable if it simply hardens red lines without solving the economics.

### To institutional investors
Your standard is the right one: durable savings, credible 2030 margins, and no cosmetic compromise.

Where I differ from a closure-first mindset is simple: closures are a tool, not a goal. If a no-closure package can deliver the required recurring improvement, then it better protects long-term value by avoiding disruption and preserving industrial capability.

But I will not support a weak compromise that merely delays the issue. If non-closure measures cannot close the gap, then I will not defend an uneconomic structure for appearances’ sake.

### Where I stand
My support is available only for a package that does all of the following:
1. Delivers **verified recurring savings**
2. Restores **competitiveness and margins**
3. Avoids **value-destructive instability**
4. Preserves **governance control**
5. Uses closures only if they are truly unavoidable

So I am not endorsing closures as the default answer.  
I am also not accepting a no-closure solution that fails economically.

Bring me the plant-by-plant bridge and the quantified no-closure alternative. Until then, I remain unconvinced that closures are justified.
