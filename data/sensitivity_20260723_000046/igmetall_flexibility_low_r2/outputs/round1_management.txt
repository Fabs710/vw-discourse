Let me be very clear: I hear the rejection of plant closures and compulsory redundancies, but I do **not** accept the premise that we can restore this company’s competitiveness without structural change.

We are not dealing with a normal adjustment. We are facing:
- a shrinking European market,
- severe pressure from lower-cost competitors,
- German factory costs that are materially above target,
- and a brand that is missing its return-on-sales target.

If we simply preserve every site, every legacy structure, and every protection through 2030 without a hard cost reset, then we are not protecting the company — we are postponing a deeper crisis.

Where I agree:
- A durable solution must be negotiated.
- Internal redeployment, qualification, working-time tools, and capacity adjustment are all part of the toolbox.
- We do need a credible future investment path for Germany.
- A settlement that is purely cosmetic, or that creates short-term savings but leaves the cost base uncompetitive, is not acceptable.

Where I disagree:
- A blanket refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is not compatible with the scale of the problem.
- A binding job guarantee through 2030, without corresponding structural relief on cost and capacity, is economically unrealistic.
- “Fair contribution” cannot mean that the workforce contributes nothing while management is expected to solve the crisis through incrementalism.

So my position is this:

We need a package that delivers **durable, structural savings**. That means real capacity reduction, real labour-cost contribution, and a lasting lower cost base. I am open on the **how** — but not on the **whether**.

If employee representatives want to avoid closures, then they need to put something equally credible on the table:
- a materially lower labour-cost structure,
- genuine flexibility on working time and deployment,
- measurable capacity reduction,
- and a binding contribution that closes the competitiveness gap.

If that package is strong enough, then yes, we can discuss alternatives to closure. But if the answer is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no guarantee changes,” then there is no restructuring — only denial.

And let me be equally direct: prolonged strikes will not improve the case for maintaining the status quo. They will damage output, revenue, and confidence. I would prefer a negotiated solution, but it has to solve the cost problem in a durable way.

So my question back to you is simple: **what concrete, structural savings are you offering that actually gets this brand to a competitive cost base by 2030?**
