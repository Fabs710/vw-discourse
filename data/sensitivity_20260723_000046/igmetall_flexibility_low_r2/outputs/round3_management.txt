### To the Works Council

- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of job security,
- no change to co-determination.

Fine. But then you must also answer a harder question: **how does this company become competitive again by 2030?**

- real headcount reduction,
- meaningful labor-cost relief,
- hard capacity concentration,
- and better utilization.

- I am open to voluntary exits.
- I am open to redeployment.
- I am open to working-time flexibility.
- I am open to limited labor-cost contributions.

But I am **not** open to a package that preserves all sites, preserves most legacy protections, and still expects management to somehow restore margins by wishful thinking.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring a quantified alternative that actually closes the cost gap. Otherwise, your position is simply asking the company to accept permanent uncompetitiveness.

   - less overlap,

3. **Real labor-cost contribution**
   - wage restraint,
   - reduced premiums or allowances,
   - flexibility,
   - and productivity commitments.

4. **Material headcount reduction**
   - preferably voluntary,
   - but real enough to matter.

5. **A timetable tied to 2030**
   - because delay is not a strategy.

What I will not accept:
- a promise that every German plant remains open with no hard cost correction,
- a deal that preserves the current cost structure,
