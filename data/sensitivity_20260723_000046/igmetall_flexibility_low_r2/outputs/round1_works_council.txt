As the Works Council, our position is clear and non-negotiable on the essentials:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany.  
We will not accept compulsory redundancies.  
We will not accept the breaking of hard-won job-security guarantees as a precondition for restructuring.

The workforce has already carried a great deal in this transformation: investment, flexibility, productivity pressure, and uncertainty. Now management wants to shift the burden yet again onto employees through closures, cutbacks, and the destruction of secure jobs. That is not a responsible restructuring strategy; it is a unilateral attack on the foundation of co-determination and the social contract in this company.

What we are prepared to discuss is a different path:
- binding site guarantees for all German plants,
- a binding job guarantee through 2030,
- no operational layoffs,
- solutions based on capacity adjustment, working-time tools, internal redeployment, qualification, and negotiated contributions,
- and a real role for employee representatives in shaping any restructuring plan.

If management claims that competitiveness requires action, then we say: prove it without sacrificing sites and jobs. There are alternatives to closures and forced layoffs. If there is to be a contribution from the workforce, it must be fair, limited, and tied to binding commitments on investment, future products, and long-term employment security.

We are here to negotiate a viable future for the company. But that future cannot be built on closing plants and throwing people out of work. If that remains the proposal, then you should expect fierce resistance from us, from the workforce, and from the union.
