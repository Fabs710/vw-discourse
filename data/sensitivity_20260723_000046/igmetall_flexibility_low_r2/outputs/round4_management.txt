## On the expert assessments
Let’s be precise about what they do and do not prove.

### They do prove:
- no-closure restructuring can be meaningful,
- but only if it is serious,
- and only if it produces real recurring savings, not just workforce churn.

### They do not prove:
- that every plant can stay open with no downside,
- that labor protections can remain unchanged while cost falls materially,
- or that closures are unnecessary regardless of the numbers.

That is the critical distinction.

So when you say, “a no-closure path is possible,” I agree.  
When you imply, “therefore closures are off the table,” I disagree.

Possibility is not sufficiency.

## What I am prepared to consider
I am prepared to consider a no-closure package **only if** it includes all of the following:

1. **Verified recurring savings**
   - not one-offs,
   - not deferred costs,
   - not accounting relief.

2. **Meaningful capacity reduction**
   - fewer overlaps,
   - fewer parallel lines,
   - better utilization.

3. **Material labor-cost contribution**
   - wage restraint,
   - reduced premiums,
   - working-time flexibility,
   - and productivity commitments.

4. **Real headcount reduction**
   - preferably voluntary,
   - but large enough to matter.

5. **A credible timetable to 2030**
   - because delay is not a strategy.

What I will **not** accept:
- a freeze dressed up as restructuring,
- a no-closure package that preserves the current cost structure,
- or a deal that simply postpones the same conflict.

The truth is in the economics, not in the slogan.
