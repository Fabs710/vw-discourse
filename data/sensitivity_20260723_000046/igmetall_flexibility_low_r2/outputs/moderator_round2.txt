### Moderator Summary — Round 2

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree that the company needs a **real structural restructuring**, not a symbolic compromise.
- There is broad agreement that **industrial peace, regional stability, and long-term competitiveness** all matter.
- Several parties accept that **non-closure measures can contribute materially** to savings, including:
  - voluntary exits,
  - internal redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - productivity improvements,
  - labor-cost contributions,
  - and capacity concentration.
- There is also shared recognition that any solution must be **quantified, durable, and credible through 2030**.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- The main unresolved issue is whether **plant closures and/or compulsory redundancies** are necessary.
- The **works council and union** reject closures and compulsory redundancies as a matter of principle.
- **Management, investors, and partly the family holding** argue that closures remain on the table unless a no-closure plan can close the cost gap with equivalent recurring savings.
- The **state government** supports job and site protection, but insists any alternative must still produce real savings and competitiveness.
- There is no agreement yet on whether a **no-closure path** is sufficient or whether it would only delay harder measures.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Savings vs. job/site security**: how much recurring cost reduction can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs.
- **One-time costs vs. long-term gains**: closure paths appear to offer stronger recurring savings, but at higher transition and conflict costs.
- **Workforce contributions vs. binding guarantees**: the extent of labor concessions depends on whether there are firm site, job, and investment commitments.
- **Capacity reduction vs. footprint preservation**: the parties have not yet reconciled “keep all plants open” with the need to remove redundant capacity.
- **Timeline credibility**: it is still unclear which path can realistically deliver the required margin improvement by 2030.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Shift from principle statements to **comparative numbers**.
- Request a **plant-by-plant financial bridge** showing:
  - closure scenario,
  - no-closure consolidation scenario,
  - and hybrid compromise scenario.
- Each scenario should specify:
  - recurring annual EBIT savings,
  - one-time cash costs,
  - workforce impact,
  - utilization effects,
  - and expected 2030 margin outcome.
- The next round should focus on identifying **which mix of measures is sufficient**, and what binding conditions would make each option acceptable.
