We agree with the **diagnosis** made by management, the controlling family, and the investor side: the company’s current German cost base and capacity are not competitive enough, and a cosmetic compromise would destroy value.

We also agree with the works council, union, and state shareholder on one important point: **prolonged conflict is value-destructive**. If the process drifts into a long strike and political stalemate, shareholders lose as well.

Where we **do not** agree is on the attempt to turn **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees through 2030”** into the default framework.

That is not a solution unless it comes with **hard, quantified economics** that actually restore margins. Otherwise it is just a way of preserving an uncompetitive structure and asking capital to fund the gap.

### To the Works Council and Union
We respect the social and industrial logic of wanting to protect sites and jobs. But from an investor perspective, your position is only acceptable if you can prove that the alternative package is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **legally enforceable**
- and **sufficient** to close the gap in cost and capacity

So far, what is on the table sounds directionally useful:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- productivity measures,
- management restraint.

But that is still not enough to let us conclude that closures can be removed from the table entirely. If those tools do not deliver the required savings at the required speed, then you are asking shareholders to accept structurally impaired returns.

### To management
We support your insistence on a real restructuring, not a symbolic one. That said, we need discipline from management too.

Do **not** turn “structural action” into a blunt confrontation that maximizes execution risk. The market will punish:
- drawn-out strikes,
- governance deadlock,
- and a plan that is technically hard but politically unexecutable.

Your job is not just to demand savings; it is to deliver them in a way that can actually be implemented. We want:
- quantified targets,
- clear milestones,
- limited conflict duration,
- and a credible path back to return-on-sales improvement.

### To the Regional State Government
Your concern for regional employment is legitimate. But if the state uses its blocking power to rule out any materially effective adjustment, it risks preserving employment only in the short term while weakening the company’s ability to support jobs at all.

A regional anchor only remains an anchor if it is competitive. We will not support a settlement that sacrifices margin recovery simply to avoid difficult decisions.

### To the Controlling Family
We agree that value destruction through confrontation must be avoided. We also agree that the business cannot be frozen in place. Where we differ is on how much structural action must remain available.

Our position is simple:
- If the non-closure package is **truly equivalent**, we support it.
- If it is not, then management and owners must keep structural options open.

We are not asking for closures as a first choice. We are saying they cannot be ruled out **unless** the alternative is proven to work.

### Our line
We will back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness** and disciplined margins.

That means:
- durable cost reduction,
- verifiable capacity adjustment,
- no cosmetic settlement,
- no open-ended conflict,
- and no package that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

### What could move us
A credible proposal could shift us if it includes:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the exact measures delivering those savings,
3. proof that the gap is fully closed without closures,
4. enforceable implementation through a defined period,
5. and a limited conflict horizon.

Until then, we cannot support a blanket no-closure commitment. It is simply not enough to say “internal tools” and assume the economics are solved.

### Bottom line
We support a serious restructuring.
We do **not** support a cosmetic settlement.
And we will not accept an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired or the cost base uncompetitive.
