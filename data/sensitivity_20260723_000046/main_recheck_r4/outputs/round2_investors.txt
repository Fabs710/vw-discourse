We are aligned with management, the controlling family, and partly with the state on the central point: **the company needs durable structural savings, not a cosmetic settlement**. If the final package leaves the German cost base uncompetitive, we will regard it as value-destructive and will oppose it through our capital-market voice.

### Where we agree
- The status quo is not acceptable.
- Overcapacity and elevated German cost levels are real.
- The solution must be **durable, quantified, and enforceable**.
- Prolonged conflict would destroy value.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a package that simply preserves every plant, preserves every guarantee, and calls that a restructuring. That would lock in inefficiency and likely leave margins structurally impaired.

We also do **not** accept the argument that social and regional concerns can override economics. They matter, but they do not change the need for a competitive operating model.

### To the Works Council and Union
Your insistence on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and binding guarantees through 2030,

is understandable, but as stated it is too rigid to be credible for investors unless you can show an alternative that delivers the same savings with the same durability.

If you want investor support for avoiding closures, then we need to see:
- exact quantified savings,
- a real capacity reduction,
- meaningful labour-cost adjustment,
- and legal certainty that the measures will hold.

Temporary contributions or voluntary programs alone will not be enough if they merely postpone the problem.

### To Management
We support decisive action, but not value-destroying confrontation for its own sake. If closures or deeper structural measures are truly necessary, they must be part of a disciplined plan with:
- clear numbers,
- a credible execution path,
- and a limited conflict horizon.

Do not mistake investor support for “any cuts at any cost.” We will not back a chaotic restructuring that damages brand, operational continuity, or medium-term cash generation.

### To the State Shareholder
Your concern about regional employment is legitimate, but we cannot treat the existing footprint as sacrosanct if it prevents margin recovery. The company’s long-term ability to invest, compete, and employ people depends on restoring economic viability first.

### Bottom line
Our support depends on one test: **does the final package credibly restore competitive margins?** If yes, we can support a negotiated outcome. If no, we will not endorse a settlement that protects appearances while weakening enterprise value.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, put forward a **fully quantified alternative** that closes the gap durably. Otherwise, management should retain the ability to pursue structural restructuring.
