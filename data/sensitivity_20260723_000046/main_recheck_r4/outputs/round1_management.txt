I hear the concerns, but I need to be equally clear: **the status quo is not an option**.

We are facing a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip. German factory costs are materially above target, the European market is shrinking, and we are competing against producers with a lower cost base. If we do not reduce cost and capacity in a durable way now, we will simply erode margin further, underinvest in future products, and weaken the entire brand.

So let me respond directly:

- **No plant closures, no compulsory redundancies**: I understand why you are drawing that line, but I cannot accept it as an absolute precondition. If we cannot achieve structural savings otherwise, then closure options remain on the table. We cannot rule out the tools that are needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Job and site guarantees through 2030**: I am not prepared to sign up to blanket guarantees that lock in today’s overcapacity for years. That would be a cosmetic solution, not a restructuring.
- **Shared sacrifice**: On this point, I agree in principle. A sustainable agreement must involve a genuine employee contribution. But it cannot be limited to temporary, symbolic measures while the company continues carrying an uncompetitive cost base.
- **Investment and future products**: Also agreed — but investment only makes sense if we first create the financial room to fund it. Saving the current structure at all costs would crowd out the very future you say you want to protect.

What I am willing to discuss is a **durable package that achieves real cost and capacity reductions**. That could include a mix of:
- capacity consolidation,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- voluntary exits,
- labour-cost adjustments,
- and, where unavoidable, plant-level restructuring.

But I want to be explicit: **a deal that leaves the German cost base fundamentally unchanged is not acceptable**. Nor is one that merely postpones the problem while preserving every site and every guarantee. We need a solution that restores margin, reduces overcapacity, and gives us a credible path to 2030 competitiveness.

If you want to avoid harsher measures, then bring forward an alternative that delivers the same structural effect. Otherwise, we will have to pursue the full restructuring agenda.
