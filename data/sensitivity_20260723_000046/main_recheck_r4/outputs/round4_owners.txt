I will be direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one essential point: **a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive**. The company needs a **durable** solution, not a delay mechanism. I also agree that prolonged industrial conflict would damage the enterprise, the workforce, and the owners alike.

I further agree that:
- the company needs **quantified savings**,
- it needs room for **future-product investment**,
- and it needs a settlement that can actually be **implemented and sustained**.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee side’s absolute position that:
- **no closures ever**,
- **no compulsory redundancies ever**,
- **binding guarantees through 2030 regardless of economics**.

That is not a restructuring framework; it is a veto on structural adjustment. If the current footprint and cost base are materially uncompetitive, then ownership cannot responsibly freeze that structure in place.

Likewise, I do **not** accept the state shareholder’s attempt to make “no closures” the governing premise without showing a fully credible alternative that closes the savings gap on comparable terms. Political legitimacy matters, but so does economic viability. Without the latter, there will be no durable employment base to defend.

### To the Works Council and Union
You are right to insist that workers should not be the sole shock absorber. I support **shared sacrifice**. I also support:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- productivity measures,
- and management restraint.

But your position still does not answer the central question: **what exact, durable savings are achieved if closures and compulsory redundancies are ruled out entirely?**

If you can produce a package that is:
1. quantified,
2. enforceable,
3. durable,
4. and sufficient to restore margins,

then I will support it. If you cannot, then you are asking the owners to sign up to structural underperformance.

### To management
You are correct that the economics cannot be ignored. But I want to be equally clear: **do not use “necessary restructuring” as a license for a value-destructive social war**. A plan that technically delivers savings but triggers prolonged strikes, political backlash, and governance deadlock is not good stewardship.

So I support discipline, but only if it is disciplined **and** governable. Do not confuse toughness with value creation.

### To the Regional State Government
Your concern for regional employment is legitimate. I do not dismiss it. But your role is not to preserve every site regardless of economics. Your role is to help secure a viable company with a future in the region.

If you want to rule out closures, then you must help secure an alternative that is economically equivalent, durable, and enforceable. Otherwise the burden of adjustment is being shifted entirely onto management and ownership while the core problem remains unresolved.

### To institutional investors
I agree with you that endless conflict destroys value. I also agree that a cosmetic compromise is unacceptable. But your support for structural action must not become a blank cheque for unnecessary escalation. If management pushes a maximalist closure strategy without exhausting real alternatives, that risks destroying enterprise value through execution failure.

### My position
I will support a **serious restructuring package** that:
- restores competitiveness,
- improves margins durably,
- preserves investment capacity,
- and avoids value-destructive instability.

I will **not** support:
- an open-ended guarantee that locks in inefficiency,
- a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value,
- or a package that treats closures as impossible regardless of the numbers.

### What would move me
A credible alternative could move me if it includes:
- a quantified savings plan at the required level,
- real internal capacity reductions,
- voluntary and negotiated measures first,
- management restraint,
- owner-side discipline,
- and legally durable implementation.

But if that cannot close the gap, then structural options remain available. I will not pretend otherwise.

### Bottom line
I am not looking for conflict, but I will not hide from the economics.

If the employee side and state shareholder want closures off the table, they must produce a **numbers-based alternative** that is genuinely equivalent in durability and effect. Until then, structural action remains on the table, because protecting long-term enterprise value requires it.
