I’ll respond plainly.

### To the Works Council
You keep repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no review later.” I understand the political logic. I do **not** accept it as an answer to the business problem.

Your position still does not close the gap between:
- our **current cost base and overcapacity**, and
- the **market reality** we face in Europe and globally.

If you want closures off the table, then you must present a package that is **quantified, durable, and sufficient** to restore competitiveness. So far, I have heard principles, not an equivalent economic solution.

And let me be clear: a blanket 2030 guarantee that locks in the current footprint is not compatible with restoring the brand to target return on sales. I will not sign up to a structure that preserves every site regardless of economics.

### To the Industrial Union
I appreciate that you acknowledge the competitiveness problem and that you are willing to discuss flexibility, voluntary exits, retraining, and temporary contributions. That is more constructive than simply saying “no.”

But you are still drawing the same hard line on closures and forced redundancies while insisting on protection first and economics second. That sequencing is exactly what I cannot accept.

If you want to avoid structural action, then show me the numbers:
- how much savings you deliver,
- how much capacity you take out,
- how much labour cost comes out permanently,
- and how that is legally and operationally enforceable.

Until then, “shared sacrifice” remains a slogan.

### To the Regional State Government
You are right that social legitimacy matters. I agree with that. But legitimacy does not pay for uncompetitive factories.

Your position is politically understandable, but economically incomplete. If you use your blocking role simply to prevent any meaningful adjustment, you are not protecting the company — you are delaying the crisis and making the eventual correction more painful.

If you want to rule out closures, then you need to back a package that really replaces those savings. Otherwise you are asking management to carry the burden of adjustment while denying us the tools to do it.

### To the Controlling Family
You are right on one crucial point: a cosmetic compromise would be value-destructive. On that we agree.

Where I differ is that you seem to think the only credible route is to keep closures and structural reductions available in the background. I’m not saying closures are the first choice. I am saying they must remain available if the alternatives do not close the gap.

That is not blackmail. It is a fiduciary response to a structural problem.

### To Institutional Investors
You have the right instinct on one thing: endless conflict destroys value. I do not want that either.

But if investors insist on margin recovery without accepting real structural change, then they are simply demanding magic. We need durable savings, not just better communication.

### Where I stand
I will repeat the core point because it still has not been answered:

- We need a **material reduction in overcapacity**.
- We need a **material reduction in labour cost**.
- We need a **durable path to restore margins**.
- We need **investment capacity for future products and technology**.

If the employee side can deliver that **without** closures, I will consider it. If it cannot, then closures and deeper structural measures remain on the table.

### What I am prepared to support
I remain open to a serious package built around:
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and investment commitments.

But I will only support that if it produces **real, verified, durable savings** at the scale required. A temporary comfort package that leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not acceptable.

### What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a binding no-closure rule as an absolute,
- forced 2030 guarantees that freeze the current footprint,
- or a restructuring that cannot credibly restore competitiveness.

That is the limit of my flexibility.

### Bottom line
You all say you want a durable solution. Fine. Then bring one that is economically sufficient.

If you cannot, then do not ask management to pretend the company can be made competitive by goodwill alone. It cannot.
