### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the current situation is **not sustainable** and that some form of **durable restructuring** is needed.
- There is broad agreement that a **cosmetic or temporary fix** would be insufficient.
- Most parties acknowledge the need for:
  - **quantified savings**,
  - **investment capacity for future products/technology**,
  - **burden-sharing** across stakeholders,
  - and avoidance of prolonged conflict if possible.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  Employee representatives and the state shareholder reject closures; management, family, and investors keep them as an option if alternatives fail.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  Labour side rejects them categorically; management and capital side do not rule them out if needed.
- **Guarantees through 2030**:  
  Labour and state side want binding job/site security; management and capital side see open-ended guarantees as incompatible with restructuring flexibility.
- **Sequencing**:  
  Labour side insists on **site and job security first**; management and capital side insist on **competitiveness and cost base first**.
- **Extent of burden-sharing**:  
  All endorse shared sacrifice in principle, but there is no agreement yet on what each side must concretely contribute.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required** and how much can be delivered without closures or forced layoffs.
- **What alternative measures** could realistically replace closure-driven savings:
  - capacity reductions,
  - working-time changes,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment/retraining,
  - management restraint,
  - owner-side contributions.
- **What level of job/site protection** is compatible with a durable restructuring.
- **What legal and operational certainty** any voluntary or temporary measures would have.
- **How to balance regional/social legitimacy** against margin recovery and investor expectations.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **single quantified options paper**:
  1. exact savings target,
  2. exact non-closure measures,
  3. exact employee, management, owner, and state contributions,
  4. exact job/site guarantees or limits,
  5. implementation timeline and durability.
- Ask each side to state:
  - what it can **concede**,
  - what it **cannot accept**,
  - and what alternative package it would support if closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table.
