As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. That is the line.

The workforce has already carried a great deal of adjustment, and we are not prepared to let management solve strategic mistakes by sacrificing jobs, sites, and the social commitments that have existed for decades. A restructuring is one thing; dismantling the German industrial base is another.

Our priorities are:

1. **Every German plant stays open.**
2. **No forced layoffs.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **A real co-determination role in shaping any restructuring, not a fait accompli.**
5. **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners, not one-sided pressure on employees.**

If savings are needed, then they must come through alternatives that protect jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, internal flexibility, working-time instruments, voluntary measures, retraining, product and investment commitments, and negotiated contributions that are limited, temporary, and proportionate. But we will not trade away the workforce’s future for short-term cost cutting.

Management has to understand this: if it comes with closures, forced layoffs, or the breaking of historic job-security guarantees, it will face decisive resistance. We are prepared to fight this in the boardroom and, if necessary, in the plants. We want a viable future for the company, but not at the expense of tens of thousands of employees and their families.
