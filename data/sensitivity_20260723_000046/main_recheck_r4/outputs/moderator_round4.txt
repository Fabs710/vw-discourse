## Core impasse

The central deadlock is **not** over whether restructuring is needed; all parties accept that some adjustment is necessary.

The impasse is over **whether plant closures and compulsory redundancies may remain as a fallback option** if internal measures do not fully close the savings gap.

- **Employee side / State shareholder:** wants a **binding no-closure, no-forced-layoff framework**.
- **Management / Ownership / Institutional investors:** want **structural options to remain available** unless an alternative is proven to be economically sufficient.
- **Sub-impasse:** the parties are also disputing the **sequencing**:
  - workers want commitments first, then contributions;
  - management/investors want quantified savings first, then remove closure risk.

So the real blocker is: **security versus optionality**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for possible compromise paths

### 1) Binding “no closure during restructuring period” with objective trigger review
A possible bridge is a **time-limited binding no-closure commitment** for a defined restructuring period, combined with:
- quantified internal measures,
- monthly or quarterly monitoring,
- and a **pre-agreed independent trigger test**.

If the agreed savings milestones are met, the no-closure commitment continues automatically.
If milestones are missed, the parties do **not** reopen unilateral closure rights immediately; instead they enter a **second-stage bargaining process** with mandatory mediation and a defined list of additional non-closure measures to exhaust first.

This may bridge the gap between:
- employee/state demands for real security, and
- management/owners’ demand for economic discipline.

---

### 2) “No forced redundancies” plus voluntary structural adjustment package
A second possible compromise is to separate the two controversial tools:
- **No compulsory redundancies** as a binding rule,
- but **voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time reduction, and capacity adjustment** remain available.

To make this credible, it would need:
- enhanced severance / bridge-to-retirement options,
- retraining guarantees,
- internal transfer rights,
- and management/owner contributions that visibly reduce the burden on labor.

This could reduce resistance because it removes the most contested labor instrument while still allowing some footprint adjustment.

---

### 3) Site-security package with conditional financial relief and investment commitments
A third option is a **site-security and investment package**:
- German plants remain open for the agreed period,
- labor accepts temporary contributions,
- management and owners commit specific savings and capital injections,
- and the state shareholder backs the package politically.

In exchange, the company receives:
- wage moderation,
- flexibility tools,
- targeted productivity measures,
- and possibly state-facilitated support for transformation investment.

This is most useful if the parties want to trade **certainty of sites** for **certainty of economic adjustments**.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**If a binding no-closure commitment is offered for a defined restructuring period, what exact internal measures and quantified savings would you accept as sufficient to make that commitment credible?**

### Industrial Union
**Would you support a model that rules out compulsory redundancies and closures during the restructuring period, provided that the economic gap is closed through voluntary exits, working-time reductions, redeployment, and verified owner/management contributions?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum savings figure, and which specific non-closure measures would you accept as an equivalent alternative before insisting that closures remain available?**

### Regional State Government
**Would you support a time-limited binding no-closure framework if it included measurable savings milestones, independent monitoring, and enforceable investment commitments, even if it limited management’s fallback options during that period?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to trade the immediate option of closures for a legally binding restructuring package if it delivers comparable savings, preserves sites for a defined period, and includes enforceable owner-side sacrifice?**

### Institutional Investors
**What exact evidence would be sufficient for you to conclude that a non-closure package is economically equivalent, so that you would no longer insist on keeping closures as a fallback option?**
