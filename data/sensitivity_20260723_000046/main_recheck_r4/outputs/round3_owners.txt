I will be direct.

I agree with the employee side and the state shareholder on one important point: **a deal that triggers prolonged industrial conflict, destroys trust, and undermines the company’s legitimacy would be value-destructive**. That would hurt the enterprise, the workforce, and the family holding alike.

But I do **not** accept the absolute red lines that have been repeated:

- **no plant closures ever**
- **no compulsory redundancies ever**
- **binding job and site guarantees through 2030 regardless of economics**

Those are not compatible with the ownership duty to restore competitiveness if the current footprint and cost base remain structurally uncompetitive. If the company needs durable savings and the alternative measures are not enough, then structural action must remain available. I will not sign up to a package that freezes an uncompetitive structure in place.

### To the Works Council and Union
You are right that workers should not pay for management mistakes alone. Shared sacrifice is the only acceptable basis for a settlement.

But your current position still relies too heavily on the assumption that **capacity, jobs, and sites can all be protected first, and the economics somehow solved later**. That sequencing is backwards. If the savings gap is real, then the package must first prove it can close that gap in a durable way.

I am open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- productivity measures,
- and investment commitments.

What I am **not** open to is an arrangement that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive while giving guarantees that prevent necessary change until 2030.

If you want closures off the table, then bring a **fully quantified alternative** that closes the gap with the same durability. If you cannot, then your position is not a solution; it is a veto.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect the concern for regional employment and the industrial base. That concern is legitimate.

But I do not accept the idea that the state’s role is to prevent any difficult restructuring because it is politically painful. The state should help secure a viable company, not preserve every site independent of economics.

You say closures are not acceptable. I understand the political stance. But if you want to rule them out, you must also help produce an alternative that is economically equivalent and legally durable. Otherwise you are asking ownership and management to carry the entire responsibility for restoring competitiveness while everyone else keeps a veto and no adjustment.

### To Management
Management is right that the company needs a durable solution and that a cosmetic compromise would be disastrous. On that point, we are aligned.

Where I am not fully aligned is tone and execution. You need to be disciplined: do not turn “necessary restructuring” into a needless social war. Value destruction through conflict is not acceptable either.

But do not misread that caution as support for a soft outcome. The final package must deliver:
1. a material reduction in overcapacity,
2. a material reduction in labor cost,
3. credible execution,
4. and investment capacity for the future.

If those conditions are not met, then I will not support a settlement just because it is politically comfortable.

### What would move me
I remain open to a negotiated alternative **if** it contains:
- quantified savings at the level management needs,
- real capacity reduction,
- voluntary and negotiated measures first,
- meaningful employee contribution,
- management restraint,
- and credible owner-side discipline.

But it must be **durable** and **enforceable**. Temporary relief, vague promises, or symbolic burden-sharing will not satisfy me.

### My position
I will support a serious restructuring package that restores margins and competitiveness.

I will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- an open-ended guarantee that locks in inefficiency,
- or a package that permanently impairs the enterprise’s value.

If the employee side and the state shareholder want closures ruled out, they need to show a real alternative that closes the gap. Until then, structural action remains on the table.
