I will be direct.

I agree with management on one point: **the company needs a durable restructuring and the status quo is not sustainable**. I also agree with the family shareholder and investors that a cosmetic fix would be pointless. But I do **not** accept the conclusion that this requires plant closures in Germany or compulsory redundancies.

### To management
You keep presenting closures as the backstop if your preferred package is not accepted. From my side, that is not a neutral bargaining position; it is a threat to regional employment and to the company’s social legitimacy.

I am not prepared to endorse a restructuring logic that starts from shutting down German sites. That is especially unacceptable in a company that has long benefited from a stable industrial base, public support, and co-determination. If the cost base is too high, then management must first exhaust alternatives that preserve sites and jobs:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits only,
- retraining and redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- and real management restraint.

If you cannot show how the savings gap is closed without breaking the industrial footprint, then your plan is incomplete, not inevitable.

### To the Works Council and Union
I share your core concern: **no gutting of regional employment, no compulsory redundancies, and no sidelining of co-determination**. Those are not negotiable for me in any package that claims public legitimacy.

That said, I also need to be honest: a simple “every plant stays open” slogan is not yet a workable restructuring plan. If the company truly has a structural capacity problem, then we need concrete alternatives that produce measurable savings. That means the workforce side must be prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time adjustments,
- limited, negotiated employee contributions,
- voluntary programs,
- internal transfers,
- and operational changes that actually reduce cost.

But I repeat: **these can only be part of a package that protects sites and avoids compulsory redundancies**. Workers should not be asked to subsidize closures or pay for their own displacement.

### To the controlling family and investors
You are right to insist on credibility and durability. But you are too ready to reduce credibility to plant closures and labor-cost cuts. That is too narrow, and in the long run it is risky.

A deal that destroys trust, triggers industrial conflict, and weakens the company’s role in the region is not value-preserving, even if it looks clean in a spreadsheet. Social partnership is not a sentimental add-on here; it is part of the company’s operating model.

So if you want a serious settlement, then support a package that delivers:
- real savings,
- stable execution,
- and a credible path back to competitiveness,
**without** sacrificing the company’s regional anchor role.

### Where I stand
My minimum position is clear:

1. **No plant closures in Germany as part of the restructuring package.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Real job and site protection, with co-determination respected.**
4. **A genuinely quantified savings plan built on alternatives to closures.**
5. **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners as well as employees.**

I am not blocking restructuring. I am blocking a restructuring that turns a competitiveness problem into a regional deindustrialization problem.

### What would move me
I could support a negotiated package if it includes:
- a quantified alternative savings path,
- binding investment and product commitments,
- voluntary-only staffing adjustment,
- temporary and proportionate employee contributions,
- and management/owner contributions that are real, not symbolic.

What will **not** move me is the argument that closures are “unavoidable” simply because they are the easiest tool. The state shareholder has a duty to protect the regional economic base, and I intend to use that responsibility.

If management and the owners want my support, they need to bring a package that preserves the company as a regional anchor **and** restores competitiveness. If they cannot do that, then I will not consent to a deal that shifts the burden onto workers and the region.
