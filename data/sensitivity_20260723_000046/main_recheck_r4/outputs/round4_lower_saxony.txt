I will respond directly.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: this company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic pause. And yes, the cost base and capacity need adjustment.

Where I **do not** agree is your repeated insistence that **closures and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table as a normal backstop**. From the state shareholder’s perspective, that is not a neutral technicality. It is a decision with lasting damage to:
- regional employment,
- supplier networks,
- industrial stability,
- and the company’s legitimacy as a regional anchor.

You have still not shown that you have exhausted the serious alternatives before reaching for the most destructive tool. If you want us to even consider deeper change, then put the full numbers on the table:
1. the exact savings gap,
2. what can be achieved through internal capacity reduction,
3. what management restraint contributes,
4. what owner-side sacrifice is real,
5. what investment commitments are guaranteed.

Until that is done, “closures if needed” is not an argument I can support. It is a threat.

### To the Works Council and the Union
I agree with your core red lines:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no one-sided burden on labor,
- and no weakening of co-determination.

That is not just a political preference for me; it is central to preserving the company as a regional economic anchor.

Where I also agree with you is that a settlement must be **real**, not symbolic. A pure declaratory “no closures” statement without quantified internal measures would not be enough. The company does need a serious adjustment.

What I can support is a restructuring built around:
- capacity reduction without site closure,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary exits only,
- retraining and redeployment,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- and binding investment commitments.

But I want to be clear: that is only acceptable if it is framed as a **binding no-closure path**, not a probation period before the company reopens closure threats later. A “review later” clause can easily become a backdoor. I am not prepared to treat it as secure enough for the workforce or the region.

### To the controlling family and investors
I understand your insistence that the company must remain competitive and that a weak compromise would destroy value. I do not dispute that social conflict is costly.

But you are still underestimating the cost of pushing closures and forced layoffs through a company that is a major industrial anchor in Germany. The damage is not just political. It affects:
- industrial know-how,
- labor relations,
- supplier confidence,
- and the company’s long-term standing in its home region.

A company like this does not win by hollowing out its base and calling that discipline.

So no, I do not accept the logic that closures are simply the “credible” option while everything else is secondary. Credibility also comes from trust, stability, and a workforce willing to carry change because it is treated fairly.

### On burden-sharing
I do agree that all sides must contribute. That includes labor, but also management and owners.

For the state shareholder, burden-sharing is only legitimate if it is:
- proportional,
- transparent,
- and tied to **real protection of sites and jobs**.

I am open to an employee contribution if, and only if, it is matched by:
- management restraint,
- owner-side discipline,
- and a binding commitment that the German footprint is not being treated as disposable.

### On the proposed “phased” compromise
I am not convinced by a model that says “no closures for phase 1, then review later.”

That may sound practical, but for us it means the threat is merely postponed. It does not create the security needed for investment, planning, and social peace. If the company wants a durable settlement, it must be willing to rule out closures in the agreed framework, not merely delay the fight.

### What could move me
I can support a serious package if it contains all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures in the restructuring package.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Binding protection for jobs and sites in the state.**
4. **Real internal restructuring measures with numbers, not slogans.**
5. **Management and owner contributions that are visible and credible.**
6. **Investment commitments that support the future products and technologies.**
7. **Respect for co-determination and the state’s blocking role.**

### What will not move me
What will not move me is the claim that closures are “necessary” simply because they are the easiest route to target savings. That may be convenient for management and some investors, but it is not acceptable as industrial or regional responsibility.

And I will say this plainly: if the only way to make the plan work is to remove German plants or force layoffs, then the plan is not yet good enough. It needs a different solution.

### Bottom line
I am not here to block restructuring. I am here to block a restructuring that sacrifices the regional industrial base and sidelines the governance safeguards that protect long-term stability.

If management, owners, and the workforce want a real agreement, then bring a package that:
- restores competitiveness,
- preserves sites and employment in the state,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and keeps the company anchored in the region.

If that cannot be done, then I will not soften my position.
