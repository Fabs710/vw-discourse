### Core impasse

The discussion is stuck on **one central conflict**:

- **Employee side + state shareholder:** closures and compulsory redundancies are off-limits; they want binding job/site guarantees and restructuring through internal measures only.
- **Management + family + institutional investors:** they accept the need for restructuring but insist that **closures and structural workforce reductions must remain available** if quantified savings cannot otherwise be achieved.

So the impasse is not about whether the company needs change. It is about **whether the restructuring must be designed under a hard no-closure rule or under an economics-first rule with closures as an available backstop**.

A second, related impasse is **sequencing**:
- Employees want **security first, then contributions**.
- Management and owners want **savings/capacity adjustment first, then any security commitments**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions

#### 1) Phased restructuring with conditional non-closure commitment
A possible bridge would be:
- all parties commit to a **first-phase package** of internal measures only:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - retraining,
  - management restraint,
  - temporary employee contributions,
  - investment commitments.
- In return, management and owners commit to **no closures or compulsory redundancies during phase 1**.
- A **joint review gate** is set at fixed milestones, with predefined thresholds for savings, investment, and capacity reduction.
- Only if the agreed targets are demonstrably missed would a second-phase discussion on structural options open.

This preserves the employee demand for immediate protection while giving management a defined mechanism to revisit unresolved gaps.

#### 2) “No closures” for one period, paired with deeper internal capacity reduction
A second compromise would be:
- a binding agreement that **German closures are ruled out for a limited period**,
- but employees accept **deeper internal capacity reductions** than they have so far explicitly endorsed:
  - shift reductions,
  - plant specialization,
  - utilization cuts,
  - temporary furlough-style instruments,
  - lower contractor use,
  - stricter productivity changes.
- Management and owners, in return, commit to **no compulsory redundancies** and to a quantified investment program.

This shifts the burden from site elimination to **operational footprint reduction without site closure**.

#### 3) Guaranteed employment envelope with optional restructuring tools inside it
A third bridge could be:
- agree on a **fixed employment corridor** and **site retention baseline** through 2030,
- but allow management to use a menu of restructuring tools inside that envelope:
  - voluntary programs,
  - internal transfers,
  - retraining,
  - role redesign,
  - temporary wage components,
  - work-time adjustments,
  - management cost cuts.
- If the corridor cannot be met with those tools, a **jointly governed arbitration step** would decide whether any exceptional structural measures are justified.

This would not fully satisfy either extreme, but it could create a governed process rather than a binary standoff.
