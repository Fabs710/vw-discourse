Let me answer the room plainly.

We are not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that externalizes management’s failures onto workers, plants, and regions. And we are certainly not here to bless a plan that keeps “closure” and “compulsory redundancies” on the table as bargaining chips.

### To management
You say you need durable savings. I do not dispute the need for change. I dispute your chosen method.

Your argument still has the same flaw: you present closures and forced layoffs as if they were the only serious tools. They are not. They are the most destructive tools. If you cannot solve the cost problem without shutting German plants or breaking the job-security framework, then that is a failure of strategy, not evidence that workers should pay the price.

So let me be very clear:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

If you want a serious answer, then show us the actual savings gap and the actual alternative package that closes it. Not slogans. Numbers.

And if the answer is simply “we need the option to close plants,” then you are not asking for a restructuring mandate — you are asking for a blank cheque to destroy trust.

### To the controlling family and investors
You keep saying “durable,” “credible,” and “economically necessary.” Those words do not become correct just because they are repeated.

A durable solution that destroys the workforce’s trust, violates longstanding guarantees, and triggers conflict across German plants is not durable. It is destabilizing. It weakens the company’s industrial base and its legitimacy.

You also need to stop pretending that the only relevant metric is short-term margin repair. The company’s value depends on know-how, production stability, social peace, and political legitimacy in Germany. All of that is at risk if you push closures as the default answer.

So no: we do not accept the logic that “if it is expensive, close it.” That is not industrial strategy. That is disinvestment by stealth.

### To the state shareholder
You are right to reject closures and compulsory redundancies. But concern is not enough. If you mean to defend the regional industrial base, then use your blocking position accordingly.

The state shareholder should not tolerate a package that reduces the German footprint under the banner of “competitiveness” while leaving workers to absorb the damage. If you want a real alternative, help enforce one:
- capacity reductions without closures,
- working-time tools,
- voluntary measures only,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and binding investment commitments.

### On the supposed trade-off
Management, owners, and investors keep repeating: “Competitiveness first, security second.”

We reject that sequencing.

**Site and job security are not rewards after the fact. They are the precondition for any credible restructuring.** If workers are asked to accept uncertainty first, they have no reason to trust any later promise of investment or stability.

That is exactly why we insist on binding guarantees through 2030. Not as a rhetorical flourish, but because without binding protection, every “voluntary” contribution becomes a one-way road toward layoffs.

### What we can support
We are prepared to discuss a serious alternative package, but only if it is built on these terms:

1. **Every German plant remains open.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.**
4. **Real co-determination in the design and implementation of the restructuring.**
5. **A fair, negotiated employee contribution tied to reciprocal contributions from management and owners.**

And yes, we are ready to discuss:
- capacity adjustment,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary cost contributions,
- voluntary programs,
- internal transfers,
- retraining,
- management restraint,
- and investment commitments.

But these are discussion points within a framework of security, not substitutes for it.

### What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- closures “if needed,”
- forced redundancies “if alternatives fail,”
- a restructuring used to break the historic job-security pledge,
- or any package that treats co-determination as a procedural nuisance instead of a real power.

### On the numbers
If you want a concrete path forward, then bring the full options paper. But understand this: we will not participate in a paper whose hidden premise is plant closure.

So the right exercise is not “how do we close the gap using closures if necessary?” The right exercise is:

- what savings are required,
- what internal measures can deliver them,
- what employee contribution is proportional,
- what management and owner contribution is real,
- and what binding guarantees make the package acceptable.

If that paper shows a gap that cannot be closed without closures, then we will not pretend the answer is simple. We will resist it.

### Bottom line
We are not drifting. Our position stands.

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No breach of job-security guarantees.**
- **Real burden-sharing from all sides.**
- **Real co-determination, not a fait accompli.**

If management and ownership want agreement, they need to stop threatening the workforce with structural destruction and start negotiating a genuine alternative. Otherwise, they should be prepared for resistance — in the supervisory board, in the plants, and publicly.
