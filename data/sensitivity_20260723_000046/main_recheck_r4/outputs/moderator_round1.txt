### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **real competitiveness and cost problem** and that **a durable restructuring** is needed.
- There is broad support for **shared sacrifice** rather than placing the burden on one side alone.
- Most parties see value in:
  - **investment in future products/technology**
  - **internal flexibility and working-time tools**
  - **voluntary measures**
  - **a solution that is durable rather than purely symbolic**

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, union, and state shareholder reject closures as a default or acceptable outcome.
  - Management, family holding, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if needed to achieve structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee representatives and the state shareholder reject them.
  - Management and ownership groups will not rule them out if alternatives are insufficient.
- **Job/site guarantees through 2030**
  - Employee side wants binding guarantees.
  - Management and investor side view blanket guarantees as incompatible with necessary restructuring.
- **Scope of cost reduction**
  - Management/investors want durable, quantified structural savings.
  - Employee/state side insist these must be achieved without destroying sites, jobs, or regional industrial capacity.
- **Governance and co-determination**
  - Employee/state side want real participation and blocking rights respected.
  - Management wants flexibility to execute restructuring; owners want a stable, credible process.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required** versus **what level of job/site protection is feasible**.
- Whether the restructuring can be made **durably cost-effective without closures**, and if so, what concrete measures would replace them.
- The size, duration, and conditions of any **employee contribution**.
- Whether management/owners will provide **counter-contributions** such as dividend restraint, capital commitment, or management sacrifice.
- How to balance **short-term cost cuts** against **long-term investment and regional employment impacts**.
- What enforcement mechanisms would make any package **credible and binding** for all sides.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and options**:
  - required savings gap,
  - which sites/processes are affected,
  - what alternative measures can close the gap,
  - and what each side is prepared to contribute.
- Ask each party to state:
  1. the **minimum acceptable outcome**,
  2. the **maximum concession** it could make,
  3. and the **specific package** it could support.
