From the perspective of dispersed institutional investors, the key question is simple: **does the final package credibly restore competitive margins and protect enterprise value?** If yes, we can support it. If not, we will not.

### Where we agree
I agree with management, and partly with the family shareholder, on the core diagnosis:

- the current German cost base is too high,
- overcapacity is real,
- and cosmetic measures are not enough.

I also agree that the company needs a **durable** solution, not a delay tactic. Prolonged drift, repeated concessions without structural savings, or a settlement that leaves the underlying economics unchanged would destroy value.

### Where we disagree
I do **not** support a “preserve everything” stance if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

That means:

- **No support for a settlement that freezes the current cost base.**
- **No support for guarantees that simply lock in inefficiency through 2030 or beyond.**
- **No support for a package that avoids hard choices but fails to close the margin gap.**

The works council and union are right that workers should not carry the burden alone. But from our point of view, that is not the decisive issue. What matters is whether the final outcome produces a credible return to target margins and sustainable cash generation. If it does, we care less whether the adjustment comes through closures, labour-cost changes, working-time flexibility, or a mix.

### On plant closures and compulsory redundancies
We are not ideologically attached to closures. We are attached to **results**.

If management can achieve the required savings and capacity adjustment through an alternative package that is:
- durable,
- legally enforceable,
- and not just temporary window dressing,

then that is preferable to a destructive confrontation.

But if closures or deeper restructuring are truly necessary to restore competitiveness, then we would not support an arrangement that blocks them entirely. What we will not back is a deal that preserves all sites and still leaves the company with an uncompetitive operating model.

### What would move us
We would be prepared to support a negotiated settlement if it includes:

- **credible, quantified structural savings**
- **a clear path back to the return-on-sales target**
- **durable capacity reduction**
- **meaningful labour-cost adjustment**
- **strong execution certainty**
- **no open-ended industrial conflict**
- **investment commitments tied to measurable performance improvements**

A temporary employee contribution could be acceptable **only if** it is part of a broader package that genuinely resets the cost base. By itself, it is not enough.

### What will not move us
We will not be persuaded by:

- appeals to social partnership alone,
- political arguments that override economics,
- or promises of future improvement without hard numbers and enforceable commitments.

### Bottom line
We are open to a compromise, but only a **credible competitive one**. If the final outcome leaves the German operations structurally too expensive, we will view that as value-destructive and oppose it through our capital allocation and public market voice.

Bring us a package that restores margins and reduces cost in a durable way, and we can support it. Bring us a cosmetic settlement, and we will not.
