I appreciate that management is stating the competitiveness problem openly. I do not dispute that the company needs a credible restructuring and that cost pressure in Germany is real. But I draw a very clear line at the method.

**What I agree with:**
- The company needs durable savings, not cosmetic changes.
- Future investment in products and technology matters.
- Shared sacrifice is better than asking only one side to carry the burden.

**What I do not accept:**
- **Plant closures in Germany as a default instrument.**
- **Compulsory redundancies.**
- **A restructuring that treats regional employment as expendable.**
- **Any solution that sidelines the state’s co-determination and blocking rights.**

From the regional and public-interest perspective, closing plants in Germany is not a neutral efficiency measure. It destroys skilled jobs, weakens supplier networks, and damages the industrial base that the company itself depends on long term. Once such capacity and know-how are gone, they do not come back easily. That is a strategic loss, not just a local inconvenience.

So if management’s position is that there must be closures unless everyone accepts a major job sacrifice, then I must say plainly: **that is not a basis for agreement from our side**.

What would be a serious path forward is a restructuring that achieves real savings **without** breaking the social partnership and without gutting regional employment. That means:
- capacity adjustments where they are genuinely reversible,
- working-time instruments and internal flexibility,
- voluntary exits and retraining,
- burden-sharing from management and owners,
- and a binding commitment to future investment and site utilization.

If management insists that “the German cost base must fundamentally change,” then the burden is on management to show how that can be done **without closures and forced layoffs**. I am not prepared to endorse a logic that starts from plant shutdowns and then asks the workforce to absorb the consequences.

Let me also be clear: the state shareholder will not stand by if this becomes an exercise in shifting pain downward while preserving strategic freedom for management. We are prepared to support a pragmatic restructuring, but not one that removes the company’s regional anchor role or strips workers and public stakeholders of meaningful protections.

So my position is:
- **Yes** to restructuring that preserves sites, jobs, and future investment.
- **No** to closures and compulsory redundancies.
- **No** to any deal that weakens the company’s long-term role in the region.

If management wants our support, it needs to bring forward an alternative that is genuinely equivalent in savings but materially different in social and regional damage. Until then, I will oppose the current direction.
