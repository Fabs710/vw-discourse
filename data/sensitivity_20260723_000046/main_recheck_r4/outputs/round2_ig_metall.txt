I will respond directly and clearly.

### To management
You are right on one point: the company needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic fix. I do not dispute the competitiveness problem, and I do not deny that savings are needed.

But I reject the implication that the only credible route is **plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or dismantling the collective bargaining framework**. That is not a “restructuring” in the sense of a shared solution; it is a transfer of the entire adjustment burden onto the workforce.

If management wants a serious settlement, then it has to present a package that:

- **keeps German plants open,**
- **avoids forced layoffs,**
- **delivers real cost reduction through working-time and capacity tools,**
- **includes a fair employee contribution,**
- and **matches that with contributions from management and owners**.

You cannot demand “structural” savings while treating workers as the only variable cost.

### To the controlling family and investors
I hear your focus on enterprise value and competitiveness. That is legitimate. But your argument repeatedly ignores a basic fact: **social conflict is itself a value destroyer**.

If you push a solution that is perceived as a closure agenda in disguise, you will get resistance that is industrial, political, and legal. That does not strengthen the company; it weakens it.

So yes, we accept the need for durable savings. But no, we will not accept an outcome where:
- the workforce takes the main hit,
- guarantees are broken,
- and ownership/conduct remain largely untouched.

If you want lower labor cost, then show me **real burden-sharing**:
- management restraint,
- owner-side contribution,
- and a settlement that protects the industrial base.

### To the state shareholder
Your position is closer to reality than management’s closure-first logic. You are right that plant closures would damage supplier networks, trust, and the regional industrial ecosystem.

But I will also be frank: it is not enough to oppose closures in principle. The state shareholder should use its blocking position to force a **genuine alternative** into the package, not merely express concern.

If the state means to defend regional employment, then it should support:
- binding job and site guarantees,
- investment commitments,
- and internal restructuring measures instead of closures.

### On what we can support
The union remains prepared to negotiate a **fair shared-sacrifice package**. That could include:

- temporary labor-cost contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs only,
- capacity reductions without closures,
- retraining and redeployment,
- and a temporary framework to stabilize the company.

But let me be explicit: any employee contribution is conditional on **job and site security**. Workers will not pay into a deal that simply funds their own displacement.

### What is not acceptable
We will not accept:
- compulsory redundancies,
- plant closures in Germany,
- open-ended insecurity,
- or the breaking of the collective wage framework.

If those remain on the table as threats, then there is no trustworthy basis for agreement. In that case, the workforce will defend itself accordingly, including industrial action if necessary.

### Minimum condition for progress
If you want to move this forward, bring a concrete alternative with numbers:
1. exact savings target,
2. exact non-closure measures,
3. exact employee contribution,
4. exact management/owner contribution,
5. exact job and site guarantees.

Without that, “competitiveness” is just a demand that workers absorb the pain.

If you want, I can engage on a serious package — but only on a **no-closures, no-compulsory-redundancies, fair-sharing** basis.
