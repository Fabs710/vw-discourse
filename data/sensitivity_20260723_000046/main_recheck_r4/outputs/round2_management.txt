I’ll respond directly.

### To the Works Council and the Union
I understand your red lines, but I need to be equally direct: **as stated, your position does not solve the competitiveness problem**.

You are asking for:
- every German plant to stay open,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- and a substantial employee contribution only on your terms.

That combination may protect the workforce in the short term, but it does **not** yet deliver the structural savings we need. If we simply preserve the current footprint and cost structure, we will continue to miss our return-on-sales target, and we will not create the financial room for future products, software, and EV investment.

I agree on one point: **a cosmetic deal is useless**. I do not want a temporary patch either. But durable restructuring requires real change in the cost base and capacity. If you reject closures and forced redundancies outright, then you must show me an alternative that produces the **same structural effect**, not just internal redistribution of pain.

What is not acceptable to me:
- a deal that locks in overcapacity,
- a settlement that leaves German labour costs fundamentally uncompetitive,
- or guarantees that make restructuring impossible until 2030.

If you want to avoid harsher measures, then bring forward a package that is truly equivalent in savings and durability. Otherwise, your “no closures” line is simply a veto on restructuring, and I cannot accept that.

### To the State Shareholder
I agree with you that social conflict and loss of regional trust are serious risks. I also agree that we should avoid unnecessary damage to the industrial base. But I cannot accept the implication that plant closures are somehow off-limits because they are politically difficult.

The state’s role is not to freeze the company in its current structure. The company’s role is to remain competitive and investable. If the German footprint remains too expensive and too large, then pretending otherwise helps no one — not workers, not regions, and not the company.

So yes, I welcome your support for a pragmatic restructuring. But “pragmatic” must mean **economically effective**, not simply politically comfortable.

### To the Controlling Family and Investors
I agree with the core point you both make: **the final package must be durable and credible**. You are right that a superficial compromise will destroy value. You are also right that social conflict itself can be value-destructive.

Where I differ from the employee side is that I do **not** believe we can guarantee security first and competitiveness second. The order has to be reversed: we need a viable cost base first, then we can secure the future with new investment.

That means:
- capacity reduction is unavoidable,
- labour-cost adjustment is unavoidable,
- and if alternative measures do not close the gap, structural plant action cannot be ruled out.

### On the numbers and the path forward
We need to stop talking only in principles. The company needs a package that delivers:
1. a **durable reduction in overcapacity**,
2. a **material labour-cost contribution**,
3. a **clear path back to target margins**,
4. and **investment capacity through 2030**.

If the employee side is serious about a no-closure alternative, then let them put concrete measures on the table that truly replace the economics of closure:
- how much capacity comes out,
- how much cost comes out,
- how quickly,
- and with what legal certainty.

Without that, “alternative restructuring” is not a solution; it is a delay tactic.

### My minimum position
I will not accept a settlement that:
- leaves the German cost base materially uncompetitive,
- protects every site regardless of economics,
- or substitutes promises for structural savings.

I am open to a negotiated package, but only if it produces real and enforceable savings. If the other side cannot meet that test, then I will continue to push the full restructuring agenda, including plant-level restructuring options.

If you want, the next step should be to put forward **concrete quantified proposals** rather than repeating red lines.
