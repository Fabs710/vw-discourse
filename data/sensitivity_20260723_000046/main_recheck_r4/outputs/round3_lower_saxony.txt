I will respond directly.

### To management
I agree that the company needs a **durable restructuring** and that a cosmetic fix would be a mistake. I also accept that the current cost structure cannot simply remain unchanged.

But I do **not** accept the way you continue to frame this as if German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are normal or necessary starting points.

From the perspective of the state shareholder, that is not a neutral economic argument. It is a decision with major consequences for:
- regional employment,
- supplier networks,
- industrial stability,
- and the company’s legitimacy as a regional anchor.

So my position is clear:

- **No restructuring package that includes German plant closures as a default element.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and blocking role.**

If you need savings, then show me the **quantified alternative** that achieves them **without destroying sites and jobs in the state**. If that alternative does not exist, then the burden is on management to prove it — not on workers or the state to accept destruction as “inevitable.”

### To the Works Council and Union
I agree with your core red lines:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and no settlement that treats workers as the shock absorber.

That said, I want to be equally honest: a settlement based only on slogans about keeping everything unchanged will not be enough either. The company does need a serious adjustment in capacity and cost.

What I can support is a **negotiated restructuring that protects the industrial base**, for example through:
- capacity reduction without closures,
- working-time instruments,
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and binding investment commitments.

But the sequence matters. Workers should not be asked to pay first while the company keeps closure threats in reserve. That is not a fair basis for social partnership.

### To the controlling family and investors
I understand your insistence on durability and competitiveness. I do not dismiss the economics.

But I disagree with your repeated assumption that credibility depends on keeping closures and forced layoffs on the table. That may look “disciplined” on paper, but in practice it risks:
- industrial conflict,
- political backlash,
- loss of trust,
- and damage to the company’s long-term regional position.

A company like this does not stay strong by hollowing out its home base. It stays strong by combining competitiveness with industrial legitimacy.

So I will not endorse a package that treats the regional footprint as disposable. If your preferred savings can only be achieved that way, then the plan is too narrow.

### Where I stand
My minimum position is unchanged:

1. **No German plant closures in the restructuring package.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **Protection of jobs and sites in the state.**
4. **Real burden-sharing from management, owners, and employees.**
5. **A durable, quantified restructuring path that preserves the company as a regional economic anchor.**
6. **Respect for co-determination and the state’s blocking role.**

### What could move me
I could support a package if it includes:
- a credible quantified savings plan,
- real internal capacity adjustments,
- voluntary and temporary measures first,
- binding investment commitments,
- and tangible contribution from management and ownership.

What will **not** move me is the claim that closures are “necessary” simply because they are the easiest route. That may be convenient for management, but it is not acceptable as regional or political responsibility.

### My bottom line
I am not here to block restructuring. I am here to block a restructuring that damages the region irreversibly and sidelines the very governance safeguards that protect long-term stability.

If management and the owners want support, they must bring a package that:
- restores competitiveness,
- preserves sites and employment in the state,
- and keeps the company anchored in the region.

If they cannot do that, then I will not soften my position.
