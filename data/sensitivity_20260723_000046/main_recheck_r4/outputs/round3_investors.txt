We agree on the **diagnosis**: the current structure is not sustainable, and a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. We also agree that prolonged industrial conflict is bad for everyone, including shareholders.

But we are **not** aligned with the employee/state side’s attempt to turn “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees through 2030” into the starting premise. That is not a restructuring framework; it is a veto on the very adjustments needed to restore margins.

### To works council and union
We understand the social logic of your position, but from a capital-markets perspective it is too rigid **unless** you can demonstrate an alternative that is truly equivalent in economics and durability.

So far, what we hear is:
- capacity reduction, working-time tools, voluntary exits, retraining,
- temporary employee contributions,
- management restraint,
- investment commitments.

Those are all useful components. But they are **not automatically enough**. If they do not close the cost gap decisively and durably, then you are asking investors to accept structurally impaired returns in exchange for headline stability. We will not support that.

What would move us is not a slogan about “shared sacrifice,” but a **quantified package** with:
1. the exact savings target,
2. the exact labor-cost reduction,
3. the exact capacity reduction,
4. legal durability through 2030 or comparable enforceability,
5. and a clear path back to competitive margins.

If you can deliver that **without** closures, fine. If you cannot, then the company must retain the option of structural action.

### To management
We support your insistence on a durable solution and on the need for real structural savings. That is the right focus.

But we also want to be clear: **value destruction through endless confrontation is not acceptable either**. If management pushes a maximalist closure agenda without exhausting credible alternatives, you may win a legal battle and lose enterprise value through strikes, political backlash, and execution risk.

So our support for management is conditional:
- the restructuring must be **quantified**,
- the execution path must be **credible**,
- and the conflict horizon must be **limited**.

We do not support symbolic toughness. We support disciplined restructuring that restores margins.

### To the state shareholder
Your concern for regional employment is legitimate. But as long as that concern is used to block any economically meaningful adjustment, it becomes a value problem for all shareholders.

The company’s long-term ability to employ people depends on competitiveness first. If the regional base is preserved at the expense of margin recovery, shareholders will simply fund a delayed decline.

### To the controlling family
We agree with you that a deal must be durable and that ownership cannot freeze an uncompetitive structure forever. Where we differ is on execution discipline: if the family backs a package that is too dependent on confrontation or too weak on savings, it will damage both valuation and credibility.

### Our bottom line
We will back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**. That means:
- durable cost reduction,
- clear capacity adjustment,
- enforceable burden-sharing,
- and no cosmetic settlement.

We will **not** support a package that leaves the cost base uncompetitive or margins structurally impaired.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, they need to put forward a real numbers-based alternative. If they cannot, then management and owners must keep structural options available.
