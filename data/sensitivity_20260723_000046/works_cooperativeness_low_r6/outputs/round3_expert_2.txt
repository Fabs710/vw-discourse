## Independent restructuring finance view

The key question is not whether non-closure measures save money; they do. The question is whether they produce **enough recurring savings, fast enough, with enough durability**, to justify avoiding closures as the company’s economic backstop.

Based on the operating logic described, a **deep non-closure package can plausibly close a substantial share of the gap**, but it is usually **not fully equivalent to plant closure economics** unless it includes:
- real permanent capacity reduction,
- large voluntary headcount reduction,
- meaningful indirect overhead cuts,
- product concentration,
- and measurable labor-cost contributions.

## Likely financial framing

### 1) Closure-heavy path
**Financial profile**
- Highest recurring savings
- Largest one-time restructuring cost
- Strongest permanent capacity removal
- Highest execution and labor-relations risk

**Typical economics**
- Recurring savings: highest benchmark
- One-time costs: severance, asset write-downs, decommissioning, transfer/logistics, remediation
- NPV: can be attractive if the company truly has excess capacity and if the savings are durable
- Downside: industrial conflict, implementation delay, political interference

### 2) Deep non-closure path
**Financial profile**
- Lower one-time cost than closure
- Lower recurring savings than closure
- Better labor stability if negotiated successfully
- More operational complexity

**Typical economics**
- Savings from:
  - shift reductions
  - working-time flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - wage moderation
  - productivity gains
  - product concentration
- Weak point:
  - residual site fixed costs remain
  - indirect overhead tends to shrink less than direct labor
  - savings can be partly reversible

**Financial conclusion**
- This can be credible if the company’s required gap is moderate and if the package is deep.
- It becomes weak if management needs a large fixed-cost reset and the package does not materially reduce capacity.

### 3) Hybrid path with escalation trigger
**Financial profile**
- Best compromise between credibility and flexibility
- Allows a non-closure first phase
- Preserves an enforcement mechanism if targets are missed

**Typical economics**
- Often the most investable structure
- Lets parties capture near-term savings from non-closure measures
- Keeps a backstop for underperformance
- But it only works if the trigger is objective and pre-agreed

## The practical NPV issue

From a DCF standpoint, the relevant comparison is:

**NPV of savings from restructuring**  
minus  
**one-time restructuring costs**  
minus  
**expected downside from disruption / delay / underperformance**

A closure-based plan often wins on pure recurring savings, but loses value if:
- it takes too long to implement,
- strikes delay realization,
- political vetoes reduce feasibility,
- or asset disposal costs are higher than expected.

A non-closure plan often wins on implementation speed and lower upfront cost, but loses value if:
- savings are too soft,
- capacity remains excessive,
- or the package depends on temporary measures.

## My threshold judgment

A non-closure package becomes **financially insufficient** when, after all negotiated measures, it still leaves:
- a large residual cost gap,
- materially under-target margins by 2030,
- or savings that rely heavily on reversible measures.

In practical terms, if the residual annual gap remains large relative to the company’s restructuring target, then a closure backstop becomes financially justified as an enforcement mechanism.

## What I would recommend as a modeling structure

Use three cases:

### Case A: Closure-heavy
- Highest annual savings
- Highest one-time cost
- Highest labor/political execution risk

### Case B: Deep non-closure
- Lower annual savings
- Lower one-time cost
- Lower immediate disruption
- Higher risk of residual cost gap

### Case C: Hybrid with trigger
- Non-closure savings in years 1–3
- Predefined milestone review
- Escalation if savings underperform
- Often the best risk-adjusted outcome

## Bottom line

Financially, **a deep non-closure package is plausible but not automatically sufficient**.  
If the company’s structural overcapacity and cost gap are large, then **some form of closure backstop is usually needed to make the package credible** — not necessarily to be used immediately, but to ensure the economics are enforceable.
