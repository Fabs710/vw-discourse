## Core impasse

The plateau is centered on **one unresolved design choice**:

- **Employees / state / union / works council:** accept deep restructuring, but reject **closures, compulsory redundancies, and any closure backstop**.
- **Management / family / investors:** accept non-closure measures only if they are **economically sufficient and enforceable**, and they want a **credible escalation mechanism** if targets are missed.

So the deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **what makes the package credible**:

1. **No closure logic at all** vs. **some fallback/enforcement path**
2. **Binding site security** vs. **bankable economic discipline**
3. **Burden-sharing** vs. **who carries the residual risk if the package underdelivers**

The technical expert has narrowed this further: a non-closure package may be viable if it is **deep, front-loaded, and quantified**, but not if it is merely symbolic. The remaining conflict is therefore about **the structure of enforcement and the size of the non-closure package**, not the existence of restructuring.

---

## Concrete compromise positions

### Proposal 1: “No-closure settlement period” with milestone-based deepening, not closure
A time-bound arrangement could be structured so that:

- **no plant closures in Germany during the settlement period**
- **no compulsory redundancies during that same period**
- **mandatory capacity reduction and cost removal** through shifts, lines, product concentration, redeployment, and voluntary exits
- **milestone reviews** at fixed dates
- if targets are missed, the escalation path is **additional non-closure measures first**:
  - more shift reductions
  - further line consolidation
  - additional overhead cuts
  - more management/owner contributions
  - tighter product concentration

This would give management and investors an enforcement mechanism **without a closure fallback in the first instance**.

### Proposal 2: “Binding non-closure package” with reciprocal commitments
A package could be made credible by pairing labor concessions with hard commitments from the top:

- workforce contribution:
  - flexibility
  - voluntary exits
  - productivity agreement
  - redeployment
  - qualification
- counterpart commitments:
  - binding investment in German sites
  - binding product allocation
  - management overhead cuts
  - owner-funded support measures
  - co-determination in implementation
  - site guarantees for the settlement period

This bridges the burden-sharing dispute by making labor contributions conditional on **visible, enforceable commitments from management and owners**.

### Proposal 3: “Two-step restructuring” with objective trigger, not automatic closure
A staged model could separate:

- **Phase 1:** deep non-closure restructuring with hard targets and a fixed deadline
- **Phase 2:** only if targets are missed, an **independent mediator/expert** determines whether the shortfall can still be closed through additional non-closure measures, revised product allocation, or added owner contributions

This would replace an immediate closure backstop with an **objective diagnostic trigger**, reducing the sense of coercion while preserving economic discipline.
