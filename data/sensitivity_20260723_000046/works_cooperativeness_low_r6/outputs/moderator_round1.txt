### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company needs a **durable restructuring** and not a short-term patch.
- There is broad agreement on the need for:
  - **investment in future products and technologies**
  - **competitiveness improvements**
  - **some form of negotiated solution**
  - **avoiding compulsory redundancies if a credible alternative exists**
- There is also shared openness to non-closure measures such as:
  - capacity adjustments
  - working-time flexibility
  - internal redeployment
  - qualification / transformation measures
  - productivity improvements
  - some form of burden sharing

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works council, union, and state: reject closures.
  - Management, family holding, investors: will not rule them out in advance.
- **Scope of job protection**
  - Employee side wants guarantees against compulsory redundancies and strong site/job security.
  - Management and capital side say these guarantees cannot be absolute if savings are insufficient.
- **Meaning of “acceptable alternative”**
  - Employee side: restructuring must preserve sites and jobs as far as possible.
  - Capital side: any alternative must deliver the same durable cost and capacity reduction as closures would.
- **Burden sharing**
  - All parties say contributions should be shared, but they disagree on **how much workers, management, owners, and capital should each bear**.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much savings are required** and from which measures.
- **What level of capacity reduction is necessary** by 2030.
- **Whether non-closure measures can realistically match closure-related savings**.
- **How to balance job security against competitiveness and investment capacity**.
- **What binding commitments each side is prepared to make**:
  - workforce concessions
  - management/owner capital commitments
  - site guarantees
  - product allocation
  - timelines and enforcement mechanisms

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and scenarios**:
  1. quantify the cost gap and required savings,
  2. compare closure and non-closure alternatives,
  3. specify what each stakeholder is prepared to contribute,
  4. test whether a package can be made **credible, enforceable, and socially acceptable**.
- The next round should aim to identify **one concrete restructuring model** rather than repeating red lines.
