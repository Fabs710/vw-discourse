I will respond plainly, because we are at the point where vague assurances no longer help.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the investors on the core issue: **this company needs a durable, structural improvement in competitiveness and margins**. A cosmetic compromise would destroy value. That is unacceptable for the family holding as long-term owner.

I also agree that:
- investment in future products is necessary,
- the workforce should contribute if a serious restructuring is to succeed,
- and any agreement must be measurable and enforceable.

## Where I disagree
### 1) A blanket “no closures” position is too rigid
I understand the works council’s and union’s political and industrial logic. But I do **not** accept that “no plant closures” can be the starting point if it prevents the company from removing enough cost and capacity to restore competitiveness.

A position that preserves every site regardless of economics is not stewardship. It risks simply postponing the same crisis.

### 2) A “closure backstop” is not just an abstract threat
I also cannot accept the opposite extreme: a package that keeps every site open while making no credible provision for what happens if the savings fail to materialize. The expert was clear that non-closure measures can cover part of the gap, but often not all of it.

So I am not prepared to endorse:
- a purely defensive no-closure package,
- no meaningful capacity reduction,
- and no enforceable consequence if targets are missed.

That would be a value-destructive delay.

### 3) Social peace matters, but it is not a substitute for economics
To the state government and employee side: I acknowledge that trust, skills, and industrial peace matter. They do. But they do not eliminate the hard arithmetic. If the business remains structurally too expensive, then social comfort today will turn into a deeper crisis tomorrow.

## What the expert assessment actually means
The expert did not say closures are automatically required. Nor did the expert say non-closure measures are enough on their own.

What I take from the analysis is:
- a **deep non-closure package may be feasible**,
- but only if it produces real, durable savings and capacity reduction,
- and if there is a credible backstop when milestones are missed.

That is the correct governance reading.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a **non-closure first** restructuring only if it includes all of the following:
1. **Binding capacity reduction**
2. **Real labor-cost contribution**
3. **Working-time flexibility and productivity gains**
4. **Internal redeployment and voluntary exits**
5. **Product concentration and site specialization**
6. **Clear investment commitments**
7. **Milestones with automatic escalation if the package underperforms**

That is a serious route. It is not a surrender to the status quo.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a plan that simply declares all German sites untouchable,
- an arrangement that excludes compulsory redundancies absolutely without sufficient structural adjustment,
- or a package that leaves the company uncompetitive and then asks the owners to absorb the erosion indefinitely.

I also will not support any outcome that weakens ownership control or sidelines the governance rights of the family holding.

## To management
You now need to present a quantified proposal that answers:
- how much cost comes out,
- how much capacity comes out,
- what the workforce contribution is,
- what the investment commitments are,
- and what happens if the package misses its targets.

That is the next step. Not more slogans.

## To the works council and union
If you want to avoid closures, then engage with the hard part:
- real flexibility,
- real productivity improvements,
- real labour-cost contribution,
- and acceptance that some current structures may not survive unchanged.

A no-closure stance is only credible if it is paired with a restructuring that is economically deep enough to work.

## To the state government
Your role is important, but a political preference against closures is not a substitute for a workable restructuring. I am open to a non-closure path first. I am not open to a deal that leaves the company structurally weak.

## Bottom line
The family holding supports a **durable, enforceable restructuring** that restores competitiveness and margins.

We are open to a deep non-closure solution — but only if it is economically credible, measurable, and backed by consequences if it fails.

If not, then we are simply delaying the same problem at greater cost.
