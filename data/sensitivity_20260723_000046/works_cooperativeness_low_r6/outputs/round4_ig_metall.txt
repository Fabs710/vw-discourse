I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, the state, and even management on one fundamental point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a temporary patch**.

I also agree that:
- the company must restore competitiveness,
- investment in future products is non-negotiable,
- non-closure measures can deliver real savings,
- and employees can make a fair contribution.

So yes: we are not defending the status quo. We are defending a **viable industrial settlement**.

## Where I disagree — clearly
### 1) Plant closures are not the benchmark
I reject the idea that closures are the standard by which every alternative must be judged.

A plant closure is not a clean efficiency measure. It permanently destroys:
- jobs,
- skills,
- supplier networks,
- regional industrial capacity,
- and trust in the company.

If a deep non-closure package can solve the structural problem, then that is the better path. Full stop.

### 2) A closure backstop is not an acceptable compromise
This is the point where I do **not** move.

Management, owners, and investors keep saying a backstop is “just enforcement.” It is not. In practice it means:
- workers concede now,
- closures remain on the table,
- and the threat of shutdown is kept alive for later.

That is not a fair restructuring. That is a delayed closure strategy with concessions upfront.

We will not sign onto a deal whose hidden logic is:  
**take the pain now, keep the knife ready for later.**

### 3) Workers will not carry the whole burden
If this settlement is to be durable, burden-sharing must be real on **all sides**:
- labor contribution,
- management restraint,
- owner commitment,
- investment in German sites,
- and protection of collective bargaining standards.

We have already signaled willingness to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity measures,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment,
- shift and line reductions,
- product concentration,
- and a negotiated labor-cost contribution.

But we will not accept a package where workers pay, workers lose guarantees, and everyone else keeps escape routes.

## To management
You still have not answered the central question: **why must German sites be put at risk if non-closure tools can deliver substantial savings?**

You say the company needs hard savings. Fine. Then bring a plan that quantifies:
- fixed-cost reduction,
- capacity reduction,
- labor-cost contribution,
- and investment commitments.

What you cannot demand is a blank cheque for closures because they are simpler on paper.

And I want to be clear:  
**No compulsory redundancies** is not an obstacle to restructuring. It is a condition for a legitimate restructuring.

If you cannot make the numbers work without forced layoffs, then you need to prove that with transparent calculations — not with pressure.

We are aligned on the core red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of collective protections,
- no unilateral management diktat.

That said, I agree with you only if the non-closure package is real and deep, not symbolic. That means:
- shift reductions,
- line rationalization,
- product concentration,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- and productivity agreements.

Those are restructuring tools. They are not a bridge to closure.

## To the state government
Your rejection of closures as a default is welcome. But it has to be more than a political preference.

If you want a non-closure settlement, then use your position to support one that is:
- binding,
- measurable,
- and protected from later shutdown logic.

Do not ask the workforce to accept concessions while leaving the door open to the very outcome you say you want to avoid.

---

## To the controlling family and investors
You say you want value preservation. Then understand this:

**destroying trust destroys value**.

You cannot build a premium industrial company on fear and permanent insecurity. If you want a sustainable solution, then support:
- binding site guarantees,
- real investment in German plants,
- product commitments,
- and governance that respects co-determination.

A package that shifts all risk downward may look efficient in the short term. It is not sustainable.

## On the expert assessment
The technical experts have made an important point: non-closure measures can cover a large part of the gap, but they may not fully replicate closure economics.

I do **not** dispute that closures can look stronger in a spreadsheet.  
I do dispute the conclusion that closures are therefore the only credible answer.

The expert also confirmed something else: a deep non-closure package can be viable if it is:
- front-loaded,
- measurable,
- structurally meaningful,
- and backed by real contributions from management and owners.

That is the path we should be discussing.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to negotiate a package with:
1. **no plant closures in Germany**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **site-specific capacity reductions through internal measures**
4. **working-time and deployment flexibility**
5. **voluntary exits only**
6. **product and investment commitments**
7. **a fair labor contribution**
8. **management and owner burden-sharing**
9. **a binding role for co-determination in implementation**

That is a serious restructuring.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- closures as the default or hidden endgame,
- forced layoffs in any form,
- a weakening of the collective wage framework,
- or a settlement where workers absorb the pain while others protect themselves.

If that is the path management insists on, then conflict will follow.

## My direct response to the central impasse
The facilitator asks whether we accept any enforcement mechanism tied to missed targets.

My answer is: **not one that keeps closure on the table**.

If management wants enforcement, then enforcement must be built through:
- measurable milestones,
- additional non-closure measures,
- further owner and management contributions,
- and intensified productivity actions.

What it must **not** be is a built-in shutdown option.

That line is non-negotiable.

---

## Final word
We are not blocking restructuring. We are blocking **destructive restructuring**.

If management, owners, and the state want competitiveness, then they need to build it:
- **around German sites, not against them**
- **with workers, not over their heads**
- **through fair sacrifice, not one-sided sacrifice**

That is our position.
