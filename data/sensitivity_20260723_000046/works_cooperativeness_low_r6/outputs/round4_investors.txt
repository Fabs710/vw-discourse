We support a **durable restructuring that restores competitive margins and earnings power**. We do **not** support a settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
- **Management:** yes, the current footprint and cost base need real change, not optics.
- **Expert:** yes, non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings, but they must be deep and structural.
- **Family holding:** yes, cosmetic compromise would destroy value.
- **State / employee side:** yes, prolonged conflict and industrial disruption would be value-destructive.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No closures” cannot be a hard principle if the economics fail
The works council and union are trying to make “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” absolute. From a shareholder perspective, that is only acceptable **if** the alternative actually closes the cost gap.

If the package still leaves margins impaired, then a blanket no-closure rule is not protection — it is a veto on competitiveness. We cannot support that.

### 2) A package with no enforcement is not investable
I understand why labor rejects a “closure backstop,” but from our side a deal with no credible consequence for underperformance is not bankable.  
If the plan misses targets and nothing happens, then we are just buying time while the structural problem persists.

### 3) Burden-sharing must be measured in euros, not rhetoric
We need quantified, recurring savings:
- labor-cost contribution,
- capacity reduction,
- indirect overhead cuts,
- productivity gains,
- and a credible timeline to margin recovery.

“We will contribute” is not enough. We need numbers.

## Our position
We are prepared to back a **deep non-closure restructuring** **only if** it includes:
1. binding capacity reduction,
2. real recurring labor-cost savings,
3. voluntary exits plus internal redeployment,
4. product concentration and site specialization,
5. measurable milestones,
6. and a credible escalation mechanism if targets are missed.

If that cannot be achieved, then ruling out closures in advance is not defensible.

## To the employee side
You say a closure backstop is coercion. We say an unenforced deal is wishful thinking.  
If you want to avoid closures, then the non-closure package has to be strong enough to stand on its own economically.

## To management
Be specific. Put the numbers on the table:
- required annual savings,
- what can be achieved without closures,
- what the residual gap is,
- and the trigger for escalation.

## To the state and family
Your preference for preserving sites is understandable, but capital will not support a structure that remains uncompetitive through 2030. Protecting value means restoring returns, not preserving every site regardless of economics.

## Bottom line
We support a **credible, enforceable, deep non-closure restructuring** if it truly restores margins and competitiveness.  
If it does not, then excluding closures up front is not a responsible outcome for shareholders.
