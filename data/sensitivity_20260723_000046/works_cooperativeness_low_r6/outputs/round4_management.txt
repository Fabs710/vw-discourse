I’ll be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: **this company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**.

I also agree that:
- we need to invest in future products and technologies,
- a negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged conflict,
- and non-closure measures can contribute meaningfully.

So no, I am not dismissing the value of flexibility, redeployment, voluntary exits, productivity gains, or working-time changes.

## Where I disagree — fundamentally

### 1) “No closures” cannot be a sacred principle if the cost base remains uncompetitive
The works council and union keep treating “no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute starting points.

That is not a restructuring strategy. That is a political line.

If the current footprint is too expensive and too large for the market, then preserving every site in place is not a solution by itself. It may protect sentiment, but it does not automatically restore competitiveness, margins, or investment capacity.

We need to be honest about that.

### 2) A no-closure package is only acceptable if it removes enough cost and capacity
I do **not** need closures for their own sake. I need a structural, durable reduction in cost and capacity.

If the employee side can deliver that through:
- real capacity reduction,
- product concentration,
- headcount reduction with actual net savings,
- lower indirect overhead,
- wage moderation,
- and sustained productivity improvement,

then we can talk.

But if the package merely preserves all sites, keeps all guarantees intact, and shifts the burden into future years, then it is not credible.

### 3) A settlement with no enforcement mechanism is not bankable
I have to reject the idea that we should sign a deal with no meaningful consequence if the numbers miss plan.

The union and works council call any backstop coercion. I call it **governance**.

If a restructuring package is not tied to measurable milestones and a pre-agreed escalation path, then it is just a promise. Promises do not restore return on sales.

## To the works council
You say closures destroy value. So does preserving structurally uncompetitive capacity.

You still have not answered the core economic question:  
**How do you permanently remove enough fixed cost and capacity without closures if the current footprint is too large for the market?**

Listing instruments is not the same as proving sufficiency.

- shift reductions help
- line rationalization helps
- redeployment helps
- qualification helps
- voluntary exits help

But do they add up to the scale we need by 2030? That is the question. Not slogans. Not moral indignation. Numbers.

And let me be clear:  
A package that preserves every German site, excludes compulsory redundancies absolutely, and keeps all legacy arrangements unchanged is **not** a restructuring package I can support.

### Where I do agree with you
I do agree that a purely cosmetic “review later” plan would be unacceptable. If the non-closure route is chosen, it must be deep and front-loaded. On that point, we agree.

But I do **not** agree that this means the company should be barred from any fallback if the package underperforms. That would make the deal non-credible from the start.

## To the union
You say closures are not the benchmark and that workers will not accept a delayed shutdown strategy.

I understand the concern. But again: the company cannot be asked to sign up to a restructuring without any enforcement if it fails.

A backstop is not a threat for its own sake. It is the mechanism that prevents a weak compromise from becoming an expensive delay.

If you want a no-closure settlement, then it must be strong enough to stand on its own:
- measurable savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- labor cost contribution,
- and a clear consequence if targets are missed.

What I cannot accept is “no closures, no backstop, and trust us.” That is not enough for the fiduciary duty I have.

## To the state government
You are right that closures are politically and socially serious. I do not dismiss that.

But a political preference against closures is not a substitute for an economically sufficient solution. If you want a non-closure path, then help make it structurally deep enough to work.

I need:
- measurable labor-cost contribution,
- support for capacity reduction through non-closure means,
- and a commitment to implementation discipline.

What I will not accept is a political veto on closures that leaves the company carrying excess cost forever.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree with you that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value.

But I also need you to be clear-eyed: **a non-closure package that is economically shallow will also destroy value**. It simply does so more slowly.

That is why I insist on:
- hard savings,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- and milestone-based enforcement.

If we are serious about 2030 competitiveness, then we need a package that is bankable in cash terms, not just acceptable in rhetoric.

## On the expert input
The independent experts have helped clarify the technical reality:

- Non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings.
- But they usually do **not** fully replicate closure economics unless they are very deep and structurally binding.
- The best non-closure route is therefore a **front-loaded, measurable, enforceable** package, not a soft promise.

That is exactly my position.

## What I am prepared to accept
I remain open to a **non-closure first** settlement if it includes:
1. binding capacity reduction
2. real labor-cost contribution
3. working-time and deployment flexibility
4. voluntary exits and internal redeployment
5. product concentration and site specialization
6. a credible investment commitment
7. measurable milestones
8. a pre-agreed escalation path if the package underdelivers

That is a serious restructuring path.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a blanket no-closure rule,
- a no-redundancy doctrine with no real structural adjustment,
- a package that leaves the company uncompetitive,
- or a settlement that has no consequence if it misses targets.

That would fail the mandate I have to restore competitiveness.

## My position in one sentence
I am prepared to support a deep, negotiated non-closure restructuring — but only if it is economically strong enough to close the structural gap, and only if it includes enforceable consequences for underperformance.

If that cannot be achieved, then refusing closures in advance is not responsible restructuring; it is postponing the same problem at higher cost.
