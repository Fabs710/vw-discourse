I will be equally direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one central point: **the company needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic pause**.

I also agree that:
- investment in future products is essential,
- competitiveness must improve,
- non-closure measures can deliver real savings,
- and the package must be measurable and enforceable.

So I am not defending the status quo. I am defending a restructuring that preserves the company’s long-term role as a regional industrial anchor.

## Where I disagree — clearly

### 1) Plant closures are not the benchmark the company should force on everyone else
To management, the family holding, and investors: I reject the idea that closures are the natural reference point and that every alternative must prove itself by matching that damage.

A closure is not a neutral efficiency tool. It means:
- lost jobs,
- lost skills,
- damaged supplier networks,
- weakened regional purchasing power,
- and a serious blow to trust.

That is especially true in this company, where the state’s role and the regional economy are part of the governance reality, not side issues.

So no: I will not accept closures as the default yardstick.

### 2) The “closure backstop” is the wrong kind of credibility
I understand why management and capital keep asking for an enforcement mechanism. But a built-in closure fallback is not an acceptable basis for a settlement from my side.

If the non-closure package is real, then it must stand on its own merits:
- binding capacity reduction,
- real labor contributions,
- product concentration,
- investment commitments,
- and clear milestones.

What I will not support is a package that tells workers:
“Make concessions now, keep the plants open for the moment, but the shutdown option stays in the drawer.”

That is not social partnership. It is a delayed shutdown logic.

### 3) The burden-sharing discussion is still too asymmetric
I agree with the union and works council that workers cannot be the only adjustment variable.

If labor is expected to contribute through:
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- voluntary exits,
- and moderation on cost,

then management, owners, and capital must also commit to:
- real investment in German sites,
- product allocation,
- overhead reduction at the top,
- and stable governance that respects co-determination.

I will not support a one-sided restructuring where the workforce absorbs the pain while everyone else preserves escape routes.

## To management
You ask how fixed cost and capacity can be reduced without closures.

The answer is: through a deep non-closure restructuring that is not cosmetic and not temporary. That means:
- shift reductions,
- line rationalization,
- product concentration,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- indirect cost cuts,
- working-time changes,
- and productivity improvement.

What it does **not** mean is shutting plants and calling that realism.

You still need to show, with numbers, why the non-closure package cannot work before you ask for a closure fallback. Until then, closures are not a serious first answer.

## To the union and works council
I agree with you that no compulsory redundancies and no hidden shutdown logic are essential if the company wants social peace.

But I also need to be honest: a viable non-closure path must be operationally real. It has to remove capacity and cost in a durable way.

So yes, I support:
- shift and line reductions,
- product concentration,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- and productivity measures.

But these must be the restructuring itself, not a bridge to later shutdowns. On that point, I stand with you.

## To the controlling family and investors
You want credibility and discipline. So do I.

But credibility is not created by threatening plant closures. It is created by:
- a viable production plan,
- good products,
- investment,
- skilled labor,
- and social peace.

Destroying trust destroys value. That is especially true in a premium industrial company with a strong regional footprint.

If you want value preservation, then support:
- binding site guarantees,
- investment in German plants,
- product commitments,
- and governance that respects co-determination.

## To the state government
The state is right to reject closures as a default route. But that position must be backed by action.

Use the state’s role to support a non-closure restructuring model that is:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- and serious enough to work.

Do not ask workers for concessions while leaving the closure option alive as the hidden endgame.

And let me say this clearly: I will not support any outcome that guts regional employment or sidelines the state’s co-determination and veto role. That is part of the legitimacy of this governance arrangement.

## On the expert assessment
The expert input was useful because it confirmed what we should already know:
- non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings,
- but they may not fully replicate closure economics unless the package is very deep,
- and the real issue is whether the company can get enough structural change **without** destroying sites.

That does **not** mean closures are necessary. It means the non-closure package must be taken seriously and fully quantified.

So I support the call for a comparison of:
- closure-heavy path,
- deep non-closure path,
- and any hybrid scenario only if it does **not** turn closures into the hidden baseline.

## What I am prepared to support
I am prepared to support a **deep, enforceable non-closure restructuring** if it includes all of the following:

1. **No plant closures in Germany in the settlement period**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding capacity reduction through non-closure measures**
4. **Working-time and deployment flexibility**
5. **Voluntary exits and internal redeployment**
6. **Product concentration and site specialization**
7. **Real investment commitments for German sites**
8. **Fair burden-sharing from management and owners**
9. **A binding role for co-determination in implementation**

That is a serious settlement and a defensible one.

## What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- closures as the default or hidden endgame,
- forced layoffs,
- a weakening of collective bargaining protections,
- or any deal that treats the state’s co-determination role as ornamental.

If the company wants a durable solution, it must be built around the existing German industrial base, not against it.

## My position in one sentence
I will back a tough restructuring that protects jobs and plants in our state, preserves the company as a regional anchor, and restores competitiveness through negotiated non-closure measures — **but not a settlement whose logic remains plant closure by another name**.

If management, owners, and investors want my support, bring a quantified package that proves the non-closure route can work on its own merits. Otherwise, I will oppose any closure-based path.
