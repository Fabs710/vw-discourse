I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family holding, and the investors on one basic point: **the company needs a real restructuring, not a symbolic one**. The current cost base, capacity situation, and margin profile are not sustainable forever. We also agree that:
- investment in future products and technology is essential,
- a negotiated solution is preferable to open conflict,
- and the company cannot simply preserve every structure unchanged.

I also agree with the union and works council that **forced redundancies should be avoided if a credible alternative exists**. That is important not only socially, but for industrial peace and long-term performance.

## Where I disagree
### 1) I do not accept closures as the default answer
Management is treating plant closures as the benchmark of seriousness. I do not accept that.  
From the state’s perspective, **German plant closures are a last resort, not a starting point**. They would damage regional employment, supply chains, skills, and the company’s role as an industrial anchor.

If management wants to argue that closures are unavoidable, it must first demonstrate that:
- non-closure measures were genuinely exhausted,
- the same savings cannot be achieved otherwise,
- and the regional damage is truly unavoidable.

That burden of proof has not been met here.

### 2) I do not accept “no closures” as empty rhetoric — but it must be matched with a real alternative
To the works council and union: I hear your red line, and I share the goal of protecting sites and jobs. But a red line is only durable if it is paired with a workable restructuring path. A blanket refusal without a concrete operating model leaves the company vulnerable.

So I need to be equally clear: **protecting every site is only defensible if the alternative is economically credible**. That means I want to see a package that actually reduces fixed costs and capacity through other instruments, not just slogans.

### 3) I do not accept one-sided burden shifting onto labour
I agree with the union that employees should not be made the sole adjustment variable. That would be unfair and politically reckless. If there is to be a labour contribution, it must be part of a broader package involving:
- management restraint,
- owner contribution,
- investment commitments,
- and a serious industrial strategy.

But I also agree with management and investors that the workforce contribution cannot be purely symbolic. It must have real economic effect.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a restructuring that:
1. **preserves German sites wherever possible**
2. **avoids compulsory redundancies**
3. **uses capacity reductions, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, and qualification instead of closures where feasible**
4. **includes a negotiated employee contribution**
5. **includes binding product and investment commitments**
6. **protects co-determination and the state’s role in governance**
7. **is measurable, enforceable, and durable**

That is the middle ground I can stand behind — but only if it is a real package, not a postponement.

## What I cannot accept
I cannot accept:
- a restructuring that guts regional employment,
- a plan that sidelines the state or weakens its veto and co-determination role,
- or a deal that restores margins only by hollowing out the company’s domestic industrial base.

I also cannot accept a package that says “no closures” while offering no credible mechanism to remove overcapacity and cost. That would not be serious either.

## On the numbers
Management keeps asking for proof that non-closure alternatives can match closure savings. Fair enough — then let us move the discussion from slogans to quantified options.

What I would expect next is:
- a clear cost-gap figure,
- a scenario comparing closure and non-closure measures,
- a quantified employee contribution,
- quantified owner/management commitments,
- and an investment plan tied to site retention.

If needed, we should not hesitate to bring in an independent industrial restructuring expert to test the scenarios objectively.

## Bottom line
The state will not back a plan that makes plant closures the first resort or treats regional employment as disposable.  
But we are also not interested in a cosmetic compromise that leaves the company uncompetitive.

So my position is:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring.
- **Yes** to negotiated burden sharing.
- **Yes** to capacity and cost reduction through non-closure measures first.
- **No** to closures as the default instrument.
- **No** to forced redundancies if there is a credible alternative.
- **No** to any outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and blocking role.

Bring forward a quantified, enforceable alternative that protects the industrial core in Germany and restores competitiveness, and we can support it.  
If not, then management is asking us to choose the politically and socially destructive path when other avenues have not been fully tested.
