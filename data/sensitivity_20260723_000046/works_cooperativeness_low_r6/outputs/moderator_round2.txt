## Core impasse

The discussion is no longer about whether restructuring is needed. The impasse is over **the means of achieving it**:

1. **Management and capital-side stakeholders** want a package that is **economically equivalent to closure-based savings** and are unwilling to accept a plan that preserves all sites and all current guarantees without hard capacity and cost reduction.
2. **Works council and union** treat **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies** as hard red lines and reject closure-based savings as the benchmark.
3. **The state government** wants to avoid closures but also needs a package that is **quantified, enforceable, and credible**.
4. **The expert input** has clarified that non-closure measures can cover a substantial share of the gap, but likely not all of it unless the package is deep and includes a backstop.

So the main blockage is this:  
**Each side wants the other side to prove feasibility first, while none is yet accepting the full cost of a binding compromise.**

## Facilitator proposals: possible bridge positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-closure pact with measurable milestones
A possible compromise framework is a **time-bound non-closure restructuring package** for Germany that:
- keeps plants open for a defined initial period,
- excludes compulsory redundancies during that period,
- includes concrete capacity and cost-reduction targets,
- and contains a pre-agreed review point with automatic escalation if targets are missed.

This could bridge:
- labor’s insistence on avoiding immediate closures,
- management’s demand for structural savings,
- and the state’s preference for a non-closure path first.

### Proposal 2: Site retention with functional consolidation
A second compromise could preserve all German sites formally, while allowing:
- product concentration,
- shift reductions,
- line consolidation,
- internal redeployment,
- and conversion of some sites to narrower strategic roles.

This would aim to satisfy:
- the works council and union on site continuity,
- management on capacity reduction,
- and investors on measurable cost relief.

### Proposal 3: Shared-sacrifice package with enforceable commitments on all sides
A third bridge is a package in which:
- labor contributes through wage moderation, flexibility, and voluntary exits,
- management contributes through overhead reduction and product allocation commitments,
- owners contribute through binding investment and capital support,
- and the state supports governance stability and monitoring.

This would convert “shared sacrifice” from a slogan into a structured burden-sharing arrangement.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are non-negotiable, **what specific structural measures would you accept that remove enough fixed cost and capacity to make that position economically credible by 2030?**

### To the Union
What level of **measurable labor contribution**—in wage, flexibility, hours, or voluntary exits—would you regard as sufficient to justify a no-closure settlement?

### To Management
Can you specify the **minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction** you require, and which of those can be achieved **without immediate closures** if the other side delivers a deep package?

### To the Regional State Government
What concrete governance or policy action are you prepared to take to support a **non-closure restructuring**, and under what measurable conditions would you accept escalation to harder measures?

### To the Controlling Family
Are you prepared to support a package that prioritizes **site retention and investment**, even if it requires accepting a slower or more complex route to margin recovery?

### To the Institutional Investors
Would you back a **time-limited non-closure deal** if it included hard milestones, a quantified savings target, and an automatic fallback mechanism if performance misses plan?

### To the Expert
What is the **most realistic threshold** at which non-closure measures become insufficient and the package would need a closure backstop to remain financially credible?
