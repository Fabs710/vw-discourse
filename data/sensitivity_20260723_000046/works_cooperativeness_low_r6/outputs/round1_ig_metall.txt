The works council is right on the core principles, and I will state our position just as clearly:

**We will not accept plant closures in Germany.**  
**We will not accept compulsory redundancies.**  
**We will not accept that the workforce carries the entire burden of management’s strategic failures.**

If this restructuring is to be serious, it has to be **fair, collective, and durable**. That means:

- **No forced layoffs**
- **No closure of German sites**
- **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
- **A negotiated contribution from employees only as part of a broader burden-sharing package**
- **Management, owners, and capital must also contribute**

Where I agree with the works council:
- We need competitiveness.
- We need a viable future for the plants.
- We need investment in new products and technologies.
- We need solutions that are socially responsible and not just financial shortcuts.

Where I disagree with any attempt to push through the current approach:
- A “deep structural cut” built on closures and job destruction is not an acceptable starting point.
- “Durable cost reduction” cannot mean permanent insecurity for workers.
- It is not fair to ask employees for wage restraint or concessions while management protects its own room for maneuver and owners remain shielded.

Let me be equally clear: **if management insists on closures, mass layoffs, or tearing up established wage standards, then it will face resistance.** The peace obligation has ended. The workforce is mobilized. Warning strikes are only the beginning if necessary.

We have already shown openness to alternative measures:
- capacity reductions instead of closures,
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and transformation measures,
- and an employee contribution if, and only if, it is matched by real commitments from management and ownership.

That means a credible deal must include:
1. **Site guarantees**
2. **Job guarantees without compulsory redundancies**
3. **Shared sacrifice across all stakeholder groups**
4. **A binding investment and product commitment**
5. **Respect for collective bargaining standards**

So our message to management is simple:  
**Come back with a plan that preserves the industrial core, protects jobs, and distributes the pain fairly — or do not expect cooperation.**
