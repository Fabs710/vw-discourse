I appreciate the expert assessment, because it clarifies the real issue: **non-closure restructuring may cover part of the gap, but not automatically all of it**. That matters.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the investors, and the expert on one fundamental point: **the company needs a durable, structural improvement in competitiveness and margins**. A symbolic compromise or a short-term truce would be value destructive. That is not acceptable.

I also agree that:
- investment in future products and technologies is essential,
- workforce contributions can be part of a serious package,
- and a negotiated solution is preferable to a prolonged confrontation.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures should be treated as the default benchmark of seriousness.

The expert’s point is useful, but it does **not** prove that closures are necessary now. It says:
- non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings,
- the real answer may be a hybrid,
- and a backstop may be needed if targets are missed.

That is materially different from saying “close plants first.”

So I reject any attempt to use the expert assessment as a blank cheque for immediate closures.

## What the expert actually supports
What I take from the assessment is this:
1. **There is room for a serious non-closure package.**
2. **That package must be deep and enforceable.**
3. **If it misses milestones, further action must follow.**

That is a reasonable governance approach. It is not an argument for doing nothing, and it is not an argument for immediate plant shutdowns either.

## What I would support
I would support a restructuring package that includes:

- binding capacity reduction,
- labor-cost contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment and voluntary exits,
- product consolidation and site specialization,
- clear investment commitments,
- and a hard review point with consequences if targets are missed.

That is the kind of package that can preserve value, restore competitiveness, and avoid unnecessary instability.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a plan that jumps straight to closures without exhausting credible alternatives,
- a settlement that leaves the company structurally weak,
- or a cosmetic deal that protects every legacy arrangement while pretending the numbers have been fixed.

I also will not support an outcome in which governance control is weakened or the owners are sidelined by a crisis-driven drift into bad decisions.

## To management
You now have a technical basis for a non-closure path. Use it seriously. I expect a quantified proposal that shows:
- how much savings come from labor-cost measures,
- how much from capacity consolidation,
- how much from productivity,
- and what the trigger is if the package underperforms.

If you cannot produce that, then we are not yet at a decision point. But if you can, then I am prepared to consider a non-closure restructuring as the preferred route.

## To the works council and union
Your red line against closures is understood, but it is not enough on its own. If you want the company to avoid closures, then you must engage with the hard part:
- real flexibility,
- real productivity gains,
- real labor-cost contribution,
- and acceptance that some current structures may not survive unchanged.

If the package is purely defensive, it will not be credible. And if it is not credible, the pressure for harsher measures will return.

## To the state government
Your role is important, but “no closures” cannot become a veto on any structural change. I agree closures should not be the first resort. But the state should support a tough, measurable alternative — not simply block the conversation.

## Bottom line
My position is now clear:

- **Yes** to a durable restructuring.
- **Yes** to a deep non-closure package if it is credible and enforceable.
- **No** to premature plant closures.
- **No** to cosmetic compromise.
- **No** to any outcome that permanently weakens the company’s value or governance control.

So the next step should not be more slogans. It should be a **quantified restructuring package with milestones, enforcement, and a backstop** if the targets are missed.
