I will respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, and the investors on the central economic point: **this company needs a durable restructuring that restores competitiveness and margins**. Cosmetic measures will not do it. A deal that merely postpones the problem would destroy value, and as controlling owner we cannot support that.

I also agree that:
- the workforce should contribute,
- non-closure measures can deliver meaningful savings,
- and any package must be measurable and enforceable.

So the question is not whether restructuring is needed. It is how serious the restructuring must be to be credible.

## Where I disagree

### 1) A blanket “no closures” rule is too rigid
To the works council, union, and state: I understand the political and industrial logic behind protecting sites. But I do **not** accept that “no plant closures” can be treated as an absolute starting point if it prevents the company from removing enough cost and capacity.

A position that preserves every site regardless of economics is not stewardship. It risks simply postponing the same crisis, with higher costs later.

### 2) A closure backstop is not just an abstract threat
At the same time, I cannot accept a package that has no credible consequence if it underperforms. The expert has been clear that non-closure measures can cover a substantial part of the gap, but usually not all of it unless they are exceptionally deep.

So I am not prepared to support:
- a purely defensive no-closure package,
- no meaningful capacity reduction,
- and no enforcement if targets are missed.

That would be value-destructive delay.

### 3) Social peace matters, but it is not a substitute for economics
To the state and employee side: trust, skills, and industrial peace matter a great deal. But they do not eliminate the hard arithmetic. If the business remains structurally too expensive, then social comfort today becomes a deeper crisis tomorrow.

## On the expert’s assessment
The expert’s analysis is useful because it confirms what governance already suggests:
- non-closure tools can deliver real savings,
- but they usually do **not** fully replicate closure economics unless they are deep, front-loaded, and structurally binding,
- and if the remaining gap stays large, some form of escalation mechanism is economically justified.

That is not a threat for sport. It is the basic logic of credible restructuring.

## What I would support
I am prepared to support a **non-closure-first** restructuring only if it includes all of the following:

1. **Binding capacity reduction**
2. **Real labor-cost contribution**
3. **Working-time and deployment flexibility**
4. **Voluntary exits and internal redeployment**
5. **Product concentration and site specialization**
6. **Clear investment commitments**
7. **Milestones with automatic escalation if targets are missed**

That is a serious route. It is not a surrender to the status quo.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a plan that simply declares all German sites untouchable,
- an arrangement that excludes compulsory redundancies absolutely without sufficient structural adjustment,
- or a package that leaves the company uncompetitive and then asks the owners to absorb the erosion indefinitely.

I also will not support any outcome that weakens ownership control or sidelines the governance rights of the family holding.

## To management
You now need to quantify the proposal properly:
- how much cost comes out,
- how much capacity comes out,
- what the workforce contribution is,
- what the investment commitments are,
- and what happens if the package misses its targets.

That is the next step. Not more slogans.

## To the works council and union
If you want to avoid closures, then engage with the hard part:
- real flexibility,
- real productivity improvement,
- real labor-cost contribution,
- and acceptance that some current structures may not survive unchanged.

A no-closure stance is only credible if it is paired with a restructuring deep enough to work.

## To the state government
Your role is important, but a political preference against closures is not a substitute for a workable restructuring. I am open to a non-closure path first. I am not open to a deal that leaves the company structurally weak.

## Bottom line
The family holding supports a **durable, enforceable restructuring** that restores competitiveness and margins.

We are open to a deep non-closure solution — but only if it is economically credible, measurable, and backed by consequences if it fails.

If not, then we are simply delaying the same problem at greater cost.
