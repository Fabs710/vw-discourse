### Core impasse
The central blockage is not whether restructuring is needed. All sides accept that it is.

The impasse lies in **the enforcement mechanism and end-state**:
- **Employee side / state**: “No closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no hidden shutdown path.”
- **Management / owners / investors**: “A non-closure package is only acceptable if it is strong enough to deliver hard savings, and there must be a credible consequence if it misses targets.”

So the real disagreement is:
1. **Whether closures may remain as a fallback**, and  
2. **Whether a non-closure package can be considered sufficient without that fallback.**

A secondary impasse is the **benchmarking dispute**:
- labor and state reject closure economics as the reference point,
- capital and management insist that alternatives must be measured against what closures would save.

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals

#### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure phase with milestone review
A structured compromise could be:

- **No plant closures for an initial protected period**  
- **No compulsory redundancies during that period**
- **Binding capacity, labor-cost, and productivity measures** implemented immediately
- **Independent quarterly review of savings and capacity reductions**
- **Pre-agreed escalation path after the review period** if milestones are missed

This preserves a genuine no-closure phase while giving capital and management an enforcement structure.

---

#### Proposal 2: “No closure backstop” replaced by “restructuring escalation menu”
Instead of an automatic closure fallback, the parties could agree to an escalation ladder:

1. deepen voluntary exits  
2. increase working-time and shift reductions  
3. expand redeployment and product concentration  
4. add owner/management contributions  
5. only then move to a separately defined decision point for unresolved residual gap

This may reduce the sense of an immediate shutdown threat while still addressing the demand for enforceability.

---

#### Proposal 3: Site-by-site settlement with differentiated treatment
A single blanket rule may be too rigid. A compromise could be:

- sites with clear future product allocation receive stronger guarantees
- weaker sites receive stronger restructuring obligations and tighter review metrics
- investment commitments are tied to each site’s role in the product plan
- no closures are off the table during the protected phase, but any later step must be site-specific, evidence-based, and jointly reviewed

This can bridge the gap between the demand for site protection and the demand for economic realism.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
If a no-closure deal is offered with binding savings, capacity reduction, and milestone enforcement, **what specific measurable trigger would you accept as sufficient proof that restructuring is working without reopening closure options?**

#### To the Industrial Union
If management accepts no compulsory redundancies and substantial worker protections, **what exact mix of labor contributions and flexibility measures are you prepared to quantify in euro terms to close the structural gap?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
If the employee side delivers a deep, measurable non-closure package, **what residual gap threshold would you accept before insisting on a closure backstop, and how will you define it transparently?**

#### To the Regional State Government
If closures are politically rejected, **what concrete enforcement and support tools will you provide to make a non-closure package credible rather than merely aspirational?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
If a non-closure package produces most, but not all, of the required savings, **would you support a staged solution before any closure decision, and what governance safeguards would you require?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
If the package is deep but not fully equivalent to closure economics, **what minimum annual recurring savings and capacity reduction would make it investable for you without immediate closure rights?**

#### To the Independent Expert
What is the **lowest technically credible savings threshold** for a non-closure package to be considered durable enough for a multi-year settlement, and what indicators would show that it is failing early?
