Management is asking the workforce to pay for a crisis we did not create, and it is doing so by threatening the very foundations of our industrial relations: plant security, job security, and trust. Let me be absolutely clear at the outset:

- **No plant closures in Germany.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

If management wants restructuring, then it must be **restructuring with the workforce, not against it**. We are prepared to discuss alternatives that secure competitiveness **without destroying jobs and sites**: capacity adjustments, smarter working-time solutions, internal redeployment, qualification and transformation measures, productivity improvements, and a fair, negotiated contribution from all sides. But there will be **no blank cheque** for closures, mass layoffs, or unilateral cost-cutting at the expense of employees.

Our priorities are straightforward:

1. **Protect every German plant.**
2. **Guarantee every current job, with no forced layoffs.**
3. **Preserve co-determination and real employee influence over any restructuring path.**
4. **Secure investment in future products and technologies so that sites remain viable through 2030 and beyond.**

If management is serious about the future, then it must present a plan that is:
- **binding**
- **reversible where possible**
- **socially responsible**
- and **negotiated on equal footing**

A fait accompli is not acceptable. We will resist closures and forced job cuts with all the means available to us, including industrial action and the use of our co-determination rights. If there is to be a solution, it must protect workers and sites first — not after the damage is done.
