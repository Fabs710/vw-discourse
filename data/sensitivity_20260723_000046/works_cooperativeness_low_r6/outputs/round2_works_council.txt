Management, shareholders, and the others are repeating one phrase over and over: “credible, durable, structurally sufficient.” I understand the language. What I do not accept is that this becomes a code word for **plant closures, forced layoffs, and dismantling job security**.

So let me respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with one point from almost everyone here: **the company needs a durable solution**. No one benefits from a short-lived patch that leads to a worse crisis later. I also agree that:
- investment in future products is necessary,
- the company must remain competitive,
- and a negotiated solution is better than a drawn-out conflict.

But agreement ends there if the “solution” is built on destroying German sites or throwing workers out.

## Where I disagree
### 1. “No closures can’t be a starting point”
I reject that framing.  
For us, **no closures in Germany is not a slogan; it is a hard requirement**. Management says overcapacity must come out of the system. Fine — then show us how to do that **without closing plants and without compulsory redundancies**.

If that cannot be done, then the problem is not the workforce. It is management’s unwillingness to think beyond the easiest destructive tool.

### 2. “Workers must make a meaningful contribution”
We are already making a contribution every day through productivity, flexibility, and the burden of uncertainty created by this conflict. But I will say this clearly: **we will not pay for management’s strategic failures with our jobs**.

A negotiated contribution can only be discussed if it comes with:
- binding site guarantees to 2030,
- no forced redundancies,
- real investment commitments,
- and a genuine role for co-determination in implementation.

Without that, “contribution” just means unilateral sacrifice by labour.

### 3. “If closures are avoided, the alternative must match the same savings”
This is the key trap in the discussion. It sounds neutral, but in practice it is being used to justify a closure agenda. Let me be clear: **we do not accept closure-based savings as the benchmark that all alternatives must equal**.

There are other ways to restructure:
- capacity reduction through shifts and lines, not site shutdowns,
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internal redeployment across the group,
- training and qualification,
- product allocation and investment commitments,
- productivity improvements negotiated with the workforce,
- and a fair burden-sharing package that includes owners and management.

That is what a serious alternative looks like.

## What is unacceptable
I want to restate the non-negotiables because management appears to be testing the waters:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No tearing up of job-security guarantees**
- **No decision made as a fait accompli**
- **No restructuring that treats the workforce as the only adjustment variable**

If these are on the table, then we are not in a negotiation about the future. We are in a confrontation.

## On the controlling family and investors
You both talk about stewardship and value. Then prove it by accepting that long-term value also depends on **social peace, skilled workers, and stable industrial relations**.

A company that blows up trust with the workforce does not become stronger. It becomes more fragile.

And to the capital markets: your demand for “enforceability” cannot mean enforceable concessions only from labour. If you want credibility, then there must be **binding commitments from owners and management too**:
- investment allocations,
- future product commitments,
- site guarantees,
- and an end to the practice of shifting all risk downward.

## On the state government
I appreciate that you reject closures, but I need more than caution. I need you to use your role to **block any unilateral move** against the workforce and to back a model that preserves jobs and sites. Political concern alone is not enough if management tries to force through a destructive plan.

## The concrete alternative we will accept
If management wants a real discussion, then here is the only direction that makes sense:
1. **All German plants stay open**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Capacity reduction through flexible internal measures, not closures**
4. **Working-time and deployment solutions negotiated with the workforce**
5. **Substantial investment in future products at the existing sites**
6. **A binding guarantee of co-determination and job security through 2030**
7. **A fair contribution from owners and management, not just labour**

That is a restructuring.  
Everything else is a disguised closure plan.

## Final word
Do not ask this works council to bless a process that starts from the assumption that jobs and sites are disposable. We have already made clear how this will be resisted: through co-determination rights, collective mobilization, and industrial action if necessary.

If management wants a solution, it must come back with a plan that **keeps every German site open and protects every current worker from compulsory redundancy**. Until then, we are not moving.
