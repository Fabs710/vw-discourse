### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one central fault line:

**Can the company reach a durable, quantified competitiveness recovery without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**

Management, the family shareholder, and capital markets insist the answer may be no unless the remaining gap is proven small. Labor, the works council, and the state insist closures must not be treated as the default instrument and that any non-closure route must be fully quantified and binding.

So the impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed — everyone now accepts that it is.  
It is over **the acceptable instruments, the burden split, and the evidentiary threshold for declaring closures necessary**.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
Below are three concrete bridging options that could be tabled for discussion.

#### 1) Quantified no-closure test with fallback inside the footprint
A package where:
- all German sites remain open for a defined period,
- no compulsory redundancies occur during that period,
- labor, management, and ownership all contribute,
- and the company must hit pre-agreed savings milestones.

If milestones are missed, the fallback is **not immediate closure**, but:
- deeper productivity measures,
- additional voluntary exits,
- stronger overhead cuts,
- capacity reallocation,
- and an independent review of whether the remaining gap is genuinely bridgeable without shutdowns.

This gives management a credibility mechanism while preserving the labor side’s red lines in the first phase.

#### 2) Hybrid restructuring with protected sites and measurable capacity adjustment
A package that allows:
- capacity reduction,
- line changes,
- product reallocation,
- and non-core footprint adjustments,

but under a binding rule that:
- no site is closed unilaterally,
- no compulsory redundancies are used,
- and any further structural move requires a jointly reviewed quantified report.

This could satisfy the need for real structural savings without an open-ended closure mandate.

#### 3) Temporary concessions in exchange for legally binding investment and job security
A time-limited contribution package from labor, such as:
- wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- working-time flexibility,
- and voluntary exits,

paired with binding commitments from management and owners on:
- investment through 2030,
- product allocation,
- overhead reduction,
- and site security.

This would make the labor-side contribution contingent on enforceable industrial promises rather than on goodwill.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact remaining annual gap still cannot be closed after all non-closure measures, and what verified evidence shows that this residual gap cannot be bridged without plant closures?**

#### To the Works Council
**If closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table, what specific combination of labor, productivity, and capacity measures would you accept as sufficient to close at least 70% of the structural gap on a durable basis?**

#### To the Industrial Union
**What level and duration of employee contribution would you endorse if it were paired with legally binding no-closure and investment commitments through 2030?**

#### To the Regional State Government
**Under what quantified conditions would you move from blocking closures to supporting a staged restructuring, and what safeguards would you require before accepting any fallback mechanism?**

#### To the Controlling Family Holding
**Will you support a no-closure or hybrid package if it is independently verified to close most of the gap, even if it requires owners to accept lower distributions or stronger capital commitment?**

#### To the Institutional Investors
**Would you support a settlement that preserves all German sites if the package closes roughly 70–80% of the recurring gap and includes enforceable milestones, or do you require a residual closure option regardless of the quantified outcome?**

#### To the Specialist
**What specific measure mix would you identify as the minimum credible non-closure package, and what would be the red line below which the plan becomes financially non-viable?**
