## Core impasse
There are two incompatible decision logics on the table:

1. **Management / controlling capital logic**
   - The company must achieve a durable, quantified cost and capacity reduction.
   - Plant closures remain on the table if non-closure measures do not close enough of the gap.
   - Workforce-side measures alone are not assumed to be sufficient.

2. **Works Council / union / state logic**
   - German sites should be preserved.
   - No compulsory redundancies.
   - Closures are viewed as a trust-breaking last resort, not a legitimate default.
   - Any restructuring must be tied to binding site, investment, and governance guarantees.

So the impasse is not over whether change is needed. It is over **what level of structural adjustment is acceptable, and who bears the burden if the gap cannot be closed without closures**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals
Below are three concrete bridge options for discussion, framed as procedural proposals rather than a position.

### Proposal 1: Staged restructuring with trigger-based escalation
- Immediate package: voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, bonus restraint, redeployment, training, overhead reduction, and productivity measures.
- A jointly agreed savings target is set in quantified terms.
- If milestones are missed by a fixed date, pre-agreed fallback measures activate automatically.
- Those fallback measures could include deeper capacity cuts or site-specific consequences, but only after a formal review.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It allows labor to avoid immediate closure decisions while giving management and capital a credible enforcement mechanism.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with one or more “non-core” capacity adjustments instead of full closures
- Instead of full plant closures, one or more sites or lines are downsized, reallocated, or converted.
- The package combines capacity reduction with binding investment in future products and technologies.
- Workforce reductions occur only through voluntary, socially managed pathways.
- Site guarantees apply to the retained German footprint for a defined period.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It addresses the cost-gap requirement without immediately crossing the full closure red line.

---

### Proposal 3: Exchange of temporary labor concessions for binding industrial commitments
- Workforce side considers time-limited wage moderation, bonus restraint, or flexibility measures.
- In return, management and owners commit to:
  - no compulsory redundancies during the agreement period,
  - no unilateral site closures during that period,
  - a defined investment program,
  - and a governance review mechanism with state and co-determination oversight.

**Why this may bridge:**  
It creates a reciprocal structure: labor contributes now, while capital and management give enforceable security and investment clarity.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
What is the minimum quantified annual savings target that you require for a settlement to be acceptable, and what share of that target can be met without plant closures?

### To the Works Council
What specific, quantified capacity or cost measures are you prepared to accept if they preserve all German sites and avoid compulsory redundancies?

### To the Industrial Union
What is the maximum time-limited employee contribution the union would consider if it were tied to binding site security and a verified investment program?

### To the Regional State Government
Would the state support a staged deal with automatic fallback measures if agreed savings are not delivered, even if that preserves the possibility of later structural site action?

### To the Controlling Family
Would you commit to ruling out closures for a defined negotiation window if labor and management jointly deliver a quantified hybrid savings package?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a phased restructuring if it includes hard milestones, enforcement triggers, and a credible path to margin recovery without immediate closures?

### To the restructuring finance expert
What threshold of annual savings, on a durable basis, would make a non-closure or hybrid package financially sufficient rather than merely postponing the problem?
