Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

I hear the insistence on no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no changes to long-standing guarantees. But I cannot accept a restructuring that leaves the company with the same structurally uncompetitive cost base. That would not be a solution; it would be postponing the crisis.

We are facing a real and immediate competitiveness gap:
- German factory costs are too high,
- European demand is shrinking,
- competition is intensifying,
- and our brand is still missing its return-on-sales target.

If we do not make a durable structural correction now, we will have less room to invest in future products, software, and EV capability through 2030. That is the core issue.

So where I agree with you:
- I agree that the company needs a credible industrial plan.
- I agree that investment in future products and technology must remain part of the package.
- I agree that any settlement has to be durable, not cosmetic.

Where I disagree is fundamental:
- “No plant closures ever” is not a workable premise if there is permanent overcapacity.
- “No compulsory redundancies” may be possible only if we get sufficient voluntary, structural reductions and labour-cost relief elsewhere.
- Binding guarantees are only credible if they are paired with real economic improvement, not just continuation of the current cost structure.

To the Works Council: protecting every site regardless of economics is not a viable answer to a market reality that has changed. To the Union: temporary concessions and working-time measures may help at the margin, but they do not solve a structural cost problem on their own. We need a package that materially reduces fixed costs and capacity, not a short-term patch.

What management needs from this process is:
- a durable reduction in labour costs,
- a real reduction in overcapacity,
- and a settlement that restores medium-term margins.

I am open to discussing the route — closures, capacity reductions, productivity measures, working-time changes, redeployment, and other structural tools — but not to a result that avoids the hard decisions and still claims to fix the business.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced layoffs, then it must put forward an offer that is economically equivalent in effect and durability. Otherwise, I will be forced to continue pressing for deeper structural cuts.
