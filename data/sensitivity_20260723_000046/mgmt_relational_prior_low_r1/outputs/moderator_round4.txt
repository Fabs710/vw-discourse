## Core impasse

The negotiation is now blocked on one central issue:

**Whether any durable restructuring can be accepted if it preserves an explicit no-closure / no-forced-layoff guarantee, or whether management and capital require a residual closure mechanism to reach sufficient fixed-cost reduction.**

That single question is splitting the group into two camps:

- **Labor / works council / regional state**: closure fallback is a non-starter because it functions as a standing threat and undermines trust, co-determination, and settlement legitimacy.
- **Management / controlling family / investors**: a no-closure absolute is seen as financially insufficient unless the quantified package clearly closes most of the structural gap.
- **Expert view**: non-closure is only viable if it closes roughly 70–80% of the recurring gap; otherwise the package may be incomplete.

So the plateau is not about diagnosis anymore. It is about **which enforcement mechanism, if any, is acceptable when the numbers still show a residual gap**.

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: Milestone-based no-closure package with external verification
A possible bridge is a **binding no-closure package for a defined period** that includes:
- quantified savings targets by function and plant,
- external verification every 6–12 months,
- automatic escalation to a joint review if targets are missed,
- but **no automatic closure right** as the first response.

Instead, the review would require:
1. documented proof of missed measures,
2. a second-round search for non-closure savings,
3. and only then a jointly agreed escalation path.

This gives management and investors a credibility mechanism while preserving labor’s core demand that closures not be the default fallback.

---

### Proposal 2: Capacity reduction without full plant closure
A second bridge is to separate **capacity reduction** from **plant closure**.

This could mean:
- eliminating one shift,
- reallocating product lines,
- partial line consolidation,
- converting plants to narrower roles,
- or reducing site-specific fixed costs without shutting the site.

This may allow management to claim meaningful structural correction while allowing labor and the state to preserve the principle that German plants remain open.

---

### Proposal 3: No forced redundancies, but stronger shared sacrifice and governance
A third compromise is a package with:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no immediate closure fallback,
- stronger labor contributions for a limited period,
- sharper management overhead cuts,
- owner capital contribution,
- and binding investment commitments through 2030.

To address the concern about underdelivery, the package could include:
- a pre-agreed “second-step bargaining round” if targets are missed,
- but not a pre-authorized shutdown decision.

This preserves political and social acceptability while still giving capital a structured path to revisit the economics later.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact combination of non-closure measures closes the gap to at least the expert benchmark, and which specific costs remain uncovered after those measures are fully exhausted?**

### To the works council
**If a binding no-closure package closes most of the gap but leaves a small residual shortfall, what additional non-closure measures would you accept before you would consider the package incomplete?**

### To the industrial union
**Would you accept a time-limited, externally verified no-closure settlement if it includes stronger labor contributions, provided there is no automatic shutdown fallback?**

### To the regional state government
**Would you support a staged restructuring that preserves sites, if it includes strict milestone control and external verification but no standing closure corridor?**

### To the controlling family
**Is your primary requirement a specific savings level, or the preservation of a residual closure option — and if the former, what exact threshold would satisfy your governance duty?**

### To institutional investors
**Would you regard a no-closure package as credible if it is independently verified to close most of the recurring gap, even if it does not include a permanent shutdown fallback?**

### To the expert
**What residual gap is small enough to be treated as bridgeable by normal performance improvement, rather than as evidence that closure or equivalent capacity exit is economically necessary?**
