I will be direct.

I agree with several parts of what has been said, but I do **not** accept the employee-side position that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” can simply be declared as a starting rule and then treated as if it solved the restructuring problem. It does not. It only shifts the burden back onto the company’s future competitiveness.

## Where I agree
- **With management:** the company has a real structural cost and capacity problem.
- **With the specialists:** the issue is quantified economics, not slogans.
- **With the state:** a reckless, confrontation-first approach would destroy value.
- **With labor:** a cosmetic deal would be pointless, and future investment through 2030 must be protected.

## Where I disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
You keep saying that closures are illegitimate and that the alternative must preserve every German site. I understand the political and social instinct, but I do **not** accept it as a governing principle.

If the German footprint is structurally too expensive and overcapacity is real, then refusing closures in principle is not stewardship. It is a refusal to acknowledge the economic reality. A deal that preserves every site regardless of economics is not durable; it is deferred damage.

Your proposed tools — working-time changes, voluntary exits, redeployment, productivity, bonus restraint, limited employee contributions — may help. But I do not yet see proof that they are sufficient to close the gap on a durable basis. If they are not, then you are asking owners and management to carry the cost of preserving an uncompetitive structure.

### To management
I also do not want closure threats used as a negotiating club. That is counterproductive and can trigger the exact instability we want to avoid. If you want support, you need to present a clean, quantified case:
- the annual cost gap,
- what can be closed without closures,
- what remains,
- and why that remainder matters for margins and investment capacity.

Right now, I hear the diagnosis, but I still need the full quantified bridge.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect the state’s role and its blocking position. But “no closures unless alternatives are exhausted” cannot become a permanent shield against necessary restructuring. If alternatives do not materially close the gap, then the state should not help entrench an uncompetitive structure simply because the adjustment is difficult.

## My position
As controlling shareholder, I will not support:
1. a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value,
2. a package that leaves the company structurally under-earning,
3. or a prolonged conflict that weakens the brand and destroys cash generation.

I **will** support:
- a serious competitiveness program,
- a quantified and durable cost reduction,
- protection of future product and technology investment,
- and a settlement that restores margins on a credible path.

## What I need next
We need the discussion to move from principle to numbers.

I want management and the specialists to table:
- the actual annual cost gap,
- savings from non-closure measures,
- savings from capacity reduction without formal closures,
- the one-time cost of each route,
- and the margin / cash flow impact through 2030.

If the employee side believes closures can be avoided, then it must show that its package is not just socially preferable, but economically sufficient. If it cannot, then it should stop treating closure as illegitimate in principle and engage on the actual trade-off.

My bottom line is unchanged: I want a durable, value-preserving restructuring. I do **not** want a deal that protects every historical arrangement while leaving the core business uncompetitive.
