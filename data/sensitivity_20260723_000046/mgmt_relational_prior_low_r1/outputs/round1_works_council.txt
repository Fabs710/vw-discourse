As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price for a restructuring that management created through years of strategic mistakes.

Our priority is to secure every German plant, protect every viable job, and preserve the co-determination and job-security framework that has made this company strong for decades. If management wants change, then it must be through a negotiated, fair, and reversible path: capacity adjustments where needed, internal redeployment, working-time solutions, qualification, and a real industrial plan that protects sites and gives the workforce a binding future through 2030.

We are prepared to discuss measures that genuinely improve competitiveness, but only on the basis of:
- binding guarantees for all German sites,
- no forced layoffs,
- credible investment commitments for future products and technologies,
- and a real role for employee representatives in shaping the transformation.

If management comes with a fait accompli — closures, mass job cuts, or unilateral cost-cutting at the expense of workers — then it will face fierce resistance. We have already shown that the workforce is organized and ready to defend its future. We expect serious negotiation, not threats.
