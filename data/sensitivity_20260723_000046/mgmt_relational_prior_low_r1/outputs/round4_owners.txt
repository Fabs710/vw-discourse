I will respond plainly.

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone who says this cannot be a cosmetic deal. The company has a **real structural cost and competitiveness problem**, and any settlement has to be **durable through 2030**, not just a temporary peace arrangement.

I also agree that:
- a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value,
- future investment must be protected,
- and any contribution from labor should be matched by real contributions from management and ownership.

On that basic diagnosis, we are aligned.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side argument that “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” can simply be declared as non-negotiable principles and then treated as sufficient strategy. They are not a strategy. They are a constraint.

From a shareholder and governance perspective, the central question is not whether closures are politically unpleasant. It is whether the company can restore **margins, cash generation, and investment capacity** without them. If it can, fine. If it cannot, then pretending otherwise would be irresponsible.

### To the Works Council and Union
I hear the concern about trust, but you are still avoiding the core economic test. You keep saying closures are unacceptable. I am saying: **show me the quantified alternative**.

If a no-closure package really closes **70–80% of the structural gap**, then it deserves serious consideration. But if it does not, then you cannot expect owners and management to sign up to a structure that remains permanently under-earning simply because closure is politically difficult.

Your insistence that any residual gap must never lead to structural action is where I part company with you. That would make the company’s future hostage to a prior commitment to preserve every site regardless of economics.

### To the Regional State Government
I respect the state’s role and do not want a confrontation that damages the industrial base. But I also do not accept a framework where the state’s blocking position is used to freeze necessary correction indefinitely.

A state-backed settlement is only credible if it is tied to a real improvement in cost, capacity, and margins. If alternatives do not close the gap, then the state should not help entrench a weak structure.

### To management
I am aligned with you on the need for a real restructuring, but I am not prepared to back vague closure threats as a substitute for a quantified bridge. You still need to show:
- the actual annual cost gap,
- what non-closure measures can deliver,
- what remains,
- and why that remainder is fatal to competitiveness.

Without that, closure talk becomes bargaining posture, not governance.

### To institutional investors
I agree that a weak compromise is value-destructive. I also agree that prolonged conflict is value-destructive. But I will not support an outcome that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive just because it avoids hard choices today.

## My position
As controlling shareholder, my line is simple:

- I support a **durable competitiveness reset**.
- I support **shared sacrifice**.
- I support **binding savings and investment commitments**.
- I do **not** support a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value.
- I do **not** support a deal that preserves every historic arrangement while the business remains structurally too expensive.

That means I am open to a **serious hybrid package** if it is independently verified to close most of the gap on a durable basis.

But I am **not** prepared to accept:
1. a package that does not materially restore competitiveness,
2. a deal that simply defers the problem,
3. or a settlement that rules out any structural correction before the economics are tested.

## Bottom line
I am not here to defend closures for their own sake. I am here to defend the **long-term value of the core asset**.

If a no-closure package can genuinely restore competitiveness, I will support it.
If it cannot, then the discussion must stay honest about what structural measures are required.

What I will not support is a “solution” that protects every site in the short term while quietly eroding the company’s value, margins, and future room for investment.
