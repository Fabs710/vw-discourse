I will be equally direct from the state shareholder perspective.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family shareholder, and investors on one central point: **the company does need a durable competitiveness improvement**. A deal that merely postpones the problem would be irresponsible. I also agree that the future product pipeline, EV transformation, software, and investment capacity must be protected.

I further agree with the union and works council that **a prolonged industrial conflict would damage value**, jobs, and the company’s reputation. Nobody here benefits from a collapse into strike logic and public confrontation.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s implicit conclusion that **German plant closures and compulsory redundancies are the necessary starting point**.

That is too aggressive, too risky, and too one-sided for a company that is not just a balance sheet, but a major regional industrial anchor with a special governance structure and a social responsibility attached to it.

I also do **not** accept the capital-side framing that says the employee side must first “prove equivalence” while management keeps closures on the table as the default threat. That is not a neutral benchmark; it is a pressure tactic. The burden of proof is on management to show that every serious alternative has been exhausted before anyone even considers closing sites in Germany.

## To management
You keep saying the current structure is uncompetitive. I do not deny that. But you are still not demonstrating that closures are the only way to get there.

What I need from you is a **quantified restructuring path** that answers all of this clearly:
- How much cost gap must be closed?
- How much comes from productivity?
- How much from capacity reduction short of closure?
- How much from voluntary workforce adjustment?
- How much from management and overhead reductions?
- What investment is secured in return?

If you cannot show that closures are truly indispensable, then I will not support them. And because of the governance structure and the state’s blocking position, you should not assume you can simply force that result through.

## To the Works Council and Union
I support your core red lines:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no unilateral dismantling of guarantees,
- no sacrifice of German sites as the easy answer.

But I also need to be frank with you: saying “no closures ever” is politically understandable, yet on its own it will not close the strategic gap. If we want to preserve sites, we need a **serious negotiated industrial package**, not just a defensive refusal.

That means I would expect the workforce side to engage on:
- temporary and limited wage moderation,
- reduced bonuses or special payments,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits and retirement windows,
- redeployment and qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and capacity adjustments that do not amount to plant shutdowns.

I can support that kind of package if it is **balanced, bounded, and legally/bindingly linked** to site security and future investment.

## To the controlling family and investors
I hear your concern about value destruction, and I share it. But value is also destroyed by a restructuring that blows up the social compact, creates long conflict, and weakens the company as a regional industrial base.

You should not underestimate the political and governance realities here:
- the state has a blocking position,
- co-determination gives the workforce a real veto,
- and the company’s legitimacy depends on keeping this transformation socially sustainable.

So if your idea is that the easiest route is to push through closures and expect the political system to absorb the fallout, I will say clearly: **that will not work**. Not with me, and not under these governance conditions.

## My line
My position remains:
1. **No German plant closures as a first or default instrument.**
2. **No compulsory redundancies.**
3. **No dismantling of co-determination or long-standing site-security arrangements.**
4. **Yes to a tough, measurable restructuring if it protects jobs, preserves sites, and restores competitiveness.**

## What I would support
I would support a negotiated package built around:
- site guarantees for the German footprint,
- voluntary and socially managed workforce reduction,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- targeted labor-cost contributions that are time-limited and balanced,
- management and overhead discipline,
- productivity improvements,
- and binding investment commitments for future products and technologies.

That is the route that can preserve the company as a regional anchor **and** keep it viable.

## What I will not support
I will not support a package whose real logic is:
- close plants first,
- cut jobs first,
- and treat the region as collateral damage.

If that remains the proposal, then I will use the state’s blocking position accordingly.

### Bottom line
I am open to a tough restructuring. I am **not** open to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and regional employment as the price of management credibility.

If management wants support, it should table a **specific alternative package** with numbers, not just pressure. I would expect:
- quantified savings by measure,
- a binding investment track,
- clear site protections,
- and a burden-sharing formula that does not put the full weight on the workforce.

That is the serious next step.
