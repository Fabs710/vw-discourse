From the capital-markets side, the discussion is now where it should have been much earlier: **quantified, durable, and enforceable**. That is the right frame.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the family shareholder, the state, and the finance experts on one core point: **the company has a structural competitiveness problem**. If the current cost base remains materially above peers, then value erosion continues, and that is unacceptable.

I also agree that:
- a cosmetic settlement is worthless,
- prolonged industrial conflict destroys value,
- and any package must preserve the ability to invest through 2030.

So on diagnosis, there is no real dispute anymore.

## Where I disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
Your position is understandable, but from a shareholder perspective it is still incomplete.

You say:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserve every German site.

That may be a valid social objective, but it is **not yet a financial solution**. If those lines are non-negotiable, then you must prove — with hard numbers — that the alternative package gets the company back to competitive margins.

So far, the experts’ benchmark is the most important thing said in this process: a non-closure package is only credible if it closes roughly **70–80% of the recurring structural gap**. Below that, it is usually just delay.

That is the test. Not rhetoric. Not trust language. The test is whether the company can really restore:
- EBIT margin,
- free cash flow,
- and investment capacity.

If it cannot, then preserving every site does not create value; it preserves an expensive structure.

### To management
I agree with your refusal to accept a cosmetic compromise. But I do **not** accept closure rhetoric as a substitute for a fully transparent bridge.

You still need to show:
1. the actual annual structural gap,
2. savings from non-closure measures,
3. savings from hybrid measures,
4. residual gap if closures are excluded,
5. one-time restructuring costs,
6. and the impact on margins and cash flow through 2030.

Without that, “closures are necessary” is an assertion, not a proven conclusion.

I also share the concern that a rushed closure-led approach could create execution risk and industrial disruption. A bad closure is not value creation. But neither is a weak compromise.

### To the Regional State Government
I agree that a reckless move would be damaging. But I do **not** support a position that effectively freezes structural correction indefinitely.

A blocking position is not a business plan. If the no-closure route truly closes most of the gap, fine. If it does not, then blocking any meaningful adjustment simply preserves weak economics and postpones the problem.

So I support the state’s insistence on quantification, but not a permanent veto on hard correction if the numbers say it is needed.

### To the Controlling Family
I agree that value preservation matters. But value is not preserved by defending every historical arrangement or by letting the business drift into permanently subpar margins.

I also agree that closure should not be used as a negotiating club. But if the residual gap remains large after serious non-closure measures, then owners should not pretend that social discomfort alone is a reason to avoid structural action.

## What would move capital markets
What would actually move us is not a slogan on closures or no closures. It is a package with:
- quantified annual savings by measure,
- a credible run-rate target,
- durable labor-cost improvement,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- management overhead discipline,
- and a binding investment plan through 2030.

A staged or hybrid package is acceptable **only if it is not a delay tactic**. If milestones are missed, there must be a real enforcement mechanism. That does not have to mean immediate closure, but it does have to mean consequences.

## My position
I will not support an outcome that leaves the company:
- structurally uncompetitive,
- below target on margins,
- or underfunded for future investment.

I also will not support a prolonged strike or an open-ended fight that destroys cash flow.

So my support goes to **the most credible, durable, value-preserving restructuring**.

That means:
- if a non-closure package truly closes most of the gap, I can support it;
- if it does not, then management must be honest that harder structural measures remain on the table;
- but I will not endorse a cosmetic settlement that simply delays the pain.

## What I need next
Before I would move further, I want the specialists and management to table a concise comparison of:
1. the annual gap,
2. savings from non-closure measures,
3. savings from hybrid measures,
4. savings from closure-led restructuring,
5. one-time cash costs,
6. and the impact on EBIT margin and free cash flow through 2030.

Until that is on the table, I will not endorse a “no closures at any price” position, and I will not endorse a “closures first” position either. What I will endorse is the **most credible, durable, value-preserving restructuring**.
