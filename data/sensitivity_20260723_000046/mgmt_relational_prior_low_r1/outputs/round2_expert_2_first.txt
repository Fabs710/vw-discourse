Below is a decision-oriented restructuring comparison based on the facts provided, with explicit assumptions. Because no plant-level data has been disclosed, this is a **scenario model**, not a factual valuation.

## Executive view

A **non-closure package can be financially credible only if it closes most of the structural gap**. In large German automotive footprints, that usually means **at least ~70–80% of the annual gap on a durable run-rate basis**. If the alternative closes materially less than that, the company likely preserves labor peace in the short term but remains structurally under-earning, which weakens margins and investment capacity through 2030.

In most cases like this, the economics rank as follows:

1. **Closure-led**: highest recurring savings, highest one-time cost, strongest margin repair.
2. **Hybrid**: often the best risk-adjusted path if political/governance constraints are binding.
3. **Non-closure only**: lowest execution risk and lowest near-term cash outflow, but often insufficient unless concessions are unusually deep.

## 2) Side-by-side scenario comparison

### A. Closure-led package
Assumes one or more plant closures plus workforce reduction and some supporting labor measures.

**Annual recurring savings**
- **EUR 300m–700m+**
- Large site closure can contribute **EUR 150m–400m+ per site per year**
- Additional overhead and logistics rationalization may add more

**One-time cash costs**
- **EUR 400m–1.2bn+**
- Severance, decommissioning, write-downs, remediation, transfer costs, social plan costs

**Timing**
- Cash cost front-loaded over 12–24 months
- Savings ramp over 1–3 years
- Full run-rate often by year 2 or 3

**Margin impact**
- Strongest improvement in EBIT margin and breakeven volume
- Best structural fix if utilization is genuinely weak

**Risks**
- Very high labor/political execution risk
- Strike and governance risk
- Supply-chain disruption
- Long settlement timeline
- Reputational and capability loss if not carefully sequenced

**Assessment**
- Best if the cost gap is large and persistent
- Financially the most powerful tool
- But may be hard to implement under the described veto structure

---

### B. Non-closure package
Assumes no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies, using only working-time, voluntary exits, redeployment, productivity, overhead reduction, and temporary labor concessions.

**Annual recurring savings**
- **EUR 100m–300m** in a typical strong non-closure package
- Stretch case: **EUR 350m–450m** if concessions are unusually deep, voluntary attrition is large, and productivity gains are real
- However, the upper end is hard to sustain unless the footprint itself is also reduced in substance

**One-time cash costs**
- **EUR 100m–400m**
- Mainly severance for voluntary exits, training, redeployment, and possible bridge costs

**Timing**
- Faster to negotiate than closures if labor is cooperative
- Savings ramp earlier, but may be phased and partly temporary

**Margin impact**
- Helpful, but often insufficient if overcapacity is structural
- Fixed-cost absorption may remain weak if the plant footprint is unchanged

**Risks**
- Savings may be temporary or reversible
- Productivity assumptions may be overestimated
- Overcapacity remains embedded
- Risk of “defer and reopen” restructuring later

**Assessment**
- Financially credible only if it closes **most** of the gap
- In many real cases, it does not
- Good for liquidity preservation, weaker for long-term margin repair

---

### C. Hybrid package
Assumes no immediate full closure, but substantial capacity reduction, strong labor concessions, voluntary exits, selective site changes, and a trigger-based fallback to deeper action if targets are missed.

**Annual recurring savings**
- **EUR 200m–500m**
- Potentially more if one underperforming line or site is effectively downsized even if not formally closed

**One-time cash costs**
- **EUR 200m–700m**
- Lower than full closure, higher than a pure non-closure deal

**Timing**
- Faster than closure
- More durable than a pure voluntary package
- Can be structured in phases

**Margin impact**
- Often the best compromise between durability and execution feasibility
- Can materially improve EBIT without forcing immediate full site exits

**Risks**
- Requires tight enforcement
- If trigger mechanisms are weak, it becomes a delay tactic
- May leave residual overcapacity if the capacity cut is too shallow

**Assessment**
- Usually the best **risk-adjusted** solution when governance constraints are strong
- Most financeable if supported by binding milestones and fallback actions

## 3) What “economically equivalent” really means

The employee-side claim that a non-closure package can be equivalent to closures is only credible if it matches three tests:

### Test 1: Scale
It must close **at least ~70–80%** of the structural annual gap.

### Test 2: Durability
Savings must last **3–5+ years**, not just one bargaining cycle.

### Test 3: Capacity effect
It must reduce true fixed-cost burden, not merely defer payroll cost.

If it fails any of those, it is not economically equivalent to closure.

## 4) Illustrative NPV logic

Using simplified discounting:
- Assume 8–10% discount rate
- Assume 5-year horizon for decision comparison

### Closure-led
- High upfront cost, but largest annual savings
- Usually strongest NPV if the site is genuinely subscale or structurally uncompetitive

### Non-closure
- Low upfront cost
- Lower annual savings
- NPV can be positive if savings are substantial, but often less than closure-led unless labor concessions are unusually deep

### Hybrid
- Often best balance of NPV, risk, and governance feasibility
- Can outperform pure closure if it avoids strikes and preserves operating continuity
- Can outperform pure non-closure if it removes enough capacity

## 5) Cash flow and investment capacity through 2030

This is the key strategic issue.

### If the company chooses closure-led restructuring:
- Near-term free cash flow is pressured by restructuring charges
- Medium-term cash generation improves meaningfully
- Better chance of funding EV/software/capex internally by 2027–2030

### If the company chooses non-closure only:
- Near-term liquidity is protected
- But if savings are insufficient, operating cash flow remains weak
- That can crowd out future investment and force additional restructuring later

### If the company chooses a hybrid package:
- Best chance of balancing liquidity preservation with durable savings
- Most consistent with a situation where governance makes a full closure package hard to pass

## 6) Practical decision thresholds

From a turnaround perspective, I would use the following thresholds:

### Non-closure is acceptable if:
- It closes **~70–80%+** of the annual structural gap
- It is durable for multiple years
- It includes measurable headcount reduction and capacity reduction
- It includes a fallback trigger if targets are missed

### Hybrid is preferred if:
- Non-closure alone only closes **~40–70%**
- Closure is politically/governance-constrained
- The company can still achieve meaningful capacity reduction without formal shutdown

### Closure-led is justified if:
- The gap is large, persistent, and not bridgeable
- The site is structurally subscale
- The alternative package cannot credibly deliver the necessary run-rate savings

---

## 7) Key risk conclusion

The biggest financial risk is **not** that labor resists closures. The biggest financial risk is **agreeing to a weak non-closure package that preserves the footprint but leaves the company under-earning**. That outcome looks stable in year one and damaging by year three.

---

## Bottom line

### My finance judgment
- **Closure-led** is the strongest structural fix, but also the most disruptive and costly upfront.
- **Non-closure** can work only if the savings are deep enough to be truly economically equivalent; in most cases, that is hard to achieve.
- **Hybrid** is usually the most realistic and decision-useful package under strong governance constraints.

### Most likely outcome consistent with the facts given
A **hybrid package** is the most financeable path:
- meaningful labor contribution,
- voluntary workforce reduction,
- working-time flexibility,
- overhead and productivity cuts,
- selective capacity reduction,
- binding investment commitments,
- and a pre-agreed trigger for deeper action if the gap is not closed.
