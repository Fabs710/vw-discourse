The union shares the core red lines you have just stated, and I want to make them unmistakable from the collective-bargaining side as well:

- No plant closures in Germany.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- No dismantling of the collective wage framework.
- No restructuring where workers alone carry the burden.

Management’s current line is not an acceptable starting point if it means permanent job destruction, open-ended insecurity, or a one-sided wage squeeze. The workforce did not create the structural problems alone, and it will not pay for them alone.

That said, we are not refusing reality. We have already signaled willingness to negotiate real contributions from employees if — and only if — they are part of a balanced package. That can include:
- temporary or limited wage concessions,
- reduced bonuses,
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reductions through negotiated instruments,
- training and redeployment,
- and productivity measures.

But the condition is clear: there must be binding site security, protection of jobs, and a durable settlement with fair burden-sharing. If management insists on closures, mass layoffs, or tearing up guarantees while owners and executives merely talk about “competitiveness,” then there will be no social peace. We have the means to escalate further, and we will use them.

So my position is: we are ready for a tough, realistic deal — but only one that protects the German sites, protects the workforce, and distributes sacrifices fairly across labor, management, and ownership. If that is not on the table, then the conflict will intensify, not fade.
