I’ll be direct.

We are not here to preserve every historical arrangement as if the market had not changed. The company has a **real structural competitiveness problem**, and repeating “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” does not make that problem disappear. It only shifts the burden into the future while margins stay weak and investment is squeezed.

## Where I agree
I agree with the union, works council, and state on one point: **a cosmetic deal is worthless**. If this restructuring does not deliver a durable improvement in cost, capacity, and margins, then it fails.

I also agree that we must protect investment in future products, software, electrification, and technology through 2030. That is exactly why we need a credible restructuring now.

## Where I disagree
### 1) “No closures” as a blanket rule
That is not a solution. It is a veto on structural correction.

If German sites remain essentially intact while the cost base stays above target, then we will not restore competitiveness. We will simply preserve overcapacity and keep paying for it. The fact that closures are politically difficult does not make them economically irrelevant.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” without equivalent savings
I am open to avoiding forced layoffs **if** the alternative delivers equivalent, durable savings. But that has not yet been shown. Voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and productivity improvements may help — but on their own they are very unlikely to close a structural gap of the scale we are facing.

### 3) Turning “site security” into an absolute
Binding site guarantees are only credible if they are matched by a binding cost reduction. Otherwise, we are freezing an uncompetitive footprint and calling it social responsibility.

## To the Works Council
You say closures destroy trust. I understand that. But trust also collapses when the company cannot compete and then has no room left to invest or grow. A “defended” structure that remains too expensive is not protecting jobs; it is postponing the loss of jobs.

You ask for all German sites to remain open. I am telling you plainly: I cannot accept that as a starting condition. If that is the line, then you are asking management to guarantee the outcome before the economics are solved.

## To the Union
Your willingness to discuss temporary wage moderation, bonuses, flexibility, and voluntary exits is the right direction in principle. But your red lines remain too rigid for the scale of the challenge.

A few concessions around the edges will not do it. If the alternative package is to be acceptable, it must:
- materially reduce fixed costs,
- reduce capacity in substance,
- and do so on a durable basis.

If it does not, then it is not an equivalent alternative. It is delay.

## To the Regional State Government
You are right that a reckless closure-first approach would be irresponsible. I am not arguing for recklessness. I am arguing for **credibility**.

But your position cannot be: no closures, no forced redundancies, no real restructuring pain, and yet somehow a full competitiveness turnaround. Those objectives do not coexist unless the workforce side makes a far larger contribution than I am hearing so far.

If you want to use your blocking position constructively, then help produce a package that truly closes the gap. If not, you are defending a status quo the market will punish.

## To the controlling family and investors
I agree with you that drift and conflict destroy value. But I also want to be clear: we cannot run this company by preserving every site because closure is uncomfortable. That would be an abdication of fiduciary responsibility.

However, I also do not want a closure-first reflex that ignores execution risk and industrial disruption. What I need from capital is not slogans — it is support for a quantified path that restores margins and frees investment capacity.

## What I need to see
We are past generalities. I need a **quantified package**.

Specifically:
1. **What is the exact annual cost gap?**
2. **How much can be closed without plant closures?**
3. **How much comes from labor contribution, productivity, overhead, and capacity reduction?**
4. **What remains if closures are excluded?**
5. **What is the timing and durability of the savings?**
6. **What investment capacity is preserved or created through 2030?**

Until those numbers are on the table, “no closures” is not a restructuring plan. It is a political preference.

## My position
I remain prepared to negotiate a **hybrid or staged package** that avoids closures **only if** it delivers real, durable structural savings. That means:
- measurable capacity reduction,
- a genuine labor-cost contribution,
- overhead cuts,
- productivity gains,
- and a settlement that restores competitiveness, not just labor peace.

What I will **not** accept:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive,
- or a package that protects every site while starving future investment.
