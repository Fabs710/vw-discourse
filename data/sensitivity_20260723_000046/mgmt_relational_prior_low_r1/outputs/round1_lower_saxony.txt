As the regional state shareholder, I need to be very clear as well: I do understand the economic pressure on management, and I do not dismiss the competitiveness problem. But I cannot support a restructuring path that relies on German plant closures, mass job cuts, or the unilateral dismantling of the co-determination and site-security framework.

That is not just a political preference; it would directly damage the regional economy, erode trust, and weaken the company’s long-term industrial base.

Where I agree with management:
- The company does need a credible, durable restructuring.
- The current cost base and overcapacity cannot simply be ignored.
- The transformation toward future products and technologies has to be financed somehow.
- A purely symbolic or temporary fix would be irresponsible.

Where I disagree:
- “No plant closures ever” may not be a philosophical absolute in every case, but at this stage management has not shown that closures are the only viable path.
- I will not accept closures as the first or default instrument when alternatives like capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, internal redeployment, productivity gains, and negotiated cost contributions still exist.
- I will not accept compulsory redundancies as a restructuring instrument.
- I will not accept a package that shifts the burden primarily onto workers while owners and management keep full discretion and influence.

To the Works Council and the Union: I support your core red lines. Protecting sites and avoiding forced layoffs is entirely legitimate. But I also want to be practical: if we want to preserve the company as a regional anchor, then we need a settlement that is credible to the capital side as well. That means we cannot ignore the need for real efficiency improvements and a disciplined industrial plan.

My position is therefore this:
- No plant closure in Germany unless every serious alternative has been exhausted and the burden-sharing is demonstrably fair.
- No compulsory redundancies.
- Any labor contribution must be negotiated, limited, and paired with binding investment and site commitments.
- The state will insist on a real role in shaping the transformation, not being presented with a fait accompli.

If management wants my support, it needs to come forward with a package that preserves regional employment, protects viable sites, and restores competitiveness through a balanced approach. If the proposal is simply “close plants and cut jobs,” then I will not support it, and I will use my blocking position accordingly.

I would therefore urge management to table a concrete alternative built around:
- site guarantees,
- voluntary and socially managed workforce reduction,
- working-time and productivity measures,
- targeted cost relief negotiated with labor,
- and binding commitments to future investment in the German footprint.

That is the only route I see to a sustainable settlement that protects both the company and the region.
