**Moderator Summary – Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a **real competitiveness and margin problem**.
- There is shared recognition that the restructuring must be **durable**, not merely symbolic or temporary.
- All sides accept that the company must continue to **invest in future products, technologies, and transformation through 2030**.
- There is broad openness to discussing **non-forced instruments** such as:
  - working-time adjustments,
  - voluntary workforce reduction,
  - redeployment,
  - training and qualification,
  - productivity improvements,
  - and negotiated cost contributions.
- Several parties agree that any settlement should be **balanced** and should avoid unnecessary prolonged conflict.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works Council, Union, and Regional State Government reject closures as the default and, in some cases, categorically.  
  - Management, investors, and the family holding see closures as potentially necessary if overcapacity is permanent.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee-side actors reject them.  
  - Management and capital-side actors say they may become unavoidable unless equivalent reductions are achieved otherwise.
- **Scope of job/security guarantees**:  
  - Employee-side actors want binding site and employment security.  
  - Capital-side actors warn that open-ended guarantees may preserve an uncompetitive structure.
- **Burden-sharing**:  
  - Labor insists sacrifices must be shared across workers, management, and ownership.  
  - Capital-side actors want clear, quantified cost reduction and structural change before agreeing to concessions.
- **Role of governance and state influence**:  
  - The Regional State Government wants a strong role in shaping the outcome and opposes unilateral moves.  
  - Management and capital-side actors emphasize the need for a decision that can actually be implemented.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to reduce costs without plant closures or forced layoffs** in a way that is credible and durable.
- **How much labor-side contribution** is acceptable, and whether it can be sufficient to close the gap.
- **What level of capacity reduction** is needed, and whether it can be achieved through voluntary or operational measures alone.
- **How to protect future investment** while also delivering immediate cost relief.
- **What binding guarantees** on sites, jobs, and redeployment can be offered without freezing needed restructuring.
- **How ownership, management, and labor each contribute** in a way that is seen as fair and enforceable.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should move from positions to a **quantified package comparison**:
- What exact cost gap must be closed?
- Which measures can close it, and by how much?
- What is the minimum acceptable package for each side?
- Which commitments can be made **binding**, and which must remain conditional?

A useful next step would be for each side to table:
1. its **non-negotiable red lines**,  
2. its **maximum acceptable concessions**, and  
3. a **concrete package** that could close the competitiveness gap without triggering the outcomes it most wants to avoid.
