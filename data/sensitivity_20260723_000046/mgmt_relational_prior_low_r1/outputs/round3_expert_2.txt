## My finance view on sufficiency
For a German auto manufacturer with a structurally high-cost footprint, a **non-closure / hybrid package is only financially credible if it closes most of the recurring gap**. In practice, that usually means:

- **~70–80% of the annual structural gap** closed on a durable run-rate basis, and
- the remaining gap is small enough to be covered by volume recovery, mix improvement, or normal productivity.

If it closes only:
- **<60%**: usually not sufficient; it is likely a postponement.
- **60–70%**: borderline; depends on execution, demand, and whether savings are truly permanent.
- **70–80%+**: generally credible, if one-time costs and implementation risk are manageable.

## Why that threshold matters
A non-closure package has to do more than reduce pain. It must:
1. restore **EBIT margin** toward target,
2. preserve **free cash flow** after EV/product investment,
3. reduce the **fixed-cost base** enough to lower break-even volume,
4. and remain durable through 2030.

If it only produces temporary relief, it tends to fail when volume softens or pricing pressure returns.

## Typical savings mix without closures
A credible hybrid package normally comes from four buckets:

- **Labor-cost contribution**: roughly 15–35% of the gap  
  (wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits)

- **Overhead / productivity**: roughly 20–40%  
  (management layers, procurement, logistics, complexity, efficiency)

- **Capacity reduction without full closure**: roughly 10–25%  
  (downsizing, shift cuts, line reallocation, partial conversion)

- **Management / ownership contribution**: variable, but often needed to support acceptance and cash protection  
  (distributions, overhead discipline, capex phasing, governance commitments)

## Side-by-side decision logic
### 1) Closure-led restructuring
Best at resetting the cost base and fixed overhead, but:
- highest political / industrial conflict risk,
- highest one-time costs,
- higher execution and legal risk,
- bigger disruption to production continuity.

### 2) Hybrid / non-closure package
Can be financially sufficient if it reaches the 70–80% range, but:
- requires disciplined implementation,
- often depends on binding labor concessions,
- may leave some residual structural drag,
- and needs trigger mechanisms if savings underdeliver.

### 3) Minimal-change settlement
Usually fails the finance test:
- too little durable savings,
- margin remains below target,
- investment capacity stays constrained,
- and the problem reappears later.

## Practical conclusion
If management’s stated gap is real and durable, then a **non-closure settlement is only acceptable if it is almost a full solution, not a symbolic compromise**.

So the key test is not “closures or no closures.”  
The key test is:

**Does the package permanently close enough of the cost gap to restore competitive margins and fund 2030 investment?**

If not, it is not a restructuring — it is delay.
