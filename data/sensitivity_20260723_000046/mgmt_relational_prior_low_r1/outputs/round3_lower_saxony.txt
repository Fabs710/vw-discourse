I will be equally direct.

I agree with management and the finance specialists on one essential point: **this company needs a quantified, durable restructuring**. I also agree that a purely cosmetic deal would be irresponsible. If the package does not improve competitiveness and preserve future investment capacity through 2030, then it is not a solution.

But I do **not** accept the conclusion being pushed toward us that German plant closures must be the default, or that compulsory redundancies are somehow the only serious way to restore competitiveness.

That would be a very high-risk path for a company that is not just any industrial firm, but a central regional anchor with a special governance structure, a strong social partnership tradition, and a legitimate public responsibility.

## Where I agree
### To management
You are right that the company has a structural cost problem. You are right that drift is not acceptable. And you are right that the company needs a credible path to margin recovery.

### To the specialist
Your point is well taken: a non-closure package is only credible if it closes **most** of the gap on a durable basis, not just a small portion. That is the right benchmark.

### To labor
You are right that a deal built on distrust and unilateral action will not hold. A settlement that destroys legitimacy will be unstable, even if it looks tidy on a spreadsheet.

## Where I disagree
### With management
I do **not** accept that you have already proven closures are necessary. You have argued that they are preferable or easier, but that is not the same thing.

Before any German closure is even discussed, I need to see:
- the actual annual cost gap,
- the savings available from non-closure measures,
- the savings from management and overhead reductions,
- the savings from capacity reduction short of shutdown,
- and the remaining gap after those measures.

Right now, I do not see evidence that closures are the only economically rational route. I see pressure to make them seem inevitable.

### With the controlling family and capital side
I agree that conflict destroys value. But value is also destroyed by a restructuring that tears up the social compact, provokes sustained industrial conflict, and undermines the company’s legitimacy in its home base.

So I reject the notion that preserving regional employment is somehow a sentimental obstacle to rationality. For this company, regional legitimacy is part of long-term competitiveness.

### With the works council and union
I share your opposition to compulsory redundancies and your insistence on social partnership. But I want to be clear: simply saying “no closures ever” is not enough to close the strategic gap.

If we want to preserve sites, we need to be prepared for **real burdens**, including:
- temporary and limited labor-cost concessions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- productivity improvements,
- and management/ownership contributions.

That is the price of a serious alternative. But it must remain within a framework that protects sites and avoids forced layoffs.

## My line as state shareholder
I will not support a package that:
1. guts regional employment,
2. removes the state’s co-determination and veto role,
3. or treats German sites as disposable collateral.

That is not acceptable, and I will not drift toward it simply because it is repeated often.

At the same time, I will not support a purely defensive stance that refuses any serious restructuring contribution from labor, management, or ownership. The region’s long-term interest is not preserved by pretending the cost problem does not exist.

## What I need before I move further
The discussion now needs actual numbers, not slogans.

I want a package that answers:
- What is the quantified annual gap?
- How much can be closed without closures?
- What is the durable run-rate savings from voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, productivity, overhead cuts, and capacity reduction short of shutdown?
- What contribution is management prepared to make?
- What contribution are owners prepared to make?
- What binding investment commitments come in return?
- What safeguards keep the German sites open while the restructuring is implemented?

## My position on the mediator’s bridge proposals
### Proposal 1: Trigger-based escalation
Not acceptable if the trigger can lead to closures or forced layoffs. That would simply be a delayed threat.

### Proposal 2: Hybrid package with non-core capacity adjustments
This is the only direction worth serious discussion at this stage, but only if it genuinely preserves the German industrial footprint and avoids forced redundancies.

### Proposal 3: Temporary labor concessions for binding industrial commitments
This is discussable, and potentially the most realistic path forward — **if** the commitments are legally binding, the site protections are real, and the burden is shared fairly across workers, management, and ownership.

## What would move me
A credible package would need to include:
- a transparent cost-gap analysis,
- quantified savings by measure,
- a binding investment and product roadmap through 2030,
- management overhead and cost discipline,
- a fair employee contribution that is time-limited,
- and a firm commitment to preserve sites and jobs in the state.

If the alternative package can truly close most of the gap without closures, I will support it. If it cannot, then management must explain why it is asking the region to accept plant closures as the price of its strategy.

Until then, my position remains clear:
- **No German plant closures as the first or default instrument.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No weakening of the state’s co-determination and veto role.**
- **Yes to a tough, quantified restructuring if it protects the industrial base and restores competitiveness.**
