I will be direct.

I agree with the central diagnosis from management, investors, and — to a point — the state shareholder: **the company’s current German cost and capacity structure is not sustainable** if we want durable margins and the ability to invest through 2030. If we pretend otherwise, we will destroy value slowly but surely.

But I do **not** agree with the employee side’s attempt to turn “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” into a de facto veto on structural change. That is not a credible answer to overcapacity and a cost base that is above target and above competitors. A settlement that merely preserves the current economics under a different label is not acceptable.

## Where I agree
- **With management:** the business needs a real, quantified competitiveness program.
- **With investors:** prolonged drift or endless conflict would be value-destructive.
- **With the state:** a purely aggressive closure-first approach would be politically and socially risky under this governance structure.
- **With labor:** any deal must be durable and protect future investment capacity; a cosmetic cut would be a mistake.

## Where I disagree
### To the Works Council and Union
You are asking for:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site guarantees,
- and strong job protection,

but you are still not offering a package that clearly and durably closes the structural gap on the same scale.

Working-time measures, voluntary exits, redeployment, training, and temporary concessions may help, but I do **not** see evidence yet that these alone can deliver the necessary fixed-cost and capacity reduction. Saying “there are alternatives” is not enough. Show me a package that is economically equivalent in effect, durable in implementation, and credible to the market.

I also reject the implication that any plant closure is automatically illegitimate. If there is permanent overcapacity, then refusing even to consider closure is not stewardship; it is denial. That said, I do **not** want closures as a reflex. They are a last resort, not a slogan.

### To management
I support the need for hard decisions, but you also need to be careful not to overplay the closure card. If your proposal is perceived as a threat rather than a plan, you will trigger exactly the instability that destroys value. You need to present a **quantified path**, not just a demand for pain.

That means:
- what exact cost gap must be closed,
- what savings come from productivity,
- what from overhead,
- what from labor contribution,
- what from capacity adjustment,
- and what remains if no closure happens.

Without that, you are asking governance to endorse a leap in the dark.

### To the Regional State Government
I appreciate your insistence on process and your blocking position. But I do need to be frank: “no closures unless every alternative is exhausted” cannot become a permanent shield against necessary restructuring. If alternatives do not close the gap, then a refusal to consider closure simply transfers the cost to the future and makes the eventual adjustment worse.

## My governance line
As controlling shareholder, I will **not** back:
1. a restructuring that permanently impairs enterprise value,
2. a prolonged industrial conflict that weakens the company and its brand,
3. or a settlement that preserves overcapacity while calling it a compromise.

I **will** back:
- a serious competitiveness program,
- real and measurable structural cost reduction,
- protection of future investment,
- and a durable settlement that restores medium-term margins.

## What I need next
We are past broad positions. I want a **quantified package**.

Specifically, I want management and labor to table:
- the estimated cost gap,
- the savings from each proposed measure,
- what can be achieved without closures,
- what would require closures,
- and what binding concessions each side is prepared to make.

If the employee side believes it can avoid closures, then it must demonstrate that the alternative package is not just politically attractive but economically sufficient. If it cannot, then it should stop treating closure as illegitimate in principle and engage on the actual trade-off.

## My bottom line
I am open to a settlement that avoids unnecessary instability. I am **not** open to a deal that locks in structural uncompetitiveness.

If a credible, durable, quantified non-closure package exists, I will support it. If not, I will not shield the company from difficult structural decisions simply because they are uncomfortable.
