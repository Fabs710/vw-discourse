We agree on the diagnosis, and that matters. The company has a real competitiveness problem: excess capacity, a high German cost base, and margins that are not where they need to be. From a shareholder perspective, that means one thing above all: **the restructuring must be large enough and durable enough to restore competitive returns**. A deal that merely postpones the problem is value-destructive.

## Where we agree
- **No cosmetic settlement**: we agree with management, the family owners, and to a point the state, that this cannot be a symbolic reset.
- **Burden-sharing is necessary**: workers, management, and owners all have to contribute.
- **Investment in Germany matters**: future products and technology need to be funded by a stronger operating base.
- **Prolonged conflict is bad for value**: a long strike or extended public stalemate would damage earnings, execution, and the share price.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No plant closures” cannot be an absolute
We understand the political and social concerns, but from a capital markets perspective, a blanket no-closures rule is not credible if the footprint is structurally oversized. If some German capacity is uneconomic, then preserving all sites by principle means the cost problem remains and margins stay impaired.

That would not protect enterprise value. It would erode it.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” cannot be untouchable
We prefer voluntary exits, attrition, redeployment, and phased adjustment. But if the numbers do not close otherwise, then ruling out compulsory workforce reduction altogether is not compatible with a serious restructuring. The objective is not to protect every current position; it is to restore a viable margin structure.

### 3) Temporary concessions are not enough on their own
Working-time changes, short-term employee contributions, and reversible measures may help, but they do not solve a persistent structural gap. We will not back a settlement that relies on temporary relief while leaving overcapacity and labour costs basically intact.

## What we would support
We would support a package that clearly delivers:
- **material, durable cost reduction**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **a disciplined workforce adjustment**
- **a credible investment plan for the remaining German footprint**
- **margin recovery with a clear path back to return-on-sales target**

The instruments can vary:
- voluntary exits and natural attrition
- internal redeployment
- working-time and shift flexibility
- negotiated employee contributions
- targeted site consolidation
- and, where necessary, closure of structurally uneconomic capacity

We are not attached to any one tool. We are attached to the result.

## What is not acceptable
We would not support:
- a deal that preserves every site and all legacy guarantees while claiming competitiveness has been restored;
- a settlement that protects labour structures but leaves the cost base uncompetitive;
- or a process that produces conflict without decisive savings.

That would be a poor outcome for shareholders and, ultimately, for the company’s ability to invest and survive in a tougher market.

## To management
You are right that the solution must be durable. But durability cannot mean maximum disruption. It must mean a package that is credible, enforceable, and financially meaningful. If the proposal is too small, markets will see through it immediately.

## To the works council and union
Your insistence on social protection is understandable, but the economic test still applies. If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that is **equivalent in economic effect**. Not politically easier — economically equivalent.

## To the state government
Regional stability is important, but it cannot become a veto on structural adjustment. A preserved footprint with weak economics is not a long-term solution. The region is better served by a viable company than by a protected but deteriorating one.

## Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring
- **Yes** to burden-sharing
- **Yes** to investment in Germany
- **No** to cosmetic compromise
- **No** to an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired

If the eventual package credibly restores competitiveness and enterprise value, we will support it. If not, we will not pretend that a socially comfortable solution is economically acceptable.
