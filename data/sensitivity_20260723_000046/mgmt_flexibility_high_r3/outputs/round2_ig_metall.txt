We agree on the diagnosis: this company has a real competitiveness problem, real overcapacity, and cannot live on postponement. But we do **not** agree that the remedy must start with closures, layoffs, and a permanent weakening of collective standards.

## Where we agree
- A **durable** solution is necessary.
- The company needs a credible **industrial plan** and future investment in Germany.
- **Burden-sharing** is unavoidable.
- Purely cosmetic adjustments are not enough.

On that basis, we are still prepared to negotiate seriously.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Plant closures are not a neutral “option”
Management, owners, and investors keep saying closures “cannot be ruled out.” For us, that is exactly where the line is drawn.

A plant closure is not just an efficiency measure. It destroys jobs, communities, skills, and trust. If you begin from closures, you begin from breaking the social contract that has sustained this company.

You have not shown that every German site is uneconomic. What you have shown is a willingness to shift the burden onto the workforce first.

That is not acceptable.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable
We reject the idea that headcount reduction must include forced layoffs. If a restructuring only works by pushing people out, then it is not a balanced restructuring — it is a displacement plan.

We will not support workers being made to pay for management’s strategic failures through compulsory job loss.

### 3) “Durable” cannot mean permanent insecurity for employees
A durable settlement is not one where management secures permanent cost cuts while employees lose job security and wage standards.

If there is to be an employee contribution, it must be:
- negotiated,
- temporary,
- reversible,
- and matched by real contributions from management and owners.

That is the only form of fairness we will accept.

## To management
You are still treating closures and layoffs as if they are the serious language of restructuring. They are not the only serious instruments. Alternatives exist and must be tested before anyone talks about destroying sites.

What we expect to see on the table:
- working-time adjustments,
- shift and line reconfiguration,
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary wage or bonus moderation,
- and management-side contributions.

If you refuse those and go straight to closures, then this is not a negotiation — it is an ultimatum.

## To the family owners and investors
If you genuinely care about long-term enterprise value, then do not destroy the industrial and social base that makes this company viable.

A settlement that tears up the job-security framework may look neat on paper, but it creates strike risk, reputational damage, conflict, and long-term instability. That erodes value rather than protecting it.

## To the state government
We appreciate your stated support for regional stability and co-determination. But words are not enough.

If you mean it, then use your governance role to block a solution built on closures and compulsory redundancies.

## What a serious solution must include
We are not asking for the impossible. We are saying the company must choose a restructuring that protects the workforce and still delivers real savings:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Preservation of the collective wage framework**
- **Capacity reduction through working time, shifts, and product allocation**
- **Temporary, negotiated employee contributions**
- **Meaningful contributions from management and owners**
- **Binding investment commitments for Germany through 2030**
- **Full co-determination in implementation**

That is the minimum basis for industrial peace.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are open to:
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- temporary wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- flexibility in working time and shift systems,
- a fund or joint package to support restructuring,
- and voluntary instruments to reduce capacity without forced dismissals.

But only if the company gives binding security on jobs and sites. We will not sign up to a “shared sacrifice” where workers pay and management simply reserves the right to close plants later.

## Bottom line
If the proposal is:
- closures,
- forced redundancies,
- and permanent insecurity for labour,

then the union will oppose it decisively, including with industrial action.

If the proposal is a **real shared-sacrifice package** that keeps German sites open, protects jobs, and distributes the burden fairly, then we are ready to negotiate hard and constructively.

We are not blocking change. We are blocking a one-sided restructuring that makes workers absorb the pain while others keep the power.
