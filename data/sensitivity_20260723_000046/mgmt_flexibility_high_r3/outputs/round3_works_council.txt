Let me be very clear in Round 3: the line from the employee side has not changed.

We are prepared to negotiate a serious restructuring. We are **not** prepared to accept closures, compulsory redundancies, or the dismantling of the job-security framework as the price of that restructuring.

## To management
You keep saying “durable” means structural cuts, footprint reduction, and the possibility of closures. We say that is a one-sided definition of durability.

A solution is not durable if it creates permanent distrust, mass resistance, strike escalation, and damage to the company’s social and industrial foundations. You are treating the workforce as the adjustment variable of first resort. That is exactly what we reject.

You still have not demonstrated that:
- every German plant is uneconomic,
- forced layoffs are unavoidable,
- or that the same economic effect cannot be achieved through less destructive means.

Until you do, closures and compulsory redundancies remain unjustified.

## To the controlling family owners and investors
You all talk about “economic equivalence.” Fine — then show us the equivalence.

Do not simply assert that a plant closure is efficient. Prove that there is no combination of:
- working-time changes,
- shift and line reconfiguration,
- product allocation,
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- voluntary exits,
- management-side contributions,
- and temporary employee contributions

that can close the gap without destroying sites and jobs.

So far, what I hear is not proof. It is pressure.

And let me be blunt: long-term value is not protected by detonating trust and threatening co-determination. This company’s strength has never been just financial engineering. It has been skilled labour, stable social relations, and a governance model that prevents reckless decisions. If you tear that up, you weaken the company more than you strengthen it.

## To the regional state government
We appreciate your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. That is the right position.

But statements are no longer enough. If you mean “last resort,” then act like it. Use your governance role to stop any package that starts from closures or compulsory layoffs. The state cannot hide behind moderation while the workforce is pushed into accepting irreversible damage.

If you want a viable company and a stable region, then support a package that preserves all German sites, protects jobs, and commits to future production and investment here.

## To the union
We remain aligned on the fundamentals. The works council stands with the union in opposing any attempt to shift the burden onto workers alone. Any employee contribution must remain:
- negotiated,
- temporary,
- reversible,
- and matched by binding commitments from management and owners.

But I want to be equally direct: the union should not let anyone recast “economic equivalence” as a blank cheque for closures. That is the trap being set here. “Equivalent” must mean equivalent **without breaking the social contract**, not equivalent by destroying the workforce’s security.

## On the “comparative scenarios” the moderator asked for
I will answer clearly.

### What is non-negotiable
- **Every German plant remains open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No unilateral dismantling of job-security guarantees**
- **Full co-determination in shaping and implementing the restructuring**

### What we can discuss
- capacity reductions through **working time**
- **shift and line adjustments**
- **product allocation**
- **internal redeployment**
- **natural attrition**
- **voluntary exits**
- **temporary, negotiated employee contributions**
- **management and owner contributions**
- **binding investment commitments through 2030**

### What we will not discuss as acceptable instruments
- closures
- forced layoffs
- threat-based bargaining
- permanent insecurity dressed up as “flexibility”

## On “economic equivalence”
If management and owners want an equivalent package, then it must be equivalent in **economic effect** while remaining socially responsible. That means:
- real savings,
- real capacity adjustment,
- real investment,
- and real burden-sharing from all sides.

It does **not** mean that the only way to reach savings is to shut plants and throw people out.

If you cannot show equivalence without closures, then your model is too narrow, not our red lines too high.

## Final point
The workforce is not asking to be shielded from change. We are asking that change be done **responsibly**, **fairly**, and **without destroying sites and livelihoods**.

If the company chooses confrontation, we are prepared to continue resistance with the full strength of co-determination and industrial action.

If the company wants a solution, bring back a plan that:
- keeps every German plant open,
- excludes compulsory redundancies,
- preserves the job-security framework,
- and shares the burden fairly.

Until then, our position stands.
