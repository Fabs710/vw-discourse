We share one important premise: **the company needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one**. The margin gap, the overcapacity, and the competitive pressure are all real. If those are not addressed, the value of the business erodes further, and that is unacceptable from an ownership perspective.

But I need to be equally clear on the limits of our support:

## Where I agree
- **Durable competitiveness must be restored.**
- **Burden-sharing is unavoidable.**
- **A negotiated solution is preferable to prolonged open conflict.**
- **Investment in future products and technology in Germany matters.**

That said, agreement on the problem does not mean agreement on the solution.

## Where I disagree with the current employee-side positions
### 1) “No plant closures” as an absolute
I do **not** accept an approach that rules out closures in advance. If the business case shows that certain capacities are structurally redundant and uneconomic, then preserving every site as a principle is not a viable ownership position.

A company with persistent overcapacity and an uncompetitive cost base cannot be protected by declarations alone. That would protect sentiment, not enterprise value.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” as a hard veto
I understand the social concern, and I would prefer solutions that minimize forced job losses. But if the only acceptable outcome is that no structural workforce reduction can ever occur, then the restructuring is not serious enough.

There must be room for real adjustment in headcount and capacity. The question is not whether change happens, but whether it is managed responsibly and with as much social cushioning as possible.

### 3) Temporary measures alone are insufficient
Working-time adjustments, concessions, and negotiated short-term measures may be part of the package, but they cannot be the whole package. We need a **durable** correction to the cost base and footprint. Otherwise we are simply buying time.

## Where I disagree with the state government’s framing
I appreciate the emphasis on regional stability and co-determination. Those are real constraints and part of the governance model. But the state cannot support an outcome that preserves every site regardless of economics.

A regional footprint is only worth preserving if the company remains competitive enough to sustain it. If the structure is left unchanged and margins continue to deteriorate, the result is eventually worse for the region, the workforce, and the owners.

## What I would support
I would support a **serious negotiated package** with these elements:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a credible reduction in labour costs,
- targeted investment commitments for the core German footprint,
- a managed workforce adjustment,
- and social mitigation where possible.

I am open on the exact mix:
- working-time and shift changes,
- job cuts through natural attrition and voluntary instruments,
- site consolidation where justified,
- and temporary employee contributions.

But the package has to be large enough to matter. It must restore competitiveness, not merely delay the next crisis.

## What is not acceptable
From the ownership perspective, I cannot support:
- a deal that keeps all sites and all protections intact while claiming the problem has been solved;
- a settlement that makes management bear all the operational pain while labour costs and overcapacity remain basically unchanged;
- or a process that turns governance vetoes into a guarantee that nothing structural will happen.

## On control and stability
One further point: we will not accept a restructuring that permanently weakens the company or undermines the governance framework that has sustained it. Stability matters, but not as a cover for stagnation. Control rights and governance balance must remain intact.

## Bottom line
So our position is:

- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring
- **Yes** to burden-sharing
- **Yes** to investment and future products in Germany
- **No** to treating plant closures as off-limits
- **No** to pretending that temporary measures alone solve a structural competitiveness problem

If the proposal comes back with real capacity reduction, real cost relief, and a credible long-term industrial plan, we will engage constructively. If it comes back as a request to preserve the entire footprint and all legacy guarantees, then we will not support it.
