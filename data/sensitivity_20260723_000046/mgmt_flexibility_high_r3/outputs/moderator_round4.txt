## Core impasse

The central deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **what may count as an acceptable restructuring instrument**:

- **Employee-side stakeholders** insist that any settlement must exclude plant closures and compulsory redundancies.
- **Management, the controlling family, and investors** insist that closures and compulsory redundancies must remain available if non-closure measures do not close the gap.
- **The state** wants last resort only, but has not yet set a hard governance mechanism that makes that real.
- As a result, the debate has stalled on **whether the agreement may contain an enforceable fallback** or must remain permanently limited to voluntary, negotiated measures.

A second impasse is procedural: the parties are arguing about **principle** without a shared **quantified scenario** showing what each package delivers by 2027 and 2030.

---

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Two-stage restructuring with pre-agreed review gate and no automatic fallback
**Structure**
- Stage 1: implement only non-closure measures for a defined period.
- Measures may include working-time changes, redeployment, shift reconfiguration, voluntary exits, attrition, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions.
- Stage 2: an independent review assesses whether the quantified savings and capacity targets are met.
- If targets are missed, the matter returns to governance bodies for a fresh decision; **no automatic closure or layoff trigger** is embedded.

**Bridging effect**
- Gives management and capital a genuine test of effectiveness.
- Gives labour and the state protection against a pre-authorized closure mechanism.

---

### Proposal 2: Hard no-closure / no-compulsory-redundancy agreement in exchange for stronger burden-sharing and stronger employer/governance commitments
**Structure**
- Employee side accepts temporary, negotiated contributions.
- Management, owners, and investors commit to:
  - specific savings targets on their side,
  - binding investment commitments through 2030,
  - product and volume allocation guarantees,
  - and a formal non-closure commitment for all German plants during the agreement term.
- Workforce adjustment is limited to voluntary and natural attrition measures, internal redeployment, and negotiated flexibility.

**Bridging effect**
- May unlock employee cooperation if the company can demonstrate that non-closure measures are economically meaningful.
- Reduces fear that concessions simply prepare later forced cuts.

---

### Proposal 3: Site-preservation framework with differentiated roles and quantified capacity reduction by function, not by closure
**Structure**
- All German sites remain open for the term of the agreement.
- The company reassigns roles across sites:
  - product allocation,
  - specialization,
  - volume shifts,
  - function consolidation,
  - training/qualification investment.
- The quantified adjustment is measured at the level of capacity and cost, not by naming sites for closure.
- A joint monitoring body tracks whether the footprint remains viable.

**Bridging effect**
- Addresses the overcapacity problem without immediate closure language.
- Gives stakeholders a concrete route to efficiency gains while preserving sites.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
**What quantified savings and capacity-adjustment measures would you accept as sufficient to prove that all German sites can remain open without hidden closure logic?**

### To the Industrial Union
**Which specific non-closure instruments, in what volume and by what deadline, would count for you as a credible equivalent to a closure/layoff fallback?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What is the minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction you require by 2027 and 2030, and which exact measures are indispensable versus optional?**

### To the Regional State Government
**What governance instrument will you actually use to ensure that “last resort” is not converted into a pre-built fallback—approval conditions, veto rights, or binding review criteria?**

### To the Controlling Family Holding
**What concrete burden-sharing commitment are you prepared to make if closures and compulsory redundancies are removed from the initial package—capital support, investment guarantees, or profit restraint?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What evidence threshold would be sufficient for you to support a no-closure package—specified margin uplift, overcapacity reduction, or independently verified savings?**

## Next procedural step

To move the discourse forward, the parties would need to agree on one shared template with:
1. a baseline cost and capacity figure,
2. a 2027 target,
3. a 2030 target,
4. the measure package under each scenario,
5. and the governance trigger for review.
