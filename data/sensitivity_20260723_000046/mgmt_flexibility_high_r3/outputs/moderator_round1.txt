### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that the company faces a **real competitiveness, margin, and overcapacity problem**.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated solution** rather than open-ended conflict.
- There is general agreement that any settlement should include:
  - **burden-sharing**
  - **future investment in Germany**
  - a **durable** rather than purely temporary solution
  - some form of **industrial restructuring**

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Employee-side actors and the state government reject them as a starting point; management, family owners, and investors say closures cannot be ruled out if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Employee-side actors oppose them; ownership and investors say headcount reduction cannot be fully excluded.
- **Scope of guarantees:** Labour and state want binding site and job guarantees, while management and owners argue that absolute guarantees would prevent a serious restructuring.
- **How burden-sharing is defined:** Labour wants negotiated, temporary, reversible contributions with management/owners also contributing; management and capital-side actors want more durable cost-base reductions.
- **Governance/co-determination:** Employee and state actors insist on full co-determination and no unilateral decisions; management and owners want enough flexibility to implement structural change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Job and site security vs. structural cost reduction**
- **Temporary employee concessions vs. permanent labour-cost adjustment**
- **Preserving the full German footprint vs. reducing overcapacity**
- **Social and political stability vs. immediate enterprise-value and margin restoration**
- **Managed adjustment tools** (working time, shifts, attrition, redeployment) **vs. harder instruments** (closures, compulsory layoffs, consolidation)

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should focus on a **concrete package comparison**: which combination of site decisions, workforce measures, investment commitments, and safeguards could satisfy the economic requirement for competitiveness while remaining acceptable to the employee, state, and ownership side. A useful next step would be to test **specific scenarios** rather than restate red lines.
