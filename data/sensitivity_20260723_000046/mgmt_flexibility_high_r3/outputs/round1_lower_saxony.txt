As the regional state government and anchor shareholder, I want to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that destroys jobs, strips the company of its regional footprint, or sidelines co-determination and our veto role**.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one central point: **the company needs a credible restructuring, not cosmetic adjustments**. The cost and capacity problem is real, and postponing decisions would only make the eventual pain worse.

I also agree that:
- the solution must be **durable**, not a short-term patch;
- **burden-sharing** is necessary;
- and the company needs a **future industrial plan**, including investment in new products and technology in Germany.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the implication that the only serious restructuring is one involving plant closures and large-scale job destruction.

That is too narrow and too risky:
- It ignores the company’s role as a **regional economic anchor**.
- It underestimates the political and social damage of shutting German sites.
- It treats workforce protections and co-determination as obstacles rather than as part of the governance model that has sustained this company.

I am also not prepared to go along with a process in which management presents closures as the default and expects others to ratify that as inevitability. If the company wants support from us, it needs to show that it has genuinely tested alternatives first.

### Our position
We are prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring package** if it includes:
- **preservation of German sites wherever economically possible**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **a fair employee contribution that is temporary and negotiated**
- **meaningful contributions from management and owners**
- **clear commitments to investment, future products, and industrial presence in Germany**
- **full respect for co-determination and the special governance rights that come with this ownership structure**

What I can support is a restructuring that restores competitiveness **without hollowing out the company’s regional base**.

What I cannot support is a “solution” that may improve short-term financial optics but leaves:
- long-term social damage,
- weakened industrial capabilities in Germany,
- and a permanent breach with the workforce and regional stakeholders.

### On the union and works council demands
I understand and share the concern over closures and forced redundancies. Those are not acceptable starting points.

At the same time, I do not think it helps to pretend that every contribution can be avoided. If the company is asking for sacrifices, they must be:
- negotiated,
- proportionate,
- and tied to hard guarantees on future investment and site security.

### Bottom line
So my message is this:

- **No unilateral closure agenda**
- **No forced redundancies as the first resort**
- **No restructuring that shifts all risk onto workers**
- **Yes to a serious, negotiated package that protects the company’s German footprint and long-term competitiveness**

If management comes back with a proposal that still centers on closures and deep cuts in Germany, then it should not expect support from us. If it comes with a real social-partnership restructuring that preserves sites and jobs while fixing the cost base through balanced measures, then we are ready to engage constructively.
