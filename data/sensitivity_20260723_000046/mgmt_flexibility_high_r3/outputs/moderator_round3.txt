**Facilitator assessment of the impasse**

The core impasse is now narrow but hard:

1. **Labour side red lines**  
   Works Council and Union reject:
   - plant closures,
   - compulsory redundancies,
   - and any weakening of job-security or co-determination as a precondition.

2. **Capital / management insistence**  
   Management, controlling family, and institutional investors require:
   - material and durable overcapacity reduction,
   - structural cost reduction,
   - and keep **closures and forced workforce reductions on the table** if needed.

3. **State’s conditional support**  
   The regional government opposes closures/forced layoffs as a starting point, but is willing to support restructuring if it is:
   - economically real,
   - socially managed,
   - and preserves the regional industrial base.

So the deadlock is not about whether restructuring is needed; it is about **which instruments are admissible before anyone can accept the package as “durable”**.

---

## Facilitator compromise options to bridge the gap

### Compromise position 1: Phased restructuring with a closure “fallback” only after quantified tests
A package could be built in **two stages**:
- **Stage 1:** mandatory testing of all non-closure measures for a fixed period, including working-time reductions, shift/line changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, attrition, bonus restraint, and management/owner contributions.
- **Stage 2:** only if an agreed target gap remains after independent verification, a limited set of structural measures becomes eligible for negotiation.

**Purpose:**  
This gives labour and the state a genuine “last resort” logic, while giving management and investors a mechanism that does not rule out deeper adjustment if the numbers still do not close.

---

### Compromise position 2: “No compulsory redundancies” replaced by a managed workforce-transition corridor
Instead of an absolute ban or absolute permission, parties could negotiate a **workforce transition corridor** with:
- a capped number of involuntary reductions allowed only as a final resort,
- priority to voluntary exits, internal redeployment, and shortened working time,
- retraining and placement commitments,
- and triggering conditions requiring joint sign-off or independent review.

**Purpose:**  
This preserves labour’s demand that forced exits not be the default tool, while addressing management and investor concerns that headcount reduction cannot be ruled out in all scenarios.

---

### Compromise position 3: Site preservation with differentiated site roles, not equal treatment of every plant
Rather than “every German plant stays open” versus “closures on the table,” the parties could negotiate:
- preservation of all **core sites**,
- consolidation of specific functions, products, or lines across sites,
- and a jointly agreed classification of plants into:
  - core / protected,
  - restructured,
  - and review category.

This can be paired with:
- binding investment commitments,
- transfer of new product lines,
- and social safeguards for any site designated for reduced volume.

**Purpose:**  
This avoids a binary closure fight while still allowing meaningful footprint adjustment.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
**What specific combination of working-time, redeployment, product allocation, and voluntary measures would you accept as a quantified test package, and what objective result would have to be achieved before any harder instrument could even be discussed?**

### Industrial Union
**Would you accept a time-limited transition framework that permits limited compulsory measures only after independently verified exhaustion of voluntary and socially managed options, provided there are binding guarantees on investment, retraining, and co-determination?**

### Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings, capacity reduction, and margin improvement targets do you require to consider a no-closure or no-forced-layoff package credible, and are you prepared to publish those thresholds to enable negotiation against them?**

### Regional State Government
**What concrete governance condition will the state use to distinguish an acceptable “last resort” package from one it will block—for example, an independent necessity test, a site-by-site review, or a binding social-impact threshold?**

### Controlling Family Holding
**Are you prepared to support a package that excludes immediate closures and forced redundancies if it contains independently verified cost reduction, fixed investment commitments, and a legally credible fallback mechanism should targets be missed?**

### Institutional Investors
**What level of durable cost reduction and capacity adjustment would be sufficient for you to support a settlement that relies primarily on voluntary and managed instruments rather than immediate closures or compulsory redundancies?**
