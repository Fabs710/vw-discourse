We appreciate the clarity from all sides. Let me respond directly.

## Where we agree
I agree with management, the state, the union, and the works council on the diagnosis that this is **not a cosmetic problem**. The company has a real competitiveness gap, a real overcapacity issue, and a cost base that is not consistent with the market reality. If we fail to address that, we damage long-term value, investment capacity, and ultimately jobs.

I also agree that:
- a negotiated settlement is preferable to open-ended conflict,
- future investment in Germany is essential,
- burden-sharing is unavoidable,
- and any durable solution must be operationally credible, not just politically attractive.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side and state position that **“no plant closures”** and **“no compulsory redundancies”** can be treated as hard starting conditions.

That is not a serious ownership position if certain capacities are structurally uneconomic. It protects the current footprint, not the future viability of the business. If a plant is redundant or materially uncompetitive, refusing to consider closure in advance is not stewardship; it is postponement.

I also do not accept the idea that temporary measures alone are enough. Working-time changes, attrition, and negotiated concessions may be part of the package, but they do not by themselves fix a persistent margin and capacity problem.

## To management
You are right that the solution must be **durable** and large enough to matter. I support that principle.

But I want to be equally clear: a restructuring that creates a prolonged social conflict, reputational damage, or a breakdown in governance would also destroy value. So “durable” cannot mean maximal disruption for its own sake. It has to be durable in the sense of restoring competitiveness **and** preserving industrial stability.

If you are asking for support for closures or headcount reduction, then I will need a package that is clearly necessary, proportionate, and linked to a credible industrial plan for the remaining footprint. I will not back a blunt cost-cutting exercise detached from future investment.

## To the works council and union
I understand your insistence on co-determination, job security, and site protection. Those are not trivial concerns; they are part of the company’s governance reality.

But I need to be direct: you cannot insist on every site remaining open and every job guaranteed while also expecting the cost base to become competitive. Those positions are incompatible.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that delivers the same economic effect through:
- capacity rationalisation,
- working-time and shift changes,
- internal redeployment,
- voluntary exits,
- and a meaningful employee contribution.

If such a package truly closes the gap, I am open to it. But it must be economically equivalent, not just socially more convenient.

## To the regional state government
I respect the state’s role and the special governance framework. But regional stability cannot become a veto on structural adjustment. Preserving every site regardless of economics is not a viable long-term policy.

At the same time, I do agree that co-determination and regional responsibility matter. A socially destructive solution would not be acceptable either. So if closures are on the table, they must be the result of a disciplined process, not a reflex.

## What I would support
I would support a package with these features:
- real and durable capacity reduction,
- meaningful labour-cost relief,
- managed workforce adjustment,
- targeted investment commitments in Germany,
- and a solution that protects the core long-term value of the company.

I am open on the instruments:
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- redeployment,
- working-time and shift flexibility,
- temporary employee contributions,
- and, where economically necessary, site consolidation or closure.

## What I will not support
I will not support:
- a deal that preserves all sites by principle and calls that restructuring;
- a package that leaves the structural cost problem intact;
- or an arrangement where ownership is expected to absorb permanent value erosion in the name of avoiding difficult decisions.

## Bottom line
My position is simple:

- **Yes** to a serious restructuring
- **Yes** to negotiated burden-sharing
- **Yes** to investment and future products in Germany
- **No** to treating closures and headcount adjustment as off-limits
- **No** to a settlement that protects legacy structures while leaving the company uncompetitive

If the employee side and the state want to avoid closures, they need to bring forward a package that is genuinely comparable in economic effect. Otherwise, structural measures remain necessary.
