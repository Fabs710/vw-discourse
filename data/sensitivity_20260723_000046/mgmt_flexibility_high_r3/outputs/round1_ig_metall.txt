We share the core red lines stated by the Works Council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no dismantling of the collective bargaining framework**.

From the union perspective, the current restructuring logic is not acceptable if it amounts to:  
- workers carrying the adjustment alone,  
- management treating layoffs and closures as the default solution, or  
- permanent insecurity being imposed on the workforce under the label of competitiveness.

We do recognise the economic pressure. A durable settlement has to address real cost and capacity problems. But if the company wants contributions from employees, those contributions must be part of a **fair burden-sharing package**. That means:

- **management and owners must contribute too**
- any labour-cost contribution must be **temporary, negotiated, and reversible**
- there must be **binding job and site security**
- capacity adjustments must be pursued through **working-time, shift, and product allocation measures**, not forced redundancies
- the company must commit to **future investment and new products in Germany**

What we can consider is an agreement that combines:
- a negotiated employee contribution,
- moderate capacity reduction,
- flexibility on working time and plant organisation,
- and a credible industrial plan that keeps all German sites open.

What we will not accept is a restructuring that uses “necessary competitiveness” as a pretext for **closing plants or forcing workers to pay for management’s strategic errors**.

So our position is clear:  
If the proposal protects jobs and sites and spreads the burden fairly, we are prepared to negotiate seriously.  
If it relies on closures, compulsory layoffs, or the erosion of collective wage standards, we will oppose it decisively — including with industrial action.
