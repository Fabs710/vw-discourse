From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the key issue is simple: **can this restructuring credibly restore competitive margins and protect enterprise value?** Everything else is secondary.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the family owners, and the regional state on the basic diagnosis:
- the current cost base is too high,
- European demand is weak,
- and the company needs a **durable** solution, not a symbolic one.

We also agree that:
- **burden-sharing is necessary**;
- investment in future products and technology matters;
- and prolonged conflict would be value-destructive.

In particular, we do **not** want a long strike, a drawn-out public stalemate, or a half-measure that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. Those outcomes would damage margins, earnings visibility, and the share price.

## Where we disagree
### 1) “No plant closures” cannot be a binding red line
We understand the social and political sensitivity. But from an investor perspective, a blanket prohibition on closures is not credible if there is structural overcapacity. If some German capacity is uneconomic, then preserving every site regardless of business case would be a cosmetic settlement.

That is not acceptable to us because it would leave the cost base impaired and simply defer the problem.

### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” is not acceptable as an absolute
We prefer managed reductions, voluntary exits, attrition, and redeployment. But if the only way to achieve a meaningful, durable cost reset is through workforce reduction, then insisting on zero compulsory redundancies at all costs may block the necessary restructuring.

What matters is not protecting every current position; what matters is restoring the business to a competitive margin structure.

### 3) Temporary concessions alone do not solve the problem
Working-time changes, temporary employee contributions, and reversible measures may help, but they are not enough if the structural gap is large. We will not back a deal that relies on short-term pain relief while leaving the underlying economics unchanged.

## What we would support
We would support a package that clearly delivers:
- a **material, durable reduction in the cost base**,
- a **credible reduction in capacity**,
- a **disciplined workforce adjustment**,
- and a clear investment plan for the remaining German footprint.

That package could include:
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- working-time and shift changes,
- targeted site consolidation,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- and, if necessary, selective closures or other hard capacity decisions.

We are not attached to any one instrument. We are attached to the **outcome**: competitive margins and a viable return on sales.

## What would be unacceptable
We would not support:
- a settlement that preserves all sites and all guarantees while claiming victory;
- a deal that protects labor structures but leaves the cost base uncompetitive;
- or a process that produces prolonged conflict without decisive savings.

That would erode shareholder value and damage the company’s ability to invest.

## Bottom line
Our position is:
- **Yes** to a serious restructuring
- **Yes** to negotiated burden-sharing
- **Yes** to investment in Germany
- **No** to cosmetic compromise
- **No** to an outcome that leaves margins structurally impaired

If the eventual package credibly restores competitiveness, we will support it. If not, we will not pretend that a politically comfortable solution is economically acceptable.
