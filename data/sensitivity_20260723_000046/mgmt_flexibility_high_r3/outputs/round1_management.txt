I’ll be equally clear: **a cosmetic settlement that leaves Germany structurally uncompetitive is not an option**.

We all know the situation. The brand is missing its return-on-sales target, the European market is shrinking, and our domestic cost base is too high. If we do not take **durable** action now, we will simply postpone the crisis and come back here in a worse position.

So let me respond directly:

### Where I agree
- **Yes, change must be negotiated.** We cannot force a sustainable restructuring through confrontation alone.
- **Yes, future investment in Germany matters.** We need credible product and technology commitments for the period to 2030.
- **Yes, burden-sharing must be part of the solution.** We are not talking about asking only the workforce to adjust while management does nothing.

### Where I disagree
- **No, “all sites stay open” cannot be a precondition.**  
  If a plant is structurally overcapacity or cannot be made cost-competitive, then refusing closures in advance is not a realistic position. We are not prepared to preserve every site regardless of economics.

- **No, “no compulsory redundancies” cannot be treated as untouchable if it prevents structural correction.**  
  We can discuss alternatives, but if the end result is that nothing substantial changes, then we have failed.

- **No, temporary measures alone are insufficient.**  
  Working-time tweaks, one-off concessions, and reversible measures may help at the margin, but they do not solve a persistent cost gap.

### What I am prepared to discuss
I am open to a **negotiated package** that combines:
- capacity reductions,
- workforce adjustments,
- labour-cost contributions,
- and targeted investment commitments for the remaining German footprint.

But that package must be **credible, durable, and large enough** to restore competitiveness. It must deliver:
- a real reduction in overcapacity,
- a meaningful and lasting labour-cost contribution,
- and a clear path back to margin strength.

### What I will not accept
- a deal that preserves all capacity and all guarantees while pretending the cost problem has been solved;
- a settlement that protects structures but not the business;
- or a process where management is expected to carry the restructuring alone while the operating cost base remains unchanged.

I also want to be explicit: if the employee side insists on absolute red lines — no closures, no job reductions, no meaningful cost contribution — then there is no serious path to restoring competitiveness. That would not be a compromise; it would be a delay.

We are prepared to negotiate hard. But the outcome has to fix the cost base in a structural way. That is the condition for any agreement from management’s side.
