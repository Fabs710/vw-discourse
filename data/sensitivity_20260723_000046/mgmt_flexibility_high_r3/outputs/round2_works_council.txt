Let me be equally direct in Round 2.

We all agree there is a real competitiveness problem. We do **not** agree on the remedy.

## Where I agree
I agree with all stakeholders on three points:
- the company needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one;
- future investment in Germany is essential;
- and burden-sharing must be part of any credible package.

But that is where the shared ground ends.

## Where I disagree — and why

### 1) Plant closures are not a “neutral option”
Management, the family owners, and the investors keep saying closures “cannot be ruled out.” That is exactly the problem.

A closure is not just another efficiency measure. It is a decision to destroy a site, break a workforce, and permanently weaken the industrial base in Germany. For us, that is not an acceptable starting point and not a bargaining chip to be casually put on the table.

You have not demonstrated that every German plant is uneconomic. What you have demonstrated is a desire to shift the burden onto workers and sites first.

That will not be accepted.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies are a red line
I hear the argument that headcount reduction cannot be “excluded.” We reject that. If management’s model only works by forcing people out, then management has not presented a socially responsible restructuring — it has presented a displacement plan.

We will not endorse a process where workers are told to pay for strategic failures through forced job losses.

### 3) “Durable” cannot mean permanent insecurity for labour
The word “durable” is being used here to justify permanent cost cuts on employees while treating job security as disposable.

That is not burden-sharing. That is one-sided transfer of risk.

If there is to be any employee contribution, it must be:
- negotiated,
- temporary,
- reversible,
- and matched by binding commitments from management and owners.

Anything else is simply a demand for surrender.

## On the other stakeholders

### To management
If you want credibility, then stop talking as if closure and layoffs are the only language the company understands. You have not exhausted alternatives like:
- working-time adjustments,
- shift and line reconfiguration,
- product allocation,
- internal redeployment,
- natural attrition,
- temporary wage or bonus moderation,
- and targeted efficiency improvements at management level.

Those options exist. If you refuse them and go straight to closures, then this is not a search for solutions; it is an ultimatum.

### To the family owners and investors
You all say you care about long-term enterprise value. Then do not destroy the very industrial and social foundations that make this company viable.

A settlement that tears up the historic job-security framework may look “clean” in a financial model, but it creates deep conflict, strike risk, reputational damage, and long-term instability. That is not value preservation; that is value erosion.

### To the state government
We appreciate your line on regional responsibility and co-determination. But we need more than statements. If you mean what you say, then you must use your governance position to prevent a solution built on closures and forced redundancies.

### To the union
We are aligned on the fundamentals. Any workforce contribution must be fair and negotiated, and no one should pretend that workers alone should carry the burden. The council will stand with the union against any attempt to dismantle collective protections.

## What a serious scenario must look like
If the company wants a real settlement, it must be built around these principles:

- **Every German plant remains open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No breaking of the job-security framework**
- **Capacity reductions through working-time, shift, and product allocation measures**
- **Temporary and negotiated employee contributions**
- **Meaningful contributions from management and owners**
- **Binding investment commitments for Germany through 2030**
- **Full co-determination in shaping the restructuring**

That is not an unrealistic wishlist. It is the minimum basis for social peace and industrial continuity.

## What I reject outright
I reject any “scenario comparison” that begins from the assumption that closures are legitimate, layoffs are inevitable, and the workforce must prove its worth by accepting permanent insecurity.

That is not a negotiation. It is an attempt to normalize a breach of trust.

## Final point
The workforce is not the problem to be cut away. The workforce is the foundation of this company’s ability to produce, innovate, and recover.

If management wants a solution, bring us a plan that preserves all German sites, protects jobs, and shares the burden fairly. If not, then we will continue to resist with the full strength of co-determination and industrial action.
