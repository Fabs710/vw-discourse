As Works Council, I will be very clear from the outset:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price of management’s restructuring.

We recognise that the company faces serious competitive pressure. But the workforce did not create this situation alone, and it must not be made to bear the full burden through one-sided cuts, site closures, or the destruction of trust. A future for this company cannot be built by sacrificing German plants and livelihoods.

What we will argue for is a negotiated solution that protects every German site, secures jobs to 2030, and preserves co-determination and the existing job-security framework. If cost reductions are truly necessary, then they must come through alternatives that avoid closures and forced layoffs: capacity adjustments, internal restructuring, working-time measures, redeployment, qualification, and a fair employee contribution that is matched by real commitments from management and owners.

We are prepared to discuss measures that are reversible, fair, and socially responsible. But the red lines are clear: no plant closures, no forced redundancies, no fait accompli.

If management wants a serious settlement, it must put binding site and employment guarantees on the table, along with a genuine role for employee representatives in shaping the plan. Otherwise, it should expect firm resistance from the workforce and the works council.
