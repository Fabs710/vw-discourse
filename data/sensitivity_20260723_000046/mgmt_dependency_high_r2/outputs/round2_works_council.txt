Management keeps saying “structural necessity” as if that phrase alone justifies closing plants and tearing up job security. It does not. And I want to be very clear: there is still no acceptable case for German plant closures, and there is still no acceptable case for compulsory redundancies.

I do agree with one point from several sides: this company needs a real, durable solution, not a cosmetic one. But durability does not mean sacrificing workers, sites, and trust to satisfy a spreadsheet. A solution that destroys the workforce’s confidence and the industrial base in Germany is not durable — it is self-destructive.

### To management
You have made claims about overcapacity and costs. Fine. Then put the facts on the table:
- site-by-site economics,
- concrete capacity utilisation,
- exact savings needed,
- and what happens to product allocation if a plant is closed or a workforce is cut.

Until you do that, your “necessity” argument is still just a demand that workers pay for management’s failure to solve the transition properly.

And even if there are cost pressures, the answer is not automatic closures or forced layoffs. The answer must be:
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time adjustments,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- and a negotiated employee contribution only if it is matched by binding guarantees from ownership and management.

### To the family owners and investors
You keep talking about competitiveness and value. That is exactly why you should not support a destructive restructuring that attacks the company’s social foundation. This business is not a portfolio asset you can strip down and then expect to remain strong. The workforce, the know-how, the sites, and the co-determination model are part of the company’s long-term value.

If you want returns and stability, then back a package that protects every German site and avoids forced redundancies. Anything else will provoke resistance, damage industrial peace, and create risks that no investor model can fully price in.

### To the regional state government
You are right to reject closures and mass layoffs in the state. But then you need to be consistent: do not give management cover for a plan that leads to those outcomes indirectly. A “competitiveness” narrative that ends in plant shutdowns is not an acceptable compromise, it is a betrayal of the region’s industrial future.

### To the union
We are aligned on the red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of job-security guarantees.

Where we must stay disciplined is on the package itself: any employee contribution must be conditional, limited, and tied to binding protection. There will be no blank cheque from the workforce.

### Our position for this round
We are prepared to discuss a negotiated restructuring only if it includes all of the following:
1. Binding guarantees that **every German plant remains open** to 2030.
2. A categorical commitment against **compulsory redundancies**.
3. Preservation of **co-determination and the existing job-security framework**.
4. Transparent, site-level disclosure of the actual savings gap.
5. Alternative measures first: working-time tools, redeployment, qualification, natural attrition, voluntary exit schemes.
6. A fair contribution from management, owners, and capital — not just from labour.

Without those conditions, there is no serious basis for agreement. We will not rubber-stamp closures, and we will not accept a restructuring built on fear. If this room wants a deal, it must start by respecting the workforce instead of threatening it.
