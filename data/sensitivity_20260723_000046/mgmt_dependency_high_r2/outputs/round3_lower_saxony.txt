I will speak plainly.

I agree with management, the family holding, and investors on **one** fundamental point: this company needs a **real** restructuring, not a cosmetic truce. The expert has also made clear that the economics must be taken seriously and that we cannot judge this without site-level data. On that, there is no dispute.

But I **do not** accept the leap from “there is a cost problem” to “therefore German plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain on the table as normal tools.”

That is exactly where I draw the line.

## Where I agree
- Yes, the company faces a structural competitiveness challenge.
- Yes, fixed costs, utilization, and capacity alignment matter.
- Yes, a negotiated package must produce durable savings.
- Yes, labour-side measures can be part of the solution.
- Yes, transparency on site economics is necessary.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Blanket closure logic
Management says closures must remain possible if the numbers justify them. I understand that as an argument, but I do **not** accept it as the starting point.

The burden is still on management to prove that:
- no viable alternative package exists,
- every less destructive instrument is insufficient,
- and the benefits of closures outweigh the long-term damage to trust, regional industrial capacity, and the company’s role as an anchor employer.

Until that proof is on the table, closures are not a justified default. They are a last resort.

### 2) Compulsory redundancies
I will not support a restructuring built on forced job losses as a routine lever. That is not consistent with the social and political responsibility attached to this company, especially given its role in the region.

If reductions are needed, then the sequence must be:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- and a negotiated employee contribution.

Only if management can demonstrate that these are genuinely insufficient would the discussion even move further. Right now, we are nowhere near that threshold.

### 3) Ignoring the broader value of the company
To the investors: you are right that value must be protected. But value is not only a near-term margin metric. Destroying skilled employment, industrial peace, and regional stability can be value-destructive in its own right.

To the family holding: long-term stewardship means more than forcing structural cuts. It also means preserving the company’s social foundation and its industrial legitimacy.

To management: if you push a plan that tears up the workforce’s confidence, you may get an accounting improvement and a strategic weakness. That is not long-term health.

## What I need before any hardening of my position
I support the moderator’s request for site-level disclosure. Specifically, I want:
1. **Site-by-site fixed and variable cost data**
2. **Capacity utilization by plant**
3. **Quantified savings from each instrument**
   - closure
   - voluntary exits
   - working-time reductions
   - redeployment
   - wage moderation
4. **One-time restructuring costs**
5. **A comparison showing whether a negotiated alternative closes the gap**

Without that, no one here can honestly claim closures are necessary.

## What I am prepared to support
I remain open to a serious negotiated restructuring if it includes:
- preservation of German sites,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding co-determination and veto rights under the special statute,
- measurable management and ownership contributions,
- and a quantified savings package that does not place the adjustment burden first and foremost on jobs.

I can support:
- capacity adjustments,
- internal transfers,
- working-time flexibility,
- qualification,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- and time-limited employee contributions

**if and only if** they are paired with binding protection for sites and employment.

## Bottom line
My position is unchanged:
- **No plant closures in the state**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No erosion of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring that protects jobs, sites, and the company’s long-term viability**

If management wants my support, it needs to bring a package that preserves the company as a regional anchor, not one that treats the region as expendable.
