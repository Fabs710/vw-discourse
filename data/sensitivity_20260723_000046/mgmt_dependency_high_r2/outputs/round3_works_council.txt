Let me be equally clear in round 3: the more management repeats “structural necessity” without delivering a binding, site-level alternative, the less credible it becomes. I do not see a justification here for German plant closures, and I do not accept compulsory redundancies as a legitimate restructuring tool. Those remain our red lines.

## To management
You keep saying the workforce must “help close the gap,” but you still have not shown that closures are the only way to do it. The expert has already made the key point: this depends on fixed costs, utilization, and whether the savings gap can be closed by other means.

So put the numbers on the table:
- site-by-site fixed and variable costs,
- actual utilization,
- avoidable savings by measure,
- one-time closure costs,
- and a comparison with a negotiated alternative.

Until then, your demand for closures is not a proven necessity; it is a choice to place the burden on jobs first.

And let me be frank: a plan that relies on forcing people out or shutting plants down while asking workers to trust “future investment” is not a turnaround. It is a rupture. We have seen enough of management promises to know that trust must be earned, not demanded.

## To the controlling family and investors
You say you want value preserved. Then stop pretending that value is only a short-term margin number. Destroying know-how, industrial peace, and the company’s social settlement destroys value too.

A “durable” restructuring cannot be built on:
- plant closures,
- forced redundancies,
- and tearing up job-security guarantees.

That may reduce accounting cost, but it increases strategic risk, labour conflict, and long-term damage to the company’s industrial base.

If you want a credible package, then back:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserved co-determination,
- and a real ownership contribution.

## To the regional state government
Your position is right in principle, but it has to become concrete in action. A temporary moratorium or “review clause” is not enough if it simply opens the door to closures later. We need binding protection, not a pause button for management to come back with the same demands.

If the state truly wants to protect the industrial base, then it should use its governance power to block any plan that includes:
- German plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or dismantling of the job-security framework.

## To the union
We are aligned on the fundamentals, and I appreciate that. But I want to sharpen the point: any employee contribution must be strictly conditional and limited. The moment “flexibility” becomes a backdoor to closures or forced layoffs, the deal is broken.

That means:
- no blank cheque,
- no concession without binding guarantees,
- no “temporary” sacrifice that becomes a permanent erosion of protections.

## On the expert’s analysis
The expert did not say closures are required. The expert said closures may be justified only if the company proves that fixed costs and underutilization make other measures insufficient.

That proof has not been delivered.

So the burden is still on management to show:
1. why every plant cannot remain open to 2030,
2. why voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time tools, and capacity adjustments are insufficient,
3. why the company cannot meet its savings target without violating job security.

Until that evidence is provided, the only responsible position is to reject closures and compulsory redundancies.

## What we can discuss
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing destructive restructuring.

We can discuss:
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- internal transfers,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- working-time instruments,
- temporary wage moderation if and only if it is matched by binding guarantees,
- and capacity adjustments that do not involve plant closures.

But I want to be explicit: “capacity adjustments” does not mean shutting sites and calling it optimization. We will not agree to a package that clears the books by sacrificing communities and jobs.

## My position in one sentence
If management wants a negotiated settlement, it must be built on **every German plant staying open, no compulsory redundancies, and a binding job-security framework through 2030**.

Anything else is not a settlement we can accept.
