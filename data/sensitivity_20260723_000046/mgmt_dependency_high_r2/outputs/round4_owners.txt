We are at the point where positions are no longer enough. The only useful discussion now is about whether there is a **credible, quantified path** to restore competitiveness without locking the company into a structurally weak cost base.

Let me be clear: I will not support a settlement that simply preserves every legacy protection while asking the owners and management to absorb the full burden of adjustment. That would be value-destructive. But I also will not support reckless disruption. Our interest is durable competitiveness, not a headline-grabbing confrontation.

## To the works council
I respect the insistence on site-level evidence. That is reasonable. What is not reasonable is the leap from “we need proof” to “there can be no closures, ever.” That is not a governance position; it is a veto before the numbers are tested.

You say closures destroy trust and know-how. They can. But keeping structurally uneconomic sites open can also destroy value, investment capacity, and ultimately jobs. I do not accept a blanket ban on closures if the data shows a site is persistently uncompetitive.

That said, I agree with you on one point: **closures must be evidence-based and last resort**. So I support full disclosure of:
- site-by-site fixed and variable costs,
- utilization,
- one-time closure costs,
- and a comparison with negotiated alternatives.

If the alternative package closes the gap credibly, I would prefer it. If it does not, then insisting on no closures regardless of the evidence is not stewardship; it is denial.

## To the union
I appreciate that you are prepared to discuss wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, early retirement, and redeployment. That is constructive.

But I do not accept the implication that only reversible, low-impact measures should be on the table while the structural cost problem remains untouched. If the company needs a materially lower cost base, then employee contributions must be real enough to matter.

At the same time, I agree that contributions must be tied to genuine reciprocity:
- clear savings target,
- defined duration,
- transparent reporting,
- and a binding management/ownership commitment.

What I will not accept is a one-sided burden on labour without a credible restructuring outcome.

## To the regional state government
You are right that industrial base and trust matter. But I do not support turning the special governance role into a permanent shield against necessary restructuring.

A no-closure stance can be justified only if it is matched by a package that demonstrably restores competitiveness. Otherwise it protects inertia, not the region.

I am prepared to support a negotiated process with strong protections during the assessment phase, but not a framework that rules out hard decisions in advance. If the economics require capacity reduction, the company must retain the ability to act.

## To the institutional investors
Your focus on risk-adjusted value is correct. A cosmetic settlement would be unacceptable.

Where I differ from you is in the assumption that the right answer is simply to keep closures and compulsory redundancies available as standard tools. They must remain possible in principle, but not as the default. The economics must decide.

If a mixed package can deliver comparable savings with lower execution risk and less long-term damage, that is the better route. If it cannot, then hard measures cannot be excluded.

## To management
You are right that the numbers matter, and you are right that a durable solution requires material change. But you still have not demonstrated that closures or compulsory redundancies are the only viable path. You need to prove that.

I expect you to table, without delay:
- site-by-site cost and utilization data,
- closure versus alternative savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and a timetable to restore margins.

Only then can this board and the governance side make a serious judgment. Until then, I am not endorsing closures. But I am also not endorsing a no-change settlement.

## My position, plainly
- I support a **serious, quantified restructuring**.
- I support **transparent site-level disclosure**.
- I support **employee contributions** if they are material, time-limited, and reciprocal.
- I support **voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time flexibility, and bonus restraint** as first-line tools.
- I do **not** support a cosmetic deal.
- I do **not** support a blanket “no closures ever” rule.
- I do **not** support compulsory redundancies as a first resort.
- I do **not** support any outcome that permanently weakens the enterprise or the owners’ governance control.

If the numbers show that the negotiated alternatives close the gap, I will support them. If they do not, then the company must retain the instruments needed to restore competitiveness. That is the only responsible position from a long-term owner’s perspective.
