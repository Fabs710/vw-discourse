## Core impasse
The main deadlock is **not** over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **which instruments are permissible before the site-level evidence is tested**.

More specifically, the impasse sits on four linked questions:

1. **Closure authority vs. prior proof**
   - Management, investors, and the family want closures and compulsory redundancies to remain available.
   - Works council and union want those instruments ruled out absent a stronger quantified necessity case.

2. **Economic efficiency vs. governance red lines**
   - Some parties prioritize restoring competitiveness through any necessary structural action.
   - Others prioritize preserving German sites, co-determination, and job-security commitments as non-negotiable constraints.

3. **Temporary concessions vs. permanent damage**
   - The labor side is open to limited, reversible contributions.
   - Management and capital want measures with durable balance-sheet impact, and doubt that reversible tools alone are sufficient.

4. **Process trust**
   - The labor side says closure talk is premature without full disclosure.
   - The management side says disclosure is coming, but refuses to pre-commit away the option of hard measures.

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Data-first, no-decision-for-closures” assessment phase
A time-limited phase in which:
- management discloses full site-level economics,
- no closures or compulsory redundancies are announced or implemented,
- all parties engage on a shared model,
- and the outcome is a quantified comparison of closure, mixed, and no-closure packages.

**Bridge it creates:**  
This gives labor the proof standard it is demanding, while preserving management’s ability to argue for harder measures if the data supports them.

---

### Proposal 2: “No compulsory redundancies, but targeted structural adjustment”
A package in which:
- compulsory redundancies are ruled out,
- German plant closures are not pre-authorized as a first option,
- but targeted capacity reduction, redeployment, and voluntary separation remain available,
- alongside working-time, wage, and bonus measures.

**Bridge it creates:**  
This preserves the labor red lines on forced layoffs, while giving management and investors a path to material savings and structural change.

---

### Proposal 3: “Conditional site-security with review triggers”
A settlement that:
- preserves site security for a defined period,
- ties employee concessions to verified savings milestones,
- includes review points if targets are missed,
- and requires management/owner contributions before any escalation.

**Bridge it creates:**  
This offers the works council and union protection against immediate closure threats, while giving management a mechanism to revisit the package if the gap is not closing.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If a fully disclosed site-level model showed that a mixed package of voluntary exits, working-time changes, wage moderation, and targeted capacity reduction **still leaves a material gap**, what specific non-closure measure would you accept to close it?

### To the Union
What minimum combination of concessions would you regard as sufficient to support a binding no-compulsory-redundancy agreement without making the package purely symbolic?

### To Management
Will you commit that no plant closure or compulsory redundancy decision will be taken **before** the site-level comparison of closure versus alternatives is tabled and jointly reviewed?

### To the Regional State Government
Will you support a temporary no-closure assessment period if it is paired with binding disclosure, milestone reviews, and reciprocal labor/owner contributions?

### To the Controlling Family Holding
Are you prepared to back a settlement that protects sites and jobs for a defined period if it demonstrates credible, durable savings without immediate closure powers?

### To the Institutional Investors
What minimum NPV and payback conditions would make a no-closure or mixed package acceptable to you, even if closure remains available only as a backstop?

### To the Expert
What specific data threshold would allow you to distinguish between a mixed package that is “close enough” economically and one that is still too weak to replace closure?
