**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the company faces a serious competitiveness and restructuring challenge.
- There is broad agreement that a purely cosmetic solution will not work.
- Most parties accept that **some form of negotiated restructuring** is preferable to prolonged confrontation.
- There is shared openness to exploring:
  - capacity adjustments,
  - redeployment,
  - qualification/training,
  - working-time measures,
  - and some form of employee contribution.
- There is also broad agreement that industrial peace, trust, and long-term viability matter.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures:** Works Council, union, and regional state government reject closures in Germany or in the state; management, owners, and investors say closures may be necessary if economics require it.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** Labour and state reject them; management, owners, and investors do not rule them out.
- **Job-security framework:** Labour side wants existing guarantees preserved; management, owners, and investors say changes may be necessary.
- **Scope of sacrifice:** Labour side insists burden must be shared; management and capital side argue employee-side measures alone are insufficient and that structural cost reduction must be material.
- **Form of restructuring:** Labour side seeks reversible, socially protective measures; capital side demands durable, measurable competitiveness gains, even if that implies harder measures.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **material cost reduction** without closures or forced redundancies.
- Whether a package can deliver the same economic effect through:
  - shorter working hours,
  - wage moderation,
  - internal transfers,
  - capacity cuts,
  - or voluntary exit schemes.
- What binding commitments from management/owners/state would match any employee contribution.
- How to define a restructuring plan that is both:
  - economically credible to investors,
  - and socially acceptable to labour and public stakeholders.
- What concrete site-by-site evidence supports or challenges the need for closures or layoffs.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **specific package design**.
- Ask management to present **site-level data and quantified savings needs**.
- Ask labour and public stakeholders to propose **alternative measures with estimated economic impact**.
- Identify which measures are **binding, reversible, and verifiable**, and which require legal or governance guarantees.
- Narrow the discussion toward a **single integrated package** that states:
  - what savings are achieved,
  - by which measures,
  - on what timetable,
  - and what protections or guarantees accompany them.
