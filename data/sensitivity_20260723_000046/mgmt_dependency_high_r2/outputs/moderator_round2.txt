The core impasse is procedural and substantive:

## Core impasse
1. **Management / owners / investors** want the right to use closures and compulsory redundancies if needed to restore competitiveness.
2. **Works council / union** reject those instruments in principle and want binding site and employment security first.
3. **Regional state government** supports restructuring only if it does not weaken the state’s industrial base or governance protections.
4. **The expert** has clarified that the economic answer depends on site-level fixed costs, utilization, and the gap between closure savings and negotiated-alternative savings.

So the deadlock is not just “whether to cut costs,” but **which instruments are allowed before the numbers are fully disclosed and a reciprocal package is defined**.

## Facilitator compromise options
These are not endorsements, but possible bridging formulations for the next round:

### 1) Conditional no-closure moratorium with review trigger
A time-limited commitment to keep all German plants open for a defined period, paired with:
- full site-by-site disclosure,
- a jointly verified savings target,
- and a review clause that reopens the question only if the alternative package fails to hit agreed milestones.

This could bridge the gap between the workforce’s need for security and management’s need for an evidence-based fallback.

### 2) Mixed restructuring package with “last resort” plant actions
A package centered on:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- wage/bonus moderation,
- and targeted sub-site or line consolidation,
with closures and compulsory redundancies reserved only as **last-resort instruments** after jointly verified alternative measures fall short.

This may create room for labour to accept adjustment without conceding a blanket veto, while giving management a restructuring pathway.

### 3) Reciprocal commitments package
A balanced package in which:
- labour accepts limited, time-bound contributions,
- management caps executive and shareholder extractions,
- owners/investors commit fresh capital and investment,
- and the state provides facilitation or support where lawful,
all tied to binding employment, training, and site-investment guarantees.

This shifts the discussion from “who loses first” to “what each side contributes and what each side receives in return.”

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
What specific site-level data will you disclose, by when, so that others can verify whether the savings gap truly requires closures or compulsory redundancies?

### To the works council
If a verified package can close most of the savings gap through voluntary exits, working-time tools, and redeployment, what limited flexibility would you be prepared to discuss to avoid an outright breakdown?

### To the union
What binding employee-contribution model would you accept if it came with enforceable guarantees on jobs, investment, and no involuntary redundancies during the agreed period?

### To the regional state government
Would you support a temporary restructuring moratorium tied to transparent reporting and a joint review, even if it does not yet rule out every future capacity reduction?

### To the controlling family holding
What measurable ownership contribution are you prepared to place on the table that would make a negotiated, no-forced-redundancy package credible to labour?

### To the institutional investors
If management can show that a mixed package delivers comparable savings with lower execution risk than closure, would you support prioritizing that route over immediate site shutdowns?
