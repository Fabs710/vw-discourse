Here is the procedural diagnosis of the impasse and a set of bridge options.

## Core impasse
The deadlock is concentrated in **three linked disputes**:

1. **Sequencing**
   - Labour/state want **evidence first**, then any discussion of closures.
   - Management/investors want **openness to closures now**, with evidence used to refine, not pre-limit, options.

2. **Red lines vs. flexibility**
   - Works council and union want:
     - no German plant closures,
     - no compulsory redundancies,
     - job security through 2030.
   - Management and capital-side stakeholders want these instruments **kept available** if the numbers require them.

3. **Trust deficit**
   - Labour and state do not trust that “temporary” concessions will not become permanent erosion.
   - Management and investors do not trust that a no-closure stance can still deliver the required savings.

So the factual dispute is about **site economics**, but the real blocking issue is **what instruments remain admissible before the numbers are fully accepted**.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### Option 1: Conditional moratorium with hard evidence gate
**Proposal:**  
A time-limited moratorium on closures and compulsory redundancies for a defined negotiation window, combined with mandatory disclosure of site-level economics and a jointly reviewed alternative package.

**Key features:**
- No closure decisions during the window.
- Management must disclose:
  - fixed/variable costs,
  - utilization,
  - one-time closure costs,
  - alternative-savings comparison.
- Labour side must table a quantified alternative by a set deadline.
- At the end of the window, all parties review whether the savings gap is closed.

**Why this could bridge sides:**  
It gives labour/state the evidence-first sequencing they want, while preserving management’s argument that closures are not ruled out forever, only deferred pending proof.

---

### Option 2: Two-track restructuring package
**Proposal:**  
Split the process into two parallel tracks:
- **Track A:** immediate cost and capacity measures that are non-destructive
- **Track B:** reserved escalation options if Track A fails to close the gap

**Track A measures could include:**
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- working-time flexibility,
- bonus restraint,
- temporary wage moderation,
- qualification and transfer support.

**Track B reserved measures:**
- closure,
- deeper capacity reduction,
- compulsory redundancies only if predefined trigger thresholds are met.

**Trigger mechanism examples:**
- savings gap not closed by a certain percentage,
- utilization stays below a defined threshold,
- inability to secure investment for future products.

**Why this could bridge sides:**  
It allows labour to pursue the least destructive measures first, while giving management/investors a pre-agreed fallback path if the alternatives underdeliver.

---

### Option 3: No-closure package in exchange for deeper, quantified employee and owner commitments
**Proposal:**  
A no-closure settlement for the agreed period, but only if the non-labour stakeholders commit materially and the labour side accepts stronger temporary concessions.

**Possible elements:**
- employee wage moderation or bonus restraint for a limited term,
- management compensation restraint,
- ownership or family holding financial contribution,
- binding investment commitments,
- preservation of sites and employment through the agreed horizon.

**Why this could bridge sides:**  
It addresses labour’s demand for binding protections while answering investors’ demand for a durable savings package. It only works if the financial contribution is substantial enough to replace the savings that closures would otherwise generate.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**Question:**  
Will you commit to a fixed deadline by which you will disclose site-by-site economics and accept a joint test of whether the negotiated alternatives can close the savings gap before any closure decision is taken?

---

### To the works council
**Question:**  
If the disclosed numbers show a measurable remaining gap after voluntary exits, working-time measures, and redeployment, what specific additional instrument, if any, would you be prepared to discuss without crossing your red lines?

---

### To the union
**Question:**  
What quantified employee contribution—by type, duration, and savings target—would you place on the table if it were paired with binding no-compulsory-redundancy guarantees for the negotiation period?

---

### To the regional state government
**Question:**  
What concrete governance action will you take if the process reaches a stalemate: a temporary freeze, conditional veto use, or a mediation mandate with binding disclosure requirements?

---

### To the controlling family holding
**Question:**  
What ownership-side contribution or guarantee are you prepared to make so that the restructuring is not perceived as shifting the full burden onto labour?

---

### To the institutional investors
**Question:**  
What level of verified recurring savings and what payback horizon would make you support a no-closure alternative, assuming it is supported by the site-level data?
