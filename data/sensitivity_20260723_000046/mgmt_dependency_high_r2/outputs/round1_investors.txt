We share one important premise with management, the family, and the state: this company needs a real, durable improvement in competitiveness. We are not interested in preserving a cost structure that keeps margins structurally weak or leaves the business unable to fund investment. From an investor perspective, that would be an unacceptable outcome.

Where we agree:
- A cosmetic settlement is worse than no settlement.
- The current German cost base appears too high relative to the market reality.
- Any durable solution must reduce overcapacity and restore margin discipline.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.

Where we disagree is on the form of the adjustment and the credibility of the proposed alternatives.

To the works council and union: we understand the desire to avoid closures and forced redundancies, but “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to guarantees” cannot be the starting point if the economics do not support it. If those red lines prevent a meaningful cost reset, then they are not a responsible defense of employment; they are a way of locking in underperformance. We would not support an outcome that preserves every legacy protection while leaving the company uncompetitive.

To management: we support decisive action, but not an open-ended confrontation. If closures, job cuts, or labour-cost reductions are necessary, the case has to be demonstrated with hard numbers, site-by-site economics, and a credible implementation plan. We will not back rhetoric alone. Investors need to see how the restructuring translates into lower fixed costs, better capacity utilisation, and a return to target margins on a clear timetable.

To the state and the family owners: your emphasis on avoiding value destruction is right, but that only matters if the final package actually fixes the cost base. Blocking a necessary restructuring without offering an equally effective alternative would be value destructive in a different form. We are not attached to any particular instrument — closures, capacity reductions, working-time measures, redeployment, employee contributions — but the result must be structurally competitive, not politically comfortable.

So our position is straightforward:
- We support durable structural cost reduction.
- We support any negotiated package that credibly restores margins and competitiveness.
- We do not support a deal that merely defers the adjustment, protects all existing commitments, and leaves the company stuck with an uncompetitive cost base.
- We do not support prolonged conflict if a credible settlement is available.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, it needs to present a package that delivers the same economic effect, not just a softer headline. If management wants support for difficult measures, it must prove that those measures are necessary, targeted, and sufficient. Otherwise, capital will not stay patient indefinitely. We can reallocate elsewhere.
