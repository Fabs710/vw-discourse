We agree with the broad diagnosis, but not with any attempt to turn that diagnosis into a defence of inaction or a cosmetic settlement.

## Where we agree
- The company has a real competitiveness problem.
- A purely symbolic deal would destroy value.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would also destroy value.
- The right answer must be **durable, quantified, and site-specific**, not driven by slogans.
- A mixed package may be preferable to an all-closure solution if it truly closes the savings gap.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and union
Your insistence on:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- and preserved legacy job-security guarantees

is understandable from your perspective, but it is **not acceptable as a hard veto** from ours.

We do **not** support a restructuring that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive just to preserve every legacy protection. If the site economics show that one or more plants are genuinely uneconomic, then closures cannot be ruled out in advance. If the savings gap cannot be closed otherwise, compulsory redundancies also cannot be excluded categorically.

That said, we are open to avoiding closures **if and only if** the alternative package is real and quantified:
- voluntary exits,
- early retirement,
- redeployment,
- working-time adjustments,
- wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- and capacity consolidation.

But those measures must be enough to restore margins, not merely postpone the problem.

### To management
We support the need for hard restructuring, but we do **not** support using “structural necessity” as a substitute for proof.

You have not yet justified every hard measure by reference to site-level economics. The expert was right: the case turns on:
- fixed versus variable cost,
- utilisation,
- avoidable savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and execution risk.

So management must put the numbers on the table in a way that allows others to test whether closures are actually necessary or whether a negotiated alternative can do the job at lower risk.

At the same time, we reject the notion that the absence of full disclosure suspends reality. Everyone here already knows the cost base is too high and margins are under pressure. The company needs action, not endless delay.

### To the regional state government
We respect your concern for the industrial base and your role under the special statute. But a blanket no-closure stance is not enough on its own if it blocks any credible restructuring.

Your position is only helpful if it is paired with a concrete alternative that closes the savings gap. Otherwise it just hardens the deadlock.

### To the controlling family holding
We agree that the company cannot be run on a cosmetic truce. But we do not accept a governance posture that implicitly treats closures and forced redundancies as the first and default answer.

If the family wants long-term value, then it should insist on a package that is:
- economically verified,
- socially workable,
- and durable.

That means supporting the least destructive solution that actually restores competitiveness. But it also means not preserving every historic commitment if those commitments are what keep the company below target margins.

## Our position
We will support a restructuring **only if** it credibly restores competitive margins and reduces the cost base in a durable way.

That means:
1. **Site-level disclosure** of economics and utilisation.
2. **Quantified comparison** of closure vs. alternative scenarios.
3. **Binding savings targets** with a clear timetable.
4. **A serious employee contribution** if it avoids harsher measures.
5. **No cosmetic settlement** that merely buys time.

## What would move us
We would back a no-closure solution if management and labour together can show, with numbers, that it:
- closes most or all of the savings gap,
- delivers durable cost reduction,
- and does so with lower execution risk than closures.

But if that cannot be demonstrated, then we will not support preserving every site and every guarantee at the expense of margins and enterprise value.

## Bottom line
We are not aligned with an ideological closure agenda.  
We are also not aligned with an ideological no-closure veto.

We are aligned only with the outcome that best restores **risk-adjusted shareholder value**:
- durable cost reduction,
- credible margin recovery,
- and enough operational flexibility to keep the business competitive.
