We appreciate the expert input, because it confirms the key point we have been insisting on: this has to be judged on **site economics and durable savings**, not on slogans.

Our position remains straightforward:

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **credible, durable restructuring**.
- A **cosmetic deal** that leaves the cost base uncompetitive would destroy value.
- **Prolonged industrial conflict** is also value-destructive.
- A mixed package — if it truly closes the gap — may be preferable to pure closure.
- We need **quantified, site-by-site evidence** before anyone can claim that closures are necessary or unnecessary.

## Where we disagree
### 1) A blanket no-closure position is not acceptable
To the works council, union, and state: your insistence that no German plant can be closed may be politically understandable, but from an investor perspective it is not enough. If a site is structurally uneconomic and cannot be made viable through alternative measures, then keeping it open would simply lock in underperformance and suppress margins.

We will not support a settlement that preserves every site regardless of economics.

### 2) No-compulsory-redundancy cannot become a veto against real restructuring
We prefer voluntary exits, redeployment, and natural attrition where they work. But if those tools cannot deliver the required savings, then insisting on an absolute ban on compulsory redundancies may prevent the company from reaching a competitive cost base. That would be value-destructive.

### 3) Employee contributions must be economically meaningful
We are open to working-time reductions, wage moderation, bonus restraint, and flexibility — but only if they deliver real and durable savings. Symbolic sacrifices or temporary gestures are not enough if they merely delay the underlying problem.

## What we need to see next
From management, we want:
- site-by-site economics,
- fixed vs variable cost breakdown,
- capacity utilisation data,
- closure and alternative-scenario savings,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- and a timetable to return to target margins.

From labour, if you want to avoid closures, we need:
- a **quantified alternative package**,
- clear evidence that it closes most or all of the savings gap,
- and binding commitment mechanisms, not aspirational promises.

From the state and owners, we need:
- confirmation that governance will not be used to block necessary economics,
- but also that value-destroying confrontation will not be allowed to drag on indefinitely.

## Our bottom line
We support the restructuring **only if it produces a credible path back to competitive margins**.  
We do **not** support:
- a cosmetic settlement,
- a deal that simply postpones the adjustment,
- or a framework that protects all legacy commitments while leaving the company structurally uncompetitive.

If the alternative package can match the economics of closures with lower execution risk, we would prefer that. If it cannot, then management must retain the tools to make hard decisions.
