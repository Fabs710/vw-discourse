I want to respond plainly, because the stakes here are too high for evasions.

We in the regional state government share one core assessment with management: the company needs a credible restructuring. Pretending that there is no competitiveness problem would be irresponsible. German operations cannot be left with a cost base that is structurally out of line and expect long-term survival.

But I also want to be equally clear where we draw the line.

We will not support a restructuring that is built on plant closures in this state, mass compulsory redundancies, or the hollowing out of the company’s role as a regional industrial anchor. That is not a negotiable preference for us; it is a fundamental condition of our support. A solution that destroys jobs and production capacity here may look neat on a spreadsheet, but politically and economically it would be a self-defeating way to “save” the company.

So to management: if your argument is that competitiveness requires deep change, then show us a plan that actually proves it can be done without sacrificing core sites and without turning the workforce into the sole shock absorber. We are open to difficult measures, including capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, redeployment, qualification, and an employee contribution — but only as part of a balanced package with real commitments from ownership and management.

To the works council and the union: I support your insistence that the burden cannot be shifted one-sidedly onto employees. You are right that trust, know-how, and industrial peace matter. But I would also say this: simply declaring every site untouchable does not by itself solve the cost problem. If we want to defend jobs and sites, we need a restructuring that is economically credible enough to keep this company investable.

That is why our position is this:
- yes to negotiated restructuring,
- yes to alternatives that avoid closures and forced redundancies,
- yes to a fair social partnership contribution by the workforce,
- no to plant closures in our state,
- no to compulsory redundancies as the default answer,
- no to stripping away the state’s co-determination and veto role.

If management wants our support, it needs to put forward a serious package that protects industrial capacity in Germany, secures employment prospects, and restores competitiveness through shared sacrifice rather than unilateral cuts. If that is not possible, then you should not expect us to simply wave through a plan that offloads the adjustment onto the region and its workers.
