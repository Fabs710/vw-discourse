I will be direct.

We are not going to preserve value by pretending the current German cost structure is sustainable. The family holding will not back a cosmetic arrangement that protects every legacy commitment and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. That would erode margins, investment capacity, and ultimately the enterprise value we are all supposed to safeguard.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the state, and investors on the core diagnosis:
- this is a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip;
- a purely symbolic deal would be worse than no deal;
- the company needs durable savings, not optics;
- prolonged industrial conflict would damage value.

I also agree with labour on one important point:
- the burden cannot be shifted entirely onto the workforce if we want a settlement that lasts.

## Where I disagree
### 1) A blanket “no closures” line is not acceptable
I understand why the works council and union are taking that line. But as a governing shareholder, I cannot endorse an advance veto on plant closures if the economics show that some capacity must be removed.

If a site is structurally uneconomic, then refusing even to examine closure options is not stewardship. It is denial. And denial would be value-destructive.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies cannot be an absolute red line
I would strongly prefer solutions that avoid compulsory layoffs. But I will not support a framework that rules them out categorically in every scenario. If the company cannot get to a competitive cost base otherwise, then management must retain the tools to adjust capacity.

### 3) Existing guarantees cannot remain untouched if they block competitiveness
Long-standing protections have value, but they are not sacred if they prevent the company from adapting. A turnaround cannot be built on the assumption that all historic commitments survive unchanged while the business model deteriorates.

## To management
I support the restructuring objective, but I will not hand you a blank cheque.

If you want governance backing, you need to prove the case with hard numbers:
- site-by-site economics,
- capacity utilisation,
- the exact savings gap,
- what has already been tried,
- and what savings each instrument actually delivers.

If closures or tougher labour measures are necessary, the board needs to see that they are necessary, targeted, and sufficient.

## To works council and union
You are right to demand a serious package and not a one-sided attack on the workforce.

But if your answer is simply:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no change to job-security frameworks,
- no meaningful structural labour-cost contribution,

then you are not offering an alternative. You are defending the status quo, and the status quo is what is destroying value.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that really closes the savings gap:
- working-time adjustments,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- natural attrition,
- targeted wage moderation or bonus restraint,
- and a credible reduction in capacity.

That package must be quantified and binding. Not aspirational.

## To the regional state government
I respect your role and the special statute. I also understand the political sensitivity.

But your support cannot be conditional on preserving every site regardless of economics. If you block necessary restructuring without offering an equally effective alternative, you are not protecting the region — you are protecting delay.

At the same time, I do agree that a plan which destroys industrial peace and regional capacity would also be value-destructive. So the question is not whether there should be change, but whether there is a credible path to change without unnecessary destruction.

## My bottom line
The family holding will support:
- a negotiated restructuring,
- real and durable cost reduction,
- capacity adjustment,
- voluntary exits and redeployment where effective,
- working-time and flexibility tools,
- and a fair shared-sacrifice package.

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic truce,
- a deal that leaves the company uncompetitive,
- or a settlement that permanently impairs enterprise value.

So the next move is clear:
1. Management must table a quantified, site-level business case.
2. Labour must table a quantified alternative package if it wants to avoid closures.
3. The package must show how margins are restored, not just how conflict is postponed.

If a credible alternative exists that matches the economic effect of closures without destroying value, I am open to it. If it does not, then I will not pretend otherwise.
