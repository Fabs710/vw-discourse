We agree on the basic premise: **this has to be a real restructuring, not a delay tactic**. The company cannot survive if the balance sheet is left intact and the burden is pushed onto taxpayers. We are prepared to accept shared sacrifice, and we have already shown that in earlier rounds.

But let me be equally clear about what **cannot** happen:

1. **The retiree health trust cannot be destroyed or turned into a hollow promise.**  
   That obligation was negotiated over decades and represents earned benefits for retirees. We will not accept a plan that wipes it out simply to maximize recovery for unsecured bondholders.

2. **Workers cannot be asked to carry the whole rescue.**  
   We will not accept a restructuring where wage cuts, job losses, plant closures, and benefit destruction fall disproportionately on labour while financial creditors are protected. That is not shared sacrifice; it is a transfer of pain downward.

3. **We will not agree to a plan that sacrifices the industrial base unnecessarily.**  
   Some plant and brand rationalization may be unavoidable if the company is to survive. But closures must be selective, justified, and tied to a credible operating plan—not used as a blunt instrument to satisfy creditors.

What we can discuss constructively:

- **Further labour concessions**, if they are tied to real job preservation and a viable future.
- **A modified structure for the retiree health obligation**, so long as it is durable and secured, not merely deferred.
- **Equity or other real participation for the retiree trust**, so that the obligation is backed by a genuine claim on the restructured company rather than an empty unsecured promise.
- **Reasonable creditor haircuts**. Unsecured bondholders are not entitled to be made whole while workers and retirees absorb the deepest losses.
- **A restructuring path that avoids liquidation**, whether consensual or court-supervised, as long as it preserves the core industrial capacity and the trust’s claim.

So our position is this:

- If the proposal includes **real equity or securities for the retiree trust**,  
- if it includes **meaningful job and plant commitments**,  
- if creditors and management accept **shared losses**,  
- and if the resulting company is actually viable,  

then we are ready to negotiate seriously.

If, however, the proposal is simply “workers and retirees take the hit so bondholders and financiers can recover,” then we will reject it. That outcome is unacceptable, and we will not lend our consent to it.
