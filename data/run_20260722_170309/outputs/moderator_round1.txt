**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree the current structure is **not viable** and a **real restructuring** is required.
- There is broad agreement that **management has changed** and that **management, labour, creditors, and public funders must all share sacrifice**.
- All parties accept that **speed matters** and that the 60-day window is tight.
- There is general openness to either:
  - a **consensual restructuring**, or
  - a **court-supervised process** if needed to achieve sufficient depth and finality.
- There is consensus that the outcome must preserve a **viable going concern**, not simply delay collapse.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Retiree health trust treatment**
  - The union/trust insists the trust must not be hollowed out and wants a durable, real claim.
  - Bondholders object to any special treatment that leaves them materially worse.
  - Management and the task force both indicate the trust may need material modification to preserve viability.

- **Relative burden allocation**
  - Bondholders reject being the primary residual loss-bearing class.
  - Labour/trust objects to workers and retirees carrying disproportionate pain.
  - The task force wants clear sacrifice from all major stakeholder groups.
  - No agreement yet on who absorbs how much.

- **Plant and footprint rationalization**
  - Management says some closures/consolidation are necessary.
  - The union wants closures to be selective and justified.
  - Neighbouring governments want binding protection for domestic plants and jobs.
  - No shared view yet on which facilities can be cut.

- **Public funding conditions**
  - The task force wants taxpayer protection and credible repayment.
  - Neighbouring governments want co-funding tied to domestic production and equity participation.
  - No agreement yet on the scale, form, or conditions of public support.

- **Creditor recovery structure**
  - Bondholders want meaningful recovery, equal treatment among unsecureds, and real upside if converted.
  - Others have not accepted that framework.
  - No agreement yet on the compensation package for impaired debt.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to balance **viability** against **fairness across claimant groups**.
- How much **operational downsizing** is necessary versus politically/industrially acceptable.
- Whether the retiree trust gets:
  - equity,
  - a modified unsecured claim,
  - secured status,
  - or some hybrid structure.
- Whether domestic plant protection can be made **binding without undermining viability**.
- How to structure public funding so it is **credible to taxpayers** while still sufficient to stabilize the company.
- What level of creditor haircut is large enough to repair the balance sheet but not so severe that a voluntary deal fails.

### 4) Brief refocus for next round
Next round should aim to narrow the gap on **three concrete package elements**:
1. **Claim treatment:** proposed recovery ranges for unsecured bondholders and the retiree trust.
2. **Operational footprint:** which plants/jobs are preserved, reduced, or closed, and on what basis.
3. **Public support terms:** funding amount, conditions, ownership/equity rights, and exit/repayment path.

The most productive next step would be for each party to state **minimum acceptable terms** on those three items, so the group can test whether a viable package exists.
