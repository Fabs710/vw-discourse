As facilitator, I see the **core impasse** as a three-way conflict over **who absorbs the largest economic loss, and in what form**:

1. **Retiree trust vs. unsecured bondholders**
   - The union/trust insists the trust must remain a real, durable claim with value.
   - Bondholders insist they cannot be the default loss bucket if the trust is given better economics.
   - The task force and management insist the trust cannot remain in its current cash burden form.
   - Result: no agreement yet on the **relative ranking and economic treatment** of these two legacy claims.

2. **Domestic plant protection vs. footprint reduction**
   - The neighbouring governments want binding protection for domestic plants and production share.
   - The task force, management, and specialist say broad protection undermines viability.
   - Result: no agreement on **how much capacity can be preserved on non-economic grounds**.

3. **Governance and control vs. funding**
   - Co-funders want real equity and a say.
   - The task force will not accept veto rights over rationalization.
   - Result: no agreement on **how far public funders’ governance rights can extend without blocking restructuring**.

## Facilitator proposals: concrete compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Split the legacy burden into two instruments
A possible bridge is to separate the legacy claims into:
- a **reduced, deferrable retiree trust instrument** with real but limited value, and
- a **deeply impaired unsecured debt conversion package** with equity/warrants.

Possible structure:
- trust receives a **smaller nominal claim plus contingent upside** tied to post-restructuring performance;
- unsecured bondholders receive **debt-for-equity plus warrants** but with a larger share of the clean equity base than the trust;
- both groups take losses, but neither is made a hollow placeholder.

This could address:
- union/trust concern about preserving earned value,
- bondholder concern about unequal treatment,
- task force concern about removing cash burden.

### Proposal 2: Protected core plants, pre-agreed closure list for the rest
A possible bridge is to create two categories of facilities:
- **protected core plants**: only those meeting objective competitiveness and strategic criteria;
- **rationalization plants**: pre-identified for closure or consolidation.

Possible structure:
- neighbouring governments get binding commitments only on a **limited set of core plants**;
- all parties sign off on a **specific closure/consolidation list** for non-core capacity;
- no party gets a veto over the pre-agreed non-core rationalization package.

This could address:
- governments’ need for tangible protection,
- task force and specialist concern about excess capacity,
- management’s need for execution certainty.

### Proposal 3: Governance rights for co-funders, but no operational veto
A possible bridge is to grant neighbouring governments:
- equity proportional to actual funding,
- board-level information rights,
- limited protective rights over misuse of proceeds,

while explicitly excluding:
- veto over plant closures,
- veto over brand rationalization,
- veto over operational restructuring needed to meet viability targets.

This could address:
- co-funders’ demand for a real seat at the table,
- task force’s insistence that public money cannot block necessary restructuring.
