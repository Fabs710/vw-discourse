We appreciate the expert’s confirmation that this cannot be a cosmetic deal. We agree with that, and we agree with the task force, management, and labour on the basic point: the company needs real deleveraging, real operational cuts, and a fast final resolution.

But I need to be equally direct about where our position differs.

## Where we agree
- The company is not viable in its current form.
- Existing equity is effectively gone.
- Some plant, brand, and dealer rationalization is unavoidable.
- Labour, creditors, and public funders all need to share sacrifice.
- A court-supervised process is acceptable if it is the only way to reach a binding, viable solution in time.

## Where we disagree
### 1) Domestic plants cannot be treated as expendable
We cannot support a restructuring where domestic plants are closed while foreign plants are protected. That is exactly the outcome we are here to prevent.

If our governments are co-funding the rescue, then the domestic production footprint must be protected in a binding way. Not through vague assurances, but through enforceable commitments on:
- production share,
- employment levels,
- and plant utilization in our jurisdictions.

If the plan preserves foreign capacity at the expense of our plants, then we are being asked to pay for our own industrial decline. That is unacceptable.

### 2) We will not fund without a commensurate say
The task force is right that viability comes first. But if neighbouring governments are bringing real capital and political risk to the table, we are not entitled to a symbolic role.

We expect an ownership position proportionate to our contribution. If we co-fund materially, then we need a real equity stake and governance participation reflecting that support.

### 3) We are not asking for a fake restructuring
We are not here to preserve an unrepaired balance sheet. We accept that bondholders and legacy claims must take losses. We accept that some footprint reduction is necessary.

But we do not accept a “viable” plan that makes our plants the adjustment variable while other interests are better protected. That would not be restructuring; it would be regional cost shifting.

## On the retiree trust and bondholders
We understand the trust is an earned obligation and that it cannot simply be wiped out. We also understand the bondholders want meaningful recovery and equal treatment among unsecureds.

We are not taking a position that one creditor class should be privileged over another. Our position is simpler:
- losses must be shared,
- but not at the expense of our domestic industrial base,
- and not through closure of our plants while others remain intact.

If the trust and bondholder packages can be balanced in a way that preserves viability, that is a matter for the lead funder and the restructuring math. We are not blocking that. But we will oppose any package that achieves balance by hollowing out domestic production.

## On process
We are open to either consensual or court-supervised restructuring. We are not fixated on the route; we are fixed on the outcome.

But if the only way the company survives is through a process that gives binding protection to domestic plants and a proportionate ownership stake for co-funders, then that protection must be part of the deal. We will not wait until after the fact to discover that our funding protected everyone else’s jobs more than our own.

## Our minimum conditions remain
1. **Binding production and employment commitments for domestic plants**
2. **A proportionate equity or ownership position for co-funders**
3. **A viable restructuring that does not sacrifice our jurisdictions to protect others**
4. **An orderly, successful reorganization, not an open-ended subsidy**

If those conditions are met, we will continue to co-fund and help make the restructuring work. If not, then we cannot commit public money.
