We are willing to be constructive, but let’s stop pretending there is symmetry in the current proposals. There isn’t.

## Where we agree

We agree on the hard facts:
- The company in its current form is not viable.
- Existing equity is effectively worthless.
- A cosmetic exchange will fail.
- Meaningful footprint reduction is unavoidable.
- A fast, binding resolution is essential.

We also agree with the specialist that **deep deleveraging plus real operational rationalization** is required for any chance of survival.

## Where we disagree

### 1) We will not accept unsecured bondholders being used as the easiest dump bucket
We have heard repeated language about “shared sacrifice,” but the actual direction of travel is still obvious:  
- workers and the retiree trust want protected value,
- neighbouring governments want protected plants,
- the task force wants clean balance-sheet repair,
- and the unsecured bondholders are expected to take the deepest cut.

That is not balanced restructuring. That is political triage.

If the retiree trust is given a materially better instrument than unsecured bonds, then there must be **clear offsetting value** for unsecured creditors. Otherwise, this is unequal treatment dressed up as social necessity.

### 2) The retiree trust is not sacrosanct, but neither is it entitled to a better deal than us
We do **not** accept the premise that the trust can be preserved in a superior form while unsecured bondholders are wiped down further because we are dispersed.

Yes, the trust reflects earned retiree benefits. We understand the sensitivity. But economically it is still a claim on the enterprise. If it receives:
- equity,
- warrants,
- a priority instrument,
- or a materially better recovery structure,

then unsecured bondholders must receive **comparable economic treatment**. Not rhetoric. Comparable value.

We will not agree to a deal where the trust is politically shielded and bondholders absorb the gap.

### 3) “Deep haircuts” cannot mean near-wipeout
The task force keeps repeating that bondholders should not expect ordinary refinancing treatment. Fine. Nobody is asking for par.

But there is a line between a meaningful restructuring and a confiscatory one. If the proposed package leaves unsecured creditors with a trivial recovery while other legacy claimants receive better economics, then the deal is not commercially acceptable.

A viable company does not require an inequitable distribution of losses. It requires a distribution that is proportionate to legal rank, economic reality, and the actual value created by the restructured business.

### 4) Domestic plant protection has real economic limits
To the neighbouring governments: we understand your political pressure. But you do not get to make domestic plant preservation a veto right over the balance sheet.

The specialist already said it plainly: only a **small number of core, efficient plants** can be protected without breaking viability. Anything broader pushes fixed costs back into the company and destroys the very enterprise you are trying to save.

So no, we will not support a restructuring where regional protections become a hidden subsidy financed by unsecured creditors.

### 5) Public funding is not a license to subordinate financial creditors
To the task force: public money can stabilize liquidity, but it cannot be used to force unsecured creditors into a politically convenient outcome while other stakeholders secure carve-outs.

If taxpayers are backstopping the rescue, then the deal should be:
- economically disciplined,
- balanced across claimants,
- and credible enough to avoid another failure.

Not a deal where public support props up protected labour claims and protected plants while bondholders are told to quietly absorb the loss.

## What we will support

We are prepared to support a restructuring only if it includes all of the following:

1. **Deep but not confiscatory impairment of unsecured debt**
   - We accept real haircuts.
   - We reject a wipe-out.
   - We reject token recovery.

2. **Equal treatment among similarly situated unsecured claims**
   - If the retiree trust gets better economics, unsecureds must receive offsetting value.
   - No political favoritism dressed as fairness.

3. **Real upside participation**
   - If our debt is converted, the equity/warrants must be meaningful.
   - We are not exchanging billions of dollars of claims for symbolic scraps.

4. **A genuinely viable footprint**
   - We accept closures of uneconomic plants and brands.
   - But the footprint must reflect actual economics, not domestic political demands.

5. **Fast finality**
   - Delay destroys value, and we know it.
   - But speed is not an excuse for inequitable treatment.

## On the court-supervised fallback

We are not intimidated by the fallback route.

If the proposed voluntary deal tries to:
- privilege the retiree trust,
- preserve domestic plants for political reasons,
- and leave unsecured bondholders as the residual shock absorber,

then we would rather let the court-supervised process impose a fairer result.

And let me be clear: a court process may well expose that the current settlement architecture is too favorable to protected stakeholders and too harsh on dispersed unsecured creditors. So the threat of court does not run only one way.

## Our minimum position

Before we can even consider consent, we need:
- the unsecured bondholder recovery range,
- the retiree trust recovery range,
- the exact basis for any differential treatment,
- the plant closure list,
- and the public funding terms.

If the answer is still “bondholders take the biggest hit because they are easiest to cut,” then there is no deal.

## Bottom line

We are prepared to take losses. We are **not** prepared to be the default dumping ground while other unsecured or quasi-unsecured claims are protected by politics.

If the restructuring delivers:
- comparable treatment across unsecured claimants,
- meaningful recovery for bondholders,
- and a real, viable company,

then we can engage seriously.

If not, we will oppose it.
