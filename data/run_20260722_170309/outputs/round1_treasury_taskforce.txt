The government task force’s position is straightforward:

We will support a restructuring only if it produces a **viable, post-restructuring company** at **credible taxpayer cost**, with **real sacrifices from management, labour, and creditors**. We will not finance an unrestructured balance sheet, nor will we provide open-ended subsidies to keep the current structure alive.

Our priorities are:

1. **Speed and finality**  
   We have a 60-day window. The company needs a fast, orderly solution that avoids uncontrolled collapse and limits spillover damage to suppliers, jobs, and the broader industrial base.

2. **Deep restructuring**  
   The company’s cost structure and debt burden must be cut enough to make it viable on a stand-alone basis. Superficial cuts are not acceptable.

3. **Shared sacrifice**  
   Management has already changed. Now labour, retiree-health claimants, and unsecured creditors must all take losses or concessions. No group gets to hold out for taxpayer-funded protection.

4. **Taxpayer protection**  
   Public financing is a bridge to a viable outcome, not a permanent subsidy. We will insist on a credible path to repayment and eventual exit.

5. **Contingent court-supervised fallback**  
   If voluntary agreement cannot deliver the necessary depth of restructuring in time, we are prepared to fund the company through a court-supervised process that can impose terms. That route is not our first preference, but it is very much on the table.

What we will argue against:

- Any plan that simply rolls over existing liabilities without fundamental balance-sheet repair.
- Any attempt to delay decisions in hopes of improved terms at public expense.
- Any restructuring that protects one class of claimant while shifting the burden to taxpayers or other stakeholders.
- Any solution that preserves too many plants, brands, or obligations to the point that the company remains unviable.

If parties want continued support, they need to bring us **verifiable, durable concessions** and a **credible business plan** that shows the reorganized company can survive without repeated emergency intervention.
