We appreciate the candid positions now on the table. Our response is straightforward.

## Where we agree
We agree with the task force, management, and the specialist on the basic economics:
- the company is not viable in its present form,
- the old equity is effectively gone,
- deep deleveraging is unavoidable,
- and some footprint reduction is necessary.

We also agree that a cosmetic deal would fail. If this is going to work, it has to be a real restructuring.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic plants cannot be sacrificed while others are protected
This is the line we will not cross.

We are prepared to co-fund an orderly restructuring, but **not** one where:
- domestic plants are closed,
- foreign capacity is preserved,
- and our contribution is used to finance our own industrial decline.

If we are putting real public money in, there must be **binding protection for a core domestic production base**. That means:
- identified domestic plants protected on objective criteria,
- commitments on production share,
- commitments on employment levels,
- and no blanket discretion to shift work away from our jurisdictions after the fact.

Selective rationalization is one thing. A restructuring that leaves our plants as the adjustment variable is not acceptable.

### 2) We will not fund without a commensurate say
We agree with the task force that co-funders should not have a veto over necessary rationalization. But we are not a passive lender.

If we are contributing capital, we require:
- a **proportionate equity stake** reflecting that contribution,
- **board-level governance rights** appropriate to that stake,
- and transparency over operational decisions affecting the protected domestic footprint.

We are not asking to control the company. We are asking not to be treated as a junior payer with no seat at the table.

### 3) We are not choosing between labour and bondholders
We understand the conflict between the retiree trust and unsecured bondholders. That is for them to resolve.

Our position is narrower and firmer:
- losses must be shared,
- the company must be viable,
- and domestic plants must not be collateral damage.

If the trust gets a real instrument and bondholders get meaningful equity, that is part of the overall balance. But not at the expense of our industrial base.

### 4) Public money cannot be used to export the cost of adjustment to our regions
We will not support a structure where the public purse helps stabilize the company only for the result to be:
- fewer domestic jobs,
- lower domestic production share,
- and weakened regional industrial capacity.

If taxpayers are helping rescue the company, then the public interest includes preserving the domestic manufacturing footprint to the extent it is economically defensible.

## What we need to see
Before we can move further, we need concrete answers to these questions:
- Which domestic plants are being protected, and on what economic basis?
- What production share and employment commitments are binding?
- What ownership stake is offered to co-funders?
- What specific rights accompany that stake?
- How are domestic plant commitments enforced if the company later changes course?

If the answer is “we can discuss domestic protection later,” then that is not enough. We need commitments now.

## On the process
We remain open to either route:
- a consensual restructuring, or
- a court-supervised process if consensus fails.

But we will not support a voluntary deal that leaves domestic plants exposed while asking us to co-fund the rescue. If the package cannot protect a core domestic base in a binding way, then it does not meet our minimum conditions.

## Bottom line
We are ready to co-fund an orderly restructuring that:
- preserves a core domestic industrial base,
- gives us a proportional ownership position,
- and produces a viable company.

We are **not** ready to fund a deal that:
- protects foreign capacity over domestic plants,
- gives us money-risk without governance,
- or uses our participation to finance our own decline.

If a workable package can meet those conditions, we will remain constructive. If not, then we cannot support the financing.
