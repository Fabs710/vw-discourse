I agree with the task force, the specialist, and the creditors on the central point: **the current structure cannot survive**. We are past the point where incremental adjustments will do. If we try to preserve too much of the old balance sheet, the trust burden, or the footprint, we will simply burn through the bridge financing and fail again.

Where I disagree is on any proposal that treats one stakeholder group’s claims as protected at the expense of the company’s viability.

## On the retiree trust
I do **not** support preserving the trust in its current economic form. That would leave the operating company with a cash burden it cannot carry.

But I also do **not** support hollowing it out. The trust must remain a **real, enforceable claim** with value. The right answer is:
- a material reduction in nominal burden,
- a longer payment profile,
- and real value in the form of equity, warrants, or another instrument the reorganized company can actually support.

If the trust remains effectively senior and cash-heavy, the company fails. That is not acceptable.

## On unsecured bondholders
I agree they should not be singled out as the only shock absorber. But they also cannot insist on meaningful recovery while the company remains overlevered.

So the bondholders will take **substantial impairment**. That is unavoidable. In return, they should receive real upside, but only after the company is properly deleveraged. Token recoveries are not enough, and neither are recoveries that force the trust and labour to absorb the largest pain.

## On labour and plants
I agree with the union that workers should not be the default adjustment variable. But I do **not** agree that we can preserve the existing labour cost structure or the current footprint.

We need:
- selective plant closures,
- brand rationalization where the economics are weak,
- dealer network reduction,
- and bounded labour concessions.

What I will not support is blanket protection of plants simply because they are domestic or politically sensitive. We need to preserve the strongest brands and the most efficient plants, not the largest footprint.

## On neighbouring governments and co-funding
We welcome co-funding, but only if it supports a viable business. I am open to a protected **core domestic plant base** where the economics justify it.

I am **not** open to:
- blanket domestic protection,
- veto rights over closures,
- or governance rights that block necessary restructuring.

Equity should be proportional to real money at risk, not a substitute for economic discipline.

## On the court-supervised route
I remain open to a consensual deal, but only if it is deep enough to be financeable. If the parties cannot accept the scale of sacrifice required, then the court-supervised path is not a threat; it is the serious fallback.

## My position
A viable package must include:
1. **Deep unsecured impairment**
2. **Material trust modification**
3. **Selective, economically justified footprint reduction**
4. **Limited domestic plant protection only where justified**
5. **Public funding as a bridge, not a substitute**
6. **Fast finality**

## Non-negotiable line
I will not support:
- an unrestructured company,
- a capital structure that remains overlevered,
- or a deal that preserves the full cash burden of legacy liabilities while calling itself viable.

If that means the voluntary process fails, then we should move to the court-supervised route rather than pretend a weak deal can save the business.
