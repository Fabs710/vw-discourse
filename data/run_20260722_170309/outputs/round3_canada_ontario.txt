We appreciate that everyone is finally speaking in concrete terms. We will do the same.

## Where we agree
We agree on the fundamentals:
- the company is not viable as-is,
- a cosmetic deal will fail,
- real deleveraging and real footprint reduction are necessary,
- existing equity is essentially gone,
- and a fast, binding resolution is essential.

We also accept the specialist’s point that this is not just a liquidity problem. The company needs a smaller, financeable operating base.

## Where we disagree
### 1) Domestic plants cannot be the adjustment variable
We do **not** accept a restructuring where domestic plants are closed while foreign capacity is protected.

If neighbouring governments are co-funding, then there must be **binding protection for a core domestic production base**. Not slogans, not “best efforts,” but enforceable commitments on:
- domestic production share,
- employment levels,
- and allocation of model lines to the protected plants.

If the plan keeps our contribution but lets our plants be sacrificed, then we are not partners; we are subsidizing our own industrial decline. That is unacceptable.

### 2) We will not fund without a commensurate say
We agree with the task force that public money cannot buy a veto over viability. But our funding is not symbolic. If we are providing real capital and taking real political risk, we need:
- a **proportionate equity stake**, and
- governance rights reflecting that contribution.

We are not asking to control the company. We are asking not to be treated as a junior payer with no seat at the table.

### 3) We are not here to preserve an unrepaired balance sheet
We agree with management, the task force, and the specialist that the balance sheet must be repaired. We are not asking for old claims to be preserved in full at public expense.

But we do **not** accept a solution that makes domestic plants the price of repairing that balance sheet. The restructuring has to be viable **and** preserve the domestic industrial base to the extent it is economically justifiable.

### 4) We do not take sides in the unsecured-versus-trust fight
We understand the bondholders’ objection to being singled out. We also understand labour’s position that the retiree trust is earned compensation, not speculative debt.

We are **not** here to choose one unsecured group over another. Our position is:
- losses must be shared,
- the structure must be viable,
- and domestic plants cannot be collateral damage.

If the trust gets a real instrument and the bondholders get meaningful equity, that is a matter of overall balance. But not at the cost of our industrial footprint.

## Our minimum conditions remain non-negotiable
1. **Binding production and employment commitments for domestic plants**
2. **A proportionate ownership/equity position for co-funders**
3. **A viable restructuring that does not sacrifice our jurisdictions to protect others**
4. **An orderly, successful reorganization, not a public-funded industrial retreat**

## On the “economic limit”
We have heard the specialist’s warning that only limited domestic plant protection is absorbable. We take that seriously. That is exactly why we are asking for a **core domestic base**, not blanket protection of every site.

We are prepared to be selective:
- protect the most efficient domestic plants,
- accept that some rationalization is unavoidable,
- but require that the domestic footprint not be hollowed out while others are preserved.

That is a reasonable and necessary condition for our participation.

## On process
We remain open to either route:
- consensual restructuring, or
- court-supervised if consensus fails.

But we will not sign up to a consensual package that leaves our domestic production exposed and our capital contribution under-recognized. If the voluntary deal cannot accommodate that, then we will not pretend it is acceptable just because time is short.

## What we need next
Before we can go further, we need specifics:
- Which domestic plants are economically core and will be protected?
- What production share and employment commitment is proposed?
- What equity stake is offered in exchange for our co-funding?
- What public funding is required overall, and how is it split across participants?
- How are the trust and unsecured bondholder recoveries balanced without shifting the burden onto our plants?

If the answer is that domestic protection is only “possible” if the company later chooses it, then that is not enough. We need binding commitments now.

## Bottom line
We are ready to co-fund an orderly restructuring that saves the company and preserves a core domestic industrial base.

We are **not** ready to fund a deal that:
- protects foreign capacity over domestic plants,
- gives us money-risk without governance,
- or uses our participation to finance our own decline.

If a viable package can protect the domestic footprint in a binding way, we will continue constructively. If not, then we cannot support the financing.
