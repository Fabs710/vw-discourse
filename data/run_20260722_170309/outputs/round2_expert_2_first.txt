Yes. On the facts given, there is a **hard economic limit** to how much domestic plant protection and co-funder equity this company can absorb before standalone viability breaks.

## Bottom-line assessment

A viable restructuring likely requires:
- **deep impairment of unsecured debt**,
- **material modification of the retiree health obligation**,
- **closure/consolidation of clearly uneconomic plants and excess dealer capacity**,
- and **only limited, economically justified domestic-plant protection**.

If domestic governments insist on keeping too much local capacity alive, or if co-funder equity comes with governance or production commitments that force the company to retain excess fixed cost, the deal becomes **structurally unviable**.

## Where the viability limit likely sits

### 1) Domestic plant protection
The company can probably accommodate:
- protection of **a core domestic manufacturing base**,
- retention of **the most efficient plants**,
- and commitments on **specific model allocation / production lines** where those plants are cost-competitive.

It likely cannot accommodate:
- blanket protection for all domestic plants,
- binding guarantees that prevent closure of high-cost or underutilized sites,
- or domestic job guarantees that freeze the footprint at pre-crisis levels.

### Practical rule
Domestic plant protection is viable only if it is **aligned with utilization and unit-cost economics**.  
Once protected capacity exceeds the post-restructuring demand base, the company starts paying for idle fixed cost, and that directly erodes cash flow and covenant headroom.

## 2) Co-funder equity
Co-funder equity is viable only to the extent it is:
- **passive or limited-governance**,
- tied to **new money** rather than a disguised purchase of control,
- and not accompanied by onerous operating covenants that force excess capacity retention.

A reasonable co-funder equity stake can be accommodated if it is part of the funding needed to bridge the restructuring and if it does **not** increase the company’s ongoing fixed-cost burden.

### What breaks the model
Co-funder equity becomes dangerous when it is paired with:
- veto rights over plant closures,
- binding domestic production floors,
- or political commitments that prevent rational footprint reduction.

That kind of structure turns equity into a hidden operating constraint. The business then carries not just financial dilution, but operational inefficiency.

## 3) The real constraint: fixed-cost absorption
For an automotive company, the key issue is not just headcount or plant count. It is whether the remaining manufacturing base can absorb:
- depreciation,
- labor overhead,
- pension/retiree legacy burden,
- dealer support costs,
- and retooling capex.

If domestic protection preserves plants that are below efficient utilization, the company loses margin on every vehicle produced. In a weak demand environment, that quickly becomes a liquidity problem.

## My judgment
### Viable
- Protect a **small number of core domestic plants** that are genuinely efficient.
- Give co-funders an equity stake that reflects their cash contribution, but with **limited operational veto power**.
- Allow the company to close or consolidate the rest.

### Not viable
- Require the company to maintain multiple politically sensitive domestic plants regardless of economics.
- Give co-funders equity plus binding production rights that preserve excess capacity.
- Use public support to subsidize a footprint larger than the post-restructuring market can support.

## Trade-off that is unavoidable
There is an unavoidable choice between:
1. **more domestic protection**, and
2. **a stronger standalone balance sheet / lower operating break-even**.

You can have some of both, but not too much of both.  
If domestic protection is too broad, then unsecured creditors must be impaired more deeply, legacy liabilities must be cut harder, and even then the company may still fail because the operating structure remains too heavy.

## Clear viability limit
My best economic judgment is:

- **Limited domestic plant protection:** absorbable.
- **Broad domestic plant guarantees:** not absorbable.
- **Meaningful but not controlling co-funder equity:** absorbable.
- **Co-funder terms that constrain footprint rationalization:** not absorbable.

## Final view
The company can survive only if domestic plant protection is **selective, economically justified, and subordinated to viability**. Co-funder equity is acceptable only if it brings capital without locking in excess capacity or giving local governments an effective veto over restructuring decisions.
