Core impasse, as it stands:

1. **Retiree trust vs. unsecured bondholders**
   - The trust wants a durable, real instrument with value.
   - Bondholders insist they cannot be left as the primary residual loss pool if the trust gets better economics.
   - The task force and management require the trust’s cash burden to be materially reduced.
   - Result: no agreement yet on **who takes the deepest relative impairment** and in what form.

2. **Domestic plant protection vs. economic rationalization**
   - The neighbouring governments want binding protection for a core domestic base, plus governance rights.
   - The task force and management reject blanket protection and vetoes.
   - Result: no agreement on **which plants are protected, by what objective criteria, and what happens if economics worsen**.

3. **How much “deep” is deep enough**
   - All sides accept the company needs restructuring.
   - They do not yet agree on the **minimum haircut, trust modification, footprint reduction, and liquidity buffer** needed to make the post-restructuring company viable.
   - Result: the parties are still arguing around the viability threshold rather than converging on it.

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Split the retiree trust treatment into a protected base plus contingent upside
- The trust could receive a **materially reduced, enforceable base instrument** with a defined cash cap and long maturity.
- Additional value could come through **warrants or contingent value rights** tied to post-restructuring performance.
- This would reduce immediate cash burden while preserving some real economic value.

**Purpose:** bridge the trust’s demand for durability with the task force’s demand for cash relief.

---

### Proposal 2: Create a “core domestic footprint” schedule with objective closure triggers
- Identify a limited set of domestic plants that qualify as **core and economically defensible**.
- For all other sites, closures or consolidation would be pre-authorized if they miss specified metrics:
  - utilization,
  - unit cost,
  - break-even volume,
  - or product-platform relevance.
- Co-funders could receive **limited governance rights** tied to monitoring compliance, not veto power over all restructuring actions.

**Purpose:** address neighbouring governments’ need for binding protection while preserving management’s ability to rationalize.

---

### Proposal 3: Use a tiered recovery structure for unsecureds to reduce zero-sum conflict
- Unsecured bondholders could receive:
  - a **base equity recovery**,
  - plus **warrants or contingent value rights** if operating performance exceeds a threshold.
- If the trust is given enhanced value, unsecureds would receive **offsetting upside participation** rather than a direct parity claim.
- This preserves the principle that no group is “made whole,” while reducing the fight over fixed recovery percentages.

**Purpose:** make the package more flexible without pretending every unsecured claimant can be treated identically.
