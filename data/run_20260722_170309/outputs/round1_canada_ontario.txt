We are prepared to co-fund, but only on terms that protect the domestic production footprint and employment in a real, enforceable way.

Let me be direct about our position.

### Where we agree
- **This must be a real restructuring.** We do not support preserving an unrepaired balance sheet.
- **Shared sacrifice is necessary.** Management is already changing, and creditors and labour both have to contribute.
- **Speed matters.** We understand the 60-day window and the need for finality.
- **A court-supervised fallback is acceptable** if it is the only way to get a viable result in time.

### Where we disagree
We cannot support a deal where:
- domestic plants are closed while foreign capacity is protected,
- our governments are asked to co-finance without a commensurate say,
- or the restructuring improves creditor treatment at the expense of the domestic industrial base.

That would be politically and economically unacceptable. We are not here to subsidize a transfer of jobs out of our jurisdictions.

### On plant and production footprint
We accept that some rationalization may be needed. But the cuts must be selective and justified by a credible operating plan. We will not support an outcome where the burden of adjustment falls disproportionately on our plants while other parts of the network are shielded.

If our funding is part of the rescue, then the domestic production share and associated employment must be protected through binding commitments. That means:
- enforceable production commitments for our plants,
- employment protections tied to the restructuring,
- and a restructuring plan that treats our jurisdictions as part of the core industrial base, not as expendable capacity.

### On ownership
We also expect an ownership position proportionate to our contribution. If we are co-funding the rescue and taking political risk to preserve domestic jobs, then we should receive a real stake in the restructured company, not symbolic treatment. Partnership means partnership.

### On the other parties
- **To management:** we support a viable company, but viability cannot mean hollowing out our industrial footprint.
- **To labour and the retiree trust:** we support protecting jobs and a durable outcome, but not if that means shifting the burden onto governments and taxpayers without structural commitments.
- **To unsecured bondholders:** you will need to take losses. But do not assume that creditor recovery can come from sacrificing domestic plants. That is not a fair restructuring.

### Our bottom line
We are willing to co-fund, but only if the deal includes:
1. **binding production and employment commitments for our domestic plants,**
2. **a proportionate equity or ownership stake reflecting our funding,**
3. **a viable restructuring that is not built on foreign plant protection at our expense,**
4. **and a process that produces an orderly, successful reorganization.**

If those conditions are not met, then we cannot commit public money.
