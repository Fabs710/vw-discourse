We are now into the point where the real conflict is fully visible, so I will be equally clear.

## My position
IG Metall remains prepared to negotiate a serious restructuring package.  
But we still do **not** see a justified case for:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a one-sided transfer of the crisis onto employees.

If management and capital want our cooperation, they must show a package that is **quantified, durable, and fair** — not just “flexible” for management.

---

## Where I agree

### To management
I agree that Volkswagen needs a structural reset.  
I also agree that a cosmetic compromise would be worthless.

You are right on one thing: the company must become more competitive. But you are still not answering the central question: **why does that require closure or forced layoffs rather than a negotiated, structural no-closure package?**

You keep saying “the numbers require it,” but you have not yet put forward a credible, fully quantified alternative package that proves closures are unavoidable. Until that happens, closures remain a **choice**, not a necessity.

### To Lower Saxony
I agree that a viable Volkswagen is essential and that hollowing out the German industrial base would be a mistake.  
I also agree that site preservation must not be merely symbolic.

But your leverage only matters if it is used to enforce a real industrial solution, not to accept a slow erosion later. So yes: use your blocking position. Do not allow “capacity rationalization” to become closure by stealth.

### To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of co-determination,
- and no one-sided burden shifting.

I support your insistence that workers should not be the only adjustment variable.

---

## Where I disagree

### To management
You are still treating closures and forced redundancies as if they were normal options that the workforce must somehow disprove.

That reverses the burden in a way we do not accept. If you want those options on the table, then you must prove:
1. they are unavoidable,
2. they produce better results than negotiated alternatives,
3. and the social and industrial damage is justified.

So far, you have not done that.

I also reject the idea that a plant can be “left open” while being stripped of product, shifts, and future until it becomes uneconomic. That is closure in slow motion, and we will oppose it just as firmly.

### To investors
You are right to demand durable savings. But you are too quick to assume that only aggressive structural cuts can deliver them.

That is not credible unless you also price in:
- strike risk,
- implementation risk,
- loss of skills,
- industrial conflict,
- and governance damage.

A deal that looks stronger on paper but blows up in execution is not “investor-grade.” It is bad risk management.

### To the controlling owners
You say you will not back a cosmetic settlement. Fine. We agree.

But you are still not showing enough readiness to support a fair burden-sharing settlement that protects jobs and the collective framework. If you want durability, then support:
- visible owner contribution,
- investment commitments,
- and binding employment security.

Do not hide behind “if the numbers require it” while refusing to test alternatives seriously.

---

## What we would accept
Let me be precise.

IG Metall can support a package that includes:
- temporary wage moderation,
- reduced bonuses or special payments,
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity agreements,
- voluntary exits,
- and capacity reduction through product and shift rationalization,

**if and only if** it is paired with:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site and employment security,
- management cost restraint,
- owner-side support,
- and measurable investment in German locations.

That is the minimum basis for serious bargaining.

---

## On the moderator’s bridge ideas

### 1) Phased no-closure pact with quantified performance gates
This is only acceptable if the gates do **not** reopen the same threat of closures or forced layoffs.

We can accept monitoring, targets, and review clauses.  
We do **not** accept a disguised ultimatum where a missed target becomes the automatic pretext for layoffs.

If performance misses, the next step must be:
- more negotiated measures,
- more internal redeployment,
- more investment discipline,
- and more owner/managment contribution.

Not a switch to plant closure.

### 2) Capacity reduction through product and shift reallocation
This is the only route that sounds practically workable — if it is real.

Acceptable:
- shift consolidation,
- product reallocation,
- internal transfers,
- retraining,
- and temporary working-time measures.

Unacceptable:
- starving a site of product,
- hollowing it out,
- and then claiming the plant is “still open” while the workforce pays for the erosion.

### 3) Burden-sharing with bounded contributions
Yes. But burden-sharing must be shared.

That means:
- employees contribute temporarily and proportionately,
- management contributes visibly,
- owners contribute materially,
- and the company commits to German sites.

Without that, burden-sharing becomes a euphemism for unilateral workforce sacrifice.

## My direct answer to the round 4 question
What is the minimum package we would support?

Here it is:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **A binding employment and site framework through 2030**
4. **Quantified employee contributions that are temporary**
5. **Management pay and overhead restraint**
6. **Owner-side support and investment backing**
7. **Internal redeployment, retraining, and productivity measures**
8. **Capacity reduction through shift and product rationalization only**
9. **Transparent monitoring**
10. **No backdoor mechanism to reopen layoffs or closures later**

If those conditions are met, we can negotiate the burden-sharing details seriously.

- compulsory redundancies,
- a hollowing-out strategy,
- a “review clause” that is really just a delayed layoff trigger,

That would not be a fair compromise. It would be pressure dressed up as strategy.
