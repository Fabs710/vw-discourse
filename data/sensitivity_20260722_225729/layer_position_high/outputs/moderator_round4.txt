The discourse is now centered on one core impasse:

## Core impasse
The dispute is **not** primarily about whether Volkswagen needs restructuring. All sides now accept that it does.

The impasse is about **what counts as a credible restructuring**:

- **Labor / Lower Saxony** require a **binding no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy framework** with real protection against hollowing out.
- **Management / institutional investors / controlling owners** require **quantified, durable, enforceable savings** and do not accept a deal that preserves sites in form but not in economics.
- The **expert assessment** indicates that a no-closure solution is possible only if it is **deep enough to approximate closure economics** through permanent cost, capacity, and complexity reduction.

So the central blockage is this:

> **Can the parties design a no-closure package that is economically close enough to closure, while also being legally and politically credible as a no-closure settlement?**

That remains unresolved.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: Hard no-closure pact with an economic equivalence test
A structured settlement could be built around:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding employment and site security through 2030**
- **Quantified annual savings targets**
- **Quarterly monitoring**
- **A pre-agreed independent assessment of whether the savings gap is being closed**

To address investor and management concerns, the pact would include an **economic equivalence test**:
- If savings, capacity reduction, and productivity gains fall below a defined threshold, the parties do **not** automatically move to closure,
- but they must first trigger a **second-round remedy phase** with additional owner support, product reallocation, management cost cuts, and deeper internal restructuring.

This keeps the no-closure commitment intact while creating a serious enforcement mechanism.

---

### Proposal 2: Capacity reduction through product and shift reallocation, with site guarantees
A second option is a footprint rationalization package that:
- reduces **shifts**
- reallocates **products**
- consolidates **internal functions**
- increases **utilization**
- and uses **voluntary exits, redeployment, retraining, and temporary working-time changes**

This would be paired with:
- **site guarantees**
- a prohibition on **closure by stealth**
- a defined minimum product allocation for German plants
- and a governance clause requiring co-determination sign-off on any major future footprint change.

This may bridge the gap between “no closure” and “real capacity reduction” if the parties are willing to quantify the site-level volume shifts.

---

### Proposal 3: Burden-sharing package with matched contributions
A third path is a balanced package in which each side commits something measurable:

- **Employees:** temporary wage moderation, flexibility, retraining support, voluntary exits
- **Management:** overhead cuts, compensation restraint, accountability for delivery
- **Owners:** capital support, investment backing, and product commitments
- **Company:** binding job and site framework through 2030, plus transparent monitoring

This would be framed as:
- **temporary employee contribution**
- in exchange for
- **binding security and investment commitments**

This may be the most realistic basis for a settlement if the parties accept that the package must be **visible, measurable, and asymmetrical but bounded**.
