### Core impasse
The dispute has converged on a single fault line:

- **Labor / Lower Saxony** require a **no-closure, no-compulsory-redundancy** settlement with binding job and site security.
- **Management / owners / investors** require **credible structural cost and capacity reduction** and do not accept unconditional exclusions of closures or forced layoffs.

So the impasse is not over whether Volkswagen must restructure. It is over **whether restructuring can be made economically credible without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, and if so, **how binding that guarantee can be made** without leaving management and capital with insufficient adjustment tools.

A secondary impasse is the **trigger mechanism**: labor wants triggers only for more negotiation and investment, while management and capital want triggers that preserve the option of harder measures if performance misses.

---

### Facilitator compromise positions
#### 1) Phased no-closure pact with quantified performance gates
A possible bridge is a **time-bound no-closure pact** for all German plants through a defined interim period, paired with:
- agreed savings target,
- quarterly joint monitoring,
- quantified productivity and utilization milestones,
- automatic escalation to a new bargaining round if targets are missed,
- and pre-agreed “next-step” measures limited first to redeployment, shift changes, retraining, and temporary burden-sharing.

**Purpose:** gives labor site security now, while giving management and investors a disciplined review structure.

---

#### 2) Capacity reduction through product and shift reallocation, not legal closure
A second bridge is to define capacity reduction strictly through:
- product allocation,
- shift consolidation,
- internal transfers,
- retraining,
- and temporary working-time flexibility,

with an explicit prohibition on legal closure or forced redundancy **during the pact term**, provided utilization and cost targets are met.

**Purpose:** allows real structural adjustment without immediate site elimination, while still producing measurable savings.

---

#### 3) Burden-sharing package with asymmetric but bounded contributions
A third bridge is a **burden-sharing agreement** in which:
- management takes visible pay restraint and overhead cuts,
- owners provide capital or investment support,
- employees take temporary, proportionate contributions,
- and the company grants a binding employment and site framework to 2030 **subject to jointly verified performance milestones**.

**Purpose:** makes sacrifices visibly shared and ties concessions to enforceable future commitments.

---

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### Works Council (Betriebsrat)
If no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies are non-negotiable, **what specific quantified measures would you accept that produce enough permanent savings to satisfy management and investors without becoming a hollowed-out site strategy?**

#### IG Metall
What is the **minimum measurable package** of wage, time, and productivity contributions you would support that is still credible as a structural solution rather than a temporary pause?

#### Group & Brand Management
What exact combination of cost reduction, capacity reduction, and productivity gain would you accept as sufficient to keep every German plant open, and **what numbers would you put on the table to prove it?**

#### State of Lower Saxony
If you insist on no closures, **what enforcement mechanism would you support to ensure the plants remain economically viable rather than merely open on paper?**

#### Controlling Owners
What level of quantified improvement would convince you that a no-closure solution is economically acceptable, and **are you prepared to back it with capital or governance support if that threshold is met?**

#### Institutional Investors
What would count for you as “durable and enforceable” enough to support a no-closure deal, and **would you accept a phased arrangement if it contained hard metrics and a credible fallback process?**
