### Core impasse
The central blockage is a **trade-off between structural cost reduction and employment/site protection**:

- **Management / investors / owners** are insisting that Volkswagen needs **durable, material savings** to restore competitiveness, and they do not accept a solution that merely postpones adjustment.
- **Works council / IG Metall / Lower Saxony** are insisting on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding site/job protection**, with co-determination preserved.
- The disagreement is not over whether change is needed, but **how far structural change may go, and whether it can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs**.

In short:  
**The parties agree that VW must restructure; they disagree on whether restructuring can be done while fully preserving all German sites and avoiding compulsory redundancies.**

---

### Possible compromise positions to explore
Below are **facilitator proposals** for bridging the gap. They are not recommendations, only concrete options for discussion.

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a binding employment pact
A package could combine:
- a **time-limited employment guarantee** rather than an open-ended one,
- **no compulsory redundancies during a defined period**,
- **internal redeployment, retraining, and reduced working time** as primary adjustment tools,
- and a **joint review clause** with trigger points if competitiveness targets are not met.

This would give labor-side stakeholders security for a defined horizon, while giving management and investors a mechanism to revisit the package if savings do not materialize.

#### 2) Capacity reduction without formal plant closures
A compromise could separate:
- **site preservation** from
- **capacity rationalization**.

That means maintaining all German plants in legal/structural terms, while still:
- reducing shifts,
- reassigning product lines,
- consolidating selected functions,
- and lowering output capacity through negotiated, reversible measures.

This may address the “no closures” red line while still delivering meaningful efficiency gains.

#### 3) Burden-sharing package tied to measurable investment and savings
A negotiated deal could combine:
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- working-time flexibility,
- management compensation restraint,
- owner-backed investment commitments,
- and a quantified savings target with transparent reporting.

In this model, employee concessions are explicitly matched by contributions from management and owners, and every concession is tied to measurable industrial commitments.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
You insist on no closures, no forced layoffs, and binding guarantees to 2030.  
**Question:** What specific restructuring measures would you accept that produce verifiable, durable savings comparable to plant closure scenarios, while still preserving jobs and co-determination?

#### IG Metall
You support burden-sharing but reject one-sided cuts.  
**Question:** What is the union’s minimum acceptable package for temporary employee contributions, and what concrete commitments from management and owners would be required in return?

#### Group & Brand Management
You say deep structural cuts are necessary, but you have not yet specified the exact boundary between acceptable and unacceptable measures.  
**Question:** Which exact combination of capacity reduction, labour-cost measures, and redeployment would you consider sufficient to rule out closures and compulsory redundancies?

#### State of Lower Saxony
You reject closures and forced redundancies, but also want a viable restructuring.  
**Question:** What forms of capacity adjustment would Lower Saxony accept if they preserve all German sites legally but still reduce output or shift allocation materially?

#### Controlling Owners
You want competitiveness restored without value-destructive conflict.  
**Question:** What level of structural savings and implementation certainty would be sufficient for you to support a no-closure settlement, even if it requires temporary concessions elsewhere?

#### Institutional Investors
You want durable savings and clear margin improvement.  
**Question:** What measurable indicators and enforcement mechanisms would convince capital markets that a no-closure compromise is economically credible rather than merely delayed adjustment?
