We agree on the diagnosis: Volkswagen needs a **real** restructuring, not a cosmetic one.

But from our perspective as dispersed capital-market shareholders, the only acceptable outcome is one that **credibly restores competitive margins, lowers the cost base durably, and is enforceable**. If the package does not do that, then preserving sites and slogans is just value destruction with nicer language.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current structure is not competitive enough.
- **The workforce side is right** that a fake compromise is worthless.
- **Lower Saxony and the owners are right** that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.

So far, the disagreement is not about whether change is needed. It is about whether the proposed “no closure” route actually delivers enough structural improvement.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council
You keep saying closures and compulsory redundancies are unacceptable in principle.

From a shareholder standpoint, that is not a defensible starting point unless you can prove the alternative closes the economic gap. We have not seen that proof.

Working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, and temporary concessions may help, but they are **not automatically equivalent** to durable structural cost reduction. If the package leaves the company with excess capacity, elevated fixed costs, or weak margin recovery, then it is not good enough.

We also do not accept semantic substitution:
- a plant that is legally open but economically hollowed out,
- capacity reduced in name only,
- or a “review clause” that simply delays the same problem.

That is not restructuring. That is postponement.

### To IG Metall
We agree that employees should not carry the burden alone. But “shared sacrifice” only matters if it is **material, measurable, and durable**.

Temporary wage moderation or labor concessions are not sufficient unless they are large enough to materially improve the economics and last long enough to matter. If the union’s proposal cannot show a quantified path to competitive margins, then it is not a credible alternative to harder measures.

### To management
You are right not to accept a cosmetic deal. But you still owe investors a **quantified bridge** from current performance to target margins.

What exactly is the savings target?
How much capacity comes out?
How much labor cost comes out?
What productivity gains are locked in?
When does return on sales reach target?

“Deep structural cuts” is not a plan. It is a category.

Also: if you want support for a no-closure settlement, you need to show that it is not just politically easier, but economically sufficient. Right now, that case is incomplete.

### To Lower Saxony
We respect the state’s role and the importance of industrial stability. But a blocking minority is not a mandate to preserve every site irrespective of economics.

We do **not** support closures for their own sake. But we also do not support an arrangement that uses political leverage to freeze an uncompetitive cost base. If the company cannot get back to target margins without meaningful capacity reduction, then “no closures” becomes a shareholder-value problem, not a principle.

### To the controlling owners
You are correct that conflict destroys value. You are also correct that a weak compromise is not enough.

But owners should not interpret “no closures” as acceptable unless it is backed by hard economics. The question is not whether a compromise is socially preferable. The question is whether it restores the business to competitive performance.

## What would move us
We could support a no-closure settlement only if it contains all of the following:

1. **Material and durable labor-cost reduction**
2. **Verifiable capacity reduction or output rationalization**
3. **Productivity gains with enforcement**
4. **A binding implementation timetable**
5. **Clear margin improvement**
6. **No open-ended deferral of the problem**
7. **Deal certainty**

If those conditions are not met, then the package is not investor-grade.

## Our bottom line
We are open to a negotiated solution. We are not open to a cosmetic settlement.

If the parties can produce a **quantified, enforceable, durable** package that restores competitiveness without destructive conflict, we will support it. If they cannot, then we will view the outcome as insufficient, regardless of how many sites are formally preserved.

If useful, we can next provide a **market-facing test list** for evaluating whether the eventual deal is actually value-accretive.
