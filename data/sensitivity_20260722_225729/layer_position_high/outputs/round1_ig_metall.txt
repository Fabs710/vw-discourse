We share the core line of the works council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no dismantling of the collective wage framework**.

Let me be equally clear on what is driving the union side. The workforce did not create this crisis, and it will not accept being asked to carry the full burden while management, owners, and capital markets remain untouched. If restructuring means job destruction, plant closures, and one-sided wage cuts, then that is not a solution — it is a transfer of the crisis onto employees.

At the same time, we are not closing the door to a negotiated settlement. We already signalled willingness to discuss **shared-sacrifice measures**: temporary or reversible labour-cost contributions, working-time adjustments, capacity reductions, internal redeployment, qualification, and productivity measures. But any contribution from employees has to be matched by real contributions from management and owners, plus binding safeguards for sites and employment.

So our position is:

- **No closures.**
- **No forced layoffs.**
- **No open-ended job insecurity.**
- **No one-sided wage dumping.**

If management wants our cooperation, it must come forward with a credible alternative that:
1. protects all German sites,
2. secures jobs through a binding agreement,
3. includes a fair burden-sharing package from all sides,
4. and preserves the collective bargaining framework.

If the only answer remains cuts at the expense of workers, then we will escalate industrial pressure further. We are prepared to bargain hard, but we are not prepared to accept that employees pay for a crisis they did not cause.
