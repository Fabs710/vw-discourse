Lower Saxony takes the concerns on both sides seriously, but I need to be equally direct: **a restructuring that destroys jobs and closes plants in Germany is not acceptable to us**.

We recognize the pressure Volkswagen is under. The competitiveness problem is real, and we do not deny that the company needs a durable path back to strength. But the answer cannot be to treat German plants and the workforce as the first and easiest lever. Volkswagen is not just any firm; it is a regional economic anchor, and the company’s long-term health depends on preserving its industrial base, skills, and social partnership.

Where I agree:
- Management is right that the current cost and capacity structure must be addressed.
- The workforce side is right that employees did not create this crisis and should not be made to bear it alone.
- A purely cosmetic solution would be irresponsible.

Where I disagree:
- **Plant closures in Germany are a red line for us.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not a credible basis for a durable settlement.**
- Any approach that tries to sideline co-determination or weaken our role under the VW Law would meet firm resistance.

We are prepared to support a serious restructuring, but it must be built on:
1. **No closures of German plants**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **A binding employment and site framework**
4. **Negotiated, reversible savings measures**
5. **A credible industrial plan for investment, innovation, and productivity**

That means the company, owners, management, and workforce all need to contribute. We can discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity gains,
- moderated and temporary labour-cost adjustments,
- and targeted capacity reductions that do not amount to site destruction.

But I want to be explicit: if the proposal remains centered on closures or large-scale job losses, then we will not support it. That would harm the region, undermine social stability, and weaken Volkswagen’s long-term foundations. We are willing to play a constructive role in a restructuring package, but not in dismantling the company’s regional base.

So my message to management is: bring forward a credible alternative that preserves sites and employment while restoring competitiveness. If you do that, we will engage seriously. If not, you should expect clear opposition from Lower Saxony.
