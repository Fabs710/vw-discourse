## Core impasse

The central impasse is **not whether Volkswagen needs restructuring**. All sides accept that it does.

The impasse is over **how much structural reduction is necessary, and what form it may take**:

1. **Management / investors / owners** want a package that produces **credible, durable cost and capacity reduction**, and they are **not willing to preclude closures or compulsory redundancies in principle** if the numbers require them.
2. **Works Council / IG Metall / Lower Saxony** insist on **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no weakening of co-determination**, and they will only accept restructuring if it is **reversible, negotiated, and site-preserving**.

So the blockage is a clash between:
- **economic sufficiency and flexibility** on one side, and
- **binding employment and site guarantees** on the other.

A second, related impasse is **trust in “capacity reduction without closure”**:
- labor and Lower Saxony fear it is closure by another name;
- management and investors fear it may be too weak to fix the problem.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise position 1: Conditional no-closure pact with hard triggers
A time-limited agreement could be structured as follows:
- **No closures and no compulsory redundancies during the pact period**
- **Binding labor-cost, productivity, and capacity targets**
- **Joint monitoring with representatives from management, labor, and Lower Saxony**
- **A narrow set of pre-agreed triggers** if targets are not met, such as escalation to additional voluntary measures, retraining, or negotiated shift reductions before any discussion of more drastic steps

This may bridge:
- **Labor / Lower Saxony**, because it preserves sites and jobs in the near term;
- **Management / investors**, because it makes the deal conditional on measurable performance.

---

### Compromise position 2: Capacity reduction through product and shift rationalization, not legal closure
A second option is to separate **capacity reduction** from **formal plant closure** by using:
- shift reductions,
- product allocation changes,
- internal transfer,
- retraining,
- voluntary early exits,
- temporary working-time measures,
- and investment-linked modernization

This could be packaged with:
- **site-level production guarantees for core models**
- **no forced redundancies**
- **a binding employment corridor**
- **clear utilization thresholds reviewed jointly**

This may bridge:
- **Works Council / IG Metall**, because it avoids formal closures;
- **Management / investors**, because it still delivers capacity and cost adjustment.

---

### Compromise position 3: Burden-sharing package with asymmetric contributions and a review clause
A third path is a balanced burden-sharing deal:
- **Employees** accept temporary contributions in wages, hours, or flexibility;
- **Management** accepts management-cost reductions and governance discipline;
- **Owners** commit visible support, e.g. retained earnings, capital backing, or foregone distributions;
- **The company** commits investment and product allocation to German sites;
- **Review clause** after defined milestones, but only with joint governance and no automatic path to layoffs

This may bridge:
- **Investors / owners**, because it creates measurable savings;
- **Labor / Lower Saxony**, because it avoids one-sided burden shifting.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Works Council
**What specific, quantified savings and capacity reductions would you accept if they were achieved without formal plant closures or compulsory redundancies, and what exact enforcement mechanism would make that guarantee credible to you?**

### 2) IG Metall
**Which combination of working-time, wage, redeployment, and productivity measures would you endorse as sufficient to support a binding no-closure deal through 2030, and what minimum burden would you require from management and owners?**

### 3) Group & Brand Management
**What is your quantified minimum requirement for labor-cost and capacity reduction, and can you specify the exact conditions under which you would commit to no closures and no compulsory redundancies for the duration of a pact?**

### 4) State of Lower Saxony
**Would you support a binding restructuring package that preserves all German sites but includes substantial capacity rationalization, and if so, what measurable safeguards would you require to prevent hollowing-out by another name?**

### 5) Controlling Owners
**What level of owner-side contribution are you prepared to support to make a no-closure settlement economically credible, and what concrete performance metrics would you use before endorsing more drastic measures?**

### 6) Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified improvement in margins, capacity, and labor cost would make you support a no-closure settlement, and would you accept a time-limited pact with hard triggers instead of immediate closures or layoffs?**
