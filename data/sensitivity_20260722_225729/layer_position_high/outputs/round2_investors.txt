We remain focused on one issue only: **does the package credibly restore Volkswagen’s competitiveness and margins, or does it merely postpone the adjustment?**

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current cost base is too high.
- **The workforce is right** that a cosmetic deal is not acceptable.
- **Lower Saxony and the controlling owners are right** that a prolonged conflict would destroy value.

So far, that is the easy part. The hard part is whether the numbers actually add up.

### Where we disagree
#### 1) “No closures” cannot be an absolute if the economics fail
We hear the red lines from the works council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony. But from a shareholder perspective, the relevant red line is **structural underperformance**.

If Volkswagen cannot reach a competitive cost base without material capacity reduction, then preserving every site and guarantee is not value-preserving. It is value erosion disguised as social peace.

That said, we are **not** demanding closures for their own sake. We are demanding a package that produces **durable savings, implementability, and margin improvement**. If there is a no-closure path that truly achieves that, we would prefer it because it reduces execution risk.

#### 2) “Temporary” employee contributions are not enough unless they are large and durable
IG Metall and the works council keep referring to working-time flexibility, qualification, redeployment, and temporary labour-cost measures. Those may help, but they are not automatically sufficient.

We need to see:
- the **quantified savings**,
- the **duration**,
- the **enforceability**,
- and the **path to return-on-sales improvement**.

Without that, “shared sacrifice” is just a slogan.

#### 3) Avoiding conflict is not the same as creating value
We agree that industrial peace matters. But peace purchased by leaving the company structurally uncompetitive is not a solution. Investors cannot support a settlement that protects process at the expense of performance.

### To management
You still owe the market a **clear, quantified bridge** from today’s cost structure to competitive margins.

That means:
- how much capacity comes out,
- how much labour cost comes out,
- what productivity gains are locked in,
- and when the brand returns to target.

“Deep structural cuts” is not enough. If you want investors behind the deal, show us the economics.

### To the works council and IG Metall
You are right that no one wants a destructive confrontation. But you are still not answering the central question: **what alternative delivers savings comparable to what restructuring needs to achieve?**

If your answer is “capacity reduction without closures,” then show:
- the exact operational mechanism,
- the savings,
- and why the arrangement is durable.

If the answer is simply “guarantee every plant and job to 2030,” that is not a restructuring package from our point of view. That is an aspiration.

### To Lower Saxony
We understand and respect your role, but your position cannot amount to a permanent veto on any economically necessary adjustment. The state’s interest is also in a viable Volkswagen, not just preserving the current footprint.

If the compromise avoids closures but still meaningfully reduces cost and capacity, we can support that. If it does not, then you are effectively defending structural underperformance.

### To the controlling owners
We agree that a governance crisis or industrial war would be destructive. But we also need you to stop treating “no closures” as acceptable unless it is backed by hard savings.

Capital markets do not reward intentions. They reward execution and margin recovery.

### What would move us
We could support a no-closure settlement **only if** it contains all of the following:
1. **Material and durable labour-cost reduction**
2. **Verifiable capacity reduction or output rationalisation**
3. **Clear productivity commitments**
4. **A binding implementation timetable**
5. **Measurable margin improvement**
6. **No open-ended delay to 2030**

If those conditions are not met, then the package is not credible from an investor standpoint.

### Bottom line
We are not here to bless a symbolic compromise. We are prepared to support whichever path actually restores risk-adjusted value.

Right now, that means:
- no cosmetic deal,
- no indefinite protection of overcapacity,
- no settlement that leaves margins structurally impaired.

If the parties can produce a quantified, enforceable package that restores competitiveness without destructive conflict, we will back it. If not, we will regard the outcome as inadequate.
