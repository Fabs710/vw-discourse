We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

From a capital-markets perspective, Volkswagen’s current structure still looks too costly and too complex. That is the core issue, and it has not been answered by slogans, site-preservation language, or repeated assertions that closures are unnecessary. What matters to us is not whether the restructuring is politically comfortable, but whether it **durably restores competitive margins and lowers the cost base in a way markets can believe**.

## Where we agree
- **Management is right** that the current situation is not acceptable.
- **Labor and Lower Saxony are right** that a cosmetic deal would be value-destructive.
- **Owners are right** that prolonged conflict destroys value.

So yes: a weak compromise is worse than no deal.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and IG Metall
You keep insisting that closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table, but you still have not produced a **fully quantified alternative** that closes the structural gap.

You mention:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- retraining,
- voluntary exits,
- temporary wage moderation,
- and productivity gains.

Those are useful tools. But they are not yet a proven solution.

The expert analysis confirms what we already suspected: a no-closure package can work **only if it is very deep structurally**, removes substantial fixed cost, reduces capacity materially, and is enforceable. That means the burden on the no-closure camp is high. At the moment, I do **not** see evidence that your preferred package achieves closure-like economics.

If all you are offering is a delay mechanism, then that is not acceptable. We will not support a settlement that leaves VW with excess capacity, an uncompetitive cost base, and another crisis deferred to 2030.

### To Lower Saxony
We respect the political and governance reality, but a blocking position is not a substitute for economics. If you want no closures, then the no-closure deal must be **hard enough** to restore margins.

That means:
- real capacity reduction,
- not just legal preservation of sites;
- measurable cost-outs,
- not just promises;
- and an enforcement mechanism that does not simply turn missed targets into endless negotiation.

A “site is open” outcome is meaningless if the site is slowly starved of product and future volume. We will treat closure by stealth as economically equivalent to closure.

### To management
You are right that VW needs structural adjustment. But “deep structural cuts” is not yet a plan. You still owe the market a bridge:
- exact cost savings,
- exact capacity reduction,
- exact productivity gains,
- exact timing to margin recovery,
- and the expected value versus a closure-based alternative.

Without that, we cannot assess whether your preferred path is actually superior.

At the same time, I agree with you on one point: if the no-closure package is too soft, it is not enough. We will not reward a deal that protects the footprint but leaves the business uncompetitive.

### To the controlling owners
You are right to insist on durability. But durability comes from hard economics, not from political comfort.

If the workforce side cannot deliver a package that is economically comparable to closure, then the owners should not pre-commit to excluding harder measures. I am not saying closures are the preferred outcome. I am saying they remain legitimate if they are the only way to restore value.

## What would move us
A no-closure settlement could earn our support only if it contains all of the following:
1. **Material and permanent labor-cost reduction**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **Verified productivity gains**
4. **A binding implementation timetable**
5. **Measurable margin improvement by 2030**
6. **Strong enforcement and monitoring**
7. **No open-ended deferral of the adjustment**

If those conditions are met, we can support a no-closure path. If not, then it is not investor-grade.

## Bottom line
We are not defending closures as an ideology. We are defending **economic reality**.

So our position remains:
- We support restructuring.
- We support burden-sharing.
- We support a negotiated no-closure deal **only if** it is truly structural and financially credible.
- We do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves sites on paper while leaving Volkswagen structurally weak.

If the other stakeholders want our backing, they need to come back with hard numbers, not just hard lines.
