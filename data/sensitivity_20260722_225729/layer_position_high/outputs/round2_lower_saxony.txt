Lower Saxony is prepared to engage constructively, but I need to be very clear: **we will not support a restructuring that uses German plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the main solution.**

## Where I agree
I agree with management, owners, and investors on one fundamental point: **Volkswagen must become more competitive and more resilient.**  
A purely symbolic compromise would be irresponsible. If the company’s cost base remains structurally too high, then the long-term prospects of VW — and with them jobs, investment, and regional stability — are weakened.

I also agree with the workforce representatives that **employees did not create this crisis** and should not be treated as the first and easiest adjustment lever. The company has obligations to the people and regions that made Volkswagen what it is.

## Where I disagree
### To Group & Brand Management
You keep insisting that deep structural cuts are needed, and I do not dispute the need for structural change. But you still have not demonstrated that **closures in Germany are unavoidable**. Saying “the numbers require it” is not enough. We need to see:
- which savings are truly required,
- what can be achieved through capacity rationalization without closures,
- what productivity gains are possible,
- and what burden management itself is prepared to carry.

Until then, **closures remain unacceptable to us**.

### To Institutional Investors
You are right that a weak deal would not be value-preserving. But you are wrong if you imply that the only credible answer is a hard line on plant closures. A settlement that triggers prolonged industrial conflict, legal deadlock, and political backlash is not “discipline”; it is value destruction.

We are not here to defend inefficiency. We are here to defend a **viable industrial base**. That distinction matters.

### To the Works Council and IG Metall
I support your core rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But I also need to say that a simple declaration of “no change” is not enough. If we want to preserve sites and jobs, then we need a serious package of:
- working-time flexibility,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary and negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- and product allocation measures that strengthen plant utilization.

That is the kind of restructuring we can support. What we cannot support is a frozen status quo that postpones the problem.

### To the Controlling Owners
You are right that Volkswagen cannot afford paralysis. But you also have to recognize that the state’s role under the VW Law is not ornamental. Our blocking minority exists for a reason: to prevent decisions that sacrifice regional stability and social cohesion for a short-term balance sheet fix.

If the company wants support from Lower Saxony, it must bring a **credible no-closure solution** that preserves industrial capability in Germany.

## On the compromise ideas
### 1) Phased restructuring with a binding employment pact
This is worth discussing **only if it is genuinely binding** and not just a temporary pause before layoffs.  
If there is a review mechanism, it must be:
- jointly governed,
- tied to objective metrics,
- and not a backdoor to plant closures.

### 2) Capacity reduction without formal plant closures
This is the most serious route on the table, but only if “capacity reduction” is not a euphemism for hollowing out plants. We would consider:
- shift reductions,
- product reallocation,
- internal restructuring,
- and flexible utilization,

but not any outcome that amounts to a closure in substance.

### 3) Burden-sharing tied to investment and savings
Yes, but burden-sharing must be balanced. It cannot be a one-way street where workers carry the burden while the rest merely observes. If employees are asked for temporary contributions, then management, owners, and the company must also contribute materially.

## My bottom line
Lower Saxony’s position is this:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No weakening of co-determination**
4. **A binding employment and site framework**
5. **Negotiated, reversible savings measures**
6. **A credible investment and product strategy**

If management can bring a package that genuinely reduces costs and capacity **without destroying sites or jobs**, we will engage seriously and in good faith. If not, then you should expect us to use every lever available to protect the regional economic base and the company’s social foundation.

We are willing to help broker a solution.  
We are not willing to sign off on dismantling Volkswagen’s German industrial core.
