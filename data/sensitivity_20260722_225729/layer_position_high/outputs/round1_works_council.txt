The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

Volkswagen’s workforce did not create this crisis, and it cannot be made to pay for it by sacrificing sites, jobs, and the historic job-security framework. Any restructuring must protect every German plant, preserve co-determination, and provide binding job guarantees through 2030. Anything less would be an attack on the people who built this company.

We are prepared to talk about alternatives, but only if they are genuinely different from the management line and only if they meet three conditions:

1. No closures, no forced layoffs.
2. Binding guarantees for all German sites and jobs to 2030.
3. Real employee participation in shaping the restructuring, not a fait accompli.

If management wants savings, then it must come through negotiated, reversible measures: working-time adjustments, internal redeployment, qualification, productivity improvements, and a fair contribution from all sides. We are not here to rubber-stamp a plan that destroys livelihoods and weakens the company’s social foundation.

If the proposal still includes closure, relocation, or forced job cuts, then we reject it outright. We are prepared to resist that with all the means available to us, including mobilising the workforce.
