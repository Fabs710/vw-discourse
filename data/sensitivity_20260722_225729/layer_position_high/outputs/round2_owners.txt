I will be direct.

I agree with everyone on one point: Volkswagen needs a real restructuring, not a cosmetic one. If the package leaves the company structurally uncompetitive, then it fails. That is not a matter of ideology; it is a matter of long-term enterprise value.

Where I disagree is on the assumption that there is only one economically serious route. I do **not** accept that German plant closures and compulsory redundancies must be the default answer. Those measures may be arithmetically simple, but they are not automatically value-maximizing once you factor in conflict, execution risk, reputational damage, and the governance damage that could follow.

### To management
You are right that “status quo” is not acceptable. I support that.  
But you have still not proven that the only way to restore competitiveness is through open-ended threats of closures and forced layoffs.

What I need from management is not rhetoric, but a quantified case:
- how much structural savings are required,
- what exact measures deliver them,
- what capacity reduction is actually needed,
- and why a negotiated package cannot achieve the same effect.

If you cannot show that clearly, then your position is too blunt and too risky for a company of this importance.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I understand your red lines, and I share part of your concern: a prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value and weaken the company. You are right to reject a cosmetic deal.

But I will not support a stance that simply freezes all current structures and calls that a solution. “No closures, no layoffs” is only credible if you can demonstrate a **real, durable economic alternative**.

So I am not asking for surrender. I am asking for substance:
- material labour-cost contribution,
- meaningful productivity gains,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- working-time flexibility,
- and actual capacity reduction.

If your package cannot deliver comparable savings, then it is not enough.

### To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your insistence on stability and the industrial base. That matters. But stability without competitiveness becomes stagnation, and stagnation eventually turns into decline.

Your blocking position is understood, but it should be used to force a better solution, not to preserve every existing structure regardless of cost. A no-closure outcome is only defensible if it is economically credible.

### To institutional investors
You are correct to insist on durable savings and measurable improvement. That is also my benchmark. But I will not endorse a path that creates a prolonged governance crisis or industrial war in pursuit of short-term optics.

The market needs a package that is:
- credible,
- executable,
- durable,
- and not value-destructive in the process of implementation.

### My position
I am open to a settlement that avoids closures and forced redundancies **only if** it delivers real structural improvement. Otherwise, I will not support it.

What I can support:
- capacity reduction,
- product and shift consolidation,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- productivity measures,
- working-time adjustments,
- temporary and negotiated labour-cost contributions,
- and investment commitments tied to the restructuring.

What I cannot support:
- a symbolic deal,
- permanent overcapacity,
- or a prolonged confrontation that damages Volkswagen’s value.

If the parties want my support as controlling owner, then bring me a package that is economically credible, governable, and stable. If not, I will not pretend that a weak compromise is acceptable.
