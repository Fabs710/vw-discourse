## On the moderator’s three compromise ideas

### 1) Conditional no-closure pact with hard triggers
This is only acceptable if the triggers are **not** triggers for layoffs or closures.

If the “hard triggers” are just a delayed mechanism to reopen the same threat, then no — that is not a pact, that is a postponement.

We can discuss performance monitoring, but the consequence of underperformance cannot automatically be:
- plant closure,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or the weakening of guarantees.

The consequence must first be:
- management accountability,
- revised product planning,
- additional owner support,
- and further negotiated efficiency measures.

### 2) Capacity reduction through product and shift rationalization, not legal closure
This is the only direction that sounds remotely constructive, but again: substance matters.

If capacity reduction means:
- shift adjustments,
- internal reallocation,
- retraining,
- and modernization without job destruction,

then we can talk.

If it means:
- starving a site of product,
- reducing it to a shell,
- and then claiming closure was never declared,

then we reject it outright.

A hollowed-out plant is a closure in slow motion.

### 3) Burden-sharing package with asymmetric contributions and a review clause
Yes — burden-sharing is necessary. But it must be fair and enforceable.

That means:
- management contributes,
- owners contribute,
- employees contribute only temporarily and proportionately,
- and the company gives a **binding job and site guarantee to 2030**.

Without that guarantee, burden-sharing becomes one-sided extraction.
