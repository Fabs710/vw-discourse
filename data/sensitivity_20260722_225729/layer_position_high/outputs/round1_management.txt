I hear the force of your objections, but I need to be equally clear: **the status quo is not viable**.

Volkswagen faces a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary dip. German factory costs are too high, European demand is shrinking, and we are under pressure from faster, lower-cost competitors. If we do not reduce our cost base and capacity materially, we will not restore the brand’s margin, and we will not have the funds to invest in the products and technology needed through 2030.

So let me respond directly:

- **No closure at any price?** I cannot accept that as a starting position.
- **No compulsory redundancies?** I would prefer to avoid them, but I will not rule out the measures needed to achieve durable savings.
- **Binding guarantees to 2030 for every site and every job?** That is not compatible with the scale of change required unless it is paired with very substantial, credible cost relief.

What I am prepared to discuss is a **serious structural package** that produces real and lasting savings. That means some combination of:
- capacity reduction,
- plant-level consolidation,
- labour-cost measures,
- working-time flexibility,
- and, where unavoidable, site adjustments.

A cosmetic compromise that preserves every overcapacity and simply defers the problem is not acceptable. Nor is a deal that gives us a headline agreement but leaves the cost base uncompetitive.

To the works council and IG Metall: I understand your insistence on social responsibility, and I agree that internal redeployment, qualification, and productivity measures should be part of the solution. But they are not enough on their own. If you want management to rule out closures and forced redundancies, you need to bring forward a package that delivers **equivalent structural savings** and a genuine labour-cost contribution. Otherwise, we are just postponing the crisis.

So my position is this:
- I am open on the **method**.
- I am not open on the **need for deep structural cuts**.
- I need a settlement that reduces overcapacity and labour costs in a durable way.

If you want to test whether a no-closure alternative can achieve that, then bring me something concrete and credible. But I will not commit this company to a weak compromise that leaves Volkswagen uncompetitive.
