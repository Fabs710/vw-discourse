The works council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines.

Volkswagen’s workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be asked to pay for it by losing sites, jobs, and hard-won protections. Management’s attempt to break the historic job-security framework is unacceptable. If there is to be restructuring, it must be through alternatives that protect all German plants and secure employment through 2030.

That means we are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- working-time and productivity measures,
- negotiated employee contributions,
- and a credible industrial plan with future products and investment.

But any agreement must include:
- binding site guarantees for every German plant,
- a binding no-compulsory-layoff commitment,
- and a genuine co-determination role for the works council in shaping the restructuring.

If management comes with a fait accompli — closures, layoffs, or unilateral cost-cutting at the expense of the workforce — we will resist it with all means available, including mobilising the workforce and escalating industrial action. The burden of adjustment cannot be dumped on employees while shareholders and management preserve their freedom of action.

We want a viable future for the company. But that future must include the workforce, not sacrifice it.
