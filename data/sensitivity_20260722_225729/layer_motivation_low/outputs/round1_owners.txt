I will be direct.

Volkswagen needs a restructuring that restores competitiveness, but it also needs to preserve the long-term value of the enterprise. Those two objectives are not opposed. What is opposed is a solution that treats every German site as untouchable while leaving the cost base structurally uncompetitive.

So from the controlling owners’ perspective:

- We **support** a credible turnaround that improves margins and secures future investment.
- We **do not support** a prolonged confrontation that destroys value through escalation, strikes, or reputational damage.
- We **do not support** preserving the current structure simply because it is politically or industrially comfortable.

Where I agree with management:
- The situation is serious.
- Incremental tweaks alone will not be enough.
- Volkswagen cannot finance its future on a permanently inflated cost base.

Where I disagree with management:
- A restructuring that immediately defaults to plant closures and forced redundancies is not the only credible path.
- If alternatives can deliver durable savings and real competitiveness, they must be taken seriously.
- The issue is not just cost cutting; it is restoring a viable industrial model without triggering avoidable instability.

Where I agree with the works council and IG Metall:
- This cannot be solved by simply dumping the burden on employees.
- Durable labour peace matters.
- A negotiated package with capacity adjustments, working-time flexibility, and employee contributions is preferable to open conflict.

Where I disagree with the works council and union:
- A blanket refusal of plant closures and compulsory redundancies, as a starting red line, is too rigid if it prevents the company from reaching a viable economic outcome.
- Binding guarantees for every German plant and employment through 2030 may sound stable, but if they block necessary adjustment they risk weakening the company further.
- Co-determination is essential, but it is not a veto on structural reality.

Where I agree with Lower Saxony:
- Volkswagen’s legitimacy depends on being a responsible industrial anchor.
- Any restructuring that ignores the regional and social dimension would be shortsighted.
- Alternatives to closures should be pursued in good faith.

Where I disagree with Lower Saxony:
- Political pressure cannot substitute for economic viability.
- “No closures as a starting point” cannot mean “no hard decisions ever.”
- The state should help secure a durable settlement, not freeze an unsustainable footprint.

Let me be clear about what would move us:
1. A restructuring that delivers **real, durable cost improvement**
2. A credible plan to restore **margins and competitiveness**
3. A package that preserves **labour peace** and avoids value-destructive escalation
4. A solution that protects **governance stability** and does not weaken ownership rights

What would not be acceptable:
- A settlement that permanently locks in overcapacity
- A deal that postpones the problem without solving it
- An arrangement that leaves Volkswagen unable to compete and forces repeated crises

So our position is not “closures at any price,” but it is also not “no structural change.” The owners will support a solution that is economically real, durable, and stable. If the parties want our backing, they need to bring forward an alternative that can genuinely match the savings and competitiveness impact of the management plan without becoming a cosmetic compromise.
