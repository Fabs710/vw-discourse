Lower Saxony takes the crisis seriously, and we are not here to defend the status quo for its own sake.

We agree with management on one point: Volkswagen needs a credible restructuring that restores competitiveness and secures future investment. If the company’s cost base is structurally too high, then that must be addressed. But we do **not** accept that the only way to do that is by threatening plant closures in Germany or by pushing the burden almost entirely onto the workforce.

Let me be equally direct:

- **We do not accept German plant closures as a starting point.**
- **We do not accept compulsory redundancies as the preferred solution.**
- **We do not accept a restructuring that destroys jobs and weakens Volkswagen’s regional role.**

Volkswagen is not just any company. It is a central industrial anchor in Lower Saxony, with deep social and economic significance. A restructuring that strips out sites and employment here would not only be politically damaging; it would also risk undermining the company’s long-term social legitimacy and industrial stability.

To the works council and IG Metall: I understand and support your insistence on protecting jobs, sites, and co-determination. That is not an obstacle to a solution; it is a condition for a durable one. A negotiated package with capacity reductions, working-time adjustments, productivity improvements, and employee contributions can be part of the answer — **if** it is paired with a real industrial strategy and binding commitments on future products and investment.

To management: if you want Lower Saxony to support a restructuring, you must show that you are pursuing **alternatives to closures** in good faith. You cannot demand a blank cheque for site cuts and then call that “necessary.” We are prepared to examine measures that reduce costs materially while preserving the company’s presence in Germany. But the burden must be shared, and the solution must be reversible enough to avoid irreversible damage.

So our position is this:

1. **We are open to a restructuring package.**
2. **We require serious alternatives to closures and mass layoffs.**
3. **We expect management and owners to contribute, not just employees.**
4. **We insist on protection of Volkswagen’s German industrial base and co-determination role.**

If a proposal comes forward that clearly preserves employment and sites while improving competitiveness, we will engage constructively. If it is a disguised closure programme, we will oppose it.

The question is not whether Volkswagen must change. It must. The question is whether it does so by destroying the foundations of its success, or by negotiating a viable path forward with its workforce, its regional stakeholders, and its owners. Lower Saxony will not support the first.
