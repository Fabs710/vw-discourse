### Core impasse
The central blockage is between two different definitions of a “serious” restructuring:

- **Labour / Lower Saxony** require a binding **no-closure, no-forced-layoff** framework as the precondition for trust.
- **Management / owners / investors** require enough **fixed-cost and capacity removal** that they do not see a no-closure package as credible unless it is economically close to closure-based savings.

So the dispute is not only about instruments; it is about **whether the deal must be judged by social red lines first or by cost-equivalence first**.

A second, related impasse is that both sides want **durability through 2030**, but they differ on what durability means:
- Labour means binding protection from closures and layoffs.
- Management means protection from a cost structure that cannot be reopened if market conditions change.

---

### Possible bridge positions
Below are facilitator-style compromise positions that could be tabled for reaction:

#### 1) “No closures through 2030, but with quantified capacity resets”
A package could be structured around:
- no German plant closures until 2030,
- no compulsory redundancies as the default rule,
- but **binding plant-by-plant capacity cuts**, shift reductions, and model consolidation,
- with explicit annual savings targets and a trigger mechanism if targets are missed.

This gives labour the site-protection they require while giving management a measurable restructuring path.

#### 2) “Hard guarantees in exchange for harder contribution”
A second bridge would pair:
- binding site and job protection,
- with a **larger employee contribution** than currently discussed,
- including wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, redeployment, and tighter productivity commitments.

This would test whether labour’s red lines can be matched by a stronger economic package.

#### 3) “Two-stage deal with review gates”
A third option is a staged settlement:
- **Stage 1:** immediate peace agreement to 2030 with no closures and no forced layoffs,
- **Stage 2:** pre-agreed review points tied to utilization, savings delivery, and investment milestones,
- with escalation only if agreed benchmarks are missed.

This would reduce immediate confrontation while preserving management’s demand for enforceability.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Management
What exact plant-level savings target would make a no-closure package acceptable to you, and what are the minimum capacity reductions you require to consider closures unnecessary?

#### To Works Council / Betriebsrat
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies are guaranteed, what specific workforce and productivity concessions are you prepared to quantify now, rather than in principle?

#### To IG Metall
What enforceable mechanism would you accept to ensure that a no-closure deal remains durable through 2030 without becoming a frozen structure?

#### To Lower Saxony
Would you support a binding non-closure package if it includes substantial capacity reduction, and if so, what measurable thresholds would you require for it to be politically defensible?

#### To the Controlling Owners
What level of economic improvement would be sufficient for you to back a non-closure solution, even if it preserves more sites than a closure-based plan would?

#### To Institutional Investors
Would you treat a deeply quantified non-closure package as investable if it clearly improves margins, or do you require footprint reduction by definition?

#### To the Expert
What minimum combination of capacity reduction, recurring labor-cost savings, and governance triggers would you specify as the threshold for a credible 2030 settlement without closures?
