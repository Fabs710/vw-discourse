I will be direct.

We agree on the diagnosis: Volkswagen needs a real restructuring, not cosmetic measures. We also agree that a prolonged conflict would destroy value. That is precisely why I am not interested in gestures, slogans, or a settlement that merely postpones the problem.

But I do **not** accept the line that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” can be treated as an absolute starting point **without** a package that proves the economics. And I do **not** accept a settlement that preserves today’s structure if it leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive through 2030.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right that incremental tweaks are not enough. You are right that the cost base is too high. And you are right that a cosmetic compromise would be a bad outcome.

### With the expert
The expert’s point is important: a non-closure solution can work **only if it is deep, durable, and enforceable**. That is credible. What is not credible is a shallow promise that avoids hard decisions and hopes the margin problem disappears.

### With Lower Saxony
You are right that industrial legitimacy and social peace matter. A destructive confrontation would be value-destroying. I also agree that co-determination and the company’s governance structure are real constraints, not inconveniences.

### With labour
You are right that a strike war and a broken social contract would hurt Volkswagen for years. You are also right that a purely cosmetic deal is worthless.

## Where I disagree

### With the works council and IG Metall
Your red lines are politically understandable, but they are not yet economically sufficient.

You keep saying:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- preserve sites and jobs,
- and still restore competitiveness.

That may be possible, but it is **not self-evident**. It must be proven with numbers. At the moment, what I hear is a refusal of one tool, not yet a complete alternative that closes the structural gap.

I also reject the idea that management is acting illegitimately simply because it keeps closures on the table. In a situation of structural overcapacity, keeping the full set of options open is responsible governance. It is not blackmail.

### With management
I also do not accept that closures should be used as a reflex or as a bargaining weapon. “Closure is possible” is not a strategy. If management wants owner backing, it must present a quantified alternative, not just a threat posture.

## What matters to us as controlling owners

Our test is simple:

1. Does the package restore competitiveness?
2. Is it durable?
3. Does it avoid value-destructive instability?
4. Does it preserve governance control and enterprise value?

If the answer to all four is yes, then we can support a non-closure solution. If not, we will not support a settlement that locks in an uncompetitive structure just to create the appearance of agreement.

## What I need to see

The discussion has now reached the point where the rhetoric is less useful than the numbers.

[SUMMON: independent plant-level restructuring expert to provide a quantified comparison of non-closure, hybrid, and closure-based scenarios, including fixed-cost removal, capacity reduction, headcount impact, annual savings through 2030, and execution risk under VW governance constraints]

We need plant-by-plant clarity on:
- how much capacity is actually removed,
- what recurring labor-cost savings are locked in,
- how much headcount reduction is achieved and by when,
- what product and investment commitments follow,
- and whether the package is durable through 2030.

## My position on the current debate

### To labour
I am open to a serious no-closure restructuring. I am **not** open to a deal that simply preserves the current footprint and calls it reform. If you want no closures, then show that the alternative removes enough cost and capacity to make closures unnecessary.

### To management
I support the demand for a real reset, but you need to stop sounding as if closure is the answer before the numbers are even on the table. Bring the quantified package first.

### To Lower Saxony
Your support for industrial legitimacy is welcome and important. But it cannot mean preserving every current structure regardless of economics. A viable company is also in the region’s interest.

## What would move me
A credible package would need to show:
- material and durable capacity reduction,
- meaningful recurring labor-cost contributions,
- headcount reduction through attrition and redeployment, and where unavoidable, stronger measures,
- binding investment and product allocation,
- and enforceable governance through 2030.

If that can be done without closures, I will support it. If it cannot, then I will not pretend that a weak compromise is value-preserving.

## Bottom line
We are open on the mechanism. We are **not** open on the economics.

I will support a settlement that restores competitiveness and protects long-term value. I will not support a settlement that preserves every site in form while weakening Volkswagen in substance.
