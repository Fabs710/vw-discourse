The core impasse is not about whether Volkswagen needs restructuring. All sides now accept that it does.

The impasse is about **what must be proven first**:

- **Labour / Lower Saxony** want a binding **no-closure, no-forced-layoff framework first**, with numbers used to design within that boundary.
- **Management / owners / investors** want a **quantified cost reset first**, with closures kept available until a non-closure package is shown to be economically sufficient.
- The **expert** has narrowed this to a technical threshold: a non-closure deal is only credible if it delivers **site-specific capacity cuts, durable savings, and enforceable governance through 2030**.

So the deadlock is a sequencing problem:
**red lines first vs. economics first**.

## Facilitator proposals for possible bridge positions

### 1) “Protected no-closure package” with hard performance gates
A possible compromise structure is:
- a binding **no German plant closures / no compulsory redundancies** commitment for the agreement period,
- paired with **plant-by-plant savings and capacity targets**,
- plus **pre-agreed review points** if targets are missed.

To address management and investors, the review would not reopen closures automatically, but would trigger a defined renegotiation process if milestones fail.

### 2) “Hybrid restructuring corridor”
A second bridge is a **hybrid corridor**:
- sites remain open,
- but each plant gets a required reduction in capacity, shifts, overhead, and model complexity,
- with some combination of wage moderation, working-time changes, attrition, and redeployment.

This would allow management to obtain real fixed-cost reduction while labour keeps its core red lines intact.

### 3) “Conditional security package”
A third option is a bargain of **security for flexibility**:
- labour and Lower Saxony accept deeper productivity and cost measures,
- management and owners accept a legally binding site-protection package,
- investors receive audited annual reporting on savings,
- and an independent expert verifies whether the package remains on track through 2030.

This does not resolve every disagreement, but it gives each side a mechanism to test credibility without immediate escalation to closures.
