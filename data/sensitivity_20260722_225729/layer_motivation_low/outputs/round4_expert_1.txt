## Minimum threshold for a credible non-closure package through 2030

A no-closure / no-forced-redundancy settlement can be credible only if it delivers **all three** of the following:

1. **Material, site-specific capacity reduction**
   - not just general efficiency gains
   - but explicit output cuts, shift removals, and model consolidation by plant

2. **Recurring labor-cost savings that are durable through 2030**
   - wage moderation, bonus restraint, working-time flexibility, redeployment/attrition
   - savings must be permanent enough to affect the structural cost base, not just one-off relief

3. **Enforceable triggers and governance**
   - verification of savings
   - agreed review points
   - consequences if targets are missed
   - but without a hidden backdoor to immediate closure commitments unless the deal is explicitly reopened

## Practical benchmark for “credible”
For a company in Volkswagen’s situation, a non-closure package usually needs to achieve roughly:

- **meaningful capacity reduction at multiple plants**, not just one isolated site
- **headcount reduction in the low five figures over time** if the overcapacity problem is genuinely large
- **recurring cost savings large enough to visibly improve margin trajectory by 2027–2030**
- **investment/product commitments** so cuts do not simply hollow out future competitiveness

If the package does not remove enough fixed cost, then it will likely be seen by management and investors as a delay rather than a restructuring.

## What makes it fail
A non-closure package fails if it is:
- mostly voluntary and therefore too slow
- dependent on temporary wage concessions only
- not plant-specific
- not tied to actual capacity removal
- not enforceable after the first year or two

## What I would need to assess it properly
To judge whether the package is sufficient, I would need:
- plant-by-plant utilization and fixed-cost data
- planned model allocation through 2030
- current and proposed shift system by site
- workforce mix by plant, including indirect labor
- recurring annual savings by measure
- capex and product commitments by site
- assumptions on attrition, redeployment, and productivity realization

## Bottom line
A non-closure package is viable only if it is a **real footprint and cost reset**, not a social compromise dressed up as restructuring. If management cannot show plant-level fixed-cost removal and durable savings, then the alternative will not be considered economically equivalent to closures.
