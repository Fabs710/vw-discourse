I’ll be direct as well.

The controlling owners’ position remains unchanged: **Volkswagen must restore competitiveness and margins, but not through a value-destructive spiral of conflict, paralysis, or permanent underperformance.**

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the investor side, and Lower Saxony on the diagnosis that **the current German footprint is too expensive and too complex** for the market environment. I also agree with the expert that:
- incremental tweaks alone are not enough,
- a durable solution must run through 2030,
- and any credible package has to be measurable and enforceable.

I also agree with the works council and IG Metall on one important point: **a prolonged confrontation would destroy value**. Strikes, reputational damage, and operational instability are not acceptable outcomes for any owner.

## Where I disagree
### 1) To the works council and IG Metall
I do **not** accept the argument that because alternatives to closure exist in theory, the company can simply preserve every existing arrangement and still remain competitive.

The expert did not say that non-closure measures are automatically sufficient. It said they can work **only if they are deep, durable, and tightly enforced** — and even then they usually do **not** fully match the structural savings of footprint rationalization.

So I do **not** accept:
- a blanket refusal of closures as an absolute starting point,
- a blanket refusal of compulsory redundancies in every circumstance,
- or binding guarantees for every German site if they freeze an uncompetitive structure.

That may preserve comfort, but it does not yet solve the economics.

### 2) To management
I also do **not** accept a posture that jumps too quickly from “we need restructuring” to “therefore closures and layoffs are the only serious answer.”

That is not a strategy; it is an escalation path. If management wants the owners’ backing, it must bring forward a **credible alternative package** and show the numbers. Threats are not enough.

A package that is “real” must still be judged on:
- durable savings,
- implementation feasibility,
- and whether it preserves labour peace without sacrificing competitiveness.

## What would move us
We will support a solution that does all of the following:
1. **material, durable capacity reduction**
2. **meaningful labour-cost contribution**
3. **headcount reduction primarily through attrition, redeployment, and voluntary mechanisms where possible**
4. **clear product and investment commitments**
5. **enforceable governance and milestones**
6. **a realistic path back to margin recovery**

We are open on the mechanism. We are **not** open on the economics.

## What we will not support
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that leaves overcapacity intact,
- a settlement that merely postpones the problem,
- or an agreement that permanently weakens Volkswagen’s value creation.

And equally, we will not support a destructive hard-landing approach if a durable, economically equivalent alternative is available.

## Bottom line
My position is not “closures at any price,” and it is not “no closures ever.”  
My position is: **show us a package that is economically real, durable, and stable.**

If the parties can produce a non-closure solution that truly matches the savings and competitiveness impact, we can support it. If not, then the discussion cannot be frozen by red lines alone. Value preservation requires honesty about what is and is not sufficient.
