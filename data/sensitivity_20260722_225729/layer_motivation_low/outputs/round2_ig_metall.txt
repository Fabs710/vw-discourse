The expert assessment is useful, but it does **not** move me off our core position.

It confirms what we have been saying from the start: **alternatives to closure can work if they are deep, durable, and enforced**. That is exactly why we are insisting on a negotiated package rather than accepting management’s closure logic as inevitable.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert, Lower Saxony, and even parts of management’s diagnosis on one point:  
**Volkswagen needs real structural savings, not cosmetic gestures.**

I also agree that:
- simple working-time tweaks alone are not enough,
- a durable agreement must run through 2030,
- and any solution has to be enforceable, not just rhetorical.

So yes: we need a package that is economically real.

## Where I disagree
The expert’s report does **not** prove that plant closures or forced redundancies are necessary. It says the opposite of what management is trying to imply:

- alternatives can work,
- but they must be **deeper** than management’s preferred quick fix,
- and they must be **institutionalized** through a binding agreement.

That is precisely the bargaining task.

What I reject is management’s repeated move from “we need savings” to “therefore closures are unavoidable.” That leap is not justified by the assessment. It says alternatives may require:
- stronger capacity reduction,
- multi-year labor contributions,
- and enforceable governance.

Fine. Let’s discuss that. But not on the basis of blackmail through closures and layoffs.

## Our clear position
We remain committed to these non-negotiables:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and employment security**
4. **A durable collective agreement framework**
5. **Management and owners must also contribute**

If those are treated as “too rigid,” then management is essentially saying workers should absorb the crisis while others retain full freedom of action. That is not burden-sharing.

## What we are prepared to discuss
The expert is right that a credible non-closure package must be substantial. We are ready to negotiate on that basis, including:

- **capacity reductions without site closures**
- **shift reductions and working-time adjustments**
- **temporary wage moderation / bonus restraint**
- **productivity and process improvements**
- **natural attrition and internal redeployment**
- **qualification and retraining**
- **long-term investment commitments for future models**

But here is the condition: these must come with **hard guarantees**.

That means:
- no compulsory layoffs,
- no hidden closure through “skeleton operations,”
- no one-sided erosion of the tariff framework,
- and no labor-only contribution while management and owners merely lecture about competitiveness.

## To management
If you want a deal, bring a quantified alternative that shows:
- how much capacity is removed,
- how much labor cost is reduced,
- what management and owners contribute,
- and how the result stays durable through 2030.

Do **not** bring a package that simply says: “accept layoffs now, and trust us that it will help later.” We have heard that before. It is not acceptable.

## To the owners, investors, and Lower Saxony
If you say you want labour peace and value preservation, then support a solution that actually secures both. A forced restructuring may look “decisive,” but it risks a prolonged conflict, strikes, loss of trust, and damage to Volkswagen’s industrial legitimacy.

That is not a side issue. It is part of the economics.
