Let me be equally clear in round 3: **our position has not changed**.

We can discuss restructuring. We can discuss capacity, working time, productivity, investment, and a fair employee contribution. We will **not** discuss German plant closures as a legitimate option, and we will **not** accept compulsory redundancies.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert, Lower Saxony, and even parts of management on one point: **Volkswagen needs a real industrial plan, not cosmetic adjustments**.  
I also agree that a prolonged conflict would hurt everyone. That is exactly why management should stop pushing the company toward confrontation.

I further agree that any deal has to be durable through 2030. A temporary patch would not protect jobs either.

## Where I disagree
### 1) On the “equivalent savings” argument
Management, owners, and investors keep repeating that non-closure alternatives are only acceptable if they are “economically equivalent” to closures.

That formulation is misleading.

We are not here to make the company’s preferred weapon mandatory. We are here to solve the crisis **without destroying sites and jobs**. If management needs savings, then it must earn them through a serious negotiated package — not use closures and layoff threats as the default benchmark.

The expert was explicit: alternatives can work if they are **deep, durable, and enforceable**. That is the path forward. It does **not** prove that closures are necessary.

### 2) On management’s threat posture
Management says it is “open” to alternatives, but keeps closures and compulsory redundancies on the table. That is not a neutral negotiating stance. It is leverage through fear.

A serious negotiation does not begin with:
- “accept cuts or we close plants.”

It begins with:
- “here is a quantified alternative that protects sites and employment while restoring competitiveness.”

Until management does that, its talk of openness is not credible.

### 3) On the owners’ and investors’ view
The owners and investors say they want results, not slogans. Fair enough. But their definition of results seems to start and end with cost removal, while ignoring the industrial and social destruction that closures would cause.

A plant is not a line item. It is:
- skilled labour,
- supplier networks,
- regional infrastructure,
- apprenticeship systems,
- and years of know-how.

Once destroyed, that cannot simply be “optimized” back into existence. So no, we do not accept the idea that closing a plant is just another efficiency measure.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a **real non-closure restructuring** with measurable effects, including:

- **capacity reductions without site closures**
- **shift reductions and working-time flexibility**
- **temporary wage moderation and bonus restraint**
- **redeployment and retraining**
- **natural attrition instead of forced exits**
- **productivity improvements**
- **investment and future-model commitments**
- **co-determination in implementation**

But let me be clear: these are acceptable only as part of a package that preserves jobs and sites. They are not a license for management to hollow out the company through backdoor closures or silent workforce cuts.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
1. **German plant closures**
2. **Compulsory redundancies**
3. **A “skeleton operation” that is closure in all but name**
4. **A deal that breaks the historic job-security framework**
5. **A settlement that weakens co-determination**
6. **A one-sided burden shift onto workers**

## On the moderator’s bridge proposals
I’ll respond directly.

### “Hybrid restructuring with no immediate closures”
If “hybrid” means plant closures later, then no.  
If it means capacity reduction, model consolidation, and investment without shutting German sites, then that is something we can discuss.

### “Time-limited peace agreement to 2030”
We are not against peace. We are against peace bought by surrender.  
A pact to 2030 is only acceptable if it includes:
- binding site protection,
- binding job protection,

Otherwise it is just a waiting room for the next attack.

### “Plant-by-plant differentiated solution”
This is the only sensible direction in the room — **if** it is used to protect sites, not to pick them off one by one.  
But again: site differentiation cannot become a euphemism for closures.

## What would actually move us
Only two things can move us:
1. **Binding guarantees**: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, protection to 2030.
2. **A quantified alternative package** that shows real savings without destroying the workforce.

Not promises. Not declarations. Not “we’ll see later.”  
A binding agreement.
