I’ll respond plainly.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: Volkswagen needs a **real** restructuring, not symbolic adjustments.  
I also agree that the solution must be **durable through 2030** and not just push the problem into the next bargaining round.

But I do **not** accept your repeated implication that this automatically means German plant closures or compulsory redundancies. The expert assessments do **not** prove that. They show something more precise: **non-closure alternatives can work if they are deep, durable, and enforceable**. That is the route we are prepared to negotiate.

What I reject is the move from “we need savings” to “therefore jobs and sites must be sacrificed.” That is not the only economic logic available, and it is not acceptable as the starting point.

### To the works council
I agree with you on the essentials:
- no German plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site protection,
- and real co-determination.

Those are not slogans; they are the minimum basis for trust. Without them, there is no burden-sharing — only one-sided pressure on workers.

### To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your support for industrial legitimacy and your refusal to let this become a pure spreadsheet exercise. That matters.

But the answer is not simply to preserve every existing structure unchanged. We do need real capacity reduction, better working-time design, productivity improvements, and a serious investment plan. If the state wants to help preserve jobs and industrial capability, then it should back a **binding non-closure restructuring**, not drift toward a closure logic.

### To the owners and investors
I hear your point: you want outcomes, not slogans. Fine. So do we.

But your definition of “outcomes” cannot be limited to immediate cost cutting at any social price. A forced restructuring that triggers prolonged strikes, destroys trust, and weakens Volkswagen’s industrial legitimacy will also destroy value. That is not a side issue; it is part of the economics.

### Where I stand
We are prepared to negotiate a **serious non-closure restructuring package** that includes:

- capacity reductions without site closures
- shift and working-time adjustments
- temporary wage and bonus moderation
- productivity and process improvements
- redeployment and qualification instead of forced exits
- investment and future-model commitments
- enforceable multi-year rules through 2030

But let me be equally clear: this is only acceptable if it comes with **hard guarantees** on:
1. **no German plant closures**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding site protection**
4. **protection of the collective wage framework**
5. **management and owner contributions as well**

### What would move us
Not rhetoric. Not threats. Not “closure if necessary.”

What would move us is a **plant-level quantified package** that shows:
- how much capacity is removed,
- how much labor cost is reduced,
- what management and owners contribute,
- and how the settlement remains durable through 2030 without forcing people out.

If that package is missing, then management is not negotiating a solution — it is demanding a concession.

### What we will not accept
We will **not** accept:
- closures in Germany,
- forced redundancies,
- a hollowed-out “skeleton” site,
- or a deal that shifts the full burden onto employees.

That remains our line.

We are ready for a hard but fair deal.  
We are **not** ready to accept that workers must pay for the crisis through plant closures or compulsory layoffs.  
If management wants a settlement, it must come back with a real alternative that protects jobs, sites, and the collective framework while delivering genuine savings.
