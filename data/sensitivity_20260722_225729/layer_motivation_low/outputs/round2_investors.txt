From the capital markets’ perspective, the discussion is moving in the right direction on diagnosis, but not yet on delivery.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the owners, and the expert on the core point: **Volkswagen’s German cost base is structurally too high**. The current footprint is not competitive enough, and incremental adjustments alone will not restore the margin profile investors need to see.

We also agree with the works council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony on one practical issue: **a prolonged strike war would destroy value**. Lost output, delayed launches, reputational damage, and management distraction all hit cash flow and margins. That is not acceptable.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support a settlement that looks politically balanced but leaves the economics broken.

That means:
- **no cosmetic compromise**
- **no deal that preserves overcapacity**
- **no package that offers employee concessions while failing to restore competitive margins**
- **no indefinite protection of every site if that freezes an uncompetitive structure**

The works council and union keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as if that by itself were a solution. It is not. It may be a legitimate negotiating stance, but it does not answer the investor test: **will the company come out with a cost base that can actually earn its cost of capital?**

## On the expert assessment
The expert has confirmed something important: **non-closure alternatives can work, but only if they are deep, durable, and tightly enforced**. That is the key point.

What I take from that is:
- alternatives to closures are acceptable **only if they deliver equivalent savings in practice**
- if they do not, then the restructuring is underpowered
- and underpowered restructuring simply postpones the next crisis

So I do **not** accept the union’s implication that because alternatives exist, closures are therefore off the table in economic terms. If the non-closure package cannot close the gap, then management is right to keep stronger measures available.

## To management
We support your insistence on a real restructuring. But we would caution against treating closure threats as a negotiation tactic without a credible quantified alternative on the table.

Investors need:
1. **specific savings numbers**
2. **a durable margin path**
3. **clear capacity reduction**
4. **enforceable labor-cost relief**
5. **a credible time line to 2030**

Bring us a package that proves the economics. Don’t ask the market to take faith on trust.

## To the works council and IG Metall
If you want investor support for a no-closure approach, you need to show that it is not just a preservation strategy. You need to demonstrate:
- how much capacity will actually come out,
- how much cost will actually disappear,
- how the savings will be locked in through 2030,
- and what happens if the numbers are missed.

A blank “no closures, no layoffs” rule is not enough. It protects employment, but it does not automatically protect equity value.

## To Lower Saxony and the owners
We appreciate the preference for industrial peace and regional legitimacy. But legitimacy does not pay the bill by itself. The company needs a settlement that is not just socially acceptable, but **economically decisive**.

That means you should back the solution that actually restores competitiveness, even if it is uncomfortable. If the politically easiest deal is also the financially weakest, it should not be supported.

## What would move us
We would back an outcome only if it includes:
- **material and durable capacity reduction**
- **verifiable labor-cost concessions**
- **meaningful simplification of the German footprint**
- **a credible path back to competitive margins**
- **reduced risk of renewed conflict**
- **binding commitments through 2030**

## What we will not back
We will not back:
- a settlement that merely defers the problem,
- a compromise that preserves too much fixed cost,
- or a deal that leaves the return-on-sales target structurally out of reach.

## Bottom line
We are not demanding closures as an article of faith. We are demanding **results**. If a non-closure package can genuinely deliver the same structural improvement, fine. If it cannot, then it is not a solution — it is a delay.
