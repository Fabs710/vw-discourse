We align with the works council on the core point: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies**.

Let me be equally clear from the trade union side: if management believes it can solve this crisis by making workers carry the full burden while protecting strategic room for shareholders and management, that will not happen. The peace obligation has ended, and our members are already prepared to act. We have mobilised warning strikes once; we can escalate further if necessary.

That said, we are not refusing restructuring in principle. We are prepared to discuss a **fair burden-sharing package** if it genuinely protects jobs and sites. That means:

- **capacity reductions** instead of closures,
- **working-time measures** and flexible arrangements,
- **temporary or negotiated employee contributions**,
- **moderation on pay elements** if there is a real reciprocal commitment from the company,
- and above all a **binding guarantee of employment and site security**.

But an “adjustment” that consists of layoffs, site closure threats, and permanent erosion of the collective agreement is not a solution. It is a unilateral cost-cutting programme disguised as restructuring.

What must be on the table if management wants a deal:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No German plant closures**
3. **A durable collective agreement framework**
4. **Management and owners also contributing**
5. **Credible investment commitments for future products and technologies**
6. **A negotiated path for capacity adjustment, not a destructive one**

We also reject any attempt to weaken collective bargaining by shifting the crisis entirely onto wages and protections. Employees can contribute, but only in a package where the company commits to their future. Otherwise, we will defend our members with all industrial means available.

So our message is simple:  
**We are ready for a solution, but not for a surrender.**
