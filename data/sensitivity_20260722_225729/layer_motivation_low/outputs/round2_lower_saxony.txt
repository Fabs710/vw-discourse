Lower Saxony takes the restructuring challenge seriously, but we will not endorse a solution that protects a spreadsheet while weakening Volkswagen’s real industrial base.

I will address each side directly.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: **Volkswagen needs a durable competitiveness reset**. The company cannot drift through this with temporary fixes alone. The expert’s assessment also confirms that.

But I disagree with your central framing: **“closures and compulsory redundancies remain on the table”** as a negotiating posture. That may be economically convenient for management, but in a company with Volkswagen’s governance, social role, and regional embeddedness, it is also politically reckless and industrially destabilizing.

You keep saying alternatives must be “equivalent.” Fine — then bring a package that is genuinely equivalent in effect, not just in rhetoric. So far, what you have presented is still a threat-led approach:
- threaten closures,
- threaten layoffs,
- then ask the workforce to accept major concessions.

That is not a credible basis for social partnership.

We are prepared to support **material capacity reductions, productivity gains, working-time flexibility, and employee contributions**. But we will not support a restructuring that destroys plant trust or uses closure threats as leverage to force one-sided sacrifice.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I agree with you that **forced redundancies are unacceptable as a preferred instrument**, and I share your view that Volkswagen’s legitimacy depends on protecting employment and site continuity.

However, I also have to be honest: a blanket refusal to discuss any structural adjustment can become self-defeating if it prevents the company from reaching a viable cost base. The expert has made clear that **non-closure solutions can work, but only if they are deep, durable, and enforceable**. That means we need more than declarations.

So I support your red line against closures and compulsory redundancies, but I want you to engage seriously on:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- measurable labour-cost contributions,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification,
- and binding investment commitments tied to future models.

What I cannot accept is a situation where the burden of adjustment is denied entirely while the company’s competitiveness continues to erode.

### To the owners and investors
I understand your concern about durability and margins. That concern is legitimate.

But I do not accept the implicit idea that regional employment protection and competitiveness are mutually exclusive. Volkswagen’s long-term value is not protected by a brutal short-term reset that tears up the company’s social compact. If that compact is broken, the political and industrial fallout will itself become a value problem.

So yes, we need real economics. But **real economics also includes industrial peace, regional legitimacy, and governance stability**. Those are not soft extras; they are part of the restructuring equation.

### Where I stand
Lower Saxony is prepared to support a serious package if it meets these conditions:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Real, quantified capacity reduction**
4. **Durable employee contribution, but not one-sided**
5. **Binding investment and product commitments**
6. **Protection of Volkswagen’s role as an industrial anchor in Lower Saxony**
7. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s veto role**

That is not “wishful thinking.” It is the minimum basis for a settlement that can survive politically and industrially.

### What would move us
A package could move us if it shows:
- which volumes are being reduced,
- how many jobs are preserved through redeployment and attrition,
- what management and owners contribute,
- and how the company secures future products without gutting sites.

### What will not move us
We will **not** move on:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced layoffs,
- or a restructuring that turns Volkswagen into a company that no longer anchors jobs and investment in the state.

If management wants Lower Saxony’s support, it must come forward with a real alternative package — not a closure ultimatum dressed up as realism.
