Here is the procedural readout of the plateau and a set of bridge proposals.

## Core impasse
The discourse is blocked on **one central question**:

**Can Volkswagen achieve a durable cost and capacity reset without German plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**

That question has split the group into two camps:

- **Employee-side and Lower Saxony**: insist that closures and forced redundancies are unacceptable as starting points or fallback tools.
- **Management, owners, and investors**: accept that alternatives may exist, but only if they produce **economically equivalent, durable savings**; they do not accept a structure that preserves overcapacity or leaves the cost base materially unresolved.

So the real impasse is not “whether restructuring is needed.”  
It is **what counts as a sufficient restructuring** and **who carries the burden of proof**.

A second, related impasse is **trust and enforceability**:
- employee-side actors want binding guarantees before any sacrifice,
- management and capital-side actors want binding savings before giving guarantees.

That creates a circular deadlock.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions
These are not recommendations, but **possible bridge formulations** that could narrow the gap:

### 1) Hybrid restructuring with no immediate closures, but binding capacity removal
A compromise could combine:
- no immediate German plant closure,
- no compulsory redundancies as the default instrument,
- but **binding capacity reductions**, shift removals, model consolidation, and site-specific output cuts,
- with a review clause tied to verified savings and market conditions.

This would let employee-side actors preserve the formal no-closure line, while giving management and capital-side actors a measurable footprint reduction.

### 2) Time-limited peace agreement with enforceable sacrifice on both sides
A second bridge is a **multi-year pact to 2030** with:
- employee-side contributions: working-time flexibility, wage/bonus moderation, redeployment, attrition;
- management/owner contributions: investment commitments, product allocation, overhead reduction, and no unilateral escalation to layoffs during the pact period;
- a jointly agreed trigger mechanism if savings targets are missed.

This would shift the dispute from “absolute guarantees” to **mutual commitments with monitoring**.

### 3) Plant-by-plant differentiated solution
Instead of a single all-sites rule, parties could negotiate:
- **site-specific packages** based on utilization, product pipeline, and fixed-cost burden,
- with stronger protection for viable strategic sites,
- and deeper consolidation measures at weaker or duplicated locations.

This gives management and investors a way to claim real structural adjustment while giving labor and Lower Saxony a basis for preserving the most strategically important plants.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### Works Council
If no closures and no compulsory redundancies remain absolute red lines, **what specific capacity reductions and labor-cost concessions would you accept as evidence of a real restructuring rather than a postponement?**

### IG Metall
What binding multi-year measures would the union be prepared to sign so that a no-closure settlement is not merely symbolic but genuinely durable through 2030?

### Group & Brand Management
What quantified alternative package can you table that demonstrates savings comparable enough to closure-based restructuring to justify dropping closure as an immediate threat?

### State of Lower Saxony
What level of capacity rationalization would the state regard as politically and industrially acceptable if it allows the company to avoid closures and preserve investment in the state?

### Controlling Owners
What concrete metrics would you require to conclude that a non-closure package is economically equivalent enough to support, rather than a delay of the same underlying problem?

### Institutional Investors
What measurable outcomes would be sufficient for you to back a no-closure settlement as value-preserving, even if it leaves some fixed costs in place?

### Independent Industrial Restructuring Expert
Can you specify the minimum combination of capacity reduction, labor contribution, and enforceability needed for a non-closure package to be credible through 2030?
