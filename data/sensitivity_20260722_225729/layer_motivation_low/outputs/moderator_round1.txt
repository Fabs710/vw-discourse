### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that Volkswagen needs restructuring and that the current situation is serious.
- There is broad agreement that **incremental tweaks alone are insufficient**.
- Most parties see value in a package that combines:
  - capacity adjustment,
  - working-time/productivity measures,
  - employee contributions,
  - and new investment / future product commitments.
- All sides prefer a negotiated solution over a prolonged escalation, because open conflict would be costly.

### 2) Core disagreements that remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  - Works Council and IG Metall reject them.  
  - Management will not rule them out.  
  - Lower Saxony and owners/investors prefer alternatives but do not fully exclude hard decisions.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Works Council and IG Metall reject them outright.  
  - Management will not give a blanket commitment.  
  - Others are open to negotiated solutions if savings are insufficient otherwise.
- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  - Labour side wants binding site and employment protection, plus strong co-determination.  
  - Management and investors warn that rigid guarantees may prevent necessary adjustment.
- **Scale and durability of savings:**  
  - Management, owners, and investors want materially lower costs and a durable competitiveness gain.  
  - Labour side insists that any savings must come with reciprocal commitments and must not be achieved by unilateral burden shifting.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to achieve sufficient cost reduction without closures or forced layoffs.**
- **How much employment and site security can be guaranteed without freezing an uncompetitive structure.**
- **What employee concessions are acceptable, and what management/owners will contribute in return.**
- **How to make any agreement durable and enforceable rather than temporary or symbolic.**
- **How to balance industrial peace, regional legitimacy, and investor expectations of margin improvement.**

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **quantified options**: compare specific restructuring scenarios by savings, job impact, and investment commitments.
- Ask each side to name:
  1. non-negotiables,
  2. acceptable substitutes,
  3. and minimum conditions for a deal.
- Focus the next round on a **single package framework**: capacity, labor contribution, investment, governance, and enforcement.
- Clarify whether any alternative to closures and compulsory redundancies can deliver **equivalent, measurable, durable savings**.
