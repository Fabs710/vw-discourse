We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

That is the starting point, and it is non-negotiable. Volkswagen has built its strength on its workforce, its sites, and the historic job-security framework. Management cannot simply tear that up because it failed to secure competitiveness through better planning, smarter product strategy, and timely investment.

Our position is clear:

- Keep every German plant open.
- Rule out forced layoffs.
- Protect the job-security guarantees.
- Preserve co-determination and real worker influence over any restructuring.
- If savings are needed, they must come from negotiated, socially responsible measures, not from closing sites and throwing people out.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect employment and industrial capacity: temporary working-time reductions, internal reallocation, qualification and transformation measures, efficiency improvements, and a fair, negotiated contribution from the workforce — but only within a framework that gives binding security to the sites and to the people working there.

What we will not do is sign off on a restructuring that makes workers pay for management mistakes through closures, mass redundancies, or the dismantling of long-standing guarantees. If the company wants cooperation, it must offer binding guarantees through 2030 and give the works council a genuine role in shaping the transformation. Without that, there is no basis for agreement.
