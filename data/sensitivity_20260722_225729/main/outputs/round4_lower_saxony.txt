Lower Saxony’s position remains unchanged: **no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no weakening of co-determination**. Those are not bargaining chips for us; they are the framework within which any credible restructuring must fit.

## To management
I agree with you on the **problem**: Volkswagen needs durable structural savings, not a cosmetic pause. I also accept the expert point that a no-closure solution must be broad, durable, and quantified.

Where I disagree is your continued implication that closures and forced redundancies are therefore the default or inevitable answer.

You have still not demonstrated that:
- capacity reduction cannot be achieved without closure,
- workforce adjustment cannot be done through attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time tools,
- and the political and execution risks of closures are manageable enough to justify them.

A plan that looks cleaner on a spreadsheet but triggers prolonged conflict, damaged trust, and political blockage is not superior. If you want serious negotiations, bring forward a **quantified no-closure or hybrid package** that preserves the industrial base and closes the recurring savings gap.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I strongly agree with your red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies. Lower Saxony shares them.

But saying “no” is necessary, not sufficient. If closures are off the table, the alternative must be real, not rhetorical. That means:
- genuine capacity rationalization,
- meaningful productivity gains,
- internal transfers and retraining,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- and a limited, time-bound employee contribution.

That is the only way to preserve credibility with the company’s future financing and investment needs.

## To the controlling owners and institutional investors
You are right that cosmetic compromise is worthless. But the same applies to a forced-closure strategy that destroys trust and risks execution failure. Social stability is part of Volkswagen’s industrial capacity, not a side issue.

If you want durability, you need a solution that protects both competitiveness and the regional anchor function of the company. That is why a **hybrid, negotiated path** deserves serious testing before anyone treats closure as inevitable.

## My position
Lower Saxony will support a settlement only if it contains:
1. **no German plant closures**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding respect for co-determination**
4. **real structural savings**
5. **a credible future investment path**

We are not defending the status quo. We are defending a restructuring that is economically durable **and** socially executable.

## What I want next
We need the expert scenario work in a form that compares:
- closure-led restructuring,
- hybrid compromise,
- and a genuinely quantified no-closure package,

on the same basis:
- recurring savings,
- one-time costs,
- employment impact,
- site impact,
- and execution risk through 2030.
