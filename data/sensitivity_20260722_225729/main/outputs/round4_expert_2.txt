## Independent quantified comparison: no-closure package vs. hybrid restructuring

Below is a neutral, industrial-restructuring view of the two paths, using **illustrative ranges** rather than company-internal data.

### Working assumption set
- Volkswagen needs **durable annual structural savings** by 2030, not just a short-lived wage pause.
- The comparison is on **risk-adjusted** basis, not headline savings.
- “No-closure” means:
  - no German plant closures,
  - no compulsory redundancies,
  - adjustment mainly via attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time measures, productivity gains, and limited labor contribution.
- “Hybrid” means:
  - no broad closure program,
  - but real footprint/capacity rationalization is allowed via line consolidation, shift cuts, product transfers, and selective site adjustment.

---

## 1) Recurring annual savings by measure

### A. No-closure package
Illustrative annual recurring savings by 2028–2030:

- **Voluntary exits / attrition / early retirement:** €150m–€350m
- **Working-time flexibility / shift optimization:** €100m–€250m
- **Redeployment / internal transfers / retraining efficiency:** €50m–€150m
- **Productivity improvements / lean measures / overhead reduction:** €150m–€300m
- **Limited employee contribution:** €50m–€150m

**Total recurring savings:** **~€0.5bn–€1.2bn per year**

### B. Hybrid restructuring
Illustrative annual recurring savings by 2028–2030:

- **Shift cuts / line consolidation:** €250m–€500m
- **Selective capacity transfers / footprint simplification:** €200m–€450m
- **Voluntary exits / attrition:** €150m–€300m
- **Productivity/overhead measures:** €150m–€350m
- **Limited labor contribution:** €50m–€150m

**Total recurring savings:** **~€0.8bn–€1.8bn per year**

---

## 2) One-time costs

### A. No-closure package
- Severance / early retirement / voluntary exit funding
- retraining and redeployment
- temporary dual-running costs
- system and process change
- possible limited contract buyouts

**One-time cost:** **~€0.8bn–€2.5bn**

### B. Hybrid restructuring
- everything above, plus:
- line moves,
- product transfer costs,
- some tooling reconfiguration,
- possible partial asset write-downs,
- additional logistics/network changes

**One-time cost:** **~€1.5bn–€4.0bn**

---

## 3) 2028–2030 cash-flow impact

### No-closure package
- **2025–2027:** better cash profile than closure-led restructuring because upfront costs are lower
- **2028–2030:** cash improvement is positive, but may plateau if savings are not deep enough
- Risk: the package can become a **maintenance exercise** rather than a full reset

### Hybrid restructuring
- **2025–2027:** heavier cash drag than no-closure, but still materially less than closure-led
- **2028–2030:** stronger and more durable cash-flow uplift
- Better chance of materially improving operating margin while remaining executable

---

## 4) Employment and site effects

### No-closure package
- **Employment impact:** mostly through attrition and voluntary exits; no forced redundancy
- **Likely headcount reduction:** roughly **5,000–15,000** over time, depending on exit rates
- **Site impact:** all German sites remain open, but with reduced shifts, reduced lines, or lower utilization

### Hybrid restructuring
- **Employment impact:** still mainly voluntary/natural attrition, but deeper reduction is possible
- **Likely headcount reduction:** roughly **10,000–25,000**
- **Site impact:** some locations retain core status, others lose lines, shifts, or functions; closure avoided, but footprint is rationalized

---

## 5) Execution risk

### No-closure package: medium to high
Main risks:
- savings too small versus structural cost gap,
- voluntary exits underdeliver,
- productivity gains take longer than expected,
- overcapacity remains embedded,
- labor contribution is temporary rather than structural.

### Hybrid restructuring: medium
Main risks:
- still subject to industrial resistance,
- requires careful sequencing,
- but more likely to close the economic gap,
- and less disruptive than full plant closures.

---

## 6) Sensitivity ranges

A useful way to think about this is:

### If the required savings need to be at the low end
For example, if Volkswagen can accept around **€0.6bn–€0.9bn** recurring annual relief, then a **well-designed no-closure package** could be sufficient.

### If the required savings need to be at the mid-to-high end
If the company needs **€1.0bn–€1.8bn+** recurring annual relief, then a **hybrid** becomes much more plausible.

### If the required savings need to exceed that
A closure-led route has the highest theoretical savings, but also the highest implementation risk and social/political friction.

---

## Bottom line

### No-closure package
- **Best on social acceptance and lower upfront cost**
- **Weakest on structural depth**
- Works only if the cost gap is not too large

### Hybrid restructuring
- **Best risk-adjusted balance**
- More likely to deliver enough savings to matter by 2028–2030
- Lower execution risk than closures, stronger economics than no-closure

### Neutral conclusion
If the question is **“Which is more likely to be executable and still materially improve competitiveness by 2030?”**, then:

**Hybrid restructuring is usually the stronger risk-adjusted option.**
