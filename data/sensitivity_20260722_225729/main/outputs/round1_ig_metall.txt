We agree on the core line: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That is not a negotiable detail; it is the minimum condition for any serious settlement.

Let me be equally clear about what this means from the union side:

- We are **prepared to contribute** to a solution.
- We are **not prepared to let the workforce carry the whole burden**.
- We will not accept a restructuring that turns “competitiveness” into a euphemism for job destruction and permanent insecurity.

So I support the works council’s demand that any adjustment must be socially responsible and contractually binding. But I want to add one important point: **a fair deal must also include management and ownership contributions**. If employees are asked for wage restraint, working-time flexibility, or the waiver of elements of pay, then the company must commit in return to:

- binding site guarantees,
- no forced layoffs,
- a credible investment plan for future products and technologies,
- and a shared burden from management, shareholders, and the company balance sheet.

We are open to discussing alternatives such as:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internally negotiated capacity adjustments,
- qualification and redeployment,
- and a limited, time-bound employee contribution.

But those are only acceptable if they are part of a **durable industrial settlement**, not a one-sided cost-cutting exercise.

To management: if your proposal still contains plant closures, mass redundancies, or the dismantling of collective protections, then you are not presenting a restructuring package — you are presenting a confrontation. The peace obligation has ended, and our members expect action. We will use the tools available to defend jobs, wages, and sites.

So the message is straightforward:  
**No closures. No forced redundancies. Shared sacrifice. Binding guarantees.**  
If that is the basis, we can negotiate seriously. If not, resistance will continue.
