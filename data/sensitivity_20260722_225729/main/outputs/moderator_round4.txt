### Core impasse
The central deadlock is:

**Management, owners, and investors want durable structural savings large enough to restore competitiveness; employee-side stakeholders require no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.**

That creates a procedural impasse around **which instruments are allowed** before the numbers are agreed.  
The employee side is asking for a quantified no-closure or hybrid plan to be proven; management is asking for acceptance of broader restructuring tools before committing to that constraint. Both sides are demanding the other side move first.

A second, related impasse is:

**Whether a “limited, time-bound employee contribution” can be temporary and reversible without becoming a permanent erosion of protections.**

---

### Facilitator compromise proposals
These are not positions, but possible bridging formats for further negotiation.

#### 1) Quantified three-scenario cap with binding selection rule
Agree to compare, on the same assumptions and by a fixed date:
- no-closure package,
- hybrid package,
- closure-led package.

Then adopt a rule:
- if a no-closure or hybrid package reaches an agreed minimum savings threshold and execution-risk ceiling, it becomes the preferred route;
- only if it fails those thresholds does the parties reopen closure-level measures.

This could bridge:
- employee-side red lines,
- management’s insistence on measurable savings,
- and owners/investors’ demand for credibility.

#### 2) No-closure first, but with automatic step-up triggers
Start from a no-closure restructuring baseline with:
- voluntary exits,
- retraining,
- shift and line consolidation,
- productivity measures,
- limited employee contribution.

Then define triggers that activate additional measures only if savings or headcount reductions fall short by set milestones.

This could bridge:
- works council / IG Metall concerns by preserving the no-closure principle initially,
- management’s need for enforceability,
- and investor demand for contingencies.

#### 3) Site-security package tied to deeper flexibility
Offer binding site guarantees through 2030 in exchange for:
- higher flexibility,
- a one-time and strictly capped employee contribution,
- guaranteed productivity programs,
- and enforceable capacity rationalization without closure.

This could bridge:
- Lower Saxony’s site-security priority,
- employee concerns about permanent concessions,
- and management’s need for structural cost relief.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To management
**What exact recurring annual savings, by measure, would be sufficient for you to accept a no-closure or hybrid package, and what specific gap would still make closures unavoidable?**

#### To the works council
**Which concrete capacity and cost reductions are you prepared to quantify now, and what minimum savings threshold would make a no-closure package acceptable to you?**

#### To IG Metall
**What precise safeguards would make a time-bound employee contribution acceptable without creating a permanent precedent for wage or protection erosion?**

#### To Lower Saxony
**What binding investment or governance commitments are you prepared to trade for an enforceable no-closure framework, if that framework requires greater flexibility from labor?**

#### To the controlling owners
**What evidence threshold would persuade you that a hybrid route protects value better than closure-led restructuring, even if the latter looks cleaner on paper?**

#### To institutional investors
**Would you support a hybrid or no-closure package if it delivered a defined recurring savings floor and a credible 2028–2030 margin path, even if plant closures were avoided?**

#### To the expert
**Can you specify the minimum savings and headcount reduction ranges that would make a no-closure package materially credible rather than merely politically attractive?**
