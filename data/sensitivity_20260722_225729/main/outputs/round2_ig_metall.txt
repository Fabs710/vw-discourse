We are aligned with the works council on the essentials, and I will say it plainly again: **no plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**. That remains the minimum basis for a serious settlement.

But I want to sharpen the discussion in this round, because “structural savings” cannot be a blank cheque for management.

### To management
You keep saying the current structure is too expensive. That may be true. But you have still not shown why the only answer must be closures or forced layoffs. If you want the workforce to accept concessions, then you need to put real numbers on the table:

- What exact savings gap must be closed?
- How much comes from labour costs?
- How much from capacity?
- What is the cost of strike escalation and lost output?
- What is the price of destroying trust and future capability?

Without that, your position is just pressure, not a solution.

We are not rejecting change. We are rejecting a one-sided restructuring in which workers carry the pain while management and owners avoid meaningful responsibility.

### To the controlling owners
You are right that a cosmetic compromise is worthless. But you are wrong if you think that means closures and mass layoffs become automatically legitimate. A “durable” deal is not durable if it breaks the workforce, triggers conflict, and undermines Volkswagen’s social legitimacy.

If you want a credible path to competitiveness, then you need to support a package with:
- **binding site security**,
- **no forced redundancies**,
- **real investment commitments**,
- and a **fair burden-sharing formula**.

### To Lower Saxony
Your rejection of closures and compulsory redundancies is important, and we expect that to be more than rhetoric. If the political side is serious, then it must help block any attempt to make workers pay alone.

### Our line
We are prepared to discuss a **limited and time-bound employee contribution** if, and only if, it is tied to:
- site guarantees through 2030,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- protection of the collective wage framework,
- and a credible future product and investment plan.

That contribution can include:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint or foregone elements of pay,
- redeployment and qualification,
- and capacity adjustments that do not involve closures.

What we will not accept is:
- open-ended concessions,
- permanent erosion of standards,
- or a deal that leaves workers exposed once management has secured its cost cuts.

### Bottom line
If management wants agreement, it must move away from the logic of ultimatum and toward a negotiated package with shared sacrifice. If it insists on closures and forced redundancies, then it should not expect industrial peace.

Our position is unchanged:
**protect the jobs, protect the sites, protect collective bargaining, and share the burden fairly.**
