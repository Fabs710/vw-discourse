We hear the position from the works council and IG Metall. I will be equally direct.

I agree on one point: we need a durable, negotiated solution. We do not want chaos, and we do not want a strike spiral for its own sake. But I cannot accept the core premise that **every German plant must remain open regardless of cost and demand**, or that **job security guarantees are untouchable** in the face of a structural competitiveness crisis.

That is exactly the problem we are trying to fix.

Let me be clear about management’s assessment:

- German factory costs are materially above target.
- European demand is shrinking.
- Competition is intensifying, including from lower-cost producers.
- The current cost and capacity base is not sustainable to 2030.
- Without a structural reset, the brand will continue to underperform and the funds needed for future products and technology will not be there.

So when you say “no closures, no compulsory redundancies,” you are effectively asking management to solve a structural overcapacity problem without using the instruments that actually remove structural cost. That is not a credible path.

Where I agree:
- I agree that this must be socially responsible.
- I agree that retraining, redeployment, working-time measures, and negotiated contributions should be part of the package.
- I agree that we need a credible investment path into future products and technologies.

Where I disagree:
- I do **not** accept a settlement that preserves all sites unchanged while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.
- I do **not** accept a purely temporary or cosmetic compromise.
- I do **not** accept that management carries all the adjustment while labour contributes only if everything else is guaranteed first.

The company needs a package that creates **real, durable structural savings**. That can include plant closures, capacity reductions, workforce reductions, pay and cost adjustments, and changes to legacy protections. I am open on the exact mix, but not on the principle that the current structure is sacrosanct.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must bring forward an alternative that is equally durable in effect:
- materially lower fixed costs,
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- a genuine labour-cost contribution,
- and a verifiable path to restoring return on sales.

A promise of “efficiency improvements” alone is not enough. Temporary working-time cuts alone are not enough. Internal reallocation alone is not enough. Those measures may bridge a period, but they do not solve overcapacity.

So my position is this:
- We are prepared to negotiate.
- We are not prepared to preserve the status quo.
- We need a structural deal, not a symbolic one.
- And if necessary, we will continue to press for the hard measures required to restore competitiveness.

If the works council and IG Metall want to avoid the most painful options, then they need to engage with the size of the problem, not just its social consequences.
