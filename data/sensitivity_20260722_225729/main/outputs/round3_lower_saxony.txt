Lower Saxony’s position remains unchanged: **no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no weakening of co-determination**. That is not rhetoric; it is the boundary of what we can support.

### To management
I agree with you on one point: Volkswagen needs durable structural savings, not symbolic gestures. I also accept the expert’s conclusion that a no-closure solution is only credible if it is **broad, durable, and quantified**.

But I do **not** accept your repeated implication that closures and forced redundancies are therefore the default answer.

You still have not shown that:
- capacity reduction cannot be achieved without closure,
- workforce adjustment cannot be achieved through attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time tools,
- and the political, industrial, and execution risks of closure are manageable enough to justify them.

A plan that may look cleaner on a spreadsheet but triggers prolonged conflict, damaged trust, and political blockage is not a superior plan. If you want serious negotiations, bring forward a **quantified no-closure or hybrid package** that preserves the industrial base and actually closes the recurring savings gap.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I strongly agree with your red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies. Those are red lines Lower Saxony shares.

Where I would slightly sharpen the point is this: saying “no” is necessary, but not sufficient. If closures are off the table, then the alternative must be real, not rhetorical. That means:
- genuine capacity rationalization,
- meaningful productivity gains,
- internal transfers and retraining,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- and a limited, time-bound employee contribution.

That is the only way to preserve credibility with the company’s future financing and investment needs.

### To the controlling owners and institutional investors
You are right that cosmetic compromise is worthless. But the same applies to a forced closure strategy that destroys trust and risks execution failure. Social stability is not a nice-to-have; it is part of Volkswagen’s industrial capacity.

If you want durability, you need a solution that protects both competitiveness and the regional anchor function of the company. That is why a **hybrid, negotiated path** deserves serious testing before anyone treats closure as inevitable.

### My position
Lower Saxony will support a settlement only if it contains:
1. **no German plant closures**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding respect for co-determination**
4. **real structural savings**
5. **a credible future investment path**

We are not defending the status quo. We are defending a restructuring that is economically durable **and** socially executable.

### What I want next
We need the expert scenario work in a form that compares:
- closure-led restructuring,
- hybrid compromise,
- and a genuinely quantified no-closure package,

on the same basis:
- recurring savings,
- one-time costs,
- employment impact,
- site impact,
- and execution risk through 2030.
