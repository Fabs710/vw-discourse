Here is a concise, scenario-based comparison within the industrial-restructuring domain.

## 1) Bottom line
On the information provided, a **pure no-closure package** can be made to work **in principle**, but it is **hard to make it fully equivalent** to a closure-led restructuring by 2030.  
A **hybrid restructuring** is the most plausible **risk-adjusted** path: it can deliver most of the durable savings while reducing the execution and conflict risk that rises sharply with plant closures.

---

## 2) Comparative stress-test

### Scenario A: Closure-led restructuring
**What it is**
- Closure of one or more German plants
- Permanent capacity removal
- Workforce reduction via redundancies plus attrition
- Structural reset of labor and footprint costs

**Indicative recurring savings by 2030**
- **€1.5bn to €3.0bn per year**

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€3bn to €6bn+**
- severance
- wind-down costs
- impairments
- logistics/network reconfiguration
- transition disruption

**Employment/site effect**
- Largest job loss
- Permanent site loss or major downsizing
- Highest social and political impact

**Execution risk**
- **High**
- strike escalation
- governance blockage
- delayed implementation
- supply-chain disruption

**Assessment**
- Highest gross savings
- But also highest probability of value leakage during execution

---

### Scenario B: Hybrid restructuring
**What it is**
- No broad closure program, or only limited selective footprint changes
- Capacity reduction via shift cuts, line consolidation, product transfers, and network simplification
- Workforce reduction mainly via attrition, voluntary exits, redeployment, and early retirement
- Limited employee contribution
- Possibly some binding investment/site-security commitments

**Indicative recurring savings by 2030**
- **€0.9bn to €2.0bn per year**

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€1.5bn to €3.5bn**
- separation incentives
- retraining
- transfer/logistics changes
- limited restructuring charges

**Employment/site effect**
- Material headcount reduction
- Most sites retained, but with lower utilization and fewer shifts
- Less abrupt disruption than closures

**Execution risk**
- **Medium**
- still complex
- some dependency on voluntary exits and negotiated concessions
- but more executable than closure-led restructuring

**Assessment**
- Best risk-adjusted balance
- Most likely to preserve industrial continuity while delivering meaningful savings

---

### Scenario C: Fully no-closure negotiated package
**What it is**
- No German plant closures
- No compulsory redundancies
- Savings from working-time tools, productivity gains, redeployment, retraining, voluntary exits, limited wage restraint, and capacity reduction without shutdown

**Indicative recurring savings by 2030**
- **€0.5bn to €1.5bn per year**
- could be higher in an unusually stringent deal, but that is not the base case

**Indicative one-time costs**
- **€0.8bn to €2.5bn**
- lower severance and impairment burden
- but likely higher need for repeated negotiation or ongoing concessions

**Employment/site effect**
- Best preservation of sites
- Lower immediate job disruption
- But more risk of persistent overcapacity

**Execution risk**
- **Low-to-medium on conflict**
- **high on underdelivery**
- main risk is not immediate disruption, but insufficient structural savings

**Assessment**
- Feasible only if the package is unusually broad and durable
- Least likely to fully close the structural gap by 2030

## 4) Key economic interpretation

### Can a no-closure package match a closure-led plan?
**Usually not fully.**  
It would need all of the following at once:
- very large labor-cost relief
- real capacity reduction without shutdown
- high voluntary exit volume
- durable productivity gains
- strong implementation discipline

In practice, one or more of those elements usually underperforms.

### Why hybrid is strongest on a risk-adjusted basis
A hybrid package gives:
- enough structural savings to matter,
- less one-time damage than closures,
- better implementation probability,
- lower probability of prolonged industrial conflict.

That combination often beats a “cleaner” but more confrontational closure-led plan in realized outcome terms.

---

## 5) 2028–2030 implication
If the goal is to restore competitiveness and protect investment capacity by 2030:

- **Closure-led**: strongest theoretical margin uplift, but highest chance of delay and value destruction
- **Hybrid**: most credible path to a durable margin improvement
- **No-closure**: best social continuity, but least likely to be sufficient unless the package is exceptionally strong

---

## 6) Neutral conclusion
- If management’s objective is **maximum structural savings**, closure-led restructuring is strongest on paper.
- If the objective is **best executable value creation**, the **hybrid restructuring** is the most plausible.
- If the objective is **avoiding closures entirely**, a **no-closure package** can work only with unusually deep, durable concessions and real capacity reduction.
