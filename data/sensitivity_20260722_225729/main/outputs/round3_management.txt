I’ll respond directly, because the positions on the table still do not solve the core problem.

## First: where I agree
I agree with everyone on three points:
1. **Volkswagen needs durable structural savings.**
2. **Temporary fixes alone are not enough.**
3. **A prolonged industrial conflict would damage the company.**

I also accept the expert’s point: in principle, a **no-closure package** can be built if it is broad, durable, and fully quantified.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the employee-side attempt to turn that into an absolute veto on the tools needed to fix the cost base.

You are saying:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding site and job guarantees through 2030,
- and then asking management to somehow make the economics work.

That is backwards.

If we lock in every site and every legacy protection first, then we are not restructuring Volkswagen — we are preserving an uncompetitive structure and calling it a deal.

## To the Works Council
You say management has not proven closures are unavoidable. But you also have not produced a package that closes the gap with the same durability.

What you’ve offered is a list of instruments:
- working-time flexibility,
- retraining,
- redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- voluntary departures.

Those are **components**, not a complete solution.

They may help, but they do not automatically remove:
- excess fixed costs,
- underutilized capacity,
- network inefficiency,
- and the margin pressure we face in Europe.

If you want to rule out closures and forced redundancies, then I need a **quantified alternative**:
- how much recurring annual savings by 2030,
- how much capacity reduction,
- how much labour cost reduction,
- how much of the workforce reduction is real and permanent,
- and what happens if voluntary exits fall short.

Until then, “no closures” is not a restructuring proposal. It is a red line without an economic answer.

## To IG Metall
I hear your insistence on no forced layoffs and no site closures. But you are ignoring the actual competitiveness constraint.

If the company does not get a meaningful reduction in fixed labour and footprint costs, then:
- the brand return-on-sales target will not be reached,
- future product investment will be crowded out,
- and the German base will remain structurally too expensive.

You keep framing this as management choosing confrontation. That is not accurate. We are choosing between:
- a hard but workable restructuring, or
- delay, deterioration, and eventual bigger pain later.

And let me be explicit: I will not accept a settlement that leaves us with a cost base that is still uncompetitive in 2026, 2027, and 2030.

## To Lower Saxony
You are right that social stability matters. You are wrong if you think that stability can be protected by freezing the footprint and blocking hard choices.

A region is not protected by preserving every site at any cost. It is protected by keeping Volkswagen competitive enough to invest, produce, and employ in the long run.

If the only way to avoid plant closures is to preserve an uncompetitive structure, then that is not a protection strategy. It is a postponement strategy.

## To the controlling owners and investors
I agree with you that cosmetic compromise is worthless.

But I also hear the caution: closures are not automatically value-creating if they provoke a drawn-out industrial war or political blockage. That is true.

Which is exactly why I am not insisting on closures as an end in themselves. I am insisting on **durable structural savings**.

If the employee side can deliver equivalent, recurring savings without closures, I am open to that. But they have not done so yet.

## What management needs
The expert was helpful, and I want to build on that rather than debate slogans.

So here is what I need next:
- a **fully quantified no-closure package**, or
- acceptance that some combination of closures, capacity cuts, and workforce reductions will remain necessary.

A viable alternative must show:
1. **recurring annual savings**, not just temporary relief;
2. **capacity rationalization**, not merely shuffled inefficiency;
3. **workforce reduction**, not only reassignment;
4. **durability through 2030**;
5. **credible execution**, even if cooperation deteriorates.

If that package is real, I will evaluate it. If it is not, then the closure option remains on the table.

## My bottom line
I am not negotiating away the core objective.

We need:
- lower fixed costs,
- less overcapacity,
- a more competitive German footprint,
- and funding for the product and technology offensive through 2030.

I will not sign up to a deal that preserves the current cost base and calls it compromise.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced redundancies, then bring a package that actually closes the structural gap. If not, then we should stop pretending there is a credible no-closure solution.
