We will be blunt.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the controlling owners, Lower Saxony, and even the employee side on the **diagnosis**:
- Volkswagen has a real structural cost problem.
- A cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive.
- Temporary fixes are not enough.
- A prolonged strike would also destroy value.
- The company needs durable savings and enough investment capacity to stay competitive through 2030.

We also accept the expert’s core point:
- a **no-closure package is possible in principle**,
- but only if it is **broad, durable, and quantified**.

That is the correct standard.

## Where we disagree
We do **not** support the employee side’s current position as a starting point if it becomes a de facto veto on structural change:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- full guarantees to 2030.

If that is absolute, then the risk is that Volkswagen preserves too much cost base and too much overcapacity. That would mean structurally impaired margins, which is unacceptable to us.

At the same time, we do **not** support management treating closures as the default answer simply because they are the cleanest headline. If a hybrid can achieve most of the savings with lower execution risk, that is better value.

## Our standard
We will back the package that gives the best **risk-adjusted** return, not the harshest or the most politically comfortable one.

That means:
1. **durable recurring savings**
2. **real capacity reduction**
3. **measurable labour-cost relief**
4. **credible execution**
5. **a clear path back to competitive margins by 2028–2030**

## On closure-led restructuring
We can accept plant closures only if management can show, convincingly, that:
- the savings are materially superior,
- the execution risk is manageable,
- and the net effect after disruption still creates more value than the alternatives.

If closures trigger a long industrial war, delayed implementation, or political blockage, the value case deteriorates quickly. So closures are not automatically the best shareholder outcome.

## On a fully no-closure package
We are skeptical unless the package is truly stringent and quantified.

Working-time tools, redeployment, retraining, voluntary exits, and productivity improvements are useful, but they are not enough by themselves unless they produce:
- recurring savings in the high hundreds of millions at minimum,
- real permanent headcount reduction,
- and durable footprint rationalization.

A vague no-closure promise would leave the cost base uncompetitive. We would not support that.

## On a hybrid package
This is the most plausible route today.

But “hybrid” only has value if it is not just delay in disguise. It must include:
- meaningful structural savings,
- actual footprint rationalization,
- workforce reduction mostly via attrition and voluntary mechanisms,
- limited, time-bound employee contribution,
- and hard milestones.

If the hybrid closes the gap credibly, we would prefer that over a closure-heavy plan with higher disruption risk.

## To management
Do not confuse shareholder support with support for the harshest measure.

We do not need a dramatic narrative. We need:
- lower fixed costs,
- a more competitive German footprint,
- better margins,
- and sustainable free cash flow.

If a quantified hybrid does that better than closures, choose it. If not, show why closures are truly necessary.

## To the works council and IG Metall
A blanket veto is not credible to capital markets.

If closures are off the table, then put forward a package that actually closes the structural gap:
- real capacity reduction,
- real fixed-cost reduction,
- real workforce adjustment,
- and durable concessions, not symbolic ones.

If you cannot do that, then you are asking shareholders to accept permanent value impairment. We will not support that.

## To Lower Saxony
We understand the political red lines. But political resistance alone does not restore competitiveness.

If you want to keep closures off the table, then you must help secure a serious alternative that delivers the savings. Otherwise you are protecting sites today at the expense of the company’s financial health tomorrow.

## Bottom line
Our position is straightforward:
- **We support real restructuring.**
- **We do not support cosmetic compromise.**
- **We do not support a settlement that leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive.**
- **We are open to a hybrid or no-closure path only if it is fully quantified and economically credible.**
