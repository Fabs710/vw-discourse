Thank you. I will be equally clear from the perspective of the State of Lower Saxony and as a long-standing anchor shareholder.

We **do** recognize the competitiveness problem. No one here is pretending that Volkswagen can ignore cost pressure, the market shift, or the need to fund future products. A durable solution has to restore the company’s earnings power. On that point, I agree with management.

But I must draw a firm line: **we will not support a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany, mass compulsory redundancies, or the erosion of the company’s co-determination and regional responsibility.** That would damage not only the workforce, but also the long-term industrial base of Lower Saxony and Volkswagen’s social legitimacy.

### Where I agree
- The cost base must come down.
- The company needs a credible path back to competitiveness.
- Savings must be durable, not cosmetic.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to a destructive conflict.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that closing plants is the only serious instrument available. That is too narrow and too risky. Once a German plant is closed, the loss is not reversible. The same is true for the political and social damage. As a shareholder and political actor, I cannot back a solution that permanently weakens Volkswagen’s regional footprint when other, less destructive options remain on the table.

I also do **not** accept that the workforce should be asked to shoulder the main burden while management and ownership simply demand “structural savings” from the sidelines. If employees are expected to contribute through working-time flexibility, wage restraint, or temporary sacrifices, then the company must reciprocate with binding guarantees on:
- site security,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- investment in future models and technologies,
- and a clear role for co-determination in shaping the transformation.

### What I consider a serious path
A serious restructuring would need to combine several elements:
- capacity reductions through negotiated measures, not plant closures by fiat,
- internal reallocation and retraining,
- working-time flexibility where needed,
- a fair labour-cost contribution that is limited and time-bound,
- and a credible investment commitment for future products.

That is the kind of settlement that can protect competitiveness **and** preserve Volkswagen as an industrial anchor in the region.

### My bottom line
If the proposal still depends on German plant closures or large-scale forced job cuts, then it will meet strong resistance from Lower Saxony. We have effective blocking rights for a reason, and we will use them if necessary. We are not here to rubber-stamp a restructuring that exports the cost of management failure onto employees and the regions that have carried Volkswagen for decades.

So I am prepared to work on a negotiated solution — but only one that:
- keeps core sites open,
- protects employment as far as possible,
- preserves co-determination,
- and delivers real competitiveness gains.

If management wants support, it needs to come forward with a plan that matches those conditions.
