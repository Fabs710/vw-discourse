### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties recognize that Volkswagen faces a serious competitiveness and cost problem.
- There is broad support for a **negotiated**, rather than purely confrontational, solution.
- Several sides accept the need for some mix of:
  - productivity improvements,
  - retraining and redeployment,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - and a credible investment plan for future products and technologies.
- There is also broad recognition that a purely cosmetic or temporary solution would be insufficient.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject them; management says they remain on the table; owners and investors are open to alternatives but do not endorse an absolute ban.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Employee-side and Lower Saxony positions reject them; management sees workforce reduction as potentially necessary for structural savings.
- **Scope of workforce contribution:**  
  Unions and employee representatives are willing to discuss contributions, but only with binding guarantees and shared burden. Management and investors want clearer, larger, and more durable cost relief.
- **Binding guarantees vs. restructuring flexibility:**  
  Employee-side actors want site and job security commitments through 2030. Management says it cannot accept rigid guarantees that prevent structural adjustment.
- **Role of co-determination and regional protections:**  
  Lower Saxony and employee representatives insist on strong involvement and safeguards; management wants more room to execute change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How to achieve durable savings without plant closures or forced layoffs:** no equivalent, quantified alternative has yet been presented.
- **How much burden employees, management, owners, and investors each bear:** contributions are acknowledged in principle, but the distribution remains unresolved.
- **How to balance social legitimacy and industrial stability against speed and scale of restructuring:** this tension is still open.
- **What level of investment commitment can be tied to any workforce concession:** the linkage is still undefined.
- **How to avoid prolonged conflict while still producing structural change:** the risk of strike, blocking, or delayed implementation remains unaddressed.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from positions to **numbers and scenarios**:
  - What exact savings are needed?
  - What savings come from each proposed measure?
  - Which measures are temporary, which are structural?
- Ask each side to present **one workable package**:
  - employee-side alternative to closures/forced redundancies,
  - management’s minimum acceptable restructuring,
  - and what each side is prepared to concede.
- Focus the discussion on **durable equivalence**:
  - if closures and layoffs are ruled out, what combination of other measures produces the same economic effect?
- Clarify the **binding package**:
  - what guarantees, investments, and time limits would make a compromise enforceable and credible?
