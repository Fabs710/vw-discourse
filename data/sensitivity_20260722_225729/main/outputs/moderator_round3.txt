## Core impasse

The impasse is now concentrated in one issue:

**Whether Volkswagen’s restructuring must include German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, or whether the required savings can be achieved through a quantified no-closure / hybrid package.**

Everything else is downstream of that split:
- Management and investors want **durable, verifiable structural savings**.
- Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony insist on **no closures and no forced layoffs**.
- The controlling owners want a solution that is **economically credible and executable**.
- The expert says a **no-closure path is possible in principle**, but only if it is unusually broad, durable, and quantified.

So the plateau is not about the goal of restructuring; it is about **the acceptable instruments and the burden of proof**.

---

## Facilitation proposals

### Proposal 1: Quantified no-closure package with hard fallback triggers
A compromise could be structured as a **time-bound no-closure package** that is tested against explicit financial and operational targets.

**Core features**
- No German plant closures for a defined period.
- No compulsory redundancies during that period.
- A quantified savings target for 2028–2030.
- Savings to come from:
  - shift reduction,
  - line consolidation,
  - internal transfers,
  - retraining,
  - voluntary exits,
  - productivity gains,
  - limited and reversible employee contributions.
- Pre-agreed fallback review if targets are missed.

**Purpose**
This gives employee-side stakeholders the red lines they seek, while giving management and investors measurable proof points and a mechanism if the package underdelivers.

---

### Proposal 2: Hybrid restructuring with protected sites and selective footprint reduction
A second compromise could accept that **some footprint adjustment is unavoidable**, but keep it away from full plant closures.

**Core features**
- No broad plant closure program.
- Capacity reduction through:
  - shift cuts,
  - product reallocation,
  - line consolidation,
  - network simplification.
- Workforce reduction primarily via:
  - attrition,
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - redeployment.
- Binding site guarantees for core locations.
- Limited employee contribution tied to investment commitments and job-security protections.

**Purpose**
This tries to bridge the main gap between “absolute no closure” and “hard restructuring” by allowing structural change without crossing the most conflictual red lines.

---

### Proposal 3: Two-stage settlement with an independent verification gate
A third option is a **phased settlement**.

**Stage 1**
- Immediate agreement on no compulsory redundancies.
- Suspension of closure decisions for a defined period.
- Launch of an independently verified restructuring design.

**Stage 2**
- If the verified package shows the savings gap can be closed without closures, implement that path.
- If not, reopen talks on a narrowly defined fallback set of footprint measures, with co-determination and political consultation built in.

**Purpose**
This reduces brinkmanship and forces the parties onto the same factual basis before the closure question is decided.

## One pointed question to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact recurring annual savings, by measure and by year, would you accept as sufficient to keep German plant closures and compulsory redundancies off the table?**

### To the Works Council
**What minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction would make a no-closure package credible enough for you to recommend it to the workforce?**

### To IG Metall
**Under what precise conditions would you support a limited, time-bound employee contribution without seeing it as a precedent for permanent erosion of standards?**

### To Lower Saxony
**What binding commitments from management would you require in exchange for using your blocking position to support a no-closure settlement?**

### To the controlling owners
**Would you prefer a slightly lower but more certain risk-adjusted outcome via a hybrid deal, or a theoretically stronger but higher-risk closure-led plan?**

### To institutional investors
**What level of implementation risk would you accept if the deal avoids closures but delivers only partial savings earlier in the cycle?**

### To the expert
**What minimum combination of capacity reduction, labor-cost relief, and one-time restructuring effort would make a no-closure package competitive with a hybrid one on a risk-adjusted basis?**
