I agree with the core line of the works council: **no plant closures in Germany and no compulsory redundancies**. Those are not acceptable instruments for solving a structural problem that management helped create through years of strategy and investment decisions.

Let me be equally clear: **we are prepared to contribute**, but only under a **fair shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs, sites, and the collective wage framework.

### What we can discuss
We are ready to negotiate:
- **capacity reductions** instead of closures
- **working-time adjustments** and flexibility measures
- **temporary waiving or reduction of bonuses**
- a **limited employee financial contribution**
- possibly other cost measures that are **reversible** and **socially responsible**

But this only makes sense if it is tied to:
- **binding site guarantees**
- **binding job security**
- a **durable collective agreement**
- clear commitments on **future investment and product allocation**
- management and owners also making a **real contribution**

### What we reject
We will not accept:
- **forced layoffs**
- **plant closures**
- a restructuring where the workforce carries the burden alone
- the dismantling of the collective wage framework under the label of “competitiveness”

If management insists that the only path is closures and job destruction, then it should expect **resistance, including industrial action**. The peace obligation has ended, and members expect us to defend their jobs and standards.

### Our message to management and shareholders
If you want a durable solution, bring a proposal that includes:
1. **no closures**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **fair participation by capital and management**
4. **a credible industrial future for the German sites**

Anything else is not restructuring in a social partnership sense; it is simply shifting the crisis onto the workforce.
