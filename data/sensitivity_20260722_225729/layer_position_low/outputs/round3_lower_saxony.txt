I will be very clear.

## My position
Lower Saxony will **not** support German plant closures or compulsory redundancies at Volkswagen. That remains the wrong answer economically, socially, and politically. It would damage the regional economy, weaken Volkswagen’s legitimacy as an anchor employer, and undermine the co-determination model that has helped make the company successful.

But I also do not support pretending that the status quo can remain untouched. We accept that Volkswagen needs a **durable restructuring**. The question is whether management and capital are prepared to do that **inside the existing German network**, rather than by destroying sites and jobs.

---

## Where I agree with management, owners, and investors
I agree on the diagnosis:
- Volkswagen has a real competitiveness problem.
- The current cost base is too high.
- Cosmetic measures are not enough.
- A prolonged conflict would hurt everyone.

I also agree with the finance assessment that a restructuring must remove **real fixed cost**, not just temporary optics.

That is exactly why a serious **no-closure** package has to be taken seriously.

---

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept management’s claim that closures are necessary because no-closure measures “do not yet add up.” That is not yet proven.

The expert has made the key point: a no-closure solution is **possible in principle** if it is large, permanent, and disciplined enough. That means management cannot jump straight to closures without first showing:
1. the exact savings gap that remains,
2. what internal capacity reductions have been fully modeled,
3. and why those measures cannot be made equivalent to closure-based savings.

Until that is shown, closures are not a necessity. They are a choice.

And I am not prepared to endorse that choice.

---

## Where I agree with the works council and IG Metall
I agree with the workforce side on the core red lines:
- no plant closures in Germany,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no dismantling of co-determination,
- no one-sided burden on workers.

I also agree that workers should not be asked to pay for management’s strategic mistakes alone.

But I will add one important point: “no closures” cannot mean “no change.” If we want to protect sites, then we have to accept a hard internal restructuring:
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity concentration within existing plants,
- voluntary exits,
- training and redeployment,
- productivity gains,
- bonus restraint,
- and management overhead cuts.

That is the price of preserving the industrial base.

---

## To management
You are asking for a structural reset. Fine. Then show us a **verified no-closure package** that proves closures are needed only if it fails.

Right now, I hear a lot of insistence, but not enough quantified proof. If you want Lower Saxony to reconsider its position, you need to put forward a package with:
- annual savings target,
- site-by-site capacity effects,
- headcount reductions through voluntary means and attrition,
- overhead cuts at management level,
- and a clear timetable to run-rate savings.

If you cannot show that, then your argument for closures is too weak to justify the political and social damage they would cause.

---

## To the works council and IG Metall
I support your red lines, but I also expect a serious offer, not just a refusal.

If we want to block closures, we must be prepared to support a **binding no-closure restructuring** that delivers real savings. That means:
- permanent capacity concentration inside the existing network,
- meaningful voluntary exits,
- working-time adjustments,
- targeted wage and bonus restraint,
- and a strong investment commitment for future products.

I would not support a deal that merely preserves every structure and hopes the numbers improve by themselves. That would be irresponsible to the company and to the region.

So: protect the plants, yes — but do it with a credible industrial plan.

---

## To the controlling owners and investors
I understand your concern for value and execution certainty. But do not confuse a “cleaner” accounting solution with a better long-term solution.

A closure-based plan may look decisive, but it risks:
- industrial conflict,
- loss of know-how,
- reputational damage,
- and weakened labor relations for years.

That can destroy value too.

If a no-closure package can credibly deliver the required savings, that is the better path. If it cannot, then we should revisit the options. But that burden of proof has not yet been met.

---

## What I want next
Before anyone pushes this toward closures, I want a **formal, quantified comparison**:

[SUMMON: Finance/Operations expert to provide a side-by-side comparison of a fully developed no-closure package versus a closure-based package, including annual savings, one-time costs, headcount impact, timing, and site-by-site capacity effects]

Specifically, I want:
1. the minimum annual savings target,
2. the no-closure package required to reach it,
3. the remaining gap if any,
4. the one-time cost of each route,
5. and the implementation timeline.

Only then can we judge whether closures are truly unavoidable.

---

## Bottom line
Lower Saxony’s position is unchanged:
- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Yes to real restructuring**
- **Yes to a serious, binding no-closure package**
- **Yes to competitiveness, but not by sacrificing our industrial base**

If management wants our support, it must come with a concrete plan that protects jobs and plants while restoring Volkswagen’s strength. Otherwise, we will use every legitimate means available to stop a course that would harm the company’s long-term role in our state.
