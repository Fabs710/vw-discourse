I will respond directly.

### First: what I agree with
I agree with the works council on the essentials:
- **no plant closures in Germany**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no solution that shifts the entire burden onto the workforce**

I also agree that Volkswagen needs a **durable** solution, not a cosmetic one. We are not defending the status quo for its own sake. If the company needs a lower cost base and more flexibility, then yes, that must be addressed.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the argument that structural savings automatically require closures or forced layoffs. That is a management narrative, not an objective truth.

I also do **not** accept the repeated pressure that workers should “prove” flexibility before management or ownership have made any real contribution. If everyone is serious about long-term value, then the adjustment must be **shared**:
- management must contribute
- owners must contribute
- the workforce can contribute too, but only fairly and only with security in return

### Our concrete position
IG Metall is prepared to negotiate a package built around:
- **capacity reductions without plant closures**
- **working-time adjustments and flexibility**
- **temporary waiver or reduction of bonuses**
- **limited employee financial contribution**
- **productivity improvements and qualification measures**
- **voluntary instruments only**, not compulsory redundancies

But this requires hard guarantees:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No forced layoffs**
3. **Binding site guarantees**
4. **Binding job-security commitments**
5. **A durable collective agreement**
6. **Clear industrial commitments for future products and investment**
7. **A real contribution from management and capital**

### On the numbers
If management wants a “minimum savings” answer, then say clearly what gap remains after:
- capacity alignment,
- work-time flexibility,
- bonus restraint,
- productivity gains,
- and voluntary measures.

Do not simply assume the only remaining lever is closure. If the economics still do not work after those measures, then management must explain:
- what it is prepared to give up,
- what owners are prepared to contribute,
- and why workers should be the only group forced to absorb permanent damage.

### To management
If you want labour peace, then stop treating it as something you can demand while leaving all strategic pain on the shop floor. A durable settlement is possible, but not on the basis of threats.

### To the controlling owners and investors
If you insist on “real structural savings,” then you must also accept real shared sacrifice. Otherwise you are asking for competitiveness at the expense of industrial peace, trust, and the long-term foundation of the company.

### Our limit remains unchanged
We will not accept:
- plant closures
- compulsory redundancies
- a restructuring that breaks the collective wage framework
- a deal where workers bear all the pain while capital and management stay insulated

If those are the terms, then there is no basis for agreement. And if necessary, we will continue to use the means available to us, including industrial action, to defend jobs and standards.

### Bottom line
We are ready for a serious restructuring deal. But it must be:
- socially responsible
- collectively bargained
- job-secure
- site-secure
- and fairly shared

If management and owners can bring that, we can talk. If not, then they are not seeking a solution — they are seeking submission.
