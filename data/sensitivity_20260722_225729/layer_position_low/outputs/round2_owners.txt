We are at the point where rhetoric has to give way to arithmetic.

I agree with the finance/operations assessment on the central fact: **Volkswagen needs a durable fixed-cost reset**, not a symbolic compromise. I also agree with management that a package that merely postpones the problem would destroy value. On that, the shareholder interest is aligned with competitiveness, not with delay.

Where I disagree is with the repeated implication that the only credible path is **plant closures or forced redundancies**. The expert has just confirmed that this is **not true**. A no-closure solution is feasible **if** it is aggressive enough to remove real fixed cost, reduce effective footprint, and improve utilization. That matters.

## My view as controlling owner

The family holding does **not** support preserving the status quo. We need:
- a material and durable margin improvement,
- a leaner German footprint,
- and a settlement that does not trigger prolonged value-destroying conflict.

But we do **not** accept a package whose main virtue is that it looks decisive while creating years of industrial rupture. A drawn-out confrontation would be highly destructive to enterprise value, and that is not stewardship.

## To management
Your diagnosis is broadly right. Your conclusion is not yet proven.

You have not demonstrated that you need to cross the red line of closures to get the required savings. The expert has given a range where a no-closure package can work: roughly **€1.0–1.5bn annually** at the upper end, with substantial capacity concentration, headcount reduction through attrition/voluntary exits, and real labor-cost concessions.

So my question back to management is simple:
- What is the **specific savings gap** after non-closure measures?
- What volume and utilization assumptions support that gap?
- What is the **minimum package** that closes it without destroying labour peace?

Until you answer that, closures are not a necessity; they are one option, and a costly one.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I hear your red lines:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no hollowing out of co-determination.

I am not dismissing those concerns. But I do **not** accept the idea that the workforce can keep all guarantees and still avoid meaningful adjustment. That is not credible.

The expert is clear: if you want a no-closure deal, it must be a **hard** deal, not a protective one. That means:
- real capacity concentration,
- shift reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- qualification and redeployment,
- bonus restraint,
- and likely a sizable employee contribution.

If your proposal is only “keep every site open and preserve all guarantees,” then it does **not** meet the economic test.

## To Lower Saxony
I understand the political and regional responsibility. But I will not support using that responsibility to freeze the business model in place. Lower Saxony should not confuse defending a regional base with defending every legacy structure.

That said, I do agree with you on one important point: **plant closures are not the only economic solution**. The expert confirmed that. So it would be a mistake for management to treat closure as the default answer before exhausting a serious no-closure package.

## To institutional investors
You are right that cosmetic measures are unacceptable. But I would caution you against assuming that the cleanest accounting solution is automatically the best value solution. If closures trigger severe disruption, the market will not reward that just because it appears tough.

The right standard is:
- durable savings,
- credible execution,
- limited disruption,
- and a path to restored returns.

Not symbolic severity.

## My governance position
As controlling owners, we will support a restructuring **only if** it satisfies three conditions:
1. **Durable competitiveness improvement**
2. **A credible path to labour peace**
3. **No unnecessary destruction of long-term enterprise value**

That means we are open to a serious no-closure package, but not to a cosmetic one. And we are not endorsing closures as the starting point unless management can show they are genuinely required.

## What I want next
Before anyone escalates this into a confrontation over closures, I want a **concrete package proposal** with numbers:
- annual savings target,
- capacity reduction by site,
- headcount reduction path,
- labor-cost concessions,
- one-time cash cost,
- timing to run-rate,
- and what guarantees, if any, are attached.
