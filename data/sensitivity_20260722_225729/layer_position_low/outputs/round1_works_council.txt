As the works council, my position is clear:

We will **not accept plant closures in Germany** and we will **not accept compulsory redundancies**. Those are red lines. Volkswagen’s crisis cannot be solved by destroying the workforce and hollowing out the industrial base that made this company strong.

What we are prepared to discuss is a **socially responsible restructuring** that preserves all German sites and protects jobs through:

- binding **site guarantees**
- binding **job guarantees through 2030**
- **capacity adjustments** without closures
- **work-time and flexibility measures**
- a **negotiated, fair employee contribution** only if it is shared, limited, and tied to long-term security
- a real **co-determination role** for labour in shaping any restructuring plan

We know the company faces serious competitive pressure. But management cannot demand that workers pay the price for strategic failures while taking away historic guarantees and threatening whole plants. That is unacceptable.

If management wants cooperation, it must come with a credible alternative to closures: a plan that secures the future of the German sites, preserves skilled jobs, and invests in the transition rather than using the crisis as an excuse for breaking the social compact. Otherwise, we will resist this with all the means at our disposal.
