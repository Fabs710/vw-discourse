### Neutral moderator summary

#### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties acknowledge that Volkswagen faces a **serious structural competitiveness problem**.
- There is broad agreement that a **purely cosmetic or short-term solution is insufficient**.
- Most parties accept the need for some form of **durable restructuring**, including:
  - capacity adjustment,
  - productivity improvements,
  - working-time/flexibility measures,
  - and some form of contribution from labor and/or capital.
- There is also shared concern that a **prolonged conflict** would damage the company, employment, and value creation.

#### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  - Works council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject closures.  
  - Management, controlling owners, and investors do not endorse closures as a goal, but will not rule them out if needed for a credible solution.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Rejected by labor and Lower Saxony.  
  - Management and capital actors insist that any deal must produce real structural change, but have not accepted this red line.
- **Scale and nature of employee contribution**:  
  - Labor is open to limited, shared, and reversible sacrifices.  
  - Management and investors argue that concessions must be substantial, durable, and economically meaningful.
- **Whether “no closures” can still deliver sufficient savings**:  
  - Labor and Lower Saxony say yes, if properly designed.  
  - Management, owners, and investors say this remains unproven.

#### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness**: how to reduce costs enough without breaking employment guarantees.
- **Site preservation vs. capacity reduction**: how much capacity can be removed while keeping all German sites viable.
- **Short-term industrial peace vs. long-term structural savings**: how much disruption is tolerable to achieve a lasting deal.
- **Fair burden-sharing**: how contributions from workers, management, owners, and possibly the state should be balanced.
- **Credibility of guarantees**: what binding commitments can realistically be offered on jobs, sites, and future investment.
- **Path to implementation**: whether reductions can be achieved through attrition, flexibility, or productivity gains instead of closures and layoffs, and whether that would be sufficient.

#### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principle statements to **concrete package options**. The next round should identify:
- the **minimum cost and capacity reduction** required,
- which measures are **acceptable alternatives to closures**,
- what **binding guarantees** could be offered in return,
- and how any **employee, management, owner, and public-sector contributions** would be structured.

The key question for the next round is: **What specific restructuring package can meet the competitiveness target while avoiding the outcomes labor and regional stakeholders reject?**
