We are not moving off our core line.

Management, shareholders, and investors keep repeating “structural savings,” but you still have not shown that this must mean **closing German plants** or tearing up the job-security framework. From our side, that remains the wrong answer. It is socially destructive, it destroys trust, and it would be a historic breach of the social compact at Volkswagen.

## Where I agree
I agree that Volkswagen needs a **durable competitiveness solution**. I agree that cosmetic measures are not enough. I also agree that the company needs real cost relief, better productivity, and a credible industrial plan through 2030.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the leap from “we need savings” to “we need closures or forced redundancies.” That is a management choice, not a law of nature.

And I reject the framing that labour is being “inflexible” because we refuse to accept plant closures. We are the ones bringing forward an alternative that is socially responsible and operationally credible:
- **all German sites remain open**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **capacity reduction through non-destructive means**
- **working-time and flexibility measures**
- **agreed productivity improvements**
- **limited, fair employee contribution only if tied to binding guarantees**

## What is required for any serious discussion
If management wants any constructive path forward, then put concrete numbers on the table and bind yourselves first:

1. **Binding site guarantees for all German plants through 2030**
2. **Binding job guarantees through 2030**
3. **No compulsory redundancies**
4. **No plant closures**
5. **A real co-determination role** for the works council and employee side in designing the restructuring
6. **Management and owners must also contribute**, not just workers

Without those guarantees, there is no basis for us to discuss sacrifice. Workers will not pay for a crisis they did not create by surrendering jobs, sites, and security.

## On the “minimum savings” question
You ask for a concrete package. Fine — here is the only acceptable route from our perspective:

- capacity adjustments **within existing plants**
- shift and working-time changes
- temporary utilization measures
- voluntary departures only
- training and redeployment
- targeted cost measures that are **limited, reversible, and negotiated**
- investment commitments to secure future volumes and new products in Germany

What we will **not** treat as negotiable:
- plant closures
- forced layoffs
- dismantling of co-determination
- ending the historic job-security framework

If your financial target cannot be met without breaking those red lines, then the problem is not labour’s resistance. The problem is that management’s strategy is built on damaging the company’s long-term foundation.

## To Lower Saxony
I welcome your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But that has to translate into a firm political stance in the supervisory board, not just statements of concern. If the state is serious, it should make clear that it will not support any package that sacrifices German sites.

## To the controlling owners and investors
You both keep saying you care about long-term value. Then stop treating workers as the adjustable cost item. Long-term value is not built by gutting the industrial base and provoking industrial conflict. It is built by preserving skills, stabilising sites, and investing in the future.

## Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a **socially responsible restructuring**.

We are **not** ready to negotiate:
- German plant closures
- compulsory redundancies
- a deal that breaks the historic job-security framework

If management wants a deal, it must bring a package that respects those limits and includes binding guarantees. Otherwise, we will continue to resist this with every legitimate means available, including industrial action and using our co-determination rights to block any unacceptable course.
