As the State of Lower Saxony, I need to be very clear: **we do not accept a restructuring that is achieved by shutting plants in Germany and hollowing out Volkswagen’s regional base.** That would be economically harmful, socially destabilising, and politically unacceptable.

I do **agree** with management on one point: Volkswagen needs a **credible, durable improvement in competitiveness**. Pretending that the current cost structure is sustainable would be irresponsible. But the route to that result matters. A solution that destroys jobs, weakens co-determination, and damages the industrial ecosystem in Lower Saxony is **not** a responsible solution.

### Where I stand
- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No forced redundancies**
- **No sidelining of the state’s co-determination and veto role**
- **Yes to a negotiated restructuring** if it preserves sites, protects core employment, and restores competitiveness in a socially sustainable way

### To management
Your diagnosis may be partly right, but your framing is too narrow. You are treating closures as if they are the only credible lever. They are not. There are other ways to achieve structural savings:
- capacity alignment without closing sites
- working-time and flexibility arrangements
- productivity improvements
- phased workforce adjustments through natural attrition and agreed measures
- targeted labour-cost contributions, but only if they are balanced and limited
- new product and investment commitments that secure future volumes

What I will not support is a plan that asks the workforce and the regions to bear the full burden while ownership and management simply declare victory through plant shutdowns. That is short-termism, and it would damage Volkswagen’s legitimacy as much as its operations.

### To the works council and IG Metall
Your red lines are understandable, and I share the concern about protecting skilled jobs and industrial capacity. But we also have to be honest: the company is under real pressure. A durable deal will require **some contribution from labour**, provided it is:
- fair
- time-limited or reversible where possible
- linked to binding guarantees on sites and jobs
- matched by commitments from management and owners

A purely defensive position that rejects every structural adjustment would also be dangerous. We need a negotiated outcome that protects the company’s future **and** the workforce’s future.

### My bottom line
Lower Saxony is prepared to support a **social-partnership restructuring**, but not a dismantling of Volkswagen’s German footprint. If management wants our backing, it must bring:
1. a plan that **avoids closures**
2. **binding protection for employment and sites**
3. a **real management and ownership contribution**
4. a credible industrial strategy for the German plants

If that cannot be delivered, then I will not help force through a decision that sacrifices our regions for the sake of a simplistic cost-cutting exercise.
