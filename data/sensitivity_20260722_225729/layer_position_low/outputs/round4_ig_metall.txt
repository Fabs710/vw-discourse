I’ll be just as direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, Lower Saxony, and the expert on the central diagnosis:

- Volkswagen needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one.
- The current German cost and capacity structure is too heavy.
- A prolonged conflict would damage the company and the workforce.
- A meaningful employee contribution may be part of any settlement.

I also agree that a no-closure path is **not impossible in principle**. That is important.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated leap from “the restructuring must be deep” to “therefore plant closures or compulsory redundancies are justified.”

That has still not been proven.

The expert’s scenarios show something decisive: a no-closure path can work **if it is large, permanent, and execution-heavy**. That means closures are not the only economically conceivable route. They are one route, and the most destructive one for trust, industrial peace, and long-term skills retention.

So I reject the idea that labor must first surrender its red lines before management has demonstrated necessity.

## To management
You say the no-closure package “does not yet add up.” Fine — then show the arithmetic.

What I still do not see is:
- the exact annual savings gap after a fully loaded no-closure package,
- the site-by-site impact of internal capacity concentration,
- the real savings from overhead cuts and management reductions,
- and the reason these measures cannot close the gap without closures.

Until you put that on the table, closures remain a preference, not a necessity.

And let me be clear: if management wants labor to contribute, that contribution must be **shared and bounded**. Workers will not bear all the pain while capital preserves optionality and management preserves its own room.

## To the works council
I share your red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies.

But I also want to be blunt: a no-closure position must be backed by a **serious industrial package**, not just a slogan. If all German plants stay open, then the restructuring still has to be hard:
- shift reductions where needed,
- capacity concentration inside the existing network,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a real, limited employee contribution.

So yes, I stand with you against closures and forced layoffs. But the alternative must be operationally credible.

## To Lower Saxony
I welcome your support against closures and compulsory redundancies. But support has to be more than rhetoric.

Use your blocking position to prevent any forced closure agenda. At the same time, back a binding no-closure restructuring that delivers real savings and protects future investment.

A political “no” without an industrial plan is not enough. And an industrial plan without binding guarantees is also not enough.

## To the controlling owners and investors
I reject the notion that a closure-based plan is automatically the better value solution.

A closure may look clean in a spreadsheet, but it also risks:
- years of conflict,
- production disruption,
- loss of know-how,
- damage to morale,
- and erosion of the social compact that has long underpinned Volkswagen’s stability.

That is not value creation. That is value destruction with a short-term accounting benefit.

If you care about long-term value, then support the route that preserves industrial strength **and** delivers savings: a deep no-closure restructuring with binding guarantees.

## What IG Metall will support
We are prepared to negotiate a package built around:
- shift reductions
- capacity concentration inside the existing network
- voluntary exits
- attrition
- training and redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- limited and fair bonus restraint
- productivity improvements
- management and overhead cuts
- investment commitments for future products

But only on these conditions:
1. **all German plants remain open**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding job guarantees through 2030**
4. **binding site guarantees through 2030**
5. **real co-determination in implementation**
6. **fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

That is not a soft position. It is the minimum basis for a durable settlement.

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- any closure “just in case”
- any forced layoff “if needed”
- any package that leaves plant security vague
- any weakening of co-determination
- any restructuring where the workforce carries the main burden while management and owners do not

## My challenge back to management
If closures are supposedly unavoidable, answer this plainly:

**What exactly have you exhausted already, in quantified terms, that makes a no-closure path fail?**

Not slogans. Not pressure. Numbers.

If you cannot answer that, then you are not proving necessity. You are escalating leverage.

## Bottom line
IG Metall is not refusing restructuring.

We are refusing a restructuring that sacrifices jobs, sites, and collective standards as the first resort.

A durable solution is possible **without closures and without compulsory redundancies** — but only if management and owners accept real shared sacrifice and binding guarantees.

If they do not, then they are not asking for a solution. They are asking workers to absorb the damage while others keep the upside. We will not accept that.
