Core impasse identified:

1. **Economic sufficiency vs. social red lines**
   - Management, owners, and investors are asking for a restructuring that produces **durable, material fixed-cost savings**.
   - Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony are insisting that this must happen **without plant closures, without compulsory redundancies, and without breaking the job-security/co-determination framework**.
   - The finance/operations expert says a no-closure path is **possible in principle**, but only if it is **large, permanent, and execution-heavy**; otherwise it will likely be insufficient.

2. **Disagreement over what counts as “equivalent” savings**
   - Management side: non-closure measures so far do not yet look large enough.
   - Labor/political side: closures are not proven necessary; show the gap first and exhaust other levers.
   - This means the parties are not just disputing values; they are disputing **the threshold of adequacy**.

3. **Trust and sequencing problem**
   - Labor wants binding guarantees first.
   - Management wants acceptance of structural change before committing to guarantees it says it cannot safely give.
   - The result is a deadlock over **who moves first**.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Compromise position 1: “No closure, but verified deep reset”
A negotiated package that keeps German plants open **for a defined period** while accepting:
- substantial shift reductions,
- permanent capacity concentration inside the existing network,
- voluntary exits plus attrition at scale,
- bonus restraint / limited pay concession,
- productivity targets with enforcement,
- and a joint review mechanism every 6–12 months.

**Bridge function:** Preserves the no-closure red line while giving management a route to meaningful fixed-cost reduction.

---

### Compromise position 2: “Conditional site guarantees with trigger-based flexibility”
Binding site guarantees through 2030, but with a **clearly defined escape/adjustment clause** tied to:
- market volume thresholds,
- EV demand shortfall,
- or failure to hit agreed productivity/savings milestones.

If triggers are met, parties reopen negotiations on capacity and footprint.

**Bridge function:** Gives labor political/security protection while giving management a contingent mechanism if the no-closure plan underdelivers.

---

### Compromise position 3: “Joint savings commission with quantified substitution test”
A time-limited, jointly governed process where finance/operations, management, works council, and union agree on:
- the exact annual savings target,
- the savings required from each lever,
- and a formal test for whether non-closure measures are economically equivalent to closure options.

No final closure decision before the commission reports.

**Bridge function:** Converts the argument from ideology to numbers and forces agreement on the minimum viable package before final escalation.
