Here is the side-by-side **finance/operations comparison** in a more decision-useful format, staying within the limits of what can be inferred from the information available.

## 1) Key assumption
We do **not** have Volkswagen’s internal plant-level cost base, utilization, or model economics. So this is a **scenario range**, not a verified plan.

For a large German auto manufacturer, the question is whether the restructuring must remove about:

- **€2.0bn–€3.5bn annual run-rate savings** to make a no-closure path credible, or
- **€3.5bn–€5.5bn annual run-rate savings** if the problem is structurally larger and requires footprint reduction.

That is the practical gap being debated.

---

## 2) Minimum credible no-closure package
A no-closure plan can work **only if it is structurally deep**, not symbolic.

### Typical measures
- shift reductions at selected plants
- capacity concentration inside existing sites
- voluntary exits / early retirement / attrition
- working-time flexibility
- wage/bonus moderation
- overhead cuts in admin, engineering, and management layers
- product-line simplification and productivity gains

### Indicative financial effect
- **Annual savings run-rate:** €2.0bn–€3.5bn
- **Headcount impact:** 10,000–20,000 fewer jobs over time
- **One-time restructuring cost:** €1.0bn–€2.5bn
- **Time to full run-rate:** 24–36 months

### Operational interpretation
This route is feasible if the main issue is:
- excess labor cost,
- underutilization,
- overhead duplication,
- and the company can absorb capacity concentration without closing sites.

It becomes hard to justify if:
- multiple plants are structurally underloaded,
- savings depend too much on temporary concessions,
- or headcount reduction cannot be achieved fast enough through voluntary means.

---

## 3) Closure-based restructuring
This is the stronger fixed-cost reset.

### Typical measures
- closure of one or more plants
- larger direct and indirect workforce reductions
- elimination of duplicate support functions
- stronger capacity concentration
- possible product reallocation across the network

### Indicative financial effect
- **Annual savings run-rate:** €3.5bn–€5.5bn
- **Headcount impact:** 15,000–30,000+ jobs reduced
- **One-time restructuring cost:** €2.5bn–€5.0bn+
- **Time to full run-rate:** 24–48 months

### Operational interpretation
This route delivers:
- greater permanent fixed-cost removal,
- clearer footprint simplification,
- stronger long-term competitiveness.

But it also brings:
- higher one-time cash cost,
- higher execution risk,
- industrial disruption,
- potential productivity loss during transition.

---

## 4) Main trade-off
The economic choice is not “closures vs nothing.”

It is:

### No-closure path
- lower one-time cost
- lower conflict risk
- better continuity
- but capped savings if the network stays too broad

### Closure path
- higher one-time cost
- more disruption
- but greater chance of restoring structural competitiveness

The decision should be based on **incremental present value**:
- do the extra annual savings from closures justify the extra restructuring cost and disruption?

If closures add only **€300m–€700m/year**, the case is weaker.  
If they add **€1bn–€2bn/year**, the economic case becomes much stronger.

---

## 5) Practical conclusion
From an operations/finance perspective:

- A **no-closure solution is possible in principle**
- But it must be **large, permanent, and execution-heavy**
- If the savings requirement is at the upper end of the range, **closures become difficult to avoid**
- If the required savings are nearer the lower end, a **deep internal restructuring without closures** could be sufficient

## 6) What is still needed to decide conclusively
To resolve this, the parties would need site-by-site analysis of:

- utilization by plant
- fixed and variable cost by site
- headcount by function and location
- product contribution margins
- severance and impairment costs
- logistics and supplier impacts
- timing to achieve run-rate savings

Without that, any side claiming “closures are necessary” or “closures are not necessary” is still making a **directionally plausible but unproven** assertion.
