I will be equally direct.

## My position
Lower Saxony will **not** support German plant closures or compulsory redundancies. That remains my line. It is not just politically undesirable; it would be economically and socially damaging for the region, and it would weaken Volkswagen’s role as an anchor employer.

At the same time, I do **not** support freezing the company in its current structure. Volkswagen needs a **durable restructuring**. The question is whether management and capital are prepared to do that **inside the existing German network**, rather than by destroying sites and jobs.

---

## Where I agree
I agree with management, owners, investors, and the expert on the diagnosis:

- Volkswagen has a real competitiveness problem.
- The current cost base is too high.
- A cosmetic package will not solve it.
- The company must remove real fixed cost.
- A prolonged conflict would hurt everyone.

I also agree with the finance/operations view that a restructuring has to be **structural**, not symbolic. That is exactly why a serious no-closure package must be taken seriously.

---

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated assertion that closures are necessary because no-closure measures “do not yet add up.” That has not been proven.

The expert has already said a no-closure route is **possible in principle** if it is large, permanent, and disciplined enough. That means closures are not a demonstrated necessity. They are one option, and the most destructive one for trust, industrial peace, and regional stability.

So my answer remains:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **Yes to deep restructuring**
- **Yes to competitiveness**
- **No to sacrificing the industrial base as the first resort**

---

## To management
You keep saying the no-closure plan is not yet enough. Fine. Then show us the numbers, not just the conclusion.

What I still do **not** see is:
- the exact savings gap after a fully loaded no-closure package,
- the site-by-site effect of internal capacity concentration,
- the genuine savings from overhead cuts and management reductions,
- and why those measures cannot close the gap without closures.

Right now, you are asking the workforce and the state to accept permanent insecurity before you have proved that insecurity is unavoidable. That is not a serious basis for agreement.

And let me be frank: trust is not neutral here. Management has already damaged it by moving against the historic guarantees first. You do not repair that by demanding that workers surrender the protections that made negotiations possible in the first place.

---

## To the works council
I agree with your core red lines. But I also want to be clear: “no closures” cannot remain only a slogan. If we want to protect sites, we must support a package that actually delivers savings.

That means:
- shift reductions where capacity is no longer needed,
- concentration of volumes inside the existing network,
- voluntary exits at meaningful scale,
- attrition,
- redeployment and training,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- management overhead cuts,
- and a fair, limited employee contribution.

That is the price of preserving the industrial base. If the alternative is only “all sites stay open and nothing substantive changes,” then that is not a restructuring.

---

## To IG Metall
I support the principle that the burden must be shared. But “shared sacrifice” must not become a back door to dismantling plant security.

I will not support:
- layoffs by attrition without safeguards,
- hidden closures through underutilization,
- or flexibility language that slowly hollows out sites while pretending the red lines are intact.

If the union wants a deal, it must be a **real protection package**, not a disguised downsizing plan.

---

## To the controlling owners and investors
I understand your concern for value and execution certainty. But do not confuse a “cleaner” spreadsheet with a better long-term solution.

A closure-based plan may look neat, but it also risks:
- years of conflict,
- production disruption,
- loss of know-how,
- reputational damage,
- and weakened labor relations for years.

That is not stewardship; that is short-term arithmetic with long-term costs.

If you really care about long-term value, then you should support the route that preserves industrial strength **and** delivers savings: a deep no-closure restructuring with binding guarantees.

---

## On the expert’s numbers
The expert’s ranges do **not** prove that closures are needed. They show the opposite: a no-closure solution is **plausible**.

Yes, it will be harder. Yes, it will require discipline. Yes, it will take longer. But that is not the same as impossible.

What the numbers show is that the key question is not whether internal restructuring is easy. It is whether management, owners, and investors are willing to accept a serious internal reset that does **not** sacrifice German plants.

My answer is yes. Yours so far is: only if workers first surrender the very protections that make a negotiated solution possible. That is unacceptable.

---

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a package built around:
- shift reductions
- capacity concentration within the existing German network
- voluntary exits
- attrition
- training and redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- limited and fair bonus restraint
- productivity improvements
- management overhead cuts
- investment commitments for future products

But only on these conditions:
1. **all German plants remain open**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding job guarantees through 2030**
4. **binding site guarantees through 2030**
5. **real co-determination in implementation**
6. **fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

---

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- any plant closure “just in case”
- any forced layoff “if needed”
- any package that leaves site security vague
- any erosion of co-determination
- any claim that the workforce must prove loyalty by accepting structural damage

---

## My challenge back to management
If closures are supposedly unavoidable, then answer this plainly:

**What exactly have you exhausted already, in quantified terms, that makes a no-closure path fail?**

Not slogans. Not pressure. Numbers.

If you cannot answer that, then you are not proving necessity. You are escalating leverage.

---

## Bottom line
We are not moving off our core position.

Volkswagen needs a deep reset, yes. But that reset must happen **without closing German plants and without compulsory redundancies**.

If management and capital want a real agreement, bring:
- the quantified no-closure savings gap,
- the site-by-site internal restructuring plan,
- and the binding guarantees that make the package credible.

Otherwise, expect continued resistance from Lower Saxony, from the works council, and from everyone responsible for the long-term industrial strength of this company.
