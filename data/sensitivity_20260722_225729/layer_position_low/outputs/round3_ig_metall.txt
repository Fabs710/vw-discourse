I will be equally clear.

## Where I agree
I agree with the works council, Lower Saxony, and the expert on the core diagnosis:
- Volkswagen needs a **durable** restructuring, not a cosmetic one.
- The current German cost base and capacity are too high for the market environment.
- A prolonged conflict would damage the company, workers, and value creation.
- A purely symbolic employee contribution would not be acceptable.

I also agree that a **no-closure package is not impossible in principle**. The expert has shown that. That matters.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated claim that because the restructuring must be deep, it therefore must include **plant closures** or **compulsory redundancies**. That has not been proven. It is being asserted by management as the convenient route, not demonstrated as the only route.

The expert’s scenarios make that very clear:
- A no-closure package can work if it is **large, permanent, and credible**.
- That means real fixed-cost removal, real capacity concentration, real headcount reduction through voluntary means and attrition, and real wage/bonus restraint.
- It does **not** mean preserving the status quo with soft language.

So I am not persuaded by management’s “nothing adds up” line unless it is backed by a quantified comparison of exhausted alternatives.

## To management
You keep saying the no-closure option does not yet add up. Then show the arithmetic in a way that can be tested.

Right now, what I hear is:
- you want durable savings,
- you want labor to pay,
- and you want to keep closures as a pressure point.

What I do **not** hear is:
- the exact remaining savings gap after a serious no-closure package,
- the exact measures you have already priced,
- and the exact reason those measures cannot be sufficient.

So let me state our position plainly:

### We are prepared to negotiate:
- capacity reductions inside the existing network
- shift reductions
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits
- redeployment and training
- bonus restraint / temporary employee contribution
- productivity improvements
- management and overhead cuts
- investment commitments for future products

### We are not prepared to accept:
- plant closures
- compulsory redundancies
- tearing up the collective wage framework
- a restructuring where workers bear the main burden while management and owners stay protected

If management wants a deal, it must stop treating closure as the default answer and put a **verified no-closure savings package** on the table.

## To the works council
I share your red lines on closures and forced redundancies. But I want to be equally honest: “no closures” is not enough if it is just a slogan. A no-closure deal must still produce real and durable savings.

That means we need a package that is hard, not decorative:
- fewer shifts where capacity is no longer needed
- concentration of volumes in existing plants
- voluntary departures at meaningful scale
- productivity commitments with monitoring
- working-time and flexibility measures
- limited, fair employee contribution tied to binding guarantees

So yes, I stand with you against closures and forced layoffs. But the alternative must be industrially serious, not just politically comforting.

## To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your support against closures and compulsory redundancies. But support has to mean more than statements. Your veto position matters. Use it to insist that management fully exhausts no-closure options before even trying to force a closure agenda.

At the same time, I will be blunt: if the state is serious about preserving jobs, it must back a restructuring package that actually secures volumes and investment in Germany. Symbolic resistance alone is not enough.

## To the controlling owners
You say you want long-term value. Then stop framing workers as the easiest adjustment variable.

If you want a durable solution, then owners must accept that:
- labor cannot carry the entire burden,
- plant closures are not automatically value-preserving,
- and industrial peace is itself an asset.

I am open to a real shared-sacrifice package. I am not open to a one-sided reset.

## To institutional investors
Your demand for credible execution is fair. But credibility does not automatically mean closures. It means:
- real savings,
- enforceable commitments,
- and a path that does not destroy the workforce relationship on which this company depends.

If a no-closure package genuinely delivers the needed annual savings and structural reset, then it is value-preserving. If it does not, then the problem is the package, not the absence of closures as such.

## The union’s concrete position
IG Metall is prepared to support a package with:
- **no plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding site guarantees**
- **binding job security**
- **capacity reductions through internal restructuring**
- **working-time and flexibility measures**
- **temporary, limited employee contributions**
- **voluntary exit pathways**
- **qualification and redeployment**
- **product and investment commitments**
- **management and owner contributions**

But that package must also include:
- a credible annual savings target,
- a clear allocation of burden across all sides,
- and binding implementation rules.

## Our red line remains unchanged
We will not accept:
- forced layoffs
- plant closures
- dismantling of the collective wage framework
- a deal that makes workers carry the crisis alone

If those are the terms, then there is no agreement, and the conflict will continue.

## What I need next
If management wants serious progress, I want a **quantified side-by-side package**:
1. the minimum annual savings target,
2. the no-closure package and its savings,
3. the one-closure package and its savings,
4. the headcount impact under each,
5. the one-time cost under each,
6. the timing to run-rate.

## Bottom line
We are not refusing restructuring. We are refusing a restructuring that sacrifices jobs, sites, and collective standards as the first resort.

A durable solution is possible **without closures and without compulsory redundancies** — but only if management and capital are prepared to make a real contribution and accept a serious internal reset.

If they are not, then they are not asking for a solution. They are asking workers to absorb the damage while others keep the upside. We will not accept that.
