I will be equally direct: **the burden of proof is still on management, not on the workforce**.

## Where I agree
I agree with the moderator, the expert, Lower Saxony, and even parts of management and the owners on one basic fact: Volkswagen needs a **durable, material restructuring**. No one here can pretend a cosmetic programme will do. I also agree that the company must protect future investment and improve competitiveness through 2030.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the frame that because a “hard” restructuring is needed, the answer must be plant closures or compulsory redundancies.

That argument has still not been demonstrated. It is being asserted.

The finance expert has said clearly that a **no-closure package can work in principle** if it is large enough, permanent enough, and disciplined enough. That is the crucial point. It means closures are **not proven necessary**. They are one option among others, and the most destructive one for trust, industrial peace, and the long-term social base of the company.

So let me be explicit:

- **No plant closures in Germany**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No bargaining over the workforce after management has already decided the outcome**

Those remain our limits.

## To management
You keep saying the current proposals “do not yet add up.” Fine — then show the arithmetic.

Do not keep hiding behind broad claims about structural cost. Put the numbers on the table:
- what exact annual savings gap remains after a serious no-closure package,
- what capacity reduction inside the existing network has been fully tested,
- what overhead cuts you have extracted from management and central functions,
- what sacrifices you are asking of owners and capital,
- and why these cannot be sufficient without destroying sites.

Until you do that, your insistence on closures is not a necessity. It is a preference.

And let’s be honest: management has broken trust by moving against the historic guarantees first. That is why we are in this conflict. You cannot now demand that workers accept insecurity as the price of repairing a problem created by your strategic choices.

## To the controlling owners and investors
I reject the idea that “cleaner” restructuring automatically means better value.

A closure-based plan may look neat in a spreadsheet, but it also risks:
- years of conflict,
- production disruption,
- loss of know-how,
- weakened identification with the company,
- damage to the VW brand in Germany,
- and long-term erosion of labor relations.

That is not stewardship. That is short-term arithmetic dressed up as strategy.

If you really care about long-term value, then you should support the route that preserves industrial strength **and** delivers savings. That route is a deep no-closure restructuring with binding guarantees, not site destruction.

## To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your statement that closures are not the only path. But that cannot remain rhetorical support. If you are serious, then use your blocking position to stop any attempt to force closures or compulsory redundancies onto the works.

A “hard no-closure package” is acceptable only if it is paired with:
- binding employment protection,
- binding site guarantees,
- real investment commitments,
- and genuine volume concentration inside the existing network.

Anything else is asking workers to absorb permanent insecurity while the state talks about responsibility.

## To IG Metall
You are right to insist that any burden must be shared. But I want to be careful here: “shared sacrifice” must not become a back door to conceding the red lines.

I will not endorse a package that:
- preserves the rhetoric of no layoffs while creating layoffs by attrition without safeguards,
- accepts hidden closures through underutilization,
- or uses flexibility language to hollow out plant security.

If the union wants a no-closure deal, then it must be a **real protection package**, not a disguised downsizing programme.

## On the expert’s scenario table
The expert has done what management still has not done: give ranges. Those ranges actually strengthen our position, because they show that a no-closure path is **not fantasy**. It may be harder. It may require more discipline. It may take longer. But it exists.

That means the question is not:
> “Can we avoid closures and still do nothing else?”

The question is:
> “Are management, owners, and investors prepared to accept a serious internal restructuring that does not sacrifice German plants?”

Our answer is yes. Your answer so far is: only if workers first surrender their core protections. That is unacceptable.

## What we would accept
If you want a concrete statement, here it is:

We are prepared to discuss a package built around:
- shift reductions
- capacity concentration within existing sites
- voluntary exits
- attrition
- training and redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- targeted and limited wage/bonus restraint
- productivity improvements
- management and overhead cuts
- investment commitments for future products

But only on the basis of:
1. **all German plants remaining open**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding job guarantees through 2030**
4. **binding site guarantees through 2030**
5. **a real co-determination role in implementation**
6. **fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- any closure “just in case”
- any forced layoff “if needed”
- any package that leaves plant security vague
- any erosion of co-determination
- any claim that the workforce must prove loyalty by accepting permanent damage

## My challenge back to management
If a no-closure package is supposedly insufficient, then answer this plainly:

**What specific combination of measures, short of closures and forced redundancies, have you exhausted and found inadequate?**

If you cannot answer that with numbers, then you are not engaging in restructuring — you are preparing a power move against the workforce.

## Bottom line
We are not retreating.

The company needs a deep reset, yes. But the reset must happen **without closing German plants and without compulsory redundancies**. That remains the only acceptable basis for a settlement.

If management wants real dialogue, bring the quantified no-closure plan and the binding guarantees. Otherwise, expect continued resistance from the workforce, from the works council, and from everyone who understands that Volkswagen’s strength was built not by tearing up its social compact, but by respecting it.
