From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the discussion has clarified the real issue: **the company needs a durable, large enough cost reset to restore competitive margins, and it needs it with execution certainty**. On that point, I agree with management and the controlling owners, and I agree with the finance/operations assessment.

Where I disagree is with any line of argument that treats **“no closures” as automatically acceptable** just because it is politically easier. It is not enough for a package to be socially palatable; it must be **financially sufficient** and **credible in implementation**.

## Where we agree
- Volkswagen’s German cost base is structurally too high.
- Cosmetic concessions are not acceptable.
- Prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.
- A durable solution needs real fixed-cost reduction, not just temporary optics.

## Where I disagree
### To the works council and IG Metall
Your red lines are understandable, but from an equity-holder perspective they are only acceptable **if** the replacement package truly closes the savings gap. Right now, I have not heard evidence that:
- all German sites can remain open,
- no compulsory redundancies occur,
- and the company still achieves the required structural improvement.

The expert’s numbers matter here. A no-closure solution is only viable if it delivers something like **€1.0–1.5 billion annually in durable savings**, with real capacity concentration, headcount reduction, and labor-cost relief. If the deal is mostly:
- bonus restraint,
- temporary flexibility,
- voluntary measures with uncertain uptake,
- and vague productivity promises,

then it is **not sufficient**. That would be a delay, not a restructuring.

### To Lower Saxony
I agree that closures are not the only path. But I do not accept the implication that site preservation itself is a sufficient objective. If you block closures, then you must be prepared to accept a package that is **hard enough to restore margins**. Otherwise you are defending industrial geography at the expense of shareholder value.

### To management
I support a decisive restructuring, but I also do not want management to overstate closures as a default solution if a serious no-closure package can do the job. The burden is on management to show:
- the **savings gap remaining after non-closure measures**,
- the **timing** to reach run-rate savings,
- and the **execution risk**.

If management cannot demonstrate that no-closure options fail economically, then forcing closures would be premature and likely value-destructive.

## My position
We will support **whatever package credibly restores competitiveness**. That means:

- **material and durable fixed-cost reduction**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **a believable path to higher margins and return on sales**
- **limited industrial disruption**
- **implementation certainty**

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that protects every legacy arrangement while keeping costs uncompetitive,
- or a prolonged standoff that erodes earnings and valuation.

## What we need next
Before anyone settles on a final direction, we need a **clear numerical comparison** of:
1. the minimum savings required,
2. the no-closure package and whether it reaches that number,
3. the closure-based package and its one-time costs,
4. the timing and risk of each path.
