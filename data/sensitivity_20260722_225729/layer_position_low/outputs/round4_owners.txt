I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, Lower Saxony, the works council, IG Metall, and the expert on the **diagnosis**:

- Volkswagen has a real competitiveness problem.
- The cost base is too high.
- Cosmetic measures are not enough.
- A prolonged industrial conflict would destroy value.
- The company needs a **durable structural reset**, not a temporary fix.

I also agree with the expert on the key analytical point: a **no-closure solution is possible in principle**, but only if it is **large, permanent, and execution-heavy**.

That matters. It means closures are **not automatically required**. But it also means a weak no-closure package is not acceptable.

## Where I disagree with labor and Lower Saxony
I do **not** accept the claim that because plant closures are undesirable, they are therefore unnecessary.

That has still not been demonstrated.

What I hear from the works council and IG Metall is:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding guarantees through 2030,
- limited and fair employee contributions,
- and a strong preference that management simply make the numbers work.

That is a **boundary**, not yet a proven restructuring solution.

If you want closures off the table, then you must show a package that truly replaces the lost fixed-cost reduction. Right now, I do not see that proof.

## Where I disagree with management
I also do **not** accept any attempt to treat closures as the default answer without a quantified demonstration that internal restructuring fails.

The burden is on management to show:
- the exact remaining savings gap after a fully loaded no-closure package,
- the site-by-site capacity effects,
- the real headcount reduction path through voluntary exits and attrition,
- and why those measures cannot close the gap.

Until that is shown, closures are not a necessity. They are a preference.

And I am not prepared to endorse a value-destructive shortcut.

## To the works council
You are right to insist that labor should not be asked to carry the burden alone. I agree.

But I will not accept an outcome where “no closures” really means “no meaningful restructuring.” The company still needs:
- shift reductions,
- capacity concentration inside the existing network,
- voluntary exits at scale,
- attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- overhead cuts,
- and a real contribution from labor costs.

If you want the German network preserved, then it must become more efficient. Preservation without hard change is not a solution.

## To IG Metall
I appreciate that you are willing to discuss serious measures. But again: the scale matters.

A temporary or symbolic contribution is not enough. If the company is to avoid closures, then labor must be prepared to accept a **durable** package, not just a politically comfortable one.

I will not support any settlement that preserves all legacy protections and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

## To Lower Saxony
I understand your political responsibility. But I do not accept a position that treats plant closures as taboo regardless of the economics.

If you want to block closures, then support a no-closure restructuring that is actually capable of delivering the savings. That means:
- visible capacity concentration,
- measurable cost reduction,
- serious internal reallocation,
- and real execution discipline.

A regional veto is not a business plan.

## To management, owners, and investors
I agree with the need for value protection and execution certainty. But let me be equally clear: I will not back a package that destroys long-term value through unnecessary industrial conflict.

The right question is not:
- “Can we force a cleaner cut?”

The right question is:
- “What is the best long-term value outcome that is still economically credible?”

A no-closure deal is acceptable **only if** it is economically equivalent. If it is not, then it is not value-preserving.

## My governance position
As controlling owners, our concern is long-term enterprise value and stewardship, not theatrical toughness.

So our position is:

- We are **open** to a serious no-closure restructuring.
- We are **not** endorsing closures as the first resort.
- We are **not** supporting a cosmetic compromise.
- We are **not** prepared to back a deal that leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive.

But I also need a plan that is more than a list of red lines. I need proof.

## What I want next
We need a **fully quantified comparison** of:

1. the minimum credible no-closure package,
2. the closure-based alternative,
3. annual savings under each,
4. one-time restructuring costs,
5. headcount impact,
6. timing to run-rate savings,
7. and site-by-site capacity effects.

## Bottom line
I am not retreating from my core position.

Volkswagen must become:
- structurally cheaper,
- structurally leaner,
- and structurally more competitive.

I will support the mechanism that achieves that **with the least destruction of long-term value**.

If the employee side can prove that a no-closure package really closes the gap, then that route remains open.

If it cannot, then the discussion changes. But until then, I will not accept closures as a starting assumption, and I will not accept a weak compromise dressed up as stewardship.
