## Where I agree
I agree with management, owners, and investors on the **diagnosis**:
- Volkswagen needs a **durable competitiveness reset**
- the current cost base is too high
- cosmetic measures are not enough
- a prolonged conflict would damage value and industrial stability

I also accept the expert’s point that a **material capacity reduction** is necessary. We are not talking about preserving every current utilization level and calling that a solution.

## Where I disagree
What I do **not** accept is the repeated leap from “material capacity reduction” to **plant closures** or **forced redundancies** as if those were unavoidable.

The expert made the crucial distinction:  
- non-closure measures can be slower and harder, yes  
- but they can still be **economically meaningful** if they involve real fixed-cost reduction, site-level volume concentration, shift reductions, voluntary exits, and productivity gains

That is the space we should be working in. Not closure as the default, but a **serious restructuring inside the existing German network**.

## To management
Your insistence that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” cannot solve the problem is too absolute.

If the savings gap is genuinely large, then the burden is on management to show:
1. **what specific cost gap remains**
2. **what non-closure package has been fully tested**
3. **why capacity reduction inside the network cannot deliver equivalent results**

So far, I have not seen a convincing case that closures are necessary rather than merely the most convenient option for management. Convenience is not a sufficient reason to damage German industrial capacity.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I support your red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies. But I also think we should be precise:
- “no closures” does **not** mean “no adjustments”
- “no redundancies” does **not** mean “no reduction in effective footprint”
- “site guarantees” must be paired with credible productivity and utilization measures

We cannot simply repeat our position and assume the arithmetic disappears. If we want to avoid closures, then we have to accept a tougher package of:
- shift reductions
- working-time flexibility
- voluntary exits
- training and redeployment
- bonus restraint
- targeted cost contributions
- product and investment commitments

Those are the instruments that can make a no-closure solution real.

## To the controlling owners and investors
You are right to reject a cosmetic deal. But I would caution against treating plant closures as the clean answer. The expert was clear that closing sites is operationally powerful, but politically and industrially destructive.

As an anchor shareholder with a regional responsibility, I cannot support maximizing accounting simplicity at the expense of:
- employment stability
- skills retention
- co-determination
- the regional supply chain
- Volkswagen’s legitimacy as a German industrial anchor

If we destroy trust and the company’s social base, we will pay for that for years.

## My practical position
I will support a restructuring only if it meets these conditions:
1. **no plant closures in Germany**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **real, permanent capacity reduction**
4. **material labour-cost relief**
5. **binding site and investment commitments**
6. **a credible path to restore competitiveness without breaking the social compact**

That means I am open to:
- shift reductions
- headcount reduction through attrition and voluntary exit
- redeployment
- temporary and limited concessions
- productivity measures
- site specialization and volume concentration

But I am **not** open to:
- closing German plants
- forcing layoffs
- using a crisis to dismantle co-determination
- imposing an asymmetric burden on workers and regions

The expert’s answer actually strengthens our position: the company must show the **minimum viable no-closure package** that genuinely closes the cost gap. Until management puts that on the table, closures remain an unproven political choice, not a demonstrated necessity.
