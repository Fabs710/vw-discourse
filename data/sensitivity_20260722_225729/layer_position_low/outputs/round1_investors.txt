From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the key issue is not who “wins” the social argument. It is whether Volkswagen returns to a **credible, durable, competitive cost base** fast enough to protect risk-adjusted equity value.

### What we agree with
We agree with management, and to a point with the controlling owners, on the core diagnosis:

- Volkswagen’s German cost base appears **structurally too high**
- margins are **not where they need to be**
- a cosmetic package or short-term postponement would be value-destructive
- the company needs **durable structural savings**, not just temporary goodwill

That said, we also agree with the caution from Lower Saxony and the works council that a prolonged conflict would destroy value. Strikes, production disruption, and legal/political stalemate are not good for shareholders either.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a solution that leaves the company’s economics basically unchanged.

So if the employee side’s position is effectively:

- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- only limited, reversible concessions,

then that is **not sufficient** from our point of view unless it clearly adds up to a cost reduction large enough to restore competitiveness. If it does not, then it is just a delay tactic, and we would view that as unacceptable.

We also do **not** want management to pursue a “win” that is mostly symbolic but comes at the cost of a long industrial conflict. A deal that technically achieves a closure but triggers months of disruption and uncertainty could easily destroy more value than it creates.

### What we need to see
We would support whichever package credibly delivers:

1. **material, durable cost reduction**
2. **real capacity adjustment**
3. **restored return-on-sales trajectory**
4. **deal certainty**
5. **limited disruption to operations**

That could include plant closures if unavoidable, but it could also include other structural measures if they truly achieve the same economic effect. The label matters less than the numbers and durability.

### Our limit
We will not back a settlement that is merely cosmetic, politically comfortable, or designed to preserve every legacy arrangement. If the final outcome leaves Volkswagen still structurally uncompetitive, investors will discount the equity accordingly, and capital will go elsewhere.

### Message to all parties
- **To management:** bring a plan that is financially credible and executable, not just forceful.
- **To labour:** if you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must be large enough and durable enough to match the economics.
- **To owners and the state:** legitimacy matters, but not at the expense of long-term value destruction.

In short: we support **whatever genuinely restores competitiveness**. We will not support an expensive compromise that merely postpones the problem.
