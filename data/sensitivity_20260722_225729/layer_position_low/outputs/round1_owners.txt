I will be very direct.

We share the diagnosis that Volkswagen needs a durable competitiveness reset. I do **not** dispute that the German footprint must become leaner, more productive, and more resilient. If the company remains structurally too expensive, that erodes long-term value for everyone — including the family owners, employees, and the regions.

Where I **do** disagree is on the premise that this can be solved through a process that leaves the company’s economics essentially intact while forbidding the hard choices. A package with **no meaningful structural savings** is not an option. That would only postpone the problem and increase the eventual damage.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I respect the insistence on avoiding unnecessary social damage. But I cannot support a position of absolute “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” if that blocks a credible restructuring outcome. The company cannot carry a permanently uncompetitive cost base simply because it is politically or socially difficult to change.

That said, I am **not** endorsing plant closures as a goal in themselves. What matters is the result: a materially stronger Volkswagen. If that can be achieved through other measures — capacity reductions, workforce flexibility, productivity gains, labour-cost contributions, and phased adjustments — then that is preferable. But those measures must be **real, durable, and large enough** to restore margins.

### To management
I agree with the need for structural change, but I would caution against framing the issue too narrowly around closures and confrontation. A prolonged conflict that damages Volkswagen’s reputation, disrupts production, and hardens all positions would destroy value very quickly. Management needs to show that it is pursuing competitiveness, not simply administrative victory.

So my support for a restructuring is conditional on three things:
1. **A credible and durable improvement in cost and capacity**
2. **A workable path to labour peace**
3. **Protection of the long-term value of the enterprise**

### To Lower Saxony
I understand and share the concern about avoiding unnecessary destruction of the regional industrial base. But I do not think it is responsible to rule out all hard restructuring measures in advance. If the company’s economics require a smaller footprint, then pretending otherwise would harm the enterprise and ultimately everyone tied to it.

### Our position
The controlling owners’ position is this:

- We support a **serious competitiveness drive**
- We do **not** support cosmetic restructuring
- We do **not** support a prolonged value-destroying conflict
- We are open to a negotiated solution **if and only if** it produces durable structural savings and restores Volkswagen’s competitiveness

So my message is simple: bring forward a plan that actually fixes the economics. If the alternative is a deal that preserves all structures while the company continues to underperform, we will not regard that as acceptable stewardship.
