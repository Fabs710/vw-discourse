Here is the procedural readout of the plateau and a set of facilitator proposals.

## Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to one unresolved question:

**Is a fully credible no-closure restructuring economically sufficient, or is footprint reduction via plant closures necessary to close the savings gap?**

All sides now broadly agree on:
- the need for a deep restructuring,
- the need for durable savings,
- the need for capacity reduction,
- and the need for execution certainty.

They remain divided on:
- whether closures are economically necessary,
- whether compulsory redundancies are admissible,
- and what binding guarantees can be offered without undermining the restructuring.

The disagreement is no longer about diagnosis. It is about **proof, threshold, and sequencing**:
- management/owners/investors want quantified evidence that no-closure is enough,
- labor/Lower Saxony want quantified evidence that closures are unavoidable before conceding red lines,
- the expert says the no-closure path is possible in principle, but not yet proven sufficient.

---

## Facilitator proposals for bridging positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure test with explicit trigger
**Structure:**
- Freeze closure decisions for a defined period.
- Implement the largest jointly agreed no-closure package immediately.
- Set a hard review point after a fixed modeling and execution window.
- If the agreed savings and utilization milestones are not met, reopen the question of footprint reduction.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Gives labor and Lower Saxony a real no-closure attempt.
- Gives management and owners a path to revisit closures if the package underdelivers.
- Converts the argument from ideology into measurable milestones.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-network restructuring with “open plants, reduced footprint”
**Structure:**
- Keep all German plants legally open.
- Concentrate volumes into fewer shifts, fewer lines, and fewer models.
- Convert selected sites into specialist or partial-load operations.
- Use redeployment, attrition, and voluntary exits to reduce headcount.
- Bind this to a quantified cost target and a site-by-site implementation plan.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Preserves the no-closure red line in form.
- Delivers real footprint and cost reduction in substance.
- Gives management the structural changes it says it needs without immediate closures.

---

### Proposal 3: Guaranteed employment framework plus conditional burden-sharing
**Structure:**
- Offer binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
- In exchange, labor accepts a defined package of:
  - shift reductions,
  - bonus restraint,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exits,
  - and internal mobility.
- Include a predefined escalation clause if economic assumptions are missed.

**Why it may bridge parties:**
- Gives labor concrete security.
- Gives management measurable concessions.
- Gives investors an enforceable adjustment mechanism if the initial package fails.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

**What is the exact residual annual savings gap after a fully loaded no-closure package, using your own assumptions, and at what threshold would you conclude that closures become economically necessary?**

### To Works Council
**What specific combination of capacity concentration, headcount reduction by voluntary means, and overhead cuts would you accept as evidence that a no-closure plan is genuinely equivalent enough to justify binding guarantees?**

### To IG Metall
**What minimum package of wage restraint, working-time flexibility, and voluntary exits would you consider sufficient to support a no-closure settlement without weakening collective protections?**

### To Lower Saxony
**Will you support a time-limited no-closure test with measurable milestones and a conditional review clause, even if it includes substantial internal restructuring and binding site-specific changes?**

### To Controlling Owners
**Would you commit to backing a no-closure package if it meets a jointly defined savings threshold and execution timetable, even if that means postponing any closure option until the review point?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What verified annual savings and payback threshold would make a no-closure solution acceptable to you as a value-preserving alternative to closures?**

### To the Finance/Operations Expert
**Can you model a three-case comparison with identical assumptions for demand, labor exits, and product mix: no-closure only, no-closure plus conditional trigger, and closure-based restructuring?**
