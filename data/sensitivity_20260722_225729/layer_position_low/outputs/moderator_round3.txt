### Core impasse
The impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed. All sides now accept that it is.

The impasse is over **the mechanism of fixed-cost reduction**:

- **Labor, Lower Saxony:** insist that restructuring must happen **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, and with binding job/site guarantees.
- **Management / owners / investors:** insist that the company needs a **durable savings package of sufficient scale**, and they are not convinced a no-closure plan yet closes the gap.

So the dispute has narrowed to a single question:
**Can Volkswagen achieve the required savings through internal restructuring alone, or are closures needed to reach the target with acceptable certainty?**

A second, related impasse is about **proof standard**:
- Labor and Lower Saxony say management has not quantified why closures are unavoidable.
- Management and capital say the no-closure side has not quantified how it reaches equivalent savings.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete bridge positions that could narrow the gap. These are proposals for discussion, not endorsements.

#### 1) Phased no-closure package with trigger-based review
**Structure**
- All German plants remain open initially.
- A hard, audited restructuring package is implemented immediately:
  - shift reductions
  - voluntary exits / attrition
  - capacity concentration
  - management overhead cuts
  - wage/bonus restraint
  - redeployment and training
- A jointly agreed review point is set after 12–18 months.
- Only if independently verified savings fall materially short would further structural measures be reopened for negotiation.

**Why it may bridge**
- Gives labor the non-closure starting point it wants.
- Gives management a performance test and an escalation mechanism.

---

#### 2) No closures, but site specialization and guaranteed volume transfer
**Structure**
- All sites remain open, but each site is assigned a narrower role.
- Non-core production is moved to fewer plants inside the existing network.
- Binding product/volume allocation rules are agreed through 2030.
- In exchange, labor accepts deeper working-time flexibility and stronger productivity commitments.

**Why it may bridge**
- Preserves the plant network politically.
- Delivers real capacity reduction and fixed-cost concentration, which management and capital say they need.

---

#### 3) Protected sites plus stronger sacrifice from all other parties
**Structure**
- No plant closures and no compulsory redundancies.
- In return, the workforce accepts:
  - meaningful but time-limited wage/bonus restraint
  - more flexible working-time arrangements
  - accelerated voluntary exits
- Management accepts:
  - deeper overhead cuts
  - executive compensation restraint
  - stricter project and investment discipline
- Owners accept:
  - a defined capital support / patience period
  - no pressure for immediate closure decisions during the implementation window

**Why it may bridge**
- Converts the debate from “who loses?” to “who contributes how much?”
- May satisfy the demand for shared burden if the savings target is met.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact annual savings gap remains after a fully loaded no-closure package, and which specific cost items are still not closed after you include shift reductions, voluntary exits, overhead cuts, and working-time measures?**

#### To the Works Council
**What binding internal restructuring measures, quantified by savings and headcount effect, are you prepared to accept so that “no closures” is matched by a credible economic plan rather than only a red line?**

#### To IG Metall
**What level of durable employee contribution—wages, bonuses, flexibility, and voluntary exits—are you prepared to recommend to your members if it is formally tied to legally binding job and site guarantees?**

#### To Lower Saxony
**Will you support a monitored no-closure restructuring plan if it includes binding volume concentration, site specialization, and a mid-course verification clause, or is your opposition absolute regardless of the quantified outcome?**

#### To the Controlling Owners
**What minimum financial return or risk threshold would make you accept a no-closure restructuring as sufficient, even if it takes longer and requires stricter monitoring than a closure-based plan?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**Would you support a restructuring that preserves all German sites if independent verification shows it reaches the same run-rate savings and margin recovery as a closure-based alternative?**

#### To the Expert
**What additional plant-level data would most decisively distinguish between “no-closure is enough” and “closures are required,” and how quickly could that be modeled if the parties supplied it?**
