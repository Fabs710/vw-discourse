I will be equally direct in this round: **the burden of proof still sits with management and capital, and it has not been met.**

## Where I agree
I agree with everyone on one point: Volkswagen needs a **real, durable restructuring**. No one here can honestly claim that a cosmetic programme will solve the competitiveness problem.

I also agree that:
- the company must become more productive,
- capacity must be adjusted to market reality,
- overhead must come down,
- and the outcome must be sustainable through 2030.

That is not the dispute.

## Where I disagree
What I reject is the leap from “we need a deep restructuring” to “therefore we must close German plants or threaten compulsory redundancies.”

That remains **unproven**.

The expert has already made the key point: a **no-closure path is possible in principle** if it is large enough, permanent enough, and disciplined enough. That means closures are not a necessity established by economics. They are a management preference that has not been justified.

So let me be absolutely clear:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework**
- **No weakening of co-determination**
- **No fait accompli**

Those are not bargaining chips for me. They are the limits of any acceptable outcome.

## To management
You keep saying the no-closure package “does not yet add up.” Fine. Then show the arithmetic in a way that is credible and complete.

What I still have not seen is:
- the exact annual savings gap after a fully loaded no-closure package,
- the plant-by-plant impact of internal capacity concentration,
- the true savings from overhead cuts, management cuts, and model simplification,
- and the reason these measures cannot close the gap without destroying sites.

Right now, you are asking the workforce to accept permanent insecurity before you have proven that insecurity is unavoidable. That is not serious restructuring; that is pressure politics.

And let’s not pretend trust is neutral here. Management has already damaged it by moving against the historic guarantees first. You do not get to break trust and then demand that workers provide the missing trust as an input to your plan.

## To the controlling owners and investors
I hear your demand for value preservation. But a closure-first mentality is not automatically value-preserving.

A plant closure may look tidy in a spreadsheet, but it can also bring:
- years of conflict,
- production disruption,
- knowledge loss,
- damage to morale,
- damage to the brand in Germany,
- and a long-term weakening of the social compact that makes this company workable.

If you truly care about long-term value, then you should support the route that preserves industrial strength and achieves savings: **a deep no-closure restructuring with binding guarantees**.

You keep saying closures are one option. Correct. Then stop talking as if they are already the conclusion.

## To Lower Saxony
Your position is only meaningful if it is operational, not rhetorical. I welcome your statement against closures and compulsory redundancies, but we need more than political sympathy.

Use your blocking power to ensure that no one forces a closure agenda through the back door. Support a real no-closure restructuring only if it includes:
- binding employment protection,
- binding site guarantees,
- real investment commitments,
- and volume concentration **inside the existing network**.

Anything less would mean the state is asking workers to absorb risk while others preserve optionality.

## To IG Metall
I agree that any burden must be shared. But I must be blunt: “shared sacrifice” cannot become a code word for hollowing out plant security.

I will not support:
- layoffs by attrition without safeguards,
- hidden closures through underutilization,
- or flexibility language that slowly dismantles sites while pretending the red lines are intact.

If the union wants a deal, it must be a **real protection package**, not a disguised downsizing plan.

## On the expert’s numbers
The expert’s scenario ranges do not weaken our position. They show something very important: **a no-closure solution is plausible**.

That matters because it destroys the argument that closures are the only realistic route.

The real question is not whether internal restructuring is easy. It is not. The real question is whether management, owners, and investors are willing to accept a serious internal reset that does **not** sacrifice German plants.

Our answer is yes. Yours so far is: only if workers first surrender the very protections that make a negotiated solution possible. That is unacceptable.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss a package built around:
- shift reductions
- capacity concentration within the existing German network
- voluntary exits
- attrition
- training and redeployment
- working-time flexibility
- limited and fair bonus restraint
- productivity improvements
- management overhead cuts
- investment commitments for future products

But only on these conditions:
1. **all German plants remain open**
2. **no compulsory redundancies**
3. **binding job guarantees through 2030**
4. **binding site guarantees through 2030**
5. **real co-determination in implementation**
6. **fair burden-sharing from management and owners**

## What we will not accept
We will not accept:
- any plant closure “just in case”
- any forced layoff “if needed”
- any package that leaves site security vague
- any erosion of co-determination
- any claim that the workforce must prove loyalty by accepting structural damage

## My challenge back to management
If closures are supposedly unavoidable, then answer this plainly:

**What exactly have you exhausted already, in quantified terms, that makes a no-closure path fail?**

Not slogans. Not warnings. Numbers.

If you cannot answer that, then you are not proving necessity. You are escalating leverage.

## Bottom line
We are not moving off our core position.

Volkswagen needs a deep reset, yes. But that reset must happen **without closing German plants and without compulsory redundancies**.

If management and capital want a real agreement, bring:
- the quantified no-closure savings gap,
- the site-by-site internal restructuring plan,
- and the binding guarantees that make the package credible.

Otherwise, expect continued resistance from the works council and from the workforce we represent.
