Let me be equally clear from management’s side.

I hear the red lines you are drawing: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful change to the cost base. But that is not a credible answer to the situation Volkswagen is in.

We are facing a structural competitiveness problem, not a temporary downturn. German factory costs are too high, our European market is shrinking, and we are under pressure from more efficient global competitors. If we simply preserve every site and every legacy arrangement, we will not restore the brand’s return-on-sales target, and we will not free up the funds needed for the product and technology investment we need through 2030.

So I will be direct:

- **I agree** that the company needs a durable, socially responsible solution.
- **I disagree** that preserving all current structures is compatible with that goal.
- **I disagree** that management and ownership can carry the full burden while labour keeps all protections intact.

A package based only on capacity tweaks, work-time changes, and temporary concessions is not enough. We need **structural and lasting** cost reduction. That includes a genuine labour-cost contribution and a real reduction in overcapacity. Whether that ultimately requires plant closures or can be achieved through other structural measures is still open — but the savings must be real, durable, and large enough to change the economics.

I also need to be frank about constraints:

- We cannot accept a cosmetic deal that postpones the problem.
- We cannot accept a package that leaves us uncompetitive in Germany.
- We cannot ask investors and owners to fund a business model that still carries excess cost and excess capacity.

That said, I am not demanding closures for their own sake. What I am demanding is the result: **a materially smaller, lower-cost, more resilient German footprint**. If the employee side can put forward a proposal that delivers that outcome credibly — including meaningful labour-cost relief and a real reduction in capacity — then I am prepared to discuss the route to it. But if the answer is simply “no closures, no redundancies, no deep change,” then we are not negotiating a solution; we are delaying a crisis.

So my counter-position is this:
- We need **binding structural savings**
- We need a **substantial workforce contribution**
- We need **capacity reduction**
- We need a deal that restores competitiveness, not one that preserves the status quo

If you want to avoid the hard route, then bring a plan that genuinely replaces it. Otherwise, management will continue to pursue the restructuring it believes is necessary.
