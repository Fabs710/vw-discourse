We are aligned with management on the core diagnosis: **Volkswagen’s cost base and capacity are too high for the market reality, and that is now showing up in margins and valuation pressure**. From a capital-market perspective, that is the central issue. If it is not fixed in a durable way, the equity remains impaired.

That said, we are **not** automatically backing plant closures as a principle. What we care about is **credible, structural, and fast enough improvement in risk-adjusted returns**. Closures may be one route; if there is a negotiated alternative that achieves equivalent savings with high certainty, that is acceptable. But it must be real, not cosmetic.

### To management
Your position is directionally right, but you need to be careful on execution.

We agree that:
- temporary measures alone are insufficient;
- natural attrition alone is too slow;
- a solution must permanently lower the cost base;
- investor confidence depends on visible structural action.

Where we are less comfortable is if the process becomes prolonged, politically blocked, and operationally destructive. A long strike or endless uncertainty would destroy near-term cash generation and delay recovery. From our standpoint, **deal certainty matters**. If the choice is between a hard restructuring package and months of damaging confrontation, we will prefer the package that gets implemented.

What we need from management is:
- quantified savings;
- a credible implementation timeline;
- clear evidence that the measures will restore competitive margins;
- and no vague language about “future reviews” instead of action.

### To the Works Council and IG Metall
We understand your red lines, but we do **not** share the view that no plant closure can ever be considered, regardless of economics. If the footprint is structurally misaligned, then refusing to address that only postpones the value destruction.

We also do **not** accept that the workforce should be insulated from the adjustment entirely. If margins are to recover, there must be a meaningful labour contribution. That can include:
- working-time changes,
- wage moderation or concessions,
- bonus restraint,
- redeployment,
- and other durable cost measures.

What we will not support is a package that protects all legacy arrangements while expecting shareholders to absorb the downside. That is not a competitive turnaround.

### To Lower Saxony
We respect the governance reality, but we do not think governance constraints should become a veto over economic necessity. If the alternative is preserving every site at all costs while the brand remains uncompetitive, then the company’s value erodes further. That is not in anyone’s interest, including long-term regional interest.

### To the controlling owners
We agree that a purely aggressive or purely political solution is not enough. But from our perspective, the test is simpler: **does the package restore returns and de-risk the earnings base?** If yes, capital will respond positively. If not, the share price will continue to reflect the structural weakness.

### Our position
We will back:
- a restructuring with **real, durable cost reduction**;
- a package that is **credible enough to restore competitive margins**;
- and a settlement that avoids drawn-out value-destructive conflict.

We will **not** back:
- cosmetic compromise;
- indefinite delay;
- or a settlement that preserves an uncompetitive cost base.

So our message is straightforward: **no half measures**. Bring a solution that materially improves margins and reduces overcapacity, or the capital markets will continue to discount the business accordingly.
