## To management
I agree with your central diagnosis: Volkswagen needs a **durable structural reset**, not another temporary patch. I also agree that the German cost base must come down and that the company cannot remain competitive if it simply preserves the current structure and hopes for recovery.

Where I disagree is on the implication that the only credible route is to keep German plant closures and compulsory redundancies on the table as the default leverage.

That is too blunt, and it is not yet proven.

If you want the support of the controlling owners, you need to show a **plant-by-plant business case** that demonstrates:
- the true fixed-cost gap,
- the utilization problem at each site,
- the capex needed to keep each site competitive,
- and the permanent savings from each alternative.

Until then, I will not treat closures as the necessary starting point. They are one possible instrument, not the conclusion.

At the same time, I am not giving the workforce a blank cheque. If the non-closure package does not deliver **equivalent, verifiable, durable savings**, then I will not support a weak compromise that merely delays the crisis.

## To the Works Council
I agree with you on one important point: a cosmetic deal would be worthless. I also agree that a conflict-driven restructuring would damage trust, output, and long-term value.

But I do **not** agree with your position that “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful changes to legacy protections” can simply be declared and then treated as a complete answer.

That is not a solution unless it can be shown — with numbers — that the same structural savings are achieved another way.

So I support your demand for burden-sharing, management contribution, and social stability. I do **not** support turning those demands into a veto against any hard restructuring. If your alternative is real, show it.

## To IG Metall
I agree that the burden must be shared and that the workforce should not be asked to carry the adjustment alone. I also agree that temporary measures are insufficient by themselves.

But I disagree with any approach that frames closures as illegitimate in principle while offering only temporary flexibility and voluntary exits. That will not close the structural gap.

If you want a no-closure settlement, then it must include:
- real capacity reduction,
- measurable productivity gains,
- durable labour-cost contribution,
- management restraint,
- and implementation milestones that are enforceable.

If those conditions are not met, then your position is not an alternative restructuring; it is resistance to restructuring.

## To Lower Saxony
I respect your governance role and your concern for the regional base. I also agree that Volkswagen is not just a financial asset; it is an industrial anchor.

But I disagree with the idea that the state’s blocking position can be used to freeze every German site regardless of economics. That would protect the appearance of stability while eroding the company’s competitive position over time.

Your useful contribution is not simply saying “no closures.” It is helping produce a **credible non-closure restructuring** that actually works. If you can help secure that, good. If not, then you are not solving the problem — you are postponing it.

## To the institutional investors
You are right to insist on measurable, permanent savings and a binding timetable. You are also right that Volkswagen cannot afford open-ended drift.

Where I disagree is any suggestion that the answer must be closure-first simply because it is faster on paper. If the closure route triggers industrial conflict, legal friction, political blockage, and implementation delay, then the supposed speed advantage disappears.

I will support the path that restores margins and certainty. That may be a negotiated non-closure package — but only if it is economically equivalent and enforceable. If not, then we should not pretend it is enough.

## My position
My position remains unchanged:

- **Volkswagen needs durable restructuring.**
- **Temporary measures alone are not enough.**
- **A non-closure solution is acceptable only if it delivers equivalent, verifiable, long-term savings and capacity reduction.**
- **I will not support a cosmetic compromise that preserves an uncompetitive structure.**
- **I will not support value-destructive instability either.**

- closure,
- shift and line consolidation,
- redeployment,
- working-time changes,
- voluntary exits,

I am open to a non-closure settlement.  
I am **not** open to a weak one.

If the employee side and Lower Saxony can help produce a package that truly matches the economics, I will back it through governance. If not, then closures cannot be ruled out simply because they are unpopular. The company’s long-term value comes first.
