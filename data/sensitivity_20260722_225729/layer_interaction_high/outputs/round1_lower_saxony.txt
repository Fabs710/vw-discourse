I appreciate that management is at least acknowledging that this cannot be decided by fiat. But I need to be equally clear: **a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany, forced redundancies, or the erosion of co-determination-based safeguards will not find support from Lower Saxony.**

Let me respond directly to the points raised.

### Where I agree
I agree with management on one important point: **Volkswagen does need a durable solution**, not a cosmetic postponement. The company must restore competitiveness, protect future investment, and improve the brand’s margin structure. I also agree that temporary measures alone are not enough if they simply defer adjustment.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the implication that the only credible path is closing German plants or putting closure threats at the center of the process.

From our perspective, that is not just economically risky; it is socially and politically unacceptable. Volkswagen is not an ordinary footloose corporation. It is a central industrial anchor for entire regions, and the company’s strength has always rested on a social partnership model. Destroying that foundation would create long-term damage far beyond the immediate savings line.

I also reject the idea that job security and site protection are somehow a luxury that must disappear before competitiveness can return. That is too narrow a view. The question is not whether there must be change. The question is **who pays, how fairly, and with what long-term consequences**.

### What must be the basis of any agreement
If management wants Lower Saxony to engage constructively, then the package has to meet three tests:

1. **No German plant closures**
   - Not as a slogan, but as a real constraint.
   - We will not support a path that sacrifices regional industrial capacity to fix a strategic problem that should be solved through restructuring, product strategy, and cost-sharing.

2. **No compulsory redundancies**
   - Workforce reductions may be addressed through natural attrition, internal transfers, qualifications, part-time pathways, and other negotiated instruments.
   - But forced layoffs would cross a line.

3. **A fair burden-sharing solution**
   - If employees are asked for labor-cost contributions, then owners and management must contribute credibly as well.
   - That means restraint on dividends, executive compensation discipline, and a restructuring plan that demonstrates management responsibility rather than shifting the burden downward.

### On structural savings
I am not dismissing the need for savings. But I do not accept that “equivalent structural savings” automatically means closures.

There are other ways to reduce costs sustainably:
- rationalizing production through internal allocation and capacity balancing,
- adjusting working time through negotiated instruments,
- stronger use of qualification and redeployment,
- targeted productivity improvements,
- supply-chain and procurement efficiency,
- management overhead reduction,
- and a serious review of investment priorities.

If management claims these are insufficient, then it needs to prove that claim with transparent data, not simply assert it.

### On the role of the state and governance
Lower Saxony is not a passive capital holder. We have a duty to the workforce, to the regions, and to the long-term stability of Volkswagen itself. We also hold a blocking minority under the VW Law for a reason: to prevent short-term decisions that would do lasting damage.

So let me say plainly: **we will not be sidelined**. Any major restructuring must be negotiated in a way that respects the governance structure and the social compact that has sustained Volkswagen for decades.

### My position
I am prepared to support a serious restructuring **if it preserves German sites, protects employment as far as possible, and restores competitiveness through negotiated adjustment rather than destruction**.

What I am **not** prepared to support:
- plant closures in Germany,
- compulsory redundancies,
- a unilateral cost-cutting regime imposed on workers,
- or any outcome that weakens the state’s co-determination and veto role.

If management wants a viable path forward, bring forward a plan that:
- keeps plants open,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- contains real but fair cost contributions,
- and secures investment in the future.

That is the only basis on which I will engage constructively.
