## Core impasse

The deadlock is centered on **sequencing and irreversibility**:

- **Employee side / Lower Saxony** want a **binding no-closure / no-forced-redundancy commitment first**, with any audit used only to design a non-closure restructuring path.
- **Management / owners / investors** want **plant-by-plant evidence first**, with closures and redundancies kept available if the numbers justify them.
- The dispute is therefore not just about economics, but about **whether closure remains a live option during the fact-finding phase**.

A second layer of impasse is that both sides demand **proof from the other side before conceding anything themselves**:
- Management says: “Show a credible no-closure model.”
- Labour side says: “Show that closures are necessary, not merely available.”

So the negotiation is stuck on **who moves first, and what is pre-committed before the audit**.

---

## Facilitation proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Two-step package with interim protection
A possible bridge is:

1. **Immediate interim commitment**
   - no German plant closures during the audit phase,
   - no compulsory redundancies during the audit phase,
   - no unilateral erosion of co-determination or job-security rules.

2. **Independent plant-by-plant audit**
   - mandated to compare:
     - non-closure restructuring,
     - capacity reduction without closure,
     - and closure scenarios.

3. **Predefined decision rule**
   - if audited non-closure measures can close the savings gap, they are adopted;
   - if they cannot, parties reconvene under a predefined escalation mechanism.

This preserves worker protection during verification while still giving management a path to an evidence-based decision.

---

### Proposal 2: Site-differentiated settlement
Another compromise is to avoid an all-or-nothing ruling on every plant:

- **Tier A sites:** binding protection and no closure path, if audit shows structural viability.
- **Tier B sites:** consolidation, shift reductions, product reallocation, and voluntary exits.
- **Tier C sites:** deeper review with strict milestones, where only if non-closure measures fail would next steps be discussed.

This would let the parties distinguish between:
- sites that should clearly stay open,
- sites that need major restructuring,
- and sites where the economics remain genuinely contested.

---

### Proposal 3: No closure now, but a contingent review framework
A third bridge is:

- **No closure decisions now**
- **Binding restructuring targets now**
- **Independent audit within a fixed timetable**
- **Pre-agreed fallback governance mechanism** if targets are missed

To make this acceptable, the fallback must not automatically mean closure; it could instead mean:
- additional capacity reduction,
- deeper shift rationalization,
- stronger labor contributions,
- management and owner contributions,
- and only then a further review.

This reduces the sense of a deferred threat while still leaving the company a governance path if the savings gap remains.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Management
**What specific recurring savings, by plant and by measure, would convince you that a no-closure package is economically equivalent to closure-based restructuring?**

### To the Works Council
**What binding capacity or productivity measures are you prepared to accept, site by site, if they are necessary to make the no-closure commitment financially credible?**

### To IG Metall
**What labor contribution can you commit to in quantified, durable terms so that a no-closure settlement is not just protective, but economically enforceable?**

### To Lower Saxony
**Would you support a time-limited audit phase with interim no-closure protection, if it is paired with a binding obligation to act on the audit results?**

### To the Controlling Owners
**Are you prepared to remove closures as an immediate bargaining lever during the audit, if the process guarantees a credible path to structural savings?**

### To Institutional Investors
**What threshold of verified permanent savings and capacity reduction would you regard as sufficient to prefer a non-closure settlement over a closure-based one?**

### To the Independent Expert / Auditor
**What minimum dataset and timeline would you require to produce a plant-by-plant recommendation that both sides can treat as decision-grade?**
