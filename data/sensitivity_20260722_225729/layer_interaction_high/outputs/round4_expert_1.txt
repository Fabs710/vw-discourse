From an industrial restructuring perspective, the discussion has now reached a fairly typical but serious deadlock pattern:

## What is clear
- The company needs a **durable structural reset**, not temporary relief.
- There is broad agreement that **overcapacity and cost must come down**.
- The real dispute is no longer whether change is needed, but **which instruments are legitimate and sufficient**.

## What the current positions imply
### Management / owners / investors
Their position is structurally coherent if the objective is:
- permanent fixed-cost reduction,
- lower break-even volume,
- and credible restoration of margins by 2030.

However, their case is still **incomplete** in one key respect:  
they keep closures and compulsory redundancies available as leverage, but they have not yet demonstrated, site by site, that non-closure alternatives cannot achieve the same savings.

### Works council / IG Metall / Lower Saxony
Their position is also internally coherent if the objective is:
- preserve sites,
- avoid forced redundancies,
- prevent escalation,
- and protect co-determination legitimacy.

But their position also has a weakness:  
saying “no closures” is not yet the same as proving a **financeable restructuring model** that closes the competitiveness gap.

## Industrial restructuring reality
In a case like this, there are only three realistic classes of outcome:

1. **Closure-led restructuring**  
   Fastest route to deep footprint reduction, but highest conflict, highest political friction, and highest execution risk.

2. **Non-closure restructuring with major capacity reduction**  
   Often the best compromise if the plants are not all equally weak. This can include:
   - shift cuts,
   - line consolidation,
   - product reallocation,
   - voluntary exits,
   - redeployment,
   - working-time changes,
   - wage or benefit concessions,
   - overhead and management reductions.

3. **Mixed-site solution**  
   Usually the most plausible outcome in a co-determined system:
   - some sites get binding protection,
   - some sites get scaled-down specialization,
   - some sites face deeper rationalization,
   - all under a verified savings framework.

## What would make a non-closure deal credible
A non-closure package is only credible if it has all of the following:
- **plant-by-plant economics**
- **quantified recurring savings**
- **a defined reduction in capacity**
- **durable labour-cost contribution**
- **management and overhead cuts**
- **binding implementation milestones**
- **a credible enforcement mechanism**

Without those, “no closures” is mostly a political stance, not a restructuring plan.

## What I would advise as a restructuring framework
If the parties want a serious settlement, the most viable sequence is:

1. **Independent site-by-site audit**
   - utilization
   - unit cost
   - capex requirements
   - product pipeline
   - fixed-cost burden
   - achievable savings by option

2. **Classify sites**
   - structurally viable
   - viable only with major consolidation
   - borderline / deferred decision

3. **Negotiate a mixed package**
   - voluntary exits only
   - no immediate forced redundancies
   - capacity reduction where needed
   - labor flexibility and contribution
   - management and owner contribution
   - binding timetable

4. **Set hard review triggers**
   - not as a threat mechanism,
   - but as a governance mechanism tied to verified underperformance.

## Main risk
The biggest risk is that the parties settle on a formula that sounds balanced but is economically weak:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- but also no real capacity reduction or cost reset.

That would likely postpone the problem and make the eventual adjustment more painful.

## Bottom line
From a restructuring standpoint, the most realistic landing zone is **not** “all closures” or “no change.”  
It is a **site-differentiated package** with:
- verified savings,
- real capacity rationalization,
- limited workforce pain where unavoidable,
- and strong guarantees where sites can truly be stabilized.
