I hear the red lines from the Works Council and IG Metall, but I need to be equally direct: **the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable**. If we simply preserve every site, every guarantee, and every existing cost commitment, then we will not restore the brand’s competitiveness, we will not hit our return-on-sales target, and we will not free the funds needed for the next product cycle and technology investment toward 2030.

So let me separate what is negotiable from what is not:

- **Negotiable:** the exact mix of measures — closures, capacity reductions, working-time changes, redeployment, qualification, labour-cost contributions.
- **Not negotiable:** the need for a **durable, structural reduction in cost and capacity**.

That means I do **not** accept a cosmetic package that delays the problem. Natural attrition alone will be too slow. Temporary working-time reductions alone will not close the gap. A short-term savings plan with a future promise to “review again” is not enough. We need a solution that materially lowers the cost base on a permanent footing.

On the point of plant closures: yes, they are on the table because management has concluded that the German footprint is too large for the market we now face. I am not using that language as theatre; I am saying it because we need real options, not taboos. But I also recognise the governance reality: we cannot simply impose a closure unilaterally. That is precisely why I am putting the case now for a settlement that achieves the same economic result, if possible, through a negotiated package.

What would move us:
- a **credible labour-cost contribution** that is meaningful, not symbolic;
- **structural capacity reduction**, not just deferred pain;
- **durable site and employment commitments where they are economically supportable**, but not blanket guarantees that freeze the structure unchanged;
- a package that clearly improves medium-term margins and restores investor confidence.

What will not move us:
- rejecting closures while also rejecting any real capacity cut;
- insisting on full employment security while expecting management to solve the cost problem elsewhere;
- asking for investment promises without accepting the structural changes needed to finance them.

So my answer is: **I am willing to negotiate an alternative to closures if — and only if — it delivers equivalent structural savings and a lasting reduction in overcapacity and labour cost.** If the response is simply “no closures, no layoffs, no guarantees change,” then there is no viable solution on the table, only a delay of the crisis.
