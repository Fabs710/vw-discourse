### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that Volkswagen needs a **durable restructuring**, not just a temporary fix.
- There is broad agreement that **temporary measures alone are insufficient**.
- Several parties accept that some combination of **capacity reduction, working-time flexibility, redeployment, qualification, productivity improvement, and labour-cost contribution** will likely be part of any package.
- There is also shared recognition that **industrial peace, investment credibility, and implementation certainty** matter.

### 2) Core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**:  
  Works Council, IG Metall, Lower Saxony, and to a large extent the controlling owners reject closures as the starting point; management and institutional investors insist closures remain on the table if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  Labour and Lower Saxony reject them outright; management and investors do not endorse them explicitly but want meaningful structural change.
- **Scope of job-security protections**:  
  Labour and Lower Saxony want existing protections preserved; management and investors say some legacy guarantees may need adjustment if structural savings are to be real.
- **How “equivalent structural savings” should be achieved**:  
  Management, owners, and investors say alternatives to closures must deliver permanent, measurable savings; labour and Lower Saxony say closures are not acceptable and that other measures have not yet been exhausted or proven insufficient.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **Jobs vs. competitiveness**: how much employment/site protection can be maintained while still achieving a durable cost reset.
- **Speed vs. social stability**: whether faster implementation requires harsher measures, and whether slower negotiated change would be acceptable.
- **Management savings vs. labour contribution**: how burden-sharing will be distributed across workers, executives, owners, and capital.
- **Guarantees vs. flexibility**: what binding commitments can be given without preventing necessary future adjustment.
- **Alternative savings measures vs. closures**: whether internal redeployment, working-time changes, productivity gains, and overhead reduction can realistically substitute for plant rationalisation.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **specific package design**.
- Ask each side to name:
  1. the **minimum conditions** under which it would accept a deal;
  2. the **specific measures** it is prepared to offer;
  3. the **quantified trade-offs** it considers acceptable.
- Focus the discussion on **verifiable savings, implementation timing, and binding guarantees**, rather than repeating red lines alone.
