Management keeps talking about “equivalent structural savings,” but you still have not answered the central question: **equivalent for whom, and at what social cost?** If the answer is plant closures and forced layoffs, then we are not talking about an equivalent solution — we are talking about shifting the crisis onto the workforce.

Let me respond point by point.

### To management
You say natural attrition is too slow and temporary working-time reductions are insufficient. Fine. But that does **not** automatically justify closures or compulsory redundancies. It means you have to bring forward a **serious alternative package** that is more than a cost-cutting slogan.

What we can discuss:
- working-time adjustments,
- internal redeployment,
- qualification and retraining,
- temporary remuneration measures,
- productivity improvements,
- management and overhead contributions,
- fair restraint on dividends and executive pay,
- capacity adjustments through voluntary pathways only.

What we will **not** discuss as an acceptable starting point:
- plant closures in Germany,
- forced layoffs,
- breaking job-security commitments,
- “legacy guarantees” being treated as disposable while management mistakes are untouched.

If you want credibility, bring numbers. Show us:
- which sites are allegedly “unviable,”
- what savings each measure delivers,
- why those savings cannot be reached through internal adjustment,
- and what management, owners, and capital are contributing themselves.

Without that, “closure is on the table” is just pressure politics.

### To the institutional investors
You keep asking for speed and certainty, but certainty at the expense of social stability is not a sustainable strategy. A conflict-driven restructuring that detonates labour relations, triggers strikes, and destroys trust is not “de-risking” the business — it is creating a new risk.

You also say the workforce should not be insulated. We are not asking to be insulated. We are asking that the burden be shared fairly and that **people are not thrown out of the company** to satisfy a short-term margin narrative. There is a difference.

If you want investor confidence, support a package that:
- protects jobs,
- avoids closures,
- preserves industrial peace,
- and still produces measurable savings through negotiated instruments.

That is the route to sustainable value, not a headline-grabbing plant closure fight.

### To the controlling owners
You say you support a serious restructuring but not one that destroys the industrial base. Good — then stop entertaining solutions that do exactly that. A position that keeps “closures on the table” while claiming to defend long-term resilience is not credible.

If you want the company to remain healthy, you need to back:
- binding site guarantees,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- real owner contribution,
- and a strategic plan that improves competitiveness without breaking the social compact.

### To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your clear rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But that has to be more than political language. It must translate into a governance line in the supervisory board: **no majority for any proposal that closes a German plant or introduces compulsory layoffs**.

If management cannot accept that, then they are effectively asking the workforce and the state to endorse a fait accompli. That will not happen.

### Our minimum conditions
If there is to be any discussion of a deal, the minimum conditions are:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding employment and site guarantees through 2030**
4. **Preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**
5. **Real burden-sharing from management, owners, and capital**
6. **A jointly negotiated restructuring plan, not unilateral announcements**

### What we are prepared to offer
We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exit options only,
- temporary labour-cost contributions,
- productivity measures agreed with the workforce,
- and a disciplined adjustment process that protects all sites.

### What we reject outright
- closure by stealth or by “economic necessity” rhetoric,
- compulsory redundancies,
- making workers pay for management’s strategic errors,
- stripping guarantees first and calling that reform.

So my message is unchanged and, after round one, even more pointed: **if your restructuring requires closing a German plant or forcing people out, then it is not a negotiable solution.** Bring an alternative that respects the workforce and the company’s long-term obligations, or expect full resistance.
