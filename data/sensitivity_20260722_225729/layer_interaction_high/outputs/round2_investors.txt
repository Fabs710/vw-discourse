We are hearing the right diagnosis, but not yet a fully credible solution.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that Volkswagen needs a **durable structural reset**. Temporary measures, slogans about social partnership, or a “review later” approach will not restore margins.
- **The Works Council and IG Metall are right** that a purely cosmetic package would be worthless and that prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- **Lower Saxony and the controlling owners are right** that Volkswagen should not be pushed into a destructive spiral that weakens the industrial base and investor confidence.

### Where we disagree
Our disagreement is with any position that treats **job protection itself** as the end goal rather than a constraint to be balanced against competitiveness.

#### To the Works Council / IG Metall
Your no-closures, no-compulsory-redundancies, no-legacy-change line is understandable, but from a capital-market perspective it is **not sufficient unless it still produces the required savings**. If the result is that the cost base remains uncompetitive, then equity value stays impaired and the problem is merely deferred.

We do **not** need a settlement that feels fair; we need one that is:
- **durable**
- **quantified**
- **implementable**
- **fast enough to matter**
- and **credible enough to restore margins**

If you want no closures, then show us a package that actually replaces them with equivalent, permanent savings. Not “maybe over time,” not “if volumes recover,” but hard numbers and binding mechanisms.

#### To Lower Saxony
We respect the governance role, but a political veto cannot become a shield for structurally excess capacity. If you block closures and also block deeper labor-cost adjustment, then you are effectively asking shareholders to accept permanently impaired returns.

That is not acceptable.

#### To the controlling owners
We agree with your insistence on value discipline. But “non-closure alternative” cannot become code for a slow, politically convenient compromise. If the company needs footprint rationalization, then either:
- the alternative must deliver the same savings, or
- some closure/rationalization must remain available.

### What would move us
We would support a deal if it includes all of the following:

1. **Quantified permanent cost reduction**
   - not just temporary wage restraint
   - not just one-off savings
   - actual reduction in the fixed cost base

2. **A credible capacity cut**
   - through closures if necessary, or
   - through an alternative that is demonstrably equivalent

3. **Binding labour contribution**
   - wage moderation or concessions
   - bonus restraint
   - working-time flexibility
   - voluntary exit pathways
   - productivity commitments

4. **Management and owner discipline**
   - overhead reductions
   - executive restraint
   - capital allocation discipline
   - no pretending labour alone can fix the problem

5. **A binding implementation timetable**
   - milestones
   - enforcement
   - no open-ended “trust us” language

### What we will not back
We will not back:
- a package that protects every legacy arrangement while leaving margins weak;
- a “socially acceptable” compromise that is financially non-credible;
- indefinite negotiations that keep the share price and cash flow under pressure;
- or a conflict outcome that destroys value through strikes and uncertainty.

### On the current discussion
At present, management has made the stronger case that the problem is structural. Labour has made the stronger case that a blunt, closure-first approach carries high execution risk. But neither side has yet fully closed the gap.

So our position is simple:
- **No cosmetic settlement**
- **No prolonged value destruction**
- **Yes to a hard, measurable restructuring**
- **Yes to an alternative to closures only if it is truly equivalent**
- **Otherwise, do not rule closures out in advance**

### [SUMMON: Independent industrial restructuring advisor]
We would benefit from a neutral, plant-by-plant financial and operational assessment to test whether the non-closure package truly matches the savings of footprint rationalization. This should cover:
- site-level cost competitiveness,
- capacity utilization,
- labor-cost elasticity,
- and implementation timing.

That is the level of evidence required before capital can judge whether the proposed alternative is real or just politically convenient.

Bottom line: we are open-minded on the instrument, but not on the outcome. The outcome must be **competitive margins, durable cost reduction, and credible execution**. If the package does not deliver that, we will oppose it.
