As Works Council, I will be very clear from the start:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany. We will not accept compulsory redundancies. And we will not accept the breaking of long-standing job-security commitments as the price of a restructuring that management failed to prepare responsibly.

If Volkswagen needs change, then the burden cannot be placed first and foremost on the workforce. We are prepared to discuss alternatives that protect all German sites and secure employment to 2030, including temporary working-time adjustments, capacity reductions through natural attrition, internal redeployment, training and qualification, and a fair negotiated contribution from labour — but only under binding guarantees.

Our priorities are:

- keeping every German plant open;
- preventing forced layoffs;
- preserving co-determination and the existing job-security framework;
- securing long-term viability of the sites, not short-term accounting fixes;
- ensuring that any restructuring is shared fairly across owners, management, and workforce.

Management is asking workers to pay for strategic mistakes and cost overruns they did not create. That is not acceptable. If this process is to lead anywhere, it must start with binding commitments, not unilateral closure threats or a fait accompli.

So my position is simple: no closures, no compulsory layoffs, and no dismantling of core worker protections. If management wants a solution, it has to bring a plan that meets those red lines.
