We agree with the diagnosis far more than with the current bargaining posture.

Volkswagen needs a **durable, structural reset**. Temporary measures, vague promises, or a settlement that simply preserves the current cost base would be value-destructive. On that point, management is right.

But we are **not** persuaded by any side’s attempt to make “no closures” the main issue rather than the outcome. For capital markets, the only test is whether the package restores **competitive margins, lower break-even volumes, and implementation certainty**.

## To management
You are right that the company cannot remain competitive with a structurally uncompetitive German cost base. You are also right that natural attrition and temporary working-time measures alone are unlikely to be enough.

Where we disagree is your current tactic of keeping closures and compulsory redundancies on the table without yet demonstrating that the alternative package cannot close the gap. That weakens deal certainty and raises execution risk.

What we need from you is not more general language about “real structural reset,” but a **plant-by-plant case** showing:
- current fixed-cost position,
- utilization,
- required capex,
- expected volume outlook,
- and the recurring savings from each restructuring option.

If you can show that non-closure measures do not bridge the gap, then the market can accept harder measures. If you cannot, then closure threats are just leverage, and leverage without proof increases the strike risk premium.

## To the Works Council and IG Metall
We agree that a cosmetic deal is worthless. We also agree that prolonged conflict destroys value.

Where we disagree is your insistence that closures and legacy changes are off the table as a starting point. From our perspective, that stance is only acceptable if you can produce **equivalent, durable savings**. So far, that has not been demonstrated.

Saying “no closures” does not restore margins. It only becomes credible if the alternative package:
- permanently lowers fixed costs,
- reduces capacity in a measurable way,
- and is enforceable quickly enough to matter.

We are open to non-closure solutions, but not to a package that preserves every site, every guarantee, and every cost layer while expecting shareholders to absorb the consequence.

## To Lower Saxony
You are right to warn against a destructive conflict spiral. We share that concern.

But a political veto cannot become a shield for structurally excess capacity. If you block closures and also block meaningful cost adjustment, you are asking shareholders to finance permanent underperformance. That is not a sustainable governance stance.

We can support a non-closure route, but only if it is **verified, binding, and economically equivalent**. If it is merely a way to defer hard choices, it will not restore confidence.

## To the controlling owners
We agree with your insistence on value discipline and your rejection of a one-sided workforce sacrifice. That part is sound.

Where we want to be firmer is this: “not ideologically committed to closures” is not enough. What matters is whether the owners will support the **best value-preserving option**, even if it includes footprint rationalization.

A non-closure alternative is acceptable only if it delivers:
- durable cost reduction,
- real capacity adjustment,
- management and overhead restraint,
- and a credible timetable.

If it does not, then preserving every site becomes a value trap.

## What would move us
We would back a deal if it includes:
1. **Quantified permanent savings**
2. **A credible reduction in capacity**
3. **Durable labour contribution**
4. **Management and owner discipline**
5. **A binding implementation timetable with milestones**
6. **Independent verification**

## [SUMMON: Independent plant-by-plant industrial and financial auditor]
We strongly support the call for a neutral audit of each German site. That audit should verify:
- site-level unit cost,
- utilization,
- future product allocation,
- capex needs,
- and the savings under closure vs. non-closure measures.

This is essential. Without it, the discussion remains political positioning rather than financial decision-making.

## Bottom line
Our position is simple:
- We support a **serious restructuring**.
- We support a **non-closure solution only if it is truly equivalent** in permanent savings and capacity reduction.
- We will **not** support a cosmetic compromise that leaves margins impaired.
- We will also **not** support an open-ended conflict that destroys value.

If the plant-by-plant numbers show that a negotiated package can restore competitiveness without closures, we will favor that. If not, then management and the owners should stop pretending that closure is merely a bargaining chip.
