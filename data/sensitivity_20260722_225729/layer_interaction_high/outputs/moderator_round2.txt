Here is the procedural read-out of the stalemate and possible bridge options.

## Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to a single unresolved issue:

**Whether “equivalent structural savings” can be achieved without German plant closures and compulsory redundancies.**

That impasse has three layers:

1. **Different definitions of equivalence**
   - Management, owners, and investors mean: permanent fixed-cost reduction, lower break-even volume, and enforceable capacity cuts.
   - Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony mean: savings must be achieved without crossing social and political red lines, and without treating closures as the default instrument.

2. **Different tolerance for risk**
   - Management and capital are prioritizing speed, credibility, and margin restoration.
   - Labour and Lower Saxony are prioritizing industrial peace, site protection, and governance legitimacy.

3. **Different burden-sharing expectations**
   - Labour is asking for verified contributions from management, owners, and capital before accepting deeper workforce measures.
   - Management and capital are asking for workforce concessions before ruling out footprint reduction.

## Potential compromise positions
Below are three procedural compromise tracks that could bridge the conflict.

### 1) “No closures for now” with hard review triggers
A time-limited agreement could suspend closure decisions for a defined period if:
- a quantified savings package is implemented immediately,
- milestones are met on cost reduction and capacity adjustment,
- and an independent monitor verifies progress.

If milestones are missed, the parties would reopen the closure question under predefined rules.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It gives labour and Lower Saxony a non-closure starting point, while giving management and capital an enforceable path to revisit footprint reduction if the alternative underperforms.

### 2) Capacity reduction without formal closure
The parties could negotiate:
- line consolidation,
- shift eliminations,
- product reallocations,
- and site specialization,

so that capacity is reduced materially even if a plant is not formally closed.

This could be paired with:
- voluntary exits only,
- redeployment and retraining,
- temporary working-time measures,
- and management/overhead reductions.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It addresses management’s need for permanent structural savings while preserving a stronger site-protection signal for labour and Lower Saxony.

### 3) Burden-sharing package with binding sequencing
A staged package could require contributions in this order:
1. management and overhead cuts,
2. owner/capital discipline,
3. labour flexibility and voluntary measures,
4. only then any discussion of footprint rationalization if the target is still not met.

This sequencing could be written into a binding governance roadmap.

**Why this may bridge positions:**  
It responds to the demand that workers should not be first and only bearers of adjustment, while keeping closure as a last-resort instrument rather than an opening threat.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To management
**What exact savings gap remains after excluding closures and compulsory redundancies, and which measures in your package are permanent rather than temporary?**

### To the Works Council
**Which specific structural measures, beyond temporary working-time reductions and voluntary exits, are you prepared to accept if they are necessary to match the required savings?**

### To IG Metall
**What binding workforce contribution would you consider sufficient to support a no-closure settlement, and how would you ensure it is durable?**

### To Lower Saxony
**If a quantified non-closure package meets the savings target, what governance mechanism will you accept to make that commitment credible and enforceable?**

### To the controlling owners
**Will you support a binding package that prioritizes non-closure measures first, even if it limits flexibility on footprint rationalization during the implementation period?**

### To the institutional investors
**Would you accept a delayed closure option if an independently verified alternative achieves the same permanent savings on schedule?**
