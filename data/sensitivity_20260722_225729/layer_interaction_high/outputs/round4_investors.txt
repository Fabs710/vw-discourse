We agree with the broad diagnosis, but not with any package that substitutes rhetoric for hard cost action.

Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring because the current cost base is not competitive enough for the volume and margin environment it faces. Temporary measures, vague productivity promises, or a settlement that merely preserves the existing footprint would be value-destructive. On that, we are aligned with management, the owners, and the expert.

Where we differ is on process and proof.

## To management
You are right that the company needs permanent, measurable savings and a lower break-even volume. You are also right that the current situation cannot be left to drift.

But we do **not** accept keeping closures and compulsory redundancies on the table as a default bargaining tool without first showing why the non-closure route fails. That increases execution risk and raises the strike premium. From a shareholder perspective, that is not discipline; it is uncertainty.

What we need from you is a **plant-by-plant business case**:
- current fixed-cost position,
- utilization,
- future product allocation,
- capex required to keep each site competitive,
- and the recurring savings from each option.

Without that, “closure is available” is leverage, not evidence.

## To the Works Council and IG Metall
We agree that a cosmetic deal is worthless. We also agree that workers should not carry the entire burden alone.

But your repeated insistence on “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no erosion of guarantees” is only credible if you can show a package that still delivers the required structural savings. So far, that has not been demonstrated.

If you want the market to back a non-closure settlement, then it must include:
- real capacity reduction,
- durable labour-cost contribution,
- overhead and indirect-cost cuts,
- productivity gains that are actually enforceable,
- and a timetable that restores margins, not just industrial peace.

A deal that protects every site and every legacy cost layer is not a restructuring. It is delay.

## To Lower Saxony
We understand your governance role and the political constraints. But a blocking position cannot become a shield for structurally excess capacity if the company is to regain competitiveness.

We support a non-closure solution **only if** it is verified to be economically equivalent. If it is not, then refusing any footprint rationalization would simply transfer the cost of inaction to shareholders and ultimately to the company’s long-term viability.

## To the controlling owners
We agree with your insistence on value discipline and on not asking workers alone to absorb the adjustment. But value discipline also means being willing to support the best economic outcome, not simply the most politically convenient one.

If the independent analysis shows non-closure options are truly equivalent, we would support them. If not, then keeping closures off the table in advance would be a value trap.

## On the audit / verification issue
We strongly support the call for an independent, plant-by-plant review.

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant industrial and financial auditor with access to site-level cost, utilization, capex, product allocation, and recurring savings data, mandated to compare closure, consolidation, and non-closure restructuring options]

That review should answer, for each German site:
- whether it is structurally viable,
- what permanent savings can be extracted without closure,
- what capex is required,
- and what the implementation risk is under each option.

That is the right way to separate a real alternative from a political slogan.

## Our bottom line
We are prepared to support a serious restructuring package if it delivers:
1. **permanent and verifiable savings**
2. **a credible reduction in capacity and fixed cost**
3. **durable labour contribution**
4. **management and owner discipline**
5. **implementation certainty**

We will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- an arrangement that preserves an uncompetitive structure,
- or an open-ended conflict that destroys value.

If the numbers prove a non-closure solution can restore competitiveness, we will back it. If they do not, then the parties should stop treating closure as merely illegitimate and start treating the economics honestly.
