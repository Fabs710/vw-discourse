I will be equally direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, institutional investors, and partly with Lower Saxony on one fundamental point: **Volkswagen needs a durable structural reset, not a cosmetic compromise**. Temporary measures alone will not restore competitiveness, and a prolonged drift would destroy value.

I also agree with labour that **a purely one-sided adjustment would be unacceptable and unstable**. If the workforce is asked to contribute, then management, owners, and capital must also show discipline. A deal that relies only on worker pain would be bad governance and bad stewardship.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept a stance that rules out any serious structural adjustment in advance and then asks the company to remain competitive anyway.

To the Works Council and IG Metall:  
Your position still reads as if “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no meaningful changes to legacy protections” is the answer. It is not, unless you can prove — with numbers, not slogans — that equivalent savings are achievable. So far, that proof has not been shown.

To Lower Saxony:  
I respect the governance role and the regional responsibility, but a blanket political veto against plant rationalisation cannot become a substitute for industrial reality. If every German site is untouchable no matter the economics, then Volkswagen will simply continue to bleed margin and flexibility.

### What I think is non-negotiable
From the controlling owners’ perspective, these are the real constraints:

- **The cost base must come down durably.**
- **Overcapacity must be reduced in a way that is economically credible.**
- **Labour peace matters, but not at the price of preserving an uncompetitive structure.**
- **No solution that permanently weakens Volkswagen’s long-term value is acceptable.**
- **No outcome that undermines governance control or traps the company in recurring crisis is acceptable.**

### On closures
I am not ideologically committed to closures. I am committed to **results**.

If management can achieve the same structural savings through a negotiated package of:
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction,
- redeployment,
- qualification,
- voluntary exits,
- productivity improvements,
- and a meaningful labour-cost contribution,

then that is preferable.

But if the “alternative” is only:
- vague promises,
- temporary wage restraint,
- natural attrition,
- and a future review,

then that is not an alternative. That is delay.

### What I would support
I would support a package that includes:

1. **Quantified permanent savings**
2. **Real capacity reduction**
3. **Meaningful contributions from labour, management, and owners**
4. **Executive and overhead restraint**
5. **Investment commitments tied to restructuring**
6. **Site-specific solutions where economically justified**
7. **A credible timetable with enforcement milestones**

### What I will not support
I will not support:
- a package that simply freezes the current footprint;
- an arrangement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive;
- a conflict spiral that damages the company’s reputation and cash generation;
- or a governance setup in which every hard decision is blocked but the value erosion continues.

### To management
You need to sharpen your case. Present a **plant-by-plant and measure-by-measure business case** showing:
- what savings are required,
- what each option delivers,
- what the investment impact is,
- and what happens if no agreement is reached.

If closures are truly necessary in some form, say so with evidence. If not, show the equivalent package. Right now, the discussion is still too abstract.

### To labour
If you want no closures and no compulsory redundancies, then you must be prepared to deliver **real structural savings** that are measurable and durable. That means more than symbolic concessions. It means acceptance of genuine flexibility and cost change.

### To Lower Saxony
Your role is to protect the long-term industrial base, not to freeze it in place. A responsible shareholder cannot support permanent paralysis. If you want to prevent closures, help produce a package that makes closures unnecessary — but it must add up economically.

### Bottom line
My position is this:

- **I support a serious restructuring.**
- **I am open to an alternative to closures.**
- **I will only support that alternative if it delivers equivalent, verifiable, long-term savings and capacity reduction.**
- **I will not support a weak compromise that merely postpones the crisis.**

If the parties can produce a credible package with real numbers and binding commitments, I will engage constructively through governance. If not, then the responsibility for prolonged instability will not lie with the owners.
