As the independent plant-by-plant industrial and financial auditor, I can answer only what is evidentially assessable from the record so far.

## 1) What can be concluded now
The discussion establishes a few **verified or strongly supported facts**:

- Volkswagen faces a **durable restructuring problem**, not a temporary dip.
- There is credible concern about **overcapacity, cost position, and competitiveness**.
- The real dispute is **not whether savings are needed**, but **which instruments can deliver them** and at what social/operational cost.
- No party has yet produced a **complete plant-by-plant evidence set** sufficient to conclusively rank closures versus non-closure restructuring on an audited basis.

## 2) What cannot yet be concluded
From the material provided, I **cannot** verify:
- that any specific German plant is definitively non-viable;
- that plant closure is economically superior to all non-closure alternatives;
- that a no-closure package is sufficient to restore competitiveness;
- or that any party’s claimed savings numbers are complete, because no audited site-level data have been provided.

## 3) What an audit would need to determine
To assess plant viability and compare restructuring options properly, I would need for **each German site**:

- annual output and utilization by line
- direct labour cost and indirect overhead allocation
- unit conversion cost
- logistics and intra-group transfer costs
- capex required over the next 3–5 years
- product pipeline and volume forecast
- fixed-cost structure and break-even volume
- quality, scrap, rework, and downtime data
- headcount by function and skill class
- achievable savings from:
  - shift reduction
  - line consolidation
  - product reallocation
  - voluntary exits
  - working-time changes
  - wage/benefit changes
  - overhead reduction
  - management cuts

## 4) How I would classify plants, once data are available
A rigorous plant-by-plant audit would typically sort sites into one of four buckets:

1. **Structurally viable**
   - competitive or near-competitive with modest internal optimization

2. **Viable with consolidation**
   - needs product transfer, line rationalization, or capacity reduction

3. **Borderline**
   - viability depends on product allocation, capex, and labour-cost reset

4. **Not economically sustainable**
   - even after credible non-closure measures, the site remains materially above target cost and underutilized

## 5) Key caution on “equivalent structural savings”
For a non-closure settlement to be economically equivalent to closures, it must provide:
- **permanent** savings, not deferrals;
- **measurable** savings, not aspirational promises;
- **enforceable** savings, with milestones and remedies;
- and enough **capacity reduction** to lower break-even volume.

Without those elements, “no closures” is not financially equivalent; it is a preference statement.

## 6) Practical conclusion
At this stage, the only defensible audit conclusion is:

> The company appears to need substantial structural cost and capacity reduction, but the evidence supplied so far is insufficient to determine whether this should be achieved through plant closures or through a differentiated non-closure package.
