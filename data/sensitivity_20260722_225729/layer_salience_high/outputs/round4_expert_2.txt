From a site-economics standpoint, the current record is **not yet sufficient** to conclude that the proposed non-closure package closes the margin gap at each German plant.

## What can be said with confidence
- Volkswagen’s German footprint likely has a **real fixed-cost and capacity problem**.
- A **closure-free** restructuring is **possible in principle** in automotive operations.
- But “possible in principle” is not the same as “economically sufficient at each site.”

## What is still missing
To test whether the package credibly closes the gap, I would need, **for each German plant**:
1. **Current and projected volume**
2. **Utilization rates** by line / body / paint / final assembly
3. **Direct labor cost per unit**
4. **Indirect labor and overhead burden**
5. **Contribution margin per model mix**
6. **Energy and logistics cost**
7. **Required capex / maintenance spend**
8. **The exact savings measures** in the package, quantified by site
9. **The timing** of those savings
10. **A comparison to break-even output**

Without that, any claim that a non-closure package “solves” the issue is an inference, not a verified conclusion.

## How I would judge the package
A non-closure plan is credible only if, **site by site**, it does at least one of the following:
- restores **adequate utilization** so fixed cost is absorbed properly,
- removes enough **fixed overhead** to offset lower volume,
- improves **model mix / specialization** enough to raise contribution margin,
- or reduces the site’s economics to a clearly sustainable role with limited capex.

If a plant remains:
- underutilized,
- overhead-heavy,
- dependent on repeated concessions,
- and requiring ongoing support just to stay open,

then it is **not** structurally turned around, even if it avoids formal closure.

## My assessment of the debate so far
- **Labor’s position** that closure is not automatically the answer is economically plausible.
- **Management’s position** that durable, quantified savings must be shown is also economically sound.
- The dispute cannot be resolved responsibly without a **plant-by-plant savings bridge** and **post-deal break-even analysis**.

## Bottom line
At present, I cannot say the non-closure package **credibly closes the margin gap at each plant**. The evidence in the record is too general.
