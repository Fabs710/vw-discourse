Lower Saxony will speak plainly.

We agree with **one core point** from management, owners, and investors: Volkswagen needs a **durable competitive future**, not slogans. The cost gap is real, and the company cannot simply carry on unchanged.

But we disagree with the idea that the only serious way forward is to keep **closures and forced redundancies** in reserve as the default pressure point. That is not a responsible starting position in a company with co-determination, a blocking minority, and a regional responsibility to Germany’s industrial base.

## To management
You are right that a temporary patch is not enough. What you are still not doing is proving that closures are genuinely necessary rather than simply convenient as leverage.

I want the **site-by-site case**:
- what each German plant contributes,
- what investment it receives,
- what capacity must be reduced,
- what savings are required,
- and why a negotiated alternative cannot achieve the same result.

Until that is laid out credibly, I do **not** accept closure as the baseline.

You keep saying “no closures” is not a plan. Fair enough. But “closures remain on the table” is also not a plan if it is being used to force concessions before the alternatives are tested.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I agree with you on the fundamentals:
- no German plant closures as a first resort,
- no compulsory redundancies as a first resort,
- no one-sided burden shifting onto employees.

That said, I need to be equally clear: a pure “no closures, no forced layoffs” line is only sustainable if it is matched by a **credible, numbers-based alternative**. The burden cannot simply be denied; it has to be managed.

So my position is:
- **capacity reduction without site closure first**,
- **voluntary and negotiated measures first**,
- **working-time and redeployment solutions first**,
- and a **temporary, limited workforce contribution** only in exchange for binding security and investment.

But I will not support a package that only says “no” without solving the economics.

## To the owners
You are right to insist on durability and value. What you must not do is treat regional employment and co-determination as obstacles rather than assets.

A destructive restructuring would damage Volkswagen’s long-term value, not just its optics. If the company loses trust with its workforce and the region, it will pay for that for years. That is not stewardship.

If a non-closure package can deliver the needed savings and capacity reduction, then you should support it. If it cannot, then we must have a hard conversation — but not by defaulting to closures as the first move.

## To investors
You are right to demand quantified savings and earnings improvement. But you are too quick to reduce durability to only spreadsheet logic. Volkswagen is not a pure financial asset; it is an industrial anchor with strategic and political consequences.

A package that technically reduces costs but destroys labour peace, regional stability, and co-determination would be a false economy.

## What Lower Saxony will support
We can support a negotiated restructuring if it includes all of the following:
1. **Binding industrial and product commitments for German sites**
2. **Capacity reduction without plant closure as the preferred route**
3. **No compulsory redundancies**
4. **A temporary, limited employee contribution**
5. **Visible management and owner contribution**
6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s role under the VW Law**

## What Lower Saxony will not support
We will not support:
- plant closures as the starting instrument,
- forced mass layoffs,
- one-sided labour cost dumping,
- or a process that sidelines the state’s blocking and supervisory role.

## Bottom line
I am not offering a blank check to either side.

To management: show us a real industrial plan, not a threat.  
To labour: show us a credible alternative, not only red lines.  
To owners and investors: accept that protecting the German industrial base is part of protecting value.

If the company wants our support, then it must come back with a **non-closure restructuring that is numerically credible and socially balanced**. If that cannot be done, then the burden of proof remains with those who want to break open Germany’s industrial footprint.
