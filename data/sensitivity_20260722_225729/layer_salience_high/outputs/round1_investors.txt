We are prepared to support a restructuring only if it is **credible, durable, and earnings-relevant**. That is the only standard that matters to us as capital providers.

### Where we agree
We agree with management, and partly with the owners and the state, on the core diagnosis:
- Volkswagen’s German cost base is too high.
- Structural overcapacity cannot simply be ignored.
- A cosmetic solution that preserves all legacy structures while pushing the problem into the future is not acceptable.
- The company needs a clear path back to competitive margins and a realistic return-on-sales profile.

That said, we also agree with the point that **a prolonged destructive conflict would destroy value**. Strikes, reputational damage, and uncertainty are not a substitute for restructuring. From a shareholder perspective, delay is costly.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support language that suggests cost discipline can be deferred or absorbed through vague “socially balanced” measures that do not materially change economics.

In particular:

- **We will not support an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.**
- **We will not support a settlement that protects every site and every legacy arrangement if the result is continued margin erosion.**
- **We will not support “alternative measures” unless they deliver savings equivalent to the structural problem.**

So to the works council and IG Metall: your red lines are understood, but they are not financially decisive unless you can show how the same economics are achieved without closures or force. “No closures” is not, by itself, a business plan.

To Lower Saxony and the family owners: we understand the desire to avoid a socially and politically costly confrontation. But stability without competitiveness is just a slower decline. If the board cannot deliver genuine savings, then the market will punish the company regardless of the political optics.

### Our position
We are open to **any package** that can credibly demonstrate:
- durable capacity reduction,
- substantial and lasting labour-cost improvement,
- measurable improvement in margins,
- and enough certainty that the plan will not unravel in the next downturn.

That could include alternatives to closures — but only if they are **quantified, enforceable, and sufficient**. We are not interested in symbolic concessions, temporary accounting relief, or vague commitments to future efficiency.

### What would move us
A credible deal would need to show:
1. Exactly how much cost is removed, and when.
2. How the savings compare to the current gap versus competitors.
3. Why the plan will hold through 2030, not just one bargaining cycle.
4. What investment is secured to support the industrial logic of the German sites.

Without that, we would view the process as an attempt to manage headlines rather than restore value.

### Bottom line
We are not asking for closures for their own sake. We are asking for **results**. If closures are the only tool that can deliver those results, then the company must be honest about that. If another package can do it, fine — but it must be real, not politically convenient.

Until we see a credible, numbers-based restructuring that restores competitiveness, we will remain skeptical and will pressure management accordingly through capital markets and analyst scrutiny.
