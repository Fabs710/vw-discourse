Core impasse: **the parties are negotiating two different things at once**.

1. **Labour side / Lower Saxony** is asking for **credible protection first**: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and enforceable site security.  
2. **Management / investors / owners** are asking for **credible economics first**: quantified, durable cost and capacity reduction, with closure kept available if needed.

The deadlock is that **each side sees the other’s precondition as the risk**:
- Labour sees closure language as coercive leverage.
- Management sees blanket no-closure demands as pre-empting the economics.

A second layer of impasse is that the discussion remains **too abstract**:
- no plant-by-plant economics,
- no quantified savings bridge,
- no agreed implementation safeguards,
- no clear definition of what “equivalent economics” means in operational terms.

## Facilitator proposals to bridge the gap

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-closure testing phase
A negotiated package could establish a **fixed testing period** for all German sites, during which:
- no closures and no compulsory redundancies are implemented,
- voluntary instruments, working-time changes, redeployment, and capacity reduction are pursued,
- management must deliver plant-by-plant economics and a quantified savings bridge,
- an independent expert verifies whether the non-closure package closes the margin gap.

At the end of that period, the parties review the verified numbers.  
This would give labour protection during the test phase while giving management a defined route to demonstrate whether non-closure is sufficient.

### Proposal 2: Site-by-site protected restructuring with trigger-based escalation
A second compromise could be a **site-specific framework**:
- each German plant receives a written industrial future, product allocation, and investment commitments,
- temporary employee contributions apply only where site protections are signed,
- a review trigger is tied to objective metrics such as utilization, margin, and product pipeline,
- any escalation beyond the agreed measures requires joint review and independent verification.

This would replace broad closure threats with **predefined, measurable triggers** and could reduce the sense of open-ended pressure.

### Proposal 3: “No forced exits, but more flexibility” package
A narrower compromise could preserve the strongest labour red lines while still giving management room:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no immediate closures,
- but stronger capacity reduction through voluntary exits, shift changes, internal transfers, and temporary concessions,
- with a binding commitment that any further structural decision must be based on verified site economics and a co-determined process.

This would not resolve the full dispute, but it could create a workable interim settlement.

## Pointed questions to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings, by plant and by measure, would convince you that a non-closure package is economically sufficient, and are you prepared to publish that threshold in the negotiation?**

### To IG Metall
**If an independent expert verifies that a no-closure package still leaves a material margin gap, what additional flexibility are you prepared to consider without reopening compulsory redundancies?**

### To the Works Council
**Would you accept a time-limited, independently monitored non-closure phase if it contained binding site-by-site product and investment commitments and objective review triggers?**

### To Lower Saxony
**Will you use your blocking and board leverage to require a verified non-closure test phase, even if that means accepting temporary workforce concessions?**

### To the Controlling Owners
**Do you support making plant-by-plant economics the formal basis for any escalation beyond the negotiated package, rather than treating closures as a standing bargaining option?**

### To Institutional Investors
**Will you support a non-closure solution if it is independently verified as economically equivalent, even if it relies on labor flexibility rather than immediate structural cuts?**

### To the Independent Expert
**What minimum set of plant-level data and threshold metrics would you require to certify that a non-closure restructuring is genuinely equivalent on economics?**
