We agree on the diagnosis, but not yet on whether the current discussion is moving to a solution that protects shareholder value.

### Where we agree
- **Volkswagen needs a durable competitiveness reset.**
- **Structural overcapacity and a too-high cost base are real.**
- **Cosmetic fixes are unacceptable.**
- **A prolonged strike, reputational damage, or endless uncertainty would destroy value.**
- **Investment in future products matters only if the underlying economics are fixed.**

That said, I want to be equally clear about our threshold:

### Where we disagree
We do **not** support a “socially balanced” outcome if it leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

To the works council and IG Metall:
- “No closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” are **not** a business solution by themselves.
- Binding guarantees through 2030 are not credible if they freeze a cost base that the company cannot carry.
- If your alternative to closures does not produce **equivalent, durable savings**, then it is not a viable substitute.

To Lower Saxony:
- We understand the political role and the regional stakes.
- But regional protection cannot become an excuse for preserving excess cost indefinitely.
- A package that protects every site in name, but leaves margins impaired, simply shifts the damage into the future and lowers enterprise value.

To the family owners:
- We agree that a destructive public confrontation would be value-destructive.
- But stability without decisive cost reduction is not stewardship; it is delayed erosion.
- If the company needs closures or stronger workforce reductions to restore economics, the board should not pretend otherwise.

To management:
- You are right that the company must improve materially.
- But “closure remains on the table” is not enough. You need to show the numbers, site by site, and prove why alternatives do or do not close the gap.
- If you cannot quantify the required savings and capacity reduction, then you are asking capital to accept uncertainty without evidence.

### Our position
We will support **only** a restructuring that is:
1. **Quantified** — with explicit savings, timing, and margin impact.
2. **Durable** — not temporary concessions that unwind in the next bargaining round.
3. **Earnings-relevant** — enough to restore competitive returns, not just headlines.
4. **Enforceable** — with real implementation mechanics, not aspirational language.
5. **Strategically coherent** — tied to future product and investment allocation.

### What would move us
A credible package would need to answer, plainly:
- How much fixed cost is removed?
- How much capacity is taken out?
- What is the effect on return on sales?
- Why does the plan hold through 2030?
- What specific investment is being secured?

If the answer is “we can avoid hard restructuring and still fix the economics,” then prove it with numbers.  
If the answer is “we need deeper structural action,” then say so directly and stop treating closures as taboo while demanding shareholder patience.

### Bottom line
We are not here to protect any stakeholder’s comfort. We are here to protect capital and enterprise value.

If the final package leaves Volkswagen’s German base structurally uncompetitive, we will not support it.  
If it delivers credible, durable competitiveness — even through painful measures — then we can back it.

At this stage, we remain skeptical and will continue to pressure management for a real, numbers-based restructuring.
