I want to be very clear: **Lower Saxony will not support a restructuring that destroys sites, guts regional employment, or turns Volkswagen into a purely financial optimization exercise.**

I do agree with management on one point: **Volkswagen needs a durable industrial future, not cosmetic fixes.** The company has to regain competitiveness. But the way to do that is **not automatically plant closures or forced redundancies**. Those are the most destructive and politically risky instruments, and once a site is closed, the damage is irreversible.

### Where I stand

**1. No closure-led restructuring.**  
I do not accept closures in Germany as the starting point. If management comes to the board with a package that treats closures as the default solution, it will meet resistance from us. The regional economic consequences are too severe, and the co-determination and blocking rights exist precisely to prevent such one-sided decisions.

**2. No compulsory redundancies as the first resort.**  
If there are adjustments, they must come through negotiated, socially responsible instruments: voluntary departures, internal transfers, qualification, working-time solutions, and capacity adjustments. We have seen these tools before; they are painful, but they preserve industrial substance.

**3. Job and site protection must be part of any deal.**  
I support a restructuring only if it is tied to a credible commitment to **German sites, employment, and future investment**. That means real product commitments and a clear pathway for the plants involved. Without that, employee contributions will not be politically or socially acceptable.

### Where I challenge management

Management says capacity cuts and labour-cost reductions are unavoidable. I am not denying the need for adaptation. But I am saying: **prove that you have exhausted alternatives before you demand irreversible measures.**

A package that simply shifts the burden onto workers while preserving strategic mistakes is not a solution. If you want labour to contribute, then the company, owners, and executive management must also make visible commitments:
- investment in the German industrial base,
- clear product allocation,
- serious efficiency measures at management level,
- and a negotiated settlement that preserves co-determination and social partnership.

### To the works council and IG Metall

Your red lines are understandable and, in core parts, I share them. But I would also urge realism: if the company truly faces structural overcapacity, then **some form of capacity reduction** may be necessary. The key question is **how** that is done.

If the answer is:
- no closures,
- no forced layoffs,
- a fair employee contribution,
- and binding investment plus site security,

then that is the only direction that has a chance of political and supervisory-board support from Lower Saxony.

### Bottom line

Lower Saxony is prepared to support a **negotiated restructuring** that protects jobs and plants while restoring competitiveness.  
We are **not** prepared to support a solution that:
- closes plants,
- forces mass redundancies,
- or sidelines the state’s co-determination role.

If management wants our support, it needs to come back with a package that is **socially balanced, reversible where possible, and committed to Volkswagen’s role as a regional economic anchor**.
