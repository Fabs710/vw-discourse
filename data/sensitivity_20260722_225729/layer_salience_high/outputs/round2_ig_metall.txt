Management is still trying to present the workforce with a false choice: either accept closures and insecurity, or accept a cost-cutting package on management’s terms. We do **not** accept that framing.

Let me state our position clearly again.

## What we agree on
We agree that Volkswagen needs a durable industrial future in Germany.  
We agree that the company needs competitiveness, investment, and a credible path through the EV transition.  
We also agree that some adjustment in capacity, working time, and costs may be necessary.

And yes: we are prepared to discuss an employee contribution — **but only as part of a fair, shared-sacrifice package**.

## What we do not accept
We do **not** accept:
- **plant closures in Germany**
- **compulsory redundancies**
- **dismantling the collective wage framework**
- **one-sided burden shifting onto employees**
- **using closure threats as bargaining leverage**

If that is the direction management is taking, then there is no honest basis for a settlement.

---

## To management
You keep saying “equivalent economics” must be achieved. Fine — then show the numbers and show the alternatives. But don’t pretend that closure is the natural baseline and that labor must now prove its worth.

That is not how social partnership works.

If you want our cooperation, then the company must commit to:
1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job security**
4. **Real investment commitments for each German site**
5. **Management and owners contributing visibly as well**
6. **A limited, time-bound workforce contribution tied to security**

We can discuss:
- temporary working-time reductions,
- capacity cuts without closures,
- voluntary instruments,
- redeployment and qualification,
- a negotiated waiver of part of variable pay or bonuses,
- and even a temporary contribution to a restructuring fund.

But none of that is acceptable if the payoff is insecurity, site erosion, or a hidden road to closures later.

---

## To the works council
We are aligned on the essentials: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no one-sided burden shifting. That is the correct line.

Where I would sharpen the argument is this: the company must not be allowed to turn “capacity adjustment” into a backdoor job-destruction program. Any reduced capacity must be paired with:
- binding future product allocation,
- qualification and transfer pathways,
- and enforceable site protection.

Otherwise “capacity adjustment” becomes just a softer word for dismantling.

---

## To Lower Saxony
I appreciate that you are resisting closure-led restructuring. But political statements are not enough. If the state’s blocking position is real, then it must be used to prevent a package that destroys jobs and sites while asking workers to pay the bill.

If Lower Saxony is serious about social responsibility, then it should support:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- negotiated reductions instead,
- and a credible industrial strategy for every German location.

---

## To the owners
You say closures are not your first choice. Good. Then do not keep them on the table as a blunt threat while demanding concessions from labor in advance.

If you want a durable settlement, then the owners must also contribute to the industrial future:
- through capital commitment,
- through acceptance of lower near-term returns,
- and through backing a package that protects the sites the company depends on.

A “solution” that preserves ownership returns while externalizing the pain to employees is not acceptable.

---

## On the actual path forward
Here is the only basis on which we would continue serious negotiations:

### Acceptable elements
- capacity reductions **without** plant closures
- no compulsory redundancies
- voluntary and negotiated workforce adjustments
- temporary and limited employee contribution
- working-time and flexibility arrangements
- investment commitments for German sites
- protection of the collective wage structure
- a binding role for co-determination in implementation

### Unacceptable elements
- closures
- forced layoffs
- permanent wage dumping
- site insecurity
- using the workforce as the sole adjustment variable

---

## Bottom line
We are willing to help build a durable settlement.  
We are **not** willing to sign off on a restructuring that destroys trust, breaks the job-security framework, or makes employees carry all the pain.

If management wants a deal, it must move from threats to guarantees.  
If it does not, then it should expect a very determined response from the workforce.
