**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that Volkswagen needs a **durable industrial and economic future in Germany**.
- There is broad support for **investment in future products, technology, and competitiveness**.
- Most parties accept that **some form of restructuring or capacity adjustment** is likely necessary.
- There is general openness to **alternatives to outright closures**, including:
  - capacity reductions,
  - working-time solutions,
  - internal transfers,
  - qualification/retraining,
  - voluntary departure instruments,
  - and negotiated employee contributions.
- There is shared recognition that a **destructive conflict, strikes, or prolonged uncertainty** would be harmful.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:** Labor representatives, Lower Saxony, and to a large extent the owners oppose closures as a starting point; management and investors keep them on the table if no equivalent alternative is found.
- **Compulsory redundancies:** The works council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject them; management and investors do not rule out stronger workforce reductions if needed.
- **Scope of labor concessions:** Management and investors want substantial, lasting cost reductions; labor insists any contribution must be limited, temporary, and linked to firm guarantees.
- **Guarantees vs flexibility:** Labor demands binding job and site security, while management and investors argue that unchanged guarantees may block necessary adaptation.
- **Decision logic:** Labor and Lower Saxony emphasize social and regional responsibility; management and investors emphasize economic sufficiency and measurable competitiveness.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **How much capacity must actually be removed** to restore competitiveness, and whether that can be achieved without closures.
- **What level of labor-cost contribution is acceptable** without undermining collective protections.
- **What concrete investment and product commitments** would justify employee concessions.
- **How job and site guarantees can be made credible** if structural change is required.
- **Who bears the burden of adjustment**: employees, management, owners, or a shared package.
- **What happens if no equivalent alternative to closures is found**: whether the process can still converge on a negotiated solution.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to **numbers and options**: specify required savings, capacity changes, and timing.
- Test **alternative restructuring scenarios** against closure-based outcomes.
- Clarify **what guarantees and investment commitments** are available in exchange for workforce contributions.
- Identify a **narrow set of deal parameters** that could be acceptable to all sides, or at least establish where the final non-negotiable boundaries are.
