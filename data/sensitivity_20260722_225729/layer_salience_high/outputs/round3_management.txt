We need to stop talking past each other.

I agree on one point: Volkswagen needs a durable future in Germany, not a temporary patch. I also agree that destructive conflict, endless uncertainty, and a purely cosmetic deal would hurt the company. But I do **not** agree with the insistence that we must first lock in blanket guarantees of “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no change to the job-security framework” before any serious restructuring is possible.

That approach does not solve the cost problem. It postpones it.

## To the works council
You say closure threats are pressure tactics. I say refusal to confront overcapacity is not a solution. We are not asking workers to “prove they deserve” their jobs. We are saying the current structure is not competitive enough, and that has to change in a durable way.

What I reject is your attempt to make **every plant untouchable** and to pre-empt the result before the economics have been tested. A restructuring that leaves all sites, all legacy protections, and the current fixed-cost base intact will not restore the brand’s return-on-sales target.

You keep asking for a binding industrial future for every German plant. Fine — but that future must be economically credible. If a site cannot be made sustainable without significant structural change, then you cannot rule out stronger measures in advance and still claim to be serious about the company’s future.

## To IG Metall
I hear your willingness to discuss working-time changes, redeployment, voluntary exits, and a limited employee contribution. That is the right direction. But you are still drawing a red line around outcomes that may be economically necessary.

A “limited, temporary contribution” is not enough if it does not produce durable savings. We need:
- substantial and lasting labour-cost relief,
- real capacity reduction,
- and a structure that holds through 2030.

Temporary concessions alone are not a structural answer.

And let me be explicit: I will not sign up to a deal that preserves the current cost base and calls it a compromise. That would be irresponsible to the company, the owners, and yes, to employees in the long run.

## To Lower Saxony
I appreciate that you reject a destruction-first approach. But your support cannot mean a veto against necessary change. You are right that Volkswagen is a strategic industrial anchor; you are wrong if that becomes an argument for freezing the structure.

If you want to protect the industrial base, then help secure a package that actually improves competitiveness. That means accepting that some capacity must come out of the system and that labour must contribute in a lasting way. Political discomfort is not a substitute for a viable industrial plan.

## To the owners
I agree with your point that we need a real, numbers-based restructuring, not a symbolic exercise. Where I differ is that you still seem to want comfort that the company can avoid hard decisions if the dialogue is managed carefully. It cannot.

If the alternatives to closures are truly capable of delivering equivalent economics, then show that clearly and commit to them. If not, then we must be honest that closures and stronger workforce reductions remain part of the necessary toolkit. Keeping that reality off the table would be governance failure.

## To institutional investors
You are right to demand quantified savings, capacity reduction, and durable earnings improvement. That is exactly what management is trying to secure.

But I also need to say this: a package that merely minimizes conflict while leaving Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive is not value-preserving. Investor value is not protected by avoiding difficult decisions; it is protected by restoring margins and freeing funds to invest in future products and technology.

## Where I stand
Let me put the boundary clearly:

### Acceptable
- capacity reductions
- working-time adjustments
- redeployment and qualification
- voluntary exit measures
- temporary, limited employee contributions
- site-by-site industrial planning
- investment commitments tied to restructuring

### Not acceptable
- a package that leaves the cost base uncompetitive
- cosmetic concessions that do not materially improve margins
- blanket guarantees that freeze adaptation
- the assumption that every German site must remain open regardless of economics

## The test
If you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must answer four questions:
1. How much cost is removed — permanently?
2. How much capacity is taken out — concretely?
3. How does that restore competitiveness through 2030?
4. Why is that better than the deeper structural options?

Until that is answered, “no closures” is not a plan. It is a position.

I remain open to a negotiated solution. But it has to be a real restructuring, not a negotiated denial of the problem.
