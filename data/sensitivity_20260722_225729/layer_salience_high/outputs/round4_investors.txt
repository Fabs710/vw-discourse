We are aligned on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

## Where we agree
We agree with management, the owners, Lower Saxony, and the expert that:
- Volkswagen’s German footprint is structurally too expensive.
- The current cost and capacity base must come down.
- Cosmetic concessions are worthless.
- Prolonged conflict or endless uncertainty would destroy value.
- Any settlement has to be quantified, durable, and enforceable.

We also agree with labour on one important point: a destructive strike cycle would be value-destructive, and the company should avoid unnecessary operational damage.

## Where we disagree
### To the works council and IG Metall
Your red lines are understandable politically, but from a shareholder perspective they are still too absolute.

You continue to insist on:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of guarantees,
- binding site security first,

before the economics have been proven site by site.

That is not yet a value-preserving answer. It is a boundary. We respect boundaries, but we do not confuse them with a restructuring plan.

If the alternative package truly delivers:
- permanent cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- flexibility,
- and a credible path back to competitive margins,

then we can support it. But if “no closures” means preserving a structurally uncompetitive cost base, we will not back it.

### To management
You are right on the economics, but you are still too vague on the proof.

Saying “closures remain on the table” is not enough. If you want capital to stay patient, you need to quantify:
- required savings by site,
- capacity reduction by site,
- the impact on return on sales,
- and the implementation risk.

At the same time, I do **not** accept closures as a bargaining club if a viable non-closure package exists. That would destroy value through unnecessary conflict.

### To Lower Saxony
We understand the political and regional stakes. But regional protection cannot become a substitute for operational competitiveness.

If you use your blocking position to prevent any meaningful restructuring, you will not protect value — you will merely defer the damage.

What we need from you is support for a package that:
- reduces cost materially,
- reduces capacity materially,
- and preserves the industrial base only where it remains viable.

### To the controlling owners
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. But durable competitiveness cannot just mean avoiding public conflict while leaving the economics unresolved.

If a site-by-site plan shows that closures are unnecessary, then back the non-closure solution. If it does not, then do not hide behind process to avoid the hard decision.

## Our position
We will support only a package that is:
1. **Quantified**
2. **Durable**
3. **Enforceable**
4. **Earnings-relevant**
5. **Credible by site**

That means:
- explicit savings,
- explicit capacity reduction,
- a clear margin path,
- and a binding implementation mechanism.

## What would move us
A credible non-closure package would need to show:
- how much fixed cost comes out,
- how much capacity is removed,
- which sites are downsized or reconfigured,
- how savings hold through 2030,
- and why the plan restores competitiveness versus peers.

If the answer is “we can keep every site open and still fix the economics,” then prove it with numbers.

If the answer is “we cannot,” then we should stop pretending otherwise and consider deeper structural measures, including closure where economically unavoidable.

## Our limit
We will not support an outcome that:
- leaves the German base structurally uncompetitive,
- preserves excess cost under the banner of social balance,
- or sacrifices shareholder value for the sake of a cosmetic settlement.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated, non-closure restructuring — but only if it is real economics, not political language.

If the package is credible and durable, we can back it.  
If it is not, we will continue to press for harder structural action.
