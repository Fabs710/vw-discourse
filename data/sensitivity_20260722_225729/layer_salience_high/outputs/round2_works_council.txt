Management, investors, and even parts of the political side are still talking as if the workforce must first prove that it deserves to keep its jobs and sites. I reject that framing.

Let me be explicit:

- **No German plant closures.**
- **No compulsory redundancies.**
- **No dismantling of the historic job-security framework.**

Those are not bargaining chips for us. They are the minimum conditions for any serious discussion.

### Where I agree
I do agree on one point: Volkswagen needs a durable future, not a temporary patch. And yes, the company must improve competitiveness. But competitiveness cannot be built by destroying the industrial base that created the company’s strength in the first place.

I also agree that purely cosmetic measures are not enough. If savings are needed, then let us discuss real alternatives:
- working-time adjustments,
- internal transfers,
- qualification and redeployment,
- voluntary exit instruments,
- capacity reductions **without** site closure,
- and a fair, time-limited employee contribution.

But these only become discussable if they are tied to **binding guarantees**:
- sites stay open,
- jobs are protected,
- investment is secured,
- and the works council has a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring.

### Where I disagree
#### To management:
You keep saying closures may be necessary if alternatives do not deliver “equivalent economics.” That is exactly the problem: you are treating closure as the default threat and asking workers to negotiate under duress.

If management wants labour to contribute, then first it must answer a simple question: **what is the concrete industrial plan for every German site?** Not in slogans, not in vague promises, but in binding commitments. Show us how each plant fits into the future product and investment strategy. Without that, your “numbers-based restructuring” is just a cover for shifting risk onto employees.

You also talk about labor-cost reductions as if the workforce alone caused the cost gap. That is unacceptable. Management decisions, product strategy mistakes, procurement, over-engineering, and governance failures all play a role. The burden cannot be pushed one-sidedly onto workers.

#### To investors:
You say “no closure for its own sake,” but then you insist on results without stating what your acceptable alternative is. From where I sit, that means you are keeping closures on the table while demanding concessions from labour in advance. I do not accept that leverage.

If you want measurable savings, then support measures that preserve jobs and sites. Do not hide behind capital-market discipline to justify breaking co-determination and social partnership.

#### To the family owners:
You say closures are not automatic, which is correct. But then you also say “if the only tool is closure, the company must be honest about that.” That is a pressure tactic, not a solution. We are not going to accept a situation where workers are told that their sites are expendable unless they surrender rights and guarantees first.

#### To Lower Saxony:
You speak about protecting regional employment, and we welcome that. But that protection must be real, not rhetorical. If the state truly stands with the workforce, then it should use its blocking position to prevent closure-led restructuring and to insist on a long-term industrial agreement.

### What would actually move us
Not speeches. Not threats. Not “flexibility” as a code word for insecurity.

What would move us is:
1. **Binding site and job guarantees through 2030.**
2. **A written commitment to keep every German plant open.**
3. **No compulsory redundancies.**
4. **Real investment and product allocation for all German sites.**
5. **A negotiated, limited workforce contribution only in exchange for those guarantees.**
6. **A co-determination role for the works council in implementation, not just consultation after decisions are made.**

If management and owners are serious, then bring that package. If not, then you should expect resistance. The workforce has already shown it can mobilise, and we will do so again if necessary.

### Bottom line
We are not rejecting responsibility. We are rejecting a restructuring that treats workers as the adjustment variable while leaving strategic failures and ownership power untouched.

If the company wants our cooperation, then it must stop talking about closures and start committing to the German sites it depends on.
