## Core impasse

The central impasse is between:

1. **Workforce representatives / Lower Saxony** insisting on **no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and binding site security**, and  
2. **Management / investors / owners** insisting that **only a restructuring with durable, quantified cost and capacity reduction is acceptable**, with closures and stronger workforce reductions still available if alternatives do not close the gap.

In procedural terms, the dispute is not just about the *size* of adjustment. It is about the **sequence and reversibility of decisions**:

- The workforce side wants **guarantees first, adjustment second**.
- The capital side wants **adjustment first, guarantees only if economics are proven**.

A second layer of impasse is that both sides invoke “credible alternatives,” but with different meanings:

- For the workforce side, alternatives must preserve sites and jobs.
- For management and investors, alternatives must produce **equivalent durable economics**, not just softer language.

---

## Facilitator compromise positions to test

### 1) Site-by-site package with conditional guarantees
A possible bridge is a **site-specific agreement**, not a blanket one:

- Each German plant gets a written industrial role, product path, and investment envelope.
- In return, labour accepts a **temporary, time-limited contribution**.
- **No closures and no compulsory redundancies** apply **for the duration of the agreement**, provided agreed savings and productivity targets are met.
- A joint review point is built in, with pre-defined triggers if targets are missed.

**Why this may bridge the divide:**  
It gives the workforce concrete protection while giving management and investors measurable restructuring outcomes.

---

### 2) Capacity reduction without closure, with enforceable redeployment rules
A second compromise is to separate **capacity reduction** from **site closure**:

- Remove capacity through shifts, models, lines, working-time reductions, and internal transfers.
- Create a binding redeployment and qualification program for affected workers.
- Use voluntary exit instruments only.
- Make plant closure a formally last-resort option, requiring a higher approval threshold and documented failure of alternatives.

**Why this may bridge the divide:**  
It addresses overcapacity and cost pressure without making closure the immediate mechanism.

---

### 3) Trade temporary concessions for hard governance guarantees
A third option is a **swap**:

- Workforce accepts temporary wage, bonus, or working-time concessions.
- Management and owners commit to:
  - no forced layoffs during the agreement,
  - protected German sites,
  - specified investments,
  - and works council co-determination in implementation.
- Lower Saxony supports the package in the supervisory board if the commitments are written and auditable.

**Why this may bridge the divide:**  
It converts abstract trust into enforceable reciprocity.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To Group & Brand Management
Can you specify, for each German site, the minimum package of cost and capacity reduction you require **before** closure becomes unavoidable, and are you prepared to place that threshold in writing for negotiation?

### To the Works Council (Betriebsrat)
If management accepts binding site roles, investment commitments, and a time-limited no-forced-layoffs clause, what concrete level of temporary workforce contribution would you consider negotiable?

### To IG Metall
What exact combination of working-time, pay, and redeployment measures would you treat as a viable alternative to closure, and what measurable savings would it need to deliver to be credible?

### To State of Lower Saxony
Will you use your governance leverage to support a conditional no-closure package if it includes verifiable savings and capacity reductions, or will you insist on no closures even if that blocks any agreement?

### To Controlling Owners
If a site-by-site plan shows that closures are not required to restore competitiveness, will you commit to backing the non-closure solution even if it reduces near-term returns?

### To Institutional Investors
What quantified savings, capacity reduction, and return-on-sales improvement would make you support a negotiated no-closure restructuring rather than continue to press for harder measures?
