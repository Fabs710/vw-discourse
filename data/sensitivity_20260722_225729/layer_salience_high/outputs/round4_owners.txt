I will be direct.

We share the same diagnosis in part: Volkswagen needs a durable competitiveness reset in Germany, not a symbolic compromise. I agree with management, investors, and Lower Saxony that the current cost and capacity structure is not sustainable. I also agree with labour that a destructive conflict, prolonged strikes, and broken labour relations would destroy value.

Where I do **not** agree is with the current attempt to turn “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” into the starting framework before the economics have been proven.

That is not a solution. It is a boundary.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I respect the insistence on protecting the workforce from arbitrary damage. But you are still not answering the central ownership question: **how do we restore competitiveness materially and durably if every serious restructuring instrument is ruled out in advance?**

A package that preserves every site, every legacy protection, and the current fixed-cost structure will not restore margins. If the German footprint remains structurally too expensive, then no amount of rhetoric about fairness changes that.

At the same time, I also reject using closure threats as a general bargaining club before a serious site-by-site case has been made. That creates instability and destroys trust, which is value-destructive. So I am not endorsing loose, open-ended pressure either.

## To management
You are right to insist on hard numbers and durable savings. I support that. But you have not yet made a sufficiently concrete case that closures are necessary rather than merely available.

That matters.

If a closure-free package can deliver the required savings and capacity reduction, then that is the preferable path. If it cannot, then say so in numbers, not in generalities. The supervisory board needs a real industrial case, site by site, not a broad threat.

## To Lower Saxony
I understand your concern for regional employment and co-determination. It is legitimate. But protection of the industrial base cannot mean freezing a structurally uncompetitive footprint.

What I can support is your demand that the burden of proof lies with those proposing deeper disruption. What I cannot support is allowing “no closures” to become a political shield against any real restructuring.

## To institutional investors
Your insistence on quantified, durable, enforceable economics is correct. I share it.

What I do not accept is the idea that closure is the benchmark of seriousness. A negotiated, closure-free solution can be better value creation if it truly delivers the same economics without igniting a long conflict. But if it cannot, then I will not pretend otherwise.

## My position
I will back a solution only if it is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **enforceable**
- **site-specific**
- and **value-preserving**

That means:
- real cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- a credible path to competitiveness through 2030,
- and no outcome that permanently impairs enterprise value or governance stability.

What I will **not** support is:
- a cosmetic compromise that leaves the problem intact,
- a blank cheque for closures before alternatives are tested,
- or a package that shifts the burden almost entirely onto labour while management and ownership remain insulated.

## What must happen now
We need a proper site-by-site industrial and economics audit, with:
- contribution margin by plant,
- utilization rates,
- fixed-cost absorption,
- product allocation,
- required savings by measure,
- and a clear comparison between a closure-free package and deeper structural options.

[SUMMON: an independent site-by-site industrial turnaround and plant-economics auditor with access to Volkswagen’s German plant data to test whether a closure-free restructuring can close the margin gap without forced redundancies.]

Until that is done, nobody should pretend the answer is already known.

## Bottom line
I am open to a negotiated non-closure solution **if and only if** it is demonstrably equivalent on economics. If it is not, then we must be honest that stronger structural measures remain part of the toolkit.

What I will not do is endorse either:
- a cosmetic settlement that preserves the problem, or
- a conflict-driven solution that destroys value in the process.

The family’s duty is long-term stewardship of the asset. That means competitiveness first, stability second, and slogans never.
