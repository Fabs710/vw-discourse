We will oppose any plan that includes plant closures or compulsory redundancies in Germany.

That is our clear position from the outset.

The workforce has already delivered productivity, flexibility, and sacrifice over many years. What is not acceptable is that management now tries to solve strategic mistakes and cost problems by breaking the historic job-security pledge and putting entire sites at risk. We will not accept a fait accompli, and we will not allow the burden of restructuring to be shifted one-sidedly onto employees.

Our priorities are clear:

- Every German plant must remain open.
- No forced layoffs.
- Binding job and site guarantees through 2030.
- A genuine co-determination role for the works council in shaping any restructuring.
- Any contribution from employees must be fair, limited, and tied to secure long-term investment and future products.

We are prepared to discuss alternative measures that protect sites and jobs: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, internal transfers, qualification, voluntary instruments, and a negotiated contribution from labour — but only if they are accompanied by hard guarantees. If management wants the workforce to carry part of the burden, then management, owners, and the board must first commit credibly to the industrial future of the German sites.

If the proposal means closures, forced job cuts, or dismantling the job-security framework, then we reject it.
