I hear the red lines you are both drawing. Let me be equally clear on management’s side:

We **do agree** on one essential point: the company needs a credible industrial future in Germany, not a one-off cut that just postpones the problem. We also agree that investment in future products, technology, and competitiveness must remain part of the package. Without that, there is no sustainable restructuring.

But I **do not accept** the core premise that the current German cost and capacity structure can remain largely intact and still deliver a viable return through 2030. That is not credible. The European market is shrinking, competition is intensifying, and our domestic cost base is too high. We cannot preserve every site, every legacy structure, and every guarantee exactly as it is and still expect the brand to hit its targets.

So my position is this:

- **Structural capacity reduction is unavoidable.**
- **A meaningful labour-cost contribution is unavoidable.**
- **The old guarantees cannot simply continue unchanged if they block adaptation.**

I am prepared to discuss alternatives to outright closures **if** they achieve the same durable economic effect. But they must be real alternatives, not cosmetic ones. That means:
- measurable and lasting capacity cuts,
- substantial labour-cost reductions,
- flexibility in working arrangements,
- and a settlement that restores medium-term margins.

What I will not accept is a package that preserves all German sites while only shifting costs around on paper. That would leave us uncompetitive and force the same crisis back onto the table later.

To be direct: if the workforce says “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no real cost contribution,” then that is not a restructuring proposal — it is a refusal to solve the crisis. And I would warn against assuming that management, owners, or the supervisory board will accept that outcome.

If you want us to avoid closures, then show me a **credible and durable** alternative that reduces capacity and fixed costs enough to restore competitiveness. If not, then the discussion has to include plant closures and deeper cuts, because the current base is not sustainable.
