We agree with the expert on the central point: **a closure-free solution is only acceptable if it is economically equivalent, durable, and enforceable**. If it is not, then it is not a solution — it is delay.

## Where we agree

We agree with management, the owners, and the expert that:
- Volkswagen’s German footprint is structurally too costly.
- The current cost base and capacity footprint must come down.
- Cosmetic concessions are worthless.
- A prolonged strike or endless uncertainty would destroy value.
- Any settlement has to be quantified, not rhetorical.

We also agree with the workforce side on one thing: **a destructive conflict is value-destructive**. If there is a credible negotiated route to lower fixed costs and restore margins, that is preferable to a public breakdown.

## Where we disagree

### To the works council and IG Metall
Your position is too absolute.

You keep saying:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of guarantees,
- binding site security through 2030,

before you have shown a restructuring that actually closes the economics gap.

That is not a business answer. It is a boundary. We respect boundaries, but we do not confuse them with value creation.

If the alternative package genuinely delivers:
- permanent cost reduction,
- real capacity reduction,
- flexibility,
- and a credible path back to competitive margins,

then we can support it. But if “no closures” means preserving a structurally uncompetitive base, we will not back it.

### To management
You are right on the economics, but you are still too loose in how you present the choice.

Saying “closures remain on the table” is not enough. If you want capital to stay patient, you need to quantify:
- the required savings,
- the specific capacity reduction by site,
- the impact on return on sales,
- and the implementation risk.

If you cannot show that, then you are asking shareholders to accept uncertainty without evidence.

At the same time, I do not accept that closures should be used as a negotiating club if a viable non-closure package exists. That creates unnecessary value destruction through conflict.

### To Lower Saxony
We understand the political and regional stakes. But regional protection cannot become an excuse for indefinite overcapacity.

If you use your blocking position to prevent any meaningful restructuring, you will not protect value — you will just defer the damage.

What we need from you is not blanket resistance. We need support for a package that:
- reduces cost materially,
- reduces capacity materially,
- and preserves the industrial base only where it remains viable.

### To the owners
You are right to reject a cosmetic compromise. But “durable competitiveness” cannot just mean avoiding public conflict while leaving the economics unresolved.

If a site-by-site plan shows that some closures are unnecessary, then back the non-closure solution. If it does not, then do not hide behind process to avoid the hard decision.

## Our position

We will support only a package that is:
1. **Quantified**
2. **Durable**
3. **Enforceable**
4. **Earnings-relevant**
5. **Credible by site**

That means:
- explicit savings,
- explicit capacity reduction,
- a clear margin path,
- and a binding implementation mechanism.

## What would move us
A credible non-closure package would need to show:
- how much fixed cost comes out,
- how much capacity is removed,
- which sites are downsized or reconfigured,
- how savings hold through 2030,
- and why the plan restores competitiveness versus peers.

If the answer is “we can keep every site open and still fix the economics,” then prove it with numbers.

If the answer is “we cannot,” then we should stop pretending otherwise and consider deeper structural measures, including closure, where economically unavoidable.

## Our limit
We will not support an outcome that:
- leaves the German base structurally uncompetitive,
- preserves excess cost under the banner of social balance,
- or sacrifices shareholder value for the sake of a cosmetic settlement.

## Bottom line
We are open to a negotiated, non-closure restructuring — **but only if it is real economics, not political language**.

If the package is credible and durable, we can back it.  
If it is not, then we will continue to press for harder structural action.
