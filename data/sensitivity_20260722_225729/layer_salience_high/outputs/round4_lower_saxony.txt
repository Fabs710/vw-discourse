Lower Saxony’s position is clear:

we support a **durable, competitive Volkswagen in Germany**, but we do **not** accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the starting instrument, and we will not let the state’s co-determination and blocking role be sidelined.

## To management
You are right that Volkswagen needs structural change. I agree that a purely cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.

What I do **not** accept is your attempt to keep closures and stronger workforce reductions in reserve as the default pressure mechanism while asking labour to pre-surrender protections. That is not “reality testing”; it is leverage.

You still have not shown, site by site, that closures are necessary rather than merely convenient. If the company wants our support, it must put forward:
- a **plant-by-plant industrial case**,
- quantified savings by measure,
- a credible future product allocation,
- and a path to competitiveness that does not start with shutting sites.

If you cannot show that closures are unavoidable, then they are not justified.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I agree with your central point: no closures and no compulsory redundancies must be the **default negotiating frame**, not a reward after concessions.

But I also need to be equally direct: saying “no closures” is not enough by itself. We need a **credible, quantified alternative** that actually closes the economics gap. Otherwise management and investors will keep saying, with some justification, that we are protecting structures rather than the company.

So yes:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- and a temporary, limited employee contribution

are all legitimate tools. But they must be part of a package that preserves the industrial base and keeps German sites viable.

## To the controlling owners
You are right that VW needs a durable reset, not postponement. Where I disagree is your implied conclusion that stronger structural measures must remain in play unless the workforce proves otherwise.

The burden is on those proposing disruptive measures to justify them. A destructive restructuring would damage not only jobs, but the long-term value of Volkswagen itself. Co-determination and regional stability are not obstacles; they are part of the company’s resilience.

If a non-closure package is economically sufficient, then it should be supported. If it is not, then we need to see why — in numbers, not in slogans.

## To institutional investors
You are right to demand quantified, enforceable economics. I support that. What I do not support is the assumption that closures are the benchmark of seriousness.

A serious test is:
- can a negotiated package materially reduce cost,
- remove capacity,
- protect jobs and sites where possible,
- and restore competitiveness without triggering a long conflict?

That is what we should assess.

## My position
Lower Saxony will support a negotiated restructuring only if it includes all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site-by-site industrial and product commitments**
4. **A real savings bridge with quantified measures**
5. **Temporary, limited workforce contributions**
6. **Preservation of co-determination and the state’s role under the VW Law**
7. **A credible path to long-term competitiveness**

What we will **not** support is:
- a package that preserves the threat of closure as a bargaining club,
- one-sided labour-cost dumping,
- or a process that treats regional employment as expendable.

## On the expert question
Yes, a specialist is genuinely needed now.

[SUMMON: an independent site-by-site industrial economics auditor with access to Volkswagen’s German plant data to test whether a closure-free restructuring closes the margin gap, including utilization, fixed-cost absorption, contribution margin, and post-deal competitiveness by site.]

But I would set the mandate clearly:
- test the **non-closure** option first,
- quantify what it would take to make it work,
- and only then determine whether any deeper structural step would ever be necessary.

Not the other way around.

## Bottom line
I am not offering a blank cheque to labour, and I am not offering a blank cheque to management.

I will back a real restructuring that protects the German industrial base, preserves jobs where possible, and restores competitiveness.  
I will not back a process that starts by threatening plant closures and then calls that responsible governance.

If the numbers prove that a closure-free solution is insufficient, then we will have a hard second conversation. But that proof must come first, and it must be credible.
