We share the core concern that the crisis must not be solved on the backs of the workforce alone.

I agree with you on several points:

- **No compulsory redundancies**: that must remain off the table.
- **No unilateral dismantling of the collective framework**: long-standing guarantees cannot simply be torn up.
- **A negotiated solution is necessary**: management cannot impose a fait accompli.
- **Investment and future products matter**: there is no sustainable answer without an industrial perspective.

Where I need to be very clear is this: **a solution that relies on plant closures is not acceptable**. Nor is one that shifts the full burden onto employees through wage cuts, loss of guarantees, and open-ended insecurity while ownership and management escape meaningful contribution.

That said, I am not ignoring the economic reality. We know the cost base must come down and capacity has to be adjusted. The union is prepared to discuss:

- temporary or structured working-time reductions,
- capacity adjustments that avoid closures,
- fair employee contributions,
- waiver or deferral of bonuses,
- and other negotiated savings measures,

**but only if** they are matched by real commitments from management and owners:
- secure German sites,
- no forced layoffs,
- a binding industrial plan,
- and a fair sharing of the burden.

So my message is straightforward: **we are open to a tough settlement, but not to a settlement of layoffs and closures dressed up as restructuring**. If management wants serious cooperation, it needs to put forward a credible alternative that protects jobs, sites, and the collective bargaining framework while delivering real savings.
