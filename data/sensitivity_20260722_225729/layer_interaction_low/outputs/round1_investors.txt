From the perspective of dispersed capital-market shareholders, the central issue is not who “wins” the negotiation, but whether Volkswagen gets back to a credible, durable margin structure.

So I’ll be direct.

I agree with management, and partly with the controlling owners, on the core diagnosis: **the German cost base cannot remain where it is**. If the package leaves the company structurally expensive, then it is not a restructuring — it is delay. That would be unacceptable for us because it would continue the erosion of returns and likely depress the share price further.

Where I disagree with the employee side and Lower Saxony is on the assumption that **site preservation can be treated as the primary objective regardless of the economics**. A blanket “no closures, no structural workforce reduction” position is not credible if the objective is to restore competitiveness. We would not support an outcome that simply protects all existing sites while leaving the company with an uncompetitive fixed-cost base.

That said, I also do **not** want a drawn-out confrontation or a cosmetic settlement. A prolonged strike or half-measure is value-destructive too. The worst outcome from our perspective would be a deal that looks socially balanced but does not materially improve margins, or a conflict that drags on long enough to damage production, customer confidence, and execution.

So our test is straightforward:

- **Does it deliver durable cost reduction?**
- **Does it restore a competitive margin trajectory?**
- **Is it implementable without years of disruption?**

On that basis, I can support a negotiated solution **if and only if** it produces real structural savings. That could include a mix of:
- capacity reductions,
- working-time flexibility,
- labor-cost moderation,
- voluntary workforce reductions,
- and possibly selective site changes,

provided the economics are actually sufficient.

What I will not back:
- a cosmetic agreement with only temporary measures,
- a structure that preserves every legacy guarantee intact,
- or an outcome that leaves Volkswagen’s margins structurally impaired.

So my message to management is: bring a quantified plan, not generalities. And to the workforce side: if you want to avoid closures, then the alternative must still close the cost gap in a way the market can believe. Otherwise, we will support whatever path most credibly restores competitiveness.
