I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with all sides on one essential point: Volkswagen needs a **durable, quantified restructuring**, not rhetoric and not a temporary patch. I also agree that a prolonged confrontation would destroy value.

I agree with management that the current German footprint must become **materially more competitive**.  
I agree with the workers and Lower Saxony that **industrial peace, trust, and implementation certainty** matter.  
And I agree with investors that a compromise that leaves the cost base structurally too high is not acceptable.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the attempt to turn “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” into absolute starting points.

That may be understandable politically, but it is not yet an economically credible restructuring answer.

If the German cost base and capacity are structurally too high, then the company must be able to remove real fixed cost. If the no-closure alternative can do that, fine — show it. If it cannot, then pretending otherwise would be value-destructive.

At the same time, I do **not** support a reckless closure strategy. A drawn-out fight, reputational damage, and governance paralysis would also destroy value. So this cannot become an ideology battle.

## What I need to see
The discussion is still too abstract. Before anyone can responsibly rule in or rule out closures, I want the independent plant-by-plant numbers.

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant industrial accountant to quantify fixed costs, utilization, recurring savings from working-time changes, voluntary exits, shift consolidation, redeployment, management-side cost cuts, and compare net present value against closure economics]

That audit must show, by site:
- fixed cost base
- utilization
- recurring savings from non-closure measures
- one-off transition costs
- and the net present value versus closure or site rationalization

## My position
My position is unchanged in substance:

- I support a **durable cost reset**
- I support a **negotiated solution**
- I support **serious employee contributions**
- I do **not** support a purely defensive no-closure position if it leaves Volkswagen uncompetitive
- I do **not** support cosmetic compromise or prolonged conflict

If the audit shows that a no-closure package really can close the gap, preserve value, and remain durable, then that is the path I would prefer.  
If it does not, then the company cannot be trapped by political comfort words.

That is the standard I will apply.
