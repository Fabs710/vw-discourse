I hear the central point from management: Volkswagen cannot ignore a structural cost problem, and a durable solution must improve competitiveness. I do not dispute that. A merely temporary fix would be irresponsible.

Where I clearly disagree is on the idea that the only credible path runs through plant closures or a weakening of the social settlement in Germany.

From the perspective of Lower Saxony, that would be a serious mistake for three reasons:

1. **It would damage the regional economic anchor role of Volkswagen.**  
   VW is not just any company in Germany. It is central to employment, supplier networks, training, and regional stability. Closing sites here would have consequences far beyond the balance sheet.

2. **It would escalate political and industrial conflict.**  
   Once closures become the starting point, the company risks a confrontation that can destroy trust, delay implementation, and create long-term damage well beyond any near-term savings.

3. **It would ignore the value of negotiated adjustment.**  
   We have a governance structure for exactly this reason: to force a serious search for solutions that preserve competitiveness without breaking the company’s social foundations.

So my position is this:

- **No forced redundancies.**
- **No unilateral dismantling of core job-security arrangements.**
- **No approach that treats plant closures as the default answer.**

At the same time, I do not dismiss the need for contribution from the workforce. If there is a serious industrial plan, I can see room for:
- capacity adjustment through negotiated working-time measures,
- temporary labor-cost moderation,
- voluntary programs,
- redeployment,
- and targeted efficiency measures that preserve sites.

But this must be part of a balanced package. The burden cannot fall almost entirely on employees and regions while other stakeholders simply insist on cuts.

What would move me is a restructuring that:
- preserves German sites where possible,
- secures employment in Lower Saxony,
- keeps the state’s co-determination and veto role intact,
- and puts forward a credible long-term product and investment path.

If management is prepared to discuss a real negotiated restructuring on that basis, then I am willing to engage seriously. If the proposal remains centered on closures and mass job loss, then I cannot support it.
