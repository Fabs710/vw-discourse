### Core impasse
The dispute is no longer mainly about whether Volkswagen needs restructuring. The impasse is about **the restructuring instrument**:

- **Employees / Lower Saxony** want a binding **no-closure, no-forced-layoff** settlement with site security and co-determination preserved.
- **Management / owners / investors** want a **structural cost reset** and will not accept a package that may leave the German footprint materially uncompetitive.
- The **missing bridge** is a **quantified, site-by-site plan** that shows whether non-closure measures can close enough of the gap to make closures unnecessary.

So the core deadlock is:  
**site guarantees versus residual cost gap**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options
Below are three concrete compromise paths that could bridge the most conflicting positions, subject to the audit data.

#### 1) Binding no-closure term with conditional midterm adjustment
A package could include:
- **No German plant closures during the agreement term**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Strong redeployment, voluntary exits, working-time changes, and capacity reductions within sites**
- A **midterm review only on objective, pre-agreed triggers**
- A **hard rule that any review cannot automatically reopen closure rights**, but instead must first exhaust agreed non-closure measures

This would address the workforce’s concern about a hidden countdown while still giving management a structured mechanism if the gap remains large.

#### 2) Site-by-site savings targets with mixed instruments
Instead of debating closures in the abstract, the parties could agree:
- a **quantified savings target per site**
- a **menu of permitted measures**: shift reductions, internal consolidation, voluntary exits, product reallocation, wage moderation, and management cost cuts
- a rule that **closure is excluded unless the auditor shows a site cannot meet a minimum savings threshold after all other measures**

This would shift the discussion from “open vs close” to “can this site reach the target without closure?”

#### 3) Security package paired with stronger structural concessions at the top
A trade package could combine:
- **binding job/security commitments**
- **no forced layoffs**
- **site protection for the agreement term**
with
- **management compensation restraint**
- **owner/investor support for capex**
- **product commitments**
- **faster internal capacity consolidation**
- **wage/bonus moderation and working-time adjustments**

This may be the most viable if the no-closure package is close, but not identical, to closure economics.

### Pointed question to each stakeholder

#### To the Works Council
**What specific quantified savings measures, by plant, would you accept as sufficient to preserve every German site without any closure option being retained?**

#### To IG Metall
**What is the maximum residual cost gap you would accept after non-closure measures before you would consider a site-level adjustment beyond redeployment, voluntary exits, and working-time changes?**

#### To Group & Brand Management
**Will you commit to publishing the plant-by-plant cost and utilization data needed to prove the residual gap before asking for any closure-related concessions?**

#### To Lower Saxony
**Will you use your governance position to support a binding no-closure package if the audit shows it closes the gap through internal capacity measures and product allocation?**

#### To the Controlling Owners
**Would you accept a no-closure settlement if the audit demonstrates that it achieves a comparable net present value to closure, even if the savings come from slower and more distributed measures?**

#### To Institutional Investors
**If the no-closure package delivers most, but not all, of the closure savings, what minimum financial threshold would still make you support it as durable and value-preserving?**

#### To the Expert / Auditor
**What single plant-level metric should the parties treat as decisive for determining whether non-closure restructuring is economically sufficient: fixed cost per unit, utilization, or net present value of savings?**
