We do not disagree that Volkswagen needs a credible industrial future, or that investment in new products and technology to 2030 is essential. On that point, I think we are all facing the same reality: if we do nothing, we lose competitiveness, margins, and ultimately more jobs.

But I need to be equally clear: **binding site guarantees through 2030 with no closures and no structural workforce adjustment is not a solution to the cost crisis**. It is a postponement of the problem. And postponing it would be irresponsible to the company, to the shareholder base, and to the long-term security of employment in Germany.

A few points:

- **We cannot accept a package that leaves the German cost base fundamentally unchanged.**  
  If we keep all sites, keep the same capacity structure, and simply layer temporary measures on top, we do not restore competitiveness. We would be locking in an uncompetitive structure while the market keeps shrinking.

- **No forced redundancies is not the same as no job reduction.**  
  We are prepared to avoid compulsory layoffs if we can secure a durable alternative. But that requires real structural savings: capacity reduction, a meaningful labour-cost contribution, and a lower fixed-cost base. Those are not optional.

- **Plant closures are not a threat for effect; they are part of the real menu of options.**  
  I would prefer a negotiated outcome that achieves the same economic effect through other structural measures. But I will not rule out closures if that is what it takes to reach a sustainable result. The company cannot be asked to carry overcapacity indefinitely.

- **A fair contribution from employees is necessary.**  
  That can take several forms — pay moderation, working-time adjustments, reduced bonuses, changes to legacy guarantees — but it must be material enough to move the economics. Cosmetic concessions will not satisfy the situation.

What I am prepared to discuss is a package that combines:
- durable capacity reductions,
- a substantial labour-cost contribution,
- investment commitments in future products and technology,
- and, if there is a credible path to equivalent savings, alternatives to outright closures.

What I am **not** prepared to accept is a package that says “no closures, no forced layoffs, no change to guarantees,” while expecting management to somehow fix a structural cost gap without structural measures. That would not restore return-on-sales, would not regain investor confidence, and would not be sustainable.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it needs to help produce a settlement that delivers the same economic outcome. Otherwise, we will have to proceed on the basis that the current structure is too expensive to maintain.
