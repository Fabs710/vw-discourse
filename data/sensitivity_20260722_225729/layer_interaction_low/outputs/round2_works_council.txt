Management is still not answering the central point: **you are asking the workforce to carry the restructuring risk while keeping closures and layoffs on the table. That is not a negotiable starting point for us.**

I will be direct.

### Where we agree
- The company needs a **credible industrial plan**.
- **Temporary measures alone** are not enough.
- We need **investment, future products, and a path to 2030**.
- A settlement must be **negotiated**, not imposed.

### Where we clearly disagree
- **German plant closures are not acceptable.**
- **Compulsory redundancies are not acceptable.**
- **Tearing up the historic job-security framework is not acceptable.**
- A restructuring that treats these as normal tools is **not socially responsible and not legitimate** from the workforce’s perspective.

Management says the current structure is too expensive. That may be true, but the answer is **not to threaten sites and jobs as the first resort**. If the economic problem is real, then management, owners, and capital must also make real contributions. The burden cannot be pushed primarily onto the workforce.

### On the other parties
- **IG Metall** is right to insist that no compulsory redundancies and no closures must remain off the table.
- **Lower Saxony** is right that closures would damage the industrial base and trigger a destructive conflict.
- **The controlling owners and investors** keep repeating that savings must be “durable,” but they have not explained why that necessarily requires breaking the social settlement in Germany.

### What would move us
We are prepared to discuss a serious package if it contains all of the following:
- **binding site guarantees through 2030**
- **no operational layoffs**
- **no German plant closures**
- **preservation of co-determination and the job-security framework**
- **a genuine industrial plan with investments and future allocation of products**
- **fair, negotiated employee contributions** only within those protections

That means capacity changes, working-time flexibility, voluntary programs, and other negotiated adjustments can be discussed. But they must be designed to **save jobs and sites**, not prepare closures by another name.

### What we will not accept
- A package built around “selective site changes”
- Any precondition that closures remain an open option
- Any “quantified” plan that is really just a dress rehearsal for forced cuts
- Any deal that leaves employees with all the insecurity and management with all the discretion

So my answer is simple: **bring a restructuring plan that closes the cost gap without closing plants or firing people involuntarily.** If that cannot be done, then the problem is not the workforce’s willingness to cooperate — it is the management strategy.

If management wants a serious discussion, it should put forward a concrete alternative showing:
- where the savings come from,
- how much comes from management/owners versus the workforce,
- what investment commitments are tied to it,
- and how every German site is protected.

Until then, our position remains unchanged.
