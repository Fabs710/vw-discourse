## What the audit must quantify
To determine whether a **no-closure package** can realistically substitute for plant closure economics at Volkswagen, the auditor needs site-by-site data on:

1. **Fixed operating cost per plant**
   - labor overhead
   - maintenance
   - utilities
   - logistics
   - depreciation / site-specific capital charges
   - management and support allocation

2. **Current utilization**
   - installed capacity
   - actual output
   - shift utilization
   - line loading
   - flexibility constraints

3. **Unit cost impact of underutilization**
   - cost per vehicle at current volumes
   - sensitivity to volume loss or transfer
   - break-even volume by site

4. **Savings from non-closure measures**
   - working-time reductions
   - temporary or permanent shift reduction
   - voluntary exits / attrition
   - redeployment
   - wage moderation / bonus moderation
   - productivity measures
   - internal capacity consolidation within the site network

5. **Closure economics**
   - one-time closure costs:
     - severance / early retirement
     - contract termination
     - dismantling
     - remediation
     - transfer costs
   - recurring savings:
     - eliminated fixed cost
     - avoided future capex
     - lower overhead
   - timing:
     - when savings begin
     - how quickly closure costs are recovered

6. **Transition risk**
   - strike risk
   - launch disruption
   - ramp-up risk at receiving plants
   - supplier disruption
   - loss of know-how
   - political / governance delay risk

## How the comparison should be made
The key comparison is not “closure vs no closure” in a rhetorical sense. It is:

### A. No-closure package
- recurring annual savings
- plus one-off transition costs
- minus implementation risk
- at each plant and in total

### B. Closure / site rationalization
- recurring annual savings
- minus closure and transfer costs
- minus delay and disruption risk
- at each plant and in total

The decision should be based on **net present value** over a defined horizon, typically 3 to 7 years for a restructuring case, and longer if strategic footprint effects matter.

---

## What typically matters most in practice
From an industrial restructuring perspective, the decisive question is usually:

### Can labor and capacity measures remove enough fixed cost?
Non-closure measures can be very powerful, but they usually run into a ceiling:
- working-time reductions help if utilization is the issue
- voluntary exits help if headcount is bloated
- shift reductions help if demand is permanently lower
- redeployment helps if there is absorbable internal demand

However, if the site has:
- structurally low utilization,
- high minimum staffing,
- duplicated body-shop / paint / logistics functions,
- or poor product allocation,

then a no-closure package often captures only part of the savings that closure would deliver.

A common outcome is:
- **no-closure package:** meaningful but partial recurring savings
- **closure:** larger recurring savings, but much higher one-time costs and political/implementation risk

---

## Typical uncertainty ranges
Without Volkswagen’s internal site data, I would treat the following as plausible rather than precise:

- **Working-time / shift moderation:** can reduce labor cost modestly to materially, but usually not enough alone to solve a large structural overcapacity problem.
- **Voluntary exits:** useful if there is an aging workforce and overstaffing, but savings are gradual and depend on replacement freezes.
- **Capacity cuts within sites:** often the most important no-closure lever; can be substantial if line consolidation is feasible.
- **Closure:** highest recurring savings, but also highest transition cost and longest implementation risk.

So the likely result is not binary. It is more likely that:
- a **strong no-closure package** can close a significant portion of the gap,
- but whether it fully matches closure economics depends on the size of the residual overcapacity and the plant fixed-cost structure.

## Bottom line
A plant-level auditor should test whether the no-closure package delivers:
1. **enough recurring savings**
2. **fast enough**
3. **with acceptable execution risk**

If it does, closures are not economically necessary.  
If it does not, then closures or deeper site rationalization remain the stronger cost model, even if they are socially and politically harder.
