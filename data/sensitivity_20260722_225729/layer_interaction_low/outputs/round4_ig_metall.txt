I will be direct.

### To management
I agree that we need a quantified, durable industrial plan. I also agree that temporary patchwork is not enough.

But I **do not** agree that you are entitled to keep plant closures and compulsory redundancies on the table as a default bargaining position. That is exactly the pressure point the workforce rejects. If your case for closures is real, then prove it with plant-level numbers after exhausting non-closure measures. Until then, closures are not “necessary”; they are the option you prefer because they shift the burden onto employees.

You also have not answered the fairness question: **why should workers bear the core pain while management, owners, and capital retain their full room to maneuver?** That is not a credible basis for a durable settlement.

### To the works council
I support your red lines: no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, no disguised countdown via review clauses. On that, we are aligned.

Where I would add a note is this: the union must insist on a **serious, quantified alternative package** so that nobody can claim we are only saying no. That means using the auditor to test whether working-time measures, voluntary exits, redeployment, shift consolidation, and capacity reduction within sites can close the gap.

But let me be equally clear: if the audit shows a residual gap, that does **not** automatically justify closures. It means management has to look harder at its own side of the equation too.

### To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your support against closures. But political support is only meaningful if it is used to secure a binding settlement, not a vague promise of later protection.

If you want credibility here, then stand behind:
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no closures during the term,
- binding investment and product allocation,
- and management contributions.

That would be a real contribution to industrial peace.

### To the controlling owners
I hear your demand for a structural reset. I do not dismiss the need for competitiveness.

But I reject the implication that a “real” reset requires making German sites disposable. A settlement that destroys trust, triggers prolonged conflict, and weakens co-determination is not durable value creation; it is short-term cost transfer.

If you want the workforce to contribute, then the owners must accept that they also have obligations:
- restraint on capital outflows,
- binding investment,
- and a willingness to preserve the industrial base instead of treating it as a variable to be cut away.

### To institutional investors
I understand your focus on net present value and recurring savings. That is legitimate. But you are not the only ones bearing the consequences. A deal that looks good on paper but creates industrial conflict and social damage is not automatically value-preserving.

If you want a credible package, then look at the full picture:
- savings,
- execution risk,
- strike risk,
- know-how loss,
- supplier disruption,
- and long-term site stability.

A “cost reset” built on conflict is not a clean solution.

### To the expert / auditor
The audit is necessary, but it must not be used to normalize closures before the no-closure alternative is fully tested.

What I need from the audit is simple:
- site fixed costs,
- utilization,
- savings from working-time changes,
- savings from voluntary exits,
- capacity consolidation potential,
- redeployment potential,
- management-side cost cuts,
- and the residual gap.

And I want the comparison framed correctly:
**Can Volkswagen achieve a durable restructuring without closures and without forced layoffs?**

That is the relevant test.

### IG Metall’s position
We remain open to a fair contribution from employees:
- working-time flexibility,
- bonus moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- internal capacity reductions,
- and negotiated labor-cost moderation.

But only under the conditions that matter:
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no German plant closures**
- **preservation of the collective wage framework**
- **real site and investment security**
- **shared sacrifice from management and owners**

That is not maximalism. That is fairness.

### Bottom line
I am not retreating from the core position.

If management can present a **verified no-closure package** that protects jobs and sites while delivering real savings, we will negotiate it seriously.

If not, then the problem is not that the workforce is refusing reality. The problem is that management is still trying to solve a structural crisis by threatening the people who built the company.
