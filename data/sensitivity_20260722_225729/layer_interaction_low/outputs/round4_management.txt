I will be direct.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I agree on one point: Volkswagen needs a **durable, quantified, negotiated** restructuring plan. A cosmetic fix will not work, and a long conflict would damage everyone.

Where I disagree is on your core red lines.  
A blanket **“no closures, no compulsory redundancies, preserve the current framework unchanged”** is not a restructuring answer. It is a request to keep the cost base as it is and hope competitiveness returns by itself.

That will not restore margins.

You keep asking management to prove that closures are unavoidable. Fair enough — but you have not yet shown that the **no-closure path** can close the structural gap either. Saying “working-time, voluntary exits, redeployment, shift consolidation” is not enough unless you quantify the recurring savings site by site and show that they are durable.

Until then, I cannot accept a package that rules out closure options in advance. That would leave us with an uncompetitive structure and no credible path to 2030.

## To Lower Saxony
I understand the regional concern, and I agree that trust and industrial peace matter. But protecting the region also means protecting Volkswagen’s viability. If we freeze a structurally expensive footprint, we create a bigger problem later.

So I do not accept the idea that political discomfort with closures can override the company’s economic reality. The state can and should support a serious restructuring, but not by demanding that economics be ignored.

## To the controlling owners and investors
I agree with you that durability and value preservation matter. I also agree that a cosmetic compromise would be worse than a hard but real settlement.

Where I need to be clear is this: **durability requires structural savings**. That means fixed cost, overcapacity, and labour cost must come down materially. If the workforce wants to exclude the strongest restructuring tools, then it must present an alternative that is economically equivalent, not merely more palatable.

## On the auditor
The independent plant-by-plant audit is essential, and I support it. But let me define the purpose correctly:

It is not there to prove a closure agenda.  
It is there to test whether the strongest no-closure package is actually sufficient.

That means the audit must compare, by site:
- fixed costs,
- utilization,
- recurring savings from non-closure measures,
- and the net present value against closure economics.

If the no-closure package closes the gap, I will say so.  
If it does not, then closures remain a legitimate part of the restructuring discussion.

[SUMMON: independent plant-by-plant industrial accountant to quantify site fixed costs, utilization, recurring savings from working-time changes, voluntary exits, shift consolidation, redeployment, management-side cost cuts, and compare net present value against closure economics]

## My position
I am not moving off the core requirement:

- **Volkswagen needs a structural, sustainable reduction in cost and capacity**
- **The solution must materially lower labour and fixed costs**
- **A purely defensive no-closure formula is not sufficient**
- **A cosmetic or temporary compromise is unacceptable**

I am open on the instrument. I am not open on the outcome.

If the audit demonstrates that a no-closure package can genuinely deliver the needed savings, then we can discuss that seriously.  
If it cannot, then I will not pretend otherwise just to avoid conflict.

That is where I stand.
