I’ll respond directly.

First, I acknowledge one point that has been made clearly by the works council, IG Metall, Lower Saxony, and even the expert: **temporary measures alone will not solve this problem**. We do need a durable restructuring, not a delay.

But I have to be equally clear on the other side of the equation: **a package that rules out closures, rules out compulsory redundancies, and keeps the historic job-security framework essentially intact does not close the cost gap we are facing.** That is not a restructuring; it is a postponement.

### Where I agree
- A negotiated solution is necessary.
- Investment and future products to 2030 must be part of the package.
- A cosmetic deal would be unacceptable.
- Voluntary exits, working-time flexibility, capacity adjustments, and labor-cost moderation are all legitimate tools.

### Where I disagree
- I do **not** accept that all German plants must remain open regardless of economics.
- I do **not** accept that no operational adjustment can occur.
- I do **not** accept that the workforce can keep legacy guarantees unchanged while the company absorbs a structural cost disadvantage.
- I do **not** accept an arrangement that leaves the fixed-cost base fundamentally uncompetitive.

The expert’s contribution is useful precisely because it confirms what management has been saying: **alternative measures can help, but they are unlikely to fully replicate the savings of structural site rationalization if the overcapacity is real and concentrated.** That is the central issue. If a site is structurally uneconomic, then reducing hours and offering voluntary exits may soften the problem, but they do not eliminate it.

### To the works council and IG Metall
You are both right that no forced redundancies should be the first choice. I am prepared to avoid compulsory layoffs **if** we can secure a durable, quantified package that delivers equivalent savings. But “no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” cannot be unconditional red lines if the resulting deal still leaves Volkswagen uncompetitive.

You say the workforce should not carry the burden alone. I agree. But that cuts both ways: management also cannot carry the burden of preserving every site, every guarantee, and every legacy cost structure. There must be a real contribution from the employee side, and it must be material.

### To Lower Saxony
I understand the political and regional stakes. But as an anchor shareholder, I have to say this plainly: preserving every site at any cost is not a strategy. Volkswagen’s role in the region will not be protected by defending uneconomic structures until the business weakens further. A destructive confrontation would be bad; so would a solution that protects optics but not competitiveness.

### To the controlling owners and investors
I agree with your insistence on durability. However, durability cannot mean a simple insistence on cuts without acknowledging implementation reality. If the package triggers prolonged strikes or becomes politically impossible, it will destroy value too. So the test is not only “does it save enough?” but also “can it actually be implemented?”

### What management needs
We need a **quantified package**, not slogans:
- how much cost reduction comes from labor measures,
- how much from capacity changes,
- how much from voluntary exits,
- and what residual gap remains if closures are ruled out.

If the alternative to closures can genuinely close the gap, then I’m open to it. But if the answer is only partial savings, then we will have to confront the uncomfortable reality that **some form of site rationalization remains on the table**.

### My position now
I am not moving to a “no closures ever” position, and I am not accepting a blanket job-security guarantee through 2030 on the terms stated so far.

What I am prepared to say is this:
- **No compulsory redundancies if a durable alternative exists**
- **No cosmetic compromise**
- **No settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive**
- **A real package must include meaningful workforce contribution and real capacity reduction**

If the other side wants to avoid closures, then it must put forward a proposal that closes the economics, not just the argument. Otherwise, management will not be able to recommend it, and I will not pretend that it solves the problem.
