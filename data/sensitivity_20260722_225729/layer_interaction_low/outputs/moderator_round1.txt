**Moderator Summary – Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree Volkswagen needs a **credible long-term industrial and investment plan**.
- There is broad agreement that a solution must be **negotiated**, not imposed unilaterally.
- Most parties accept that **costs must come down** and that some form of **structural adjustment** is necessary.
- There is shared openness to **non-forced measures** such as working-time adjustments, capacity changes, voluntary programs, labor-cost moderation, and other negotiated savings.
- There is also general agreement that a **cosmetic or temporary fix is not sufficient**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany:**  
  The Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject closures; management says closures remain on the table if needed; shareholders/investors are open to selective site changes if economically necessary.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  The employee side and Lower Saxony reject them; management says no forced layoffs may be possible only if a durable alternative is found, but job reduction remains necessary in some form.
- **Job-security framework:**  
  Employee representatives want existing guarantees preserved; management and capital-side actors argue that legacy protections cannot remain unchanged if the cost base is to be fixed.
- **Extent of structural change:**  
  The employee and political side favor adjustment without breaking the social settlement; management, owners, and investors insist the current structure is too expensive to maintain.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How to close the cost gap** while avoiding or minimizing closures and forced layoffs.
- **Who bears what share of the burden** between workforce, management/owners, and the company.
- **What level of capacity reduction is required** and whether it can be achieved through alternatives to closures.
- **How to balance competitiveness with industrial peace**, given the risk of conflict if red lines are crossed.
- **What concrete investment and product commitments** would justify employee concessions.
- **Whether a quantified alternative exists** that delivers the same economic effect as closures without triggering them.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from general positions to a **quantified restructuring scenario**: cost gap, savings targets, and timing.
- Ask each side to specify **which measures are negotiable, which are non-negotiable, and why**.
- Focus on **equivalent alternatives to closures** and what savings they would have to deliver.
- Clarify the **conditions for job-security commitments** and what structural changes would be accepted in exchange.
- Require a **single integrated package** linking competitiveness, investment, employment, and site decisions.
