## Core impasse

The dispute has now converged on one central break point:

**The workforce-side parties insist that any acceptable restructuring must exclude German plant closures and compulsory redundancies, with binding site and employment guarantees.  
Management, the controlling owners, and institutional investors insist that a durable solution must materially reduce fixed costs and overcapacity, and they are not prepared to rule out closures if savings cannot otherwise be achieved.**

So the impasse is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **whether the needed savings can be achieved without site closure as a structural option**, and if so, **how much burden each side must carry**.

A secondary impasse is **trust and sequencing**:
- the workforce wants guarantees before concessions,
- management and capital want quantified concessions before guarantees,
- and each side fears the other is using ambiguity to preserve leverage.

---

## Facilitator compromise options

### 1) “No closure during the agreement term” with a verified savings test
A possible bridge would be:

- **No German plant closures for the duration of the agreement**  
- **No compulsory redundancies during that period**
- A **quantified restructuring package** using:
  - working-time reductions,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - bonus moderation,
  - capacity and shift adjustments
- An **independent verification mechanism** to test whether the package closes the cost gap
- A **review trigger** only after a defined period and only against agreed financial benchmarks

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It gives the workforce immediate site protection, while giving management and capital a measurable cost-reduction path and an evidence-based review point.

---

### 2) “Mutual conditionality” package: protections in exchange for verified contributions from all sides
A second compromise path would be:

- **Binding site and job-security guarantees through 2030**
- In exchange, the workforce accepts:
  - temporary pay moderation,
  - working-time flexibility,
  - reduced variable pay,
  - voluntary departure schemes
- Management and owners commit to:
  - concrete investment amounts,
  - model allocation to specific sites,
  - overhead reductions,
  - governance restraint on dividends or other capital outflows during the restructuring window
- A **pre-agreed burden-sharing formula** sets out what each side contributes

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It addresses the demand for shared sacrifice and makes protections conditional on visible commitments from management and owners, not just employees.

---

### 3) “Partial capacity reduction only” as the hard middle path
A third compromise would be to exclude full closures for now, but allow:

- **one-shift reductions or line consolidation at selected sites**
- **no operational layoffs**
- **voluntary exits only**
- **product transfers and internal reallocation**
- **a binding investment plan for the retained footprint**

This would aim to achieve **real fixed-cost reduction** without the social and political shock of closures.

**Why this may bridge the gap:**  
It recognizes the management/investor concern about overcapacity while staying within the workforce’s core red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies.
