From the perspective of dispersed institutional shareholders, the only test here is whether the eventual package credibly restores Volkswagen’s risk-adjusted equity value.

Where I agree with management and the controlling owners:
- The diagnosis sounds right: the German cost base is too high, capacity is too large, and margins are not where they need to be.
- A cosmetic deal that preserves structural overcapacity would be value-destructive.
- A durable solution has to improve competitiveness, not just postpone the problem.

Where I agree with the workforce side:
- A one-sided restructuring that loads all the pain onto employees is unlikely to be stable or efficient.
- Industrial peace has real value, especially when it avoids prolonged disruption and execution risk.
- If labour is asked to contribute, that should be tied to a broader package, not just asked in isolation.

Where I disagree with the employee representatives:
- “No plant closures ever” and “no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances” are not acceptable as absolutes if they leave the company with an uncompetitive footprint.
- We will not back a deal that protects every site while leaving the cost base structurally impaired.
- If the alternative measures do not deliver equivalent, durable savings, then they are not a real alternative.

Where I disagree with management if that is the implied fallback:
- Threatening closures without demonstrating that alternatives have been seriously tested is not enough.
- If management wants capital markets support, it must show the numbers: how much cost comes out, when, and with what certainty.
- We do not reward confrontation for its own sake, and we do not reward restructuring plans that create endless uncertainty.

Our position is straightforward:
1. We need a credible, quantified path back to competitive margins.
2. The savings must be durable and enforceable.
3. The solution must avoid prolonged strikes and operational drift.
4. If closures are avoided, the alternative must still close the competitiveness gap in full.
5. If that cannot be done, then the company needs to be honest that deeper capacity rationalisation is unavoidable.

So we are not siding with a “protect everything” approach. Nor are we automatically endorsing closures. We are siding with the outcome that best restores returns: decisive, credible, and economically sufficient restructuring.

If the parties can produce a settlement that hits the margin target with enforceable cost reduction and acceptable execution risk, we will support it. If not, we will treat any cosmetic compromise as a delay, not a solution.
