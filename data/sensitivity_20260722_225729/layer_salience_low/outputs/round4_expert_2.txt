### Independent analytical response

On the evidence in the scenario, a **pure no-closure package can be viable only if it delivers very deep, recurring, enforceable savings and a real reduction in effective capacity**. If it does not, then the company is likely just deferring the footprint problem.

## My threshold view

For a large high-cost auto manufacturer with structurally underutilized domestic plants, I would treat a no-closure deal as credible only if it can close roughly **most of the structural gap** through measures such as:

- durable labor-cost relief
- working-time and shift reductions
- substantial voluntary headcount reduction
- productivity improvements and simplification
- internal redeployment
- reduced planned output at specific sites

If the negotiated package closes only a **partial** share of the gap, it is usually not enough to restore competitiveness on a durable basis.

## Where the line likely is

A no-closure scenario becomes weak when:

- plants remain structurally underutilized
- fixed costs stay too high relative to output
- labor concessions are temporary or reversible
- output/capacity is not reduced enough to match demand
- operating margin still remains materially below target

At that point, **keeping every site open becomes economically similar to preserving excess capacity**.

## What this means for the three scenarios

### 1) Hard restructuring with closures
Most reliable for durable fixed-cost removal.

- **Strengths:** highest certainty of cost and capacity reduction
- **Weaknesses:** highest conflict cost, one-time charges, and execution risk

### 2) Negotiated no-closure package
Potentially workable, but only if concessions are unusually deep.

- **Strengths:** preserves sites, lowers immediate conflict
- **Weaknesses:** may not remove enough structural cost, may preserve overcapacity

### 3) Hybrid conditional package
Often the most realistic bridge.

- **Strengths:** gives labor near-term site security while preserving later correction if targets fail
- **Weaknesses:** can become a delay mechanism unless triggers are real and enforceable

## My bottom line

A **no-closure package is financially viable only if it can deliver near-full structural correction through recurring measures and meaningful capacity release**.  
If it cannot, then **some form of footprint reduction becomes financially hard to avoid**.
