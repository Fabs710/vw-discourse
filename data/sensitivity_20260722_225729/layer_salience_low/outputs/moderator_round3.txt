**Round 3 Moderator Summary — Neutral**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All sides agree Volkswagen has a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a temporary one.
- There is broad agreement that the solution must be **durable**, not cosmetic.
- There is also agreement that the discussion needs to move from slogans to **fully quantified, plant-level scenarios**.
- Most parties accept that **industrial peace, investment in future products, and long-term value** matter.
- There is shared recognition that **voluntary exits, productivity measures, working-time adjustments, and internal redeployment** are relevant tools.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject German plant closures as a starting point; management, owners, and investors say closures must remain available if needed.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labor side rejects them categorically; management, owners, and investors will not rule them out in advance.
- **Burden of proof:**  
  Labor side says management must prove that no-closure options fail; management and investors say the employee side must also prove its red lines do not block necessary savings.
- **Role of governance and politics:**  
  Lower Saxony emphasizes regional responsibility and blocking power; management and investors caution against political protection of the current footprint overriding economic viability.
- **Interpretation of “capacity reduction”:**  
  Labor side is wary that this becomes disguised site erosion; management/owners see meaningful capacity reduction as essential to restoring competitiveness.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How much **fixed-cost reduction** is required, and from which sites/functions?
- Whether savings can be achieved through **labor-cost relief, simplification, and utilization improvements alone**, or whether **footprint reduction** is necessary.
- The balance between **voluntary exits** and **compulsory redundancies** in terms of speed, certainty, conflict risk, and skill retention.
- Whether a **no-closure package** would be structurally sufficient, or merely delay the same restructuring problem.
- How to weigh **industrial peace and regional stability** against the risk of preserving excess capacity and weak margins.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should be built around a **common scenario model** with the same metrics for all options:
- fixed-cost savings,
- utilization impact,
- headcount impact,
- implementation risk,
- conflict cost,
- and margin recovery timeline.

Please compare, on the same basis:
1. **Hard restructuring with closures**
2. **Negotiated no-closure package**
3. **Hybrid conditional package**

The discussion should then focus on which scenario is **economically sufficient, operationally feasible, and socially sustainable**.
