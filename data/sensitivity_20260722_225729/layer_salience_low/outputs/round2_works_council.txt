We have heard the same diagnosis from everyone: the company faces a real structural challenge. On that point, there is no dispute.

But let me be equally clear: **we are not accepting plant closures in Germany, and we are not accepting compulsory redundancies.** Those are not “options to keep on the table” as a matter of convenience. They are a direct attack on the workforce, on trust, and on the co-determination framework that has made this company stable for decades.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.
- Capacity, productivity, and working-time arrangements may need adjustment.
- Labour can contribute if the burden is shared fairly.
- Investment in future products and technologies is essential.
- Industrial peace has value.

### Where I disagree
I strongly reject the idea that the only credible path runs through closures and forced layoffs.

Management says alternatives must be “equivalent” economically. Fine — then put the numbers on the table and prove it. But do not use that as a pretext to threaten sites and jobs first and call it realism later. We have already seen what unilateral pressure looks like, and that is exactly what destroys the basis for negotiation.

To the controlling owners and investors: if you want credibility, then show that you are willing to protect the company’s industrial base instead of treating German plants as disposable cost centres. Long-term value does not come from breaking the workforce and then hoping for peace.

To Lower Saxony: I appreciate your clear rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But support has to be more than words. If the state wants to defend employment and industrial legitimacy, then it must use its blocking power accordingly and not allow management to turn this into a fait accompli.

### What we are prepared to discuss
We are prepared to discuss:
- capacity adjustments that do **not** mean plant closures,
- working-time solutions,
- internal efficiency measures,
- fair workforce contributions,
- and negotiated productivity improvements.

But any such package must include:
1. **Binding guarantees for all German sites through 2030**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **No plant closures**
4. **A real role for co-determination in shaping the restructuring**
5. **Clear investment commitments for future products and technologies**

### What is not on the table
What is not on the table:
- closure of any German plant,
- forced job cuts,
- dismantling of the job-security framework,
- or using “competitiveness” as a slogan to shift all risk onto employees.

### On the next step
If management and the owners want a serious negotiation, then they should present **two or three concrete scenarios** that:
- keep every German plant open,
- exclude compulsory redundancies,
- quantify the savings,
- and show how the burden is shared fairly.

If they cannot do that, then they are not offering a restructuring package — they are offering a demand for surrender.

And that is not something the workforce will accept.
