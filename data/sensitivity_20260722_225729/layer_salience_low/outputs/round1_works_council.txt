The Works Council’s position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies. The workforce did not create this crisis, and it cannot be made to pay for it by losing sites, jobs, and hard-won rights.

Our priorities are:

1. Keep every German plant open.
2. Exclude forced layoffs.
3. Protect co-determination and the existing job-security framework.
4. Secure binding guarantees for sites and employment through 2030.
5. Any restructuring must be negotiated, fair, and reversible where possible.

We are prepared to discuss alternatives that genuinely preserve jobs and sites: capacity adjustments, working-time measures, internally negotiated efficiency gains, and a fair workforce contribution if management shares the burden and provides credible long-term investment commitments. But we will not be moved by threats or by a fait accompli.

If management wants the workforce to help secure the company’s future, then it must come with real guarantees, not just demands for sacrifice.
