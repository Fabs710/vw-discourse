Thank you. I want to be equally clear from the start.

Lower Saxony recognises the seriousness of the company’s situation. We are not denying that Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring, a stronger cost base, and a credible industrial plan for the electric and digital transition. On that point, I agree with management: cosmetic savings will not solve a structural problem.

But I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the default answer, and I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as a first resort. For us, those are not technical details; they would have lasting consequences for employment, the regional economy, and trust in Volkswagen’s co-determined governance.

Where I agree with the workforce representatives:
- The burden cannot fall on employees alone.
- Any contribution from labour must be matched by contributions from management, owners, and the company itself.
- A settlement needs binding investment and future-product commitments, not just cuts.
- Negotiated, reversible measures are preferable to irreversible damage.

Where I disagree:
- I do not think “no change” is a realistic position.
- I do not think every existing structure can be preserved unchanged and still restore competitiveness.
- I do not think the company can simply absorb overcapacity without consequences.

But the line I will draw is this: if restructuring means gutting German plants, stripping out co-determination, or making the state’s role irrelevant, then that is not a balanced solution and will not be acceptable to us. The company’s long-term health depends on social stability and industrial legitimacy, not just accounting reductions.

So if management wants support for a serious package, it must present an alternative that:
1. preserves core sites and employment in Germany,
2. avoids compulsory redundancies,
3. shares the burden fairly,
4. includes credible productivity and capacity adjustments,
5. and secures investment in future technologies and products.

That is the kind of restructuring we can discuss. A plan built around closures and one-sided labour cuts is not a constructive starting point, and I will say so plainly.
