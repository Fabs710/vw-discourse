The core impasse is now clear:

## Core impasse
There are **two non-negotiable logics colliding**:

1. **Employee-side red lines**  
   Works Council and IG Metall reject:
   - German plant closures
   - compulsory redundancies

2. **Management/owner/investor requirement**  
   Management, controlling owners, and institutional investors insist that:
   - structural costs must come down materially
   - capacity must be reduced
   - any deal must be economically equivalent to closures if closures are excluded

So the deadlock is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **whether the needed savings can be achieved without closures or forced layoffs, and who must provide proof of that**.

A second, related impasse is about **sequence**:
- labor wants binding site and job guarantees first, then numbers;
- management wants economic flexibility first, then commitments.

## Facilitator proposals: possible compromise positions

### 1) Conditional site-security package
A possible bridge is:
- **no immediate plant closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies during an initial period**
- in exchange for:
  - working-time reductions
  - wage moderation / temporary labor-cost relief
  - productivity commitments
  - voluntary exit and transfer programs
  - management and owner investment commitments

This would be paired with **review milestones** at fixed dates.  
If savings targets are missed, pre-agreed contingency measures would activate.

Why it may bridge the gap:
- gives labor site protection now
- gives management enforceable cost reduction tools
- preserves the possibility of structural correction later without immediate closure

### 2) Hybrid restructuring with targeted footprint adjustment
A second compromise is:
- **keep all German sites open for now**
- but allow:
  - model reallocation
  - shift reductions
  - partial line consolidation
  - internal transfer pools
  - early retirement and voluntary departure programs
  - capped headcount reduction through attrition

In return:
- the company would commit to **no compulsory redundancies**
- and would only revisit site-level changes after a jointly reviewed cost test

Why it may bridge the gap:
- management gets real capacity and cost reduction
- labor avoids immediate closures and forced layoffs
- the burden is spread across several instruments rather than one blunt one

### 3) Quantified scenario contest with binding arbitration trigger
A third compromise is procedural:
- each side must submit **one fully quantified restructuring scenario**
- an independent expert validates them using the same criteria:
  - savings
  - capacity reduction
  - employment impact
  - site impact
  - implementability
- if neither side’s package closes the gap, a **pre-agreed arbitration or mediation stage** is triggered

Why it may bridge the gap:
- moves the dispute from assertion to numbers
- forces comparability
- creates a path out of repeated positional statements

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To the Works Council
If plant closures and compulsory redundancies remain excluded, **which exact combination of working-time, wage, productivity, and capacity measures will close the quantified cost gap without shifting the burden onto future investment?**

### To IG Metall
What concrete sacrifice package can the union commit to—on wages, flexibility, or work rules—that is large enough to be economically credible, yet still compatible with your red lines?

### To Group & Brand Management
Will management publish a plant-by-plant, scenario-by-scenario savings bridge showing the minimum savings required and the exact point at which alternatives become insufficient, so the other parties can test your claim?

### To Lower Saxony
If you reject closures and forced redundancies, **what enforceable mechanism will you support to ensure the restructuring is still financially credible and not merely postponed?**

### To the Controlling Owners
Are you prepared to back a conditional no-closure package if it includes independently verified savings, investment commitments, and a time-limited review clause?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you support a negotiated alternative if it produces a verified risk-adjusted NPV comparable to closures, even if the implementation path is slower and politically more complex?

### To the Expert Analyst
What minimum combination of labor-cost relief and capacity reduction would you regard as the threshold for a viable no-closure scenario, and where is the line beyond which closures become financially unavoidable?

## Procedural next step
A practical next move would be to request **three fully costed scenarios** using a common template:
1. hard restructuring
2. negotiated no-closure package
3. hybrid conditional package

Each should show:
- cash savings
- one-time restructuring cost
- employment impact
- site impact
- timing
- implementation risk

That would turn the dispute from principle-vs-principle into a structured comparison.
