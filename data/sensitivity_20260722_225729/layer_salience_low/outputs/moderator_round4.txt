### 1) What has been agreed
There is broad agreement that Volkswagen faces a **structural competitiveness problem** and that a **durable, not cosmetic, solution** is needed. Most parties also agree on the usefulness of:
- plant-by-plant quantified scenarios,
- productivity improvements,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- internal redeployment/retraining,
- and a serious comparison of alternatives.

There is also shared recognition that **industrial peace has value**, though parties weigh that value differently.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
The main unresolved issue is whether **German plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** must remain available as restructuring tools.

- **Works Council / IG Metall / Lower Saxony**: reject closures and forced redundancies as acceptable outcomes, and want a no-closure solution proven first.
- **Management / Controlling Owners / Institutional Investors**: say those instruments must remain available if needed to restore competitiveness.
- **Lower Saxony**: supports negotiated restructuring but insists on protecting the regional industrial base.
- **Expert**: says a no-closure solution may be viable, but only if the savings gap is not too large and the package delivers deep, durable, enforceable effects.

A second disagreement is about **burden of proof**: whether management must first prove that no-closure options fail, or whether the employee side must prove that its red lines can still produce sufficient savings.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
Several trade-offs remain unresolved:
- **Competitiveness vs. site/job protection**
- **Speed and certainty of cost reduction vs. conflict, trust, and implementation risk**
- **Permanent structural savings vs. temporary or reversible concessions**
- **Capacity reduction vs. preserving all sites**
- **Political/regional stability vs. purely economic restructuring logic**

The discussion still lacks a shared, fully quantified answer on whether a no-closure package can close the gap at the required scale.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
The next round should stay strictly on **comparative, quantified scenario testing**. The key task is to compare, on the same basis:
1. no-closure / no-forced-layoff package,
2. hybrid package without closures,
3. closure-based restructuring.

Each scenario should be tested for:
- fixed-cost savings,
- utilization effects,
- headcount impact,
- implementation risk,
- conflict cost,
- and margin recovery timeline.

The discussion would benefit from a clear statement of the **minimum savings and capacity reduction** needed for viability, and from identifying which measures are **recurring and enforceable** versus temporary or symbolic.
