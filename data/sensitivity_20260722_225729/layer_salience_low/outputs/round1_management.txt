Let me be direct: I hear the insistence on protecting every site and excluding compulsory redundancies, but I do **not** accept that as a starting line for this restructuring.

We are in a structural crisis, not a temporary downturn. Volkswagen’s German cost base is too high, capacity is too large for the market we actually face, and the current path is not compatible with restoring the brand to a durable return on sales. If we settle for a cosmetic arrangement that preserves too much overcapacity, we will simply postpone the problem and weaken the company further.

Where I **agree**:
- We need a durable solution, not a short-term patch.
- A workforce contribution can and should be part of the package.
- Investment, future products, and a credible industrial plan matter.
- Negotiation is necessary if we want a settlement that can actually be implemented.

Where I **disagree sharply**:
- I cannot commit to keeping every German plant open.
- I cannot accept the idea that compulsory redundancies are automatically off the table.
- I cannot preserve the existing cost structure and still claim we have solved the crisis.

The reality is that we need a **structural and sustainable reduction in cost and capacity**. That means real choices, not just internal savings and hopeful promises. If alternative measures can deliver the same durable effect — including meaningful labour-cost relief, capacity reduction, and binding productivity improvements — then I am prepared to consider them. But they must be **credible, enforceable, and large enough** to restore competitiveness. Anything less is not a solution.

So my position is this:
1. The status quo is not acceptable.
2. We need substantial cost and capacity reductions.
3. Labour must make a material contribution.
4. Investment commitments must be tied to real restructuring, not used as a substitute for it.
5. I am open on the exact route, but not on the necessity of deep change.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures and forced layoffs, then it has to put forward a package that delivers equivalent economic effect. Until then, I will continue to argue for the full range of restructuring options, including plant closures.
