We agree on the diagnosis, but not on the conclusion.

Yes, Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring. Yes, the current cost base and capacity situation must be addressed. And yes, a workforce contribution can be part of the package. That is exactly why IG Metall already put a counter-proposal on the table and why we are not rejecting change as such.

But I need to be explicit: **we do not accept plant closures in Germany, and we do not accept compulsory redundancies.** Not as a “temporary bargaining position,” not as leverage, and not as an open-ended option. Those measures would shift the burden onto employees alone and destroy the trust needed for any durable settlement.

### Where I agree with management, owners, investors
- The problem is structural, not cosmetic.
- Savings must be durable and enforceable.
- A serious package must include capacity and productivity changes.
- Investment and future products matter.
- Industrial peace has value and should not be wasted.

### Where I disagree
What I reject is the assumption that “structural” automatically means closures and forced layoffs. That is a choice, not a law of nature.

If management wants to argue that alternatives are insufficient, then it must show the numbers transparently:
- how much savings are required,
- where they come from,
- by when they are realised,
- and what the long-term effect is on employment and sites.

Until then, “closures may be needed” is not a solution; it is a threat.

### What we are prepared to put on the table
We are prepared to negotiate a serious shared-sacrifice package, including:
- working-time adjustments,
- capacity reduction without plant closures,
- internal efficiency measures,
- fair employee financial contributions,
- and temporary restraint on wage components if this is balanced by binding job and site security.

But that only works if the burden is shared fairly.

That means:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No German plant closures**
3. **Binding guarantees for sites and employment through 2030**
4. **Protection of the collective bargaining framework**
5. **Credible investment commitments from management and owners**

### Response to Lower Saxony
I appreciate your clear rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But support also has to be active. If the state is serious about protecting industrial legitimacy, then it should use its blocking position to prevent a one-sided solution. Words about social stability are not enough if the final package still makes workers pay for management failure.

### Response to the controlling owners and investors
If you want long-term value, then do not confuse value creation with workforce pressure. A settlement that breaks trust, triggers prolonged conflict, and leaves employees insecure is not a stable value solution. We will not accept a deal that protects margins by hollowing out the company’s social foundation.

### Response to management
If you want a negotiated solution, stop treating closures and forced redundancies as the benchmark. Bring forward a concrete alternative package with:
- quantified savings,
- site protection,
- no compulsory layoffs,
- and real management and owner contributions.

If you do that, we can negotiate seriously. If not, then the conflict will continue — and our members have already shown they are willing to act.

### Our minimum acceptable line
A deal is only viable if it includes:
- no plant closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- a fair workforce contribution,
- management/owner contributions too,
- and binding guarantees for jobs, sites, and future investment.

Anything that fails that test is not a compromise; it is a demand that employees absorb the crisis alone. We will not agree to that.
