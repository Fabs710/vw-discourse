I will be direct.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, institutional investors, and Lower Saxony on the core diagnosis: Volkswagen has a **structural competitiveness problem**, not a cyclical one. The cost base, capacity footprint, and margin profile in Germany are not where they need to be. If we avoid hard decisions entirely, we will erode long-term value, which I cannot support.

I also agree with the employee side on one important point: a one-sided solution would be unstable and therefore value-destructive. If the restructuring produces prolonged conflict, repeated strikes, and reputational damage, then whatever savings are achieved will be partially or fully offset. Industrial peace matters.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position that **no German plant closures** and **no compulsory redundancies** can be treated as absolute preconditions before the economics are tested.

That is not a restructuring plan; it is a veto line. If the company needs deeper structural relief than the no-closure path can provide, then ruling out the relevant tools in advance is not responsible stewardship. It may be understandable from a labour perspective, but it does not solve Volkswagen’s problem.

I also do **not** accept the idea that preserving every current site and structure is automatically the same as preserving long-term employment. Sometimes refusing to adjust the footprint faster just means a larger value destruction later. That would hurt the workforce, the company, and the owners.

### On the works council and IG Metall
You are right to insist that workers should not carry the burden alone. I support shared sacrifice. But so far, what you have offered is a **defensive line**, not a fully quantified substitute for deep restructuring.

If you want closures and compulsory redundancies off the table, then you need to show, in hard numbers, how the full gap is closed through:
- labor-cost relief,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- capacity reductions with real economic effect,
- voluntary exits,
- and operational simplification.

I have not yet seen proof that this can be done at the scale Volkswagen needs while keeping all German sites intact. Until I do, I will not endorse a package that simply preserves the status quo under another name.

### On management
Management is right that we need substantial structural change. I support a serious scenario comparison.

But I also want discipline: do not use closure language as a reflex if there is not yet a tested alternative. A threat is not a plan. I want the comparison to be real, plant-by-plant, and quantified, not just a bargaining posture.

### On Lower Saxony
I respect the concern for the regional industrial base. It is legitimate. But blocking power cannot become a guarantee that no hard choice will ever be made. If the company’s economics do not work, political protection of the existing footprint will only postpone the damage.

So I would say this plainly: I will support preserving viable German production if, and only if, it is backed by a package that restores competitiveness. I will not support protecting every site regardless of economics.

### On investors
You are right that cosmetic compromise would destroy value. I agree. But I also caution against thinking that aggressive restructuring is automatically value-maximizing if it triggers prolonged industrial conflict. Value is not created by force alone.

### What would move me
I can be moved by a package that is:
- **quantified**
- **durable**
- **enforceable**
- and clearly capable of restoring margins and competitiveness

That means I need to see:
1. the gap to be closed,
2. the cost bridge by scenario,
3. the timing of savings,
4. the implementation risk,
5. and the conflict cost of each route.

If a no-closure package can prove equivalence, I will consider it. If it cannot, then closures and stronger workforce reductions remain admissible tools.

### My position, plainly
I am not here to preserve the status quo.  
I am here to preserve the long-term value of the enterprise.

So:
- I will not endorse a cosmetic deal.
- I will not endorse a one-sided burden on labor.
- I will not rule out deep structural measures in advance.
