I will engage directly.

First: I agree with everyone on the basic diagnosis. Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic fix. The company must restore competitiveness, protect its ability to invest, and address the structural cost issue. That part is not in dispute.

But I must be equally clear: I do **not** accept plant closures in Germany as the default answer, and I do **not** accept compulsory redundancies as an acceptable first route. For Lower Saxony, those are not bargaining decorations. They would have real and lasting consequences for employment, for the regional economy, and for trust in Volkswagen’s governance.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, owners, and investors that:
- the cost base has to improve,
- capacity has to be adjusted somehow,
- and a purely cosmetic deal would be irresponsible.

I also agree with the workforce side that:
- employees cannot be the only ones carrying the burden,
- industrial peace matters,
- and any package must include credible future investment.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with management when it speaks as if closures and forced layoffs are the natural benchmark. They are not the only serious instruments. They are irreversible instruments, and irreversibility matters. If alternatives can achieve a comparable economic effect, then the burden of proof is on management to show why those alternatives are insufficient.

I disagree with the controlling owners and investors if “competitiveness” is treated as a license to weaken the company’s industrial base in Germany without regard to the long-term political and social consequences. Value is not protected by detonating trust.

And I disagree with the employee side insofar as it implies that the full burden can simply be refused without offering a quantified, credible alternative package. Saying “no” is not enough if the company genuinely needs structural relief.

### My position
Lower Saxony’s position remains:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies as the preferred route**
3. **Yes to negotiated restructuring**
4. **Yes to real capacity, productivity, and working-time adjustments**
5. **Yes to a fair workforce contribution if management, owners, and the company also contribute**
6. **Yes to binding investment and future-product commitments**

### What would move us
What could genuinely move Lower Saxony is a package that:
- preserves core sites in Germany,
- avoids forced layoffs,
- delivers real and durable savings,
- and gives credible investment and product commitments for the future.

That is the standard. Not slogans about realism, and not threats dressed up as economics.

### To management
If you want support, bring a fully quantified package that shows:
- how much cost is removed,
- from where,
- by when,
- and how it avoids unnecessary damage to sites and jobs.

If that package exists without closures, then present it. If it does not, then we should be honest that the restructuring design is still incomplete.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I hear your demand for binding guarantees and I understand the political weight of that demand. But guarantees will only be sustainable if the company’s economics are sustainable too. So I will support a negotiated protection framework only if it is paired with a credible restructuring that does not simply postpone the crisis.

### To the controlling owners
You are right that Volkswagen cannot live indefinitely with an uncompetitive cost base. But you should also recognise that Volkswagen is not just a financial asset. It is a regional industrial anchor. A solution that damages that role would be strategically short-sighted.

### Practical next step
I support the moderator’s suggestion to compare **fully specified scenarios**. But those scenarios must not be framed as “closures versus collapse.” I want at least one serious scenario that:
- keeps German sites open,
- excludes compulsory redundancies,
- includes capacity adjustment and productivity gains,
- and quantifies the required labour and management contributions.

If management cannot produce such a scenario, then it has not yet shown that closures are necessary — only that it prefers them.

So my message is simple: Lower Saxony is open to hard restructuring, but not to a restructuring that sacrifices German plants and jobs as the price of managerial convenience.
