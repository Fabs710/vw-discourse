I will be direct.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the expert, investors, and Lower Saxony on the core diagnosis: Volkswagen has a **structural competitiveness problem**. This is not a temporary dip, and cosmetic measures will not restore margins.

I also agree that:
- a durable solution is needed,
- industrial peace has value,
- future-product investment must be protected,
- and we need **plant-level, quantified scenarios** rather than slogans.

That said, agreement on the diagnosis does **not** mean agreement on the remedy.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the employee-side position that:
- **no German plant closures**, and
- **no compulsory redundancies**

can be treated as absolute preconditions before the economics are tested.

That is not a restructuring plan. It is a veto line. If the company requires deeper structural correction than a no-closure package can deliver, then ruling out the relevant instruments in advance is not responsible stewardship.

At the same time, I do **not** accept the view that closures are automatically the answer if they appear in a model. A closure-based solution that triggers prolonged conflict, reputational damage, and industrial instability can destroy value almost as effectively as inaction.

So my position is not “closures first.”  
My position is: **show me the numbers, and show me which path truly restores competitiveness durably.**

## To the Works Council and IG Metall
You keep saying closures and forced redundancies are unacceptable. I understand that politically and socially.

But you still have not shown, in hard terms, how a no-closure package:
- closes the fixed-cost gap,
- restores utilization,
- and delivers durable margin improvement

without simply postponing the same problem.

Your proposed tools — working-time changes, productivity gains, voluntary exits, internal transfers, simplification — are all useful. The question is whether they are **sufficient at the scale required**. So far, that has not been proven.

I also need to be frank: “capacity reduction” cannot just mean shifting some volume around while every site remains protected. If the plants stay structurally underutilized, then the problem remains hidden, not solved.

## To management
I support your demand for a serious structural reset, but I am not comfortable with closure rhetoric being used as a reflex before the alternatives are properly tested.

You want flexibility. Fine. But flexibility without a quantified comparison is just leverage.

If you believe the no-closure path fails, then bring a **plant-by-plant savings bridge** and show:
- fixed-cost savings,
- utilization effects,
- headcount implications,
- one-time restructuring costs,
- implementation risk,
- and conflict cost.

Not a headline. Not a warning. A hard comparison.

## To Lower Saxony
I respect the state’s role and the regional consequences. But political blocking power cannot become a guarantee that no hard decision is ever taken.

If the economics do not work, protecting every existing structure indefinitely only delays the damage. That would not be stewardship.

What I can support is a package that preserves **viable** German production because it restores competitiveness — not because it avoids difficult decisions.

## To Institutional Investors
You are right that a cosmetic compromise would destroy value. I agree.

But value is also destroyed if restructuring is pursued in a way that triggers prolonged industrial conflict and leaves the company permanently weakened operationally. Value is not created by force alone.

So I would say this clearly: I am open to a tougher restructuring route **if it is proven necessary**. I am not open to a deal that preserves the current footprint by simply loading the adjustment onto future margins.

## What would move me
I can be moved by a package that is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- enforceable,
- and capable of restoring margins and competitiveness.

That requires a comparison of at least three scenarios:
1. **Hard restructuring**
2. **Negotiated no-closure package**
3. **Hybrid conditional package**

And each must be assessed on the same basis:
- fixed-cost savings,
- utilization impact,
- headcount impact,
- implementation risk,
- conflict cost,
- and margin recovery timeline.

## My bottom line
I will not support a cosmetic settlement.

I will not support a one-sided burden on labor.

And I will not rule out deep structural measures in advance.

If a no-closure package can prove that it restores Volkswagen’s competitiveness with sufficient durability, then it deserves serious consideration. If it cannot, then tougher measures remain admissible because the long-term value of the enterprise comes first.
