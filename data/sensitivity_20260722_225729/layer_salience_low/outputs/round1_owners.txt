I will be equally direct.

I agree with management on the core diagnosis: Volkswagen cannot defend an uncompetitive cost base, persistent overcapacity, and weak margins in a much harsher market. If the group’s German operations do not become materially more competitive, then we are not protecting jobs in the long run — we are just delaying a larger erosion of value.

At the same time, I do **not** support a destructive, one-sided restructuring that permanently damages the enterprise or turns the company into a battlefield. As controlling owners, our interest is long-term value, industrial strength, and governance stability. That means we need a solution that is economically real, socially durable, and implementable.

Where I agree with the employee side and Lower Saxony:
- A durable solution is needed, not a short-lived headline.
- The burden cannot fall on employees alone.
- Investment and future products matter.
- Industrial peace has real value.

Where I disagree:
- “No closures ever” and “no compulsory redundancies under any circumstances” are not credible positions if the economics do not work.
- Co-determination is not a veto on reality; it is a framework for negotiated restructuring.
- Job guarantees through 2030 are only meaningful if they are tied to a viable cost and capacity settlement.

My position is this:
1. Volkswagen needs a substantial and durable improvement in competitiveness.
2. Any solution must materially reduce structural costs and capacity.
3. Workforce contributions are legitimate, but they must be part of a balanced package.
4. I am open to alternatives to closures and forced layoffs only if they deliver equivalent economic effect and are enforceable.
5. I will not support an outcome that preserves overcapacity, weak margins, and future value erosion simply to avoid conflict.

So I am **not** endorsing plant closures as an ideology, but I am also **not** prepared to rule them out in advance if no credible alternative delivers the required turnaround.

What I would regard as a serious path forward is a negotiated package that includes:
- meaningful capacity reduction,
- substantial labour-cost relief,
- productivity and working-time measures,
- clear investment commitments,
- and a credible route to restore margins without endless confrontation.

What I will not support is a settlement that buys peace today by sacrificing the company’s competitiveness tomorrow.
