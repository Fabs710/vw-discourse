**Moderator summary — Round 1**

### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that Volkswagen faces a **serious structural problem**, not just a short-term downturn.
- There is broad agreement that a **durable solution** is needed, not a cosmetic or temporary fix.
- Most parties accept that some mix of **capacity adjustment, productivity improvements, working-time measures, and labour-cost relief** may be part of the answer.
- There is shared recognition that **investment in future products and technologies** matters and that industrial peace has value.
- There is general openness to **negotiation**, provided the outcome is credible and enforceable.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures in Germany**
  - Works Council, IG Metall, and Lower Saxony reject them as a starting point.
  - Management, controlling owners, and investors say they cannot rule them out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Compulsory redundancies**
  - Employee representatives and Lower Saxony reject them.
  - Management, owners, and investors will not exclude them in advance.
- **Scope of restructuring**
  - Management, owners, and investors want **substantial structural cost and capacity reduction**.
  - Employee representatives want any change to be negotiated, limited, and paired with strong protections.
- **Job and site guarantees**
  - Employee side seeks binding guarantees through 2030.
  - Other parties say such guarantees must be conditional on economic viability and a workable restructuring package.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to achieve **meaningful cost and capacity reduction** without plant closures or forced layoffs.
- What level of **labour contribution** is acceptable, and what corresponding commitments management, owners, and the company will make.
- Which measures are **reversible** versus irreversible, and how risk is shared if market conditions worsen.
- What specific **investment commitments** and future-product assurances can be made in exchange for workforce concessions.
- How to reconcile **industrial peace and co-determination** with the need for decisive restructuring.
- What the **quantified economic target** is: how much savings, by when, and with what degree of certainty.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
Please move from principles to **specific package options**. Each side should identify:
- the minimum acceptable elements of a deal,
- the maximum concession it could make,
- and the concrete measures it is willing to place on the table.

A useful next step would be to compare **two or three complete restructuring scenarios** against the same criteria: cost impact, employment impact, site impact, investment commitments, and implementability.
