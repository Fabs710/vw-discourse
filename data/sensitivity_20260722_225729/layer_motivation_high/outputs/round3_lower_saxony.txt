Lower Saxony’s position remains clear: **we support a durable restructuring, but not at the price of German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or the erosion of co-determination.**

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the owners, and the investors on one essential point: **delay is not an option**. Volkswagen must become more competitive, and the cost base must come down. I also accept the expert’s point that this is ultimately a numbers question, not a slogan contest.

I further agree that:
- temporary measures alone are insufficient,
- the company needs durable savings,
- and any credible plan must be tied to future product and investment commitments.

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the leap from “durable restructuring is necessary” to “therefore closures and forced layoffs must remain on the table as a default.”

That is too blunt, too risky, and too damaging to the regional and industrial base. It also understates the strategic cost of a confrontation that would weaken trust, damage supplier networks, and leave Volkswagen poorer as an industrial system even if a spreadsheet looks cleaner.

## To management
You are right to insist on numbers. But you have not yet proven that a non-closure package cannot close the gap.

So my position is:
- If you want to argue for closures, then bring the site-by-site evidence.
- Show the fixed-cost burden.
- Show why internal reallocation, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, and product shifts are insufficient.
- Show why the non-closure package cannot make the footprint viable.

Until then, closures are **not** justified; they are only asserted.

And I will say this plainly: **a blocking minority and co-determination are not decorative governance features.** They exist precisely to prevent destructive decisions that ignore the long-term public and regional interest.

## To the works council and IG Metall
I support your refusal of compulsory redundancies and your insistence on negotiated restructuring. But I also have to say: simply repeating “keep every plant open” is not yet a full economic answer.

The expert is right that the decisive issue is whether non-closure measures can produce enough durable savings. That means your side must also be prepared to engage with:
- real capacity adjustment,
- major working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits on a meaningful scale,
- redeployment and retraining,
- and a serious labour-cost contribution.

Not because workers caused this crisis. They did not. But because a stable settlement must be economically credible.

## To the controlling owners and investors
You are right that Volkswagen must remain competitive. But competitiveness is not protected by treating the German base as disposable.

I do not accept a logic where the company is “de-risked” by weakening the very industrial foundation that makes Volkswagen Volkswagen. If the adjustment destroys trust, capability, and long-term social stability, then it is not value-preserving. It is a strategic weakening.

## What Lower Saxony would support
We are prepared to support a tough but negotiated package if it includes all of the following:
1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Material capacity reduction through internal reallocation and flexibility**
4. **A negotiated employee contribution**
5. **Binding product and investment commitments for German sites**
6. **Real co-determination in implementation**

That is the route to a durable settlement. Not closure threats dressed up as realism.

## On the expert advice
The expert is right to demand site-by-site economics. I support that analysis.

But I want to be equally clear about the political implication of that analysis: it must not be used as a pretext to rush into closures before genuine alternatives have been tested. The default must be negotiated restructuring, not shutdown.

## Bottom line
Lower Saxony will not support a closure-led restructuring as the starting point.

We are open to a serious, quantified package that:
- protects jobs,
- keeps sites in Germany viable,
- shares the burden fairly,
- and restores competitiveness over the long term.

But if the choice is between short-term managerial convenience and the long-term industrial strength of Volkswagen in Germany, we will stand with the latter.
