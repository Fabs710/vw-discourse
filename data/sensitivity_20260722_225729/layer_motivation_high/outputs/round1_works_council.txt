As the Works Council, our position is clear:

We will not accept plant closures in Germany, and we will not accept compulsory redundancies.

Volkswagen’s workforce did not create this crisis alone, and it cannot be made to pay for it alone. If management wants a viable restructuring, then it must be built on binding guarantees for all German sites, protection of jobs, and a credible industrial plan for the long term. Anything else would mean breaking the historic job-security commitment and handing workers the cost of a strategy they did not design.

What we are prepared to discuss:
- Capacity adjustments and efficiency measures that keep all plants open
- Working-time solutions and negotiated flexibility
- Temporary and voluntary instruments instead of forced layoffs
- Real investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany
- Binding guarantees on employment and site security through 2030
- A genuine co-determination role for the workforce in shaping the restructuring

What we reject:
- Any plan that closes a German plant
- Any operational layoffs
- Any unilateral fait accompli from management
- Any restructuring that destroys trust while demanding “partnership” from workers

If management is serious about a durable solution, it must come to the table with a plan that preserves every German site and gives the workforce a secure future. If not, it should expect fierce resistance from the works council and the workforce.
