I’ll respond directly.

## First: what I agree with

I agree with everyone on one basic point: **Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not delay**.  
The cost base is too high, European demand is weak, competition is intensifying, and the brand cannot hit its return-on-sales target without a structural reset. On that, there is no disagreement.

I also agree that the company needs:
- investment in future products and technology,
- a credible industrial plan for Germany,
- and a settlement that is durable rather than cosmetic.

So yes: **a simple patch-up deal is not acceptable**.

## Where I disagree — strongly

What I do **not** accept is the idea that structural change must exclude closures, compulsory redundancies, or meaningful labour-cost adjustment.

The works council and IG Metall keep repeating “keep every German plant open, no compulsory redundancies, binding guarantees through 2030.” I understand the political logic. But economically, that is not yet a solution. It is a demand that management somehow fixes an overcapacity problem **without removing enough capacity or cost**.

That does not restore competitiveness. It preserves the problem.

## To the Works Council

You ask: “Why must structural change mean plant closures and the end of job security?”  
It doesn’t have to mean that in every case. But it **does** have to mean one thing: **the cost and capacity base must come down enough to make the business viable**.

If you can show a package that achieves the same structural savings without closures, then I am open to it. But so far, what I hear is:
- keep all sites open,
- keep guarantees,
- avoid compulsory redundancies,
- and somehow still achieve the savings.

That is not a quantified alternative. It is a hope.

So let me be explicit:

- **No plant closures** is not a credible starting assumption.
- **No compulsory redundancies** is not guaranteed if the economics do not work.
- **Binding job security through 2030** cannot be unconditional if it blocks the necessary restructuring.

If you want to avoid closures, then you need to bring something equivalent in financial effect:
- major shift reductions,
- substantial redeployment,
- significant working-time changes,
- voluntary exits on a scale that actually matters,
- and a real labour-cost contribution.

Not slogans. Numbers.

## To IG Metall

You say you are open to temporary wage restraint, working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, and some reduced guarantees — if matched by binding protections and investment. That is at least closer to a serious discussion.

But I need to be clear: **temporary measures alone are insufficient**.  
A one-time concession, a deferred payment, or a short-term working-time cut does not solve a structural cost gap.

If the package leaves fixed costs too high, then in two years we are back in the same crisis, only with less credibility and less room to manoeuvre.

So if labour wants to avoid closures, then the labour contribution must be:
- **material**,
- **durable**,
- and **economically measurable**.

That means wage moderation, flexibility, revised guarantees, and exit instruments that are large enough to move the cost base.

## To Lower Saxony

I respect the role of Lower Saxony as anchor shareholder and political stakeholder. But I reject the idea that your role is to pre-emptively rule out the hard choices.

A governance veto cannot become a veto against reality.

If the company’s economics require a smaller, leaner footprint, then blocking that indefinitely is not stewardship — it is postponing the adjustment until the damage is larger. That would ultimately weaken the employment base you say you want to protect.

I agree that the solution must be socially responsible.  
I do **not** agree that “socially responsible” means “nothing truly changes.”

## To the controlling owners

You are right on one point: a headline closure strategy is not enough by itself.  
Closures are only justified if they are part of a quantitatively sufficient package.

But the same applies in reverse: if you rule closures out entirely, then you have to accept that the alternative package must still produce the same structural savings. I do not hear that yet.

So yes, I am open to alternatives to closures.  
No, I am not open to an alternative that merely softens the optics while leaving the cost base uncompetitive.

## What management requires

Let me state the conditions plainly.

We need a package that delivers:

1. **Durable capacity reduction**  
   Enough to remove structural overcapacity, whether by closures or equivalent measures.

2. **Material labour-cost relief**  
   Not symbolic. Not temporary. Real effect on the cost base.

3. **A credible path to competitiveness by 2030**  
   So we can fund future products, software, EV transition, and industrial investment.

4. **A negotiated settlement**  
   Because yes, we need agreement to make this work.

## What I can accept

I remain open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit programs,
- redeployment,
- shift adjustments,
- temporary wage restraint,
- redesigned guarantees,
- and investment commitments tied to the restructuring.

I am also open to a solution that avoids closures **if** it produces equivalent structural savings.

## What I cannot accept

I will not accept:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a package that preserves the full cost base,
- a blank cheque of site guarantees with no real adjustment,
- or a settlement that protects every entitlement while asking management to carry the full burden.

## Bottom line

We are not here to preserve the status quo. We are here to restore Volkswagen’s competitiveness.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must help produce a **real, quantifiable, durable** alternative. If it cannot, then closures remain on the table because management has a fiduciary duty to secure the company’s future.

So my position is unchanged:

- **We need structural cost reduction**
- **We need structural capacity reduction**
- **We need a meaningful labour contribution**
- **We need investment for the future**
- **And we will not sign up to a deal that leaves Volkswagen uncompetitive**

If the works council or IG Metall want to move this forward, bring a package with numbers that closes the gap. Otherwise, we are still talking past the crisis.
