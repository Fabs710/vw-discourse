We agree with the broad diagnosis, but not with any outcome that leaves Volkswagen’s cost base structurally uncompetitive.

## Where we agree
- **With management:** delay is value-destructive, and the restructuring must be durable, not cosmetic.
- **With the expert:** this is fundamentally a **site-by-site economics problem**. We need real numbers on utilization, fixed cost, and savings.
- **With the owners:** value preservation matters, and a weak compromise that merely postpones the problem will hurt shareholders.
- **With labour and Lower Saxony:** a blunt closure-first approach can destroy trust, execution, and industrial capability, which also destroys value.

## Where we disagree
### On the works council / IG Metall line
We do **not** accept “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, keep every plant open” as a sufficient restructuring answer.

That is a red line, not a solution.

If the non-closure package can fully and durably close the cost gap, then fine. But so far, we have heard assertions of feasibility, not proof.  
If it does **not** close the gap, then insisting on it would mean preserving excess fixed cost and structurally impaired margins. That is unacceptable.

### On Lower Saxony’s stance
We respect the governance role, but a blocking position is not a business case. If some sites are economically non-viable, that reality cannot be vetoed away indefinitely. Protecting the regional base is only credible if the company remains competitive.

### On management’s posture
We also do **not** want management using closures as a reflex or bargaining weapon without evidence. Closure must be justified, not assumed.

So our position is not “close plants at any cost.” It is: **prove the economic necessity of whatever footprint change you propose**.

## What would move us
We would support a no-closure or closure-last settlement only if it delivers all of the following:
1. **Verified annual savings** large enough to materially restore margins
2. **Durable capacity reduction**, not just temporary measures
3. **Meaningful labour contribution** through wage restraint, flexibility, voluntary exits, and redeployment
4. **Site-by-site viability** with no hidden excess fixed-cost burden
5. **Clear investment and product commitments** that support future earnings
6. **Execution certainty** that avoids prolonged conflict and repeated renegotiation

If those conditions are met, we can back a negotiated package.

If they are not met, then we will not support a “peace deal” that protects every current structure while shareholders absorb continued margin erosion.

## What we need next
This discussion still lacks the decisive input:
- plant-level utilization
- fixed-cost burden by site
- savings from non-closure measures
- closure/consolidation economics
- residual margin impact under each scenario

[SUMMON: independent plant-network restructuring analyst to produce a site-by-site comparison of closure, consolidation, and the strongest feasible non-closure package, including fixed-cost relief, utilization, one-time closure costs, and residual margin impact]

## Bottom line
We will back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**.  
Right now, that means:
- show the numbers,
- prove the savings,
- and demonstrate that the final package leaves Volkswagen with a cost structure that can support competitive margins.

Absent that, a no-closure settlement is not a solution; it is a delay mechanism.
