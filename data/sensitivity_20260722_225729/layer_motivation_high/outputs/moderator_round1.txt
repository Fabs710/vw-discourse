**Moderator Summary — Round 1**

### 1) Agreed so far
- All parties agree the crisis is real and that Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring.
- There is broad agreement that the current cost base is too high and that delay is not viable.
- Most parties accept that some combination of capacity adjustment, working-time flexibility, voluntary instruments, wage restraint, and efficiency measures may be part of a package.
- There is shared recognition that Germany needs a credible industrial future for VW, including investment and product commitments.
- There is general openness to negotiation rather than immediate unilateral action.

### 2) Core disagreements remaining
- **Plant closures:**  
  Works Council, IG Metall, Lower Saxony, and strongly the owners are opposed to closures as the starting point; management and institutional investors say closures remain on the table if needed to achieve real structural savings.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  Labour representatives and Lower Saxony reject them; management will not rule them out if needed for competitiveness.
- **Scope of guarantees:**  
  Labour and Lower Saxony seek binding job/site security through 2030; management says unconditional guarantees are not compatible with necessary restructuring.
- **Depth of labour contribution:**  
  Management, owners, and investors want a substantial and durable contribution on labour cost and capacity; labour says any contribution must be tied to binding protections and cannot be one-sided.
- **Role of governance/power:**  
  Lower Saxony emphasizes its co-determination and veto role; management and capital-side actors caution against governance blocking necessary change.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- How to reduce fixed costs enough to restore competitiveness **without** plant closures or forced layoffs.
- What level of capacity reduction is necessary, and whether it can be achieved through voluntary exits, redeployment, working-time changes, or other non-forced measures.
- What binding employment and site-security commitments are feasible if restructuring must remain economically credible.
- How labor concessions, investment commitments, and product allocation will be balanced so that sacrifices are matched by long-term industrial certainty.
- Whether there is a middle ground between “no closures” and “closure-led restructuring” that achieves the same financial effect.

### 4) Refocus for the next round
Please move from positions to **specific package design**:
- Identify which cost-reduction measures are quantitatively sufficient.
- Specify what non-closure alternatives could replace closures, if any.
- Clarify what binding guarantees are possible, and under what conditions.
- Define the investment/product commitments that would make concessions acceptable.
- Test any emerging proposal against one question: **Does it restore competitiveness durably without destroying trust and industrial capability?**
