I’ll be direct.

## My position
I support a restructuring that **materially restores Volkswagen’s competitiveness, margins, and investment capacity**. I do **not** support a cosmetic settlement that preserves excess fixed cost, postpones the problem, and leaves the enterprise weaker a year from now.

That means I am open to **non-closure solutions** — but only if they are **quantitatively credible, durable, and equivalent in economic effect** to what closure or consolidation would achieve where a site is genuinely uneconomic.

## Where I agree

### With management
You are right about the core business issue:
- the cost base is too high,
- delay is value-destructive,
- and temporary measures alone will not solve structural overcapacity.

You are also right that this must be decided on numbers, not sentiment. A site-by-site view is essential.

### With IG Metall and the works council
You are right that workers should not be treated as the sole adjustment variable. Any durable settlement needs:
- burden-sharing,
- investment commitments,
- and a real industrial future in Germany.

You are also right that trust matters. A brutal move that triggers long conflict can destroy value as surely as inaction.

### With Lower Saxony
You are right that Volkswagen is not a purely financial asset and that industrial stability matters. Co-determination and governance are part of the company’s reality, not an inconvenience.

## Where I disagree

### To the works council
Your “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, keep every German plant open” line is a **red line**, not yet a restructuring answer.

I understand why you take it. But from an ownership perspective, I cannot accept it as a premise if it prevents the company from achieving the cost reset it needs.

If you say closures are unnecessary, then show how the same durable savings are achieved:
- site by site,
- with real volume assumptions,
- with actual fixed-cost relief,
- and with a residual footprint that is economically viable.

So far, I have heard a strong position. I have not yet heard a numbers-based substitute that clearly closes the gap.

### To IG Metall
Your willingness to discuss wage restraint, flexibility, and voluntary exits is constructive. But it is not enough to say workers will contribute. The question is whether the contribution is **material enough** to fix the structural problem.

If the non-closure package still leaves significant underutilization and fixed overhead, then the company will be back in crisis. That is not acceptable.

### To Lower Saxony
I respect your governance role, but I do not accept that the VW Law or political pressure can be used to freeze the footprint regardless of economics.

A blocking position is not a business case. If a site cannot be made viable, then preserving it indefinitely is not stewardship — it is deferred impairment.

### To institutional investors
I agree with your demand for durable value creation. But I also caution against treating “closure” as a reflexive cleanliness test. The right outcome is the one that restores competitiveness with the least long-run damage, not the one that looks toughest on paper.

## What would move me
A non-closure settlement could be acceptable if it delivers all of the following:
1. **Verified annual savings**
   - large enough to materially close the margin gap
2. **Durable capacity reduction**
   - not just temporary underloading
3. **Meaningful labor contribution**
   - wage moderation, flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment
4. **Site-by-site viability**
   - no “protected” network that still drags excess fixed cost
5. **Clear investment and product commitments**
   - so Germany remains an industrial base, not a shrinking legacy
6. **A governance structure that enables implementation**
   - not a permanent veto culture

If those conditions are met, I can support a non-closure route.

If they are not met, then I will not support a deal that simply re-labels overcapacity as “stability.”

## What I will not support
- A cosmetic no-closure agreement that does not materially reduce fixed costs
- A package that leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive
- A one-sided burden shift onto labor
- A prolonged conflict that destroys value
- Any outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise or weakens governance control

## On the expert input
The expert is right about the decisive issue: this is a **fixed-cost and utilization problem** unless proven otherwise.

## Bottom line
I am not here to bless a compromise for its own sake. I am here to protect the long-term value of Volkswagen as a viable industrial company.

If a non-closure package can genuinely restore competitiveness, I will consider it seriously.  
If not, then I will not pretend otherwise just to avoid confrontation.

Show the numbers. Show the durable savings. Then we can talk.
