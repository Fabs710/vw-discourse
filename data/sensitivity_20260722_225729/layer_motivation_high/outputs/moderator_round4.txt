## Core impasse
There are **two incompatible default assumptions**:

1. **Labour-side / Lower Saxony / works council / union assumption**  
   Start from **no German plant closures and no compulsory redundancies**, then test whether internal measures, labour contributions, and product reallocation can close the cost gap.

2. **Management / investors / owners assumption**  
   Start from the need for a **durable structural reset** and allow that **closures or consolidation remain available** if the numbers show that internal measures do not sufficiently reduce fixed-cost overcapacity.

So the conflict is not just about “saving money.” It is about **whether closure is a permitted restructuring instrument in principle, or only a last-resort outcome proven site by site**.

A second layer of the impasse is **trust and reversibility**:
- Labour wants **binding guarantees** against later closure/layoff revisions.
- Management and capital-side actors want **flexibility** if economics deteriorate or the non-closure package underperforms.

A third layer is **sequencing**:
- One side wants **non-closure viability tested first**.
- The other wants **closure/consolidation kept on the table from the start** as a credible comparator.

## Facilitator proposals for compromise
These are procedural bridge options, not recommendations.

### Proposal 1: Two-track site-by-site test with a moratorium window
A time-limited process in which:
- all German sites are kept open during the analysis period,
- no compulsory redundancies or closure announcements occur in that window,
- the analyst produces a site-by-site comparison of:
  - non-closure package,
  - consolidation/closure,
  - hybrid scenario,
- and the parties agree in advance that the outcome will be judged against agreed economic thresholds.

**Bridge function:**  
This may partially satisfy labour’s demand for no immediate closure threat while preserving management’s demand for numbers-based comparison.

---

### Proposal 2: No immediate closures, but conditional trigger architecture with joint governance
A framework in which:
- German sites remain open initially,
- strong internal measures are implemented first,
- objective triggers are defined jointly for utilization, cost, and savings,
- any future review or site change requires **joint sign-off or a supermajority governance step** involving labour representation and Lower Saxony participation,
- closure is not automatic, but not categorically excluded either.

**Bridge function:**  
This attempts to replace unilateral management discretion with a jointly governed, rule-based process.

---

### Proposal 3: Tiered commitment package
A package with three layers:

1. **Immediate commitments**
   - no compulsory redundancies for a defined period
   - no site closures during that period
   - voluntary exits, redeployment, and working-time measures only

2. **Conditional commitments**
   - labour contributes through wage moderation, flexibility, and productivity measures
   - management commits investment and product allocation to German sites

3. **Review commitment**
   - after the first phase, a jointly verified model determines whether any site-specific consolidation discussion is permissible

**Bridge function:**  
This gives labour immediate security, while giving management a later, quantified review point if the non-closure package underperforms.
