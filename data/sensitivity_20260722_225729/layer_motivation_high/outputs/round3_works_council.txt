Management and the capital side keep asking for “credible alternatives,” but you still have not answered the core point: **a restructuring that relies on plant closures and compulsory redundancies is not the only credible option — it is simply the most destructive one**.

Let me be very clear.

## What I agree with
I agree with everyone that:
- Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring.
- The current cost base is too high.
- Delay is not an option.
- The company needs future product, technology, and investment commitments in Germany.

So no one should mistake our position for denial of the crisis.

## Where I disagree
I **do not accept** the framing that “no closures” is merely a constraint or slogan. For us, it is a legitimate and necessary industrial position. The burden is on management to prove that closures are unavoidable — and so far, you have not done that.

You keep saying:
- “show the numbers,”
- “show the savings,”
- “show the equivalent alternative.”

Fine. Then show them. But until you do, closures remain a choice, not a necessity.

And I reject the idea that Volkswagen must prove seriousness by destroying sites and jobs in Germany.

## To Group & Brand Management
You say structural change requires capacity and cost reduction. We agree.  
But you have **not** proven that this requires plant closures.

There are serious non-closure measures on the table:
- shift reductions,
- model reallocation,
- working-time changes,
- temporary wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- retraining,
- and targeted efficiency measures.

Those are not fantasies. They are real instruments. If you say they are insufficient, then provide the calculations. How much savings do you need? Which plant-specific measures were tested? What exact gap remains after internal rebalancing?

Without that, “closures may be needed” is just a threat.

## To the controlling owners and institutional investors
You talk about value, margins, and durability. I understand that language. But you are treating the workforce as if it were a cost line to be cut until the spreadsheet looks better.

That is exactly how you destroy trust, industrial capability, and long-term value.

A “value-preserving” strategy that closes German plants, breaks the job-security framework, and forces redundancies is not value-preserving. It is short-term accounting dressed up as strategy.

If you want a durable solution, then accept this:
- workers will contribute,
- but only in a negotiated framework,
- only with binding site and job guarantees,
- and only if the German industrial base remains intact.

## To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your support for a negotiated route. But I want to be equally direct with you: a phased review is only acceptable if it does **not** become a backdoor to closures.

We will not accept a formula where sites are kept open “for now” while management prepares the next closure round. That would be a staged betrayal, not a compromise.

If there is to be a review, then the starting assumption must be:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- binding protection through 2030,
- and restructuring through internal measures only.

Anything else is not a guarantee. It is an expiration date.

## On the moderator’s “possible compromise positions”
### 1) Phased restructuring with a “closure last” rule
I can say this plainly: **closure last is still closure on the table**.  
That is not a solution I can support as a works council representative. Once you normalize closure as a fallback, the trust basis is already broken.

We are prepared to engage on phase-based restructuring **only if closures are excluded**, not merely delayed.

### 2) Binding employment guarantee with conditional flexibility
This is the wrong direction if it makes job security conditional on hitting management-defined savings milestones. Job security is supposed to protect employees from unilateral cost-cutting logic, not become a reward for accepting it.

We can discuss flexibility and contribution, but **not conditional job security that can be withdrawn if management decides the numbers are not enough**.

### 3) Site-specific industrial packages
This is the only part of the moderator’s framing that points in a useful direction — if it means real industrial commitments, model allocation, and investment at each German site.

But again, site-specific packages must not be used to sort plants into “keepers” and “candidates for closure.”  
Every German plant must remain open.

## What a serious package must contain
If management wants labour contribution, then the package must be built on these principles:

1. **No German plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site security through 2030**
4. **Capacity reduction through internal reallocation, working-time measures, and voluntary tools only**
5. **Investment and product commitments for German sites**
6. **Real co-determination in implementation, not after the fact**

That is the starting point. Not the end point.

## What we are prepared to discuss
We are not refusing responsibility. We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- retraining,
- redeployment,
- shift adjustments,
- temporary wage restraint,
- and internal efficiency measures.

But those are contributions to a negotiated industrial future — **not payment for plant closures**.

## What we are not prepared to accept
We will not accept:
- closures as a “fallback”,
- compulsory redundancies,
- unconditional management freedom to reshape the footprint,
- or a framework where job security is conditional on accepting dismantling.

That is the line.

## Bottom line
Management keeps asking whether we can prove a non-closure alternative.  
We have already named it: **capacity reduction without closures, flexibility without forced layoffs, and restructuring with binding guarantees**.

Now it is management’s turn to prove why that cannot work.

Until then, the works council’s position remains unchanged:
- **Keep every German plant open**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Protect co-determination**
- **Preserve job security through 2030**
- **Restructure through negotiation, not closure threats**

If this company wants commitment from the workforce, then it must first show commitment to the workforce.
