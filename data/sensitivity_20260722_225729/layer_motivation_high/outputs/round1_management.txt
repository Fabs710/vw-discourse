I hear the position from works council and IG Metall clearly. Let me be equally clear from management’s side:

We do **not** dispute that the crisis is real, that German operations need a future, or that any durable solution must include investment, product commitment, and a structural industrial plan. On those points, we are aligned.

Where we are **not** aligned is on the idea that all German sites can simply be protected unchanged, with no closures, no meaningful labour-cost contribution, and no forced adaptation of the footprint. That is not credible in the current market. We are facing:
- structurally uncompetitive factory costs,
- shrinking European demand,
- intense pressure from lower-cost competitors,
- and a brand that is not meeting its return-on-sales target.

In that context, **a purely defensive approach is not a solution**. If we preserve every site regardless of economics, we will preserve the cost problem as well. That would leave the company weaker, not stronger.

So my position is this:

- **I am prepared to discuss alternatives to closure** if they deliver the same structural result.
- But I will **not** accept a cosmetic package that avoids plant closures in name while leaving overcapacity, rigid staffing, and excessive labour costs untouched.
- I will **not** accept a deal that relies only on temporary flexibility or voluntary measures if it does not materially reset the cost base.
- And I will be frank: if we cannot achieve a durable settlement, then management will have to keep every structural option on the table.

What would move management:
1. A **credible reduction in capacity and fixed costs** that is durable, not temporary.
2. A **material labour-cost contribution** that brings German operations back toward competitiveness.
3. A package that creates room for **investment in future products and technology through 2030**.
4. A solution that is socially responsible, but not one that freezes the status quo.

On your specific points:
- **Binding guarantees through 2030**: I cannot accept an unconditional blanket guarantee for all sites if it prevents the restructuring needed to restore competitiveness.
- **No compulsory redundancies**: I understand the concern, but if the alternative is preserving structurally unviable capacity, then this cannot be ruled out a priori.
- **Working-time and flexibility solutions**: yes, these can be part of the package.
- **Voluntary instruments**: yes, they are useful, but likely insufficient on their own.
- **Investment commitments**: yes, but they must be matched by real cost relief.

So the real question is not whether change happens — it must. The question is whether the employee side is prepared to accept a **substantive structural adjustment**, including a genuine contribution on labour cost and capacity, rather than asking management to carry the entire burden.

I am ready to negotiate a tough but fair settlement. I am **not** ready to sign up to an agreement that keeps the old cost structure intact and simply postpones the crisis.
