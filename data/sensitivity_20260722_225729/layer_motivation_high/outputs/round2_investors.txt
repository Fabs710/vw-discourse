We agree with the basic diagnosis from management, the family owners, and even the labour side: **Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring, not delay**. The current cost base is too high, and a cosmetic settlement would be value-destructive. We would penalize that, and we would expect the market to do the same.

But we are **not** persuaded by the repeated insistence that “no closures” can simply be declared as a binding starting point and still somehow deliver the needed economics. That is not a strategy; it is a constraint. If the package does not materially reduce fixed costs and overcapacity, then it does not solve the problem.

### Where we agree
- With **management**: the company needs structural cost reduction, not a temporary patch.
- With **owners**: reform must be durable and value-preserving, not symbolic.
- With **Lower Saxony** and the **works council/union**: a hollowed-out industrial plan would also be unacceptable; competitiveness must be tied to investment and future products.
- With **IG Metall**: labour can contribute through flexibility, wage moderation, voluntary exits, and working-time measures.

### Where we disagree
- With the **works council**: “no German plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantee through 2030” is not, by itself, a credible restructuring offer. It may protect jobs in the short term, but if it leaves overcapacity and cost structure intact, it simply defers the crisis.
- With **Lower Saxony**: a political veto against any hard choice is not stewardship if it blocks the company’s return to competitiveness.
- With **management**, if it treats closures as the default answer without proving why non-closure measures are insufficient, that would also be too blunt and potentially value-destructive.

### What we need to see
We will support a settlement only if it meets a simple test: **does it restore competitive margins on a durable basis?**

That requires:
1. **Quantified, durable capacity and cost reduction**
   - Show the exact gap.
   - Show the savings.
   - Show why the package is sufficient.

2. **A material employee contribution**
   - Temporary wage restraint alone is not enough.
   - Working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, redeployment, and redesigned guarantees can be part of it.
   - But the contribution must be large enough to move the cost base.

3. **A real industrial plan for Germany**
   - Product allocation.
   - Investment commitments.
   - Technology roadmap.
   - Enough certainty to justify near-term pain.

### Our bottom line
We are **not** interested in defending every existing structure. We are interested in **risk-adjusted shareholder value**. That means:
- no cosmetic compromise,
- no prolonged conflict,
- no outcome that leaves the company structurally uncompetitive.

If an alternative to closures can deliver the same durable financial result, we will support it. If it cannot, then the opposition to meaningful restructuring is not credible from an owner’s perspective. We would rather see a hard but effective adjustment than a protected status quo that continues to erode margins.
