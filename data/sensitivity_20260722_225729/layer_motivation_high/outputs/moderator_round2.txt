### Core impasse
The dispute has narrowed to a single structural conflict:

- **Labour-side stakeholders** insist that restructuring must be possible **without plant closures or compulsory redundancies**, and that job and site guarantees are the price of trust.
- **Management and capital-side stakeholders** insist that restructuring must produce **quantified, durable cost and capacity reduction**, and they do not accept that “no closures” can be a binding premise unless an equivalent savings package is shown.

So the plateau is not about whether Volkswagen needs change. It is about **what counts as a credible substitute for closures** and **who carries which risk**.

---

### Possible compromise positions
Below are facilitator proposals that could bridge the strongest conflicts:

#### 1) Phased restructuring with a “closure last” rule
- Agree a first phase focused on:
  - shift reductions,
  - working-time adjustments,
  - voluntary exits,
  - redeployment,
  - retraining,
  - product reallocation.
- Closures would remain possible only if an agreed set of metrics is not met by a fixed review date.
- This creates a **sequenced path** rather than an immediate closure decision.

**Why it may help:**  
It gives labour-side stakeholders a real chance to demonstrate a non-closure alternative, while giving management and capital stakeholders a defined fallback if savings do not materialize.

#### 2) Binding employment guarantee with conditional flexibility
- Extend job and site protection through a fixed horizon, but make it conditional on:
  - agreed labour-cost relief,
  - productivity measures,
  - implementation of working-time flexibility,
  - and delivery of agreed savings milestones.
- In return, management commits to no forced redundancies and no unilateral closures during the guarantee period.

**Why it may help:**  
It preserves trust while avoiding an unconditional promise that management and investors will reject. It links security to measurable performance.

#### 3) Site-specific industrial packages
- Instead of a single all-plant rule, negotiate **plant-by-plant packages**:
  - future model allocations,
  - battery/EV/software-related investment,
  - workforce adjustments,
  - and local redeployment plans.
- Sites with stronger future product potential could receive stronger guarantees; weaker sites would receive structured transformation support rather than immediate closure.

**Why it may help:**  
It allows the parties to move from an abstract closure/no-closure fight to concrete industrial planning.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Works Council
**What quantified cost reduction can you commit to through non-closure measures alone, and by when?**

#### IG Metall
**What level of temporary labour contribution would you consider economically material enough to justify a binding no-forced-layoff settlement?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**Which non-closure measures have been tested, in numbers, and at what point do you conclude they are insufficient?**

#### State of Lower Saxony
**Would you support a phased plan that keeps sites open initially but allows a conditional review if agreed savings and investment milestones are not met?**

#### Controlling Owners
**What level of structural savings would you regard as an acceptable equivalent to closures, if achieved through negotiated flexibility and voluntary exits?**

#### Institutional Investors
**What concrete milestones on margin improvement and capacity reduction would make you supportive of a no-closure settlement?**
