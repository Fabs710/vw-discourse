Lower Saxony takes the crisis seriously, and we are not here to defend the status quo for its own sake. We agree that Volkswagen needs a durable industrial solution, lower costs, and a credible investment path for future products in Germany.

But I need to be equally clear: **we will not support a restructuring that relies on plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or a unilateral weakening of the social partnership model.** Those are not acceptable tools for solving this problem.

To management, I say this directly:

- You are right that the cost base must come down.
- You are right that the company needs structural change, not just delay.
- But you are **not** entitled to define “structural change” as closing German plants and shifting the burden disproportionately onto workers and regions that have carried this company for decades.

That would be economically shortsighted and politically unacceptable. Volkswagen is not just any firm; it is a regional anchor, and its German footprint has strategic significance. Cutting capacity without preserving industrial depth in Germany risks weakening the company’s long-term resilience, not strengthening it.

To the works council and IG Metall: I agree with your core line that the workforce cannot be made to pay alone. A negotiated contribution from labor can be part of a solution, but only if it is paired with:
- binding site and employment security,
- real investment in German production and future platforms,
- and a restructuring that preserves the company’s industrial base in this state and in Germany overall.

What I would support is a **socially balanced restructuring package** that may include:
- capacity adjustments,
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint or deferred compensation elements,
- voluntary exits and retraining,
- and targeted efficiency measures.

What I will not support is a package that treats plant closure as the default answer or uses the threat of closures to force a one-sided settlement.

And let me add one governance point clearly: **Lower Saxony’s co-determination and veto role is not negotiable.** Any attempt to sideline the state as an owner and stakeholder would damage trust and would not produce a stable outcome.

So my message is:
- Yes to competitiveness.
- Yes to serious restructuring.
- Yes to negotiated burden-sharing.
- **No to closures as the starting point.**
- **No to compulsory redundancies as the preferred instrument.**
- **No to an outcome that undermines regional employment and Volkswagen’s role as an anchor of the German industrial economy.**

If management wants our support, it needs to come back with a plan that restores competitiveness **without gutting the German footprint**. If that means the company must accept less than it initially wanted, that is the price of a legitimate and durable settlement.
