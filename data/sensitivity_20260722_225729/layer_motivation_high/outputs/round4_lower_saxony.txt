Lower Saxony’s position remains unchanged:

We support a durable restructuring, but **not** at the price of German plant closures, compulsory redundancies, or weakening co-determination. That is not an emotional reflex; it is the responsible line for a shareholder with a regional public mandate and a blocking minority.

## Where I agree

I agree with management, the owners, and the investors on the basic diagnosis:
- Volkswagen needs a real restructuring.
- The current cost base is too high.
- Delay is not an option.
- The company must regain competitiveness and preserve investment capacity.

I also agree with the expert that this must be decided on **numbers**, not slogans.  
If management believes closures are necessary, then it must prove that case plant by plant.

## Where I disagree

### To Group & Brand Management
You say “no closures” is not a restructuring strategy. I say that **closure-first thinking is not a strategy either**; it is the bluntest instrument and the one with the highest regional and political damage.

You have not yet shown that all serious non-closure tools are insufficient. Until you do, closures are an assertion, not a necessity.

I do accept that Volkswagen needs capacity reduction and durable savings. But I do **not** accept that this automatically means closures or forced layoffs. If the company cannot make the case quantitatively, then it should not ask the state and workforce to accept irreversible damage.

### To the works council and IG Metall
I support your refusal of compulsory redundancies and your insistence on a negotiated solution. That is legitimate.

But I have to be equally clear: a simple declaration of “keep every plant open” is not yet a complete restructuring answer. If we want to protect sites in the long run, we also need:
- material labour contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment and retraining,
- and real productivity improvements.

I am willing to back that. What I will not back is turning that contribution into a pretext for a rolling closure clock.

### To the controlling owners and institutional investors
You are right that the solution must be durable and economically meaningful.  
You are wrong if you think that protecting the German industrial base is merely a political obstacle.

Volkswagen’s strength is not only financial. It is also industrial, social, and regional. A settlement that damages trust, supplier networks, and co-determination may look efficient on paper and still be strategically weaker.

## What would move Lower Saxony

A serious compromise is possible only if it contains all of the following:

1. **No German plant closures as the starting point**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding job and site security for a defined horizon**
4. **Material but negotiated employee contribution**
5. **Investment and product commitments for German sites**
6. **Real co-determination in implementation**
7. **A site-by-site review based on objective economic criteria, not political convenience**

That is the route to a durable settlement.

Yes, we need it. And we need it now.

But I want to be very precise about the purpose of that analysis:  
it must test whether non-closure measures can restore competitiveness **without** pre-deciding that plant closures are acceptable. The default assumption should be that sites remain viable unless the numbers prove otherwise beyond reasonable doubt.

## Bottom line

Lower Saxony will not support a restructuring that begins with closures or forced redundancies.

We are open to:
- capacity adjustment,
- negotiated labour contributions,
- voluntary instruments,
- working-time flexibility,
- product reallocation,
- and binding investment commitments.

But the outcome must preserve:
- jobs,
- industrial capability,
- co-determination,
- and Volkswagen’s role as a regional economic anchor.

If management wants support for hard measures, it must first show that it has exhausted the non-closure path with real numbers. Until then, the case for closures is not made.
