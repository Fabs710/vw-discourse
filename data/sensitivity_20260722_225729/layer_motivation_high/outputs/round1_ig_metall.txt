The union shares the core line of the works council: **no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no restructuring that shifts the burden onto employees alone**.

Management cannot treat the workforce as the shock absorber for years of strategic failure. If the company wants acceptance for change, then it must be a **shared-sacrifice settlement** with real commitments from all sides: management, owners, and the business itself. That means labour can discuss contributions, including **temporary wage restraint, flexible working-time solutions, capacity reductions, and voluntary instruments** — but only if these are tied to **binding job protection, site security, and a credible industrial future in Germany**.

What we agree on:
- The crisis is real and requires action.
- Capacity must be adjusted and costs reduced.
- A durable solution needs an industrial plan, not just accounting cuts.
- Investment and product commitments for German sites are essential.

What we reject:
- Plant closures as a “solution”
- Compulsory redundancies
- A one-sided package where workers pay through wages, jobs, and insecurity while management and owners are spared
- Any attempt to use the end of peace obligation to force concessions

Let me be explicit: **if the restructuring is built on closures or forced layoffs, the union will oppose it and will mobilise the workforce accordingly**. If management is serious, it should present a package that combines cost relief with **binding guarantees, fair burden-sharing, and a long-term commitment to German production**. That is the only basis for a durable agreement.
