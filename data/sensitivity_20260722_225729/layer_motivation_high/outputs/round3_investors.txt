We agree with the central diagnosis: Volkswagen needs a **durable, numbers-based restructuring**, not delay or a cosmetic settlement. If the outcome leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive, we will not support it.

## Where we agree
- **With management:** the status quo is not sustainable. The cost base and overcapacity problem must be addressed decisively.
- **With the owners:** the solution has to be durable and value-preserving, not politically comfortable.
- **With Lower Saxony and labour:** a purely closure-led approach can destroy trust, execution capability, and long-term industrial value.
- **With IG Metall:** workers should not carry the whole burden alone; wage restraint, flexibility, and voluntary exits can be part of a serious package.

## Where we disagree
### 1) The works council’s “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, guarantees through 2030” line
We understand the position, but we do **not** accept it as a sufficient restructuring answer. It is a red line, not a solution.

If that stance prevents a materially effective cost reset, then it does not protect value. It only delays impairment.

### 2) Lower Saxony’s resistance to closures as a starting point
We agree that closures are blunt and risky. But a political veto against hard choices is not stewardship if it blocks competitiveness. If the numbers show a site cannot be made viable, that reality does not disappear because the governance structure is uncomfortable.

### 3) Management’s tendency to treat closures as the default
We also do **not** want management to use closures as a reflex or bargaining weapon without proving the case. If non-closure measures can close the gap, they should be preferred. But the proof has to be real.

## What we need to see
This debate is still too abstract. We need a **site-by-site, quantified comparison** of:
- fixed-cost burden,
- utilization,
- savings from labour flexibility and voluntary exits,
- closure economics,
- and the residual margin impact under each scenario.

Without that, nobody is demonstrating that their preferred option actually restores competitiveness.

## Our position
We would support a no-closure or closure-last solution **only if** it delivers:
- materially lower fixed costs,
- durable capacity reduction,
- credible labour-cost relief,
- and a clear return to competitive margins within a defined timeframe.

What we will **not** support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- a deal that preserves excess fixed cost,
- prolonged industrial conflict,
- or a package that protects all existing structures while leaving shareholders with continued margin erosion.

## Bottom line
We are prepared to back **whatever credibly restores competitiveness**.  
Right now, that means:
- show the numbers,
- prove the savings,
- and demonstrate that the proposed package is economically equivalent to closure where needed.

If that proof is not forthcoming, then the resistance to serious restructuring is not credible from a shareholder perspective.
