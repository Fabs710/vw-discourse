Management keeps repeating “structural change,” but it still has not answered the central question: **why must structural change mean plant closures and the end of job security?** It does not.

I will be very direct.

### Where I agree
I agree that Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not a temporary patch.
I agree that the cost base must come down.
I agree that the company needs investment, product allocation, and a credible industrial plan for Germany.

But those points do **not** justify forcing the workforce to accept plant closures or compulsory redundancies as the price of survival.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with management, and I also disagree with the capital-side arguments, on one key issue: you are treating closures as if they were the only serious way to reduce costs. That is not true.

If you want a package that is economically credible **without destroying trust**, then it must be built on:
- keeping **every German plant open**,
- avoiding **compulsory redundancies**,
- and securing a **binding employment and site guarantee through 2030**.

That is the minimum basis for any agreement.

### What a real alternative looks like
If the company is serious, then the package must combine the following:

1. **Capacity reduction without closures**
   - shift volumes between plants,
   - reduce shifts where needed,
   - adjust model allocation,
   - and eliminate overcapacity through internal rebalancing rather than shutting sites.

2. **Working-time and flexibility instruments**
   - temporary working-time reductions,
   - shift adjustments,
   - short-time work where appropriate,
   - and more flexible deployment across sites.

3. **Voluntary tools only**
   - retirement rules,
   - voluntary severance,
   - redeployment,
   - retraining.
   
   But I will say again: **voluntary tools only**. No forced layoffs.

4. **Real industrial commitments**
   - future products in Germany,
   - battery, EV, and software-related investment,
   - and binding allocation of new platforms to German plants.

5. **Governance involvement**
   - co-determination must not be bypassed,
   - the works council must be involved in shaping implementation,
   - and there must be no unilateral management fait accompli.

### On “durable cost reduction”
To management and investors: if you claim closures are necessary, then show the numbers.
What exactly is the cost gap?
What exact savings are required?
Which non-closure measures have been tested and rejected?
What capacity reduction target cannot be achieved through redeployment, shift changes, or voluntary exits?

So far, I hear assertions, not a convincing proof that closures are unavoidable.

### On guarantees
Lower Saxony says it will not support closures. Good. But I want to be clear: **we will not trade away job security for vague promises**. If there is no binding guarantee through 2030, then there is no basis for trust. And without trust, there will be resistance — industrial and public.

### On burden-sharing
We are not saying that workers contribute nothing. We are prepared to discuss:
- working-time flexibility,
- temporary wage restraint,
- voluntary exits,
- and redeployment.

But burden-sharing must be fair. It cannot mean:
- workers give up guarantees,
- workers absorb insecurity,
- workers pay for management mistakes,
- while management demands the right to close plants and cut jobs permanently.

That is not a partnership. That is a surrender.

### Bottom line
So my position remains unchanged:

- **No German plant closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **Binding job and site guarantees through 2030**
- **A negotiated restructuring with real co-determination**
- **Investment and future product commitments in Germany**

If management cannot build a viable plan on that basis, then it is not offering restructuring — it is demanding that workers pay for failure.

And let me be clear: if management continues to treat closures as the default option, then the works council will continue to resist with all available means.
