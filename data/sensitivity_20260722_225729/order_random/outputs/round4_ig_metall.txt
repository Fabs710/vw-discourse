The specialist has helped clarify the operational question, but let me be equally clear: **technical feasibility is not the same as a fair settlement**.

### Where I agree
I agree with the expert and with several points from management and even the owners:
- Cosmetic savings are useless.
- A durable deal must be measurable and enforceable.
- Capacity has to come out.
- Working-time changes, voluntary exits, product allocation, and overhead cuts are all part of the answer.
- Employees are prepared to contribute.

So no one should claim the union is refusing restructuring. We are not.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with the conclusion being pushed by management, owners, and investors: that because a no-closure package must be strong, **plant closures and compulsory redundancies must remain live pressure tools**.

That is precisely the wrong sequencing and the wrong burden logic.

The expert has said something important: **closure-equivalent savings can be achieved without closures if the package is deep enough**. That means closures are not the only serious solution. They are one possible fallback, but not the starting point and not the benchmark of credibility.

### To management
You keep saying “economics first.” Fine. Then show the economics of a package that:
- keeps German sites open,
- excludes forced layoffs,
- and still delivers the required savings.

Do that with facts, not threats. If you want union flexibility, you must stop treating our jobs and sites as negotiable collateral.

Your position still asks employees to accept insecurity first and hope for fairness later. We do not accept that.

### To the controlling owners
You say you support a tough, balanced restructuring. Then use your influence to force management to design one that does not destroy the industrial base.

A responsible owner does not default to closure logic just because it is administratively simple. If a non-closure path can produce equivalent savings, then that is the path you should back.

### To Lower Saxony
I appreciate your rejection of closures and forced redundancies. But your support must be more than rhetorical. If management tries to turn closure threats into a bargaining club, then your blocking position must remain active. Otherwise, the burden will fall on the workforce.

### To investors
You are right to demand real savings. But you are too quick to equate credibility with headcount destruction. A credible package can be quantified through:
- shift reductions,
- annualized hours,
- voluntary exits,
- deferred pay,
- temporary wage moderation,
- bonus restraint,
- product transfer,
- plant specialization,
- and fixed-cost cuts.

What you want is not just savings. You want savings in a particular social distribution. That is exactly why we are here to bargain.

---

## What a serious compromise would actually require
I am prepared to discuss a package that includes:
1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Substantial voluntary exits and attrition**
4. **Temporary wage moderation and deferred compensation**
5. **Bonus restraint**
6. **Working-time flexibility and shift redesign**
7. **Product and volume reallocation**
8. **Management overhead cuts**
9. **Binding investment commitments**
10. **Enforceable milestones and review points**

That is a real burden-sharing package. It is not a free pass. It is a fair bargain.

### What I will not accept
I will not accept:
- a deal where employees pay first and everyone else calls that “responsibility”,
- a package that preserves owners’ optionality while removing workers’ security,
- or a “non-closure first” plan that quietly keeps closures as the default threat.

If you want to test the non-closure route, then test it honestly:
- agree a quantified savings target,
- agree a time-limited implementation window,
- agree site and job protections during that window,
- and agree that closures and forced redundancies are not used as bargaining pressure in the meantime.

That is the minimum for a fair test.

---

## My answer to the moderator’s question
You asked what combination would count as truly equivalent to closure-level savings.

My answer is:
- a serious reduction in capacity,
- permanent removal of excess cost,
- real headcount reduction through voluntary means,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- product and volume shifts away from high-cost sites,
- and binding management/owner contributions.

But I repeat: **we do not accept that closure options stay open while we do the work of proving alternatives**. If the alternative is to be tested, then it must be tested without the gun to the head of the workforce.

---

## Final line
The union is ready for a tough, fair restructuring. We are not ready for a one-sided restructuring dressed up as realism.

If management, owners, and investors want a durable settlement, then stop using plant closures and compulsory redundancies as the first answer and start negotiating a package that shares the pain fairly.

If that is not possible, then there will be conflict — and it will be because the burden was being pushed onto employees alone.
