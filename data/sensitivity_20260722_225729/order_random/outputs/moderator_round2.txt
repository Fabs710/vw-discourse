### 1) What has been agreed
- Volkswagen needs a **durable, enforceable restructuring**.
- **Cosmetic savings are not sufficient**.
- The company must remain **competitive** and able to **invest in future products/technologies**.
- **Prolonged conflict would be harmful** to value, trust, and execution.
- There is openness, in principle, to:
  - working-time flexibility,
  - voluntary exit/attrition programs,
  - productivity improvements,
  - capacity reductions,
  - and some form of employee contribution.
- All sides accept that any settlement should be **measurable** and **implementable**.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures**:  
  - Union, works council, and Lower Saxony reject closures as a starting point and want them off the table.  
  - Management, owners, and investors insist closures must remain available if economics require them.
- **Compulsory redundancies**:  
  - Employee side and Lower Saxony reject them.  
  - Capital-side actors treat them as possible if no alternative delivers sufficient savings.
- **Burden-sharing**:  
  - Union and works council want employees to contribute only if management/owners also make visible commitments.  
  - Management, owners, and investors argue the package must first solve the economics, with employee-side protections following from that.
- **Guarantees through 2030**:  
  - Employee side wants binding site/job guarantees.  
  - Capital-side actors say broad guarantees cannot come before a credible economic package.
- **Role of co-determination / state influence**:  
  - Lower Saxony and employee representatives want these protections to be central.  
  - Management, owners, and investors accept consultation but not a structure that blocks necessary change.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- The discussion still lacks a quantified answer to:  
  **How much savings are required, and through which measures can they be achieved without closures?**
- There is still no agreed balance between:
  - **job/site security** and
  - **capacity/cost reduction**.
- The parties have not yet resolved whether:
  - employee concessions should be conditional on binding guarantees,
  - or guarantees should depend on a fully specified economic package first.
- There is no shared fallback if **non-closure measures** prove insufficient.
- Investment commitments for future technologies are acknowledged as important, but not yet tied to a concrete restructuring package.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principles to a **side-by-side package comparison**:
  1. required savings target,
  2. list of non-closure measures,
  3. expected contribution from employees, management, owners, and the state,
  4. any remaining use of closures or redundancies as fallback,
  5. binding implementation and enforcement mechanism.
- Ask each side to specify:
  - what they can **accept now**,
  - what they need in **exchange**,
  - and what they consider an **absolute fallback line** if the numbers do not work.
