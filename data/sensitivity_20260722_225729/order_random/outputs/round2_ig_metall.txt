We have heard the same basic message from management, owners, and investors: they want a restructuring that is “credible” and “durable.” I agree with that principle. What I do **not** accept is the conclusion they are trying to smuggle in: that credibility requires plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and a one-sided transfer of the crisis onto the workforce.

Let me be equally direct.

### Where I agree
- Yes, Volkswagen needs real cost relief and capacity adjustment.
- Yes, cosmetic savings are not enough.
- Yes, the settlement must be durable and enforceable.
- Yes, the company must remain competitive and invest in future products.

We are not defending the status quo. We have already shown openness to:
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- productivity improvements,
- and employee contributions.

### Where I disagree
I disagree with management’s framing that the only “hard” solution is closures and layoffs. That is not the only way to achieve savings; it is the easiest way to shift the burden onto the workforce. If the company wants a sustainable settlement, then the burden must be shared by **management, owners, and the workforce**.

I also disagree with the investors’ demand for “hard numbers” while dismissing the social costs as secondary. A restructuring that destroys trust, triggers long conflict, and tears up the collective wage framework is not a credible value solution. It is a short-term accounting fix with long-term damage.

And I disagree with the owners’ suggestion that we should treat closure as a normal option if economics are difficult. A site is not just a line item. Plant closures in Germany would mean permanent industrial and social damage, and once you go down that path you undermine confidence across the group.

### Our line remains clear
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No unilateral erosion of the collective bargaining framework**
- **No settlement where workers carry the pain alone**

Those are not slogans. They are the minimum basis for a durable settlement.

### What we can discuss concretely
If management is serious about avoiding conflict, then let us move from generalities to a real package architecture:

1. **Capacity reduction without closures**  
   Reconfiguration, shift adjustment, line consolidation, and product allocation changes must be examined before any site is sacrificed.

2. **Employment security**
   Site and job guarantees through the restructuring period, with compulsory redundancies excluded.

3. **Shared labour-cost contribution**
   We are prepared to discuss wage moderation, deferred elements, or the temporary forgoing of bonuses — but only if management and owners also contribute and only if this is tied to binding security commitments.

4. **Investment commitments**
   There must be a clear investment path for future products, battery-related activities, and strategic technologies at German sites.

5. **Governance and enforcement**
   Any agreement must be measurable and enforceable, with the works council and union involved in implementation, not just informed after the fact.

### To management
If you want us to accept pain, then show us real reciprocity:
- no hidden closures,
- no forced redundancies through the back door,
- no wage framework erosion,
- and no demand that we sign away job security before you commit to the future of the sites.

If you can present a package that delivers closure-level savings through other means, we will examine it seriously. But that package has to be real, quantified, and balanced. We will not trade away jobs and standards for vague promises.

### To the owners and Lower Saxony
You both say you want the company’s future secured. Then use your governance power to force management into a social-partnership solution rather than a confrontation model. If you insist on “hard decisions,” then I insist on **shared sacrifice** — not just hard decisions for employees.

### Bottom line
We are ready to negotiate a tough settlement. We are **not** ready to accept a restructuring built on closures, forced layoffs, and unilateral wage pressure.

If management wants an integrative solution, bring:
- concrete non-closure savings,
- binding employment guarantees,
- and a fair burden-sharing package.

If not, then the workforce will continue to resist — and we are prepared to escalate.
