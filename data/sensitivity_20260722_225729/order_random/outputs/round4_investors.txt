We agree with management, the specialist, and the owners on the central point: **Volkswagen needs a restructuring that materially improves the economics, not a cosmetic peace deal**.

## Where we agree
- The current cost base is too high.
- Capacity must come out.
- Durability and enforceability matter.
- A prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- A non-closure package can be acceptable **if it is truly closure-equivalent** in savings and footprint reduction.
- Employees, management, and owners should all contribute.

## Where we disagree

### To IG Metall and the works council
Your position is understandable politically, but as capital providers we cannot accept **“no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute red lines** unless you can prove the economics still work.

The specialist has already made the critical point: avoiding closures is only credible if the package includes:
- real capacity reduction,
- substantial headcount reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- site specialization/consolidation,
- and hard enforcement with milestones.

If you can deliver that, fine. If not, then insisting that closures are off the table is not protecting Volkswagen — it is preserving overcapacity and transferring risk to shareholders.

What we will not support is a deal that:
- keeps every structure intact,
- reduces costs only marginally,
- and calls that restructuring.

That would be value-destructive.

### To Lower Saxony
We respect the regional and political role, but we do not accept a blanket veto on closures or redundancies if the economics require them. Protecting every site regardless of competitiveness would be a mistake. It would leave the group with a structurally weak cost base and poorer returns.

If the result is a genuinely equivalent non-closure package, that is preferable. But it has to be proven, not asserted.

### To the controlling owners
You are right that value destruction must be avoided. But we would caution against using closure threats as a substitute for a well-designed restructuring. The package must be real, quantified, and enforceable. “Toughness” is not enough on its own.

### To management
You are right to insist on hard numbers and durable savings. But investor support will depend on seeing:
- the savings bridge,
- the implementation timeline,
- the enforcement mechanism,
- and the fallback if delivery slips.

We do not want a deal that looks strong in the press and weak in execution.

## Our position
We will support a restructuring if it delivers:
1. **material annual savings**
2. **permanent capacity reduction**
3. **meaningful headcount reduction**
4. **durable labour-cost relief**
5. **clear milestone-based enforcement**
6. **a credible return to competitive margins**

That can be done through:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- wage moderation or deferred compensation,
- product and site rationalization,
- overhead reduction,
- and, if necessary, closures or equivalent footprint rationalization.

## What is unacceptable
- Cosmetic compromise
- Broad guarantees that block necessary adjustment
- A package that preserves structural overcapacity
- A settlement that buys peace now and leaves margins impaired later

## Bottom line
We are open to a **non-closure settlement only if it is economically equivalent** to hard restructuring. Otherwise, closures must remain a live option, because the market will not reward wishful thinking.

If the employee side wants closures removed from consideration, it must help produce a package that is demonstrably equivalent in cost and capacity impact. If it cannot, then it is asking capital to underwrite permanent underperformance, and we will not support that.
