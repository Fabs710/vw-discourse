We agree with the central diagnosis, but not with any solution that is too weak to change the economics.

From the capital markets’ perspective, Volkswagen does need a **durable, enforceable restructuring**. The current cost base, capacity structure, and margin profile are not acceptable. If the outcome does not materially improve return on sales and competitive positioning, then it will not be supported by capital.

### Where we agree
- **Management is right** that cosmetic savings are not enough.
- **The owners are right** that value-destructive conflict helps no one.
- **The state is right** that a settlement must be durable and socially workable.
- **The union and works council are right** that prolonged confrontation and pure burden-shifting would damage the company.

### Where we disagree
We do **not** accept a settlement that preserves the current footprint, current cost base, or current labour-cost structure and then calls it restructuring.

We also do **not** accept a deal that relies on:
- vague future productivity promises,
- open-ended attrition without scale,
- or guarantees that are so broad they eliminate the possibility of real adjustment.

That would be a cosmetic compromise, and capital will punish it.

### Our position on the current lines
- **No plant closures**: not an absolute rule. If a site is structurally uneconomic and no credible alternative exists, closures cannot be ruled out in advance.
- **No compulsory redundancies**: preferable, but not an absolute if the economics cannot otherwise be fixed.
- **No erosion of the collective framework**: the framework matters, but it cannot be used to block necessary structural cost relief.
- **Site/job guarantees through 2030**: only if they are tied to a package that delivers measurable savings and credible implementation.

### What would move us
We would support a package if it credibly includes:
1. **material capacity reduction**
2. **durable labour-cost relief**
3. **headcount reduction at sufficient scale**
4. **fixed-cost reduction that is enforceable**
5. **a clear path back to competitive margins**
6. **implementation certainty, not just announcements**

That can come through a mix of:
- working-time flexibility,
- wage moderation or deferred compensation,
- voluntary exit programmes,
- attrition,
- reallocation,
- product-line consolidation,
- and, if necessary, site rationalisation.

### To IG Metall and the works council
Your refusal of closures and compulsory redundancies is understandable, but it cannot be the final answer if the numbers do not close. Investors are not asking for ideology; we are asking for economics. If you want sites protected, then you must help produce a package that is economically equivalent to closure-level savings. If that cannot be done, then the current red lines are too restrictive.

### To management
You are strongest when you present a **quantified, credible, and enforceable plan**. If you want investor support, show:
- the scale of savings,
- the timing,
- the transition cost,
- the impact on margins,
- and why this package is better than protracted conflict.

If you cannot show that, then the market will treat this as delay, not restructuring.

### To the owners and Lower Saxony
We understand the governance constraints and the regional stakes. But political protection of every site is not a substitute for competitiveness. If the final deal leaves structural overcapacity unresolved, then the long-term value problem remains.

### Bottom line
We will back a tough restructuring if, and only if, it is:
- **durable**
- **measurable**
- **enforceable**
- and **sufficient to restore competitive margins**

We will **not** back a settlement that merely buys peace while leaving Volkswagen structurally impaired.
