I will be direct.

Lower Saxony supports a credible restructuring of Volkswagen. We do **not** support a restructuring that relies on plant closures in Germany, compulsory redundancies, or a unilateral shift of the burden onto the workforce. That would damage the regional economy, fracture the social partnership model that has long underpinned Volkswagen’s stability, and create political and industrial risks that could easily outweigh any short-term savings.

### To management
I agree that the current cost base and capacity situation cannot simply be ignored. Volkswagen needs durable savings, better productivity, and a clearer path to competitive returns. On that point, I am not going to defend inertia.

But I do **not** accept the idea that closures must remain a normal or default instrument. Before any such step is even contemplated, management must show that it has exhausted serious non-closure alternatives with comparable economic effect. That means:
- capacity reductions through reconfiguration and product allocation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit and attrition programs,
- productivity improvements,
- and a negotiated labour-cost contribution.

If management comes forward only with a closure-and-cut strategy, then I will oppose it. The state cannot endorse a plan that weakens Volkswagen’s regional anchor role and endangers jobs in Lower Saxony.

### To the controlling owners
I understand your concern that this must be economically real, not cosmetic. I share that concern. But your position still underestimates the damage a closure-driven conflict would do to Volkswagen’s long-term value and legitimacy.

I agree that “symbolic toughness” is useless. I do **not** agree that the appropriate answer is to keep closures on the table as a pressure tool while expecting the workforce to concede first. That is not a durable governance solution; it is confrontation by another name.

If you want the state’s support, then support a package that:
- protects viable sites,
- avoids forced redundancies,
- and delivers measurable savings through negotiated means.

### To IG Metall and the works council
I agree with you on the core red lines: no compulsory redundancies and no plant closures as the starting point. That is exactly the right political and industrial baseline.

But I also have to be candid: those red lines alone are not enough to secure a settlement. If the workforce wants the state to stand behind a negotiated solution, then the package must produce real and enforceable relief for the company. That means being prepared to discuss:
- wage moderation or deferred components,
- flexibility in working time,
- voluntary programs,
- and productivity commitments.

What I will not support is a formula where employees are asked to contribute, but management receives no meaningful operational improvement in return. The burden has to be shared, and the savings have to be durable.

### My position
So let me state it plainly:

- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to capacity adjustment and productivity gains.
- **Yes** to fair employee contributions if matched by management and owner commitments.
- **Yes** to investment in future products and technologies in Germany.
- **No** to plant closures as the default solution.
- **No** to compulsory redundancies where alternatives exist.
- **No** to a settlement that weakens co-determination or sidelines the state’s role under the VW Law.

### What would move Lower Saxony
A serious package would need to include:
1. credible non-closure savings of sufficient scale,
2. binding site and employment protection for viable German plants,
3. a clear investment path for future products and technologies,
4. and implementation that respects co-determination and the special governance structure of Volkswagen.

If those conditions are met, I can support a tough but balanced restructuring. If they are not, I will use the blocking position the VW Law gives us to prevent a one-sided solution.

That is not obstructionism. It is responsible stewardship of Volkswagen as an industrial anchor for Lower Saxony and for Germany.
