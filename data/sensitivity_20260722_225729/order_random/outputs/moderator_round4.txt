Here is a procedural readout of the plateau, framed as facilitator proposals rather than opinions.

## Core impasse

The central deadlock is not whether restructuring is needed. All parties now accept that it is.  
The impasse is over **sequencing and risk allocation**:

- **Employees / Lower Saxony / works council** want **job and site protections first**, so the non-closure route can be tested without closure threats shaping the outcome.
- **Management / controlling owners / investors** want **credible structural change first**, and insist that closure and compulsory redundancy remain live fallbacks unless a non-closure plan proves equivalent in savings and footprint reduction.

So the real clash is:

> **Should closures and forced redundancies be removed as bargaining pressure up front, or remain available until a non-closure package proves it can match closure-level economics?**

A second, related impasse is over **what counts as “equivalent”**:
- The workforce side emphasizes fairness, trust, and regional responsibility.
- Capital and management emphasize fixed-cost elimination, capacity reduction, enforceability, and fallback triggers.

---

## Facilitator proposals for compromise

### Proposal 1: Time-limited non-closure test with strict gates
A structured trial period could be agreed in which:
- plant closures and compulsory redundancies are **suspended for a defined period**,
- a quantified savings target and capacity-reduction target are set,
- all sides sign up to milestone reviews,
- and a pre-agreed fallback option is activated automatically if milestones are missed.

This would bridge:
- the union/Lower Saxony demand for a genuine test without immediate closure pressure,
- and management/investor demand for enforceability and an exit if the plan underdelivers.

### Proposal 2: Two-tier restructuring package
The parties could separate the package into:
- **Tier A: immediate, agreed measures**
  - voluntary exits
  - working-time changes
  - wage moderation / deferred pay
  - bonus restraint
  - overhead cuts
  - product and volume reallocation
- **Tier B: conditional structural measures**
  - site consolidation
  - deeper footprint rationalization
  - closures only if Tier A does not achieve agreed structural targets

This lets the parties lock in real savings now while deferring the most contested measures unless the numbers fail.

### Proposal 3: Protected sites plus explicit capacity adjustment
A possible landing zone is:
- no closures at specifically designated **viable core sites**,
- substantial capacity reduction achieved by shifting products, reducing shifts, and concentrating output,
- voluntary workforce reduction with enhanced exit packages,
- and a jointly monitored list of non-core locations or functions where stronger restructuring remains possible if required.

This may bridge:
- Lower Saxony’s and the works council’s desire to protect the industrial base,
- while still giving management and investors visible, permanent capacity reduction.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### To IG Metall
If closures and compulsory redundancies are suspended for a fixed period, what exact combination of capacity cuts, headcount reduction, and labour-cost relief would you commit to deliver by when?

### To the controlling owners
Will you support a time-limited non-closure test if it includes binding milestones, automatic review triggers, and a pre-agreed fallback if savings are not achieved?

### To Lower Saxony
If a site-specific compromise protects core German plants but still requires deep capacity and workforce adjustment elsewhere, would you treat that as an acceptable basis for support?

### To Group & Brand Management
What minimum quantified savings and capacity reduction would make you willing to remove closures from the table for the duration of a negotiated test period?

### To Institutional Investors
Would you accept a non-closure settlement if it is independently measured, milestone-based, and includes automatic escalation if delivery falls short?

### To the Works Council
Are you prepared to discuss a package that preserves open plants but includes binding workforce reduction and product consolidation, provided there are no compulsory redundancies during the test phase?

### To the Expert
What single metric should be treated as the decisive threshold for deciding whether the non-closure route has actually achieved closure-equivalent restructuring?
