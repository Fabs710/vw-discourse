We are still circling the same central issue: **Volkswagen needs a restructuring that is economically real, not politically comfortable**.

I have listened carefully to IG Metall, the works council, Lower Saxony, the owners, and the investors. Let me be equally direct in response.

### Where I agree
I agree that:
- a purely cosmetic deal would be worthless,
- prolonged conflict would damage the company,
- the outcome must be durable and enforceable,
- and Volkswagen must preserve its ability to invest in future products and technology.

I also agree that restructuring should not become a self-inflicted value destruction exercise. I do not want endless confrontation for its own sake.

### Where I disagree
But I do **not** accept the repeated attempt to treat **“no plant closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute starting lines**. That position may be understandable from a workforce perspective, but it is **not sufficient** to solve Volkswagen’s structural cost problem.

#### To IG Metall and the works council
You say you are prepared to discuss flexibility, productivity, voluntary exits, and employee contributions. That is constructive in principle.

But you continue to insist that:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of collective bargaining,
- and site/job guarantees through 2030

must come first.

That is **not a solution**. That is a demand that management certify the outcome before the economics are fixed. I cannot accept that.

If you want closures off the table, then I need a package that produces **closure-level savings with equal durability and enforceability**. Not slogans. Not vague productivity promises. Not an open-ended hope that attrition will do the job over time. I mean real, quantified, structural relief.

That would need to include, at minimum:
- significant and immediate labour-cost reduction,
- meaningful working-time flexibility,
- substantial headcount reduction,
- fixed-cost reduction at site level,
- and a credible mechanism to lock in those savings.

If that cannot be delivered, then closures remain an option. I am not making that threat for effect. I am stating the economic reality.

#### To the works council
You say closures and compulsory redundancies cross a line. I understand the political and social meaning of that. But I will not allow that line to become a veto over competitiveness.

A company cannot remain viable by promising that no site will ever be touched. The question is not whether a plant has history; the question is whether it can remain part of a sustainable cost structure. If not, then preserving it indefinitely would be irresponsible to the rest of the group.

#### To Lower Saxony
I respect your concern for the regional base, but I reject the idea that the state can support restructuring while ruling out the instruments that may be necessary to make it real.

Yes, there should be a negotiated solution. Yes, we should exhaust non-closure measures. But no, I do not accept that closures are illegitimate in principle if the economics require them. A blanket rejection of hard decisions is not stewardship; it is delay.

#### To the owners
I agree with you on the need for structural improvement. I also agree that management must present hard numbers, not just rhetoric.

But let me be clear: I will not let “economics first” become an excuse for pushing all the pain onto the workforce while avoiding a real governance decision. If the owners want a durable turnaround, then they need to support a package that actually changes the cost base and capacity footprint. That means backing management when necessary, not merely asking for toughness from the sideline.

#### To the investors
You are right to demand measurable savings and a credible path back to competitive margins. That is exactly what management is trying to deliver.

But I do not accept the notion that only one form of restructuring counts as credible. There are alternatives to closures — but they must be **large enough** and **fast enough**. If they are not, then we are wasting time.

### My position, plainly stated
I remain open to:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- attrition,
- reallocation,
- product allocation changes,
- site reconfiguration,
- productivity commitments,
- and negotiated labour-cost relief.

I am **not** open to:
- a deal that keeps every site intact regardless of economics,
- a settlement that leaves the cost base fundamentally uncompetitive,
- or a package that postpones the necessary adjustment until the problem becomes worse.

### What I need to see
If the employee side wants closures removed from the table, then bring forward a package that delivers:
1. material capacity reduction,
2. a substantial and durable labour-cost contribution,
3. a real reduction in fixed costs,
4. and enforceable implementation.

If that package exists, I will engage seriously. If it does not, then I will continue to argue that closures and deeper restructuring remain on the table.

### Bottom line
I am not here to protect comfort. I am here to restore Volkswagen’s competitiveness and create room to invest through 2030. That requires structural change, not declarations.

So let us stop pretending that absolute red lines can substitute for an economically workable solution. They cannot.
