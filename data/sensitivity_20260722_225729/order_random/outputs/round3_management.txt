Thank you. The specialist input confirms what management has been saying from the start: **non-closure alternatives are possible only if they are economically equivalent to hard restructuring**. That is exactly the standard I am applying.

Let me respond directly.

## Where I agree
I agree with the expert on several points:
- A cosmetic package will fail.
- A no-closure solution can only work if it removes **real capacity**, **real fixed cost**, and **real headcount**.
- Working-time changes, voluntary exits, product allocation, and overhead cuts can contribute materially.
- Enforcement and milestones are essential.

That is useful, because it moves the discussion from ideology to economics.

## Where I disagree with the employee side
IG Metall and the works council keep saying “no closures, no compulsory redundancies” as if that were the solution. It is not. It is a **constraint**. If they want closures off the table, then they must help deliver a package that achieves the same effect on cost and capacity. The specialist has now made clear what that requires:
- material capacity reduction,
- significant headcount reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- and plant-level simplification.

That is not the same as preserving the current footprint and calling it restructuring.

So I will be blunt: **I do not accept a settlement that protects every site and every legacy structure while leaving the cost base fundamentally uncompetitive.** If the package does not remove enough fixed cost, it is not sufficient.

## Where I disagree with Lower Saxony
Lower Saxony is right about one thing: Volkswagen is a regional anchor and social damage matters. But the state is still overreaching when it treats closures as something that should effectively be ruled out before the numbers are solved.

The expert’s analysis confirms that if the economic gap is large enough, then **some form of footprint rationalisation is unavoidable**. That may be formal closures, or it may be a no-closure package that is closure-equivalent in effect. What it cannot be is a political promise that preserves all sites regardless of economics.

So no: I will not accept a blanket “no closures” doctrine as a veto on competitiveness.

## Where I agree with owners and investors
I agree with the owners and investors that:
- value destruction is unacceptable,
- structural overcapacity cannot remain untouched,
- and the package must be measurable and durable.

That is the right lens. If Volkswagen is to fund future products and technology through 2030, it must free up cash now. We are not discussing comfort. We are discussing survival and competitiveness.

## What this means for the actual package
The expert has effectively defined the condition under which closures might be avoidable. I will take that seriously — but only if the employee side accepts the consequences.

A credible no-closure package would have to include, at minimum:
1. **quantified annual savings**
2. **permanent capacity reduction**
3. **substantial headcount reduction through voluntary exits and attrition**
4. **durable labour-cost relief**
5. **site specialization / consolidation**
6. **fixed-cost reductions at plant level**
7. **hard enforcement with triggers if targets are missed**

If those are not on the table, then “no closures” is just wishful thinking.

## My position
So let me restate management’s position clearly:

- We are open to a **no-closure restructuring**.
- We are **not** open to a weak compromise that leaves overcapacity intact.
- We are **not** open to a package that depends on vague promises or temporary concessions.
- We are **not** open to site/job guarantees that pre-empt the economics.

If the workforce wants closures removed from consideration, then the burden is on them to help build a package that is genuinely closure-equivalent in savings and durability.

## My challenge to the other parties
### To IG Metall / works council:
Bring:
- a quantified capacity reduction plan,
- a real headcount reduction path,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- and enforceable productivity commitments.

Not slogans. Not red lines. Numbers.

### To Lower Saxony:
If you want to avoid closures, use your influence to support a package that is economically sufficient. Do not simply repeat a political refusal and expect the problem to disappear.

### To the owners and investors:
Support management if we bring a real package. But do not drift into a settlement that looks tough in press releases and soft in the factory. I will not sign up to a cosmetic compromise.

## Bottom line
The expert has made the key point: **closure-level savings can be achieved without closures, but only through deep restructuring elsewhere**. That is acceptable. What is not acceptable is a deal that avoids the headline of closures while leaving the substance of overcapacity and uncompetitive labour costs untouched.

So my position remains:
- **deep structural savings, yes**
- **non-closure if truly equivalent, yes**
- **cosmetic peace, no**
- **preservation of the current cost base, no**

If the employee side can now engage on a package that actually closes the savings gap, I will engage constructively. If not, then closures remain a live option because the economics do not care about slogans.
