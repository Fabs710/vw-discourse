We agree with the expert and with management on the central point: **Volkswagen needs a restructuring that materially changes the economics**. A deal that preserves the current cost base and merely buys industrial peace would be unacceptable to capital markets.

### Where we agree
- The company needs **durable, measurable, enforceable savings**.
- **Cosmetic measures are not enough**.
- A prolonged conflict would destroy value.
- Non-closure measures can be valid **if** they are large enough to produce closure-equivalent savings.
- Implementation discipline matters as much as the headline agreement.

### Where we disagree
#### To IG Metall and the works council
Your insistence on **“no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” as absolute red lines** is not credible if the numbers do not work. It may be understandable politically, but it is not sufficient economically.

The expert has made clear what a real no-closure package would require:
- permanent capacity reduction,
- substantial headcount reduction,
- durable labour-cost relief,
- plant specialization or consolidation,
- and hard enforcement with milestones.

If you can help deliver that, then fine. But if your position is simply “no closures, no layoffs, and keep the footprint intact,” then you are not offering a restructuring — you are offering delay.

#### To Lower Saxony
We appreciate the concern for regional stability, but a blanket refusal of closures or forced redundancies cannot be allowed to function as a veto over competitiveness. If the alternative is a permanently uncompetitive cost base, then protecting every site becomes value-destructive.

#### To the owners
We agree that social conflict is bad governance. But “economics first” must not become code for a slow compromise that leaves the structural problem unresolved. Owners should back a package only if it truly resets the footprint and cost base.

#### To management
You are right to insist on hard economics, but the market will only reward a package that is:
- quantified,
- durable,
- and enforceable.

Threats alone are not enough. If you want investor support, show the savings, timing, transition costs, and enforcement mechanism clearly.

### Our position
We will support a restructuring if it delivers all of the following:
1. **material capacity reduction**
2. **durable labour-cost relief**
3. **meaningful headcount reduction**
4. **fixed-cost reduction at plant level**
5. **a credible path back to competitive margins**
6. **implementation certainty**

That can be achieved via:
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- wage moderation or deferred compensation,
- product and site rationalization,
- and, if necessary, closures or equivalent footprint rationalization.

### What is unacceptable
We will not support:
- a cosmetic compromise,
- broad guarantees that prevent real adjustment,
- or a settlement that leaves Volkswagen structurally impaired while claiming success.

### Bottom line
We are open to either closure or a genuinely closure-equivalent no-closure package. What we are **not** open to is a politically convenient settlement that fails to restore competitive margins.

If the employee side wants closures off the table, it must prove that its alternative is economically equivalent — not just symbolically preferable. Otherwise, the market will treat the outcome as insufficient.
