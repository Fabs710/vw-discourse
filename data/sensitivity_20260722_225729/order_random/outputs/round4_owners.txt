I will speak plainly.

I agree with the specialist and with management on one central point: **Volkswagen needs a restructuring that materially changes the economics**. A package that merely postpones the problem, preserves excess capacity, and buys a few months of peace would destroy value. That is unacceptable to us as controlling owners.

Where I diverge from the employee side is equally clear: **“no closures” and “no compulsory redundancies” cannot be absolute preconditions before the economics are solved**. If that becomes the rule, then the supervisory board is effectively being asked to approve permanent overcapacity and an uncompetitive cost base. We will not underwrite that.

## To IG Metall
You are right that workers should not carry the burden alone. I agree with that.  
You are also right that working-time flexibility, voluntary exits, product reallocation, wage moderation, and productivity gains are real tools. The specialist has confirmed that.

But you still have not answered the key question: **what happens if those tools do not close the savings gap?**  
Saying “closures must be off the table first” is not a solution; it is a constraint. If the non-closure route is truly equivalent, prove it with numbers and hard milestones. If it is not, then insisting on absolute red lines is not protecting the company — it is trapping it.

## To the works council
I understand your concern for jobs and sites. That is legitimate. But viability is not protected by preserving everything unchanged. If a site remains open but structurally underutilized, the problem only reappears later in a worse form.

So I support your willingness to discuss:
- voluntary exits,
- attrition,
- working-time flexibility,
- shift redesign,
- product and volume reallocation,
- and management-side cuts.

But I do **not** support a blanket guarantee that rules out every hard decision in advance.

## To Lower Saxony
Your concern for the regional industrial base is understandable and, in part, justified. Volkswagen’s legitimacy in Germany matters.  
But I do not accept a position that treats closures as politically impossible regardless of economics. If the footprint remains too large, then the company bleeds value. That hurts the region too.

If you want to protect viable sites, then help produce a package that genuinely restores competitiveness. Protection without competitiveness is temporary.

## To management
You are right to insist on real savings and enforceability. I support that.  
But I also want to be clear: governance support is not a blank cheque for threat-driven bargaining. If closures remain on the table, they must be there because the economics are real, not because pressure is the only design tool.

Bring a package that is:
- quantified,
- implementable,
- legally robust,
- and durable.

That is what will earn backing.

## To investors
You are right to reject cosmetic compromise. We share that view.  
But you should not confuse toughness with value creation. The best outcome is not the one that “hurts the most”; it is the one that restores margins and future investment capacity while avoiding needless instability.

## My position, clearly
As controlling owners, we will support:
- capacity reduction,
- labour-cost moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- working-time flexibility,
- productivity improvements,
- site and product reallocation,
- and targeted investment commitments.

We will **not** support:
- a settlement that preserves structural overcapacity,
- an outcome that permanently impairs competitiveness,
- or a deal that sacrifices long-term value for short-term calm.

## What would move us
A non-closure package can replace closures only if it is truly closure-equivalent in effect. That means:
1. quantified annual savings,
2. permanent capacity reduction,
3. meaningful headcount reduction,
4. durable labour-cost relief,
5. site specialization / consolidation,
6. and hard enforcement with milestones and fallback triggers.

Without that, closures remain a live option.

## Final point
I do not seek conflict. But I will not pretend that refusal to accept hard choices is a plan.  
If the employee side wants closures removed from consideration, it must help deliver an economically equivalent package. If it cannot, then insisting on absolute red lines is not stewardship — it is risk shifting onto the owners and, ultimately, onto Volkswagen itself.

We remain prepared to back a tough, balanced settlement. We are not prepared to underwrite permanent structural weakness.
