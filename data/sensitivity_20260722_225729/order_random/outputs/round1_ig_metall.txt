As IG Metall, our position is clear:

We will not accept a restructuring in which employees bear the entire burden through plant closures, compulsory redundancies, and unilateral wage cuts. That is not a viable basis for a durable settlement.

What we are prepared to discuss is a fair, shared-sacrifice package that secures sites and jobs, preserves the collective bargaining framework, and contributes to the company’s competitiveness without destroying its industrial base. That means:

- no compulsory redundancies,
- no plant closures,
- no erosion of the collective wage agreement,
- and no transfer of the crisis costs onto the workforce alone.

We have already shown willingness to negotiate constructive alternatives: capacity adjustments, working-time solutions, moderation on labour costs, and even employee contributions if management, owners, and the company also make a credible contribution. But any contribution from the workforce must be matched by binding guarantees on employment, sites, and investment.

Our priorities are:
1. Protect jobs and sites.
2. Preserve collective bargaining standards.
3. Achieve a fair distribution of the burden.
4. Secure a sustainable industrial future for the plants in Germany.

If management insists on closures, forced layoffs, or permanent one-sided cuts, then we will resist that forcefully, including with industrial action.
