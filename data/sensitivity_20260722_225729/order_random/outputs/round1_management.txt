Let me be very clear.

I agree with one point that has come through strongly: Volkswagen needs a **credible, durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic package that leaves the cost base unchanged. We do not have time for delay, and we cannot continue with a structure that keeps the brand below target and starves investment into 2030.

But I disagree with the idea that we can solve this by simply protecting every site and every rule as they are.

### To IG Metall
I hear your refusal of compulsory redundancies, unilateral wage cuts, and a one-sided burden on the workforce. I also hear your willingness to discuss working-time, productivity, and employee contributions. That is the right direction in principle.

But I need to be equally direct: **a package with no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no meaningful labour-cost contribution is not enough**. If that is the ceiling, then we will not restore competitiveness. We will merely slow the decline.

The company’s German cost base is too high. Capacity is too high for the market we now face. That must be reduced in a way that is **structural and enforceable**. If you want me to rule out closures, then I need a substitute that produces savings of comparable scale and permanence — not just promises, not just natural attrition over many years, and not a short-term truce.

What I could consider:
- significant working-time flexibility,
- real wage moderation or deferred elements,
- substantial job reductions through attrition and voluntary programs,
- site-specific productivity commitments,
- and binding measures that reduce capacity and fixed cost in a durable way.

What I cannot accept:
- a deal that preserves all sites at current volumes,
- a deal that protects every job regardless of economics,
- or a framework that locks in uncompetitive labour costs.

### To the controlling owners
I appreciate the support for structural change and the recognition that value-destroying conflict helps no one. That said, I want to be equally clear: this cannot become a search for theoretical toughness without delivery.

If we are serious, then we need governance that supports a real outcome, not a symbolic endorsement of “hard decisions.” I am not asking for theatre. I am asking for a package that can actually be implemented and that will restore margins. If the answer is only “be tougher,” but no willingness to back a durable settlement, then that helps no one.

### To Lower Saxony
I understand the political and regional concern, and I understand the importance of Volkswagen to the industrial base of the state. But I cannot pretend that the regional footprint is untouchable if the economics do not work.

I will not accept a solution that preserves the status quo and leaves the company structurally uncompetitive. That would ultimately be worse for the plants, the workforce, and the state. A settlement must be judged by whether it secures Volkswagen’s future — not by whether it postpones hard choices.

### My position
Here is the line I am drawing:

- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring.
- **Yes** to capacity reduction.
- **Yes** to durable labour-cost contributions.
- **Yes** to investment commitments tied to future products and technologies.
- **No** to cosmetic savings.
- **No** to a settlement that leaves the cost base fundamentally intact.
- **No** to a package that gives away competitiveness in exchange for peace.

I remain open on the exact instrument:
- closures,
- site reconfiguration,
- transfers,
- working-time measures,
- voluntary exit programs,
- productivity agreements.

But the outcome must be **material and lasting**. If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must come forward with a package that achieves the same economic effect at the required scale. Otherwise, I will continue to argue that closure and capacity reduction remain on the table.

That is the reality of the situation, and I will not dilute it.
