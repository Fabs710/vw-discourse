### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties accept that Volkswagen needs a **credible, durable restructuring**.
- There is broad agreement that the current **cost base and capacity structure require adjustment**.
- Most parties accept that some mix of **capacity reduction, productivity gains, working-time flexibility, and investment commitments** will be needed.
- There is shared concern that **open-ended conflict or purely cosmetic measures** would damage the company.

### 2) Where the core disagreements remain
- **Plant closures:**  
  - Union, works council, and Lower Saxony reject closures as a default or acceptable solution.  
  - Management, controlling owners, and investors keep closures on the table if needed for economic reasons.
- **Compulsory redundancies:**  
  - Union, works council, and Lower Saxony oppose them.  
  - Management and owners will not rule them out absolutely.
- **Collective bargaining / wage framework:**  
  - Employee representatives want no erosion of standards.  
  - Management and capital-side actors want meaningful labour-cost adjustment and do not accept current terms as fixed.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  - All sides say it should be shared, but they disagree on what counts as a fair and sufficient contribution.
- **Security versus flexibility:**  
  - Employee-side actors want binding guarantees on jobs, sites, and investment before accepting sacrifices.  
  - Management and investors want hard, enforceable savings commitments before ruling out structural measures.

### 3) What key trade-offs are still unaddressed
- **Which specific measures can deliver savings at the scale of closures without closing plants?**
- **How much labour-cost relief is required, and through what instruments** (wage moderation, working time, deferred pay, attrition, voluntary exits)?
- **What binding guarantees can be offered on sites, jobs, and investment, and for how long?**
- **How will implementation be enforced** so that any compromise is durable and measurable?
- **How to balance regional/political stability with economic restructuring** without leaving overcapacity unresolved?

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from general positions to a **menu of concrete options** with estimated savings, timelines, and enforceability.
- Identify which **non-closure alternatives** could realistically replace closure-level savings.
- Clarify the **minimum guarantees** employee representatives would need and the **minimum economic improvements** management and investors require.
- Narrow the discussion to a **package architecture**: site protection, capacity reduction, labour-cost measures, and investment commitments.
