I agree with one part of the owners’ message: Volkswagen needs a credible restructuring, not symbolic gestures. The company cannot ignore its cost base or structural overcapacity, and we all have a duty to secure the long-term future of the group.

But I draw a clear line on the means.

A settlement that relies on plant closures in Germany, compulsory redundancies, or a one-sided transfer of the crisis onto employees is not acceptable from Lower Saxony’s point of view. That would damage the regional economy, weaken the company’s social legitimacy, and likely harden resistance rather than restore competitiveness. It would also be politically and economically shortsighted.

To the union: I share your central concerns. No forced redundancies and no blanket closure strategy are exactly the right starting point. But I would add that preserving jobs and sites will only be sustainable if the package also delivers real productivity gains and cost relief. We cannot ask the company to shoulder all the adjustment while leaving structural problems untouched.

So my position is this:

- I support a negotiated restructuring.
- I support capacity reductions, flexibility, and phased adjustment.
- I support employee contributions if they are fairly shared and matched by binding commitments from management and owners.
- I do not support plant closures as the default solution.
- I do not support compulsory redundancies if there are workable alternatives.

What would move me is a serious social-partnership package that:
1. preserves core sites and the state’s industrial base,
2. avoids mass layoffs,
3. produces durable savings,
4. includes credible investment commitments for future products and technologies,
5. and respects co-determination and the special governance arrangements that are part of Volkswagen’s stability.

If management wants Lower Saxony’s support, it needs to show that restructuring can be done without gutting the regional footprint. If the proposal remains a closure-and-cut plan, then I will oppose it.
