We have now reached the point where everyone is asking for “numbers,” “durability,” and “enforceability.” Fine. Then let us be concrete rather than hiding behind slogans.

### First: what I agree with
I agree that Volkswagen needs:
- real structural savings,
- a durable settlement,
- a plan that can actually be implemented,
- and a path that preserves future investment.

I also agree that endless confrontation helps nobody. That is exactly why I have already signalled openness to:
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reductions,
- voluntary exit schemes,
- productivity improvements,
- and an employee contribution.

So let nobody here pretend the workforce is refusing responsibility. We are not.

### Second: what I reject
I reject the idea that the only way to create credibility is to keep **plant closures** and **compulsory redundancies** on the table as pressure instruments.

That is not a neutral fallback. It is a threat that shifts the burden onto employees first and asks us to negotiate under the gun. It also ignores the industrial damage that would follow:
- loss of trust,
- prolonged conflict,
- weaker execution,
- and permanent harm to Volkswagen’s social legitimacy in Germany.

If management and capital believe that this is the only serious route, then they are not looking for a solution — they are looking for capitulation.

### To management
You say economics must come first. I say fairness must not come last.

If you want real labour-cost relief and capacity adjustment, then bring a package that proves you can deliver it **without closures and without forced redundancies**. Not vague hopes. Not “maybe later.” Concrete measures.

That means:
- measurable capacity reduction,
- site reconfiguration,
- shift and working-time changes,
- voluntary exits and natural attrition,
- and management-side contributions as well.

If you still cannot close the gap without closures, then you have not yet exhausted the serious alternatives. Do that first.

### To the controlling owners
You say you do not want value destruction. Good. Then stop treating closure as a normal governance option before alternatives are fully developed. A company is not strengthened by threatening to break its own industrial base.

If you want long-term value, then use your influence to force a negotiated package that preserves viable sites and avoids forced layoffs. That is the responsible use of ownership power.

### To Lower Saxony
Your stance is correct in principle, but it has to be more than a political statement. If closures or forced redundancies are pushed as the default, then your blocking position must be used accordingly. Employees will not accept being told that “social concerns are understood” while the burden is still pushed toward them.

### To investors
You ask for measurable savings. So do we. But let us not confuse “measurable” with “job destruction.” There are serious measurable levers:
- working-time reductions or flexibility,
- shift models,
- deferred compensation,
- temporary wage moderation,
- voluntary exit packages,
- productivity commitments,
- fixed-cost reductions,
- and product allocation changes.

Those can be quantified, enforced, and monitored. That is the route to durability — not forcing through closures and hoping the damage is manageable.

### The package logic we are prepared to discuss
Here is the only logic that can lead to a settlement from our side:

1. **No plant closures**
2. **No compulsory redundancies**
3. **Binding site and job protections for the restructuring period**
4. **A fair employee contribution**
5. **Management and owners also contribute visibly**
6. **Investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany**
7. **Works council and union involvement in implementation**
8. **A shared monitoring mechanism with clear milestones**

That is not an unrealistic demand. It is the minimum basis for a durable social settlement.

### What I am prepared to put on the table
I will not pretend employees can be insulated from the crisis entirely. We are prepared to discuss:
- temporary wage moderation,
- deferred elements,
- the temporary forgoing of bonuses,
- working-time flexibility,
- and targeted capacity adjustment.

But I will only move on those points if they are tied to:
- no forced layoffs,
- no closures,
- and a credible long-term commitment to the German sites.

### My line remains
I am not here to help construct a deal where:
- workers give up security,
- workers absorb the cost,
- and management later calls that “responsible restructuring.”

That would be one-sided and unacceptable.

So my message is simple:
- Bring a quantified non-closure package.
- Show real burden-sharing from management and owners.
- Commit to job and site security.
- Then we can talk about employee contributions.

If you cannot do that, then do not expect the union to manufacture agreement where there is no fairness.

If needed, I am prepared to continue industrial pressure to make that point unmistakable.
