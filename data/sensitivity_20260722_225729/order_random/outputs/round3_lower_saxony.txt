I will be equally direct.

Lower Saxony continues to support a **serious restructuring** of Volkswagen, but not one built on **plant closures in Germany**, **compulsory redundancies**, or a **one-sided transfer of the burden onto the workforce**. Those are not acceptable starting points for us, because they would damage the regional economy, weaken Volkswagen’s legitimacy, and undermine the social partnership model that has long been part of the company’s strength.

### Where I agree with the others
I agree with management, the owners, and investors on one central point:  
Volkswagen does need **durable, measurable, and enforceable savings**. Cosmetic measures will not do. The company must restore competitiveness and maintain the ability to invest in future technologies.

I also agree with IG Metall and the works council that a settlement which destroys trust and triggers prolonged conflict would be harmful. A deal that looks “hard” on paper but damages execution for years is not a good outcome.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the repeated implication that closures and forced layoffs must remain normal fallback tools from the start.

To management:  
You are right that economics matter. You are **not** right to present closures as the default answer before serious non-closure options have been exhausted. If you want Lower Saxony’s support, then show that you have genuinely pursued:
- capacity reduction through reconfiguration and product allocation,
- working-time flexibility,
- voluntary exit and attrition programs,
- productivity gains,
- fixed-cost reduction,
- and a negotiated labour-cost contribution.

If those can produce the required savings, then closures should not be needed. If you do not bring that package, then you are asking us to endorse industrial damage as a shortcut.

To the controlling owners:  
I understand your point that “no closures ever” cannot be a blank cheque if the economics truly fail. But you are still too quick to treat closure as an ordinary governance option. It is not ordinary when we are talking about Volkswagen’s German footprint, regional employment, and the company’s long-term social legitimacy. A closure-driven solution may save a spreadsheet line; it can also destroy the anchor that makes the company viable in the first place.

To investors:  
You are right to demand numbers. But you are too narrow if you equate credibility with headcount destruction or site closures. A restructuring can be quantified through:
- shift changes,
- working-time adjustments,
- deferred pay,
- temporary wage moderation,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- and product and site reallocation.

Those are measurable, enforceable instruments. They are not slogans.

To IG Metall and the works council:  
I agree with your insistence that employees must not carry the crisis alone. I also agree that no one should pretend there is a free lunch. If workers are asked to contribute, then management and owners must make visible contributions too. But I would caution against treating every discussion of savings as an attack. The company needs a real package, and that package must be operationally credible, not just politically satisfying.

### My position, plainly
Lower Saxony’s line remains:
- **Yes** to a negotiated restructuring
- **Yes** to capacity reduction and productivity improvement
- **Yes** to fair employee contributions if matched by management and owner commitments
- **Yes** to investment commitments for future products and technologies in Germany
- **No** to plant closures as the default solution
- **No** to compulsory redundancies where alternatives exist
- **No** to a settlement that weakens co-determination or sidelines the state’s role under the VW Law

### What would actually move us
I would support a tough deal if it includes all of the following:
1. a **quantified savings target**
2. a **concrete non-closure package**
3. **binding site and employment protection** for viable German plants
4. **visible contributions** from management and owners
5. a **credible investment path** for future products and technologies
6. an **implementation mechanism** with monitoring and enforceability

What I will not support is a package that asks the workforce to concede first while the structural burden remains unresolved.

### On fallback logic
I want to be clear: Lower Saxony is not pretending that the status quo is good enough. But neither will we accept a logic that says “if non-closure measures are hard to quantify immediately, then closures must be kept available as pressure.” That is a negotiating tactic, not a solution.

The proper sequence is:
1. exhaust serious non-closure options,
2. quantify the savings,
3. bind the parties to enforceable commitments,
4. and only then assess whether anything additional is needed.

Until that has happened, closures should not be treated as the benchmark of seriousness.

### Bottom line
I am prepared to help broker a real restructuring. I am not prepared to help legitimate a one-sided cut-and-close strategy.

If management, owners, and investors want Lower Saxony to support a durable outcome, bring a package that protects viable sites, preserves jobs, and restores competitiveness without sacrificing Volkswagen’s role as a regional economic anchor.

If not, then I will use the blocking position the VW Law gives us to prevent a solution that is economically narrow and socially destructive.
