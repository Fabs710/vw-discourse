We have now heard the numbers, and they do **not** change the core position of the Works Council.

### To management
You say the expert confirms that a no-closure package might deliver **€2.5bn to €4bn** annually if it is serious. Fine. That is exactly the point: **serious**.  
So stop talking as if closures are the natural starting point. If there is a real restructuring route without closures, then that route must be pursued first — not used as a bargaining prop while plants are held hostage.

You still have not shown that every non-closure option has been exhausted. And you have certainly not shown why workers should accept the destruction of jobs and sites when the expert says a strong negotiated package can deliver substantial savings.

What I do **not** accept is your framing that we must choose between:
- plant closures and
- “real” restructuring.

That is a false choice.

### To the investors
You want certainty and durable savings. So do we — but not by tearing up the company’s industrial base. The expert has just confirmed that a serious no-closure package can be meaningful, not cosmetic. That means your insistence on closures as the benchmark is overreach.

If the package delivers:
- durable labor-cost contributions,
- capacity reduction without closures,
- productivity gains,
- and management restraint,

then it is real restructuring. It may not be the easiest solution for the market, but it is the one that preserves the company’s legitimacy and avoids self-inflicted damage.

What you are still missing is this: **strikes, morale damage, and loss of know-how are not side effects; they are costs.** If you force closures, you may improve an Excel sheet and damage the enterprise for years.

### To the controlling owners
You speak about value destruction through confrontation. Correct. But closures can also destroy value through escalation, permanent distrust, and the collapse of the employment foundation that Volkswagen was built on.

You say you are open to a no-closure route if it is large and enforceable. Then support it — not rhetorically, but in governance terms. Use your weight to stop a closure-first agenda. The workforce has already signaled willingness to contribute. What is missing is management and owner willingness to treat the sites as assets, not expendable cost units.

### To Lower Saxony
Your intervention is important, but I need to be blunt: your refusal to accept plant closures must be more than sympathy. You have a blocking position and you should use it. The expert has given you the economic basis for a negotiated path that protects sites.

So the question is no longer whether alternatives exist. They do. The question is whether the board majority is willing to respect the social and industrial reality of Germany’s largest carmaker.

### Our position, plainly
We are prepared to negotiate a **serious, quantified restructuring package**. But that package must include:
- **binding guarantees for every German plant**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **no closure decisions by the back door**
- **durable co-determination**
- **real investment commitments through 2030**
- **shared burden-bearing from management and owners**

What we are **not** prepared to accept:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or a deal that treats employee concessions as one-way street sacrifices.

### On the expert assessment
The expert said a no-closure route can deliver a substantial restructuring effect. That supports our position, not yours.

It means:
- there is an alternative,
- it can be economically meaningful,
- and it can preserve industrial peace if management stops threatening closures.

So if management now says “the numbers are not enough unless we keep closures on the table,” then my answer is:  
**Bring a concrete, transparent bridge to prove that. Otherwise, you are not negotiating — you are pressuring.**

### Bottom line
The Works Council will not move on its red lines.

If Volkswagen wants a durable solution, then it must be a **site-preserving, no-forced-layoff package** with real savings, real shared sacrifice, and binding guarantees. Anything else will be resisted with the full leverage of co-determination and the workforce.
