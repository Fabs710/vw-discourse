We agree on one thing: **Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.** But let me be equally clear: **durable does not mean that workers carry the whole burden while management, owners, and capital are spared.** That is exactly the kind of “solution” that will fail both industrially and socially.

### Where I agree
- The cost base has to improve.
- The current situation is serious and cannot be brushed aside.
- Deal certainty matters.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to a long conflict — **if** it is fair and balanced.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept that plant closures and forced redundancies are the starting point or the default answer.

If the company’s competitiveness is to be restored, then the adjustment must be shared:
- management must contribute,
- owners must contribute,
- capital must accept lower near-term returns,
- and the workforce can contribute through agreed measures.

But there will be **no acceptance of a package where jobs are cut, sites are closed, and employees pay the price alone**.

### Our position
We have already shown willingness to negotiate:
- labour-cost contributions,
- capacity reductions,
- working-time adjustments,
- and giving up parts of employee income and guarantees.

That remains on the table. But in return we need:
1. **No compulsory redundancies**
2. **No plant closures**
3. **Site and future-investment commitments**
4. **A fair distribution of sacrifice**
5. **A collective agreement that preserves the wage framework**

### On the management line
You say closures remain on the table because capacity exceeds demand. We say: **then prove that every alternative has been exhausted before talking about shutting plants and destroying livelihoods.** We will not accept a restructuring logic that begins with layoffs and ends with workers expected to absorb the pain.

### On the investors’ line
You want “real restructuring.” Fine. But real restructuring is not the same as social demolition. If you demand structural savings, then you must also accept structural employee contributions and management restraint — not just headcount reduction.

### On the owners’ and state’s concerns
You both talk about long-term value and legitimacy. Then you should understand this: **there is no long-term value if the workforce is treated as disposable.** Industrial peace and legitimacy are not nice extras; they are preconditions for execution.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to reach a tough settlement, but only on a **shared-sacrifice package** that protects jobs and sites. If the company insists on closures or compulsory redundancies, then you should assume we will use the full force of our leverage. That is not a threat for effect; it is the reality of this bargaining situation.

If management wants seriousness from us, then it must show seriousness about **keeping people employed and keeping sites open**.
