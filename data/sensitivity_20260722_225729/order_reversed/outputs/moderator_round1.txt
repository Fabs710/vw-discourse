### 1) What has been agreed
- All parties agree Volkswagen needs a **real, durable restructuring**, not a cosmetic compromise.
- There is broad agreement that the current **German cost base and capacity structure are not competitive**.
- All parties prefer a **negotiated settlement** over a prolonged conflict if it can deliver credible savings and implementation certainty.
- There is general acceptance that the solution must include **material economic improvement** and **be implementable**.

### 2) Core disagreements
- **Plant closures / capacity reduction:**  
  Management, institutional investors, and the controlling owners treat closures and deep capacity reduction as potentially necessary; Lower Saxony, IG Metall, and the Works Council reject closures as the default or acceptable starting point.
- **Job security:**  
  The employee side insists on **no compulsory redundancies** and strong job guarantees; management and investors have not accepted that constraint.
- **Burden-sharing:**  
  Unions and employee representatives want a shared-sacrifice model including management/owner contributions; investors emphasize economics first and have not committed to specific non-labor concessions.
- **Role of state and co-determination:**  
  Lower Saxony and employee representatives insist on preserving their governance influence and regional industrial base; management and investors prioritize speed, certainty, and competitiveness over preserving every site.
- **Definition of acceptable restructuring:**  
  Investors and management define it by measurable cost reduction and margin improvement; employee-side stakeholders define it by preserving employment, sites, and industrial legitimacy alongside savings.

### 3) Key trade-offs still unaddressed
- **How much cost reduction is required** versus **how many jobs/sites can be protected**.
- Whether savings will come primarily from **closures/headcount reduction** or from **wage, working-time, productivity, and overhead measures**.
- How to balance **deal certainty** against the risk of **industrial conflict** if site closures or layoffs are pursued.
- Whether the package can be large enough to restore competitiveness **without undermining labor peace, co-determination, and regional legitimacy**.
- How to ensure any labor contribution is **durable and enforceable**, while also preserving **future investment capacity**.

### 4) Brief refocus for the next round
- Move from principle statements to **specific package elements**: cost target, expected savings, timing, and implementation mechanism.
- Ask each side to specify its **minimum acceptable terms** and **non-negotiable red lines**.
- Test alternative combinations:  
  **(a)** closures/headcount reductions,  
  **(b)** no-closure packages with deeper labor and productivity concessions,  
  **(c)** mixed packages with phased implementation.
- Focus discussion on **who bears what share of the adjustment**, and what level of savings each side would consider sufficient to support a deal.
