I hear the concerns, but let me be direct: **Volkswagen does not have the luxury of a soft, symbolic adjustment.** The current German cost and capacity structure is not competitive enough, and that is the core problem we have to solve.

### Where I agree
I agree with all of you on one essential point: **the restructuring must be real and durable**.  
A cosmetic deal, a delay, or a package that preserves the current cost base would be a failure. I also agree that **deal certainty matters**. Prolonged conflict, strikes, and uncertainty would hurt the company, the brand, and investor confidence.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the idea that plant closures are somehow an unacceptable framing from the outset. If capacity exceeds demand and sites are structurally uncompetitive, then **closures have to remain on the table**. We cannot pretend that every site can be protected regardless of economics.

To the Lower Saxony point: I understand the political and social importance of the sites. But protecting regional employment cannot mean freezing the company in a cost structure that makes the core brand weaker every year. **A viable company is the best protection for employment in the long run.** Without material restructuring, we are simply postponing bigger losses.

To the investor point: yes, the package must be credible and measurable. But credibility requires more than vague assurances — it requires **hard savings**. If we cannot secure a genuine labour contribution and structural capacity reduction, then we will not restore margins.

### What I am insisting on
Any acceptable outcome must include:
- **material capacity reduction**
- **permanent labour-cost improvement**
- **a meaningful workforce contribution**
- **a durable path to the 2030 return-on-sales target**
- **implementation certainty, not open-ended negotiations**

That can be through plant closures, workforce reductions, pay and working-time measures, or a combination — but the scale has to be sufficient. **I am not interested in a deal that preserves excess capacity and calls it restructuring.**

### On the boundary
Let me be explicit: if the discussion ends in a package that avoids the difficult decisions but leaves us structurally uncompetitive, **I will not consider that acceptable**. We need a settlement that restores competitiveness for the medium term, not one that merely buys a few quarters of peace.

So my position is simple:  
**We are prepared to negotiate on the means, but not on the need for deep structural change.** The economics must improve materially, and the workforce and governance stakeholders have to engage with that reality.
