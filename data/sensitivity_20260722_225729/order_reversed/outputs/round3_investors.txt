We agree with the basic framing from management and the expert: **Volkswagen needs durable, quantified restructuring, not a cosmetic deal**.

But we are not interested in a theoretical debate about “site preservation” versus “closures.” We care about a narrower question:

**Can the package credibly restore competitive margins on a durable basis, at acceptable execution risk?**

## Where we agree
- A **real restructuring** is necessary.
- The current German cost base is **not competitive**.
- **Deal certainty** matters.
- A prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would be value-destructive.

## Where we disagree
### To IG Metall and the Works Council
Your red lines are still too absolute.

- **No closures**
- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No erosion of guarantees**
- **No structural damage to sites**

If those are non-negotiable, then you are effectively asking shareholders to accept an outcome that may leave the company **structurally overcosted**. That is not a restructuring; it is deferral.

The expert’s numbers do **not** support your claim that a no-closure package is automatically sufficient. They support only this: it **might** be sufficient **if** it is at the upper end of the range, durable, and enforceable. That is a very different proposition.

### To Lower Saxony
We understand the political and regional stakes. But your position still risks privileging industrial legitimacy over economic repair.

If the package avoids closures but does not fully fix margins, then you are protecting a regional footprint at the expense of long-term enterprise value. That is not a sustainable bargain for shareholders.

### To management
We support firmness, but not a maximalist position that triggers unnecessary escalation.

If you cannot show that the no-closure route is insufficient, then do not pretend closures are the only credible instrument. Markets will see that as brinkmanship. You need numbers, not just leverage.

## What would move us
A no-closure package could be acceptable **only if it is quantitatively equivalent or close to equivalent** to closure-led savings, and if it is hard to unwind.

That means we would need:
- a **clear annual savings target**
- a **specific capacity reduction**
- **permanent labor-cost adjustments**
- **headcount reduction through attrition/redeployment/voluntary exits**
- **management overhead cuts**
- and **a built-in trigger** if savings are missed

## Our position on the facilitator options

### 1) No-closure first, with automatic review trigger
This is the only route that sounds remotely credible to us **if** the trigger is real and not political theater.

But the review cannot be a loophole to stall. It must be:
- date certain,
- metric driven,
- and tied to actual savings delivery, not intentions.

If the package underdelivers, then footprint reduction must become mandatory, not optional.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies plus voluntary footprint adjustment
This is acceptable **in principle**, but only if “voluntary” is not code for “too small to matter.”

Voluntary exits, early retirement, and redeployment can help. They are not enough on their own unless they produce meaningful permanent savings.

### 3) Shared-burden package
We support management and owners contributing too. That is reasonable.

But we will not confuse symbolic top-end restraint with operational restructuring. Executive pay freezes or owner messaging do not substitute for real cost reduction in the plant network.

## What we would need to support
A package could earn our support if it gets close to these conditions:

- **Annual durable savings in the upper end of the expert range**
- **Visible capacity rationalization**
- **Permanent labor-cost reductions**
- **Implementation certainty**
- **A credible fallback to footprint reduction if targets are missed**
- **No open-ended timetable**

If the proposal stays vague, or relies on hope that productivity will somehow appear, we would oppose it.

## Our bottom line
We are open to a negotiated no-closure solution.  
We are **not** open to a settlement that preserves excess structure and calls it compromise.

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, it must offer a package that is:
- quantified,
- enforceable,
- durable,
- and economically large enough.

Otherwise, investors will conclude — correctly — that the problem has merely been postponed.
