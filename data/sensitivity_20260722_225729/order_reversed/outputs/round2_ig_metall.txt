We have heard the expert. The numbers matter, and they confirm something we have said from the start: **there is room for a serious restructuring without plant closures** — but only if everyone else is prepared to contribute in a real way.

### Where I agree
I agree with management, the owners, investors, and Lower Saxony on one thing:  
**Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.**

I also accept the expert’s core point:
- a no-closure package can produce **meaningful, durable savings**,
- but it requires **deep labor-cost, productivity, and capacity measures**,
- and it is not a free lunch.

That is exactly why IG Metall has already put a counter-proposal on the table and why we accepted warning strikes once the peace obligation ended. We are not defending the status quo. We are prepared to contribute.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the leap from “the restructuring must be real” to “therefore closures and forced redundancies are necessary.”

The expert’s own assessment shows that a no-closure package can plausibly deliver around **€2.5bn to €4bn per year** if it is serious. That is not cosmetic. That is a substantial restructuring.

So when management says closures remain on the table as a necessary instrument, I say:  
**only if the no-closure alternative is not genuinely enough — and that has to be proven, not assumed.**

And when investors say they will not support any package that preserves too much structure, I say:  
**fine — but then you must accept that the workforce’s contribution has to be real, durable, and shared.** Not just job cuts on the shop floor while management and capital remain protected.

### Our red lines remain
Let me be explicit, because this is the point where the pressure usually starts:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No erosion of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided adjustment where workers bear all the pain**

Those are not bargaining decorations. They are the conditions under which we can even talk about a durable settlement.

### What we are prepared to accept
We are still prepared to negotiate a serious shared-sacrifice package, including:
- a **meaningful employee financial contribution**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **capacity reductions without closures**
- **productivity improvements**
- **temporary or phased wage moderation**
- **redeployment and attrition-based headcount reduction**
- **management overhead restraint**
- **owner-side restraint on distributions and expectations**

That is a real contribution. It is also the only route that preserves industrial peace and gives Volkswagen a future as a large manufacturing employer in Germany.

### To management
You say you are open to a no-closure route only if it lands at the upper end of the savings range and is enforceable.

Then put your numbers on the table, but do not dress up threats as strategy. If you want a serious offer from us, you need to specify:
- what annual savings you require,
- how much comes from management and overhead,
- what you are willing to forgo at the top,
- and what investment guarantees accompany the package.

If the answer is simply “more labor cuts,” then you are not asking for partnership. You are asking the workforce to finance the entire turnaround.

### To investors
You ask whether the no-closure package is large enough. That is fair.

My answer is: **it can be, if you accept that the workforce’s contribution is matched by contributions from management, owners, and the company structure itself.**

Do not mistake “no closures” for “no restructuring.” A serious labor agreement can deliver durable savings through:
- wage moderation,
- flexibility,
- productivity gains,
- internal redeployment,
- and lower capacity utilization.

What it cannot and will not deliver is a blank check for indefinite sacrifice.

### To Lower Saxony
You are right that legitimacy matters. On that, we agree.

But legitimacy is not preserved by saying everything must remain unchanged. It is preserved by securing:
- jobs,
- sites,
- future investment,
- and fair burden-sharing.

That is why your support for a site-preserving negotiated path is important. Use it to push for a balanced settlement, not just to avoid confrontation.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to make a real contribution to Volkswagen’s recovery.  
But if management insists that restructuring means closures and compulsory layoffs, then we will resist that with all available means.

If you want a durable solution, then bring forward a **quantified no-closure package** with:
- binding job security,
- shared sacrifice,
- management restraint,
- and real investment commitments.

That is the only kind of compromise we will treat as serious.
