We are still converging on the diagnosis, but not yet on the remedy.

I agree with the institutional investors, the controlling owners, and management on one central point: **Volkswagen needs a durable improvement in competitiveness, not a cosmetic compromise**. If the package leaves the company structurally uncompetitive, it will fail the business, the workforce, and the regions that depend on it.

But I need to be equally clear: **I will not support a restructuring that uses plant closures or mass job losses in Lower Saxony as the default answer.** That is not a minor preference for me. It would damage the regional economy, undermine trust, and jeopardize Volkswagen’s legitimacy as an industrial anchor. That is not an acceptable price for speed.

### Where I agree
- **Real savings are necessary.**
- **The current cost base has to come down.**
- **A negotiated settlement is better than a prolonged strike**, if it is credible.
- **The solution must be durable and measurable**, not just a temporary truce.

### Where I disagree
To the investors: I understand your insistence on numbers and enforcement. That is legitimate. But if you insist that the only credible route is deep cuts through closures and headcount reduction, you are underestimating the political and industrial damage that can create. A package can be economically serious without being socially destructive.

To management: I agree that “soft” restructuring is not enough. But you cannot treat closures as a starting assumption and expect social peace to follow. A plan that begins by threatening plants will likely produce exactly the conflict you say you want to avoid.

To the controlling owners: I share your concern about value destruction through confrontation. But that cuts both ways. A closure-heavy strategy can also destroy value through strikes, reputational harm, and weakened workforce commitment. If you want durable value, then the workforce must see a future in the company.

### What I can support
I am open to a **serious, quantified restructuring package** if it is built around:
- capacity adjustments that do not automatically mean closures,
- working-time and flexibility measures,
- a meaningful but negotiated labour-cost contribution,
- management overhead discipline,
- productivity improvements,
- and binding investment/product commitments for the German sites.

I would also expect:
- **no sidestepping of co-determination**
- **no attempt to weaken the state’s blocking role**
- **no unilateral forcing through of plant decisions**

### What I need to see before I can consider support
If management and investors want me to back a package, then put concrete numbers on:
- expected annual savings,
- timing,
- the share coming from management and overhead,
- the share coming from labor,
- the capacity reduction required,
- and the specific site commitments that protect the industrial base in Lower Saxony.

If the only proposal is “closures plus layoffs,” then I will resist it.

### Bottom line
I am not defending the status quo. I am defending a **credible restructuring that preserves jobs, sites, and Volkswagen’s role as a regional anchor while restoring competitiveness**. If that can be achieved through a negotiated, quantified package, I am prepared to engage constructively. If not, I will not endorse a solution that sacrifices Lower Saxony’s industrial base for the sake of a faster accounting fix.
