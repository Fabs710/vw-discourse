We are aligned on the **diagnosis** and not yet on the **acceptable means**.

### Where I agree
I agree with institutional investors that:
- the current German cost base is not competitive,
- restructuring must be real and durable,
- and a negotiated settlement is preferable to an open-ended conflict.

I also agree with management that:
- the package must be large enough to matter,
- and “soft” measures that preserve the same economics under a different label are not acceptable.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the implication that the only credible route is immediate plant closure and large compulsory job cuts.

That is too narrow, and it risks destroying value through:
- prolonged industrial conflict,
- reputational damage,
- political escalation,
- and loss of execution capacity.

From a controlling-owner perspective, **value destruction through confrontation is not a restructuring strategy**. If closures become the only route because no one will negotiate serious alternatives, then we will have failed before the real work begins.

### What I need to see
I am open to supporting a difficult package, but it must be **quantified and enforceable**. Specifically:

1. **A credible annual cost reduction**
   - not aspirational, but measurable and locked in;
2. **Durable capacity adjustment**
   - not necessarily every plant closure, but real reduction where economics demand it;
3. **A meaningful labour contribution**
   - wage, working-time, or guarantee adjustments that are permanent enough to matter;
4. **Management discipline**
   - overhead and execution savings cannot fall only on the workforce;
5. **Implementation certainty**
   - whatever is agreed must survive beyond the current bargaining cycle.

### On “no closures”
I do not share the employee side’s absolute red line that all German plants must be preserved regardless of economics. That would be an unacceptable outcome if it leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive.

But I also do not support closures as a slogan. They must be justified by:
- economics,
- enforceability,
- and the broader industrial consequences.

If an alternative package can achieve the same savings with higher certainty and less damage, I would prefer that. If it cannot, then deeper structural measures remain on the table.

### To IG Metall and the Works Council
You are right that there is no long-term value if the workforce is treated as disposable. But there is also no long-term value if the company keeps carrying an uncompetitive structure for the sake of comfort.

If you want to avoid closures, then bring forward a package that is **at least as effective**:
- lower labour cost,
- lower capacity burden,
- higher productivity,
- and genuine durability.

Not intentions. Numbers.

### To management
You need to be careful not to turn a necessary restructuring into a governance crisis. If the plan is perceived as maximalist or politically naive, you will trigger resistance that undermines the very competitiveness you seek. I support firmness, but not self-defeating escalation.

### Bottom line
My support is available for a package that restores Volkswagen’s competitiveness **durably** and protects long-term value. I will not support a hollow compromise, and I will not support a conflict-driven outcome that damages the asset.

If the next step is to narrow this to a concrete package, then put numbers on:
- savings,
- timing,
- capacity reduction,
- and labour contribution.

Without that, this remains principle, not decision.
