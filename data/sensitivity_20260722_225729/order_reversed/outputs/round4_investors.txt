We are close to a decision point, and from the capital markets’ perspective the test is simple:

**Does the package credibly restore competitive margins on a durable basis, with enough certainty to justify holding the stock?**

Right now, our answer is: **not yet, unless the no-closure route is made materially more rigorous than what the employee side is describing.**

## Where we agree

- We agree with management that Volkswagen needs a **durable cost reset**, not a symbolic deal.
- We agree with the expert that a no-closure package **can** be economically meaningful.
- We agree with Lower Saxony and the workforce that a prolonged strike would destroy value.
- We agree that **deal certainty** and enforceability matter as much as the headline savings number.

## Where we disagree

### To IG Metall and the Works Council
Your position is still too absolute.

A package with:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of guarantees,
- and no real footprint change,

is **not automatically acceptable** just because it is politically easier. If it does not close the structural gap, then it is not restructuring; it is delay.

The expert’s range does **not** prove your preferred outcome works. It says only that it **might** work if it reaches the upper end of the savings range and if those savings are durable. That is a high bar, and you have not yet shown how you will clear it.

### To Lower Saxony
We understand the political and regional importance of site preservation. But from an equity-holder perspective, preserving every site is not a strategy if margins remain impaired. A structurally expensive German footprint will ultimately hurt the regional base too, through weaker investment and weaker returns.

### To management
We support firmness, but not brinkmanship. If you want investors to back a harder line, then show the economics clearly. Do not use closure threats as a substitute for evidence. We need quantified comparison, not leverage theater.

## What we need to support a no-closure settlement

A no-closure package could be supportable only if it includes all of the following:

1. **A quantified annual savings target near the upper end of the expert range**
   - In practical terms, we would look for something in the **€3bn-plus** neighborhood, not vague promises.

2. **Permanent labor-cost reduction**
   - Not one-off wage restraint.
   - Something durable enough to matter over several years.

3. **Real capacity reduction**
   - Through shifts, utilization, model simplification, line rationalization, and redeployment.
   - Not just spreading the same cost across fewer people.

4. **Meaningful headcount reduction**
   - Through attrition, early retirement, voluntary exits, and internal transfers.
   - “Voluntary” must be large enough to move the economics.

5. **Management overhead and complexity cuts**
   - This has to be a company-wide restructuring, not only a labor-side contribution.

6. **A binding review with a real fallback**
   - If targets are missed, the company cannot just reopen the same debate endlessly.
   - The fallback must be credible enough that everyone takes delivery seriously.

## Our position on the compromise options

### 1) Time-limited no-closure first phase with hard metrics
This is the only option we view as remotely credible.

But the metrics must be hard:
- date-certain review,
- measurable savings,
- measurable capacity change,
- and a pre-agreed consequence for underdelivery.

If the review is just a political pause, it will not be taken seriously by capital markets.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies plus structural adjustment
This is acceptable **in principle** if it produces real economics.

But we will not accept “voluntary” measures that are too small to matter. If the numbers do not move meaningfully, then the label does not matter.

### 3) Shared-burden package
Yes, management and owners should contribute. But symbolic restraint at the top does not solve an industrial cost problem. It supports a deal; it does not replace one.

## Our bottom line
We are prepared to back a **serious no-closure-first restructuring** only if it is:

- quantified,
- durable,
- enforceable,
- and economically close enough to a closure-led solution.

If the employee side insists that closures and compulsory redundancies are off the table under all circumstances, then the burden is on them to prove the remaining package is strong enough to restore margins. Until that proof exists, we remain skeptical.

**We will not support a cosmetic settlement that leaves the German cost base structurally uncompetitive.**
