We agree on one point: Volkswagen needs a durable solution, not a cosmetic one.

But I must be equally clear: **a “durable” solution cannot mean plant closures and forced redundancies in Germany.** That is not a negotiable preference for us; it is a red line.

### To management
You keep saying “closures remain on the table.” For the workforce, that is exactly the wrong starting point. It turns a restructuring discussion into a threat. We will not accept a fait accompli where plants are declared uncompetitive and workers are expected to pay with their jobs.

If the company wants seriousness, then it must put forward a plan that protects:
- **every German plant**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **binding job guarantees through 2030**
- **real investment and product commitments for the sites**

Without that, there is no basis for trust.

### To the investors
I understand the demand for competitiveness and credible savings. But do not confuse **real restructuring** with **social destruction**. You will not get sustainable value by breaking the company’s employment foundation and triggering long conflict. Strikes, loss of morale, and the destruction of expertise are not “noise”; they are economic damage.

If you want structural savings, then they must come from:
- internal efficiency measures,
- capacity adjustments without closures,
- working-time arrangements,
- management and overhead contributions,
- and a fair employee contribution that does not destroy the wage framework.

### To the controlling owners
You speak of long-term value and stability. Then you should know that value depends on the workforce’s trust and on preserving the company’s industrial base. A strategy that weakens co-determination, tears up historic guarantees, and pressures through closures is not stability — it is escalation.

### To Lower Saxony
I agree with your point that the company needs competitiveness, and I appreciate the recognition that legitimacy matters. But I want to be precise: **protecting sites is not a secondary issue**. It is the condition for any credible settlement.

### Our position
We are prepared to discuss:
- productivity improvements,
- capacity adjustments,
- flexible working-time solutions,
- wage and cost contributions,
- and a negotiated restructuring path.

But we are **not** prepared to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory layoffs,
- or the breaking of historic job-security commitments.

That is where we stand.

If management wants a settlement, it must come back with a package that keeps the German plants open, secures jobs to 2030, and treats the workforce as a partner in recovery — not as the reserve you cut when other options are exhausted. Until then, our resistance remains absolute.
