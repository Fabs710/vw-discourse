I agree with the central diagnosis from both the institutional investors and the controlling owners: Volkswagen needs a **real, durable improvement in competitiveness**, not a cosmetic arrangement that leaves the company with the same structural problems.

But I want to be equally clear about the boundary: **a solution that achieves that by gutting regional employment, forcing closures as the default option, or sidelining the state’s role is not acceptable.** I will not support a restructuring that treats sites and jobs in Lower Saxony as disposable.

### Where I agree
- The cost base must come down.
- The current structure is not sustainable in the long run.
- The company needs certainty and a solution that can actually be implemented.
- A negotiated settlement is preferable to a prolonged conflict if it is credible.

### Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the implication that **plant closures are the necessary starting point**. That may be one instrument, but it is not the only one, and it should not become the default answer before all other options are exhausted.

We have other levers:
- capacity adjustments without blanket closures,
- working-time flexibility,
- negotiated wage and cost contributions,
- redeployment and internal restructuring,
- investment commitments tied to productivity improvements,
- and product allocation decisions that strengthen existing sites.

A strategy that focuses too quickly on closures risks:
- destroying skilled employment,
- undermining local supply chains,
- weakening Volkswagen’s social legitimacy,
- and creating political and industrial backlash that could make implementation harder, not easier.

### My position
I am open to a **serious restructuring package** if it meets three conditions:
1. **It delivers real savings and competitiveness gains**
2. **It protects core sites and avoids mass layoffs wherever possible**
3. **It preserves co-determination and the state’s blocking and oversight role**

That means I can support a negotiated package with substantial workforce contributions and structural measures, **but not one that sacrifices Lower Saxony’s industrial base as the price of speed**.

### On certainty
I understand the investors’ demand for deal certainty. But certainty cannot be purchased by forcing through an outcome that is socially and politically unstable. A durable settlement requires legitimacy. If employees are pushed into a corner, you may get confrontation, not certainty.

### Bottom line
I am prepared to work toward a restructuring that restores Volkswagen’s competitiveness. I am **not** prepared to endorse a solution that hollowly “fixes” the balance sheet while damaging the company’s regional anchor role and employment base. If the savings can be achieved through a negotiated, site-preserving package, that is the path I will support. If not, I will resist a closure-driven approach.
