We are at the point where the discussion must stop circling and face the economic test.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, investors, the expert, and Lower Saxony on the central point: **Volkswagen needs a durable, enforceable restructuring that restores margins and competitiveness**. I also agree that **a prolonged strike or open-ended conflict would destroy value** and that no one should want that outcome.

I further accept the expert’s core conclusion: **a no-closure package is not automatically insufficient**. If it is large enough, durable enough, and enforceable enough, it can be economically credible.

That is important. It means we do **not** need to start from a closure-first mindset.

## Where I disagree

### To management
You are right to insist on numbers, but you are still too quick to treat footprint reduction as the default fallback.

That is not acceptable unless you can show, clearly and plant by plant, that the no-closure route cannot close the structural gap. Until then, closure talk remains leverage, not proof. I will not support bluffing as strategy.

### To investors
Your skepticism is justified, but your threshold is drifting toward a standard that may ignore the real cost of a destructive conflict. You are right that “no closures” alone is not enough. You are wrong if you imply that only closures can produce real discipline.

The expert has given a plausible route to a serious no-closure settlement. If it reaches the upper end of the range and is locked in properly, that can be enough. Anything less than that is not enough.

### To IG Metall and the Works Council
Your position is still too absolute in form, even if your economic instincts are not entirely wrong.

I do **not** accept the idea that:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no erosion of guarantees,
- and no meaningful footprint adjustment

can be declared sufficient by principle alone.

If you want the no-closure route to stand, you must show a **durable, quantified contribution** that really changes the cost base. Otherwise, you are asking shareholders to accept deferment dressed up as stability.

### To Lower Saxony
I respect the regional and political stakes. But regional legitimacy cannot be preserved by defending every site regardless of economics.

If the company remains structurally uncompetitive, the industrial base will still weaken — just more slowly and with more damage to trust. So I will not back a settlement that protects symbolism over value.

## My position
I am prepared to support a **serious no-closure-first settlement** if it contains all of the following:

- **quantified annual savings**
- **permanent labor-cost reduction**
- **real capacity reduction**
- **meaningful headcount reduction through voluntary exits, redeployment, and attrition**
- **management overhead and complexity cuts**
- **a fixed review point**
- **a credible fallback if delivery falls short**

That is the minimum standard.

What I will **not** support is a package that:
- preserves excess structure,
- relies on temporary concessions,
- or leaves Volkswagen structurally uncompetitive while calling the result a compromise.

## On the compromise options

### 1) Time-limited no-closure first phase with hard metrics
This is the only route I see as potentially workable.

But I want to be explicit: the review cannot be a ritual. It must be date-certain, metric-driven, and tied to actual savings delivery. If targets are missed, the fallback must be real enough that everyone takes the first phase seriously.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies plus structural adjustment
This is acceptable **only if** the adjustment is large enough to matter. “Voluntary” cannot be a euphemism for too little, too late.

### 3) Shared-burden package
Yes, management and owners should contribute. I support that. But symbolic restraint at the top is not a substitute for operational restructuring. It supports legitimacy; it does not solve the economic problem.

## What would move me
A no-closure package can earn my support if it is:
- near the upper end of the expert’s savings range,
- durable in law and practice,
- backed by real capacity and labor-cost changes,
- and paired with a credible enforcement mechanism.

If it does not meet that test, then I will not pretend it is sufficient simply because it avoids conflict. Avoiding conflict is desirable only if the company is actually fixed.

## Bottom line
I remain open to a negotiated no-closure solution.  
I am **not** open to a cosmetic settlement.  
And I am **not** prepared to accept a package that preserves structural overcost and calls it stability.

If needed, I support the expert’s call for more detailed evidence. At this stage, the decisive question is not rhetoric, but whether the no-closure package can genuinely deliver enough fixed-cost removal and capacity adjustment to protect long-term enterprise value.
