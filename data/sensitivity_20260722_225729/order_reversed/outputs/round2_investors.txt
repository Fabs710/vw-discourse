We are still in the same place on the economics, and I want to be explicit about that.

### What we agree on
- Volkswagen needs **real restructuring**, not a symbolic compromise.
- The current German cost base is **not competitive**.
- A **negotiated settlement** is preferable to a prolonged strike if it can deliver the savings.
- The outcome must be **durable**, not just a temporary patch.

### Where we do not move
We will **not** support any package that leaves:
- excess capacity substantially intact,
- the cost base structurally uncompetitive,
- or margins below what is needed for a credible return to target.

A “no closures, no compulsory redundancies, no pain” outcome is not restructuring; it is delay.

### Our key point to management and the employee side
If you want investor support, the package has to be judged on one thing only: **does it restore competitive margins with certainty?**

That means we need specifics:
1. **How much annual cost reduction is being delivered?**
2. **By when?**
3. **Is it permanent or reversible?**
4. **How much capacity is removed from the system?**
5. **What prevents the agreement from unraveling in 12–24 months?**

Without answers to those questions, we cannot call this credible.

### To IG Metall and the Works Council
We hear the insistence on site protection and no compulsory redundancies. But from a shareholder perspective, those are not sufficient conditions if they prevent the economics from being fixed.

If the alternative package can genuinely deliver:
- equivalent savings,
- permanent labor-cost improvement,
- enforceable productivity gains,
- and credible capacity reduction,

then we would consider it. But if “no closures” simply means preserving too much structure and pushing the problem onto future earnings, we would reject it.

### To the controlling owners and Lower Saxony
We understand the desire to avoid open conflict and protect governance legitimacy. But that legitimacy will erode further if the company keeps underperforming and capital is asked to tolerate weak returns indefinitely.

So we support your preference for a negotiated settlement **only if** it is large enough. A soft landing that leaves the business weak is not a solution.

### What would move us
A package would have our support if it includes most of the following:
- **meaningful and durable capacity reduction**
- **permanent labor-cost adjustments**
- **management overhead discipline**
- **a credible timetable for savings**
- **enough scale to restore margins toward target**
- **implementation certainty, even if achieved through a mix of instruments**

We are open to a mix: plant rationalization, working-time changes, pay and guarantee adjustments, internal redeployment, and headcount reduction. But we will not back a compromise that is smaller than the problem.

### Bottom line
We are prepared to support tough measures. We are **not** prepared to support a settlement that preserves the current cost structure under a more polite label.

If there is a serious alternative to closures, it must be **quantified, durable, and at least as effective**. Otherwise, the market will treat it as another delay — and so will we.
