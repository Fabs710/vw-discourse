We share the core diagnosis: Volkswagen cannot continue with a German cost base and capacity structure that leave the core brand structurally uncompetitive. If the company’s margins do not improve materially and durably, then value destruction continues, and that is not acceptable.

That said, I want to be equally clear on the boundary conditions.

### Where I agree
- **The restructuring must be real, not cosmetic.**
- **Durable cost reduction is necessary.**
- **Management needs execution certainty**, because prolonged conflict would damage earnings, reputation, and investment capacity.
- **A negotiated solution is preferable** if it delivers the required economics and avoids open-ended industrial confrontation.

### Where I do not agree
I do **not** support a “hard cuts at any price” approach if it creates avoidable long-term damage to the enterprise:

- **Permanent impairment of value through uncontrolled conflict** is worse than a difficult agreement.
- **A rushed closure strategy** that ignores legal, industrial, and political realities could lock in delay, escalation, and reputational harm.
- **A plan that simply shifts the burden onto the workforce without securing labour peace and implementation certainty** is not credible.

The central test is not whether the package is harsh; it is whether it **restores competitiveness sustainably**.

### What I would regard as acceptable
A package can be supported if it achieves all of the following:
1. **Material and durable cost reduction**
2. **Capacity reduction sufficient to match market reality**
3. **A credible labour contribution**
4. **A stable path to implementation**
5. **Protection of long-term investment capability**
6. **No outcome that undermines governance control or permanently weakens the asset**

That means I am open to:
- plant rationalization if it is unavoidable and legally executable,
- workforce reductions,
- working-time and pay measures,
- removal or adjustment of legacy guarantees,
- and other negotiated instruments,

**but only if the total package is large enough and enforceable enough to fix the economics.**

### What I would reject
I would reject:
- a compromise that preserves excess capacity while claiming victory,
- delays that merely postpone the reckoning,
- a settlement that secures short-term peace but leaves Volkswagen uncompetitive,
- or any escalation that damages the company more than the restructuring saves.

### Bottom line
I support management’s objective of restoring competitiveness and margins. I do **not** support hollow compromise. The outcome must be judged by whether it produces a credible, durable improvement in Volkswagen’s economics while preserving the company’s long-term value and stability.

If the employee side and other stakeholders can deliver that through alternative measures, I am open to it. If not, then deeper structural measures will have to remain on the table.
