We are at a point where the lines are clear, so I will be equally clear.

## What I agree with

I agree with management, the owners, investors, and Lower Saxony on one thing: **Volkswagen needs a durable restructuring, not a cosmetic one**.  
I also agree with the expert that:
- a serious no-closure package can create **meaningful savings**,
- but it only works if it is **deep, durable, and enforceable**,
- and it is not credible if it simply preserves the current structure under a new label.

That is exactly why IG Metall has already brought a counter-proposal and why we have mobilized warning strikes. We are not defending the status quo.

## Where I disagree

I do **not** accept the conclusion that plant closures and forced redundancies are the necessary starting point.

The expert’s numbers actually support our position more than yours:
- a no-closure package can realistically deliver **around €2.5bn to €4bn annually** if it is serious,
- which means there is a real restructuring path **without** closures,
- provided all sides contribute.

So when management says closures remain necessary unless the employee side accepts deeper cuts, I say: **show that the no-closure route is insufficient in concrete numbers, not in general warnings.**

And when investors say “no closures” is only acceptable if it is close enough to closure-led savings, I say: **fine — then accept that the workforce’s contribution must be matched by management, owners, and the company structure itself.**

## Our red lines remain

I will not retreat from these:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No erosion of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided adjustment where workers bear all the pain**

Those are not bargaining slogans. They are the conditions for any settlement that we can responsibly take to the membership.

## What we are prepared to do

We are prepared to make a real contribution, and we have already signaled that. That includes:
- a **meaningful employee financial contribution**
- **working-time flexibility**
- **capacity reduction without closures**
- **productivity improvements**
- **temporary or phased wage moderation**
- **redeployment and attrition-based headcount reduction**
- **management overhead restraint**
- **owner-side restraint on distributions and expectations**

That is a serious package. It is not a free pass. It is burden-sharing.

## To management

You say the no-closure route is only acceptable at the upper end of the savings range. Then stop speaking as if closures are the natural baseline before the alternative has been fully negotiated.

If you want a deal, put your numbers on the table:
- how much annual savings you require,
- how much comes from management and overhead,
- what you will forgo at the top,
- and what investment commitments are binding for the German sites.

Do not ask the workforce to accept sacrifice in the abstract while management keeps all strategic options and all protections for itself.

## To investors

You are right to insist on durability and enforceability.  
But you are wrong if you think that only closures can deliver that.

A negotiated agreement can be durable if it includes:
- binding labor-cost adjustments,
- productivity commitments,
- capacity reductions through shifts and redeployment,
- a clear implementation timetable,
- and an automatic review mechanism.

What you are still not acknowledging is that **strikes, loss of trust, and destruction of know-how are real costs too**. A closure-heavy solution may improve your spreadsheet and weaken the company for years.

## To the controlling owners

You are right that a conflict-driven outcome destroys value.  
That is why I am saying again: do not push us into a confrontation by treating closures and layoffs as the default answer.

If you want a negotiated settlement, use your weight to support a **site-preserving, no-forced-layoff path** with real concessions from management and owners as well. If workers carry the burden alone, there will be no durable peace.

## To Lower Saxony

Your position is important because it recognizes the industrial and regional consequences of closure logic. But you must do more than express concern. Use your blocking position to insist on a real alternative:
- no closures,
- no forced redundancies,
- binding investment,
- and a package that proves the company can become competitive without sacrificing its industrial base.

## On the facilitator’s compromise options

### 1) No-closure first, with automatic review trigger
This is the only acceptable route to continue discussing.

But the review must be:
- date-certain,
- metric-based,
- and tied to actual savings delivery.

What I will not accept is a “review” that is just a polite pretext for reopening the same fight later.  
If the targets are met, the no-closure settlement holds.  
If not, management will have to show why — with transparent numbers, not pressure tactics.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies plus voluntary footprint adjustment
This is workable if “voluntary” is real and properly designed:
- early retirement,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- internal transfers,
- and negotiated capacity reduction.

But let me be clear: this must not become a hidden closure by attrition. Sites need a future, not a slow disappearance.

### 3) Shared-burden package
Yes — and this is non-negotiable for us:
- management overhead cuts,
- executive discipline,
- owner-side restraint,
- and commitments to future investment.

If everyone asks the workforce to contribute, then everyone must contribute.

## What would move us

A durable settlement is possible if it includes:
- **a quantified savings target**
- **real labor-cost contributions**
- **capacity reduction without closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **management and owner contributions**
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **a strict review mechanism with transparent metrics**

That is the only kind of compromise I will treat as serious.

## Bottom line

IG Metall is ready for a serious restructuring deal.  
We are **not** ready to accept:
- closures,
- forced layoffs,
- or a one-sided settlement that makes workers pay for a problem created at the top.

If management and investors want a durable solution, they should negotiate the real alternative now instead of treating plant closures as the default threat. If they refuse, then they should not be surprised that the workforce uses the tools it has.
