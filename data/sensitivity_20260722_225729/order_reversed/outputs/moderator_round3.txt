## Core impasse

The dispute has narrowed to one central fault line:

**Can Volkswagen achieve a durable, quantified, and enforceable cost reset without plant closures or compulsory redundancies?**

More specifically, the impasse is over:

1. **Economics**
   - Management, owners, and investors want proof that the no-closure route can close the structural cost gap.
   - IG Metall and the Works Council argue the expert’s savings range is sufficient to justify a no-closure deal.

2. **Credibility of fallback**
   - Management/investors want a real fallback to footprint reduction if targets are missed.
   - Employee representatives want any review mechanism to remain a true no-closure settlement, not a staged route to closures.

3. **Scope of sacrifice**
   - Employee side insists on no closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no erosion of guarantees.
   - Other stakeholders say those red lines may prevent a restructuring deep enough to restore competitiveness.

4. **Time and enforceability**
   - Everyone wants an outcome that is not open-ended.
   - The disagreement is whether enforceability requires a hard closure fallback or can rest on binding no-closure metrics plus non-closure remedies.

## Facilitator compromise positions

### Proposal 1: Time-limited no-closure first phase with hard metrics and a pre-agreed second-step menu
**Structure**
- A first phase of 12–18 months with:
  - quantified annual savings target,
  - explicit capacity reductions,
  - labor-cost contributions,
  - management overhead cuts,
  - voluntary exits/redeployment/retirement,
  - and a fixed review date.
- The review is triggered automatically by date and metrics, not by discretion.
- If targets are missed, the second step is **not automatic closure**, but a pre-agreed menu of deeper measures, which may include broader footprint changes if all other remedies fail.

**Why it bridges**
- Gives employee side a genuine no-closure first phase.
- Gives investors/management a credible enforcement mechanism.
- Gives Lower Saxony a chance to preserve sites while still acknowledging a hard contingency.

---

### Proposal 2: No compulsory redundancies, but a mandatory structural adjustment package
**Structure**
- No forced layoffs.
- Mandatory workforce reduction through:
  - voluntary exits,
  - early retirement,
  - redeployment,
  - internal transfers,
  - and attrition.
- Mandatory production-side changes:
  - shift reductions,
  - line rationalization,
  - complexity reduction,
  - model/variant simplification,
  - overhead cuts.
- Savings target set near the upper end of the expert range, with quarterly reporting.

**Why it bridges**
- Preserves the employee red line on compulsory redundancies.
- Addresses investor and management concerns by making “voluntary” measures large and measurable.
- Forces the debate onto actual output and cost effects rather than symbols.

---

### Proposal 3: Shared-burden package with binding commitments from all sides
**Structure**
- Employees contribute through wage moderation, flexibility, and productivity measures.
- Management contributes through overhead cuts, executive restraint, and simplification.
- Owners contribute through governance restraint and support for long-term investment.
- Lower Saxony contributes by backing a stable restructuring framework and using its influence to secure implementation discipline.
- Each side’s contribution is written into a single monitored package.

**Why it bridges**
- Reduces the sense that one group is bearing all the pain.
- Makes burden-sharing visible and legible.
- Creates a political and financial basis for a compromise without immediate closures.

## Pointed questions for each stakeholder

### To Institutional Investors
**What minimum combination of permanent labor-cost reduction, capacity reduction, and management overhead cuts would you regard as sufficiently close to a closure-led solution to support a no-closure settlement?**

### To Controlling Owners (Porsche SE / family)
**Would you support a time-limited no-closure first phase if the agreement includes a binding fallback process, clear metrics, and a quantified savings floor near the upper end of the expert range?**

### To State of Lower Saxony
**What concrete measures are you prepared to back, in governance and voting terms, to ensure that a no-closure settlement is not merely symbolic but also economically enforceable?**

### To Group & Brand Management
**What exact savings target and capacity reduction level do you require before you would declare the no-closure route insufficient and activate the fallback discussion?**

### To IG Metall
**What specific permanent concessions on flexibility, labor cost, and site utilization are you prepared to put on the table to show that no-closure is not just a political demand but a viable restructuring package?**

### To Works Council
**How would you distinguish a legitimate no-closure-first adjustment from a hidden closure-by-attrition, and what safeguards would you accept to make that distinction credible to the company and investors?**

### To the Expert
**What measurable threshold would you use to determine that the no-closure package has failed—specifically, what savings level, capacity change, and durability profile would make footprint reduction economically unavoidable?**
