We are prepared to support restructuring, but only if it is real restructuring.

Our position is straightforward:

- Volkswagen needs a credible, durable reduction in its cost base.
- The current German cost structure and capacity are not competitive.
- We will not support a cosmetic compromise that preserves excess capacity, delays decisions, or pushes the problem into the future.
- At the same time, we prefer a solution that restores margins without prolonged industrial conflict, because strikes and uncertainty destroy value.

What we care about most is the risk-adjusted value of the business:

1. **Competitive margins restored**
   The company must get back to a return-on-sales level that is defensible against peers. If the restructuring does not materially improve margins, it is not enough.

2. **Durable cost reduction**
   We want structural measures, not temporary savings. That means capacity reduction, permanent labor-cost improvement, and management discipline.

3. **Deal certainty**
   An agreed path is preferable to a prolonged confrontation. Extended strikes or political stalemate would hurt earnings, execution, and investor confidence.

4. **Credible execution**
   Any plan must be specific, measurable, and implementable. We want evidence that the savings are real, timely, and sufficient.

So we would argue for a package that can include plant rationalization, workforce reduction, and labor-cost adjustments — provided it is large enough to fix the economics. If there is an alternative that avoids closures but achieves the same scale of savings with high certainty, we would consider it. But if the alternative is just a softer version of the status quo, we would reject it.

In short: **we support the company taking the hard decisions needed to become competitive again, and we will not back an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive.**
