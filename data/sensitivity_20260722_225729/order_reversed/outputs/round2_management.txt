Thank you. The expert assessment is useful because it confirms the central point I have been making from the start: **a real restructuring can be achieved without closures only up to a point**. And that point is dictated by economics, not by comfort.

Let me respond directly.

### To the expert
Your range is broadly consistent with our internal view. A no-closure package may generate something in the **€2.5bn to €4.0bn annual improvement** range if it is serious. That is not trivial. But it is also **not automatically enough** unless the workforce contribution is deep, durable, and enforceable.

That is exactly why I have been insisting on:
- a material labour-cost contribution,
- permanent capacity reduction,
- and implementation certainty.

So the expert view does **not** weaken management’s case. It strengthens it. It shows that:
- **yes**, alternatives to closures can work,
- **but no**, they do not come for free,
- and **no**, a soft compromise will not solve the problem.

### To IG Metall and the Works Council
Your position still has a hard contradiction in it.

You say:
- no closures,
- no compulsory redundancies,
- no weakening of job guarantees,
- and no structural damage to sites,

while also saying the company must become competitive again.

Those demands are only compatible if the workforce is willing to make a **very significant, durable contribution**. Not symbolic gestures. Not temporary pauses. Real savings.

So let me be explicit: if you want to avoid closures, then you must accept a package that delivers on all of the following:
- **permanent labor-cost reduction**
- **meaningful working-time and flexibility concessions**
- **headcount reduction through attrition, redeployment, and targeted separation**
- **removal or substantial dilution of legacy guarantees**
- **productivity and overhead improvements with measurable delivery**

If you cannot accept that scale of change, then your “no closures” line is not a restructuring proposal. It is a request that management absorb the whole burden and pretend the company will somehow be fixed.

That is not acceptable.

### To Lower Saxony
I respect the point that a socially legitimate solution matters. It does. But legitimacy does not mean preserving every site and every guarantee regardless of cost.

A company with a structurally uncompetitive German base cannot invest properly in future products, battery technology, software, and platform competitiveness. If we do not fix the base, then the regional anchor you want to protect will weaken anyway.

So I am not asking you to endorse destruction. I am asking you to recognize that **some capacity and cost reduction is unavoidable** if Volkswagen is to remain an anchor at all.

### To the controlling owners
You are right that confrontation for its own sake is value-destructive. But so is pretending that the cost gap can be closed with vague productivity slogans and minimal concessions.

The decision is not “closures or peace.” The decision is whether the company gets a package that is:
- large enough,
- durable enough,
- and fast enough

to restore margins and fund 2030 investment.

### Where management stands
I am prepared to support either of two routes:

1. **A closure-led route**, if the negotiated alternative fails to deliver sufficient durable savings; or
2. **A no-closure route**, but only if it produces savings at the upper end of the expert range and does so with real enforceability.

What I will **not** support is:
- a compromise that keeps too much excess capacity,
- a labour deal that is reversible or temporary,
- or a settlement that postpones the structural fix.

### My minimum conditions
Any final package must include:
- a **quantified annual savings target**
- a **durable labour-cost contribution**
- a **clear capacity reduction**
- a **credible headcount reduction path**
- and **investment protection for the future portfolio**

If the employee side wants to avoid closures, then it must put something equally strong on the table. If it does not, then closures remain on the table — not as rhetoric, but as a necessary instrument.

I am not here to negotiate around the diagnosis. I am here to solve it.
