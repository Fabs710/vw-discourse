### Core impasse

The central blockage is a **three-way mismatch** on the acceptable form of restructuring:

1. **Investors / management** require a package that is large, durable, and credible enough to restore margins, and they remain unconvinced that a no-closure path can reliably reach the needed savings.
2. **IG Metall / Works Council** reject plant closures and compulsory redundancies as red lines, and want a site-preserving solution with binding protections.
3. **Lower Saxony** also rejects closures as the default, while demanding a durable solution that preserves regional legitimacy.

So the dispute is not over whether restructuring is needed. It is over **whether the required savings can be achieved without closures and forced layoffs, and if so, how to make that credible and enforceable**.

---

### Facilitator compromise options

#### 1) Quantified “no-closure first” package with automatic review trigger
A compromise could be structured as a **binding first-phase no-closure package** with:
- specific annual savings targets,
- labor-cost and working-time concessions,
- overhead reductions,
- capacity reductions through shifts, variants, and redeployment,
- and a fixed review point.

If, at the review point, agreed savings are not achieved, a second-phase footprint review would be triggered automatically.

**Why this may bridge sides:**  
- Gives unions and Lower Saxony a chance to preserve sites first.  
- Gives investors and management a measurable fallback if the alternative underdelivers.

#### 2) “No compulsory redundancies” plus voluntary footprint adjustment
A second compromise could pair:
- **no compulsory redundancies**,  
with
- **voluntary exits, early retirement, redeployment, internal transfers, and negotiated site downsizing** where economics demand it.

This would allow capacity reduction and headcount reduction without immediate forced dismissals or a closure-first posture.

**Why this may bridge sides:**  
- Preserves the union’s strongest labor-protection line.  
- Still gives management and investors real structural reduction.

#### 3) Shared-burden package with governance and investment commitments
A third route is a broader package in which employee concessions are matched by:
- management overhead cuts,
- limits on executive remuneration growth,
- owner-side restraint on distribution expectations,
- and binding investment commitments for German sites.

**Why this may bridge sides:**  
- Addresses the labor side’s demand that burden-sharing not be one-sided.  
- Gives investors a clearer signal that restructuring is not just deferred or shifted onto workers.

### Pointed questions to each stakeholder

#### Institutional Investors
**What minimum quantified annual savings and capacity reduction would make a no-closure package acceptable to you, and what evidence would you require to treat it as durable rather than reversible?**

#### Controlling Owners / Porsche SE / Family
**Are you prepared to support a time-limited no-closure settlement with an automatic escalation mechanism if targets are missed, even if that means accepting a later footprint reduction as a fallback?**

#### Lower Saxony
**Would you support a site-preserving restructuring only if it includes enforceable productivity, labor-cost, and capacity targets, even when those targets require significant workforce adjustments short of closures?**

#### Group & Brand Management
**What specific savings, by source and by year, must a no-closure package deliver before you would rule out closures as necessary?**

#### IG Metall
**If closures and compulsory redundancies remain off the table, what permanent labor-cost, flexibility, and capacity concessions are you prepared to lock in so the package is economically credible?**

#### Works Council
**Would you accept a binding no-closure agreement that includes voluntary exits, redeployment, and site-specific capacity reductions, if it is paired with strict investment and job-security guarantees?**

#### Expert
**What is the clearest threshold at which a no-closure package stops being economically sufficient and footprint reduction becomes unavoidable?**
