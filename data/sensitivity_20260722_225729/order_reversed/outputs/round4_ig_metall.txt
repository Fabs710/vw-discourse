We are at the point where the positions are clear, so I will be equally clear.

## Where I agree
I agree with management, the owners, investors, Lower Saxony, and the expert on one basic point: **Volkswagen needs a real, durable restructuring**.  
I also agree that:
- a prolonged conflict would damage the company,
- the package must be quantified and enforceable,
- and burden-sharing cannot be one-sided.

And yes: I accept the expert’s point that a **serious no-closure package can be economically meaningful**. That is exactly why I have been bargaining on a real counter-proposal and not just saying “no.”

## Where I disagree
I do **not** accept the conclusion that plant closures or compulsory redundancies are the necessary answer.

The expert’s assessment does **not** prove that closures are required. It says a no-closure package can work if it is deep enough, durable enough, and enforceable enough. That is a very different statement.

So to management and investors:  
Do not treat “closure-led” restructuring as the default benchmark and then ask us to prove an alternative in the abstract. If you want to argue that the no-closure route is insufficient, then show it **plant by plant, site by site, with real numbers**.

## Our red lines remain
I will not retreat from these:

- **No compulsory redundancies**
- **No plant closures**
- **No erosion of the collective wage framework**
- **No one-sided adjustment where workers bear all the pain**

Those are not tactical talking points. They are the boundary of any settlement that can be carried by the workforce.

## What we are prepared to do
We are not defending the status quo. We are prepared to make a real contribution, including:
- a meaningful employee financial contribution,
- working-time flexibility,
- capacity reduction through shifts and redeployment,
- productivity improvements,
- temporary or phased wage moderation,
- voluntary exits and attrition,
- and a fair contribution from management and owners.

That is burden-sharing. What we will not accept is burden-shifting.

## To management
You say the no-closure route only works at the upper end of the expert range. Fine. Then stop speaking as if closures are already proven necessary.

If you want a deal, put the economics on the table transparently:
- what exact savings do you require,
- how much comes from labor,
- how much comes from overhead,
- how much comes from management restraint,
- what investment commitments are binding for German sites,
- and what is actually missing from the no-closure path.

Do not ask workers to sign a blank cheque for a restructuring whose burden is still being defined by management alone.

## To investors
You are right to insist on durability and enforceability.  
You are wrong if you think only closures can create that.

A negotiated agreement can be durable if it includes:
- binding labor-cost relief,
- productivity commitments,
- capacity reduction through working-time and redeployment,
- a clear implementation timetable,
- and a strict review mechanism.

And let me be direct: strikes, loss of trust, and destruction of know-how are real costs too. A closure-heavy approach is not “more credible” if it burns value and weakens the company for years.

## To the controlling owners
You are right that conflict destroys value. That is exactly why you should not back a strategy that forces conflict by insisting on closures and layoffs as the starting point.

If you want a negotiated settlement, use your weight to support a **site-preserving, no-forced-layoff solution** with real contributions from management and owners as well. If workers carry the burden alone, there will be no durable peace.

## To Lower Saxony
Your position matters because you understand that Volkswagen is not just a balance sheet; it is an industrial and regional anchor. But that understanding must be matched with action.

If you truly want a site-preserving solution, then use your blocking position to defend it against closure pressure. Do not just call for balance; help enforce it.

## On the compromise options
### 1) Time-limited no-closure first phase with hard metrics
This is the only route I consider worth continuing.

But it must be a **true no-closure first phase**, not a waiting room for closures later.  
That means:
- date-certain review,
- hard metrics,
- actual savings delivery,
- and no hidden trapdoor to shut plants if management later decides it wants a quicker answer.

If the targets are met, the agreement holds. If management believes they are not, then it must prove it with transparent numbers, not pressure tactics.

### 2) No compulsory redundancies plus structural adjustment
This is workable if the adjustment is real:
- early retirement,
- voluntary exits,
- redeployment,
- internal transfers,
- and negotiated capacity reduction.

But I will repeat: this must not become a closure by attrition. Sites need a future, not a slow disappearance.

### 3) Shared-burden package
Yes — and this is essential:
- management overhead cuts,
- executive restraint,
- owner-side restraint,
- and binding commitments to future investment.

If everyone asks workers to contribute, then everyone must contribute. Symbolic restraint at the top is not enough, but it is not irrelevant either. It must be part of a real package.

## What would move us
A serious settlement is possible if it includes:
- a **quantified savings target**
- a **durable employee contribution**
- **real capacity reduction without closures**
- **no compulsory redundancies**
- **management and owner contributions**
- **binding investment and product commitments**
- **a strict review mechanism with transparent metrics**

That is the minimum standard for a responsible deal.

## What will not move us
What will not move us is:
- closure threats dressed up as realism,
- forced layoffs dressed up as efficiency,
- a settlement that leaves the workforce as the only party paying,
- or a review mechanism that is really just a delayed closure decision.

If that is the direction, then we will resist it with the instruments we have.

## Bottom line
IG Metall is ready for a **serious restructuring deal**.  
We are **not** ready to accept:
- plant closures,
- compulsory redundancies,
- or a one-sided settlement that makes workers pay for a problem created at the top.

If management and investors want a durable solution, they should negotiate the real alternative now instead of treating closures as the default. If they refuse, then they should not be surprised that the workforce uses its leverage.
