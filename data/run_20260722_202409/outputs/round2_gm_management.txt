We are ready to move to terms, but I need to be equally clear: **any package that leaves the company nonviable or triggers consumer flight is unacceptable**. Our first duty is to keep the enterprise intact as a going concern.

## Where I agree

### To the task force
You are right that this must be **deep, fast, and credible**. The prior plan was not enough. We cannot preserve the current cost structure and expect survival. We accept:
- major labour cost reductions,
- a real restructuring of the retiree obligation,
- substantial unsecured creditor losses,
- and major footprint rationalization.

### To labour and the trust
You are right that the retiree obligation cannot simply be erased. A settlement that wipes out retirees entirely would be destructive to trust, labour peace, and the franchise. We need a **durable solution**, not a symbolic one.

### To bondholders
You are right that sacrifice must be shared and that the trust cannot be treated as if it were outside the restructuring. The company cannot survive if one legacy obligation is preserved too close to par while the rest of the capital structure is cut to the bone.

### To the neighbouring governments
You are right that public money should not be handed over without protection for real industrial value in your jurisdictions. But that protection must be consistent with a viable operating plan.

## Where I disagree

### 1) Labour and the trust: “not destroyed” is not enough if the numbers do not work
I understand the insistence on durable value for retirees, but I cannot accept a structure that makes the reorganized company uncompetitive.

That means:
- no preservation of the retiree claim near par,
- no claim to full protection from the restructuring,
- and no insistence that jobs and plants be preserved regardless of economics.

The trust needs **real but reduced value** — likely a mix of:
- reduced face amount,
- long-dated contingent value,
- and some equity or warrants.

But if the trust is too protected, the company simply fails later. That is not an acceptable result for anyone.

### 2) Bondholders: you cannot demand symmetry while ignoring viability
I do not accept the argument that any special treatment of the trust is automatically confiscatory. The relevant question is not abstract equality; it is whether the **total package produces a viable company**.

Bondholders will take meaningful losses. That is unavoidable. But I will not support a structure that:
- wipes them out entirely,
- triggers avoidable litigation,
- or undermines the company’s access to a workable post-restructuring capital structure.

### 3) Neighbouring governments: domestic protection cannot become a veto
I can support binding commitments where they fit a viable plan. I cannot support a blanket promise to protect every domestic plant regardless of economics. If a facility is structurally uneconomic, preserving it just because it is local would jeopardize the whole company.

### 4) On process
I am open to an expedited court-supervised process if it is the fastest route to finality and financing certainty. But the process must protect:
- consumer confidence,
- warranties,
- dealer continuity,
- and the strongest brands that still have future value.

We cannot allow uncertainty to destroy the franchise while the paperwork is being settled.

## What I think the term sheet must look like

### Retiree trust
- **Material restructuring**
- No wipeout, but definitely not close to par
- Likely a combination of:
  - reduced claim,
  - contingent long-dated value,
  - and some equity participation

### Unsecured bondholders
- **Significant haircuts**
- Some recovery in equity/warrants is reasonable
- But not enough to crowd out the company’s ability to invest and compete

### Labour
- Wage and work-rule concessions are necessary
- Staffing levels must come down
- Plant/productivity adjustments must reflect market reality

### Plants, brands, dealer network
- We should preserve the **strongest brands and most viable plants**
- We should close or consolidate the weakest facilities
- Dealer network rationalization is necessary, but it must be managed carefully to avoid consumer panic

### Public funding / co-funding
- Funding should be tied to a credible exit
- Co-funders should receive governance rights or equity proportionate to their contribution
- But no stakeholder should receive a veto that prevents viability

---

## Bottom line
My line is this:

- **Yes** to a deep restructuring.
- **Yes** to major losses for labour, retirees, and bondholders.
- **Yes** to preserving only the strongest brands, plants, and dealer network.
- **Yes** to a court-supervised process if needed for speed and finality.
- **No** to any settlement that preserves legacy claims so fully that the company cannot survive.
- **No** to any “protected” footprint that leaves us with a weaker, not stronger, enterprise.

If we are serious about saving the company, then every claim has to bend to the same fact: **viability comes first**.
