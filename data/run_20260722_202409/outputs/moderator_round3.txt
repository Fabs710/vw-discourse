## Core impasse

The plateau is now centered on **two linked valuation-and-control disputes**:

1. **How much value the retiree trust gets, and in what form**
   - Task force: wants a substantial impairment, no meaningful floor.
   - Labour/trust: wants real, durable value, not a token stub.
   - Bondholders: oppose any trust package that is better than other unsecureds unless fully offset.

2. **Who controls the post-restructuring footprint and what public money buys**
   - Neighbouring governments want binding domestic production commitments and governance rights.
   - Task force and management insist plant decisions must remain economics-based.
   - Bondholders worry domestic carve-outs will be funded through lower recoveries.

In short: **the parties disagree on both the allocation of losses and the allocation of control over the reorganized company**.

---

## Facilitator compromise proposals

### Proposal 1: “Large haircut + mixed-value trust package”
A possible bridge is a structure in which the retiree trust receives:
- a **material haircut**,
- **a small or no cash-like floor**,
- plus a package of **equity, warrants, and/or contingent value rights** tied to post-emergence performance.

To reduce bondholder objections, this could be paired with:
- **similar upside instruments** for unsecured bondholders,
- and a requirement that total present value across unsecured classes be **approximately parity-adjusted**, not nominally identical.

This proposal addresses:
- task force: viability and no near-par treatment,
- labour/trust: real value, not a hollow stub,
- bondholders: no hidden superior recovery for the trust.

---

### Proposal 2: “Viability-tested domestic footprint with conditional public co-funding”
A second bridge is a footprint package where:
- domestic plants receive **binding production commitments only if they pass a published viability test**,
- closures are permitted for sites that fail that test,
- neighbouring governments receive **proportionate equity or governance rights** in exchange for co-funding,
- but those rights are limited so they do not amount to a veto over uneconomic rationalization.

This proposal addresses:
- neighbouring governments: not just symbolic influence,
- task force/management: no political veto over closures,
- bondholders: fewer concerns that public money is subsidizing uneconomic capacity.

---

### Proposal 3: “Fast-track court fallback with pre-negotiated guardrails”
If voluntary alignment remains blocked, a third bridge is a court-supervised process with agreed guardrails:
- the trust must receive **material but impaired value**,
- bondholders receive **significant haircuts plus upside participation**,
- labour concessions are explicit,
- plant closures follow a published economic test,
- and public financing remains **temporary, capped, and conditional**.

This would not settle every dispute, but it could reduce uncertainty by defining:
- minimum treatment standards,
- what cannot be preserved at par,
- and what the court is being asked to confirm rather than redesign.
