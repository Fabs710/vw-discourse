We can engage, but let’s stop pretending that “viability” is a blank cheque for political preference.

## Our position, plainly
The unsecured bondholders will support a restructuring that is:
- deep,
- fast,
- and credible,

but **not** one that uses urgency to impose a **preferred recovery for the retiree trust** while bondholders are pushed into the deepest cut.

The trust is an unsecured legacy obligation. If it gets better treatment, there must be **equal compensation**. Otherwise, that is not a rescue structure — it is selective subordination.

## To the task force
We agree on several basics:
- the company needs a deep restructuring,
- excess capacity must come out,
- public money should not prop up an unreformed balance sheet,
- and a court-supervised process is a real backstop.

Where we disagree is your repeated assumption that bondholders can be the residual shock absorber while the trust is granted a “durable” recovery package.

That is not acceptable.

If you want our consent, the package must show:
1. **no material preference for the retiree trust over other unsecureds**,  
2. **meaningful bondholder recovery**, and  
3. **clear evidence that the imposed court outcome would not be better for us**.

Right now, your language about a “modest floor” for the trust still sounds like a political carve-out. We do not accept that.

## To labour and the retiree trust
We are not asking for a zero. We are not pretending retirees should be wiped out.

But we reject the claim that the trust deserves a “real economic value” package while bondholders are told to absorb losses as if that is somehow neutral. It is not neutral if the trust ends up materially better off.

If the trust is restructured, fine. But then it must be restructured **materially** and **even-handedly**. What we cannot accept is:
- a trust with a protected floor,
- equity or warrants reserved as a political consolation prize,
- and bondholders left with whatever is left over.

That would be an engineered unfairness, not shared sacrifice.

## To management
You keep saying viability comes first. We agree — but viability does not justify **selective favoritism**.

Your own statements still leave open the possibility that:
- the trust gets meaningful upside,
- bondholders get deep haircuts,
- and then everyone calls the result “durable.”

Durable for whom?

If the trust is receiving equity or warrants, then bondholders must receive **comparable total value**. If not, you are simply shifting value from one unsecured class to another under pressure.

## To the neighbouring governments
Your funding interest does not give you the right to convert domestic plant protection into a creditor recovery lever.

We understand you want production commitments, and where a facility is truly viable, that can be discussed. But if domestic footprint demands worsen the company’s economics, then they also worsen creditor recoveries. You do not get to claim industrial protection and then ask bondholders to subsidize it through lower recoveries.

If you want commitments, they must be tied to:
- demonstrable viability,
- and not to political entrenchment.

## What we can support
We can support a structure with:
- substantial haircuts for all legacy unsecured claims,
- equity or warrants for both trust and bondholders,
- and no class receiving a superior headline recovery without offsetting value.

That means:
- if the trust gets a floor, bondholders get equalized upside,
- if the trust gets equity, bondholders get equity too,
- if the trust gets contingent value, bondholders do not get left with a token stub.

## What we will not accept
Let me be explicit:
- **No near-par treatment for the retiree trust**
- **No protected recovery floor for the trust unless bondholders receive equal compensation**
- **No deal where bondholders are the sacrificial class**
- **No court proposal that would likely leave unsecured bondholders worse off than a consensual exchange**

If the court process would impose a fairer distribution than the proposed deal, we will go there.

## On the expert input
The pension specialist confirms exactly what we have been saying: the trust cannot be preserved near par, and any fixed-value promise risks breaking the company. Fine. That supports a **material** restructuring of the trust.

But the expert also says something important: any recovery floor for the trust must be **deeply contingent** and **subordinated to viability**. We take that seriously. It also means the trust should not be using “durability” as a cover for superior treatment.

## Our minimum terms
For us to even consider support:

1. **Equal treatment of unsecured claims**
   - No materially better treatment for the retiree trust without equal compensation.

2. **Real bondholder recovery**
   - Not a token stub.
   - Not a disguised wipeout.

3. **Upside participation for all unsecured classes**
   - Equity or warrants distributed in a way that preserves parity.

4. **Plant closures based on economics**
   - Not on politics, and not at the expense of creditor recoveries.

5. **Court-supervised fallback must not be worse than the deal**
   - If the imposed route is better, we will not consent to being pushed into a worse voluntary exchange.

## Bottom line
We are willing to do a deal.
We are **not** willing to be the class that pays for everyone else’s preferred story.

If the trust is given a durable, meaningful package, bondholders must receive the same economic respect. If not, there is no consensual deal from our side, and we will test the court route.

If you want movement, put on the table a term sheet that:
- quantifies the trust haircut,
- quantifies bondholder recovery,
- and shows both are equalized in total present value.

Until then, our position remains unchanged.
