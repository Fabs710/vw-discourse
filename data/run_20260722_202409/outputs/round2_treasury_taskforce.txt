We are at the point where positions must become terms, not rhetoric.

I’ll be direct: **there will be no financing for a plan that preserves the current cost structure, protects legacy claims near par, and leaves taxpayers carrying the bulk of the pain.** If that is the direction of this discussion, we will move to the court-supervised route.

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **deep, fast, credible restructuring**.
- **Excess capacity must come out**.
- **Labour, retirees, creditors, management, and public stakeholders all need to absorb losses**.
- A **court-supervised process remains on the table** if it is the fastest way to force finality.

## Where I disagree
### 1) Retiree trust treatment cannot be privileged
To labour and the trust: I understand the history, but the retiree health obligation is not immune from restructuring. It is part of the legacy burden that helped bring the company here.  

What I will not accept:
- preserving the trust close to par,
- placing the trust ahead of unsecured creditors in a way that effectively socializes losses onto bondholders and taxpayers,
- or granting the trust value that makes the reorganized company unviable.

That said, I am **not** asking for a zero. I am asking for a **material haircut and conversion into a mix of reduced value and longer-dated, contingent value** so the company can survive.

### 2) Bondholders cannot expect to be made whole, but neither will they be the sole dumping ground
To the bondholder committee: you are right that you should not be the only class absorbing pain. You will not be. But your argument that any special treatment for the trust is “confiscatory” goes too far unless you can show the total package leaves the reorganized company viable.  

What I need is:
- **substantial unsecured creditor haircuts**,
- a recovery that is real but clearly reduced,
- and no attempt to use litigation risk to block the restructuring depth required for survival.

### 3) Domestic plant protection is not a veto over viability
To the neighbouring governments: I appreciate the funding willingness, but domestic plant preservation cannot become a blanket veto over rationalization. We are not financing an industrial museum.  

If your plants are economically essential and can be supported in a viable plan, they will be. If not, they cannot be shielded merely because your taxpayers are at the table.

## Minimum direction of travel
Let me put the table where it needs to be:

- **Labour and retiree trust:** meaningful concessions, including labour cost reductions and a substantial restructuring of the retiree health obligation.
- **Bondholders:** significant losses, with some recovery and possibly warrants/equity participation, but not a recovery that impairs viability.
- **Plants/brands/dealers:** major footprint reduction, not cosmetic trimming.
- **Public money:** bridge financing only, tied to a credible exit and enforceable restructuring.
- **Ownership:** new equity must reflect who is actually contributing value and absorbing loss; we will not underwrite a capital structure that simply restores the old balance sheet in new clothing.

## On process
If we can get a credible package quickly, fine. If not, we will use the court-supervised route to impose it. I am not interested in endless bargaining over who is “more deserving” while the company burns cash.

## What I need next
Each stakeholder should now state, in concrete terms:
1. the **maximum haircut / restructuring** it will accept,
2. whether it accepts **equity or warrants instead of cash**,
3. which **plants or brands are non-negotiable** versus available for closure,
4. and whether it will support a **court-supervised backstop** if consensus fails.

If parties cannot move to term-sheet level concessions, then we are done debating principles and we will proceed accordingly.
