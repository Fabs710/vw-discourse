We are prepared to stay constructive, but I need to be equally direct: **we will not co-fund a restructuring that leaves our domestic plants exposed while giving us only a symbolic voice.**

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **deep, fast, credible restructuring**.
- Excess capacity must come out.
- Labour, retirees, creditors, management, and public funders all need to share sacrifice.
- A court-supervised backstop may be necessary if time runs out.

## Where we disagree

### 1) Domestic footprint is not a side issue
To the task force and management: we understand viability comes first. We are **not** asking for a museum. But viability cannot mean that our jurisdictions simply absorb closures while foreign or politically easier sites are protected.

If our money is part of the rescue, then we require:
- **binding production commitments** for domestic plants that remain viable,
- **no closure of those plants absent a clear, transparent viability case**, and
- **employment protections** for core jobs tied to that production.

That is not a veto over rationalization. It is a condition for public co-financing.

### 2) We will not pay without a proportionate say
To the task force: your “bridge financing only” language is fine, but not if it means we fund and then are treated as junior spectators.

We need an **ownership position and/or governance rights proportionate to our contribution**. If we are putting in public money to preserve domestic industrial capacity, then the restructured company must reflect that with real equity or equivalent control rights.

A token advisory role is not enough. Nor is a vague promise that our interests will be “considered” later.

### 3) We support restructuring, not footprint hollowing
To management: we accept closures where they are economically necessary. We do **not** accept a plan that rationalizes mostly by shifting production away from our domestic base.

If a plant is viable, it should stay in the production plan. If it is not, say so and show the numbers. But do not use “competitiveness” as a blanket justification for cutting domestic capacity first and asking us to underwrite the result.

### 4) On the creditor/trust dispute
We are not taking sides in the unsecured class fight. But we do care about one thing: **a settlement that is durable enough to keep the company operating and the plants running**.

So:
- We agree with the task force that the retiree trust cannot be near par.
- We agree with management and the specialist that a pure wipeout is unstable.
- We agree with labour that the workforce should not be the residual dumping ground.

But our support is conditional on the final package preserving the domestic footprint and giving co-funders a real ownership stake.

## Our minimum position
We need all of the following in the term sheet:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - specified production allocation to domestic facilities,
   - enforceable commitments for viable plants,
   - and closure only where justified by a published viability case.

2. **Employment protections**
   - protection for core domestic jobs where operations remain viable,
   - transition support where reductions are unavoidable.

3. **Proportionate equity/governance**
   - an ownership stake, board representation, or equivalent governance rights matching our funding contribution.

4. **No funding without footprint protection**
   - we will not co-fund if the result is a materially weakened domestic production base.

5. **Fast, final execution**
   - we can support either a consensual deal or a court-supervised route, but only if these protections survive the process.

## Bottom line
We are constructive co-funders, not passive cheque-writers.

If the rescue is to include our money, it must:
- preserve domestic production share,
- protect meaningful domestic employment,
- and give us a proportionate stake in the restructured company.

If that is not in the package, then we cannot support it.
