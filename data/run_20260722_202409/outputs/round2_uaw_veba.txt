We are prepared to move from principles to terms, but not on the premise that workers and retirees should carry the deepest cut while financial creditors argue for symmetry only when it benefits them.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **deep, fast, credible restructuring**.
- **Excess capacity must be reduced**.
- **Management, labour, retirees, creditors, and public stakeholders all need to share sacrifice**.
- A **court-supervised backstop** is acceptable if it is the only way to force finality and keep the company alive.

## Where we disagree

### 1) The retiree health trust cannot be treated as just another unsecured claim to be gutted
I reject the framing that the retiree obligation should simply be marked down alongside bond debt as if decades of negotiated service meant nothing.

That does **not** mean I am insisting on par. It **does** mean:
- no wipeout,
- no token recovery,
- and no structure where retirees are left with a hollow promise while bondholders preserve a cleaner claim.

What we can discuss is a **real restructuring** of the trust obligation into something durable:
- a **reduced but meaningful claim**,
- a **mix of equity/warrants and long-dated contingent value**,
- and, where needed, a deferred funding schedule that the company can actually carry.

But if the trust is reduced too far, or treated as economically disposable, there will be no labour peace and no credible settlement.

### 2) Bondholders are not entitled to dominate because they are numerous and politically easier to pressure
I hear the bondholders’ complaint about fairness. But “fairness” does not mean the people who financed debt at a premium to risk now get to insist the workforce and retirees absorb the biggest loss so that their recovery is protected.

You will take losses. Significant ones. That is part of the deal.

What I can support is:
- **substantial unsecured creditor haircuts**,
- a **limited but real equity or warrant position**,
- and parity of sacrifice across the unsecured classes, including the retiree trust where appropriate.

What I will not support is a structure that leaves bondholders materially better protected than the trust while demanding workers make the largest operational concessions.

### 3) Plant closures must be economically justified, not used as bargaining theater
We are not defending every plant. We understand there will be closures.

But we will not accept a package where:
- communities are sacrificed to satisfy creditor optics,
- domestic production is hollowed out without a viability case,
- or a rushed footprint reduction becomes a pretext for shifting pain onto workers while preserving more favorable treatment elsewhere.

If closures are necessary, they must be tied to a credible product and production plan, not just balance-sheet simplification.

### 4) Neighbouring governments’ funding must come with enforceable commitments, not symbolic influence
If co-funding is on the table, then yes, it should come with real protection for the plants and jobs in those jurisdictions. That is not unreasonable.

But I want to be equally clear:
- public co-funding should not become a mechanism to lock in one region’s production at the expense of another,
- and it should not be used to override the workforce’s broader interest in preserving the industrial base.

If governments are putting money in, they can reasonably expect commitments. But those commitments must be part of a viable company, not a political carve-up.

---

## Minimum acceptable direction from our side

### Retiree trust
- **No destruction of the trust**
- **Meaningful durable value**, including equity or equivalent long-term instruments
- A **restructured claim**, not a zeroed-out promise

### Labour
- Further wage and work-rule concessions can be discussed
- But only if paired with:
  - **job protection where feasible**,
  - **plant retention where economically supportable**,
  - and a credible future for the company

### Bondholders
- **Significant haircuts**
- Some equity/warrant participation is possible
- But not a “fairness” argument that effectively forces retirees and workers to absorb more than creditors

### Plants and brands
- We are willing to discuss **selective rationalization**
- We are **not** agreeing to broad closure commitments today without seeing the operating logic

### Process
- We can support a **court-supervised route** if necessary
- But only if it produces:
  - a durable retiree settlement,
  - not an over-reward to bondholders,
  - and enforceable job/plant commitments

---

## Bottom line
If the task force wants our signature, the deal must include:
1. **A real, durable settlement for the retiree trust**
2. **Meaningful unsecured creditor losses, including bondholders**
3. **Operational rationalization that is viability-based, not politically convenient**
4. **Enforceable job and plant commitments where public money is involved**
5. **A capital structure that does not treat the workforce as the residual dumping ground**

We are willing to make the company viable. We are not willing to be the class that pays for everyone else’s insistence on speed and finality.

If you want us to move further, put forward a term sheet that gives the trust real value and does not ask labour to absorb a disproportionate loss.
