1) **What has been agreed**
- All parties agree the company needs a **deep, fast, credible restructuring** rather than a delay.
- All accept that **some sacrifice is required from multiple stakeholders**.
- There is broad agreement that **excess capacity must be reduced** and the company’s future footprint must be more viable.
- No one is advocating preservation of the current structure unchanged.
- There is general openness to either a **consensual deal or an expedited court-supervised process**, provided it delivers finality and financing certainty.

2) **Core disagreements remaining**
- **Retiree health trust treatment:**  
  - Labour insists it cannot be wiped out and wants durable value, potentially equity.  
  - Bondholders want it treated as part of the unsecured creditor pool, not privileged.  
  - Management and the task force say it must be materially restructured, not preserved near par.
- **Allocation of losses:**  
  - Bondholders object to being singled out for the deepest cut.  
  - The task force wants broad shared sacrifice, including labour and legacy obligations.  
  - Labour says workers and retirees should not bear a disproportionate burden.
- **Plant and footprint rationalization:**  
  - The task force and management want major rationalization.  
  - Labour wants closures justified by viability, not politics.  
  - Neighbouring governments want domestic plants protected.
- **Domestic production commitments vs. economic flexibility:**  
  - Neighbouring governments want binding plant/employment commitments and an equity stake tied to co-funding.  
  - Management and the task force have not accepted those limits.
- **Creditor recovery/fairness in a court scenario:**  
  - Bondholders are signaling they may prefer court if the proposed deal appears confiscatory.  
  - Others are not yet aligning on what the likely court outcome would be relative to a consensual deal.

3) **Key trade-offs still unaddressed**
- **Retiree trust value vs. bondholder recovery:** how much value can be preserved for the trust without making the capital structure unworkable.
- **Jobs/plants vs. viability:** which facilities are retained, which are closed, and on what economic basis.
- **Public funding vs. governance:** how much control and equity public co-funders receive in exchange for their money.
- **Speed vs. negotiation fairness:** whether a fast settlement can be reached without forcing any one class into a perceived unfair outcome.
- **Court-supervised certainty vs. negotiated concessions:** whether the parties can agree voluntarily or will rely on an imposed process.

4) **Brief refocus for the next round**
- Move from principles to a **term-sheet level discussion**: specify ranges for retiree claim treatment, unsecured creditor recovery, plant closures, and public equity participation.
- Ask each party to state its **minimum acceptable terms** on:  
  1. retiree trust,  
  2. unsecured bondholder recovery,  
  3. plant/production footprint,  
  4. public funding conditions.
- Identify any **package deals** where one concession is linked to another, so trade-offs can be tested quickly.
- Keep the discussion anchored to **viability, finality, and enforceability** rather than broad position statements.
