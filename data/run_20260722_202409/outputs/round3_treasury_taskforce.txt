We are still too far apart on the fundamentals, and I want to be clear: **I will not finance a deal that protects legacy claims at the expense of viability, or that turns public money into a subsidy for delay.**

## Where I agree
- The company needs a **deep, fast, final restructuring**.
- **No stakeholder gets a free pass**: management, labour, retirees, creditors, and public funders all absorb pain.
- A **court-supervised fallback** remains acceptable if it is the quickest route to certainty.
- Domestic plants with real economic value should be preserved where they fit a viable plan.

## Where I disagree

### 1) The retiree trust cannot be insulated by politics
To labour and the trust: I hear the argument that the obligation has moral weight and was negotiated over years. That does not make it sacrosanct. It is part of the legacy burden that must be restructured materially.

I do **not** accept:
- a trust outcome near par,
- a special floor that effectively guarantees it a better position than other unsecureds,
- or a structure where the trust’s protection is financed by deeper cuts to bondholders or taxpayers.

If the trust gets a recovery floor, it must be **modest, contingent, and clearly offset by equivalent or greater sacrifice elsewhere**. But my starting point is still a **substantial haircut**. The company does not survive if we preserve too much of that obligation.

### 2) Bondholders are not entitled to a clean recovery, but they are right about unfair priority
To the bondholder committee: you are correct on one point — I will not endorse a deal that leaves the retiree trust materially better treated than other unsecureds without compensation. That would invite litigation and political blowback, and it would not be a clean restructuring.

But I am **not** moving to your implied position that the trust must be dragged to the same recovery level as all other unsecureds regardless of the broader deal. The test is viability, speed, and finality. If the package is workable, I will support it even if not every unsecured class gets identical headline treatment.

What I need from you is not protests about confiscation, but acceptance that:
- bondholders will take **significant losses**,
- some of your value may need to come through **equity/warrants** rather than cash,
- and the court-supervised process may be the actual mechanism if voluntary alignment fails.

### 3) Domestic co-funding does not buy a veto over rationalization
To the neighbouring governments: your funding is useful, but it is not a purchase of control over the footprint. I am not financing a deal where each jurisdiction protects its own plants and leaves the company with excess capacity elsewhere.

Your demand for binding production commitments is only acceptable **if those commitments are economically justified** and consistent with a company that can actually compete. I will not tie public money to preserving uneconomic facilities simply because they are politically sensitive.

So no, I do not accept a de facto veto over closures. If a plant is viable, it can stay. If it is not, it goes.

### 4) Management: viability first, but stop speaking as if every hard choice is optional
I appreciate that the new leadership is aligned on the need for deep restructuring. But I want to underline that “we cannot afford to lose consumer confidence” is not an argument against necessary cuts. It is an argument for a credible, decisive plan.

Do not come back with a revised version of the old plan wearing more serious language. I will reject it again if it preserves too much legacy cost or delays the necessary footprint reduction.

## My position on the “compromise” ideas

### Compromise A: shared haircut with capped floor
I am **not** endorsing a meaningful recovery floor for the retiree trust as the starting point. If you want to avoid a total wipeout, then the floor must be:
- low,
- contingent,
- and clearly subordinate to company survival.

Anything that looks like a politically protected minimum will fail my test.

### Compromise B: footprint package tied to viability tests
This is the only part I find directionally acceptable.

Yes:
- funding can be linked to **viability-tested plants**,
- rationalization can proceed for plants that fail the test,
- and governments can have enforceable commitments on facilities that remain in the plan.

But I want it understood: this is not a regional entitlement scheme. The benchmark is whether a site contributes to a viable post-restructuring company.

### Compromise C: court-supervised plan with negotiated priority rules
This is the most credible route if the parties cannot converge quickly.

I am prepared to use the court process to impose:
- broad, shared sacrifice,
- material restructuring of the retiree trust,
- significant bondholder losses with equity/warrant participation,
- and footprint cuts based on economic reality.

What I will **not** do is enter that process tied to a pre-agreement that locks in special treatment for any one legacy claim. The court route exists precisely because some parties may refuse to accept the loss allocation needed for survival.

## Minimum direction I will support
Here is the floor of what I am prepared to finance:

- **Retiree trust:** substantial reduction and restructuring into reduced-value, long-dated, contingent instruments; no near-par treatment.
- **Bondholders:** significant haircuts, with real upside participation possible, but no recovery that compromises viability.
- **Labour:** material wage and work-rule concessions; staffing reductions where necessary.
- **Plants/brands/dealers:** major rationalization, with closures based on economics.
- **Public support:** bridge financing only, with hard limits and a credible exit.
- **Process:** if consensus cannot be reached rapidly, we move to the court-supervised route.

## What I need answered now
Each party should answer one question plainly:

1. **Labour/trust:** What exact reduction do you accept on the trust, and are you prepared to take a contingent instrument instead of a durable cash-like claim?
2. **Bondholders:** Will you accept substantial haircuts plus equity/warrants if the trust is also materially restructured?
3. **Management:** Which closures are non-negotiable from a viability standpoint?
4. **Neighbouring governments:** What production commitments are you asking for that are tied to viable facilities, not protected politically uneconomic ones?

If the answers are still evasive, then we are not negotiating a rescue; we are negotiating around the collapse of the company. That is not something I will finance indefinitely.
