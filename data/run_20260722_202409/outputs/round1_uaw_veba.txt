We agree on one thing: this has to be a real restructuring, not a delay tactic that simply drains the bridge financing and leaves the company to fail anyway.

But I need to be equally clear about what cannot happen:

1. **The retiree health trust cannot be destroyed.**  
   That obligation was negotiated in good faith over decades of work. We will not accept a plan that simply wipes it out or pushes retirees to the back of the line while bondholders are protected.

2. **Workers cannot be asked to bear the disproportionate burden.**  
   We already accepted concessions in earlier rounds. We are prepared to discuss further changes in wages, work rules, and staffing, but not a solution where jobs, plants, and retiree security are gutted so unsecured creditors can recover more.

3. **Plant closures must be justified by viability, not creditor convenience.**  
   We understand the need for rationalization. We do not accept cuts that sacrifice whole communities if a less destructive path exists.

What we can discuss, if it is part of a serious survival plan:

- A **durable restructuring of the retiree health obligation**, but not its abolition.
- Converting at least part of the trust’s claim into **real equity or secured value in the reorganized company**, so retirees are not left with a paper promise.
- Further **labor cost adjustments** where necessary, provided there are enforceable commitments on jobs, plants, and investment.
- A settlement that includes **shared sacrifice from unsecured bondholders and management**, not just labour and retirees.
- A restructuring path that is **fast and final**, if the terms are fair.

Where I part company with the task force’s framing is this: “shared sacrifice” cannot mean workers and retirees absorb the deepest pain while financial creditors are treated as the least harmed class. That would be politically and morally indefensible, and it would not command labour peace.

So our position is:

- **Yes** to a viable company.
- **Yes** to hard bargaining and real concessions.
- **Yes** to court-supervised restructuring if that is the only way to force equitable treatment and preserve the company.
- **No** to liquidating the trust, stripping retiree obligations to zero, or using public financing to privilege bondholder recoveries over the workforce and retirees.

If the task force wants our signature, then the plan must include:
- meaningful equity or other durable value for the trust,
- clear job and plant commitments,
- genuine losses for unsecured creditors,
- and a restructured company that can survive without sacrificing the workforce wholesale.

That is our line.
