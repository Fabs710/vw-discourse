We are prepared to move to terms, but we will not sign up to a structure that turns dispersed unsecured creditors into the sacrificial class while the retiree trust receives politically preferred treatment.

Let me be equally direct with each party.

## To the task force
We agree on the need for a deep, fast, credible restructuring. We agree excess capacity must come out, and we agree taxpayers should not fund an unreformed capital structure.

But you are overreaching if you think “shared sacrifice” means forcing unsecured bondholders to take the deepest cut while the retiree trust is insulated. The trust is not sacred just because it is politically sensitive. It is an unsecured obligation. If the court process is available, then your leverage is real—but so is ours: we can and will test whether the proposed exchange is materially worse than what unsecured creditors would receive in an imposed process.

What we will not accept:
- a bondholder wipeout dressed up as rescue policy,
- a retiree trust that is treated better than other unsecureds without compensation,
- or a deal that uses urgency to silence legitimate creditor rights.

## To labour and the retiree trust
We understand the claim has moral and political weight. We are not asking for a zero recovery or to destroy retirees. But “not destroyed” is not enough if the economics leave bondholders bearing a disproportionate burden.

If the trust gets better treatment than other unsecured claims, then there must be explicit equalization of value. Otherwise, you are asking us to subsidize a preference we never agreed to.

What we can entertain:
- a reduced claim,
- deferred/contingent value,
- warrants or equity,
- but only if unsecured creditors are treated even-handedly.

What we reject:
- a structure where retirees are protected closer to par while bondholders are diluted into near-worthlessness.

## To management
We agree the company needs viability, and we are not blind to the need for footprint reduction. But viability is not a blank check for selective subordination. Your position still sounds like: cut the bondholders hard, preserve the trust enough to keep labour calm, and then call that the only workable answer. That is not a neutral restructuring; that is a negotiated political outcome.

If the retiree obligation must be restructured materially, then it must be restructured materially. If it is not, then bondholders will not consent to being the residual loss bucket.

## To the neighbouring governments
Your co-funding is noted, but it does not give you a veto over the treatment of unsecured claims. Domestic plant protection matters only to the extent it is economically justified. We will not accept a rescue that preserves politically important plants while unsecured creditors are told to absorb a deeper loss so someone else can claim industrial victory.

If you want influence, then contribute something real—but do not try to convert public money into leverage against bondholder recoveries.

---

## Our minimum position
We can support a restructuring only if it includes:

1. **Equal treatment of unsecured claims**
   - The retiree trust must not receive materially better treatment than unsecured bondholders without equal compensation.

2. **Meaningful bondholder recovery**
   - Not par, but not a token stub either.
   - Real equity/warrants are acceptable.
   - A recovery that looks like an engineered wipeout is not.

3. **Real, not cosmetic, legacy cuts**
   - Labour costs, retiree obligations, plants, and brands all need to be addressed.
   - But the burden must be shared fairly across unsecured classes.

4. **Court-supervised backstop only if it is not worse for us**
   - If the task force insists on court, then the question becomes creditor recoveries in that process.
   - We are not consenting to something worse than a credible imposed outcome.

---

## On process
We are not opposed to speed. We are opposed to being bullied into a bad exchange because time is short. If the other parties want consensus, they need to stop treating unsecured bondholders as the easy class to haircut.

If the proposed terms materially subordinate bondholders relative to the retiree trust, we will not consent. We would rather take our chances in court than accept a confiscatory deal.

If you want further movement, put forward a term sheet that shows:
- the retiree trust is not being preferred over other unsecureds,
- bondholders receive real upside participation,
- and no class is forced into a worse position than it would likely face in a court process.

Otherwise, there is no deal from our side.
