## Core impasse

The central deadlock is **who absorbs the “priority” loss and how much protection the retiree trust and domestic footprint receive relative to unsecured bondholders and overall viability**.

More specifically, the parties are split on four linked issues:

1. **Retiree trust treatment**
   - Government task force and management want a **material haircut** and reduced, contingent value.
   - Labour/trust wants a **durable, meaningful recovery** and rejects being treated as disposable.
   - Bondholders oppose any structure where the trust is effectively **preferred over other unsecured claims**.

2. **Relative burden among unsecured stakeholders**
   - Bondholders want **equal treatment of unsecured claims** and real recovery.
   - Labour/trust wants sacrifices to be **shared**, not concentrated on workers/retirees.
   - The task force wants **deep losses across all legacy claims**, but with the package still viable.

3. **Plant closure vs. domestic protection**
   - Management and the task force want closure rights based on economics.
   - Neighbouring governments want **binding domestic production and employment commitments** as the price of funding.
   - Labour wants plant cuts only where justified, not politically targeted.
   - Bondholders resist footprint protection if it worsens recoveries.

4. **Control and finality**
   - All parties accept court-supervision as a fallback, but each wants it to improve or at least not worsen its position.
   - No one yet accepts a term sheet that clearly defines **who gives what** in exchange for what.

---

## Concrete compromise positions

### Compromise A: “Shared haircut with capped floor + contingent upside”
A possible bridge on the retiree trust and unsecured claims:

- **Retiree trust**
  - receives a **material reduction** in face value,
  - but with a **minimum recovery floor** in the form of long-dated contingent value and/or warrants,
  - plus limited cash or funded instruments to preserve durability.

- **Bondholders**
  - take a **comparable or slightly larger nominal haircut** than the trust,
  - but receive a clearer equity/warrant upside package,
  - with no class receiving a near-par outcome.

- **Purpose**
  - avoids a “wipeout” for the trust,
  - while addressing bondholder objections to preference.

### Compromise B: “Footprint package tied to viability tests”
A possible bridge on plants and co-funding:

- **Neighbouring governments**
  - provide funding only for plants meeting a **defined viability test** and minimum production thresholds.

- **Management/task force**
  - retain authority to close or consolidate facilities **that fail the test**.

- **Labour/trust and governments**
  - receive **binding commitments** for the facilities that pass the test:
    - production allocation,
    - employment transition support,
    - and investment obligations.

- **Purpose**
  - gives governments real protection without a blanket veto,
  - preserves management’s ability to rationalize uneconomic sites.

### Compromise C: “Court-supervised plan with negotiated priority rules”
A possible bridge on process and recoveries:

- Parties agree to a **court-supervised restructuring** as the backstop,
- but pre-negotiate:
  1. a **shared haircut band** for labour/trust/bondholders,
  2. a **priority-neutral principle** that no unsecured class gets materially better treatment without offsetting value,
  3. a **minimum domestic footprint package** tied to funding.

- **Purpose**
  - reduces litigation risk,
  - gives all sides a clearer expected outcome than open-ended court conflict,
  - and may accelerate finality.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Government Task Force
**Would you accept a capped recovery floor for the retiree trust, so long as unsecured bondholders receive at least equivalent upside participation and the overall package remains viable?**

### 2) Industrial Union & Retiree Health Trust
**What specific reduction in the trust obligation, and what mix of equity, warrants, or contingent value, would you accept as the minimum durable settlement?**

### 3) Company Management
**Which plants, brands, or dealer relationships are non-negotiable from a business viability standpoint, and which ones would you commit to close if the economics do not support them?**

### 4) Unsecured Bondholders’ Committee
**Would you accept a restructured package where the trust is not near par, but receives a defined minimum recovery floor, provided bondholders receive comparable or better total value through equity or warrants?**

### 5) Neighbouring Governments
**What exact production, employment, and governance commitments would satisfy your co-funding requirements without amounting to a de facto veto over rationalization?**
