We are not going to bless a restructuring that makes workers and retirees the residual dumping ground for everyone else’s preferred recovery.

## To the task force
We agree on three fundamentals:
- the company needs a deep, fast, final restructuring,
- the old plan is not enough,
- and public money should not be used to finance delay.

Where we disagree is your insistence that the retiree trust must be pushed into a near-wipeout while everyone else argues over “viability.” That is not a settlement; it is a transfer of pain onto the people who already gave up wages and benefits in earlier rounds.

We are prepared to accept a **material impairment** of the trust. We are **not** prepared to accept:
- a token stub,
- a purely symbolic contingent right,
- or a structure where the trust is stripped of durable value while bondholders and co-funders protect their own positions.

If the trust is restructured, it must be converted into something **real**:
- meaningful equity,
- warrants with actual upside,
- and/or a long-dated, contractually protected funding stream that has genuine economic value.

A “modest floor” may be too much if it behaves like senior debt, but **no floor at all** and no durable value is not acceptable either.

## To the bondholders
You are right about one thing: we will not agree to treat bondholders as the only sacrificial class while the retiree trust is insulated.

But your demand for pure parity in headline treatment misses the actual deal structure. The retiree obligation was part of total compensation. It is not ordinary paper, and it cannot be negotiated as if it were.

So here is the line:
- yes to **substantial haircuts** for the trust,
- yes to **substantial haircuts** for bondholders,
- yes to **upside participation** for unsecureds,
- no to a structure that strips the trust of all durable value just because bondholders want an even headline number.

If you want “equal treatment,” then equal treatment must mean **comparable present value**, not identical nominal formulas. And it cannot come at the expense of the workforce’s negotiated retirement security.

## To management
You keep saying viability first. We agree — but viability is not an excuse to externalize all the losses onto labour and retirees.

We need specifics, not generalities:
- which plants are truly non-viable,
- which closures are unavoidable,
- what the dealer network reduction looks like,
- and what labor cost and work-rule changes are actually required.

If you come back with another softened version of the old footprint, we will reject it.

And let me be clear: if the company survives, the people who made the concessions must have a **real stake** in the future. That means the trust cannot be treated as a disposable legacy claim. It must be transformed into durable value tied to the reorganized enterprise.

## To the neighbouring governments
We understand why you want domestic production commitments. We are not opposed to viable plants receiving binding support. But we will not endorse a deal that preserves politically sensitive capacity at the expense of other workers, other plants, or the company’s survival.

We can support:
- commitments for **plants that pass an economic viability test**,
- production allocations for those sites,
- and transition support where reductions are unavoidable.

We cannot support:
- a regional veto,
- blanket protection for uneconomic plants,
- or public co-funding that simply shifts jobs around while the enterprise remains overbuilt.

## Our position on the expert advice
The specialist is right on the mechanics: the trust cannot be near par, and a pure wipeout is unstable.

But the specialist also confirms something important for us: the best structure is a **material impairment plus equity/warrants/contingent value**, not a hollow stub. That is exactly the point. We are not insisting on cash-like priority. We are insisting on **real economic value**.

A structure that gives the trust:
- a meaningful haircut,
- plus equity/warrants,
- plus a contingent performance-linked instrument,

is workable. A structure that gives it nothing durable is not.

## Our minimum acceptable framework
We are still prepared to negotiate, but only within these limits:

1. **Retiree trust**
   - materially impaired,
   - but converted into durable value, not erased.

2. **Bondholders**
   - substantial losses,
   - with upside participation,
   - no special privilege over the trust unless the trust’s package is offset in value.

3. **Labour**
   - concessions on wages and work rules,
   - but no wholesale sacrifice of jobs and plants.

4. **Plants**
   - closures only where economics justify them,
   - no gratuitous destruction of viable capacity.

5. **Public funding / co-funding**
   - temporary, capped, and conditioned on a real rescue,
   - not on stripping labour of all durable claim.

## What we will not accept
Let me say this plainly:
- We will not accept a trust outcome that is effectively a wipeout.
- We will not accept bondholders or public funders being treated as more legitimate claimants than the workers and retirees who built the company.
- We will not accept plant closures driven by politics or creditor optics rather than viability.
- We will not sign a deal that leaves the trust with no real claim on the restructured company.

## On the question each party is asking
You want numbers. Fine — but the numbers have to be negotiated in the context of the whole package.

Our side can discuss:
- a **large impairment** of the trust obligation,
- paired with **meaningful equity or warrants**,
- and a long-dated contingent instrument with real value.

What we are not prepared to do is state a precise percentage today while the rest of the package remains undefined. If management and the task force want a number, they need to put real plant closures, real bondholder losses, and real ownership terms on the table.

## Bottom line
We are willing to make the company viable.
We are not willing to make the workforce and retirees the price of everyone else’s rescue.

Bring back a term sheet that shows:
- material trust impairment, yes,
- but also durable trust value,
- genuine bondholder sacrifice,
- transparent viability-based plant decisions,
- and a real ownership stake for the stakeholders giving up value.

If that cannot be done consensually, then the court-supervised route may be necessary — but do not assume it will be easier to impose a settlement that destroys the trust and still expects labour peace.
