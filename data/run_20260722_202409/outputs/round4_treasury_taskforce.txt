## My position remains unchanged

I will **not** finance:
- a near-par or politically protected treatment for the retiree trust,
- a footprint plan that preserves uneconomic capacity,
- or a structure that pushes the burden primarily onto bondholders or taxpayers.

The company needs a **deep, fast, final restructuring**. Anything else is delay financed by the public.

## To labour / the retiree trust
I agree with the specialist and management on one central point: the trust cannot be preserved near par. That is economically impossible and politically indefensible.

But I also reject the idea that the trust should be wiped out or reduced to a decorative stub. That would be unstable, and I am not interested in financing an outcome that simply converts insolvency into a labor war.

So here is the hard line:
- the trust must take a **substantial impairment**;
- it can receive **non-cash upside** only if that is clearly subordinate to viability;
- but there will be **no protected floor** that effectively privileges it over other unsecured legacy claims.

If you want a settlement, bring me a package that reflects real impairment, not moral argument.

## To the bondholders
You are right to object to any hidden preference for the trust. I am not endorsing that.

But your position goes too far when it implies the trust must be treated as just another unsecured claim with no room for settlement value. This is not a textbook liquidation; it is an emergency restructuring of a systemically important industrial company. Some unevenness in form may be necessary if the **total package** is fair and viable.

What you do **not** get is:
- a demand for clean relative parity that leaves no room for labour settlement,
- or an argument that any non-cash trust recovery is confiscatory.

What you **do** need to accept:
- significant haircuts,
- equity/warrants as the likely value form,
- and a court-supervised fallback if you refuse to align.

## To neighbouring governments
I will say this plainly: your funding does **not** buy a veto over economic rationalization.

I am prepared to consider co-funding and limited governance rights, but only if they support a **viable** company. You do not get binding production guarantees for plants that fail the viability test.

So:
- viable plants can be supported;
- uneconomic plants can be closed or consolidated;
- and your participation can translate into proportionate governance or equity, but **not control over operational decisions** that destroy the company’s economics.

If you insist on domestic entitlements divorced from viability, then you are not co-funding a rescue — you are subsidizing future failure.

## To management
You have my support only if you stop speaking in generalities and start naming the cuts.

I need:
- the non-negotiable plant closures,
- the brands or platforms to exit,
- the dealer reductions that actually matter,
- and the labor cost actions that make the numbers work.

If the new management comes back with another softened version of the old structure, I will reject it again.

## On the specialist advice
The pension specialist has confirmed the key point: the trust cannot be near par, and a pure wipeout is destabilizing. That supports my position, not weakens it.

The right structure is:
- **material impairment** of the trust,
- **non-cash residual value** if needed for settlement,
- and **no fixed-value promise** that burdens the reorganized firm.

That means no “modest floor” that turns into a backdoor priority. I am not financing that.

## What I am prepared to finance
Here is the range I can support:

- **Retiree trust:** substantial haircut; possible equity/warrants/contingent value, but only if clearly impaired and not economically senior.
- **Bondholders:** substantial haircut; upside participation possible; no clean recovery.
- **Labour:** wage, work-rule, and staffing concessions.
- **Plants/brands:** major rationalization based on economics.
- **Public money:** capped bridge financing only.
- **Process:** court-supervised fallback if a fast consensual deal is not reached.
