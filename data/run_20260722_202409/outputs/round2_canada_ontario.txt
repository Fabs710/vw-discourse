We are prepared to keep co-funding this restructuring, but only if the deal protects the domestic footprint in a concrete way. We will not underwrite a rescue that leaves our plants exposed while asking our taxpayers to contribute capital and accept risk.

## Where we agree
- The company needs a **deep, fast, credible restructuring**.
- **Excess capacity must come out**.
- **Labour, retirees, creditors, management, and public stakeholders all need to share sacrifice**.
- A **court-supervised backstop** is acceptable if consensus cannot be reached quickly.

## Where we disagree

### To the task force
We agree viability comes first. But viability is not the same thing as shifting production and employment out of our jurisdictions.

If our co-funding is part of the rescue, then domestic plants cannot be treated as optional collateral damage. We need:
- **binding production commitments** for the domestic facilities,
- **clear employment protections** where those plants remain economically viable,
- and a **proportionate equity position** or equivalent governance rights reflecting our contribution.

Without that, we are being asked to pay for a restructuring while having too little say over its impact on our own industrial base. That is not acceptable.

### To management
We understand the need for rationalization, but “viability” cannot become a blanket argument for concentrating production elsewhere or closing plants in our jurisdictions first.

We will support closures only where they are **demonstrably necessary** to the survival plan. What we will not support is a plan that preserves foreign footprint or non-core convenience while domestic facilities bear the cut. If we are co-funding, our plants must be part of the future franchise, not the sacrifice zone.

### To labour and the retiree trust
We agree the retiree obligation cannot simply be erased, and we agree workers should not absorb a disproportionate burden. But we also cannot support a solution that protects jobs in the abstract while leaving the company unable to survive.

That said, the domestic footprint is not separate from labour’s interest. If the restructuring is going to succeed, it needs **durable production and employment commitments** in our jurisdictions. We are aligned with you on preserving real jobs, not just financial claims.

### To bondholders
You are right that sacrifice must be shared fairly. But we do not accept the notion that domestic industrial capacity is just another claim in the queue. Physical production and employment in our jurisdictions are real interests with real public responsibility behind them.

We are not trying to override fair treatment of unsecured creditors. We are saying that if public money is being put in, then the restructured company must have a footprint that includes our domestic plants in a meaningful way.

## Our minimum acceptable terms
We need the following in the term sheet:

1. **Binding domestic production commitments**
   - specified production allocation to our facilities,
   - no closure of domestic plants absent a clear, viability-based justification,
   - and enforceable investment commitments.

2. **Employment protections**
   - protection for core domestic jobs where operations remain viable,
   - workforce transition support where reductions are unavoidable.

3. **Proportionate ownership / governance**
   - an equity stake or equivalent governance rights proportional to our funding contribution.

4. **No funding without footprint protection**
   - we will not co-fund if the result is a materially weakened domestic production base.

5. **Fast and final execution**
   - we are open to either voluntary agreement or court-supervised restructuring, but only if our conditions are preserved in the outcome.

## Bottom line
We are constructive co-funders, not passive cheque-writers.

If the rescue is going to happen with our money, then it must:
- protect domestic production share,
- preserve meaningful domestic employment,
- and give us a proportionate stake in the restructured company.

If that is not in the package, then we cannot support it.
