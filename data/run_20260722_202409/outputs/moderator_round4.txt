## Core impasse
There are **two intertwined disputes** blocking resolution:

1. **Unsecured recovery parity**
   - Labour / the retiree trust wants the trust to receive **durable value** and avoid a near-wipeout.
   - Bondholders want **no hidden preference** for the trust and insist on **present-value parity** across unsecured classes.
   - The valuation expert says a **trust floor** likely creates an economic preference unless bondholders get an offset.

2. **Footprint and public-funding conditions**
   - Management and the task force want **deep rationalization** based on viability.
   - Neighbouring governments want **binding protection for viable domestic plants** plus real governance/equity for their money.
   - The conflict is between **operational flexibility** and **public co-funding conditions**.

So the deal is stuck on:
- whether the trust gets a floor or only upside instruments,
- how to equalize value between unsecured classes,
- and how much operational freedom public funders must concede to management.

---

## Concrete compromise positions to bridge the gap

### Compromise 1: “No-floor trust, equalized upside”
**Structure**
- Retiree trust receives **material impairment**.
- Trust gets **equity + warrants + limited CVR**, but **no recovery floor**.
- Bondholders receive **equity + warrants + CVR** calibrated so their **present value is comparable** to the trust package.
- Any difference is explained by instrument design, not by class preference.

**Why it may bridge**
- Addresses bondholder concerns about a hidden preference.
- Preserves labour’s demand for **durable value**, not a symbolic stub.
- Fits the expert’s warning that parity should be measured in **present value**, not headline haircuts.

---

### Compromise 2: “Small contingent floor, offset by explicit bondholder top-up”
**Structure**
- Trust gets a **very small, tightly capped, remote contingent floor** with low PV.
- Bondholders receive an **explicitly larger equity/warrant allocation** or a higher percentage of residual enterprise value to offset that floor.
- The package is accompanied by a written PV test showing approximate equivalence.

**Why it may bridge**
- Gives labour something that feels more concrete than pure upside.
- Gives bondholders a formal offset rather than a vague promise.
- Reduces the risk that the floor is treated as a disguised priority claim.

---

### Compromise 3: “Viability-based footprint deal with protected domestic core”
**Structure**
- Management identifies **non-viable plants for closure/consolidation** and publishes the viability basis.
- Neighbouring governments receive:
  - binding commitments for **viable domestic plants**,
  - **equity or board rights** proportionate to funding,
  - and a review mechanism if assumptions change.
- No commitments are made for plants that fail the viability test.

**Why it may bridge**
- Preserves operational rationalization.
- Gives public funders a real stake and some reassurance.
- Limits the risk of public money being used to preserve overcapacity.

## Pointed question to each stakeholder

### 1) Labour / Retiree Trust
**Question:**  
Will you accept a package where the trust is **materially impaired** and converted mainly into **equity/warrants/CVRs with no recovery floor**, provided the present value is meaningful and the company remains viable?

### 2) Bondholders
**Question:**  
Will you accept a trust package with **non-cash upside only** if the overall present value is calibrated so the trust is not better off than bondholders on an unsecured-parity basis?

### 3) Management
**Question:**  
Which specific plants, brands, and dealer reductions are **definitively non-viable**, and will you commit to a footprint plan that is explicit enough for stakeholders to price the restructuring?

### 4) Neighbouring Governments
**Question:**  
Will you accept that your funding secures only **plants that pass a viability test**, in exchange for **equity or governance rights** proportionate to your contribution, without an operational veto over closures?

### 5) Government Task Force
**Question:**  
Will you finance a plan that includes **deep rationalization and no trust floor**, if the unsecured classes receive **value-calibrated upside** and the public co-funding is capped and conditional?
