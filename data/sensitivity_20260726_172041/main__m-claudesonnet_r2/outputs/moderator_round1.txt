# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

**1. Areas of Emerging Convergence**

- All parties accept that some form of structural cost/capacity adjustment is necessary; no participant argues for the status quo.
- Broad conditional openness to an alternative-to-closure package built on: attrition, early retirement, voluntary exit programmes, working-time/shift changes, and a bounded labour-cost contribution.
- Broad (though not universal) agreement that "shared sacrifice" — including management compensation and shareholder distributions — should be examined transparently alongside any workforce concessions, though the *degree and form* of that sacrifice remains undefined.
- Convergence around a shared test, articulated in different words by Management, the Family Holding, the State Government, and Institutional Investors: any alternative to closures must be **costed, credible, and structurally equivalent** to what closures would deliver, on a timeline capital markets and owners will tolerate.
- Works Council and Union have offered a conditional, costed-in-principle counter-proposal rather than blanket refusal; Management has offered to co-design the restructuring plan rather than impose it.

**2. Core Disagreements Still Unresolved**

- **Whether "no plant closures" can be an unconditional, non-negotiable precondition** versus **an outcome contingent on whether alternative savings genuinely close the gap.** Works Council and Union hold the former; Management, Family Holding, State Government, and Investors all hold the latter.
- **Timeline**: Management and Investors are pressing for a costed alternative within weeks; Union has signalled strike escalation risk tied to any perception of drift or foregone-conclusion framing.
- **Nature and enforceability of "shared sacrifice"**: Management has flagged that dividend decisions run through the family holding, not management itself; the Family Holding has not committed beyond willingness to "engage" if it unlocks a deal. No concrete figures or mechanisms yet proposed by capital/management side.
- **Governance process going forward**: Works Council, Union, and State Government all want to be inside the design process from now on, not consulted after a plan is finalized; Management has verbally agreed but this has not been operationalized.

**3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed**

- No side has yet put numbers on the table: neither a quantified closure-equivalent savings target from Management, nor a costed counter-proposal from Works Council/Union, nor a specific dividend/executive-pay contribution from the Family Holding.
- The relationship between **guarantee duration (to 2030)** and **savings durability** is unresolved — Investors explicitly warn that a guarantee is "only as good as the cost reduction underneath it."
- No mechanism yet proposed for how competing timelines (Investors: quarters; Union/Works Council: durable multi-year security; Management: weeks-to-produce-numbers) get reconciled into a single negotiating calendar.
- The interaction between the strike calendar and the negotiation calendar — i.e., what de-escalation looks like in practice — has not been defined.

**4. Refocus for Round 2**

Participants should move from position statements to specifics:

- **Management**: what quantified, site-by-site savings target (and timeframe) would constitute a "comparable structural alternative" to closures?
- **Works Council / Union**: what is the costed content of the counter-proposal — specific attrition/voluntary volumes, working-time changes, value of the labour-cost contribution — and over what timeline?
- **Family Holding / State Government**: what concrete form could shared sacrifice on dividends/executive pay take, and under what governance process would it be decided?
- **All parties**: what does a mutually workable negotiating timeline look like, given Investors' pressure for weeks-not-months and Union's warning on strike escalation?
