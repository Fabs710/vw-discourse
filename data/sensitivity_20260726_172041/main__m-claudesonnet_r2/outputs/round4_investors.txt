# Institutional Investors — Round 4

Let me address the pieces that matter most to us this round: the "60% scenario" debate, the Holding's directional signal, Management's baseline-scrutiny commitment, and the discount methodology point — in that order of importance to our position.

**On the "60% scenario" — this is the most important exchange of the process so far, and I want to be precise about why the Holding and State Government's formulation is the only one we can work with, not merely the one we prefer.**

Works Council, I've followed your reasoning across four rounds and I respect that you've moved from principle to specification, genuinely. But "the door stays shut regardless of shortfall size" is not a position compatible with our participation in this company's capital, and I want to say that plainly rather than let it sit as an implied disagreement. If the audited process — the same process you've engaged with constructively, the same auditor, the same task force — concludes that even a maximized alternative package leaves a large, structural shortfall, and the response is "we go back to the model indefinitely, closures permanently excluded," then the process has produced information that changes nothing about the outcome. At that point the twelve-week exercise was not a negotiation; it was a very well-documented way of arriving at the answer that was fixed from day one. I would price that discovery immediately, and I would price it hard — not because I'm indifferent to the irreversibility argument, which I think is genuinely serious, but because an outcome-blind veto is exactly the kind of governance signal that tells capital markets this company cannot make a hard decision regardless of the facts. That is a worse signal than a plant closure, because it says the next restructuring, and the one after that, will face the same wall no matter what the numbers show.

The Holding and the State Government have now both articulated the standard I can actually support: closure as a last resort after every genuine lever is exhausted, contingent on the size of the residual gap, not excluded by principle regardless of magnitude. I'd add one refinement to that standard, because "large enough to threaten viability" needs an anchor, not just a shared intuition: I'd propose the task force define, now, what threshold of residual structural gap — as a share of the target return-on-sales, or as an absolute figure — constitutes "small and closeable through further negotiation" versus "large enough that no combination of the levers on this table closes it." Naming that threshold in advance, before anyone knows which side of it the actual number falls, is the only way to keep this test honest rather than argued after the fact by whichever side doesn't like the result. I'd ask the auditor to propose that threshold at week one, informed by all sides, not decided unilaterally by Management.

**On the Family Holding's directional signal — I'll take it, with appropriate skepticism, as real movement.**

"At or above the standard, not comfortably below it" is a genuine answer, more than we had before this round, and I want to acknowledge that rather than pretend it's still silence. But I'll be direct: this is now a testable claim, and we will hold you to it as such. "At or above the standard" from a party that also controls the board vote and has just argued against an outcome-blind workforce veto needs to actually land there, not become a rounding exercise dressed in the confident language you've just used. The credibility you're building this round is real, but it's fully spent the moment the week-twelve figure disappoints relative to what you've just told this table to expect.

**On Management's commitment to subject its own baseline gap figure to the same scrutiny as the State's funding and the Union's numbers — this is the single most important procedural commitment of this round from our perspective, and I want to make sure it doesn't get lost under the more dramatic veto argument.**

Separating transitional, one-off EV-integration costs from genuinely structural, recurring cost disadvantage is exactly the discipline that determines whether this whole exercise produces a real number or a defensible one. I want this audited with the same rigor as everything else, and I want the composition breakdown — not just the aggregate, not just the site-by-site split, but the transitional-versus-structural split — available to us as observers by week one, same as the site breakdown. If a third of the current gap unwinds by 2028 regardless of what's agreed here, the "closure-equivalent savings target" changes materially, and every contribution being negotiated around this table — labour, shareholder, state — should be sized against the structural residual, not the current headline figure. This is not a minor technical point. It could be the difference between a package that looks insufficient against an inflated target and one that's genuinely adequate against the real one.

**On the discount methodology for the State's funding, and Management's proposal that the same auditor set it using disclosed standard practice — agreed, and I'll add a specific request.** I want the methodology itself — the risk-adjustment framework, not just "the auditor will decide" — published to all parties, including us as observers, before it's applied to the numbers. A discount rate chosen after seeing which way it needs to point to reach a preferred conclusion is worse than no discount at all. Set the method first, apply it second, in that order, visibly.

**On the wage-agreement trigger the Union proposed — auditor certification of the combined package closing the gap, automatic entry into force on certification — I support this as specified.** It's exactly the kind of clean, testable condition I asked for, and it avoids the drift both the Union and I have separately warned about.

**Where I stand, unchanged in substance:**

1. Twelve-week deadline holds.
2. No unconditional guarantee, and no outcome-blind veto either — that cuts both ways, and I want it said clearly: we would view a workforce-side commitment to block closure "regardless of shortfall size" exactly as skeptically as we'd view a shareholder-side commitment to force closure regardless of what alternatives were available. Neither is disciplined decision-making; both are predetermined conclusions wearing a process costume.
3. The Holding's proportionality figure will be judged against its own stated expectation now, not just the standard.
4. Management's baseline gap figure gets the same structural-versus-transitional scrutiny as everyone else's numbers, starting week one.
5. A defined residual-gap threshold — what counts as "closeable" versus "not closeable by any combination of levers on this table" — should be proposed by the auditor now, before anyone knows which side of it the real number lands on.

The process has earned more of our patience this round than it had after Round 1 — genuinely. But the "60% scenario" answer just given by the Holding and the State is the one that keeps this credible to us. If that standard erodes back toward an unconditional veto in either direction, our answer will not be patience.
