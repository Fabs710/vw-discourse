I've been asked directly, three times now, for a directional signal on the proportionality figure, and avoiding it a fourth time would itself become the answer. Let me address that, and then engage with the "60% scenario" debate, because it now bears directly on the Holding's own vote.

**On direction, since silence itself has become a data point.** I will say this much concretely: I expect the eventual figure to be a real, multi-year reduction in dividend distribution and executive compensation, not a token adjustment — I would not have accepted the Works Council's proportionality standard as the test if I intended to fail it deliberately. But I want to be honest about the constraint I actually face, rather than offer false precision to relieve pressure in this room. The holding's own return depends on this company's health; a dividend cut that is announced as generous now but turns out to be poorly calibrated against what the workforce is actually contributing would be worse for the holding's long-term credibility than a delayed number that lands correctly. I recognize that reasoning has now been used, in different forms, by several parties to justify not naming a figure early, and I recognize the pattern is starting to look, from your side of the table, like coordinated delay rather than independent discipline. It isn't coordinated. But I take the concern seriously enough to say plainly: I expect to be at or above the standard, not comfortably below it, and if week twelve shows otherwise, I accept that the criticism landing on the holding at that point will be deserved, not unfair.

**On the "60% scenario" — I want to engage with this directly, because Management is right that it can't stay unanswered, and I think the State Government's answer this round is closer to correct than the Works Council's.**

The Works Council's position — that a shortfall of any size sends us back to the model, with closure permanently excluded regardless of magnitude — is not a position the holding can accept as a standing precondition. I understand the reasoning behind it: the irreversibility argument is real, and I said last round I don't dismiss it. But "the door stays shut regardless of shortfall size" converts a negotiating stance into an absolute veto over evidence, and that is precisely the "board rubber-stamping a predetermined result" that Investors warned about — just held by a different party than the one they had in mind. I will not commit the holding's votes in advance to an outcome-blind rule any more than I'll commit them in advance to ratifying whatever the task force produces. Both are the same error in opposite directions.

The State Government's formulation — that the size of the residual shortfall matters, that every genuine lever must be exhausted first, and that closure is a last resort rather than a first response to any gap — is the position I can actually stand behind as a matter of stewardship, not just tactics. A small residual shortfall after every lever is genuinely pulled is a negotiating problem: extend the sunset trigger, deepen the working-time measure modestly, find another quarter of state co-investment. A large, structural shortfall that persists even after every contribution named in this room is maximized is not a negotiating problem anymore — it's a signal that the company's German cost base cannot be made competitive without capacity reduction, and at that point an absolute no-closures rule stops protecting jobs and starts guaranteeing that the whole enterprise, and every job in it, erodes more slowly but just as completely. That is exactly the outcome I said in Round 1 I will not accept: permanent impairment of the enterprise's value, arrived at gradually rather than in one visible decision, is not a better outcome than a hard decision taken honestly. It is a worse one, dressed as patience.

So to Works Council and Union directly: I will not treat "no closures regardless of the number" as an acceptable precondition for the holding's vote. I will treat "closure only after every genuine lever is exhausted, and only if the residual gap is large enough to threaten the company's viability, not merely inconvenient to management" as a serious, defensible standard — because that is the standard the State Government just articulated, and it's the one I intend to apply myself when the numbers arrive. If you want the holding's ten additional votes — beyond the workforce bench's ten — you will get them for a negotiated outcome that closes the gap. You will not get an advance guarantee that forecloses closure as a matter of principle no matter how large an unaddressed structural gap turns out to be. That is not a threat; it's the boundary of what stewardship of this asset actually requires of me, and no argument this round has moved me off it.
