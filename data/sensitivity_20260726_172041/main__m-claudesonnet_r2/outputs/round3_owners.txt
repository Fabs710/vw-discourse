I've now been asked directly, by the Union, the State Government, and implicitly by Management, to answer the one question I left open last round: not the figure, but the proportionality standard. I said I would not manufacture a number under pressure, and I hold to that. But three parties in good faith have told me that silence on the standard itself is starting to read as evidence of bad faith, and I have to take that seriously rather than let it fester into the next round.

**On the proportionality standard — yes, I will commit to it.** The Works Council's formulation is fair and I accept it as the standard the holding will be judged against: the dividend restraint and executive compensation reduction, measured as a share of their respective pools, should be comparable in proportional terms to the workforce contribution measured as a share of total labour cost. Not identical in euros — nobody serious is asking for that, given the relative size of the pools — but comparable in proportional sacrifice, audited by the same mechanism reviewing everything else. I will bring the actual figure to the board at the twelve-week mark, tested against this standard, and I accept that if it falls short, the Union, the State, and Investors will say so publicly, and they will be right to. I'd rather commit to a standard I can be held to than keep offering reassurance that isn't falsifiable. That has been a fair criticism of my position across two rounds, and I'm closing it now.

**On the board vote question, since the Works Council pressed me specifically and Management and the State Government have now both answered in a similar register.** I want to be precise, because I think Management's formulation was the right one and I'm not going to soften it to make anyone more comfortable. The task force informs the board; it does not bind it. I said that last round because it is true, not because I intend to use it as an escape hatch. Let me be concrete about what that means for the holding specifically, since we control the majority of votes involved: if the joint-costed package genuinely closes the structural gap on the agreed timeline, with an auditable comparison both sides accept, the holding will vote for it and will not entertain a management or board attempt to reintroduce closures as a preferred option once that condition is met. That is a real constraint on our own vote, not just rhetoric. But I will not promise in advance to ratify whatever emerges from the task force regardless of what the numbers actually show, and I don't think any of you should want me to — a board that pre-commits to an outcome before seeing the evidence is not exercising governance, it's performing it. The distinction between "informs" and "binds" is not a hedge against the workforce. It is the same discipline I am asking everyone else to accept: real numbers, decided through the proper process, not committed to in advance under pressure of the discussion itself.

**On the regional/social-cost dispute — I want to register agreement with where Management, Investors, and now the State Government itself have landed.** The State Government's move this round — proposing real transition funding and co-investment tied to specific sites and product allocation, built into the model as an actual euro input rather than asking the audited comparison to absorb an unquantifiable externality — is exactly the right way to resolve that argument. It respects the model's integrity while giving the regional dimension genuine weight through an instrument that's actually yours to deploy. I'd encourage that to be formalized quickly, because it may materially change the site-by-site arithmetic Management is bringing to week one, and the sooner it's in the model, the more useful it is.

**On the Works Council's specific numbers and Management's response to them — I think Management asked the right follow-up questions, and I want to be clear I expect the same rigor applied to every side's figures, including our own eventual dividend and executive-pay proposal.** Attrition numbers that don't specify capacity consequences, working-time reductions that don't specify fixed-cost absorption — these are legitimate gaps to close in the task force, not reasons to distrust the Works Council's good faith. I'd say the same about our own contribution: when it lands, I expect it to be interrogated with the same seriousness, not simply accepted because it comes with a sunset clause.

**Where I have not moved, and will not.** I said last round I will not ratify an unconditional site-closure guarantee independent of whether the joint-costed numbers actually close the gap, and nothing in this round changes that. I understand the Works Council's and Union's position that a closed plant cannot reopen and that this asymmetry is categorical for them. I don't dismiss it — the state's and management's answers on irreversibility this round were more complete than mine would have been, and I largely align with Management's framing: an unresolved structural gap is also a form of irreversibility, just a slower one, and the holding cannot treat a guarantee as sacred in a way that lets that slower failure compound unchecked. If the alternative package closes the gap, the holding backs it, fully, and I've now made that commitment concrete rather than provisional by accepting the proportionality standard. If it doesn't close the gap, an unconditional guarantee is not something I will sign merely because it was demanded first — that would not be stewardship of this asset, it would be deferring a real problem onto a future generation of owners and workers who will have fewer options than we do now to fix it.

Twelve weeks, real numbers, a standard I've now committed to being judged against. That is where the holding stands.
