[Regional State Government — Round 3]

Let me take stock of what actually changed this round, then be precise about where I stand on the two disputes that most directly touch my role: the treatment of regional/social cost, and the relationship between the task force and the board vote.

**On the embedded seat: thank you, Management, and I'll take you at your word.** Our two board representatives will engage in the task force in good faith, as co-authors, not observers. I want to be equally direct about what I expect in return: full visibility into the site-by-site breakdown you've now committed to producing, from week one, on the same terms as everyone else at that table. No filtered summaries.

**On the regional/social-cost question — I've heard Investors and Management make essentially the same argument this round, and I want to respond to it honestly rather than simply restate my position louder.** Management, your distinction is a real one, and I won't pretend it isn't: costs that land directly on this company's own books are different in kind from costs that land on the region, the tax base, and third-party suppliers, even if both are real and even if both eventually feed back into the company's fortunes over a longer horizon. I accept that folding an inherently contestable regional-impact estimate into the task force's audited savings model would weaken the one thing that makes that model credible to everyone at this table, including Investors, including me. I'm not going to insist on corrupting the instrument that's doing the actual work.

But accepting that the task force's savings arithmetic should stay clean does not mean I accept that regional cost is therefore irrelevant to this decision, or that it only gets a hearing after everything else is settled, in my vote, as an afterthought. It means it belongs somewhere specific and real, and I'm going to make it concrete rather than leave it as a rhetorical claim: **the state is prepared to bring genuine transition funding and investment co-financing to the sites covered by the alternative package** — infrastructure support, retraining programmes, and conditional investment incentives tied to future product allocation at the plants carrying the heaviest overcapacity. This is not a symbolic gesture and it's not a demand that management do something for free on my account. It is the state acting as the instrument Investors themselves said was the right one for this — using our own policy and fiscal tools to help close the structural gap on the alternative-package side of the ledger, rather than asking the task force to pretend an unquantifiable externality is a unit-cost figure. Management, Family Holding: if this genuinely helps the numbers close on the twelve-week timeline, I want it built into the joint model as a real input — a euro figure attached to specific sites and specific investment commitments — not treated as separate from the arithmetic entirely. That is my answer to the "keep the model clean" argument: I agree, and I'm bringing something real to make the clean model close, rather than asking you to dirty it on my behalf.

**On the board vote versus the task force — Family Holding, Management, I understand the legal point you're both making, and I won't argue the law. But I want to state, plainly, what that legal fact means for how I will actually use my vote, because that is the part that is mine to decide, not yours to characterize for me.**

I will judge the eventual package against three tests I named last round, unchanged: does it genuinely close the structural gap with real, audited numbers; does it include a concrete, dated, and — this is new and important — *proportionate* shareholder and executive contribution, not a token gesture; and does it deliver durable site and job guarantees with no compulsory redundancies. If the task force does honest work and the package meets those three tests, I will vote for it, and I will defend it publicly against pressure from any side to reopen it later — including, if necessary, pressure from within my own political constituency to hold out for more. That is a real commitment, and I want it heard as one.

But I will not pre-commit my vote to whatever the task force produces regardless of content, any more than Management will pre-commit to a guarantee regardless of what the numbers show. Works Council, Union — I understand your anxiety about a fait-accompli-in-reverse, and I share the underlying concern, but I don't think the answer is asking the board to rubber-stamp the task force's conclusion. The answer is what I'm doing right now: stating clearly, in advance, exactly what I will judge the package against, so that nobody discovers my criteria for the first time in week twelve. If Management or the Family Holding bring a package to the board that reintroduces closures despite a task force finding that a comparable alternative was available and adequately costed, or despite an unresolved asymmetry on shareholder sacrifice, I will use the veto. Not as a threat deployed for effect — as the plain, stated consequence of my three conditions not being met. That is exactly what the special statute is for, and I have no discomfort saying so directly.

**On the Family Holding's proportionality standard — I want to add my voice to the Union and Works Council here, because this is the one place where vagueness is now actively corroding trust in the whole process.** You've given a mechanism and a date. That was real progress last round. But "mechanism and date, not a figure" cannot also mean "mechanism and date, not a standard." The Works Council's proposal — comparable proportional sacrifice, dividend distribution and executive compensation measured against their own respective pools, workforce contribution measured against total labour cost — is a fair, articulable standard that doesn't require you to name a premature number. I am asking you, directly, in this round: will you commit to being judged against that standard, or a clearly stated alternative, when the figure lands in week twelve? If the answer is silence again, I will read that as evidence that the eventual figure is likely to be a rounding error, and I will say so at the board before the vote, not after.

**On the sunset/recovery trigger — I endorse the specification as landed: two consecutive full fiscal years at the ROS target, audited by the same auditor, applied symmetrically to the labour contribution and to any shareholder/executive restraint.** This is exactly the kind of precise, durable mechanism I need to see more of, and I'd like the state co-investment commitment I've outlined above built with comparable discipline — tied to delivery milestones, not an open-ended pledge.

**Where I stand, restated with this round's specifics:** I support the task force as structured, now with our seats confirmed inside it. I am bringing a concrete, conditional transition-funding offer to help close the structural gap on the alternative-package side, rather than asking the audited model to absorb an unquantifiable externality. I will vote against any package that reintroduces closures without the three tests being met, and I will vote for a package that meets them, publicly and durably. What remains unresolved, and needs to be resolved by next round, is the Family Holding's answer on the proportionality standard — that is the single clearest signal left of whether shared sacrifice is real or rhetorical, and I will not treat continued silence on it as neutral.
