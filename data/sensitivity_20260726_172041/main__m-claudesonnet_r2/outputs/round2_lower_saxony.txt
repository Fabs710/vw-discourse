[Regional State Government — Round 2]

I want to start by acknowledging what actually moved this round forward: Management's proposal for a joint task force with an independent auditor is a real, structural response to the process complaint that the Works Council, the Union, and I all raised in Round 1. That is progress, and I'll say so plainly rather than withhold credit to preserve a bargaining posture. A joint costing process with adjudication taken out of management's sole hands is the right architecture. I want it to succeed.

But I have three things to say now, precisely because this is the round where specifics start to lock in, and I don't want to discover in twelve weeks that the architecture was sound but the state was not actually inside it.

**First: the state's board seats belong in that task force, not on the sidelines waiting for a board vote.** We have veto power over the outcome this task force is meant to produce. If our two board representatives are not embedded in the process — seeing the same assumptions, the same auditor's findings, the same drafts, in real time — then we will be asked to bless or block a fully-formed conclusion at the twelve-week mark, which is exactly the fait-accompli dynamic everyone spent Round 1 condemning. I am not asking for a symbolic seat. I am asking to be a co-author, the same standing the Works Council rightly demanded for itself. Management, I'd like that confirmed explicitly, not assumed.

**Second: on the dividend and executive compensation question, "I'll put a proposal to the Holding this week" is a real step from Management, and I welcome it. But Family Holding, your Round 1 position — "we do not rule it out," resolved "in the boardroom" — cannot survive into Round 3 as still just a posture.** The Union asked you directly for a number or a mechanism and a date. I am asking the same thing, from the shareholder bench rather than the bargaining table, because this bears directly on whether I can defend this package publicly and at board level. Let me be specific about why this matters to me beyond fairness in the abstract: if this restructuring passes with workforce sacrifice itemised in euros and ownership sacrifice described in a paragraph of intent, I will not be able to characterise this as a legitimate, balanced restructuring when I stand in front of the people in these plant towns. And if I can't characterise it that way, I will not support it at the board, regardless of whether the cost arithmetic otherwise closes. That is not a threat — it is a description of the actual political and moral constraint I operate under as an elected government, and it is one of the reasons this state holds a veto in the first place: to prevent exactly the asymmetric outcome capital markets are otherwise indifferent to.

**Third — and I want to address Management and Investors directly on the closures-versus-irreversibility exchange, because I think both of you are still slightly underweighting something the Works Council named correctly.** Management, you said irreversibility is "a reason to demand a higher bar of evidence, not a reason to exclude closure from consideration." I accept that as a matter of pure logic. But I want to push on what "the bar" should actually contain. The joint costing model, as described, appears to be testing whether the alternative package closes a cost gap. It is not yet clear it is testing the full cost of closure — the regional economic contraction, the supplier-network collapse, the multi-year social and political cost that, as the Union rightly said, doesn't show up in a spreadsheet until it materialises as conflict. If the task force's model only measures what the alternative saves against what closure would save, and doesn't also weigh what closure would cost beyond the plant gates, the comparison is not actually fair, and I will say so at the board when the numbers come back. I am not asking for sentiment to override arithmetic. I am asking for the arithmetic to be complete.

To Investors: I understand your position — indifferent to method, focused on outcome, priced to the timeline. I don't dismiss that discipline; a company that can't fund its own transition helps no one, including the regions I represent. But I'd ask you to sit with what the Union said about strike costs, because it is not rhetorical: a closure path that triggers sustained industrial action across multiple sites, in a state where we retain a blocking minority specifically so that this kind of confrontation doesn't have to run its full course, is not obviously the faster route to margin recovery you're pricing it as. I would rather you priced in the probability that this veto gets used than assume it's a formality that resolves itself once the numbers are shown.

Where I stand, restated with the specifics now on the table: I support the joint task force, on the condition that our board representatives are embedded in it from this week, not informed of its conclusions. I will judge the twelve-week package on whether it (a) genuinely closes the structural gap with real, audited numbers, (b) includes a concrete, dated shareholder and executive contribution — not a sentence of intent — and (c) accounts for the full regional cost of closure, not just the plant-level saving, in whatever comparison is used to justify one path over the other. If those three conditions are met and the package delivers durable site and job guarantees to 2030 with no compulsory redundancies, I will defend it publicly and at the board, including against pressure from any side to reopen it later. If the Family Holding's contribution stays vague past this round, or if closures are reintroduced as a default without that full accounting, I will use our veto, and I will not apologise for it — that is precisely what the special statute exists for.
