I'll speak plainly, though briefly, because the holding's practice is to work through governance rather than through public argument — and I intend to keep it that way here too.

First, what should be obvious but bears stating: the family holding's interest in this company is measured in decades, not quarters. We did not build a controlling position to preside over a slow-motion loss of competitiveness, and we will not preside over a strike-driven collapse either. Both are value destruction. We regard them as symmetrical risks, not as a choice between a "management" outcome and a "labour" outcome.

To Management: we back the diagnosis. The cost base is genuinely uncompetitive, and margin recovery at the core brand is not optional — it is the precondition for everything else, including any investment commitments anyone here wants to make credible. We do not accept a cosmetic deal that leaves the structural gap unresolved and re-emerges as this same crisis in three years. On that, you have our support in principle. But you should also hear this clearly from the ownership side: a two-thirds board threshold is not an obstacle to be muscled past by press pressure or public escalation. If your plan cannot pass the board, it is not a plan, it is a press release. Spend your energy closing the gap with a credible alternative, not on public framing battles that cost us reputation and market confidence while achieving nothing.

To the Works Council and the Union: your leverage is real, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But "no closures, unconditionally, regardless of what the numbers show" is not a position the holding can simply ratify as a governing principle either. Our obligation runs to the enterprise's long-term value as a whole, not to any single site's permanence as an article of faith. If your alternative — attrition, voluntary programmes, working-time changes, a genuine cost contribution — actually closes the structural gap on a credible timeline, we will support it over closures, because it preserves capability and labour peace, both of which matter to long-term value. If it does not close the gap, we will not treat "no closures" as sacred merely because it was declared first.

To the State Government: we hear the point on shared sacrifice, and I will not dodge it the way management understandably hedged. Dividend and executive compensation are matters that run through the supervisory board, and the holding will engage with that question seriously if it is what stands between a durable deal and continued conflict. I make no commitment beyond that today, but I do not rule it out, and I would rather resolve it in the boardroom than have it forced by escalation.

Our position: bring us a costed, verified alternative that closes the competitiveness gap and secures durable labour peace, and we will use our influence to support it over closures. Fail to close that gap, or let this drift into prolonged confrontation that erodes the company's value while everyone postures, and we will not stand in the way of management pursuing the harder path. We are not moved by pressure from any side — we are moved by numbers that hold up and by stability that lasts.
