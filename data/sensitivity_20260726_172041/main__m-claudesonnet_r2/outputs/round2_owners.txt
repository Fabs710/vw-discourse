[Controlling Family Holding — Round 2]

I said last round that the holding works through governance rather than public argument, and three of you have now told me, in three different ways, that this round is not the round where that posture is still available to me. Fair. I'll answer directly.

**On dividend and executive compensation, since I was named specifically by the Union, the State Government, and Management.** Here is what I can commit to now, and I want to be precise about the boundary between what I am committing to and what I am not.

I commit to this: the supervisory board will take up a defined proposal on executive compensation reduction and a time-bound dividend restraint, mechanically linked to the same twelve-week timeline Management has proposed for the joint task force's output, and structured with the same sunset logic being discussed for the workforce contribution — if margins recover to target, the restraint scales back, just as the labour contribution would. That is a mechanism and a date, not a paragraph of intent. I will not, today, name a figure, because I am not going to manufacture a number in this room that hasn't gone through the board process any more than I'd accept a workforce contribution figure invented outside the joint costing exercise. Both sides of this ledger should be held to the same discipline: real numbers, arrived at through the proper process, not numbers produced under pressure in a discussion round to satisfy an optics deadline. If that reads as insufficiently concrete to some of you, I understand the frustration, but I'd rather give you a mechanism I will actually deliver than a number I'd have to walk back.

I want to be honest about why this matters to me beyond fairness optics: a package that is financially real but politically unsellable is not stable, and instability destroys value as surely as an uncompetitive cost base does. So this is not charity toward the Union's or the State's fairness argument — it is consistent with protecting the asset. Noted, and accepted as a genuine input to what the board decides.

**On the joint task force and the State Government's request to embed board representatives in it.** I support the task force in principle — Management's proposal to take adjudication out of its own hands and put it to an independent auditor is the right structural fix for the fait-accompli problem everyone rightly flagged. But I want to be clear about one governance point, because it matters and I don't think anyone has said it plainly yet: the task force can inform the board's decision, it cannot substitute for it. Whoever sits inside that process — Works Council, Union, State representatives, management — the outcome still requires a two-thirds supervisory board vote, and the holding's obligation runs to that vote, not to whatever consensus emerges in a working group beforehand. I say this not to undercut the process — I want it to succeed — but because I will not let the architecture drift into a situation where the board is presented, at week twelve, with something everyone has publicly committed to and calls a done deal, and the vote becomes a formality under a different name. State Government, you made exactly this point about not wanting to be handed a fait accompli. I am telling you the same discipline applies to the board itself, including to us.

**On the irreversibility and regional-cost-accounting question the Works Council, the State, and Management have been circling.** I think the State Government's point is a fair one and I'll say so directly: if the model built by the joint task force only measures alternative-package savings against closure savings, without at least acknowledging the broader cost of closure — supplier disruption, regional contraction, the multi-year conflict cost the Union has flagged — then the comparison is incomplete, and an incomplete comparison is not a sound basis for a board decision either way. I am not asking for sentiment to be smuggled into the arithmetic. I am saying that a complete arithmetic includes costs that are harder to quantify but still real, and the holding will want to see them addressed, not waved away, before we form a view on any closure recommendation that reaches the board.

**On what has not moved, and won't.** I heard Management's position that it will not sign an unconditional site guarantee independent of whether the numbers close, and I heard the Works Council and Union restate that "no closures" is a red line regardless. I said last round I would not ratify "no closures, unconditionally" as a governing principle, and nothing this round changes that. Twelve weeks, a joint costed model, and a genuine shared-sacrifice mechanism is the path I will support. If the alternative package closes the structural gap credibly, the holding backs it over closures — I'll repeat that plainly, because it is not a small commitment and I don't want it lost in the process discussion. If it doesn't close the gap, I will not treat an unconditional guarantee as sacred merely because declaring it first was someone's opening move.

One last point, briefly, because it bears on all of you equally: the family holding's tolerance is for a resolved outcome, not for the process itself running long. Twelve weeks is acceptable. A twelve-week process that produces another round of position-restating rather than a package the board can actually vote on is not. I would rather see this closed properly than closed fast, but I will not watch it drift indefinitely either — that is the one place where my patience has a limit, and I want that understood now rather than discovered later.
