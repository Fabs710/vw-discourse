# Moderator Summary — Following Round 3

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **State Government's task force seat confirmed** by Management: the two board representatives participate as full co-authors from day one, with complete access to assumptions, drafts, and audit findings.
- **Site-by-site cost breakdown**, not just an aggregate figure, will be produced by Management at week one of the task force. Management has disclosed in advance that the gap is unevenly distributed across sites.
- **Sunset/recovery trigger mechanics specified**: two consecutive full fiscal years meeting the brand ROS target, averaged and audited by a single named auditor, applied symmetrically to workforce contributions and to any dividend/executive-pay restraint. All six parties have endorsed this formulation; Investors and the Union add that the averaging methodology and specific ROS measure should be fixed in the written task force mandate itself.
- **Parallel wage-bargaining track**: Management agrees collective bargaining can open alongside the task force rather than after it, on condition that the agreement's entry into force is contingent on the overall package — capacity and cost together — closing the structural gap. Investors endorse this and add that dividend restraint should carry the same capacity-contingency, symmetrically.
- **Proportionality standard accepted**: the Family Holding has now committed to being judged against the Works Council's formulation — dividend restraint and executive-pay reduction, measured against their own respective pools, should be comparable in proportional terms to workforce contribution measured against total labour cost. The actual figure is still to come at week twelve.
- **Regional/social cost placement resolved in principle**: broad convergence (Management, Investors, State, Holding) that the task force's audited savings model should stay "clean" of unquantifiable externalities, while the State's proposed transition funding/co-investment — as a real, committed euro figure tied to specific sites and milestones — can properly enter the model as a cost offset, subject to durability discounting and milestone-conditionality (Investors' three conditions).
- **Board vote relationship to task force clarified, though not resolved to all parties' satisfaction**: Management and the Holding both state the task force informs but does not bind the vote, while committing personally/institutionally to advocate for or support a package that genuinely closes the gap. The State Government has stated three explicit tests it will apply to its own vote, including willingness to exercise its veto if closures are reintroduced despite the tests being met.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Bindingness of the task force outcome on the board vote.** The Works Council and Union maintain that an honest process should produce a vote that follows it, and frame any other outcome as functionally equivalent to the original unilateral announcement. Management, the Holding, and Investors maintain that pre-committing the vote would convert governance into rubber-stamping, and that accountability lies in advocacy and in the State's veto, not in a guaranteed outcome.
- **Whether attrition/working-time numbers currently constitute a "model" or only "inputs."** Management, backed by Investors, argues the Works Council's 15,000–18,000 retirement-eligible positions and 5–8% working-time reduction figures must be tied to specific line consolidation and capacity removal before they count toward closing the gap; the Works Council has supplied volumes but not yet this level of specification.
- **No unconditional site/job guarantee.** Management, the Holding, and Investors all continue to refuse an unconditional guarantee in advance of the audited numbers. The Works Council and Union continue to hold the irreversibility argument (a closed plant cannot reopen) as categorical and unaddressed on its own terms, though Management and the Holding have now offered a fuller counter-framing (slow structural decline as a different form of irreversibility) rather than dismissing it.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **The actual proportionality figure** from the Family Holding — the standard is agreed, but the number is not due until week twelve, leaving several parties (Union, State, Investors) reserving judgment and signaling this is the clearest remaining test of good faith.
- **Discounting methodology for the State's transition funding** — how much political/legal risk (change of government, EU state-aid challenge) gets applied to the face value of the State's proposed co-financing before it counts in the model; this affects how much weight that funding can actually bear in closing the gap.
- **Capacity-consolidation specificity** — whether attrition and working-time reductions translate into genuine unit-cost and capacity relief, or get absorbed via overtime/redistribution, leaving the underlying cost-per-unit problem intact.
- **Sequencing risk on the wage agreement** — Management's condition that the collective agreement's entry into force awaits capacity resolution is agreed in principle, but not yet specified mechanically (what happens if bargaining concludes before the capacity question, or vice versa).

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

Parties should come prepared to address, concretely:

1. **Family Holding**: any further specification possible on the proportionality figure's likely order of magnitude or timeline discipline, ahead of week twelve.
2. **Works Council/Union**: capacity-consolidation detail — which lines, which plants, what output/fixed-cost consequence — attached to the attrition and working-time figures already tabled.
3. **State Government**: milestone structure and legal durability terms for the transition-funding proposal, so it can be modeled with a discount rate rather than as a general pledge.
4. **Management**: the site-by-site breakdown itself, as committed, at week one.
5. **All parties**: whether any further specification is possible on what evidence or trigger would lead the State Government to exercise its stated veto, so this is understood in advance rather than contested after the fact.
