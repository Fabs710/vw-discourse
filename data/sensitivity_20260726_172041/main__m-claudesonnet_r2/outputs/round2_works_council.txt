[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

I'll take the moderator's refocus seriously and put specifics on the table — but let me first correct a framing that crept into almost every other statement in Round 1, because if it stands unchallenged it will shape this whole negotiation on false premises.

Management, the Family Holding, the State Government, and Investors have all converged on a version of: "closures versus alternatives should be judged as equivalent levers, tested purely against the arithmetic." I reject that framing, and I want to say precisely why, not just assert it.

A plant closure is not a line item that happens to have a larger short-run number attached. It is the irreversible destruction of a site's industrial base, the supplier ecosystem around it, and the economic viability of a town or region — for a company that, by every account given at this table, has a viable long-term future. Reversibility matters enormously to me and to the people I represent. A working-time reduction can be reversed if conditions improve. A voluntary programme can be recalibrated. A closed plant does not reopen. Treating these as interchangeable "levers" to be selected by whichever produces the larger spreadsheet number this quarter is exactly the kind of decision-making that got this company into a credibility crisis with its own workforce in the first place. So no — I will not accept "show us equivalent numbers or closures proceed" as the standard. The standard has to account for irreversibility and social cost as real, weighted variables, not externalities.

Now, to the substance, because I did hear the moderator and I don't intend to hide behind principle where I can offer specifics.

**On the costed counter-proposal:** We are prepared to put forward, on a site-by-site basis, a combination of: natural attrition and retirement-linked headcount reduction over the coming years (a substantial share of the workforce is retirement-eligible within this decade — that is a real, quantifiable lever, not a rhetorical one); voluntary severance programmes at commercially reasonable terms; working-time reduction with partial pay adjustment, structured similarly to models this industry has used before in downturns; and a bounded, time-limited contribution on variable pay and bonus elements. We can bring detailed, site-level numbers to a joint working group within weeks — not months — provided that group includes works council representation from the outset, not as a formality but as co-authors of the model. I will not hand over a wish list and wait for management to tell us it's insufficient in isolation; the costing has to be done jointly, with shared assumptions, or it will simply be disputed number-for-number and we'll lose the weeks everyone says we don't have.

**On timeline:** Investors want quarters. Management wants weeks. I will meet that pace on the condition that the process is genuinely joint from day one — this is not me stalling, it's me telling you the fastest way to lose six months is to have management model a plan alone, hand it to us, and then negotiate backward from a fait accompli. That has already happened once with the closure announcement. It will not happen twice without triggering exactly the mobilisation the Union and I have both signalled.

**On Management's position specifically:** You say you won't pre-concede closures before the numbers are tested. I understand the logic, but I want to be blunt about what "testing" actually means procedurally, because this is where good faith will be measured. If "testing the alternative" means your team produces a target, declares our package insufficient, and reintroduces closures as the default — that is not a negotiation, that is a delay before the outcome you'd already decided. If it means a joint team, our numbers and yours reconciled transparently, with disagreements adjudicated by the board rather than pre-empted by management alone, I will engage in good faith and at pace.

**On the Family Holding and Investors' point that "no closures unconditionally" cannot be ratified as a governing principle:** I hear the argument, and I'm not going to pretend it isn't a coherent position from where you sit. But I want to be equally clear about where I sit: this is not a principle I hold because I refuse to look at numbers. It's a red line because I have watched this industry treat site guarantees as disposable the moment a quarterly target is missed, and I am not going to be the works council leader who signs away the last hard guarantee this workforce has in exchange for a promise that next year's numbers might look different. If the alternative package I've outlined gets costed jointly and comes reasonably close to closing the structural gap — and I believe it can, particularly once genuine investment commitments for future product allocation are factored in on the other side of the ledger, since a plant with a confirmed future product line reduces its own cost-per-unit problem — then there is no need for this fight at all. That is the outcome I am working toward. It is not the outcome I am willing to concede in advance to prove flexibility.

**On shared sacrifice:** I want the Family Holding's "we do not rule it out" to become concrete this round, not next. My members are being asked to accept pay-element reductions in specific, quantified terms. I am not bringing them a deal where the dividend and executive compensation question remains an abstraction "to be engaged with" while our contribution is itemised to the euro. If that asymmetry persists into the next round, I will treat it as evidence that "shared sacrifice" was a phrase for this room, not a commitment.

**On Investors:** you say you're indifferent to method provided the arithmetic closes. I'd ask you to sit with the fact that the arithmetic itself is contested — a closure carries reputational, legal, and social-conflict costs that rarely make it into your models until they materialise as strikes, boycotts, and years of labour conflict that cost far more than the savings claimed on announcement day. Ask this sector's history. The "quick, clean" closure option has a way of not staying quick or clean.

To close: I am moving from position to specifics, as asked. I am not moving off the red line, because nothing said this round gave me a reason to. Joint costing, joint process, within weeks — bring the same to the table, Management, and we make real progress. Reintroduce closures as a default outcome of an alternative you tested unilaterally, and we are back to the picket line, at scale.
