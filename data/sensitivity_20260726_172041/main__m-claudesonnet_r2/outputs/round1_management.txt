[Group & Brand Management — Round 1]

I'll respond to both of you together, because you've each drawn the same line, and I want to be equally direct about where that line sits against what this company can actually survive.

First, the facts I don't dispute. Yes, labour holds ten of twenty board seats. Yes, no closure passes without a two-thirds majority. I am not pretending otherwise, and I am not pretending management can simply impose an outcome — if I could, we wouldn't be sitting here. I also don't dispute that trust is damaged. We ended guarantees that people built their lives around, and I understand why that reads as a breach. I'm not going to spend this discussion apologising for it, because the alternative — pretending the cost base is sustainable when it isn't — would be a worse breach, of my duty to this company's future and, frankly, to the jobs that do survive.

Here is where I disagree, and disagree firmly. "Closures off the table, full stop, non-negotiable" is not a negotiating position I can simply accept as the starting premise of this process. It would mean management enters every subsequent conversation having already conceded the one lever with the clearest, fastest, most durable structural effect on cost and capacity — before a single euro of alternative saving has been costed, verified, or committed to paper. I am not going to do that. Not because I am wedded to closures for their own sake — I am not — but because I will not let the shape of the outcome be dictated by who holds veto power rather than by whether the numbers add up.

So let me be precise about what would actually move me, because both of you asked for clarity and deserve it.

I am genuinely willing to explore an alternative path — capacity reduction through attrition and voluntary programmes, working-time and shift changes, a bounded labour-cost contribution — instead of closures. But it has to be a real substitute, not a softer-sounding label for the same shortfall. That means: quantified, audited savings that match what closures would deliver, on a credible timeline, not spread so thin over so many years that the structural problem is still there in 2028. "No compulsory redundancies" I can work with if attrition, early retirement and voluntary exit genuinely get us to the headcount and cost reduction we need on a timetable the market and our investors will tolerate. "No plant closures, ever, unconditionally" I cannot accept as a standing principle regardless of what the alternative numbers show — because if they don't show equivalent structural savings, we are back to square one with a cosmetic deal, and that I will not sign.

On co-determination in shaping the plan itself — I'll go further than you might expect: I'd rather negotiate the design with you now than have it dictated to me by a board vote later. That's a genuine offer, not a concession extracted under duress. But shaping the plan together means engaging honestly with the size of the gap, not starting from a position that forecloses the largest lever before we've even measured it.

On the fairness point — I hear it, and I won't pretend management compensation and capital distribution are irrelevant to whether a deal is sellable to your members. I am prepared to bring shared sacrifice on the management side into this conversation, including how it's structured and communicated. I will be honest with you: the dividend is not mine to unilaterally cancel — that runs through the family holding and the board — but I will not hide behind that as an excuse to dodge the question either. If it helps get a durable deal done, I will put it on the table with the owners.

On timing — I know the strike calendar is not a bluff, and I'm not asking you to stand down while nothing moves. What I am asking is that the alternative package be put on the table in specifics — sites, numbers, timeline — within weeks, not months, so we are negotiating substance rather than positions. I have investors and owners asking me why costs remain far above target with no resolution in sight, and every week without a credible plan erodes the room I have to protect any German site at all.

So here is my position, stated as plainly as yours: I will not walk in with closures as the only outcome I'll accept, and I will not walk out having pre-committed to no closures regardless of what the numbers say. Show me a costed, credible, structural alternative that gets us to a comparable place by a comparable date, with a genuine labour-cost contribution attached, and I will fight for it at this table and with the owners. Fail to close that gap, and I will put closures back in front of the board and let the vote happen in public, strikes and all — because a cosmetic deal that leaves this cost base uncompetitive is the one outcome I am not willing to sign, whatever it costs in the short term.
