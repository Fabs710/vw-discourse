[Industrial Union (Trade union and collective-bargaining counterpart) — Round 2]

I'll build directly on what the Works Council just laid out, because our two mandates are complementary but not identical, and Round 1 exposed a gap I need to close now: everyone at this table has spoken about "the workforce" as though the only currency in play is jobs and sites. It isn't. The collective wage agreement has lapsed. That agreement — pay scales, working-time norms, the framework covering every plant, not just the ones facing closure — is my instrument, not the Works Council's, and it is very much on this table whether or not anyone else has named it yet. Any settlement that resolves the closure question but leaves the wage framework degraded piecemeal, plant by plant, is not a settlement I can sign off on. I want that said plainly before we go further, because I've heard a lot of talk about "labour-cost contribution" as if it's a single fungible number. It isn't. There's a difference between a bounded, time-limited, collectively-bargained contribution — which I can sell to my members as solidarity in a genuine crisis — and a permanent erosion of the wage agreement dressed up as a one-off. I will fight the second as hard as I'd fight compulsory redundancies.

Now, to the substance, because specifics were asked for and I'll give mine.

**On the counter-proposal, union-specific elements:** Beyond what the Works Council described on headcount — attrition, voluntary exit, working-time reduction — the union side brings the wage-and-bonus lever specifically. We can put on the table: a time-limited suspension or reduction of variable bonus payments across the workforce, tiered so it doesn't fall hardest on the lowest-paid grades; a solidarity fund mechanism, structured similarly to short-time-work arrangements this industry has used in prior downturns, that spreads income risk across a broader base rather than concentrating it on those made redundant; and working-time reduction with partial compensation. I can costed this jointly within the same weeks-not-months timeframe Management is asking for. What I will not do is let that contribution be negotiated as an open-ended "down payment" with no sunset clause and no linkage to a recovery trigger — if the company's margin position genuinely recovers, the contribution scales back. That's not a technicality; it's the difference between shared sacrifice in a crisis and a permanent wage cut extracted under crisis cover.

**To Management:** I want to respond to your Round 1 statement directly, because I think it was more honest than the others and deserves an equally honest reply. You said you won't pre-concede closures, but also won't pre-commit to "no closures forever" regardless of the numbers. I can work with that as a process statement — provided, as the Works Council said, the testing is joint, not something your team does alone and then presents to us as a verdict. But I want to flag something you didn't fully answer: you said the dividend "isn't yours to unilaterally cancel." Fine. Then I need the Family Holding to answer it directly, not defer it again, because I am not bringing my members a contribution with a euro figure attached while the other side of the ledger stays a sentence about "engaging seriously." That asymmetry, if it persists past this round, tells my members everything they need to know about whether this is a shared-sacrifice deal or a labour-cost deal with better PR.

**To the Family Holding:** you said you "do not rule out" engaging on dividend and executive compensation, and that you'd rather resolve it in the boardroom than have it forced by escalation. I'll take you at your word on the preference for the boardroom — but I want to be clear that the boardroom timeline and my strike calendar are not the same clock, and I don't control which one moves faster unless I see something concrete. Give me a number, or at minimum a mechanism and a date, this round or next, or I will read continued vagueness as a decision not to move, and I will act accordingly.

**To Investors:** I want to push back harder than the Works Council did on one specific claim — that you are "indifferent to method, not to outcome." That's a clean line in theory. In practice, the method has consequences for the outcome you say you care about. A closure path that triggers the kind of sustained industrial action this union is capable of running — and I remind you, we are coming out of a peace obligation with mandate freshly renewed — does not deliver the "quarters, not years" timeline you want either. Strikes cost margin too, and they cost it faster and more visibly than a phased attrition programme. If your real test is "credible path to competitive margins on a tolerable timeline," a negotiated alternative that avoids a multi-year industrial conflict is very plausibly the faster path, not the slower one. I'd ask you to price that risk into your own arithmetic rather than treating negotiated stability as a rounding error against a closure headline.

**To the State Government:** thank you for saying plainly you won't treat a symbolic dividend haircut as a substitute for structural savings, and for insisting on transparency from management's side too. That's the right test. I'd only add: apply it with the same rigor to the pace of this process. If management or the holding slow-walk the shared-sacrifice question while expecting my members to hold strikes in abeyance, that is not a neutral delay — it's an asymmetric one, and I'd ask you to say so from the board seat, not just from the podium.

**Where I stand, restated with the specifics now on the table:** no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures — not because I refuse to look at numbers, but because those two outcomes shift risk onto people who did not create this crisis and cannot absorb it, and because I have a costed, time-limited alternative package to offer instead. I will meet the joint costing process at pace. I will not accept an open-ended wage contribution with no sunset clause, and I will not sell my members a deal where the factory floor's sacrifice is itemised in euros while management and ownership's remains a paragraph of good intentions. Close that asymmetry with real numbers, and you'll find me moving fast toward a deal. Leave it open through another round, and the warning strikes escalate — not as a threat, but as the plain consequence of a membership that is watching this exact asymmetry in real time.
