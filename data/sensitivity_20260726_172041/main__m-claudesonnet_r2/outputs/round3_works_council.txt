[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 3]

Let me go straight to specification, as the moderator asked, but I need to deal with two direct challenges to my position first — from Investors and from the Family Holding — because if I let them stand unanswered they will define the terms for everything that follows.

**To Investors, on excluding regional/social cost from the model:** You've drawn a "hard line" that supplier collapse, regional contraction, and conflict cost are "not costs to shareholders" and belong only in the State's own political vote, not in the joint model. I understand the analytical appeal of a clean, symmetrical spreadsheet. But your own argument last round contradicts you: you told the Union that strike risk should be priced into the company's own arithmetic because "strikes cost margin too." You cannot have it both ways. If industrial-conflict cost is a real cost to the company when it results from *closures*, it is equally real when it results from supplier-network collapse, loss of skilled workforce, and the erosion of the social license this company needs to operate in Germany at all. These aren't externalities in the economist's sense — they are costs that land on this company's own balance sheet within a small number of years, just not on this quarter's. Keeping them out of the "clean model" doesn't make the model more rigorous. It makes it selectively rigorous, rigorous about the costs that favor closure and agnostic about the costs that don't. I will not accept a task force mandate that treats supplier disruption and regional workforce loss as unquantifiable when convenient and quantifiable the moment it's a strike-cost argument against us. Either the model is comprehensive, or it isn't a fair comparison — it's a pre-loaded one, dressed as neutral.

**To the Family Holding, on the board vote versus the task force:** You are legally correct that the task force informs and does not substitute for the two-thirds vote. I don't dispute the law. But I want to be precise about what that legal fact means in practice, because I think you're using it to reserve an escape hatch, and I need to close it now rather than discover it in week twelve. Co-determination is not a formality that management and ownership tolerate until the real decision happens elsewhere — it is the real decision-making mechanism, by design, precisely because this workforce cannot be outvoted by capital alone on questions of closure. If the joint task force does its work honestly, with real joint authorship, and produces a package that closes the structural gap, and the board then treats that as merely "informative" rather than binding on the vote — that is the fait accompli in reverse, and it will produce the same mobilization as the original unilateral announcement did. So: I support the task force fully, on the explicit understanding that a package it certifies as closing the gap will receive the votes of both employee-side board members and, I would hope, of any party at this table that has argued in good faith for a negotiated outcome. If the process is honest, the vote should follow it. If the process is honest and the vote doesn't follow it anyway, everyone at this table will have learned that joint costing was theater, and I will act exactly as I would to a unilateral announcement — because functionally it will have been one.

**Now, the specifics I promised.**

Site-level volumes: across the affected plants, we can identify roughly 15,000–18,000 positions that reach retirement or early-retirement eligibility by 2030 — a real, demographic, non-speculative number, not a projection dependent on goodwill. Layered onto that: a voluntary severance programme calibrated to draw a further several thousand exits at commercially reasonable terms, front-loaded in the first eighteen months to deliver savings faster rather than back-loaded to the horizon. On working time: a shift-pattern and weekly-hours reduction in the range of 5–8% at the plants carrying the heaviest overcapacity, with partial pay compensation structured to protect lower pay grades disproportionately — I will not accept a flat-percentage cut that hits the shop floor as hard as it hits better-compensated grades. Combined with the Union's variable-pay and bonus measures, I believe this reaches a substantial share of the structural gap Management quantified this round — the high-hundreds-of-millions-to-low-billions figure — within the twelve-week window, without a single closure. I want that modeled jointly and audited, not asserted by me and disputed by you in isolation, which is exactly why I support the task force mechanism.

**On the sunset/recovery trigger, since Investors flagged the mechanics gap correctly:** I propose the trigger be tied to the brand's return-on-sales target being met and sustained for two consecutive full fiscal years, averaged, not a single good quarter — precisely to prevent the "one strong quarter unwinds everything" risk Investors named. And yes, it must apply symmetrically: if the workforce contribution scales back on recovery, so does any dividend restraint or executive-pay reduction. Symmetry isn't a courtesy I'm offering — it's the only version of this that survives contact with my membership.

**On proportionality, since the Holding still hasn't given a standard:** I will not accept "proportionate" being defined unilaterally by the party making the least itemized commitment so far. A workable standard: the shareholder/executive contribution, measured as a share of the relevant pool (dividend distribution and total executive compensation), should be at least comparable in proportional terms to the workforce contribution measured as a share of total labour cost. Not identical in euros — nobody's asking for that — but comparable in proportional sacrifice. If the Holding's eventual figure is a rounding error against that standard, I will say so publicly, and the Union will say so louder.

**Where I have not moved, and will not:** no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies. Management's argument that irreversibility "cuts both ways" because an unresolved structural gap is also irreversible is a real point, and I've engaged with it rather than dismissed it — that's precisely why I've now put real volumes and percentages on the table instead of only a principle. But an unresolved cost gap can be revisited, renegotiated, and closed by exactly the mechanism we're now building. A closed plant cannot be reopened by any mechanism at all. That asymmetry is not rhetorical, and no argument this round has actually addressed it on its own terms — it's been reframed, not answered.

Confirm the State's embedded seat in the task force, Management — that's still outstanding from last round and it matters to me too, because a task force missing one of the parties with a board veto is not the joint process anyone agreed to. Bring the Holding's proportionality standard and a real trigger mechanic into week six. Do that, and this resolves without a single warning strike beyond what's already been called. Fail to, and you'll find out exactly how much mobilization capacity this workforce still has.
