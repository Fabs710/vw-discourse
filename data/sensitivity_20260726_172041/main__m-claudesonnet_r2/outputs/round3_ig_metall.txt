**To Investors, adding to what the Works Council just said:** I want to press this harder, because your position this round is internally inconsistent in a way that matters to my mandate specifically. You told me directly that strike risk should be "priced into your own arithmetic" — and I welcome that you accepted the logic. But then in the same round you tell the State Government that regional and social costs of closure don't belong in the task force's model because they're "not costs to shareholders." Which is it? Strikes are precisely a mechanism by which regional and social cost becomes a shareholder cost — through lost production, reputational damage, and exactly the sustained conflict I have the mandate and calendar to run. You cannot accept the strike-cost argument when it's convenient for urging me toward a fast negotiated settlement, and then reject its logical twin — that supplier collapse and regional contraction eventually hit the P&L too — when it's inconvenient for the closure comparison. Either the model accounts for how social cost converts into financial cost over a multi-year horizon, in both directions, or it doesn't, and you don't get to choose which direction it's rigorous in. I will not accept a "clean model" that is clean only where it favors the closure conclusion.

**On the collective wage agreement, since this is squarely mine to hold the line on:** Management, I heard your commitment that any bonus/variable-pay contribution runs through proper collective bargaining rather than being imposed plant by plant, and I want to acknowledge that plainly — it addresses a red line I named in Round 1, and I won't pretend otherwise. But I need to be precise about what "runs through proper collective bargaining" means procedurally, because the task force and the collective bargaining process are not the same instrument, and I will not let one substitute for the other. The task force can cost the package. It cannot ratify a wage agreement. That happens at the bargaining table, between this union and the employer association or company directly, with a real agreement text, signatures, and a defined term — including the sunset trigger the Works Council and I have both proposed. I want that bargaining track to open in parallel with the task force, not sequenced after it, so that by week twelve we have both a costed package and a draft collective agreement ready to sign, not a costed package followed by another three months of separate wage negotiations. If the task force finishes its work and I'm then told the actual wage agreement negotiation starts from scratch, that is not the pace anyone promised, and my members will read it as exactly the kind of drift I warned about in Round 1.

**On my specific numbers, since I owe the same specificity I asked of others:** on variable pay, I can bring a proposal for a two-year suspension of the annual bonus payment above a protected floor — meaning the lowest pay grades retain their bonus in full or near-full, with the suspension scaling up progressively for higher grades — rather than a flat percentage cut that would, in cash terms, hurt the shop floor far more than it hurts better-compensated staff. On the solidarity fund: a mechanism funded jointly by a modest company contribution and a redirected share of the suspended bonus pool, used to top up income for workers on reduced hours under the working-time measures the Works Council described, so the working-time reduction doesn't simply become an unstated pay cut for the people least able to absorb it. I can bring modeled euro ranges for both to the task force in week one, not week six — there's no reason to wait, since these numbers depend on our own wage data, not on management's cost model.

**On the sunset/recovery trigger — I endorse the Works Council's proposal exactly as stated: two consecutive full fiscal years at the ROS target, averaged, applied symmetrically to both the labour contribution and any dividend/executive-pay restraint.** I want to add one refinement Investors raised legitimately: the averaging period should be specified now, in the task force mandate, not left to be argued over later when one side has an incentive to claim recovery early and the other has an incentive to claim it hasn't really happened. Two full fiscal years, audited, same auditor as the task force uses. That closes the gap Investors flagged, and it should satisfy their concern about a mechanism that "quietly unwinds" — it can't unwind quietly if the trigger requires two years of audited, sustained performance.

**To the Family Holding, directly, because "mechanism and date, not a figure" cannot be where this stays through another round:** I accept your argument that manufacturing a number under discussion pressure is worse than a disciplined process producing a durable one — that's a fair point, and I won't pretend it isn't. But the Works Council has now put a proportionality standard on the table: comparable proportional sacrifice, dividend distribution and executive compensation measured against their own pool, workforce contribution measured against total labour cost. I need you to commit to that standard — not the figure yet, the standard — this round. If you can commit to the mechanism and the date but not to the standard by which "proportionate" will be judged, then I have no way of knowing, going into six weeks of joint costing, whether the shareholder side of this ledger will look like shared sacrifice or like a rounding error dressed up in a sunset clause. Commit to the proportionality standard now. Bring the number in six weeks. That is a reasonable sequencing; silence on the standard itself is not.

**On Management's disclosed gap figure — high hundreds of millions to low billions — I want a site-by-site breakdown, not an aggregate, in week one of the task force.** An aggregate number is easy to defend and hard to contest. A site-by-site breakdown is where the real negotiation happens, because it will show whether the gap is concentrated in one or two plants with genuinely uncompetitive structures or spread thinly across the network in a way the alternative package can close more easily than the aggregate suggests. I will not accept the aggregate figure as the basis for judging whether our package "closes the gap" — that's not a rigorous test, it's a headline.

**On the board-vote question the Works Council raised with the Holding — I align with them completely and want to add the industrial-relations consequence explicitly, since that's my instrument, not theirs.** If this task force does honest, joint work, produces a package that closes the structural gap, and the board then treats it as merely "informative" rather than decisive — I will not read that as a legal technicality. I will read it as confirmation that co-determination and collective bargaining were procedural theater while the real decision was always going to be made by capital alone. At that point I am not escalating a labour dispute over wages; I am escalating a dispute over whether this company's own governance structure means anything, and that is a fight I will run at a scale well beyond warning strikes.

**Where I stand, restated with this round's specifics:** no compulsory redundancies, no plant closures, no piecemeal erosion of the wage framework. I have brought costed bonus and solidarity-fund numbers, a precise sunset trigger, and a demand that the wage-bargaining track open in parallel with the task force rather than after it. I am not moving off the red lines — nothing this round has offered a genuinely fair, shared-sacrifice package with numbers on both sides of the ledger yet, only a mechanism and a date from the Holding and an aggregate figure from Management. Bring the proportionality standard and the site-by-site breakdown to week one, and I will hold the current warning-strike posture rather than escalate it while the task force works. Fail to, and my members will not read "twelve weeks" as patience — they will read it as delay, and I will act on their mandate, not on this room's preference for a quiet process.
