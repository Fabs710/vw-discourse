# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

**1. Areas of Emerging Convergence**

- All parties accept that some form of structural cost/capacity adjustment is necessary; no participant argues for the status quo.
- Broad conditional openness to an alternative-to-closure package built on: attrition, early retirement, voluntary exit programmes, working-time/shift changes, and a bounded labour-cost contribution.
- Broad (though not universal) agreement that "shared sacrifice" — including management compensation and shareholder distributions — should be examined transparently alongside any workforce concessions, though the *degree and form* of that sacrifice remains undefined.
- Convergence around a shared test, articulated in different words by Management, the Family Holding, the State Government, and Institutional Investors: any alternative to closures must be **costed, credible, and structurally equivalent** to what closures would deliver, on a timeline capital markets and owners will tolerate.
- Works Council and Union have offered a conditional, costed-in-principle counter-proposal rather than blanket refusal; Management has offered to co-design the restructuring plan rather than impose it.

**2. Core Disagreements Still Unresolved**

- **Whether "no plant closures" can be an unconditional, non-negotiable precondition** versus **an outcome contingent on whether alternative savings genuinely close the gap.** Works Council and Union hold the former; Management, Family Holding, State Government, and Investors all hold the latter.
- **Timeline**: Management and Investors are pressing for a costed alternative within weeks; Union has signalled strike escalation risk tied to any perception of drift or foregone-conclusion framing.
- **Nature and enforceability of "shared sacrifice"**: Management has flagged that dividend decisions run through the family holding, not management itself; the Family Holding has not committed beyond willingness to "engage" if it unlocks a deal. No concrete figures or mechanisms yet proposed by capital/management side.
- **Governance process going forward**: Works Council, Union, and State Government all want to be inside the design process from now on, not consulted after a plan is finalized; Management has verbally agreed but this has not been operationalized.

**3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed**

- No side has yet put numbers on the table: neither a quantified closure-equivalent savings target from Management, nor a costed counter-proposal from Works Council/Union, nor a specific dividend/executive-pay contribution from the Family Holding.
- The relationship between **guarantee duration (to 2030)** and **savings durability** is unresolved — Investors explicitly warn that a guarantee is "only as good as the cost reduction underneath it."
- No mechanism yet proposed for how competing timelines (Investors: quarters; Union/Works Council: durable multi-year security; Management: weeks-to-produce-numbers) get reconciled into a single negotiating calendar.

Participants should move from position statements to specifics:

- **Works Council / Union**: what is the costed content of the counter-proposal — specific attrition/voluntary volumes, working-time changes, value of the labour-cost contribution — and over what timeline?

# Synthesis Document: Restructuring Confrontation at the German Carmaker

## 1. Decision Context

The company faces a structural cost and overcapacity crisis requiring durable savings, with plant closures on the table for the first time in its history. Germany's co-determination architecture — a 20-seat supervisory board requiring a two-thirds majority (14/20 votes) for closure, with the workforce bench holding 10 seats — combined with a controlling family holding, a state government with blocking-minority veto rights, and dispersed capital-market investors, means no single party can impose an outcome. Over four rounds, the parties moved from adversarial position-taking toward a jointly governed task-force process with a twelve-week timeline, while the central substantive dispute — what happens if the costed alternative package falls short of fully closing the structural gap — has surfaced as the decisive, still-unresolved fault line.

## 2. Stakeholder Assembly

- **Group & Brand Management** — drives the restructuring case, controls information (site-by-site figures, baseline gap composition), advocates to the board but does not control the vote.
- **Works Council** — plant-level employee representation; holds 10 of 20 board seats, giving a structural blocking position over any closure vote (14 needed).
- **Industrial Union** — negotiates collective bargaining; holds the strike weapon, now legally available since the peace obligation lapsed.
- **Regional State Government** — anchor shareholder with statutory blocking-minority veto and political accountability.
- **Controlling Family Holding** — majority voting shareholder acting through governance; effectively co-decisive with the workforce bench and the State on any board vote.
- **Institutional Investors** — dispersed, largely non-voting capital; no formal veto but significant market-signaling power and credibility pressure on all other parties.

## 3. Final Positions

**Management:** Accepts joint task-force governance and has committed to subjecting its own baseline gap figure to the same structural-versus-transitional scrutiny it demands of others. Will advocate for any package that closes the gap, but explicitly rejects an outcome-blind commitment to foreclose closures regardless of residual shortfall size.

**Works Council:** Has delivered concrete, plant-specific attrition and shift-consolidation detail as promised, converting inputs into a testable model. Maintains an unconditional floor — no closures, no compulsory redundancies — grounded in the irreversibility of closure versus the revisibility of a shortfall, and asserts this is already secured by its 10 board seats rather than something it needs to be granted.

**Industrial Union:** Has specified a costed bonus-suspension range and a solidarity fund tied mechanically to the Works Council's consolidation plan, plus a clean auditor-certification trigger for the parallel wage agreement. Holds the current warning-strike posture but has not yet stated whether it accepts the State/Holding's magnitude-contingent shortfall standard or remains aligned with the Works Council's absolute exclusion — the single most consequential unresolved position in the process.

**Regional State Government:** Offers the most developed integrative formulation — closure only as a last resort after every genuine lever (including its own transition funding) is exhausted, contingent on the size of the residual gap, not excluded by principle. Will not indefinitely block resolution if a genuinely large structural gap remains after honest effort, but will not accept closure as a first response to any shortfall.

**Family Holding:** Has accepted the Works Council's proportionality standard and signaled directionally that its contribution will land "at or above the standard," a testable but still unquantified commitment. Explicitly refuses to pre-commit its votes to either an outcome-blind veto against closure or an automatic ratification of the task force's findings.

**Institutional Investors:** Support the State/Holding shortfall standard as the only one compatible with continued capital-market confidence, treating an unconditional workforce veto as symmetrically undisciplined as an unconditional shareholder push for closure. Push hardest for procedural rigor (auditor-defined thresholds, published discount methodology, equal scrutiny of Management's own figures) as the mechanism that keeps the process credible.

## 4. Genuine Conflicts (Structurally Incompatible Positions)

- **Outcome-blind guarantee vs. magnitude-contingent test.** The Works Council's position — any shortfall, regardless of size, returns the parties to the model with closure permanently excluded — is structurally incompatible with the standard now shared by Management, the Holding, the State, and Investors, which makes closure a last-resort option contingent on the size of the unclosed gap. This is not a rhetorical gap; it is a difference in what triggers board-vote behavior at week twelve, and it cannot be split by more process alone.
- **Bindingness of the task force on the board vote.** Works Council/Union treat an honest process as one that should produce a vote following it; Management, Holding, and Investors treat pre-committing the vote as converting governance into rubber-stamping. Both cannot be simultaneously true as stated — one side's "convergence" framing (Management's) and the other's "veto held in reserve" framing (Works Council's) describe the same conditional structure but assign radically different weight to what happens outside the "package closes the gap" scenario.
- **Locus of discount/risk judgment.** Investors' insistence on a pre-defined, auditor-set residual-gap threshold — decided before the number is known — is in tension with the State's and Holding's preference to judge "genuinely exhausted" contextually and politically at the time. This is a conflict over whether the decision rule is mechanical (Investors) or judgment-based (State/Holding), not merely a timing question.

## 5. Areas of Convergence

- Universal acceptance that some structural adjustment is necessary; no party defends the status quo.
- Full agreement on task-force governance, the twelve-week timeline, and auditor-based certification for both the sunset/recovery trigger and the wage-agreement entry-into-force condition.
- Symmetry principle accepted by nearly all parties: labour, shareholder, and state contributions should be measured, audited, and disciplined by the same standards (proportionality, discount methodology, structural-versus-transitional scrutiny).
- Broad — though not complete — convergence on a magnitude-contingent shortfall standard (State/Holding/Investors/Management), with the Union's position still pending and the Works Council dissenting on principle.
- Agreement that the Family Holding's directional signal, while not a figure, constitutes genuine (if incomplete) movement, now treated as a testable commitment rather than rhetoric.

## 6. Key Trade-offs for the Decision-Maker

- **Speed of certainty vs. quality of the package.** Compressing to twelve weeks satisfies Investors' and Management's urgency but risks under-specifying exactly the mechanisms (discount methodology, residual-gap threshold) whose absence is most likely to produce disputed outcomes at the margin.
- **Precision of the shortfall rule vs. negotiating flexibility.** Defining a residual-gap threshold now (Investors' proposal) makes the test auditable and harder to contest post hoc, but forecloses the contextual judgment the State and Holding say they need to apply "stewardship" reasoning to an unknown future number.
- **Workforce solidarity vs. differentiated leverage.** The Works Council's board-seat veto and the Union's strike weapon are distinct instruments with different escalation profiles; the Union's undeclared position on the magnitude-contingent standard means the decision-maker cannot yet assume the workforce bench will act as a single bloc if a shortfall scenario materializes.
- **Symbolic vs. material shareholder sacrifice.** The Holding's qualitative commitment ("at or above the standard") preserves flexibility for the Holding but leaves every other party's willingness to hold the line through week twelve partly hostage to a number not yet disclosed.

## 7. Integrative Pathways

- **Tiered shortfall protocol:** Adopt the State/Holding standard but operationalize it via Investors' proposed auditor-defined threshold, set now, distinguishing "small/closeable via further negotiation" (extended sunset windows, deeper working-time measures, added state co-investment) from "large/structural" gaps — with the threshold itself co-designed by all parties rather than imposed, addressing the Works Council's concern about unilateral discount-setting.
- **Sequenced escalation, not binary veto:** Reframe the Works Council's position as a described conditional vote (as Management requests) while preserving its substantive content — a strong presumption against closure that must be rebutted by an auditor-certified, exhaustively-levered residual gap — rather than an absolute precondition, which could reconcile the "convergence vs. reserved veto" framing dispute without requiring either side to abandon its substance.
- **Front-loaded transparency exchange:** Deliver Management's transitional-versus-structural gap composition, the Holding's numeric figure, and the Union's full costed range on the same disclosure timeline (week one to week twelve) rather than staggered, so no single party's continued ambiguity becomes a bottleneck for the others' good faith.
- **Union alignment clause:** Resolve the Union's undeclared position on the magnitude-contingent standard early, since it determines whether the workforce bench acts as a unified 10-vote bloc or splits — this is likely the single highest-leverage clarification available before week one.

## 8. Open Questions

- Will the Union adopt the State/Holding magnitude-contingent standard, remain aligned with the Works Council's absolute position, or stake out an intermediate position tied to the strike calendar rather than the board vote?
- What quantitative threshold will the auditor (or joint process) set for "closeable" versus "structurally unclosable" residual gaps, and who has final say if parties dispute the auditor's proposed threshold?
- What numeric figure will the Family Holding ultimately bring at week twelve, and will it in fact meet its own stated directional expectation ("at or above the standard")?
- How much of Management's baseline gap figure is transitional (EV-integration, one-off) versus structural, and how materially will this recalibrate the savings target every other contribution is being sized against?
- If the workforce bench and the Holding/State reach different conclusions about whether "every genuine lever" has been exhausted, what adjudicates that disagreement short of an actual contested board vote?

## 9. Recommended Next Step

Convene a dedicated pre-week-one session, before substantive task-force costing begins, whose sole purpose is to (a) obtain the Union's explicit position on the magnitude-contingent shortfall standard, and (b) jointly define — with the auditor facilitating rather than deciding unilaterally — the residual-gap threshold distinguishing "closeable" from "structural" shortfalls. Sequencing this before the costing work begins is the highest-leverage move: it either produces a shared decision rule that all parties can measure the eventual numbers against, or it surfaces the Works Council/Union split (or unity) on the central conflict identified in Section 4 while there is still time to negotiate around it, rather than discovering it in week twelve under maximum pressure.

## 10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Interaction

- **The central conflict was not visible until Round 4.** A single-prompt elicitation would likely have captured "closures vs. no closures" as the headline conflict; four rounds of iterative pressure-testing revealed that the actual fault line is narrower and more tractable — not whether closures happen, but what decision rule governs an *incomplete* alternative package. This reframing (from binary to conditional/magnitude-based) only emerged because Management persistently asked the same pointed question across two rounds until the other parties were forced to answer it concretely.
- **Power asymmetries were reframed through argument, not just asserted.** The Works Council's move from "we want a guarantee" to "we already hold a veto, exercised through patience" — and Management's subsequent counter-reframing of that same fact as "conditional commitment, not unconditional power" — shows how the same governance arithmetic can be narratively weaponized or neutralized depending on framing. A static description of formal voting rights would have missed this dynamic entirely; it only surfaced through direct rebuttal across turns.
- **Symmetry-of-scrutiny became a self-reinforcing norm.** No single party proposed at the outset that all sides' numbers (labour, shareholder, state, and management's own baseline) be held to equivalent audit discipline. This norm emerged incrementally — each party invoking it against another — until Management pre-emptively applied it to itself in Round 4, a commitment that likely would not have been extracted by a one-shot request for "management's cost justification."
- **Silence became informationally costly in a way only visible over time.** The Family Holding's lack of a directional signal was not treated as neutral; three separate parties (Works Council, Union, State) independently escalated their reading of that silence across consecutive rounds, eventually forcing a partial disclosure. This demonstrates how repeated interaction converts the absence of a position into a de facto negative signal — an effect invisible in any single-round design.
- **Process convergence and substantive divergence decoupled.** By Round 4, procedural architecture (task force, timeline, auditor, triggers) was almost fully agreed, creating a surface impression of consensus that could mask the fact that the substantive shortfall question remained as unresolved as in Round 1. Only sustained multi-round tracking exposed that agreement on *process* was outrunning agreement on the *decision rule* the process is meant to serve — a gap a single-touchpoint negotiation summary would likely have obscured entirely.
