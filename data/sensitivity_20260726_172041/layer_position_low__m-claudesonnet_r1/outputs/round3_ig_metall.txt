I'll build on the Works Council's position and press on the items that remain unresolved from the union's specific vantage point — the tariff framework, the enforcement architecture, and capital's number.

**On the gap number — I fully align with the Works Council, State Government, and Investors, and I'll sharpen the point.** Three rounds in, management has been asked directly, by four separate parties, for a single figure, and three rounds in, we have "well above benchmark." I want to be blunt about what that non-answer does to the credibility of everything else management has offered. The joint-review mechanism, the executive pay proportionality, the openness to alternatives — all of it is being offered against an unstated target. That means management retains sole discretion to declare, at any point, that whatever we bring "doesn't close the gap," without ever having to defend the gap itself against scrutiny. That is not a negotiation, that is a moving goalpost with a compliant referee. I will not scale the union's package again until that number is on the table, audited or at least specific enough to be modeled by the works council's and union's own economists. If management cannot produce it, that tells the room something important about how solid the closure option was ever supposed to be in the first place.

**On the Family Holding's refusal to foreclose closures — I want to address this squarely, because I think the Family Holding is trying to hold two positions that don't actually fit together.** You say you accept the board arithmetic — that closures don't have the votes. Fine. Then tell me what "stewardship flexibility to revisit in five or ten years" is actually protecting, if not the option to force this fight again down the line once the workforce has already banked a cost reduction. You can't simultaneously tell me the mathematical path to closure doesn't exist *and* insist on preserving the option as if it might. Either the co-determination structure is real and binding, in which case there's nothing to preserve by refusing site guarantees, or you believe that structure is more contestable than you're letting on — which is exactly why my members won't sign a wage sacrifice without the guarantee locked down now. I'd ask you to resolve that contradiction rather than let it sit as constructive ambiguity, because ambiguity here reads as "we're keeping our options open regardless of what labour delivers," and that is precisely the asymmetry every party at this table, including your own allies in the investor community, has said they can't defend.

**On the standing joint oversight body and binding determination — I support the Works Council's formulation exactly as stated, and I want to add the union's institutional weight behind it.** A body with binding, self-executing determination on the narrow factual question — were the milestones met — with the board retaining strategic authority elsewhere, is a fair, bounded structure. It is not a takeover of governance; it is a dispute-resolution mechanism scoped to the one question where trust has actually broken down. I'd note for the Family Holding: your proposed alternative — findings "binding on how the board applies the review" — reintroduces exactly the discretion problem you're asking us to trust you past. If you want us to believe the review is genuinely joint, the joint body's factual determination has to be the determination, not an input the shareholder-side votes can outweigh. I'll go further than the Works Council on one design point: I want the composition of that body's tie-break structure specified now, in this negotiation, not left to be worked out after we've already signed a contribution package. Sequence matters here too — we are being asked to trust a mechanism whose actual voting weight hasn't been described.

**On capital's number — I'll restate my round-two challenge because it hasn't been answered with the precision I asked for.** Family Holding, you've given me a principle — "proportionate to labour's actual magnitude." I want to know: proportionate measured how? As a share of total compensation? As a share of the euro savings the company books from the labour package? Those are very different numbers, and "proportionate" can mean almost anything depending on the denominator you choose. I'm not asking you to invent a figure ahead of the union's final number — I accept that sequencing logic, same as I extended it to management. I am asking you to commit now to the *method* of calculation, so that when the final figures land side by side, nobody discovers "proportionate" meant something much smaller once you did the arithmetic. That is a reasonable, immediate ask, and it costs you nothing to answer now unless the intention is to leave room to under-deliver later.

**On Investors' point about the joint body creating execution risk and "shared paralysis."** I understand the concern, and I don't dismiss it — a body that deadlocks helps no one, including my members, who need this company financially healthy to keep the jobs we're fighting to protect. But I'd push back on the framing that speed and labour's genuine co-determination role are in tension. A body with fixed, short review intervals and a binding factual determination is *faster* than the ambiguity we have right now, where every party is guessing whether management's "consequences" language means anything enforceable. Clarity and enforceability are not slower than discretion — discretion is what produces the multi-year drift and missed targets that got this brand here in the first place. If you want fast correction, back a mechanism with teeth now rather than a vaguer one that gets litigated informally every quarter.

**To be fully clear about where I stand, so there's no ambiguity carried into round four:** I am prepared to bring my members a real, substantial cost and capacity contribution — the shape I laid out in round two remains genuine and on the table. But three conditions are not negotiable for me, and I want to say explicitly why each one is a line rather than a preference. First, the competitiveness gap must be quantified and shared — without it, I have no way to know whether I am negotiating in good faith or being used to legitimize a predetermined outcome. Second, site guarantees must be binding and delivery-conditional, with closures actually removed as a contingent fallback — not "no current intention," an actual foreclosure, because anything short of that makes every concession my members make a down payment on an outcome we're still trying to prevent. Third, the joint body's determination on milestone delivery must bind, not advise — because a review process that ends in board discretion is the same structure we're trying to replace, dressed in new language.

Meet those three, with a capital-side number calculated on a clear, comparable method disclosed alongside ours — and this moves to a real deal, fast. Leave any of them unresolved, and I will not recommend my members accept a framework that asks for their wages now against a guarantee that isn't one.
