# Synthesis Document: Restructuring Negotiation at the German Carmaker

## 1. Decision Context

A German carmaker facing structural overcapacity (management-quantified at "in the order of 20%" unit-cost gap, requiring "several billion euros annually") is negotiating a path between plant closures and an alternative package of capacity, working-time, and labour-cost measures. The decision is constrained by a governance architecture in which no closure can proceed without a two-thirds supervisory-board majority — a threshold labour's seats alone can block — while a controlling family holding and a state government with a statutory blocking minority also hold effective vetoes. Over four rounds, the parties moved from stated principles toward a provisional architecture (guarantees, oversight body, graduated milestones, symmetric burden-sharing) but have not yet closed the arithmetic gap between proposed savings and the stated cost target.

## 2. Stakeholder Assembly

- **Group & Brand Management** — executive authority and technical/financial legitimacy; no independent board-blocking power, must build a two-thirds coalition.
- **Works Council** — plant-level representation; holds half of supervisory-board seats and therefore a structural veto over closures — the highest-leverage labour actor.
- **Industrial Union** — collective-bargaining authority and strike power (peace obligation lapsed); legitimacy derives from mobilization capacity and alignment with the Works Council.
- **Regional State Government** — statutory blocking-minority shareholder with board seats; political legitimacy tied to regional employment.
- **Controlling Family Holding** — majority voting control exercised through governance; ultimate steward of enterprise continuity and capital allocation.
- **Institutional Investors** — dispersed, largely non-voting capital; lower formal blocking power but functions as an external market-discipline check on the credibility and sufficiency of any deal.

## 3. Final Positions

**Management:** Offers an unconditional no-closure commitment through 2030 and accepts a no-compulsory-redundancy floor (conditional on voluntary/structural tools delivering headcount targets), but explicitly retains product/platform allocation as its real accountability lever for missed milestones. Commits to deliver a plant-level cost breakdown and an indicative (quarter-to-third) non-labour savings range before further labour scaling is requested.

**Works Council:** Demands unconditional, plain closure removal through 2030 (arguing conditionality is rhetorical given board arithmetic) and holds an absolute, non-negotiable floor against compulsory redundancy at any stage. Will not scale its labour package further until the plant-level breakdown separates structural from utilization-driven cost, and questions whether dividend recovery will be symmetric with wage recovery.

**Union:** Fully aligned with the Works Council on both the board-arithmetic argument and the redundancy floor, adding that any breach of the redundancy line would trigger mobilization rather than negotiation. Withholds further package scaling pending the cost breakdown, non-labour lever sizing, and a symmetric wage-recovery mechanism matching whatever the Family Holding offers on dividends.

**State Government:** Credits management's unconditional closure move but flags that product-allocation authority could function as a disguised substitute for closure, demanding joint-body transparency (not veto) over such decisions. Treats the plant-level breakdown, firm non-labour numbers, and the Family Holding's dividend-symmetry answer as preconditions before committing board weight.

**Family Holding:** Offers dividend-recovery symmetry with wage recovery, but only for the term of the agreement through 2030, refusing a permanent link between labour outcomes and long-run capital-allocation policy. Supports joint-body transparency over product allocation as a check against a "hollow" closure guarantee, while defending management's ordinary capital-allocation authority.

**Investors:** Endorse the governance architecture built so far (closure guarantee, redundancy floor, graduated mechanism, time-bounded dividend symmetry) as necessary but explicitly withhold an "investable" verdict until three firm numbers — plant breakdown, non-labour lever size, and total gap closure — are delivered. Oppose any construction giving the joint oversight body de facto influence over capital allocation, distinguishing transparency from co-management.

## 4. Genuine Conflicts (Structurally Incompatible Positions)

- **Product-allocation lever:** Management and Family Holding treat retained control over product/platform allocation as non-negotiable ordinary board authority; Works Council and Union (implicitly) regard any undisclosed equivalent-decline mechanism as violating the spirit of the closure guarantee. These positions cannot both be fully honored — either allocation stays wholly at management's discretion or it becomes subject to binding joint oversight, and the two views of what "transparency" permits (reporting vs. de facto co-decision) are incompatible as currently stated.
- **Dividend symmetry scope:** Family Holding and Investors insist symmetry is bounded to the agreement's term, reverting afterward to ordinary board judgment; Works Council and Union have not accepted this limitation and may read it as a structurally guaranteed asymmetry (capital rebounds on recovery, labour's concession risks becoming a new permanent baseline). This is not yet reconciled and may be irreconcilable without a redefinition of "recovery."
- **Sufficiency of the arithmetic:** Investors demand proof the combined package closes the ~20% gap before treating the deal as real; Works Council and Union will not scale their contribution until the plant-level breakdown proves the gap is not partly a management-caused utilization problem. Each side's condition for movement depends on data the other side does not yet trust — a potential deadlock if the breakdown does not clearly resolve attribution.
- **Nature of the closure "win":** Works Council/Union treat the unconditional guarantee as closing the leverage question entirely; State Government and Investors both flag that it may be substantially hollowed out by the undefined product-allocation lever, meaning the parties disagree on whether the central round-four concession is actually resolved or merely relocated.

## 5. Areas of Convergence

- Unconditional no-closure commitment through 2030 (management-offered, welcomed by all).
- No compulsory redundancies at any stage, with an agreed non-compulsory fallback sequence (extended timeline, deeper working-time measures, redeployment).
- A two-cycle, 12–18-month graduated milestone-review structure with binding (not advisory) factual determination by a joint oversight body.
- Executive pay and dividend reductions proportional to labour's contribution, with symmetry of trigger-linkage during the agreement's term.
- Sequencing principle that non-labour savings (procurement, platform consolidation) should be sized and locked before further labour-side scaling is requested.
- Shared acknowledgment that asymmetric sacrifice (quantified labour, vague capital) is untenable, and that the plant-level cost breakdown is now a blocking prerequisite for all further numerical movement.

## 6. Key Trade-offs for the Decision-Maker

- **Precision vs. urgency:** Delaying resolution until the full plant-level breakdown and firm non-labour figure arrive protects credibility with Investors and labour but extends the period of unresolved industrial-action risk (peace obligation already lapsed).
- **Governance transparency vs. capital-allocation agility:** Extending joint-body visibility to product/platform allocation satisfies State Government and partially reassures labour, but risks the governance drag Investors warn could blunt the very competitiveness gains the restructuring seeks.
- **Symmetric burden-sharing duration vs. long-term capital flexibility:** A dividend-wage symmetry bounded to 2030 satisfies Family Holding's stewardship concerns but leaves an unresolved fairness question that could become the point on which Works Council/Union decline to scale their package.
- **Retained leverage vs. guarantee credibility:** Management's retention of product allocation as an accountability mechanism preserves real influence over underperforming sites, but its lack of definition currently threatens to undercut the perceived value of the headline closure guarantee.
- **Structural veto power vs. negotiated leverage:** The Works Council's board-arithmetic argument shows that some "concessions" (conditionality on closure) cost management nothing real, which reframes what counts as genuine trade versus cosmetic movement — decision-makers must distinguish substantive concessions from ones with no real alternative.

## 7. Integrative Pathways

- **Bounded transparency formula:** Define the joint body's role over product/platform allocation strictly as binding *reporting and disclosure* against milestone tracking, with no approval or veto right — satisfying State Government's fait-accompli concern and Investors' anti-co-management concern simultaneously.
- **Symmetric-but-bounded recovery, explicitly reciprocal:** Lock both wage recovery and dividend recovery to the same milestone triggers and the same 2030 horizon, with an explicit sunset clause on both sides (not just dividends) — addressing Works Council/Union's asymmetry objection without requiring Family Holding to accept a permanent constraint.
- **Attribution-tiered breakdown:** Structure the plant-level cost breakdown to explicitly separate structural, utilization-driven, and market-driven components of the 20% gap, with utilization-driven costs assigned to the non-labour/product-allocation lever rather than the labour package — resolving the union's "don't ask wages to fix under-filled lines" objection while giving management a legitimate, separately tracked mechanism.
- **Named end-state for failed second cycle:** Formalize "intensified non-labour measures + further labour/capital renegotiation + product-allocation deprioritization (with joint-body transparency)" as the explicit, exhaustive consequence set — removing "closures" and "compulsory redundancy" from the space of possible outcomes at any stage, which would likely secure Works Council/Union sign-off.
- **Sequenced disclosure protocol:** Commit to simultaneous release of the plant-level breakdown, the firm non-labour figure, and the Family Holding's final dividend mechanics in a single package to Round 5 — preventing further sequencing disputes and allowing Investors, Union, and Works Council to evaluate sufficiency together.

## 8. Open Questions

- Does the combined labour package, non-labour lever, and any further measures actually sum to the stated ~20% gap on a credible timeline — the determinative question for Investors' "investability" verdict?
- Where precisely does "joint-body transparency" over product allocation end and "management's ordinary capital-allocation authority" begin — a boundary requested but not yet defined by Management or State Government?
- Will Works Council and Union accept product/platform-allocation deprioritization as a legitimate substitute consequence for missed milestones, or do they consider it functionally equivalent to the closure leverage they have just extracted a guarantee against?
- Is a time-bounded (through-2030) dividend-wage symmetry acceptable to Works Council/Union, or will they insist on a mechanism that survives the agreement's horizon?
- What specific number of renegotiation cycles and consequence structure applies if the *second* graduated cycle also fails — parties have specified the process but not the ultimate floor beneath it.

## 9. Recommended Next Step

Convene Round 5 with a single, simultaneous data release: (a) management's plant-by-plant, cost-category breakdown separating structural from utilization-driven cost; (b) a firm (non-indicative) figure and timeline for the non-labour lever; and (c) the Family Holding's finalized dividend-mechanics text. Pair this with immediate, direct responses from Works Council and Union on (i) acceptance of product-allocation deprioritization as the named consequence for a failed second milestone cycle, and (ii) the acceptability of a 2030-bounded (rather than permanent) dividend-wage symmetry. This sequencing converts the negotiation from architecture-building to a testable arithmetic and acceptance decision, which is the necessary condition — repeatedly named by the highest-standing stakeholders (Works Council, Union, State Government, Investors) — for treating any agreement as real rather than provisional.

## 10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Interaction

- **The board-arithmetic argument reframed the entire negotiation's power structure only because it was tested across rounds.** A single-shot prompt would likely have taken management's "conditional guarantee" at face value; the multi-turn exchange let Works Council expose that conditionality was costless leverage rather than a genuine trade-off, which in turn forced management's own admission (Round 4) that its real accountability mechanism lay elsewhere (product allocation) — a disclosure that would not have surfaced without the pressure of sequential rounds.
- **Investors' independent, non-governance-based objection function emerged as a distinct check.** Rather than contesting the co-determination politics, Investors consistently withheld judgment on sufficiency ("necessary but not sufficient") — revealing that governance convergence and financial credibility are separate axes that can diverge even when all political stakeholders agree, a distinction only visible because Investors repeated the same unmet condition across three rounds while everything else moved.
- **The "second lever" discovery reveals how procedural guarantees can be hollowed out by adjacent authority.** The interaction surfaced a previously invisible equivalence — product/platform allocation as a functional substitute for plant closure — only because State Government and Investors, from different motives (job protection vs. capital efficiency), both pressed management to be explicit about what it intended to retain. Neither would likely have found this from an isolated statement.
- **"Shared sacrifice" was renegotiated from a magnitude question into a timing question.** Early rounds framed proportionality as about *size* of contribution (wages vs. dividends vs. executive pay); by Round 4, Works Council and Union relocated the fairness test to *recovery symmetry* — whether capital and labour concessions unwind on the same curve. This reframing only emerged because the Family Holding's initial concession invited scrutiny of its exact wording, which a single-round exchange would not have produced.
- **Sequencing disputes resolved themselves through iterative disclosure rather than negotiation over principle.** The Round 1–3 standoff over "guarantees first" vs. "numbers first" was not resolved by either side conceding the principle; it dissolved as concrete guarantees (closure, redundancy) and partial numbers (the 20% gap, indicative non-labour range) were delivered in parallel across rounds — demonstrating that progressive, reciprocal disclosure substituted for a resolved sequencing rule.
