Let me engage directly with the sharpest argument this round — the board-arithmetic point — because it deserves a real answer, not a dodge.

**On the works council's and union's structural argument.** You're right on the mechanics: no closure resolution passes this board without labour's votes. I'm not going to pretend otherwise or argue the statute away. But I want to correct where you take that fact, because I think it proves less than you're claiming.

You're treating "management can't force a closure" as equivalent to "management has no real lever left, so conditionality is pure theatre." That's wrong, and here's why: a formal plant-closure resolution is not the only path to a plant winding down. Product and platform allocation decisions — which model gets built where, which plants receive the next generation of investment — sit with management and the board's ordinary capital-allocation authority, not the special closure statute. A site that keeps its full workforce and never gets a closure vote can still shrink to a fraction of its footprint over a decade through simple non-replacement of product, the same way plants across this industry have quietly wound down without a single closure announcement. So no — I don't accept that removing "conditional" language from the closure guarantee costs me nothing. It costs me the second lever, unless I say plainly what I actually intend to retain.

So here's my real offer, stated honestly rather than defended by vague conditionality: **I will make the closure commitment unconditional in the narrow sense you're asking — no plant closures, full stop, for the term through 2030, no hedge, no "conditional on delivery" caveat on that specific point.** I accept your argument that the board math makes the hedge largely rhetorical anyway, and I'd rather remove a phrase that costs me nothing real than keep fighting over words.

So: no compulsory redundancies, agreed, on the condition I'm stating plainly now — if voluntary uptake falls materially short of the headcount reduction the package requires, that shortfall gets addressed through deeper working-time reduction, redeployment, or an extended timeline — not through a quiet reopening of the compulsory question later.
