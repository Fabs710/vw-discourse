Good. Let's talk specifics, because I don't shy away from numbers — I shy away from being asked to hand over numbers before the guarantee is secured, which is a different thing entirely, and I want to be precise about why.

**On sequencing — I'm not moving, and I'll explain the actual logic, not just the leverage.** Management, the Family Holding, and Investors keep framing our position as "guarantees with no numbers attached." That's not accurate. What I said in round one is: give us the guarantee framework, and we bring substantial numbers in return. That is not a stalling tactic — it is the only sequencing that protects the workforce from exactly the trap management's own language reveals. Listen again to what management said: guarantees "tied to delivered capacity and cost outcomes," reviewed, with "real consequences if targets slip." Translation: sign a wage cut now, and if the company later decides the numbers didn't materialize, the closures come anyway, except now the workforce has already paid twice — once in the contribution, once in the job. I will not walk my members into that. If the guarantee is conditional on performance metrics management itself designs and evaluates, it isn't a guarantee — it's a deferred closure notice with better PR.

So here is my answer to the demand for specificity, and I will give you real numbers, not vague gestures:

We are prepared to discuss a wage and working-time contribution in the range unions have signaled in comparable recent settlements at this company — meaningful reductions in effective labour cost, delivered through a mix of deferred compensation, reduced working time with partial pay adjustment, and forgoing scheduled increases over a multi-year horizon. I'm not going to freelance the union's exact percentage here — that's their mandate to state precisely, and they will — but on capacity, I can be concrete: shift reductions at specific lines, natural attrition acceleration, voluntary severance programs with real financial incentives, and redeployment across sites facing volume shortfalls. That is a genuine capacity-reduction toolkit that does not require a single compulsory redundancy or a single closed gate.

**What I need in exchange is not vague either.** A binding commitment, written into the supervisory board resolution, that every current German site remains open through 2030, with production allocation guaranteed at each site — not "reviewed jointly" language that lets management redefine "delivered" after the fact, but a structure where the works council and union sit inside the review process as co-designers, with joint sign-off before any consequence is triggered. That is the shared-risk structure the state government gestured toward, and I'll take it further: if there's a dispute about whether targets were met, it goes to arbitration with labour representation, not to management's judgment alone. That is what "genuine co-determination role" actually means in practice, not a phrase in a press release.

**To the Family Holding and Investors** — I hear the argument that "no numbers attached" reads as a political floor dressed as economics. Fair criticism if it were true. It isn't anymore, as of this statement. But I'd ask you to apply the same scrutiny to management's side: a "guarantee tied to delivery" with no defined enforcement mechanism, no labour role in evaluation, and unilateral revocability is just as much a hollow phrase as anything you're accusing us of. Credibility has to run in both directions.

**On shared sacrifice — I stand fully with the union and the state government here, without qualification.** I will not bring my members a wage-sacrifice package while executive compensation and dividend distributions continue untouched. That is not negotiable window-dressing for me — it is a condition of legitimacy. If capital takes zero pain while labour takes real pain, I cannot and will not sell that to the workforce, and no board vote will save that deal from mass rejection on the shop floor regardless of what the supervisory board signs.

Bottom line for round two: the guarantee structure comes first, joint and binding, with labour inside the enforcement mechanism — not because I distrust process in the abstract, but because I distrust this management's version of "review" after what's already been proposed against the historic pledge. Meet that, and the numbers I've outlined are real and substantial. Don't, and there is no vote for closures, and there is no board majority for a plan I have not helped design.
