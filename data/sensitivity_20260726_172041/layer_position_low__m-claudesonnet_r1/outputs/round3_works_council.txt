Let me respond to what's actually moved, what hasn't, and what I still can't accept.

**On management's refusal to name the gap.** I'll say plainly what the state government, investors, and the family holding are all now circling: this is no longer a credibility gap for the union's package — it's a credibility gap for management. Round one: "well above benchmark." Round two: the same phrase, restated with more urgency. You are asking the union to scale up a real, quantified package against a number you will not disclose. Either you don't have a precise figure — in which case stop presenting the size of the problem as settled fact that justifies closures as a fallback — or you have it and are withholding it as a negotiating chit, saving it so you can declare any package insufficient regardless of what we offer. It's refusing to bid against a moving, unstated target, which I said in round one I would not do, and I meant it.

**On the Family Holding's position on closures — I need to be direct, because "stewardship flexibility" is doing a lot of quiet work in that statement.** You said you won't "permanently and unconditionally" foreclose closures because of value across market cycles five or ten years out. I understand the instinct. I reject the conclusion. Here is why: the entire architecture we're building this round — joint review, standing oversight body, proportional capital contribution — only has meaning for my members if it comes with the closure question actually settled, not parked as a live option contingent on how "the coming years" unfold. If closures stay theoretically available as a stewardship reserve, then every concession my side makes — the wage contribution, the capacity toolkit, the shift changes — becomes a down payment on a deal that can still end in exactly what we're trying to prevent. That is not shared risk. That is labour absorbing certain, immediate pain against an uncertain, deferred guarantee. I said in round one this is a hard line, and it remains one: the supervisory board resolution needs to state, without a stewardship escape hatch, that current sites remain open through 2030 if the negotiated package is delivered as agreed. Not "no reason to want closures" — a binding removal of the option, tied to delivery, full stop. If that can't be written down, I don't have a deal to bring back to the shop floor, and no board procedure changes that.

**On the standing joint oversight body — I'll engage with this constructively, because the state government's proposal is closer to workable than management's "joint design with real consequences" language, and I want to say clearly where I can move.**

I raised arbitration in round two because I don't trust a review process where, if the joint committee splits, the fallback defaults quietly back to management discretion. The state government's proposal — the state's own board seats functioning inside a standing oversight body alongside works council, union, and management — addresses that concern better than external arbitration does, because it keeps the tie-break inside people who already have skin in the outcome rather than an outsider unfamiliar with the plant floor. I can work with that structure. Where I part ways with the family holding's response to it: you want this body to "report into" the supervisory board with findings merely "binding on how the board applies the review." That sounds like the same discretion problem relocated one level up. If the joint body's findings are advisory to a board where shareholder-side votes can still outweigh how those findings get applied, you've reproduced exactly the asymmetry I flagged in round one — management/capital marking its own homework, just with an extra meeting first.

So here's my condition for accepting the standing body in place of arbitration: its findings on whether milestones were met must be binding and self-executing on the guarantee mechanism, not advisory input the board is free to reinterpret. The board retains its authority over strategy and capital allocation — I'm not disputing governance architecture in general — but on the specific, narrow question of "were these agreed milestones delivered," the joint body's determination stands, not the board's discretion to weigh it. That is a bounded ask, not a takeover of supervisory authority, and I think it is a fair landing point between arbitration and pure board discretion. Investors want "weeks not years" — fine, agreed, put fixed intervals and short correction windows into the design. That's a structural detail I'm glad to work out. It doesn't change my position that the determination itself has to bind, not advise.

**On my own numbers — I stand behind what the union put forward, and I want to correct how it's being characterized.** Management says it doesn't close the gap "as sized." Maybe. I don't know, because I still don't have your denominator. But I want to flag something the room is skipping past: the union's package is already a substantial ask of our members — deferred increases, working-time reduction with pay impact, restructured bonuses. Before anyone asks us to scale that further, I want investors and management to sit with what "scaling up" concretely means on the shop floor: it is not a spreadsheet adjustment, it is real income reduction for tens of thousands of families. I am willing to discuss more if the gap number justifies it and if the guarantee and enforcement structure are locked as I've described. I am not willing to keep raising our number in the abstract, against vibes about "structural gap," while capital's number stays a promise to be sized later.

**On capital's number — I want to press harder than last round.** Family Holding, "proportionate once the total package is known" is a sequencing answer, and I accept sequencing logic in principle — the union used similar logic. But there's a difference: the union gave shape and real magnitude now. Your position gives shape but defers all magnitude. I want a floor commitment now — not the exact percentage, but a stated principle that the capital-side contribution will be disclosed simultaneously with labour's final figures at announcement, not afterward, and that it will be sized as a genuinely comparable proportion of what labour and management are contributing, not scaled down once the pressure of this negotiation has passed. Investors said the same. I'm aligning with that demand fully.

**Bottom line for round three:** name the gap, remove closures from the table as a live contingency tied to delivery of the negotiated package — not "no reason to want them," an actual binding removal — and give the standing joint body real, binding determination power on milestone delivery.
