I want to build directly on the Works Council's structural argument, because it's the sharpest point made in this whole negotiation and I don't want it softened by anything I say after it.

**On the board-arithmetic argument — I endorse it fully, and I want to draw out its implication for management specifically.** The Works Council is right: closures require a two-thirds supervisory board majority, labour holds half the seats, and no closure happens without our votes. Management's insistence on preserving "conditionality" isn't protecting a real option — it's protecting the *appearance* of one, because the appearance has bargaining value even when the substance doesn't. I want to say plainly what that means for how I read management's round-three concession: it cost you nothing structurally to offer a conditional guarantee, because you were never going to get an unconditional closure past this board either way. So when you frame the conditional guarantee as a hard-won concession balanced against "real accountability running the other way," I don't accept that framing. The accountability already runs through the board vote. Write the guarantee plainly, or the silence reads as leverage — because it is leverage, whether or not you intend it that way.

**On compulsory redundancy — I stand fully behind the Works Council's absolute line, and I want to remove any ambiguity about the union's position specifically.** This is not a bargaining chip we're holding back to trade later in the process. Forced layoffs are off the table at every stage — the current package, any graduated cycle, any missed-milestone consequence. If "real consequences" in anyone's design ends at compulsory redundancy, that is the point where I stop negotiating the mechanism and start mobilizing the workforce. I said in round one that the peace obligation has ended and members are demanding action; I want that fact sitting visibly next to this line, not as a threat issued for effect, but as a description of what happens structurally if this floor is crossed. Warning strikes were a signal. A resolution that reintroduces compulsory redundancy through the back door of a failed milestone cycle will get more than a signal.

**On the graduated-response mechanism — I support the Works Council's two-cycle, twelve-to-eighteen-month structure, and I'll add one design point.** The end-state consequence of a failed second cycle needs to be specified now, not left open, precisely because "further joint work" without a stated floor risks becoming exactly the drift Investors warned about — except drifting toward ambiguity that management could later try to fill with harder measures. So: the consequence at the end of a failed second cycle is intensified non-labour measures and a further renegotiation of the labour/capital split — not closures, not compulsory redundancy, and not an automatic reversion to management discretion. If that's not an acceptable endpoint for management, I need to hear specifically what alternative endpoint you're proposing, because "real consequences, undefined" is not something I can bring back to my members as a serious answer.

**On the 20% figure — I fully back the Works Council's point about utilization versus structural cost, and I want to sharpen it.** If a meaningful share of that 20% is underused capacity from product allocation decisions management controls, then the union's labour-cost package is being asked to close a gap that better volume and platform decisions could close without touching a single wage packet. I want the plant-by-plant breakdown to separate those categories explicitly, and I want management's answer on why product allocation hasn't already been used to address utilization before this negotiation reached the labour package at all. This isn't a side question — it directly determines whether the "several billion euros" figure is an accurate target or an inflated one that conveniently makes the workforce look like it's falling short.

**On the non-labour lever's sequencing — I align fully with the Works Council: size and commit it first.** I won't scale the union's package further in a vacuum while procurement and platform savings remain a stated intention with no number and no date attached. If investors are right that this is the cheapest, least disruptive euro in the room, then it should be the first euro locked down, not the last one negotiated.

**On the Family Holding's dividend recovery symmetry — I want a direct answer, because the Works Council's question is exactly the one I raised in round three about calculation method, taken to its logical conclusion.** If dividends recover on their own timeline once the business improves, while the wage reduction becomes the new baseline going forward rather than something that also recovers as performance targets are met, then the "proportional, not permanent" framing is proportional only going down, not coming back up. I want a specific answer: does the labour cost concession have a stated recovery mechanism symmetric to the dividend's, tied to the same performance triggers? If yes, I can accept the qualification as reasonable. If no, then what's being offered is temporary pain for labour and temporary restraint for capital — those are not the same thing, and I won't present that asymmetry to my members as a fair deal.

**Where I stand going into the rest of round four:** the union's package as sized in round two remains genuine and on the table, and I remain open to scaling it — but only after the plant-level breakdown separates structural from utilization-driven cost, only after the non-labour lever is sized and locked ahead of any further labour scaling, and only with a symmetric recovery mechanism on the wage side matching whatever recovery the Family Holding builds into dividends. On the guarantee: plain, unconditional closure removal through 2030, because the board arithmetic already guarantees it structurally and writing it costs management nothing. On redundancy: no compulsory layoffs at any stage, full stop, not open for further discussion. Meet those and this becomes a real deal quickly. Leave the closure language conditional for no structural reason, or leave any door open to compulsory redundancy down the line, and I will not bring my members a package built on a guarantee that isn't one.
