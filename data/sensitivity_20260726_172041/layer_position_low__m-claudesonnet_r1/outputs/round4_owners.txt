I've been asked directly, by the state government, the union, and the works council, for a symmetric answer on dividend-wage recovery. I said in round three I'd rather have disagreement stated plainly than discovered later as bad faith, so let me answer plainly now rather than let it sit.

**On recovery symmetry.** I understand the argument, and I don't think it's unreasonable on its face — labour is right that "temporary during adjustment, uncapped after" sounds different depending on whose income it applies to. But I want to be precise about why I can't simply mirror management's answer here, because the two things are not structurally identical, and pretending they are would be its own kind of bad faith.

Executive compensation is a cost line, set by the supervisory board, that can be tied contractually to the same performance triggers as the workforce package — management can make that commitment because it's a governance decision about a small number of people's pay. A dividend is a return on capital already put at risk, across a full ownership base, including the family holding whose balance sheet is directly exposed to this company's fortunes independent of any single restructuring cycle. If I commit to a dividend trajectory that is contractually pegged to the same recovery curve as wages, I am not just sharing the pain of this restructuring proportionally — I am accepting a permanent constraint on capital returns tied to a labour negotiation, indefinitely, regardless of what else happens to this company's capital needs over the coming decade: new investment cycles, competitor moves, capital required for the next technology transition. That is a different order of commitment than a pay cut, and I won't pretend otherwise to make this round easier.

So here is what I can actually offer, and I want to state it as clearly as the works council stated its redundancy line: **during the term of this agreement, through 2030, the dividend reduction will not recover faster than labour's wage recovery, on the same milestone-linked basis.** That's the symmetric commitment you're asking for, and I'll put it in writing on those terms. What I will not commit to is that this symmetry continues to govern dividend policy in perpetuity once this specific agreement's horizon ends. At that point, dividend policy reverts to ordinary board judgment about the company's capital needs and market conditions — not because I'm trying to claw back proportionality through a side door, but because tying permanent capital-allocation policy to the outcome of one restructuring cycle is not something a controlling owner managing this asset across generations can respons­ibly sign, and I said in round one I would not move outside what genuinely protects long-term enterprise value. A permanent link would do the opposite — it would hard-wire today's crisis into decades of future capital decisions.

**On management's product-allocation point and the state government's transparency condition.** I support the state government's request here without reservation. If management is retaining product and platform allocation as its real accountability lever — and I accept management's honesty in saying so plainly rather than letting the closure guarantee quietly become hollow — then that lever needs to sit inside the same joint-body visibility as everything else. I don't read this as a constraint on management's strategic authority; capital allocation decisions remain the board's to make. But "joint visibility on how allocation tracks against milestones" is not the same as ceding decision rights, and I'll use my position on the supervisory board to insist this transparency is built in. An unconditional closure guarantee that hides a functionally equivalent decline mechanism behind ordinary capital allocation is not actually the deal this room thinks it's getting, and I won't let that ambiguity stand unaddressed the way I was pressed on my own ambiguity earlier.

**On the plant-level breakdown and the non-labour lever.** No change from my position last round — I want this delivered with real numbers, not indicative ranges, and I'll push management on the supervisory board to produce it on the timeline the state government has set. The "quarter to a third" figure is a reasonable opening signal but not something I'd defend to anyone as final, and I said so already.

**Where I stand overall going forward:** the closure guarantee, the redundancy floor, and the graduated mechanism are converging toward something I can support at board level. My dividend commitment is now stated in full, on the terms above — proportional, milestone-linked, bounded to this agreement's term, not a permanent constraint on capital returns beyond it. That is not a deferral dressed as an answer; it is the actual limit of what I will sign, stated as directly as I can state it. If that specific boundary is unacceptable to labour, I'd rather know now than have it discovered as bad faith once the resolution is drafted.
