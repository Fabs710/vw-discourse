I've been asked directly, twice now, for a method on dividend calculation, and I said in round one I'd resist symbolic gestures calibrated for optics. Let me answer honestly rather than let the deferral read as evasion, because that reading is now costing me credibility I need for the rest of this process.

**On the dividend calculation method.** The state government and union are right that "we'll size it later" is a weaker answer than the standard management just set. I'll match it, with one necessary qualification. Method: the dividend adjustment will be measured as a percentage reduction from the baseline that would otherwise have been distributed, and that percentage will not be smaller than the percentage figure applied to the workforce's effective labour cost reduction over the same performance horizon. Same comparable-basis logic management applied to executive pay. I'm stating the method now, as asked.

The qualification: I will not accept a mechanical read where "labour takes X%, so dividend automatically drops X%" regardless of what the total package actually delivers in restored earnings. If this restructuring works — genuinely closes the gap, restores margins — a healthier dividend funded by a healthier company is not a betrayal of proportionality, it's the point of the exercise. What I'm committing to is that the *reduction during the adjustment period*, relative to what would otherwise have been paid, tracks the same proportion as labour's contribution. I don't want that principle mistaken for a permanent cap on returns once the company is fixed. If that distinction isn't acceptable to this room, say so now, because I'd rather have that disagreement explicitly than have it discovered later as bad faith.

**On the plant-by-plant breakdown of the 20% figure.** I support the state government's demand. I need that breakdown as much as anyone at this table — I cannot judge whether the union's package, scaled up, actually closes the gap without knowing how much of it is structural versus utilization-driven. I'd add: I want the non-labour lever — procurement, platform consolidation — pursued with real urgency, not as a rhetorical softener. If a meaningful share of that 20% can come from measures that don't touch a single wage packet, that is unambiguously the best outcome for the long-term value of this asset, and I will use my position on the supervisory board to push management to size it seriously rather than let it stay a footnote.

**On the graduated-response mechanism for missed milestones.** This is where I want to be careful, because it's close to the line I stated in round two and I don't want to blur it now under pressure. I can support a graduated response — renegotiation and a documented good-faith process before closures re-enter consideration, rather than automatic reversion on a single missed interval. That is reasonable design and consistent with what I said about wanting the guarantee to mean something in both directions. What I will not support is a graduated process with no realistic end point — one that can be extended indefinitely through repeated "one more attempt" cycles regardless of how badly targets are missed, because at that point the mechanism has quietly become the unconditional guarantee I already said I cannot sign. A fixed number of renegotiation cycles, on a defined timeline, after which the parties genuinely face the consequences that were agreed — that I can support. An open-ended process that never actually concludes — I cannot.

**On the works council's and union's framing that my position on closures is internally contradictory.** I don't accept that characterization, and I'll say why plainly rather than let it stand unanswered. Accepting the board-vote arithmetic today is not the same as guaranteeing that arithmetic, or the market conditions underneath it, are fixed for a decade. I am not holding closures in reserve as leverage against this negotiation — I've said what I'll sign if the package delivers, and I mean it. But refusing to write an irrevocable, outcome-independent commitment into a board resolution is not bad faith; it is the ordinary discipline of not binding an enterprise's hands permanently against futures none of us can see from here. Management's formulation — binding through 2030, conditional on jointly verified delivery — is the version of this I can actually support. That is where I land, and it hasn't moved because it doesn't need to: it already reflects the stewardship logic I described in round one, just with real teeth attached now that management has put a number and a mechanism behind it.

Where this stands for me going into round four: name the plant-level breakdown, size the non-labour lever, fix the graduated-response timeline with a real conclusion, and I will support the family holding's dividend method as stated above sitting inside the total package. That combination — a quantified, testable gap; delivery-conditional guarantees with binding fact-finding; proportionate capital contribution on both fronts — is a deal I can bring my weight behind at board level. I don't need this resolved by rhetoric. I need it resolved by numbers that hold up, and we are closer to that than we were two rounds ago.
