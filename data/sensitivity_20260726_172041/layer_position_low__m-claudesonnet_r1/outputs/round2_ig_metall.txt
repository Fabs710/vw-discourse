I want to build on what the Works Council just laid out, put actual union numbers on the table, and answer management, the family holding, and investors directly on the sequencing question, because I think it's being mischaracterized.

**On numbers — here they are, concretely.** The union is prepared to negotiate a labour-cost contribution in the order of magnitude of a mid-single-digit percentage reduction in effective labour costs, delivered through a combination of: forgoing the next one to two scheduled tariff increases, a working-time reduction model with partial pay compensation, and restructuring of bonus and profit-sharing components tied to company performance rather than fixed guarantees. On capacity, we can support a defined reduction in shift patterns at specific overcapacity lines, an acceleration of natural attrition through demographic turnover, and a voluntary severance program sized to actually move headcount — not a token scheme designed to fail so management can point back to closures. Taken together, across a three-to-four-year horizon, this is a substantial, real number — not a symbolic gesture. I am not going to invent a false precision to the euro in this room before my bargaining commission has finalized it, but the shape and scale is genuine, and it is on the table now, not contingent on guarantees being signed first. The Works Council is right that we don't hand over a blank number before a framework exists, but I want to be precise: we have already moved further than "vague willingness" — this is a real package, and management's own round-one framing acknowledged our counter-proposal exists. Don't let the absence of a single audited figure be used to pretend nothing concrete has been offered.

**On sequencing — I disagree with how management and the Family Holding are framing this as our inflexibility.** You're asking us to accept a wage cut and capacity reduction now, in exchange for a guarantee that survives only if *you* later judge the targets were met. That is not a parallel process — it is workers taking the first cut with a promissory note redeemable at management's discretion. I will not sign my members up for that. What the Works Council proposed — joint design of the review mechanism, labour representation in any dispute resolution, arbitration rather than unilateral management judgment — is not an obstruction of accountability. It is accountability, just not one-sided. If management's "shared risk" model only has consequences running one direction, downward onto the workforce, it isn't shared risk. It's risk transfer with better vocabulary.

**To the Family Holding directly** — you said "leverage is not a plan." I've just given you a plan with real magnitude. Now I'd ask the same rigor of you: what, specifically, are you prepared to contribute? "Open to proportional executive compensation and dividend discussion, if it comes to it" is not a plan either — it's the mirror image of what you're criticizing us for. If you want us to be specific first, be specific back. What's the actual dividend and executive compensation adjustment on the table, sized against what workers are being asked to give up?

**To Investors** — I take your point that dividend suspension shouldn't substitute for structural savings, and I'm not asking for that. I'm asking for it *alongside* structural savings, as the visible signal that this isn't only labour absorbing the adjustment. You said proportional executive pay cuts are "cheap and buy legitimacy" — good, then let's see the number, not the principle. Legitimacy isn't bought with a sentence in a press release; it's bought with a figure sitting next to ours.

**On management's claim that our position is "asking to give up your only structural lever before seeing the alternative."** I'd reframe that: the board arithmetic isn't a lever we're choosing to wield — it's the existing legal structure of this company, and it will not evaporate because a spreadsheet says closures are efficient. You don't get to negotiate around co-determination; you negotiate within it. So the real question on the table isn't whether closures happen — structurally, they don't have the votes, full stop, and this should stop being treated as a live option in the numbers you present to the board. The real question is whether we build a durable alternative together, with real contribution from every side, or whether we spend the next six months in an escalating strike calendar while the brand's numbers get worse and nobody's position improves. I would strongly prefer the former. I am not bluffing about the latter.

So: guarantee framework with joint enforcement, on the table now, alongside the contribution package I've just outlined — both moving together, not sequenced as ultimatums in either direction. Meet that, with real capital-side numbers attached, and this moves fast. Ask the workforce to absorb this alone while ownership's contribution stays a vague "if it comes to it," and you will find warning strikes are the mild phase, not the ceiling.
