[Regional State Government (Anchor shareholder, blocking-minority stake under special statute) — Round 1]

Let me set out where the state stands, because our vote will decide whether any closure scenario has a majority, and I'd rather everyone plan around that reality now than test it later.

First, agreement with the works council and the union on the fundamentals: this state will not vote for plant closures in Germany, and we will not vote to gut the workforce guarantees that have underpinned regional economic stability for decades. That is not a tactical position I am prepared to trade away for a marginally better savings number. Our mandate as an anchor shareholder is explicitly to protect this company as a regional economic anchor — the jobs, the supply chains, the tax base, the social contract around it. A closure list, however elegantly justified by return-on-sales arithmetic, is a decision this government will treat as a fundamental breach of what this company is supposed to be for the region, and we hold the board seats and the blocking minority to make that position matter, not just declare it.

So to management: I heard your offer to engage seriously with an alternative package if it closes the competitiveness gap credibly and durably. I take that offer at face value, and I want to be equally direct back — I am not interested in closures "if the numbers don't add up otherwise." That is not a contingency I am prepared to accept as legitimate. Find the durable alternative. That is the assignment, not a fallback.

But I am not naïve about the underlying problem either, and here I want to be honest with the works council and the union too, because fairness cuts both ways. I am risk-averse and long-horizon in how I think about this: a settlement that protects every site on paper in 2025 but leaves the brand bleeding money and missing its targets in 2028 is not a win for the region — it is a deferred crisis, and deferred crises tend to land harder, with less capital and less patience available to soften them next time. I have seen that pattern before in other industries in this state, and I am not willing to repeat it. So yes, I want real numbers. I want a capacity, working-time, and wage-contribution package that is actually costed and actually closes the gap — not a symbolic gesture that lets us all say we tried.

Where I differ from management's sequencing: I don't accept that site guarantees have to wait until the numbers are proven. That inverts the trust problem. Workers are not going to negotiate a serious contribution against the threat of closure hanging over them indefinitely — that's not a negotiation, that's coercion, and it will produce exactly the instability management claims to want to avoid. What I can support is a framework where guarantees are given now, structured around delivering the agreed capacity and cost outcomes on a credible timeline — reviewed jointly, not unilaterally revoked by management if targets slip. That is different from an unconditional blank check, and different from your "prove it first" position. It is a shared-risk structure, and it is the only version of this I will put my board seats behind.

On shared sacrifice — I side with the union here without qualification. I will not support asking the workforce to carry a wage or working-time contribution while executive compensation and dividend policy sail through untouched. That is not a matter of ideology; it is a matter of what will actually hold together politically and socially in this state, and what I, as a public shareholder, can defend to the public that put me in this seat. Proportional contribution from management and ownership is not optional decoration on this deal — it is a precondition for me backing it at board level.

So here is my position going forward, and it will not move far from this: I will use the blocking minority against closures, full stop. I will support a negotiated restructuring — capacity reduction, working-time reform, a genuine wage-side contribution — if it comes with binding site and job guarantees on a credible review structure, proportional pain-sharing from management and capital, and a real joint design process rather than consultation after the fact. Bring me that, and I am a constructive partner, and I will use my board weight to help it pass. Bring me a closure list, however dressed up, and you will find out exactly how firm this statute's veto is.
