# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Auditor mandate (broad convergence):** The auditor certifies (a) the baseline gap size and (b) whether a specific tested package closes it, in euros, on the agreed timeline. This is treated as arithmetic. Five of six parties (Works Council, State Government, Family Holding, Investors, and Management with a narrower framing) now converge on this scope. What the board *does* with a certified shortfall remains a board vote — this too has broad agreement in principle, though its practical meaning is disputed (see below).
- **Joint costing process:** Model access opens this week; six-week clock and week-three family holding disclosure stand unchallenged as dates. Works Council and Union both specify staged joint sign-off (baseline figures, demand/pricing assumptions, offset methodology) with disputes routed to the auditor.
- **Dual sign-off requirement:** Union's requirement that wage-side content requires both Works Council co-determination sign-off and separate union collective-bargaining sign-off is stated and not contested this round.
- **Proportionality test mechanics:** Works Council's and Union's formulation (capital-side sacrifice vs. labour-side contribution, same period, comparable basis) is now accepted without reservation by Management, State Government, Family Holding, and Investors.
- **Investment reallocation disclosure:** State Government's demand that any German-site investment reallocation be disclosed to the joint steering group before finalization is supported by Family Holding and Investors; Investors add a market-disclosure dimension. Management has not yet explicitly confirmed this commitment.
- **No reopening of signed labour contribution:** Management explicitly disclaims any intent to reopen labour's contribution after signature based on a later shortfall finding; Union treats this as a live concern to monitor rather than fully resolved.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

- **The central fault line, newly sharpened this round:** Whether a certified residual shortfall carries any operative consequence.
  - Works Council: veto unconditional, full stop — no consequence attaches to the veto's exercise.
  - Union: rights (board votes, strike weapon, tariff autonomy) not staked against any future number — materially aligned with Works Council but not identically worded.
  - State Government: veto unconditional *on outcome* but conditional *on genuine process*; explicitly distinguishes itself from Works Council by committing to continued pressure (investment reallocation, industrial policy, further levers) after veto exercise rather than treating veto survival as endpoint.
  - Family Holding: will not accept "gap certified, veto holds, nothing follows" as a stable outcome; treats this as materially equivalent to denying the gap's reality.
  - Management: has stated that if this is truly the labour/union position, it changes what Management is willing to invest in the process going forward, and signals investment reallocation as a lever outside board veto.
  - Investors: explicitly ask Works Council and Union to clarify whether their position is "full stop" or closer to the State Government's "constrained but continuing search" — this question is now on the table, unanswered.
- **Whether investment reallocation is a legitimate fiduciary lever or a deniable substitute for closure:** Management asserts the right; State Government, Family Holding, and Investors all flag conditional acceptance contingent on transparency and genuine (not backdoor) rationale. Management has not yet responded to the specific disclosure commitment requested.
- **Auditor mandate — residual dispute:** Works Council's original position (certify arithmetic only, not "closure adequacy") has been effectively superseded by convergence around State Government's formulation, but Works Council has not explicitly restated its position in light of this — worth confirming rather than assuming.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **What "genuine exhaustion of non-closure alternatives" concretely requires** before a shortfall can be treated as certified-and-final — no party has defined the threshold or process for determining when alternatives are "exhausted" versus merely "tested."
- **What State Government's "further pressure" actually consists of in practice** — investment reallocation, industrial policy, demand-side measures are named but not specified, costed, or timed.
- **The direct question Investors posed to Works Council and Union** — full stop, or constrained-but-continuing search — remains unanswered and is likely the single highest-value clarification available for the next round.
- **Management's commitment (or non-commitment) to the disclosure protocol** on investment reallocation is outstanding.
- **How a certified shortfall interacts with the family holding's and investors' capital-allocation expectations** if the veto holds — none of the capital-side parties have specified what they would actually do (beyond re-pricing equity or withholding board support) if the answer to the question above is "full stop."

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

1. **Works Council and Union:** directly answer the Investors' question — is the veto an absolute endpoint, or a constraint operating within a continuing joint search for closure? Precision here is now the pivotal issue in the negotiation.
2. **Management:** respond explicitly to the disclosure commitment requested by State Government, Family Holding, and Investors regarding investment reallocation decisions.
3. **State Government:** specify, even preliminarily, what "further pressure" and continued mechanisms would concretely involve if a genuine residual gap survives rigorous testing.
4. **All parties:** confirm whether the auditor-mandate convergence (gap size + closure adequacy, board decides response) is now shared language across the table, including explicit Works Council confirmation.
