I want to build on the works council's framing but sharpen it with the union's specific instruments — the collective agreement and the strike mandate are levers the works council doesn't hold, and I intend to use them precisely.

**On sequencing — full agreement with the works council, no daylight between us here.** Guarantee and contribution signed simultaneously, same document, same table. I'll add the union's version of why this matters: a collective wage agreement is not a memo, it's a legally binding instrument with real duration and real enforceability. If management wants a wage-side contribution — pay restraint, deferred variable comp — that goes into the same collective agreement that carries the job and site guarantees. Not a side letter, not a management assurance, not something that lives in board minutes while the wage concession lives in a signed contract. Equal legal weight or no deal. I've seen "we'll formalize the guarantee once the numbers are final" become "the numbers were worse than expected, revisit the guarantee" too many times in this industry to accept it on trust again.

**On management's "rounding error" comment about executive compensation — I want to return to this directly, because it was too easily let go in round one.** You're right that executive pay alone doesn't close a structural gap measured in hundreds of millions or billions. Nobody claimed it did. But you're wrong to treat that as closing the argument. The demand was never "executive pay closes the gap" — it was "capital and management share the pain proportionate to what they're asking labour to share." If workers are asked to give up real income — deferred bonuses, working-time cuts with partial compensation — while dividend policy continues untouched because the family holding "makes no commitment yet on scale or form," that is not shared sacrifice, that's a euphemism. I want a number from the family holding this round, not a "direction is not in question" — direction without a figure is not a commitment, it's a mood.

**On the investors' position — I'll engage this one directly because it deserves a direct answer.** You say you'll judge the deal on whether it's quantified and closes the gap on a credible timeline, and that you have no attachment to mechanism. Fine — but you also said something else worth pressing on: that a "cosmetic" capital contribution wouldn't satisfy you if the underlying cost base isn't addressed. I'd ask you to apply that same standard symmetrically. If the cost base gets addressed entirely through labour concessions — working-time cuts, pay restraint, voluntary programmes — while capital returns are untouched, that is not durable either, because it is not sustainable politically or industrially. A deal that provokes an indefinite strike because it was one-sided doesn't deliver your "credible, quantified, time-bound return to competitive margins" — it delivers the opposite. So when you say "bring us the numbers and we'll judge on merits," understand that the numbers you'll be judging include the capital-side contribution, not just the labour-side one. If that side of the ledger stays blank, you will get exactly the "value destruction in real time" you say you fear — not because the union is being obstinate, but because a package perceived as one-sided will not hold with the membership, full stop.

**On the joint costing process and transparency — I back the works council's demand for full access to the actual cost model, unfiltered.** I'd add one thing from the union's seat: the model needs to include the assumption set behind why closures are treated as necessary at all — capacity utilization by plant, product allocation decisions made by management over the past several years, and how much of the "structural gap" is genuinely a German cost-competitiveness problem versus a consequence of underinvestment or under-allocation of new models to specific sites. If we're going to be tested against management's benchmark, we get to interrogate whether the benchmark itself was shaped by decisions the workforce had no part in and no responsibility for.

**On strikes during the process — to be precise, since the moderator flagged this as unresolved.** The works council speaks to board-level pressure; I speak to the shop floor and the collective agreement. My position: warning strikes continue during the costing window. If that good faith breaks — if the process becomes a management monologue with labour numbers demanded on a deadline while capital's side stays vague — escalation resumes immediately, and it resumes harder, because a stalled process after a demonstrated willingness to engage is worse than no process at all.

What I am adding this round is: no collective agreement that places the entire quantified adjustment on the labour side while capital's contribution remains a promise without a figure. Keep asking us for concessions in figures while offering restraint in adjectives, and this remains a standoff — one that gets more expensive for everyone the longer it runs.
