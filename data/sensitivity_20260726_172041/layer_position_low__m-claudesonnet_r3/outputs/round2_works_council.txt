Good, let's get concrete, because I've heard six parties agree on process and none of them yet commit to what actually matters: numbers and sequencing. I'll give you mine.

**On sequencing — I want to correct a drift happening in this room.** Management, the family holding, and the investors keep repeating that guarantees must "follow" a sized savings package. Listen carefully to what that actually means in practice: it means the workforce commits to pay restraint, working-time cuts, and voluntary programmes *first*, on trust, and finds out only afterward whether the company still closes a plant anyway because the number didn't add up to management's satisfaction. I have seen this sequencing before in this industry, and I will not accept it again. It is exactly how this company arrived at broken pledges the last time. So no — the guarantee and the contribution get negotiated and signed *together*, as one package, in the same document, at the same table. Not guarantee-then-contribution, not contribution-then-guarantee. Simultaneous. That is not a procedural nicety, it is the only sequencing that protects my members from being asked to bleed first and hope second.

**On the state government's framing — I want to be precise, because there's a subtle drift here too.** You said "guarantees follow from a credible plan." I agree the plan has to be credible. I do not agree that credibility means waiting for management to size the number alone and then handing us a guarantee as a reward. Credibility comes from the joint costing process itself — built together, tested together, signed off together. That's the whole point of co-determination: we are not a stakeholder consulted on someone else's model, we are a party to building it.

**On management's challenge — "show me a costed alternative that closes the gap."** Fair, and I'll answer it directly rather than dodge it. Here is what I can put on the table for the joint process to test, in outline, pending real access to your cost model, which we have not yet had:

- A defined multi-year working-time reduction across affected plants, with partial pay compensation — this has closed gaps of comparable scale before in this industry, and I expect it can again.
- An accelerated voluntary separation and early-retirement programme, sized against actual demographic attrition data over the next three to four years — not vague, actuarially calculable.
- Deferred variable compensation elements for the workforce, matched — and I stress *matched* — against the executive and dividend restraint the union has already demanded.
- Capacity and shift-pattern adjustments at specific lines, which reduce fixed cost without touching the site itself.

I am not going to pretend this closes 100% of whatever number management eventually names, because I don't know that number yet — nobody in this room does, because management has not shared its actual cost model with us. So here is my condition on the joint process: full transparency into that model, immediately, not filtered through a summary deck. If we are going to be tested against a benchmark, we get to see the benchmark and interrogate its assumptions. I want the state government and the family holding on record supporting that — you both said you want a genuine joint process; genuine means we see the same numbers management sees.

**On the investors' comment that this looks like "every restructuring cycle for fifteen years."** That's a fair historical point and I won't deny previous rounds didn't fully close the gap. But I'd ask the investors to also apply their own discipline to management's side: closures haven't been tested either, because this company has never actually run one. So don't treat "voluntary programmes have limits" as proof that only closure works — that's an assumption, not evidence. Test both models in the same joint process, with the same rigor, and let the numbers speak. I'll accept that discipline. I expect management to accept it too, including opening up whether their own product allocation and investment decisions — not just headcount — are part of why plants are underutilized in the first place.

**On timeline.** I can accept "weeks, not months" for the costing exercise itself. What I will not accept is strikes pausing as a precondition for that process to start. The union will speak to its own mandate, but from the works council side: the process runs, and the pressure continues in parallel, because the two things have always run in parallel in this industry and stopping one to be polite about the other has never produced a fair outcome for the workforce historically.

**To be completely clear about my red lines, because I think there's a temptation in this room to treat them as opening bids to be worn down through five more rounds:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies. Not "closures are unlikely if the numbers don't work" — closures do not happen, because I do not have the votes to allow them, and neither does the state government's seat. That is not posture, it is the board math everyone has already acknowledged. What is genuinely negotiable is everything about how we close the cost gap without closures. I've just shown you four real levers. Let's cost them, together, transparently, and sign the guarantee in the same breath as the contribution — not before, not after.
