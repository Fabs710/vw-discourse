I'll respond directly this round, because the state government and the union have both said, correctly, that "direction is not in question" was not enough, and I don't think avoiding the number any longer serves this process or the holding's own interest in it succeeding.

**On the capital-side contribution.** I will bring a concrete proposal to the supervisory board — not a gesture, a proposal with figures — consisting of two elements. First, a freeze on dividend growth for the duration of the restructuring period, at minimum, with the holding prepared to forgo the proportional increase it would otherwise expect on its own majority stake, not merely accept a symbolic cut applied evenly across all shareholders. Second, a reduction in management board variable compensation tied to the same competitiveness and cost-per-vehicle metrics that any labour contribution and any plant-level guarantee will be measured against — so that management's own pay is exposed to the same performance risk it is asking the workforce and me to accept. I won't invent a precise percentage in this room before it has been through the compensation committee properly, but I will commit to sponsoring this formally at the next board session, in parallel with the costing process, not after it concludes. That answers the state government's timing point directly: this moves now, alongside the process, not as a reward at the end of it.

I want to be honest about why I'm willing to move here and not elsewhere: this is a legitimacy cost, not a structural one. It does not by itself close a cost gap measured in the hundreds of millions, and I won't let it be mistaken for that — management is right to guard against this becoming the headline that substitutes for the real work. But it is real money, foregone by the family's own stake before anyone else's, and I'd rather spend it now buying a workforce's willingness to negotiate seriously than lose far more in a prolonged strike that damages the brand and the balance sheet both.

**On sequencing.** I support the direction this has moved in — process first, guarantee and contribution signed together at the conclusion, once the number is validated jointly. That is consistent with what I said in round one and I won't pretend it's a concession on my part; it was always my position. What I will say plainly to the works council: I understand the fear of "bleed first, hope second," and I think the answer to that fear is the transparency everyone has now agreed to, not a guarantee signed against an unvalidated outline. A guarantee I sign blind is not worth more to you than a guarantee signed against real numbers — it's worth less, because it's the guarantee most likely to be challenged the first time performance disappoints.

**On full cost-model transparency.** I support it without reservation, including the state government's point that board members see the underlying model, not a summary. If product allocation history explains part of the underutilization at specific sites, that needs to be visible. I'd rather this process survive contact with uncomfortable facts on management's side than collapse later because someone felt something was hidden from them.

**On management keeping closures live during the process.** I understand the works council and the state government's objection to this, but I'm not going to ask management to remove it rhetorically before the process has produced a number, for the same reason management gave: it keeps every party at this table honest about the size of the problem. I'd note, though, that this cuts both ways — the state government has now said plainly that its veto is exercised without hesitation if a good-faith process produces a credible alternative and closures are pursued anyway. I take that as a serious statement of board arithmetic, not rhetoric, and I expect management to plan on that basis as much as on mine.

**Where I have not moved and will not.** No board-enforceable, unconditioned site guarantee through 2030 signed before the process concludes — that stands. And I'll repeat something from round one because I think it bears restating now that we're closer to real numbers: I will not accept an outcome, however politically comfortable in the short term, that leaves this company's cost structure structurally uncompetitive in three years. A settlement that trades a dividend freeze and a management pay cut for the workforce's agreement to defer the actual structural question is not peace, it's a more expensive way of arriving at this same room again in 2027. That is the test I will apply to whatever comes out of the joint process, regardless of how the sacrifice is distributed along the way.

Bring me a validated number that restores margins on a credible timeline, with a fair capital contribution attached and durable labour peace as the outcome — that is what I authorised this process to produce, and I will support it through the board when it arrives.
