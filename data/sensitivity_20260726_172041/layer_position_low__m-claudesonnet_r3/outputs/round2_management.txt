**On simultaneous signing of guarantee and contribution.** I understand the logic — you don't want to bleed first and hope second. What I *can* accept is this: the joint costing process runs, produces a jointly-validated number, and the guarantee and the contribution are drafted and signed together *at the end of that process*, once both sides have agreed the number closes the gap. That's not "contribution first, guarantee maybe" — it's "process first, signature simultaneous." **On full cost-model transparency.** Agreed, without reservation. If the model shows product allocation decisions contributed to underutilisation at specific plants — and at some sites, it will — I'm not going to hide from that. German labour cost per vehicle versus our competitive set is real and it isn't explained away by allocation history. Test both, as the works council said. I accept that discipline.

**On the four levers you outlined.** Working-time reduction with partial pay compensation, accelerated voluntary separation sized against attrition, deferred variable comp, capacity/shift adjustments — these are the right categories. I'll be blunt about where I'm skeptical: working-time reduction with pay compensation has a way of becoming cost-neutral for the company rather than cost-*reducing*, which defeats the purpose. If the compensation offset eats most of the saving, you haven't closed a gap, you've relabelled it. I want that tested rigorously in the joint process, not waved through because it sounds structurally serious. Same with voluntary separation — "sized against demographic attrition" needs a real timeline, because attrition-only headcount reduction on the scale I need could take seven or eight years, and I don't have seven or eight years of shrinking European volume to wait it out.

**On the union's demand for a number from the family holding, now.** I don't control that commitment and won't speak for the family holding beyond what they've already said. Bring me the costed package through the joint process, with real transparency both ways, a genuine capital contribution with a figure attached, and a timeline that gets me the bulk of the savings within two to three years, not eight — and closures become negotiable per plant, potentially off the table entirely where targets are met.
