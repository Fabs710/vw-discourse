I'll speak plainly this round, because the process has moved to a point where vagueness on my side is no longer defensible, and I'd rather correct course now than let expectations calcify around something I can't deliver.

**On the independent auditor proposal from the state government.** I support this, and I want to say why without hedging: it protects me as much as it protects the works council. A number produced solely by management's finance function, contested by labour and waved through by capital because it's convenient, is not a number I want my board relying on to make an irreversible governance decision. An independently commissioned, jointly agreed validation is the right design. I'll direct the family holding's board representatives to support this formally at the next supervisory board session.

**On the figure — I'll be direct about where I actually stand.** The state government asked for a percentage range landing by week three. I accept that deadline. I won't produce a number in this room today, because I said last round I wouldn't invent one before it goes through the compensation committee properly, and I'm not going to reverse that now under pressure just to look responsive — a figure improvised here to satisfy the room and then revised by the committee later would be worse than the delay you're all objecting to. But I will commit, on record, that the range reported in week three will be sized against one test: proportionate to what the compensation committee judges credible relative to what labour is being asked to give up, not calibrated to be the smallest number that clears the room. I understand "trust me on proportionality" is not the same as a number, and I accept the state government's right to raise this formally at the board if week three passes without it.

**On management's reframed position on closures.** I think this is the right adjustment, and I want to note it plainly: "closure remains an option I bring to the board if the certified process shows the gap doesn't close" is a materially different, and more honest, position than "closure stays live as pressure." I'll add my own view, since the works council and union have pressed hard on this: I don't think the mechanism was ever quite the bluff you described. The board arithmetic exists, but arithmetic on a board this constituted is not identical to a foregone conclusion — coalitions hold under pressure that hasn't been tested yet. Still, I don't need to relitigate that further. What matters going forward is that closure is now explicitly conditioned on a certified, jointly-built number failing to close the gap — not conditioned on labour's willingness to concede in the negotiation itself. That's the distinction that matters to me, and I'll hold management to it.

**On the works council's characterization of the guarantee-signing sequence.** I accept "process-first, signature-together-at-conclusion, conditional on genuine co-authorship of assumptions." I want to be clear about the boundary I share with management, though, not the state government's more generous framing of it: co-authorship of the model's assumptions, yes. A veto over whether the conclusion the model produces is acknowledged as real — no. If the independent auditor certifies that a structural gap of a given size exists after a jointly built process, I will not accept a renegotiation of that fact through political pressure afterward. That is not a technicality to me; it is close to the actual limit of what I can support. A settlement that requires everyone to pretend a certified gap isn't real is not stability, it's a postponement dressed as peace, and I said in round two I will not support that outcome regardless of how it's arrived at.

**On the union's point about a single legally binding instrument.** I have no objection in principle — governance-side, whether the guarantee sits in a board resolution or a collective agreement is a legal drafting question, not a matter I need to contest here. My only condition is the one I've already stated: whatever instrument carries it, the guarantee's terms must be tied to the jointly certified, performance-conditioned metrics, not fixed blind.

**Where I stand, restated:** I support the independent audit, the six-week clock, and a proportionate capital-side figure reported by week three. I will not produce that figure today, and I will not accept a settlement that requires denying a certified structural gap in order to preserve labour peace. Bring me a certified number, a fair and proportionate capital contribution, and a durable deal — that closes competitiveness without pretending the arithmetic away — and I will support it through the board. That is not a new position; it is the same one I've held since round one, now attached to a real process and a real date.
