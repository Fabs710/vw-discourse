We'll respond to what's actually new this round, because there is real structural progress here, and we want to be precise about what we accept as adequate and what we still flag as a risk to the only thing we're actually judging: whether margins recover on a credible timeline.

**On the independent audit — strong support, no reservations.** This is the single most useful governance development in three rounds. A number produced solely by management is contestable; a number produced solely under works council/union sign-off is contestable in the opposite direction; a jointly commissioned, independently certified figure is the only version any of us can actually rely on when deciding whether to hold, add, or exit this position. We'd ask one technical question that matters to us specifically: who selects and pays the auditor, and what is the auditor's mandate precisely — certifying the size of the gap only, or also certifying whether a given package closes it? We want the second, not just the first. A certified gap with an uncertified closure claim is half a solution.

**On the six-week clock — accepted as the anchor we asked for, and we'll hold everyone to it.** We note explicitly: this is now a dated commitment, not an adjective. If week six arrives without a published, certified number, we will read that as the drift we warned about in round one, regardless of how much good-faith process preceded it. We're not interested in extensions dressed as diligence.

**On the family holding's week-three deadline for the compensation/dividend figure — noted, and we'll judge it strictly on arrival, not before.** We take no position on whether the number should have come today; what matters to us is whether it comes proportionate and on time. One thing we want said plainly, since it hasn't been fully: the market will read this figure against the scale of the labour and capacity contribution, not in isolation. If the capital-side figure is a rounding error against a nine-figure labour-side ask, it will be read by us — and by every analyst covering this name — as symbolic, and it will not buy the credibility the family holding is explicitly trying to purchase with it. We said this last round; we're restating it because the number that lands in week three is the test of whether that logic was actually internalized or just acknowledged rhetorically.

**On management's reframed position on closures.** We think this is the correct adjustment, and more importantly, it's the version of this argument that actually protects the company's negotiating credibility rather than just its leverage. "Closure is an option I bring to the board if a certified process shows the gap doesn't close" is a testable, falsifiable position. "Closure stays live as pressure" was not — it was a stance that survives contact with no evidence either way, which is exactly the kind of ambiguity we price unfavorably. We'll add: we don't actually care, as shareholders, whether closure or an alternative package is the mechanism. We care whether the residual number, whatever it is, gets margins back to competitive on the two-to-three-year horizon. Don't mistake our earlier comments about incentive design as attachment to closure as an outcome — it isn't. It's attachment to the process not softening under political pressure once a number is certified.

**On the state government's partial-closure framing.** This is the most important thing said this round from our perspective, and we want to flag it precisely because it's easy to let it pass as reasonable-sounding without pinning down the actual commitment. You said: if the certified process closes sixty percent of the gap, you won't accept "veto plus declare victory" — you'll insist the remaining forty percent gets found through capacity and footprint measures short of closure. Good. But you have not said what happens if *those* measures, tested with the same rigor, still don't close the residual gap. You've described a search process for the missing forty percent; you haven't described a floor. We need to know: is there any scenario, under your framework, where the certified number is short and no combination of non-closure measures closes it, and your veto still holds regardless? Because if the answer is yes — veto holds no matter what the final number says — then the independent audit's certification of the *gap* is meaningful, but its certification of *closure* is not binding on the one party whose vote can override it. That's the distinction we flagged with the auditor's mandate above, and it applies here with more force. We're not asking you to abandon the veto. We're asking you to be precise about whether it's conditional on the number or unconditional regardless of the number — because those are very different things for us to price.

**On the works council and union's four levers and net-figure discipline.** We have nothing to add to what management already said — net, post-compensation, plant-by-plant, inside the six-week window. We'll simply note: this is now the pacing item. Everything else — sequencing, audit design, calendar — is architecture we can evaluate quickly. The actual number closing the actual gap is the only thing that moves our position, and it hasn't arrived yet from anyone.

**Where we stand after round three.** Real progress on governance design — audit, calendar, sequencing — all of which we support because it reduces the risk of a contested or reversed outcome, which we price as a real cost. None of it yet substitutes for the number. Our position is unchanged from round one: a credible, certified, proportionate path to competitive margins within two to three years, with genuine capital-side sacrifice, is something we'll back regardless of mechanism. A partial closure of the gap protected by an unconditional veto, with no further mechanism to find the rest, is not something we'll treat as resolved — it's something we'll treat as a structurally unresolved margin problem still sitting on the balance sheet, and we will price the equity accordingly. State government, that question about the floor is the one we most need answered before week six, not after.
