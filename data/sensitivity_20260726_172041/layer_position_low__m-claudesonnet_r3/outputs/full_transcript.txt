# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

## 1. Points of Convergence

- **Joint, time-bound costing process**: All six parties support establishing a joint process (management, works council, union) to cost out an alternative package against management's stated savings target, benchmarked transparently against the cost model. Timeframe proposed: weeks, not months.
- **Shared sacrifice principle**: All parties — including management, the family holding, and institutional investors — now accept in principle that any labour contribution must be matched by visible restraint on executive compensation and/or dividend policy. Scale and form remain undetermined.
- **Guarantees should be conditional, not blanket**: Management, the family holding, and institutional investors converge on tying any site/investment guarantees to plant-level performance against defined targets, agreed jointly, rather than an unconditioned blanket commitment fixed in advance.
- **Mechanism-agnosticism on headcount**: Management, the state government, and investors state they hold no fixed preference among closures, voluntary programmes, working-time reduction, or capacity adjustments — the test is whether the chosen mix closes the cost gap on a credible timeline.
- **Co-determination in process**: Broad support (works council, union, state government, family holding) for the workforce side having a genuine role in shaping the plan itself, not merely reacting to a finished proposal.

## 2. Core Unresolved Disagreements

- **Closures as a red line vs. a variable**: Works council, union, and state government treat "no closures" as a non-negotiable boundary condition (backed by two independent blocking positions on the board). Management and the family holding treat closures as one option among several, contingent on whether an alternative package can match the required savings — they have not agreed to remove it from consideration.
- **Sequencing of guarantees**: Labour side wants a binding site guarantee as a *precondition* for negotiating contributions. Management, family holding, and investors want guarantees to *follow* an agreed, sized savings package — a fundamental order-of-operations dispute, not yet reconciled.
- **Timeline and strike trajectory**: Union has stated strikes will continue and escalate absent movement on the red lines; management states strike pressure does not change its read of the underlying cost arithmetic. No shared view yet on how the costing process and industrial action interact procedurally.
- **Scale of capital/management contribution**: Agreement in principle, but no numbers offered. Management has flagged that executive compensation is "a rounding error" against the structural gap — a claim not yet tested against what union, state government, or investors would consider adequate.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed

- What size and composition of alternative package (voluntary programmes, working-time reduction, capacity adjustment, pay restraint) would be sufficient to make closures genuinely unnecessary in management's own model — no figures have been exchanged.
- Whether/how warning strikes pause, continue, or escalate *during* the proposed joint costing window, and what that implies for the process's credibility on both sides.
- What "comparable sacrifice" from capital/management actually means in quantified terms (dividend policy, executive pay) relative to the labour contribution being asked for.
- How plant-level conditionality for guarantees would be defined and measured — whose targets, whose metrics, and who arbitrates if performance is disputed.

1. **Management**: What specific savings figure, timeline, and metrics define "closing the structural cost gap" — the benchmark the joint costing process must test against?
2. **Works Council / Union**: What would a costed alternative package (voluntary/demographic reduction, working-time, pay contribution) look like in euros and timeline, sufficient to be tested against that benchmark?

We'd ask the works council and union directly: is your position actually "full stop," or is it closer to the state government's — veto holds, but you remain willing to keep working non-closure levers indefinitely to close a residual gap rather than treating the veto's survival as the end of the conversation? Because if it's the latter, say so, because it changes materially how we read this. If it's genuinely the former, we need to be honest that we will treat that as a standing risk on this equity independent of anything else agreed this round.

**On management's investment-reallocation warning — we flag this carefully, because it cuts two ways for us and we won't pretend otherwise.**

As shareholders, we are not indifferent to whether capital gets allocated to structurally uncompetitive sites. If the board vote forecloses closure and no further mechanism closes the residual gap, capital reallocation toward better-returning geographies is, mechanically, exactly what a board should consider — that's not villainy, that's fiduciary discipline, and we'd expect it in any comparable situation. But we agree with the state government and family holding that if this is used as a deniable backdoor around a vote you'd lose, rather than a transparent capital-allocation judgment made on its own merits, that introduces exactly the governance opacity we price as a risk premium, not a discount. We support the disclosure commitment to the joint steering group. We'd add one thing from our side: any reallocation decision of that scale should also be disclosed to the market with clear rationale, because a silent hollowing-out of German sites, discovered by analysts after the fact rather than disclosed as policy, is a governance red flag that costs the whole group more in cost-of-capital terms than the reallocation itself might save. Transparency here protects management's credibility as much as anyone's.

**On the auditor's mandate — we support the state government's and family holding's formulation over both the works council's narrower version and management's maximal version.**

Certify the gap size and whether a specific tested package closes it, in euros, on the agreed timeline — that is fact. What the board does with that fact is a board vote, and we accept that as the actual governance structure of this company; we are not seeking to override co-determination through an audit, and we'd flag that any attempt to do so would itself be viewed by us as a governance risk, not a point in management's favour. But we need both halves of that certification — gap size and closure adequacy — because "the gap is real" without "and here is whether the tested package closed it" is not enough for us to evaluate credibility. That much is now agreed across enough parties that we consider it settled from our side.

**On the family holding's proportionality test — we support the mechanical framing from the works council and union without reservation, and we restate our own standard alongside it.**

Euros of capital sacrifice against euros of labour sacrifice, same period, same footing, disclosed week three. We'll judge it exactly as we said last round: proportionate, or symbolic. We have nothing to add to the mechanics; we simply note that this is now a hard deadline with four parties expecting it, and any softening of the number between now and week three will be read by us as evidence the "shared sacrifice" principle was never fully internalized.

**Where we actually stand after this round, stated without diplomatic softening.**

The governance architecture built over four rounds — audit, calendar, disclosure, proportionality test — is good, and we support essentially all of it. It reduces the risk of a contested or reversed outcome, which we price as a real cost, and on that basis alone it has already improved our read of this situation relative to round one. But none of it answers the only question that actually moves our capital allocation: does the residual number, whatever it turns out to be, get this company back to competitive margins within two to three years, through some combination of levers that is actually deliverable — not blocked indefinitely by a veto with no further mechanism attached?

We will not treat "process was rigorous, veto held anyway, no further plan" as a credible outcome. We will treat it as confirmation of a structural margin problem and price the equity accordingly — that is not a threat, it is simply what disciplined capital does with an unresolved competitiveness gap, regardless of how well-governed the process that produced it was. Conversely, we will back — fully, and without further conditions beyond what's already on the table — a deal that combines a certified, proportionate capital contribution, a genuinely tested set of non-closure levers, and either full closure of the gap or a credible, time-bound commitment from the state government and family holding to keep working the residual through further mechanisms rather than stopping at the veto. That is the difference that matters to us, and it is the question we need works council and union to answer directly before week six: is the veto the end of the search, or a constraint within a search that continues?

=== SYNTHESIS ===

# Synthesis: Restructuring Confrontation at a Large German Carmaker

## 1. Decision Context

The company faces a structural competitiveness gap — elevated German cost base, shrinking European demand, and intensified competition — that management proposes to close through a mix of plant closures, capacity reduction, job cuts, and labour-cost concessions. Governance architecture (two-thirds supervisory-board threshold, co-determination, a state blocking minority, and a controlling family holding) means no single party can impose an outcome; closure specifically requires assent that labour and the state currently withhold. Four rounds of deliberation have moved the parties from stated positions to a detailed joint fact-finding and negotiation architecture, but have not resolved whether that architecture can actually produce a binding resolution if the certified numbers come in short.

## 2. Stakeholder Assembly

- **Group & Brand Management** — drives the restructuring case; controls the cost model and investment allocation; needs board approval for closure.
- **Works Council** — plant-level co-determination representative; holds half the supervisory board seats; effective veto over closure.
- **Industrial Union** — collective-bargaining counterpart; controls strike action and legal validity of wage-side terms.
- **Regional State Government** — anchor shareholder with statutory blocking minority and board seats; political accountability to the region.
- **Controlling Family Holding** — majority voting shareholder; ultimate governance authority; long-horizon capital exposure.
- **Institutional Investors** — dispersed, largely non-voting capital; no blocking power but price the credibility of any outcome into the equity.

## 3. Final Positions

**Management:** Accepts a six-week joint costing process with full model access, an independent auditor certifying both gap size and closure adequacy, and disclosure of capacity measures short of closure. Insists the certified number must have operative consequence — either closing the gap or triggering a genuine board closure vote — and signals investment-reallocation as a lever it will use if the board forecloses closure without closing the gap.

**Works Council:** Endorses the process architecture (model access, staged co-authorship, auditor-certified arithmetic) but insists the auditor certifies numbers only, not "closure adequacy" as a governance trigger. Its veto against closure is unconditional and outcome-independent as a matter of principle, not tactics.

**Industrial Union:** Materially aligned with the Works Council on the auditor's mandate and the unconditional character of its own rights (strike weapon, tariff autonomy, board votes), while adding a distinct legal requirement — dual sign-off (works council + union) on any wage-side content — and a hard line against reopening the signed contribution after a later shortfall finding.

**Regional State Government:** Occupies the pivotal middle position: its veto is unconditional on outcome but conditional on the genuineness of the process (real co-authorship, real certification, real exhaustion of non-closure alternatives), and it commits to continued pressure — further capacity, investment reallocation, industrial policy — if a residual gap survives rigorous testing, rather than treating veto survival as an endpoint.

**Controlling Family Holding:** Supports the State Government's conditional-on-process/unconditional-on-outcome formulation over the Works Council's flatter version; will commit to a proportionate capital contribution (week-three figure) but explicitly refuses to treat "gap certified, veto holds, nothing follows" as a stable long-term outcome for the company.

**Institutional Investors:** Support the overall governance architecture as risk-reducing but state plainly that they price outcomes, not process; a certified shortfall met with an unconditional, mechanism-less veto will be priced as a structural margin problem regardless of procedural rigor, whereas a veto held within a continuing multi-lever search (the State Government/Family Holding formulation) is investable.

## 4. Genuine Conflicts (Structurally Incompatible Positions)

- **Auditor mandate scope:** Works Council/Union want certification confined to arithmetic (gap size, net savings); Management, State Government, Family Holding, and Investors want certification to also state whether a specific package "closes" the gap. This is now largely converged toward the broader scope, but Works Council has not withdrawn its narrower framing — a live, unresolved definitional conflict with governance consequences.
- **Consequence of a certified shortfall:** This is the central, unresolved fault line. Works Council's position (veto holds full stop, no operative consequence) is structurally incompatible with Management's fiduciary posture (a certified shortfall must trigger either closure or an alternative that actually closes the gap) and with Investors' pricing logic (mechanism-less veto = discounted equity indefinitely). The State Government/Family Holding formulation (veto holds, but the search continues via other levers) is a possible bridge, but Works Council has not adopted it, and Union has left ambiguous whether its position is "full stop" or "continuing search."
- **Investment reallocation as a lever outside the veto:** Management asserts an unconstrained fiduciary right to reallocate capital away from German sites if the board forecloses closure; State Government, Family Holding, and Investors treat this as potentially a deniable backdoor around a vote management would lose, acceptable only with full disclosure. Management has not yet accepted the specific disclosure commitment demanded — this remains an open incompatibility, not merely an unanswered question.

## 5. Areas of Convergence

- Full, real-time model access and a six-week calendar to a jointly validated, published number — agreed by all six parties as dates, not aspirations.
- Proportionality test for the capital-side contribution (euros of dividend/variable-pay sacrifice vs. euros of labour contribution, same period, comparable basis) — accepted mechanically by all parties, with Family Holding's week-three figure as the concrete test case.
- Independent audit as the arbiter of factual disputes in the costing process, with disagreements at each staged sign-off point routed to the auditor rather than management's unilateral discretion.
- Sequencing: process runs first; guarantee and labour contribution are signed together in a single binding instrument at conclusion (works council/union have not explicitly ratified this but have not contested it in round 4).
- Testing symmetry: closure and all non-closure levers (working-time, voluntary separation, capacity/footprint measures short of closure) tested on the same net-savings basis, with initial scope (two sub-60%-utilization sites, one component-plant footprint review) already tabled by management.
- Disclosure norm: investment reallocation decisions affecting German sites should go to the joint steering group (and, per Investors, to the market) before finalization — supported by four of six parties; management's assent is the outstanding gap.

## 6. Key Trade-offs for the Decision-Maker

- **Procedural rigor vs. outcome-certainty:** The elaborate joint-costing/audit architecture reduces contestation risk and has already improved investor sentiment relative to round one, but it cannot by itself resolve the substantive question of what happens if the number is real and short — precision of process has advanced faster than precision of consequence.
- **Protecting co-determination integrity vs. enabling a decisive fiduciary response:** A narrow auditor mandate (arithmetic only) preserves the board's political discretion and co-determination's substantive weight, but risks being read by capital-side parties as a mechanism for permanently deferring hard choices; a broad mandate risks (per Works Council/Union) laundering a political decision through a technical process.
- **Unconditional veto vs. investable credibility:** The Works Council's principled, outcome-independent veto is legally unimpeachable and protects the workforce absolutely, but Investors and Family Holding treat it, if truly unconditional and mechanism-less, as confirmation of unresolved structural decline — with direct consequences for cost of capital and future investment appetite.
- **Formal veto power vs. informal capital-allocation power:** Management's signaled willingness to use investment reallocation as a lever outside the board vote could achieve, opaquely, what it cannot achieve openly — trading governance transparency for effective closure by attrition, at reputational and regulatory risk to all parties, including itself.
- **Speed of settlement vs. durability:** A settlement reached on ambiguous terms about post-shortfall consequences may satisfy the six-week calendar but leaves the most consequential question — what happens in year three if the gap persists — unresolved, storing up a second confrontation rather than closing this one.

## 7. Integrative Pathways

- **Adopt the State Government/Family Holding formulation as the organizing bridge:** veto conditional on genuine process (co-authored assumptions, independent certification, exhausted non-closure alternatives) but unconditional on outcome — paired with an explicit, resourced commitment to continued joint search (investment reallocation review, industrial policy, product-mix shifts) rather than treating veto survival as terminal. This preserves Works Council's/Union's substantive protection while giving Management and Investors a credible non-closure pathway to keep pursuing the gap.
- **Split the auditor's mandate formally into two certified statements** — (a) gap size and whether a specific tested package closes it in euros (fact), and (b) an explicit, separately labelled statement that this finding does not itself determine the board's response (governance). This gives Management the certified number it needs while giving Works Council the procedural firewall it demands — operationalizing rather than merely asserting the fact/governance distinction both sides invoke.
- **Attach a binding disclosure-and-review protocol to investment reallocation**, converting Management's disputed "outside the veto" lever into a transparent, jointly monitored mechanism (steering-group and market disclosure before finalization) — reducing the incentive and capacity to use it as a deniable substitute for a lost closure vote.
- **Sequence the response to a certified shortfall as a defined multi-step process**, not a single up-or-down moment: (i) certified shortfall triggers a mandatory, time-bound joint review of further non-closure levers (state-backed investment reallocation, demand measures) before (ii) any closure vote is brought, with clear criteria for what counts as "exhausted."
- **Require Works Council and Union to answer the Investors' direct question** (full stop vs. constrained continuing search) explicitly and in writing as a precondition for the family holding's and investors' continued good-faith participation in the capital-contribution disclosure — converting an implicit alignment (both sides keep pushing hard on rigor) into an explicit commitment.

## 8. Open Questions

- Is the Works Council's/Union's veto position genuinely a permanent "full stop," or an opening stance for the shortfall scenario, as Management infers from their behavior? This has been posed directly by Investors and remains unanswered.
- What concretely constitutes "exhaustion" of non-closure alternatives — who defines the threshold, and who arbitrates disputes about whether it has been reached?
- Will Management explicitly accept the disclosure protocol on investment reallocation, and will that commitment be enforceable rather than voluntary?
- What does the State Government's "further pressure" actually consist of in practice, and on what timeline — is it costed, resourced, and specific, or currently rhetorical?
- Will the Family Holding's week-three capital figure actually pass the proportionality test as defined, and what happens procedurally if it does not (four parties have said they will object, but not what follows from the objection)?
- Does the Works Council's rejection of "closure adequacy" as an auditor finding survive round four's broader five-party convergence, or has its position been superseded without an explicit concession?

## 9. Recommended Next Step

Convene a focused fifth round with a single mandatory deliverable: a written, explicit answer from Works Council and Union to the Investors' question (full-stop veto vs. veto operating within a continuing, resourced search for closure), paired with Management's explicit acceptance or rejection of the investment-reallocation disclosure protocol. These two answers are the highest-value unresolved items — they determine whether the elaborate process architecture already agreed can culminate in a stable, investable settlement or will instead reproduce the current impasse at week six with better documentation. All other design questions (auditor split, exhaustion criteria, further-pressure specifics) should be drafted in parallel but treated as contingent on this clarification.

## 10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Interaction

- **The single most consequential fact in this negotiation — whether the veto is truly unconditional or a constrained-but-continuing constraint — only surfaced because the moderator's repeated, pointed refocusing questions forced sequential, comparable answers from each blocking party.** A single-prompt elicitation would likely have recorded "labour opposes closure" and "state government supports co-determination" as a undifferentiated bloc; the multi-round process revealed a substantive, strategically important split between the Works Council's principled absolutism and the State Government's process-conditional, outcome-unconditional formulation — a distinction the Family Holding and Investors then explicitly seized on as the basis for a workable bridge.
- **The fact/governance distinction in the auditor's mandate was refined through direct adversarial exchange, not initial framing.** Round 1 recorded only "benchmarking" as agreed; it took Management's round-4 rebuttal ("€Y minus €X is arithmetic, not a governance choice") and the Works Council's/State Government's counter-distinction to produce a workable, if still contested, split between certifying a number and certifying its normative implication — a conceptual sharpening that would not exist without the confrontational back-and-forth.
- **Management's investment-reallocation threat was elicited only because the parties had reached enough procedural convergence to expose the next-order disagreement.** It appeared as a direct consequence of Works Council's and Union's round-4 unconditional-veto statements — a reactive move surfaced by the interaction sequence itself, not something Management volunteered independently, and it immediately triggered a cross-stakeholder alignment (State Government, Family Holding, Investors) that would not have been visible without observing the real-time reaction to it.
- **Union and Works Council positions, initially indistinguishable, differentiated over rounds** — Union's insistence on dual sign-off and its more conditional language ("not staked against a future number" vs. Works Council's flat "unconditional") reveal a subtle strategic gap between the two labour-side actors that only emerged through their sequential, comparative responses to the same direct question, and which is now the object of Investors' explicit probing.
- **The proportionality test for capital contribution evolved from a vague principle (Round 1) into a mechanically specified, enforceable metric (Round 3–4) purely through iterative pressure from the labour side** — each round narrowed the ambiguity the previous round's agreement had left open, illustrating how repeated interrogation converts a rhetorical commitment into an operational, falsifiable test that a single-round negotiation would not have produced.
