We start from a simple fact: management wants to break a promise. The job-security agreement was not a courtesy — it was a binding pledge to this workforce in exchange for years of concessions, flexibility and productivity gains that the company banked and then reported as improved margins elsewhere in the group. Now, at the first sign of serious pressure, the answer is to tear that up and put plant closures on the table. That is not restructuring. That is a breach of trust, and I will say plainly: it is unacceptable.

Let me be direct about where we stand.

First, plant closures are off the table. Not "difficult to accept" — off the table. This board cannot reach a two-thirds majority for closure, full stop, and everyone in this room knows it. If management brings a closure plan to a vote, it will fail. That is not a threat, it is arithmetic under co-determination, and I would rather we save everyone's time and not pretend otherwise.

Second, no compulsory redundancies. Tens of thousands of families are not a line item to be netted against a return-on-sales target. If the company wants headcount reduction, it will happen through voluntary programmes, early retirement, attrition, and negotiated working-time reduction — instruments we have used before and will use again. What it will not do is walk workers out the door by force.

Now, I am not going to stand here and pretend the workforce owes nothing. I have read the cost numbers. I know German plant costs are out of line, and I know we cannot simply say "no" to everything and expect this company to still be building cars here in 2030. That is precisely why my mandate is not blind obstruction — it is protecting jobs and sites over the long run, not just this quarter. So yes, there is room to talk about a genuine employee contribution: temporary pay restraint, working-time and shift-pattern changes, capacity adjustments at specific lines, deferral of bonuses — all of that is negotiable, and I will bring it to my members, hard as that conversation will be.

But that contribution only happens on conditions, not as a blank cheque handed over before closures are ruled out. What I need on the table, as a precondition for any serious negotiation, is:

- A binding, board-enforceable site guarantee for every German plant through 2030 — not a management promise, a contractual one.
- Explicit exclusion of compulsory layoffs, with all headcount reduction routed through voluntary and demographic mechanisms.
- Real co-determination in shaping the restructuring plan itself, not consultation after the fact on a plan already fixed — we decide the "how" together, not just react to it.
- Concrete, funded product and investment commitments tied to German sites, so that any concession we make today buys real future volume, not just a cost cut that gets us back here again in two years.

Without those four elements, there is nothing to negotiate — there is only confrontation. And you should know: the warning strikes already underway are not a bargaining tactic I intend to wind down as a gesture of goodwill. They will continue and escalate until this board sees a plan that does not contain closures or forced layoffs. I did not come here looking for consensus for its own sake. I came here to defend this workforce, and I will use every seat, every vote, and every worker on the shop floor to do it.
