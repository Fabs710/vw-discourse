I want to address the sequencing dispute directly, because I think both sides have moved closer than they're admitting, and I want to close that gap rather than let it calcify into a new stalemate.

**On sequencing.** Management's revised position — process first, signature simultaneous at the end — is closer to the works council's demand than management is presenting it. The works council wants guarantee-and-contribution signed together; management now accepts that too, just after the joint process concludes rather than before it starts. I think that's actually the right sequencing, and I'll say so even though it means telling the works council something they may not want to hear: I will not insist on a guarantee signed before the costing process produces a validated number. Signing a board-enforceable, site-by-site commitment against an unvalidated outline of levers is not something I can defend to my own board colleagues, let alone the public, if it later turns out the number didn't close the gap and we're back here in eighteen months with the guarantee already spent. That would be worse for the workforce, not better — a guarantee that isn't credible is not a guarantee, it's a document that gets challenged the first time the company misses a target.

What I will insist on, and where I side with the works council and union without qualification: the *guarantee and the contribution get signed in the same instrument, at the same moment*, once the process concludes. Not guarantee now, contribution later. Not contribution now, guarantee as a reward later, contingent on performance metrics defined unilaterally by management afterward. Simultaneous, at the end of a transparent process — that's the design I'll use my board seats to enforce.

**On transparency.** I fully back the works council and union's demand for full, unfiltered access to the cost model. I'll go further: as a shareholder with board seats, I expect to see the same model, and I will not accept a summary deck presented to the board while the underlying assumptions stay with management's finance function. If product allocation decisions contributed to underutilization at specific plants — and management has now conceded this is likely true at some sites — that needs to be visible and accounted for in the benchmark, not buried. A benchmark shaped partly by decisions the workforce and this board had no part in is not a neutral test.

**On the family holding's contribution — I'm going to press this harder than round one.** Management said they expect to bring "a specific figure" on executive compensation restraint. The family holding said "direction is not in question" but committed to no figure. I'll say plainly: that gap needs to close this round, not next. My support for the joint process, and my willingness to push the workforce side toward difficult contributions, is conditional on a concrete number from ownership — not after the costing exercise concludes, but in parallel with it, so the workforce isn't asked to negotiate blind while capital's side stays an abstraction. I understand the family holding treats dividend policy as a board matter it won't pre-negotiate in this forum. I understand that. But "this forum" includes two of my board seats, and I will raise this formally at the next board session if a figure doesn't appear here.

**On management's "closures stay live to keep pressure honest" argument.** I understand the logic — remove the threat and you remove the incentive to negotiate hard. But I want to be honest about what that logic costs. It asks the workforce to negotiate a costed alternative in good faith while the axe stays visibly raised over their heads, and it asks me to tolerate that as a "mechanism" rather than call it what it also is: a pressure tactic aimed partly at my board seats and the works council's, not just at getting a good number. I won't pretend that doesn't work as leverage — it might. But understand my position clearly: if this process produces a real, costed alternative that closes the gap on a credible timeline, and management or the family holding still attempts to bring a closure plan to the board vote afterward, my veto is exercised without hesitation and without further negotiation. That's not a threat I'm making to extract something now — it's simply what happens, because I do not have discretion to spend down the region's economic anchor on a number that a good-faith process has already shown can be closed another way.

**On timeline.** Weeks, as everyone has said. I will chair, or co-chair, the oversight of this window from the board side specifically to prevent it becoming the "drift" the investors rightly fear — I share that concern completely, a slow-walked process is as dangerous to jobs as a rushed closure, because it burns the company's credibility and cash position while nothing gets resolved.

**Where I stand after round two:** I've moved on sequencing — process first, simultaneous signature at the end, not guarantee-first. That is a real concession on my part toward management's position, and I'd like it recognized as one. What has not moved, and will not: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full co-determination in building and validating the model, and a concrete capital-side figure this round, not a promise of one later. Bring me those, and I bring the board votes needed to make a negotiated deal durable. Without the capital figure specifically, I don't see how the union brings this to its members, and I don't see how I ask them to try.
