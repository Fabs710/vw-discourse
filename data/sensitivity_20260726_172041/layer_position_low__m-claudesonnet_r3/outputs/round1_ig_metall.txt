We stand with the works council on the two red lines — no closures, no compulsory redundancies — and I want to be precise about why that isn't just solidarity rhetoric, it's a bargaining reality management needs to internalise now, not after weeks of warning strikes escalate into something harder to walk back.

On the four preconditions: broadly yes, but let me sharpen them from the union's side, because the works council operates at board and plant level, while we hold the collective agreement and the strike mandate — and those are not the same lever.

First, the wage agreement has lapsed and the peace obligation has ended. That is not a footnote, it's the operative fact of today's meeting. Every week this drags out without a credible counter-offer from management, warning strikes continue and will widen in scope. That is not a threat either — it is the plain consequence of members who have watched years of productivity gains, flexibility concessions, and now watch closures floated as the answer.

Second, we already put a counter-proposal on the table before this session: labour-cost contribution, capacity reduction, an employee financial contribution. That offer stands, but it was never unconditional and it is not a starting point for management to now negotiate down. It was calibrated against site and job security. If management reads our openness as weakness and comes back asking for more concessions while offering less protection, that reading will cost them — the warning strikes will escalate accordingly.

Third — and this is where I want to be sharper than the works council's framing — any labour-cost contribution must be matched by comparable sacrifice from management and ownership. Pay restraint, bonus deferral, working-time reduction from the workforce is only fair if executive compensation, dividend policy, and shareholder returns move in parallel. I have not yet heard management or the family ownership put a single euro of their own on the table. Until they do, "shared sacrifice" is a phrase, not a fact. We will not accept a deal where workers absorb the entire adjustment while returns to capital continue undisturbed. That is a line we will not cross, full stop.

Fourth, on the site guarantee and investment commitments — agreed, and I'd add: it must be enforceable and specific, tied to product allocation and volume commitments plant by plant, not a vague pledge revisited at the next downturn. We've been here before with promises that didn't survive the following cycle.

So to be direct about where this leaves us: we are not moving off no-closures and no-compulsory-redundancies — those aren't opening positions, they're the boundary of what we can bring back to our members at all. Within that boundary, we've already shown willingness to negotiate real cost contribution. What we need from this room today is not applause for our flexibility — it's management and ownership showing their own contribution, in numbers, on the table, now. Until that happens, the strikes continue, and they will escalate, not de-escalate, the longer this goes unanswered.
