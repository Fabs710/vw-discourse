I've listened carefully, and I'll be brief, because that is how this holding conducts itself in these matters — through the board, not through public sparring.

**On the substance.** I did not authorise this restructuring push to win a fight with the workforce. I authorised it because the return-on-sales numbers on our core brand are unacceptable for a company of this scale, and because I have seen this cost gap discussed for years without durable closure. The family holding's horizon is measured in decades, not quarters — we have sat through worse cycles than this one. But a horizon that long is exactly why I take structural underperformance seriously: it compounds. So let me be plain about where I stand, because I don't intend to restate it repeatedly as this negotiation continues.

I back management's diagnosis of the cost gap. I am not wedded to any particular instrument for closing it. Closures, working-time changes, voluntary programmes, capacity adjustments — I have no doctrinal attachment to any of these as a method. What I care about is whether the outcome restores competitiveness durably and does so without inflicting a level of conflict on this company that destroys more value than it recovers.

**On the union's shared-sacrifice point.** This lands with me as it did with management and the state government. I will not defend, and will not have the family holding's name attached to, a restructuring where labour absorbs the full adjustment while capital returns continue untouched. That is not a moral concession on my part so much as a governance judgment: a package perceived as one-sided will not hold, will invite exactly the escalating strike action the union is already signalling, and will cost us more in brand damage and lost production than whatever it saves. I will raise dividend policy and capital discipline with the supervisory board as part of this process. I make no commitment yet on scale or form — that is a board matter, not something I will pre-negotiate in this forum — but the direction is not in question.

**On the works council's four preconditions.** I will not support a board-enforceable site guarantee through 2030, unconditioned, covering every plant, agreed before the savings package is even sized. That inverts the logic of what an owner can responsibly commit to. Guarantees should follow from a credible plan, tied to plants and lines that demonstrate they can meet competitive targets — management's formulation on this point is the right one, and I will not move off it. What I will support is real, structured co-determination in shaping the plan itself — not as a courtesy, but because a plan built jointly is more likely to actually hold when implemented, and durability is what I care about most.

**On the state government's proposed joint, time-bound costing process.** This I support. A defined window, real numbers from management's cost model, tested jointly against the alternative package labour has proposed — that is exactly the kind of process that could produce something durable rather than another two years of drift followed by the same confrontation. I would rather see weeks spent on that than months spent on a strike that damages the asset all of us depend on.

**Where I will not move.** I will not accept an outcome that leaves this company's cost structure structurally uncompetitive in three years' time — that is value destruction dressed as peace, and I have seen that film before in this industry. Nor will I accept a process that becomes a public battle of ultimatums, board leaks, and prolonged strikes that damage the brand and the share register's confidence in this company's governance. If this deliberation cannot produce a credible path within a reasonable window, the family holding will not simply wait passively — but I would much prefer, and expect, that it does not come to that.

Bring me a plan that restores margins and buys genuine labour peace, built through the joint process on the table. That is what moves me. Ultimatums from any side do not.
