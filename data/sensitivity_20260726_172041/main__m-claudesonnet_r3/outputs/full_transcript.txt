# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

**1. Areas of Emerging Agreement**

- All six parties accept the underlying premise: the core brand's cost base is not competitive, and structural change of some kind is required. No one is disputing the diagnosis.
- There is convergence on process, if not yet substance: management, the state government, the family holding, and institutional investors have all explicitly stated they are *not* wedded to closures as the only path — provided an alternative delivers comparable, durable savings.
- All parties agree that any non-closure alternative must be **quantified**: a euro figure, capacity/shift reductions, and a timeline, comparable to what closures would deliver.
- There is broad (though not identically worded) support for the principle of **shared sacrifice** — visible movement on executive compensation is accepted by management and the family holding as a legitimate component of any package, not merely a workforce-side ask.
- Everyone acknowledges the board arithmetic as real: closures require a two-thirds supervisory majority that neither labour (50%) nor the state (20% plus veto) will supply absent a negotiated alternative.

**2. Core Disagreements Still Open**

- **Scale vs. sufficiency:** Management and investors have not yet seen a quantified counter-proposal and remain skeptical it will close the full cost gap; the Works Council and Union have not yet produced the euro-figure/capacity modelling requested.
- **Dividend policy:** The Union and state government treat ownership distributions as a legitimate part of the shared-sacrifice ledger; the family holding regards this as a board-level matter not to be settled by table pressure, and investors explicitly resist treating dividends as a symbolic bargaining chip rather than a function of the restructuring's actual credibility.
- **Nature and duration of site/job guarantees:** Works Council and Union want binding guarantees to 2030; management, the family holding, and investors want guarantees conditioned on demonstrated competitiveness and investment commitments, not unconditional multi-year pledges.
- **Compulsory redundancy and closures:** Works Council and Union maintain this is an absolute red line; management has not withdrawn closures as an option and states it will not sign a "deferral dressed up as a settlement" regardless of the political/board obstacles.

**3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed**

- No party has yet specified what precisely labour would receive **in exchange** for a labour-cost contribution — the "buys something durable" question (guarantee scope, duration, conditionality) remains abstract on both sides.
- The mechanism for tying site guarantees to "competitiveness outcomes" has not been defined — what metric, measured how, over what period, with what consequence if unmet.
- The relationship between management/ownership sacrifice and its *scale* relative to workforce concessions has not been quantified by anyone — it has been agreed to matter, not sized.
- No party has addressed sequencing: whether guarantees are offered contingent on savings being delivered, or savings are contingent on guarantees being granted first — a chicken-and-egg question that will need resolution.

**4. Refocus for Round 2**

The table has converged on a shared test: **a side-by-side, quantified comparison of (a) savings from closures and (b) savings from capacity reduction, working-time measures, and attrition-based headcount reduction — including magnitude, timeline, and durability.**

Round 2 should focus on:
1. Works Council and Union presenting their costed counter-proposal in the terms management, the state, and investors have requested.
2. Management responding with its own comparative modelling of the closure scenario, to enable genuine side-by-side evaluation rather than assertion.
3. Initial specificity from management/family holding on the shape (not necessarily final figures) of an executive/ownership contribution.
4. Beginning to define what conditional site guarantees would actually look like in structure, so the guarantee-for-concession trade can be evaluated concretely rather than in principle.

1. **Confirmation or rejection** by Works Council and Union of the reciprocal three-week pause structure, specifically addressing the State's direct question of whether the sticking point is substance or public framing.
2. **Concrete parameters for the joint audit**: who conducts independent verification if required, what constitutes "durability" (time horizon, sensitivity to demand/competitive shifts), and how disputes over the comparison will be resolved.
3. **Movement toward a magnitude or range** for ownership/executive contribution, even preliminary, to give the workforce parties something concrete to weigh against their own numbers.
4. Clarification of the **precise exceptional circumstances** that would permit guarantee renegotiation, so this can be tested rather than argued in the abstract.

# Synthesis: Restructuring Confrontation at a German Carmaker

## 1. Decision Context

A German carmaker's core passenger-car brand faces a structural cost and overcapacity crisis. Management proposes plant closures, headcount reductions, and labour-cost cuts to restore competitiveness ahead of a 2028–2030 product/investment cycle. Germany's co-determination law requires a two-thirds supervisory-board majority for any closure — a threshold neither shareholder representatives (family holding, state, investors' non-voting capital) nor management can reach without either labour's assent or labour's abstention/absence. A regional state government holds an additional blocking-minority veto. The collective-bargaining peace obligation has lapsed, making strikes a live instrument. Over four rounds, the parties moved from disputing diagnosis to negotiating a shared audit process — until Round 4 surfaced a governance fact that reframes the entire negotiation: the workforce's board veto is not contingent on the audit's findings.

## 2. Stakeholder Assembly

| Stakeholder | Basis of standing |
|---|---|
| **Group & Brand Management** | Executive authority, fiduciary duty, initiator of restructuring; no formal veto but controls agenda and board resolutions |
| **Works Council** | 50% of supervisory board seats (co-determination); effective veto over closures |
| **Industrial Union** | Collective-bargaining authority; strike capability; acts in concert with Works Council on the board question |
| **Regional State Government** | Blocking-minority stake with statutory veto; political accountability to a legislature |
| **Controlling Family Holding** | Majority voting control; multi-generational asset exposure; decisive board weight |
| **Institutional Investors** | Largely non-voting capital; legitimacy derives from market/financing consequences, not board votes |

Formal governance power is concentrated in Management, Works Council/Union, the State, and the Family Holding. Investors hold legitimacy without a vote — their leverage is reallocation of capital and cost of capital, not board arithmetic.

## 3. Final Positions (as of Round 4)

**Management**: Accepts the asymmetric durability standard and the named secondary reviewer, but insists a genuine structural shortfall must remain answerable by a real board resolution, not permanently foreclosed by labour's fixed vote. Rejects the characterization of such a resolution as "theatre" if grounded in genuine audit findings, while disclaiming intent to force a resolution it knows to be pure pressure.

**Works Council**: Holds an unconditional, non-negotiable position: no audit finding, however structural, will convert its 50% board seats into support for closure or compulsory redundancy; any shortfall is to be met with deeper/longer alternative measures only. Supports the audit and secondary reviewer as tools to size the alternative package and test management's cost claims, not as a gate to its vote. Will end restraint on mobilisation immediately if Management brings a resolution perceived as pressure rather than substance.

**Industrial Union**: Fully aligned with the Works Council on the fixed-vote position and its rationale (bargaining-position protection rather than mere solidarity); adds that any threshold number would become a public opening bid it cannot afford to concede. Demands the secondary reviewer and asymmetric durability standard be locked in this round.

**Regional State Government**: Recalibrates its own veto use in light of labour's declared fixed position — shifting from "performing a threshold test with no operative effect" to concentrating its leverage on shaping the alternative package's adequacy. Retains, but downgrades the practical relevance of, its "exhausted alternatives" standard, and will not support a closure resolution it judges to be politically motivated given known-losing arithmetic.

**Controlling Family Holding**: Accepts labour's veto as fact but explicitly refuses to let it substitute for the family's own independent judgment on long-term value; will support a genuine board discussion of closures if structural shortfall is found, regardless of labour's stated fixed position, while continuing to oppose manufactured/expected-to-fail resolutions. Commits to a dividend/executive-contribution magnitude range within the three-week window, sized to be "comparable" to workforce sacrifice.

**Institutional Investors**: Reframes the entire negotiation given labour's fixed vote: with no functioning closure fallback, the alternative package's own adequacy — not the board threshold debate — becomes the sole test the market will apply. Will price the company as a governance-constrained laggard if the package is merely "politically achievable" rather than genuinely gap-closing; maintains dividend policy is a capital-need test, distinct from labour's parity test.

## 4. Genuine Conflicts (Structurally Incompatible Positions)

- **Fixed veto vs. conditional threshold logic**: Works Council/Union's position ("no finding converts our votes, any shortfall means extend, never redundancy") is logically incompatible with the four-party convergence ("substantial majority closes gap keeps closures off table; structural shortfall reopens them genuinely"). These are not negotiating distance apart — they operate on different axes and cannot be reconciled by more data from the audit, only by a change in one side's position or by the closure question becoming moot through board arithmetic.
- **Fiduciary resolution vs. "theatre" framing**: Management's stated obligation to bring a genuine post-audit resolution regardless of expected outcome is incompatible with labour's (and increasingly the Family Holding's own conditional) standard that any resolution known to fail is illegitimate pressure. The Family Holding has flagged the incoherence in labour's own combination of positions (fixed vote + delegitimizing any resulting vote), but has not resolved it.
- **Dividend evaluation frameworks**: Labour's parity/proportionality test and Investors' capital-need test are stated by all sides to be non-convertible into one another — both "gates" must be passed independently for the package to reach ratification (labour) and market credibility (investors), and no mechanism yet reconciles a case where a figure passes one test and fails the other.

## 5. Areas of Convergence

- Universal agreement on the underlying diagnosis (uncompetitive cost base) and the need for a quantified, audited comparison between closure and non-closure paths.
- Full six-party support for an independent, jointly-appointed auditor with a pre-fixed terms of reference, and (as of Round 4) a **named secondary reviewer** issuing a binding supplementary opinion on contested methodological findings.
- Unanimous, explicit support for an **asymmetric durability standard**: irreversible measures (closures) bear a higher evidentiary burden than reversible ones (working-time/attrition measures) — with Management securing agreement that the reverse "ratchet" risk (temporary measures becoming permanent) must be tested with equal rigor.
- Agreement on process architecture: reciprocal pause, no shadow closure preparation, joint communication to the workforce, single simultaneously-ratified package rather than staggered concessions.
- Convergence (Family Holding, Management, Investors) that a dividend/executive-pay magnitude range will be presented within the three-week window as a "shared sacrifice" signal, even though its sufficiency will be judged by incompatible tests.

## 6. Key Trade-offs for the Decision-Maker

- **Depth of alternative package vs. speed to resolution**: A package deep enough to satisfy Investors' and the Family Holding's adequacy test may require concessions (duration, magnitude of contribution) that Works Council/Union resist naming in advance, risking indefinite negotiation absent a forcing mechanism.
- **Guarantee flexibility vs. credibility**: Binding, irrevocable guarantees preferred by labour increase ratification odds but reduce the company's ability to respond to a genuine downturn; conditional/revisitable guarantees preferred by capital-side actors preserve flexibility but risk being read by labour as guarantees in name only.
- **Bringing a closure resolution vs. preserving trust**: Management gains a "record" and preserves fiduciary optionality by holding a vote even if it is arithmetically certain to fail, but risks the State's and Family Holding's confidence, and triggers labour's declared end of restraint — a trade-off between formal governance completeness and negotiated stability.
- **Naming a threshold vs. protecting negotiating position**: Precision on "how much gap-closure is enough" would let all parties evaluate the package objectively, but both labour parties view naming a number as self-defeating; the absence of a number preserves labour's position but leaves Management, Investors, and the Family Holding unable to design toward an agreed target.
- **Public/private framing of the pause**: A publicly board-minuted suspension of closures increases labour's confidence and de-escalation, but the capital-side parties see it as pre-judging the audit's outcome and constraining fiduciary and market signaling.

## 7. Integrative Pathways

1. **Decouple the audit's evidentiary function from its board-conversion function.** Explicitly frame the audit as determining the depth and shape of the alternative package (which all six parties value) while acknowledging — rather than resolving — that its findings do not mechanically produce board votes for closure. This removes the false premise that consensus on "structural shortfall" implies a shared trigger, and lets the table focus energy on package adequacy, which Investors, the State, and the Family Holding have all now identified as the operative question.
2. **Symmetric ratchet mechanism.** Bind Works Council/Union's alternative measures to the same asymmetric-durability logic they demand of closures: a joint monitoring body (already agreed in principle) reviews measures against pre-agreed trigger metrics for *both* deepening (if conditions worsen) and tightening/unwinding (if conditions improve), addressing Management's, the State's, and Investors' shared concern about a one-way ratchet.
3. **Early, credible ownership/executive figure as a confidence-building move.** Given that the Family Holding has already committed to presenting a magnitude range inside the window, sequencing this earlier rather than later could partially de-risk the labour side's suspicion of asymmetric sacrifice and create space for Works Council/Union to engage more concretely with package depth, even without naming a threshold themselves.
4. **A defined, narrow protocol for "genuine vs. political" board resolutions.** To resolve the Management/labour/State standoff over the "regardless" framing, the table could pre-agree objective criteria (e.g., resolution only follows a completed audit and secondary-reviewer process, with defined minimum shortfall documentation) under which a board vote is presumptively legitimate — reducing the risk of ex post relabeling as "theatre" while still constraining pure pressure tactics.
5. **Two-gate dividend disclosure.** Since labour's parity test and Investors' capital-need test cannot be merged, present the ownership contribution with both framings explicitly reconciled in the same document (absolute cash figure plus capital-adequacy rationale), allowing each constituency to apply its own test transparently rather than contesting the figure's framing after the fact.

## 8. Open Questions

- Will the Works Council/Union commit to supporting the tightening of "temporary" measures if conditions improve (the ratchet question), or does this remain unanswered/refused?
- What standard should the alternative package be judged against, given that the closure lever is arithmetically inoperative regardless of audit findings — the State's political test, the Family Holding's stewardship test, and Investors' market test all differ and are unreconciled?
- What precisely constitutes "contested on defined methodological grounds" sufficient to trigger the secondary reviewer?
- What is the actual dividend/executive-contribution magnitude, and can any single figure satisfy both the labour parity test and the investor capital-need test simultaneously?
- If Management brings a post-audit closure resolution it expects to lose, what protocol determines whether this is legitimate governance or "leverage theatre" — and is that determination made prospectively or only in retrospect?

## 9. Recommended Next Step

Convene Round 5 with two mandatory deliverables rather than further position restatement: (1) a direct, specific answer from the Works Council and Industrial Union to the ratchet question, including any conditions under which they would support unwinding a temporary measure; and (2) a joint statement — from Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors — of the standard by which the alternative package's *adequacy* will be judged, now that all four have separately acknowledged the closure threshold has no operative board-arithmetic effect. In parallel, finalize the auditor's terms of reference (asymmetric durability definition, secondary-reviewer trigger definition) within the agreed sub-deadline, and bring forward the ownership/executive magnitude figure to enable early testing against both labour's and investors' distinct evaluation frames.

## 10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Interaction

- **The central governance fact was withheld until leverage made it strategically necessary to state.** Three rounds of apparent process convergence obscured, rather than resolved, the fact that labour's veto is unconditional. This only surfaced once the four-party "shared threshold" language became concrete enough that Works Council and Union judged it necessary to correct the record — a dynamic invisible to any single-turn design of the negotiation, where the threshold framing might have been presented as settled.
- **The revelation triggered independent, non-uniform recalibration by each of the other four parties**, rather than a unified response — the State reallocated its leverage toward package-shaping; the Family Holding asserted independent judgment regardless of labour's fixed position; Investors reframed the entire market test around package adequacy alone; Management sharpened its demand for an actual answer to structural failure. This differentiation would not have emerged from a single-shot elicitation of each party's "position on closures" — it required the sequential, reactive structure of rounds to expose how each actor's leverage model depends on the others'.
- **The audit's function migrated during the discussion** from "a mechanism to determine whether closures are justified" to "a mechanism to size an alternative package that is now, practically, the only outcome" — a reframing produced by the interaction itself (particularly Investors' and the State's responses), not by any party's original proposal.
- **The asymmetric durability standard emerged as a genuinely co-authored concept**: Works Council introduced it, Union sharpened it, Management partially conceded it while introducing the mirror-image ratchet concern, and the State and Family Holding then held labour accountable to the same symmetry — a concept no single party's opening position contained in this complete, bidirectional form.
- **The Family Holding's articulation that labour's position contains an internal tension** (fixing votes in advance of facts while treating any resulting vote as illegitimate) is a critique that could only surface after the fixed-vote declaration and the "theatre" objections had both been placed on the record — a second-order argument dependent on the accumulated transcript, not derivable from any party's initial stance.
