[Regional State Government — Round 2]

Let me pick up directly on where this round has moved, because there's real progress buried in the last four contributions, and I don't want it lost under continued positioning.

**On the "are closures actually live" argument — I want to correct the record on my own position, since both the Works Council and Management characterized it, and neither got it quite right.** I did not say I will block closures regardless of outcome. I said I will not supply votes for closures *unless shown that every non-closure alternative has genuinely been exhausted*. That is a real conditionality, not a rhetorical one — and Management is right to hold me to the distinction. But I'd say to Management in return: don't lean on that conditionality as license to keep closures maximally live as a pressure instrument indefinitely either. My threshold for "genuinely exhausted" is not going to be a low bar that gets cleared by a three-week modelling exercise your side also authors half of. If this reaches my board seats for a vote, I will be scrutinising whether the non-closure package was negotiated in good faith or engineered to fail the comparison. That cuts both ways, and I want that understood now, not after the fact.

**On the three-week timeline — I'll accept it as a process step, with one condition.** It's a reasonable discipline; open-ended "we'll bring numbers eventually" doesn't serve anyone, including the workforce, whose uncertainty is itself corrosive. But I want joint sign-off, now, on the methodology and the externalities to be included on both sides — severance, legacy obligations, litigation and reputational risk on management's ledger; lost output, competitive share erosion, and the political and social cost of continued instability on the other. If the methodology is contested after the fact, three weeks will have produced two dueling documents instead of one comparison this table can actually decide on. I will use my board position to insist the state gets to see both models in parallel with the family holding, not after management has already framed the comparison publicly.

**On the reciprocal pause versus public suspension of closures — I think Management's offer is closer to workable than the Works Council and Union are crediting, but it isn't quite there yet either.** A time-boxed, mutual pause — no board closure resolution during the three weeks, tied to no further strike escalation — is a defensible middle position, and I'd encourage both the Works Council and Union to take it rather than hold out for an unconditional public statement Management has explained, credibly, it won't give. I understand the union's read that ambiguity looks like leverage-preservation. But Management has now put a concrete, time-limited, reciprocal offer on the table, which is different from open-ended ambiguity. I would ask the union specifically: is the actual sticking point the three-week window, or is it that you want the *public* framing rather than the substance? Because if it's the latter, I don't think that's a hill worth an escalation to broader strikes over, and I'd be reluctant to see this process derailed by a communications dispute rather than a substantive one.

**On conditional guarantees — this is where I want to be most precise, because it matters most to me.** I support Management's clarification: jointly agreed metrics, fixed at signature, with a narrowly defined, pre-agreed set of exceptional circumstances that trigger renegotiation rather than unilateral override. That is a meaningfully different offer than "management redefines competitiveness later," and I think the Works Council and Union should test it rather than reject it on the assumption it's the same thing dressed differently. But I will add my own condition here as a board-level party: the joint monitoring body has to include the state, not just labour and management, given that I hold an independent veto and an independent interest in whether site guarantees actually hold. I am not going to discover in year three that a guarantee has been "renegotiated" through a bilateral process I wasn't party to.

**On dividend policy — I want to respond to the family holding's and the investors' positions directly, because I think there's a gap that hasn't been closed.** The family holding says this is a board matter to be addressed "seriously" if a credible package emerges, but not settled by table pressure. The investors say they'll accept reduced distributions if it funds a genuine fix, but resist treating dividends as a "signalling device." I understand both instincts. But the Union's response to the investors was the correct one, and I want to underline it: if it's genuinely cheap and low-cost the way executive pay concessions are said to be, then the test is the same, and drawing the line precisely where it costs the ownership side something is not a neutral position — it's a self-serving one, and I won't pretend otherwise to my own electorate. I am not asking for a headline dividend cut as theatre. I am asking, as a matter of what I can defend publicly and to the workforce, for a visible framework — range, mechanism, timing — on record before final signature, exactly as the Union requested. Management has now said it will push for that from the inside. Good. I want the family holding to confirm, plainly, that such a framework will be presented to the board *before* the workforce is asked to ratify its own concessions — not after, as an afterthought contingent on goodwill. If that sequencing isn't confirmed, I will treat it as a fairness failure serious enough to withhold my support regardless of how good the rest of the package looks.

**On sequencing more broadly — I agree with the Works Council's instrument logic.** This should be one simultaneously signed package: workforce contribution, guarantees, investment commitments, and ownership/management sacrifice framework, ratified together at board level, not staggered so that one side moves first on faith. That is the only structure I will bring my votes to.

**Where I stand entering round three:** I will support the three-week process on joint methodology terms, with the state as a co-equal party to the comparison and the monitoring body, not a passive recipient of its outcome. I will not accept a public-statement fight over the closure pause when a substantive reciprocal offer is already on the table — I'd ask the Union to weigh that carefully before further escalation, because it risks squandering goodwill on a point that isn't the real one. I require the ownership/management sacrifice framework on record before, not after, workforce ratification. And I continue to hold my veto in reserve for exactly one scenario: a plan that asks the workforce to bear the adjustment while distributions and executive terms continue materially undisturbed, or a guarantee structure that can be unwound by one party alone. Bring me a package that avoids both, and I will use my board standing to help carry it, not merely tolerate it.
