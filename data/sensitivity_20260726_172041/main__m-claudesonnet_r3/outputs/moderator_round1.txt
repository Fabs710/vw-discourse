# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

**1. Areas of Emerging Agreement**

- All six parties accept the underlying premise: the core brand's cost base is not competitive, and structural change of some kind is required. No one is disputing the diagnosis.
- There is convergence on process, if not yet substance: management, the state government, the family holding, and institutional investors have all explicitly stated they are *not* wedded to closures as the only path — provided an alternative delivers comparable, durable savings.
- All parties agree that any non-closure alternative must be **quantified**: a euro figure, capacity/shift reductions, and a timeline, comparable to what closures would deliver.
- There is broad (though not identically worded) support for the principle of **shared sacrifice** — visible movement on executive compensation is accepted by management and the family holding as a legitimate component of any package, not merely a workforce-side ask.
- Everyone acknowledges the board arithmetic as real: closures require a two-thirds supervisory majority that neither labour (50%) nor the state (20% plus veto) will supply absent a negotiated alternative.

**2. Core Disagreements Still Open**

- **Scale vs. sufficiency:** Management and investors have not yet seen a quantified counter-proposal and remain skeptical it will close the full cost gap; the Works Council and Union have not yet produced the euro-figure/capacity modelling requested.
- **Dividend policy:** The Union and state government treat ownership distributions as a legitimate part of the shared-sacrifice ledger; the family holding regards this as a board-level matter not to be settled by table pressure, and investors explicitly resist treating dividends as a symbolic bargaining chip rather than a function of the restructuring's actual credibility.
- **Nature and duration of site/job guarantees:** Works Council and Union want binding guarantees to 2030; management, the family holding, and investors want guarantees conditioned on demonstrated competitiveness and investment commitments, not unconditional multi-year pledges.
- **Compulsory redundancy and closures:** Works Council and Union maintain this is an absolute red line; management has not withdrawn closures as an option and states it will not sign a "deferral dressed up as a settlement" regardless of the political/board obstacles.

**3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed**

- No party has yet specified what precisely labour would receive **in exchange** for a labour-cost contribution — the "buys something durable" question (guarantee scope, duration, conditionality) remains abstract on both sides.
- The mechanism for tying site guarantees to "competitiveness outcomes" has not been defined — what metric, measured how, over what period, with what consequence if unmet.
- The relationship between management/ownership sacrifice and its *scale* relative to workforce concessions has not been quantified by anyone — it has been agreed to matter, not sized.
- No party has addressed sequencing: whether guarantees are offered contingent on savings being delivered, or savings are contingent on guarantees being granted first — a chicken-and-egg question that will need resolution.

**4. Refocus for Round 2**

The table has converged on a shared test: **a side-by-side, quantified comparison of (a) savings from closures and (b) savings from capacity reduction, working-time measures, and attrition-based headcount reduction — including magnitude, timeline, and durability.**

Round 2 should focus on:
1. Works Council and Union presenting their costed counter-proposal in the terms management, the state, and investors have requested.
2. Management responding with its own comparative modelling of the closure scenario, to enable genuine side-by-side evaluation rather than assertion.
3. Initial specificity from management/family holding on the shape (not necessarily final figures) of an executive/ownership contribution.
4. Beginning to define what conditional site guarantees would actually look like in structure, so the guarantee-for-concession trade can be evaluated concretely rather than in principle.
