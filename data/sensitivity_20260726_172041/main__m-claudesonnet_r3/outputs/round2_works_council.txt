[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

Let me respond to the specifics on the table, because I don't want this round to dissolve into everyone repeating their opening position with slightly softer adjectives.

**On quantification — yes, and here is a first cut.** Management wants a euro figure, a capacity number, and a timeline. Fair. Based on the attrition profile across our sites — retirements, voluntary departures, and natural turnover over the next four to five years — combined with a negotiated working-time reduction model and a capped, time-limited bonus/labour-cost contribution from the workforce, we believe a capacity reduction in the range of what management has floated for closures is achievable without a single forced redundancy. I am not going to hand over our full internal modelling in a public multi-party session before we've agreed the framework it will be judged against — that would be negotiating against ourselves. But I will commit, concretely, to producing detailed site-by-site attrition curves, shift-pattern options, and a costed labour-contribution package within the timeframe this table sets, provided it is evaluated against the same rigor management applies to its own closure case. If management's closure numbers include externalities — severance costs, legacy obligations, brand and reputational damage, the cost of the fight itself that the family holding and the investors have both flagged — those need to be in the comparison too. A closure "saves" nothing if the true cost, including eighteen months of strikes and litigation, is higher than the alternative.

**On the family holding's and investors' framing that they are "not wedded to closures":** I welcome that being said out loud, on the record, by both. It matters. But I want to note something that hasn't been said plainly yet: if neither the family holding nor the investors are committed to closures as such, and the state and labour together hold the board votes to block them outright, then closures are not actually a live option here — they are a negotiating device management is using to pressure the scale of our concessions. I'd rather we all acknowledge that plainly, because it changes the character of this discussion. We are not choosing between closures and an alternative. We are negotiating the size and shape of a non-closure settlement. Let's stop pretending otherwise.

**On conditional guarantees tied to "competitiveness outcomes":** I hear management, the family holding, and the investors converging on this language, and I want to flag a serious problem with it before it hardens into the assumed framework. "Guarantees conditioned on demonstrated competitiveness" sounds reasonable in the abstract. In practice, it hands management the unilateral power to declare, eighteen months from now, that a site hasn't hit some metric they define, and reopen the closure question anyway — making the guarantee worthless exactly when workers need it most. I will not sign a guarantee with an escape clause controlled entirely by the party asking us to trust them after they just tried to break the last one unilaterally. If there is to be conditionality, it has to be **mutual and jointly defined**: the workforce delivers the attrition and cost contribution we've outlined, on a schedule; in exchange, management and ownership deliver binding site guarantees and investment commitments on the same schedule. Both sides bound, both sides monitored — through co-determination structures, not management's internal reporting alone. That is a negotiation. A guarantee that management can dissolve by redefining "competitiveness" later is not a guarantee — it's a postponement of the same fight the family holding itself says it wants to avoid.

**On sequencing:** this needs to be resolved directly, because right now everyone is implicitly waiting for someone else to move first. My answer: this has to be a single, simultaneously signed package — not guarantees first, not concessions first. We table the quantified attrition and contribution plan; management and ownership table the guarantee terms and investment commitments; the board ratifies both as one binding instrument. Anything that asks labour to deliver concessions now against a promise to "discuss" guarantees later is a fait accompli in slow motion, and I will not recommend it to the workforce.

**On dividend policy:** I want to push back directly on the investor position here. You've said executive pay concessions are "cheap" and you'll accept them, but that dividend policy shouldn't be treated as a bargaining chip "disconnected from whether the restructuring closes the cost gap." I understand the logic from a capital-allocation standpoint. But from where I sit, if workers are being asked to accept capped bonuses, reduced working time, and accelerated retirement as their contribution to closing that gap, then a distribution policy that continues undisturbed while the workforce absorbs income reduction is not a technical capital question — it is the single clearest signal of whether "shared sacrifice" is real or rhetorical. The family holding said, correctly, that a lopsided deal "will not hold." I agree completely, and that cuts both ways: if the workforce is asked to fund the gap through wage restraint while distributions continue at prior levels, that deal does not survive contact with our membership, no matter what guarantees are attached. This doesn't need to be settled today, but I want it clearly on record as a condition of a durable settlement, not a courtesy.

**On timing and pressure:** I'll say plainly — the warning strikes continue while this is unresolved. That is not an escalation beyond what I said in round one; it is the plain consequence of no unilateral withdrawal of the closure threat having happened yet. If management wants the strikes to de-escalate while we work through the quantified comparison, the fastest way to achieve that is a public, board-minuted statement that closures are off the table for the duration of this negotiation, in good faith, while the alternative package is built. Absent that, our members will reasonably read continued ambiguity as management keeping the closure threat live as leverage, and the mobilisation will continue accordingly.

To summarize where I stand entering round three: I will bring detailed numbers on attrition, working time, and a bounded labour-cost contribution. I require binding, mutually conditioned site and job guarantees to 2030, signed as one instrument alongside our contribution, not sequenced after it. I require visible movement on executive pay and distribution policy, not as symbolism but as the precondition for our membership accepting anything. And I require closures to be explicitly and publicly taken off the table for the duration of this process as a condition of continued good-faith engagement on our side.
