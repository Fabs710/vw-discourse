# Moderator Summary — Round 3

## 1. Areas of Agreement

- **Reciprocal pause accepted by all parties**, subject to conditions: board-minuted (not merely verbal), no shadow closure-preparatory steps (no site consultations, supplier renegotiations, or press/analyst briefings premised on closure), and explicitly limited to process during the three-week window — it does not bind any party's final substantive position.
- **Joint workforce communication** (Management, State, Family Holding) confirming pause terms and "no shadow preparation," to be issued this week — agreed by all who addressed it.
- **Independent, jointly-appointed, jointly-funded auditor**, reporting to all six parties simultaneously, with binding terms of reference fixed *before* either side submits numbers — now supported without reservation by every party, including Management. Investors added a requirement that findings be externally defensible/disclosable; State and Family Holding want visibility into the terms of reference before finalization; Management wants the terms of reference themselves fixed within week one.
- **Numerical trigger for "exceptional circumstances"**: Management has offered provisional categories (sustained multi-quarter European demand contraction beyond base case; verified widening cost-competitiveness gap) with exact figures to be developed inside the audit process. State and Family Holding insist the specific percentages/horizons be set or co-signed through the independent process, not asserted unilaterally by Management.
- **Dividend/ownership contribution**: Family Holding has committed to presenting a magnitude range (not just sequencing) within the three-week window, sized as "comparable order of magnitude" to workforce sacrifice. Investors accept this as a legitimacy test for labour/ratification purposes but explicitly reject it as their own test (theirs is capital-need/durability, not parity).
- **Executive compensation figure** to be brought alongside the modelling, sized against the actual package.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

- **What happens if the audited package falls short of closing the gap**, and by how much:
  - *Works Council / Union*: shortfall — regardless of size — should be met with deeper working-time reduction, longer attrition/contribution horizons, never redundancy or closure. This is held as a non-negotiable floor.
  - *Management*: will not pre-commit to any blanket rule; distinguishes modest shortfall (open to extension) from large/structural shortfall (closure remains live).
  - *State / Family Holding / Investors*: converge on a middle test — "substantial majority of the gap closed durably" keeps closures off the table; "material, structural shortfall" reopens the closure question genuinely, decided by the audit, not foreclosed by principle in advance.
- **Management's stated intent to bring a closure resolution "regardless" of an expected losing vote**: State and Family Holding characterize this as potential "leverage theatre" against the State's veto and labour, and warn it would damage Management's credibility/good faith. Investors side with State and Family Holding on this specific point while still insisting closures must remain a genuine live option if the audit shows structural shortfall. Management has not withdrawn the framing, only reasserted its fiduciary rationale.
- **Basis for judging ownership's dividend contribution**: Works Council/Union want it judged against workforce sacrifice (proportionality); Investors explicitly reject that as their test and will judge any redirected cash against capital-need/durability logic only.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- No party has yet specified **what counts as "durable"** in quantified terms (time horizon, recurrence, sensitivity to demand cycles) — this term is doing heavy work in every party's shortfall test but remains undefined.
- The **exact percentage/quarter thresholds** for both the demand-decline and cost-gap triggers are still outstanding — categories have been named, numbers have not.
- **Who arbitrates a disagreement between Management's and labour's models** if the auditor's findings themselves are contested by one side has been requested (Works Council, Union) but not yet answered procedurally.
- The **actual dividend/ownership magnitude figure** — and whether it will be judged sufficient by Works Council/Union/State versus adequate-to-capital-need by Investors — remains open; two different tests exist and have not been reconciled.
- **Consequence if Management proceeds with a closure vote perceived as "theatre"** — State and Family Holding have signaled this would affect their support, but no concrete mechanism (e.g., withholding votes, guarantee terms) has been specified.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

With the process architecture (pause, auditor, joint communication) now largely settled, the next round should move to **specification**:
1. Finalize and lock the **auditor's terms of reference** — including the definition of "durable," the dispute-resolution mechanism for contested findings, and external-validation standards — ideally within the sub-deadline Management proposed.
2. Attach **actual numbers** to the exceptional-circumstances triggers (percentage thresholds, time horizons, verification method).
3. Begin narrowing the **shortfall threshold question** — what percentage/degree of gap closure constitutes "substantial majority" versus "structural failure" — since three parties have now converged on this framing but none have quantified it.
4. Surface the **ownership magnitude range** and executive compensation figure as early as feasible, given multiple parties have tied their continued good faith to seeing this within the window, not at its end.
