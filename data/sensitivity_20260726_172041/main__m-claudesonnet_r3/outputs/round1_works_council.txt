We're not starting this discussion from a neutral place, so let me be direct about that first.

Management is proposing to break a job-security pledge that has stood for decades and to close German plants for the first time in this company's history. That is not a technical adjustment to a cost line — it is a unilateral attack on the foundation of trust between this workforce and this company. The warning strikes you've seen across our sites are not a negotiating tactic. They are the workforce telling you, in numbers management cannot ignore, that this proposal as tabled is unacceptable.

Let me be precise about where we stand.

**What is off the table:** Plant closures. Compulsory redundancies. Either of these, on its own, is a red line. I am not posturing when I say that — the co-determination structure means I do not have to posture. Any closure or relocation requires a two-thirds supervisory board majority. Labour holds half the board. We will not supply the votes. That is not a threat, it is arithmetic, and everyone at this table should plan accordingly.

**Why this matters beyond this year's balance sheet:** Tens of thousands of families depend on these sites. Communities are built around these plants. The job-security framework you're proposing to shred wasn't a gift from management — it was hard-won, and it's the reason this workforce has delivered flexibility, quality, and loyalty for decades. Tear it up unilaterally and you don't just lose this round of restructuring, you lose the cooperation you'll need for every transformation after this one.

**What I am prepared to discuss:** I am not going to pretend the company faces no cost problem — I'm not blind to overcapacity or to the competitive pressure from lower-cost producers. If management wants a serious conversation, I will engage seriously on capacity reductions that don't cost jobs through forced layoffs, on working-time models, on a negotiated and fairly shared labour-cost contribution, and on early retirement or attrition-based headcount reduction. But any of that is conditional, not unconditional. I need binding site and job guarantees running to 2030, and a genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring plan from here on — not a fait accompli handed to us for ratification.

**What I will not accept:** Being told the decisions are already made and our role is to manage the social consequences. That approach guarantees escalation — more strikes, a sustained public campaign, and a board that will not deliver the two-thirds majority management needs. If management wants a durable outcome rather than a prolonged war of attrition, the path is a negotiated alternative to closures, not a repeat of the announcement that provoked this crisis in the first place.

That is my starting position. I am ready to negotiate hard on everything except the closures and the forced layoffs.
