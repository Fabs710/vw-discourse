# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Named secondary reviewer for contested audit findings**: All five parties now support a pre-agreed, named secondary reviewer issuing a binding supplementary opinion within a fixed window when either side contests the primary auditor's finding on defined methodological grounds. Management adds a request that "contested on defined methodological grounds" be itself precisely defined, to prevent indefinite relitigation.
- **Asymmetric durability standard, in principle**: All parties accept that closure's irreversibility warrants a higher evidentiary burden than reversible alternative measures, and support writing this into the auditor's terms of reference.
- **A reciprocal asymmetry flagged by Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors**: that "temporary" labour measures must also face scrutiny on whether they will genuinely be tightened if conditions improve, or function as a one-way ratchet. This is raised as a direct question to the Works Council and Union, not yet answered.
- **No party disputes that the audit should proceed**, or that it should be genuinely independent, regardless of disagreement over what its findings will be permitted to trigger.

## 2. Core Disagreements

- **What the audit's findings are for**: Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors treat "substantial closure of the gap" vs. "structural shortfall" as a threshold with operative consequence for the closure question. The Works Council and Industrial Union state directly that no audit finding, however characterized, converts their board seats — their response to any shortfall is extended/deepened alternative measures, not redundancy or closure. This is now stated as fixed, not conditional.
- **Consequence of the fixed labour position, contested among the other four**:
  - The **State** now indicates its own veto use will shift toward shaping the alternative package rather than performing a threshold test with no operative effect, given labour's fixed 50%.
  - The **Family Holding** states it will still form and act on its own independent judgment about closures if a structural shortfall is found, regardless of labour's stated position, and views an indefinitely extendable shortfall as slow value destruction.
  - **Investors** state that with no functioning closure fallback, the entire weight of their judgment falls on whether the alternative package is adequate on its own terms — and that a package resulting only from fixed board arithmetic, rather than genuine adequacy, will be read as unresolved and priced accordingly.
  - **Management** asks Works Council and Union directly whether "always extend, never redundancy" constitutes an answer to structural failure at all, and states it cannot treat that as compatible with restoring competitiveness by 2030.
- **Management's conditional resolution ("regardless" framing)**: Works Council and Union state that a resolution brought as a pressure tactic — expected to fail, meant to force a public record — will end their side's restraint on mobilization immediately. Management maintains that bringing a genuine resolution after a genuinely bad audit result is a fiduciary obligation, not theatre, and objects to any standing threat that outcome-based relabeling would apply regardless of intent. The State and Family Holding align with labour's view of manufactured/expected-to-fail resolutions as illegitimate, but the Family Holding also states that labour cannot both fix its votes in advance of the facts and treat any genuine closure discussion as illegitimate theatre.
- **Threshold quantification**: Works Council and Union both decline to name any shortfall percentage, for related but distinct reasons (avoiding a target to "just clear," and avoiding a public bargaining anchor). This remains unresolved as a four-against-two dynamic heading into the audit's design.

## 3. Key Trade-Offs Still Unaddressed

- **The ratchet question is now squarely on the table but unanswered**: Will the Works Council and Union commit to supporting the tightening of "temporary" measures if conditions improve, or does "temporary" carry the same one-way character they attribute to management's language? No response has yet been given.
- **What the alternative package must achieve if it is the only realistic path**: If the closure lever is not functionally live given fixed labour votes, no party has yet specified what standard the alternative package must meet to be treated as genuinely resolved — by the State's political test, the Family Holding's value-stewardship test, and Investors' market test — versus merely "the best achievable given the arithmetic."
- **The definition of "contested on defined methodological grounds"** for triggering the secondary reviewer remains unspecified, per Management's request.
- **Dividend/executive pay magnitude**: still not quantified by the Family Holding or Investors; all labour and non-labour parties have stated tests for evaluating it once it appears, but no figures are yet on the table.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

Two concrete items need direct answers, not restatement of positions:

1. **From the Works Council and Industrial Union**: a direct response to the ratchet question — will previously-agreed "temporary" measures be tightened if conditions improve, and if so, under what mechanism or trigger?
2. **From all parties**: given that the closure vote may fail on arithmetic regardless of audit findings, what standard should the alternative package itself be judged against to be treated as adequate — since this is now, by multiple parties' own account, the operative question rather than the closure threshold?

The dividend/executive pay magnitude figure remains outstanding and due within the stated window.
