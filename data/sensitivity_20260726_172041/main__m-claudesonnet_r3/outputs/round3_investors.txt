I'll respond to where this round has moved, because several things said directly to us and about us need a precise answer.

**On the Family Holding's magnitude commitment:** this is a genuine improvement over "it's a board matter," and I want to acknowledge it as such rather than pocket it silently. "Sized as a genuine proportion of the total sacrifice, comparable order of magnitude" is directionally right and testable — we'll hold you to the "comparable order" standard when the range appears, not the vaguer language from round one. That said, I want to flag precisely what we will and won't accept when that range lands: proportionality to workforce sacrifice is the wrong benchmark for us to be judged on, and I want to say why now rather than dispute it in three weeks. Our test is not "did ownership sacrifice match labour sacrifice in size" — that is a fairness metric, and it matters to the Union and Works Council for good reason, but it is not the metric that determines whether we support the deal. Our test is: does the total package, including whatever ownership contributes, produce a credible, durable path to competitive margins. A large, symbolic dividend cut that doesn't correlate to actual capital need doesn't move us any more than a trivial one would satisfy the Union. So — support the proportionality test as a legitimacy and ratification question for labour; don't expect it to be our test too.

**On the independent auditor — full support, no reservations, and I want to add the specific thing we need from it.** Everyone has converged on jointly appointed, binding terms of reference, reporting to all six parties. Good. What hasn't been said clearly enough: the auditor's output needs to be structured so it can be disclosed, in substance, to the market once this concludes. If the number that emerges is presented internally as "closes the gap" but the methodology or the durability assumptions wouldn't survive an analyst's first question, we will find that out fast, and the market reaction will be worse than if the process had been transparent from the start. I'd ask that the terms of reference explicitly include a requirement that the findings be capable of external validation — not necessarily public in full, but robust enough that when this is disclosed to investors and rating agencies, it holds up. That protects every party at this table, including labour, because a package that gets picked apart publicly six months after signature is worse for job security than a rigorous one now.

**On the shortfall question — I want to be direct with the Works Council and Union: we do not accept the position that a shortfall never leads to closures or redundancy, full stop, regardless of size.** I understand why you're asking for that as a floor. But we said from round one that we would not support an outcome that leaves the cost base structurally uncompetitive, and an open-ended commitment to "extend and deepen measures indefinitely" if the audited package falls materially short is exactly the kind of outcome we cannot underwrite. We are aligned with the State and Family Holding's formulation here, not the Works Council's: substantial, durable closure of the gap — extend and deepen, no objection, we'd prefer that path and said so in round one. Structural, material shortfall — that is not a case where we support indefinite deferral dressed as patience, and we won't pretend otherwise to keep this room comfortable. I'd rather say this plainly now than have it read as bad faith later: if the number comes back well short and this table nonetheless signs a settlement calling it resolved, we will not treat that as a fix, and we will act on our own judgment regardless of what the board ratifies.

**On Management's "regardless" framing and the State's and Family Holding's pushback on it:** I think the State and Family Holding are right to push back on the idea of a doomed board vote used as political theatre — that would be value-destructive in exactly the way we'd penalize, because it manufactures the "eighteen months of strikes and litigation" cost the Works Council flagged in round one, for no operational gain. But I want to separate that from the underlying substantive point, which I do still support: if the audited numbers show a structural shortfall, the closure question has to remain a genuine live option, decided on the numbers, not foreclosed by a pre-negotiated principle that rules it out regardless of what the audit finds. Management should pursue that through the audited process and the board's genuine judgment, not through a symbolic vote designed to force blame onto other parties. Those are different things, and I'd ask Management to take the State's and Family Holding's correction seriously rather than defend the "regardless" framing on principle.

**On dividends more broadly — responding to the Union's challenge that fungible cash retained during restructuring funds the restructuring, so we can't argue both irrelevance and resistance:** that's a fair logical point on its own terms, and I want to concede it partially rather than repeat the earlier distinction unchanged. If a magnitude range is presented, sized to actual capital needs of the investment programme and severance-avoidance measures — not sized to match labour's number symbolically — we will support that redirection, because it's consistent with what we already said: reduced distributions in exchange for durable margin recovery. What we still resist is treating dividend policy as set by this table's negotiating dynamics independent of the capital structure math. If the Family Holding's number, once specified, is genuinely tied to funding needs identified in the audit, we're aligned with it. If it's calibrated instead to what will placate the workforce vote regardless of capital need, we'll say so, and it will affect our view of the package's discipline.

Where we stand entering the audit: independent, externally-defensible auditor — required. Ownership magnitude tied to capital need, not symbolic parity with labour sacrifice — our test, separate from the ratification test others are applying. Structural shortfall keeps closures genuinely open, not foreclosed by principle — non-negotiable for us. Vote-as-theatre — we side with the State and Family Holding against it. Bring us the audited number. That, still, is the only thing that moves us.
