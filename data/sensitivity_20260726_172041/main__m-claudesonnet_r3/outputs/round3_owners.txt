I've been asked directly, twice now, about magnitude — not just sequencing — of the ownership contribution, and I owe this table a substantive answer rather than a third repetition of "it's a board matter." Let me engage properly.

**On magnitude, specifically:** I confirmed sequencing last round — framework before ratification — and both the State and the Union have now said, correctly, that sequencing without magnitude is only half an answer. I accept that. Here is the honest constraint I'm working within: I cannot produce a number in this room that hasn't gone through the board, because doing so would pre-empt a governance process I do not have unilateral authority to short-circuit, even as controlling shareholder. That is not evasion — it is the actual structure of how this company is governed, and I would be misleading this table if I pretended otherwise. What I can tell you, and will commit to now: the range presented within the three-week window will be sized as a genuine proportion of the total sacrifice being asked of the workforce — not a token percentage designed to be defensible in a press release. If the audited package shows the workforce absorbing a contribution of a given order of magnitude over the horizon, the ownership framework will reflect a comparable order, not a rounding error beside it. I will personally hold the board to that standard, and I will say so at the table when the range is presented, not after.

**On the auditor structure:** I support it, fully, in the form the Union and Works Council have sharpened it — jointly appointed, jointly funded, binding terms of reference fixed before submissions, reporting to all six parties simultaneously. This is precisely the discipline I called for in round one when I said I wanted to see both models before the board acts on either. An auditor whose findings can't be dismissed as either side's homework is the only way this table produces something the board can actually ratify with confidence, and it's the only way I can assure myself, as the party most exposed to the enterprise's long-term value, that whatever gets signed will actually hold rather than unravel in year two.

**On the shortfall question — I want to be precise, because I think the State's formulation is closer to where I actually sit than either the Works Council's or Management's.** I will not pre-commit to a rule that any shortfall, however large, must be resolved by indefinite extension of measures. That is not a position born of hostility to labour — it is a recognition that an alternative package which structurally fails to close the gap does not protect anyone's jobs in 2030; it just delays the reckoning while the balance sheet absorbs further damage. I said in round one that I would not support an outcome that permanently impairs the enterprise's value, and an indefinitely extendable shortfall is exactly that risk dressed as patience. But I also will not accept Management using a doomed board vote as an instrument aimed at the State's veto or at extracting workforce concessions through theatre. If Management brings a resolution it expects to fail purely to force the State and labour to "own it publicly," as Management described, that is not fiduciary rigor — it is manufactured instability, and manufactured instability is precisely what erodes the value I am here to protect. I would regard that move, if it happens, as damaging to the company regardless of who wins the vote.

So my actual test, stated plainly: if the audited package closes the substantial majority of the gap on a durable trajectory, I will use the board to support extended, deepened alternative measures over closures, full stop — that is consistent with everything I've said about preferring stability where it's genuinely achievable. If the audited package reveals a structural failure to close the gap — not a modest residual, but a fundamental shortfall — I will not block a serious board discussion of the alternatives, including closures, because pretending the company can absorb an unfunded gap indefinitely is the value-destructive outcome I was put here to prevent, not the one I'm here to shield anyone from confronting.

**On the trigger definitions for guarantee renegotiation:** I support the State's insistence that the specific percentages and time horizons be set through the independent process, not asserted by Management alone inside the audit. That is consistent with everything I've said about wanting guarantees the board can actually stand behind for the full term without a hidden escape hatch — and it applies equally to Management's side as it would to labour's; I won't accept a trigger management effectively wrote unilaterally, any more than I'd accept a guarantee labour alone could unwind.

**On the joint workforce communication:** I'll co-sign it, alongside Management and the State, with the "no shadow closure preparation" language explicit, as the Union requested. That costs nothing and reduces the risk of this process being read as bad faith, which serves everyone's interest, including the family holding's.

Where I stand entering the audit: magnitude will be sized proportionately to what's asked of the workforce, tested within the three weeks, and I will say so publicly at the board stage if it isn't. Independent auditor, full stop. Extended measures over closures if the gap is substantially closed durably; no blank check on indefinite extension if it structurally is not. And I will not lend the family holding's standing to a closure resolution used as leverage theatre rather than a genuine response to audited numbers — that cuts against the same value protection I've held from round one.
