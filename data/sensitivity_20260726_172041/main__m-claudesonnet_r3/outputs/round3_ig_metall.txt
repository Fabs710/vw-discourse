That sentence assumes Management's model of "adding up" is dispositive. It is not, and I want to be blunt about why: Management has spent months building the closure case internally with its own assumptions about severance costs, timeline, and market recovery. Fine — that cuts in my favor: an auditor whose findings would survive external scrutiny is exactly what protects against either side gaming the comparison, including mine. Management's own words in round two were "I will bring the closure resolution forward regardless of how uncomfortable that vote is" — I want to note plainly that this assumes a binary that the board arithmetic does not actually support. The board needs two-thirds. Labour holds half. The state has said it needs "genuinely exhausted alternatives" shown, and deeper working-time and attrition measures are, by definition, alternatives not yet exhausted if the first package falls short. So if the audited number comes in short of full closure of the gap, my answer is not "we accept redundancies are now on the table" — my answer is we extend the horizon and deepen the measures, and negotiate that extension in good faith, before any closure vote is even brought forward. I want this understood now, at full volume, so nobody is surprised in week four.

**On the exceptional-circumstances trigger for guarantees — agreed with the Works Council, and I'll add the union's angle.** A trigger without a number is not a trigger, it's a hope.
