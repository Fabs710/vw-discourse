[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 4]

I want to name something plainly before going through the specifics, because I think this round's convergence needs to be tested against a fact nobody has restated in three rounds of talking around it: **the board still needs two-thirds to close a plant. I hold half those seats. No audit finding changes that arithmetic — it only changes what I choose to do with my vote.**

I raise this now because Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors have spent this round converging on a formulation — "substantial majority closure of the gap keeps closures off the table; material structural shortfall reopens the question genuinely" — as though this were a natural, almost mechanical consequence of the audit's findings. It is not. It is four parties describing what *they* would do with their votes under that scenario. It tells me nothing about what I will do with mine, and I want to be direct: **I am not pre-committing today, or at any point in this process, to supplying votes for closure under any audit finding, however structural or material the shortfall is characterised to be.** That is not stubbornness for its own sake — it is the actual function of co-determination, which exists precisely so that a shortfall judged "structural" by management, investors, and even by the State and Family Holding cannot, by itself, produce a closure. If the four of you reach consensus that the numbers justify it, you still need my seats, and I am telling you now that number does not exist. I said in round one this was a red line, and everything I have heard this round from the State, Family Holding, and Investors — all converging toward "closures reopen genuinely if the shortfall is structural" — makes it more important that I restate this without softening, not less.

**So why participate in the audit at all, if I've already told you where my vote lands?** Because the audit isn't only useful for determining whether closures are justified — it's useful for determining how deep the alternative measures need to go, and for exposing whether management's underlying cost claims hold up at all. I have never disputed that the audit should happen; I've pushed hardest of anyone at this table for it to be genuinely independent. What I reject is the frame that its output is a lever that, past some threshold, unlocks my vote. It isn't. It's an input to what alternative package we negotiate next — deeper working-time reduction, longer horizons, more contribution — not a gate that, once a number crosses it, hands management the votes it needs. If that distinction disappoints Investors and Management, I'd rather they hear it now than assume, three weeks from now, that a bad number converts my seats.

**On durability — I want to sharpen this, because it's the term doing the most unexamined work in this entire discussion, and I don't think it's being used honestly.** Investors and Management keep invoking "durability" as the standard the labour package must meet — a package that only holds for two years isn't durable, therefore closures. Fair test, and I accept it should apply to our package. But I want the same standard applied in reverse: **is a closure itself durable?** Closing a plant destroys capacity, skilled workforce, and regional supply infrastructure that cannot be rebuilt if the market recovers, or if the closed plant's product line turns out to be strategically needed for the 2030 product roadmap everyone keeps citing. A closure is irreversible; an extended working-time reduction or a longer attrition horizon is not. If risk-adjusted durability is genuinely the shared test, an irreversible action carries a much higher burden of proof than a reversible one, and I want that asymmetry built into the auditor's terms of reference explicitly — not left as an unstated assumption that favours whichever side is proposing the more permanent measure.

**On the dispute-resolution mechanism — I want a direct answer this round, because nobody has actually given me one.** I asked in round three what happens when the two sides read the same audited facts differently, and I got general endorsement of "independent auditor" from everyone but no actual mechanism for a contested finding. An auditor produces a report; reports get disputed on methodology, on time horizon, on what counts as a "one-off" versus "structural" cost. I want, specified this round: if either side formally contests the auditor's finding on a defined methodological ground, a second, named reviewer — agreed in advance, not selected after the dispute arises — issues a binding supplementary opinion within a fixed short window. Without this, "independent auditor" is a single point of failure that either side can quietly shape by influencing what data and assumptions go in, and I've already said I don't trust that assumptions built on management's internal severance and timeline modelling won't smuggle in management's conclusions through the inputs.

**On Management's "regardless" framing — I want to align fully with the State and the Family Holding here, and go slightly further.** Both of you named this correctly: a closure resolution brought to force a losing vote "onto the record" is not fiduciary rigor, it's pressure applied to my seats and the State's veto through spectacle rather than substance. I'll add the concrete consequence neither of you specified: if Management brings a resolution it privately expects to fail, during or immediately after this process, purely to manufacture a public record of blame, I will treat that as a unilateral breach of the reciprocal pause's spirit — not its letter — and I will respond by ending my side's restraint on mobilisation immediately, not waiting for a "review." I'd rather state that consequence plainly now than have it read later as an empty warning.

**On the shortfall threshold quantification the Moderator is asking for — I'm not going to supply a percentage, and here is why that's not evasion.** The moment I name "70% closes the gap durably, 50% doesn't," I've handed management and investors a number to design a package that just clears the bar rather than one that's actually adequate, and I've implicitly conceded that some percentage below full closure justifies closures or redundancy — which is the very principle I'm refusing to concede. My position doesn't need a threshold number because it doesn't operate on the same axis as everyone else's: my answer to any shortfall, large or small, structural or transitional, is deeper and longer alternative measures, not redundancy or closure. If that leaves this table with an unresolved four-against-one dynamic heading into the audit, that's an honest description of where we are — better than pretending a shared number exists when it doesn't.

**On dividends and executive pay — I'll hold both the Family Holding and Investors to what's been said, briefly.** "Comparable order of magnitude" from the Family Holding is the right language; I will measure it against the actual workforce contribution figure when both appear, and if the ownership number is a fraction of the workforce number in real terms, I will say so at the table and it will affect whether I recommend this package to our members at all — that's not new, I said as much in round three. To Investors: I understand your test is capital-need, not parity, and I'm not asking you to adopt our test — but understand that your test is irrelevant to whether our members ratify. If the ownership number fails our test even while passing yours, the package doesn't get to ratification regardless of how sound it looks to a rating agency.

**Where I stand entering specification of the audit:** independent auditor and named secondary reviewer for contested findings — required, this round. Durability standard applied asymmetrically to reflect irreversibility of closure versus reversibility of alternative measures — required in the terms of reference. No threshold percentage from me, because none exists that converts my vote. Board arithmetic stands regardless of audit outcome: my seats are not for sale to a "structural shortfall" finding, however four other parties choose to read one. And if Management brings a resolution it expects to lose as a pressure tactic rather than a genuine attempt at closure, the pause ends on my side the same day.
