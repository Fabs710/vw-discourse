[Group & Brand Management — Round 2]

Let me deal with the framing problem first, because if it hardens uncorrected, it will distort everything that follows.

**On the claim that "closures aren't actually live":** I understand the arithmetic the Works Council and Union are pointing to, and I've never disputed it. But I want to correct a leap both of you have made from that arithmetic to a conclusion it doesn't support. The state government said it will not supply votes for closures *unless* shown that every non-closure alternative has genuinely been exhausted — that is a conditional position, not a blanket refusal. The family holding said it is "not wedded to closures" and prefers a negotiated path *if* it delivers comparable, durable savings — that is a preference, not a veto commitment. The investors said the same. None of those four parties has said "we will block closures regardless of what the alternative delivers." They have said the opposite: show us the alternative closes the gap, and we'll back it; if it doesn't, several of you have explicitly reserved the right to reassess. So no — I do not accept that closures are a fiction I've been using as leverage. They remain the fallback if this table cannot produce a credible, quantified, durable non-closure package by the timeline this crisis actually requires. I would be negotiating in bad faith with the board, the family holding, and the market if I pretended otherwise, and I won't do that to score a point in this room.

That said — and I want to be equally clear about this — I have said from round one that I *prefer* the non-closure path if it delivers equivalent savings. That preference is genuine, not tactical. So let's stop debating whether the threat is "real" and focus on whether your alternative is real, because that is the actual question in front of us.

**On quantification — commitments to produce numbers are not numbers.** Both the Works Council and the Union have now twice told me you'll bring detailed modelling "within the timeframe this table sets." Fine — I am setting it now: **three weeks.** Site-by-site attrition curves, shift-pattern options, the working-time reduction model, and the costed labour contribution, structured so it can be benchmarked against the closure case on a like-for-like basis, including the externalities you've both rightly flagged — severance, legacy obligations, litigation and reputational risk on my side; lost output, customer defection, and competitive share loss on yours if this drags on unresolved. I will provide our closure-case modelling on the same basis, on the same timeline, to the same standard of detail, with independent verification if that's what it takes to get this accepted rather than re-litigated. I have no objection to a joint audit. What I will not accept is an open-ended process where "we'll bring numbers eventually" becomes the basis for indefinite strikes now. Three weeks, both sides, full transparency to each other and to the state and family holding as co-determination and governance parties. If either side is not ready in three weeks, that itself tells the rest of this table something about how serious the counter-proposal actually is.

**On publicly suspending closures for the duration of negotiation — no.** I understand why you're asking for it, and I understand the logic that it would "cost me nothing" if I'm genuinely open to the alternative. But it isn't costless, and I want to explain why precisely, not just assert it. The moment I take closures off the table unconditionally and publicly, I have removed the only mechanism that makes a genuinely structural counter-proposal urgent rather than aspirational. I have watched restructuring processes in this industry stretch for years once the pressure is released, and I am not willing to recreate that dynamic here, given how acute the cost position already is. What I *am* willing to do: commit publicly that management will not bring a closure resolution to the board during the three-week window while the quantified comparison is being built in good faith, provided the warning strikes do not escalate further during that same window. That is a real, reciprocal de-escalation — not a unilateral concession extracted by strike pressure, which I will not reward as a matter of principle, but a mutual pause tied to mutual restraint and a hard deadline. If the three weeks produce a credible package, the closure question becomes moot on the merits, not because I promised it away. If they don't, both sides know exactly where we stand and why.

**On conditional guarantees and joint monitoring:** Here I actually think we're closer than either of you is giving credit for. Jointly defined metrics, agreed in advance, with a joint monitoring body including co-determination representation — I can work with that. What I cannot accept is the Union's formulation that once targets are met, the guarantee becomes "irrevocable, full stop, not subject to re-litigation," regardless of what happens to the market between now and 2030. I am not asking for an escape hatch to redefine competitiveness unilaterally — I've already conceded that's not a serious offer, and I withdraw any implication otherwise. But a guarantee that cannot be revisited even if European demand collapses further or a competitor undercuts us by another order of magnitude is not a guarantee, it's a blindfold. What I can support: guarantees that are binding and irrevocable for their term *based on the conditions known and modelled at signature*, with a jointly agreed, narrowly defined set of exceptional circumstances — not "competitiveness" as a floating concept — that would trigger renegotiation, and even then, renegotiation, not unilateral override. That's a meaningfully different thing from what either of you has accused me of proposing, and I'd ask you to engage with that distinction rather than the worst-case version of it.

**On dividend policy:** I'll say this plainly, because I think both the Works Council and the Union are entitled to a direct answer rather than a deflection to "it's a board matter." I am not going to negotiate the family holding's or the institutional investors' capital allocation from the management side of this table — that genuinely isn't mine to give, and pretending otherwise would be a promise I can't keep. But I will say this: I support a framework, visible before final signature, showing how ownership distributions are treated during the restructuring period, on the same logic the investors themselves offered — a rational trade of temporary distribution restraint for durable margin recovery, not a symbolic number pulled from nowhere. I'll take that position into the board discussion myself, and I'd rather it come from me pushing for it than have this table assume management is indifferent to it. It matters for whether your membership accepts a deal, and a deal your membership won't accept is not a deal at all — I understand that much.

**On executive compensation:** same as round one — visible, quantified movement, tied to the restructuring outcome, not a flat percentage announced for optics. I'll bring a concrete figure alongside the closure-case modelling in three weeks, not before, because I want it sized against the actual package, not offered as a stand-alone gesture that gets banked and forgotten.

To close where I stand: I am not conceding that closures are dead, because that isn't true and saying it would be dishonest to my own board and to the market. I am offering a genuine, time-boxed, reciprocal pause — not an open-ended surrender of leverage — in exchange for the quantified package this whole table has now agreed is the actual question. Three weeks. Full transparency both directions. If the numbers close the gap on a durable basis, I will sign a non-closure settlement and defend it to the board myself. If they don't, I will bring the closure resolution forward regardless of how uncomfortable that vote is, because a cosmetic deal that leaves the cost base where it is now does not survive to 2030, and neither, eventually, do the jobs you're trying to protect by refusing to test the numbers honestly.
