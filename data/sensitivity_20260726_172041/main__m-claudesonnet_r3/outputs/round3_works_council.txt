[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 3]

Let me take the State's direct question first, since it was put to the Union but applies equally to me, and I don't want to hide behind ambiguity on it.

**Is the sticking point substance or framing?** Both, but not in the way that lets management off easy. I will accept the time-boxed reciprocal pause as the working structure for the three-week window — Management's offer is not nothing, and I said in round one that I engage where jobs and sites are actually protected. This qualifies as a step, not a concession. But I want three things attached to it, and I want them explicit before I call it accepted:

First, **the pause has to be board-minuted, not merely stated at this table.** A verbal commitment among six parties in a private negotiating room has no value to a workforce that has already watched a decades-old written pledge get set aside. If Management wants the reciprocal pause treated as real by our members, it goes into the supervisory board minutes this week, not after the three weeks conclude.

Second, **reciprocity has to mean more than "no board resolution."** No closure-preparatory steps either — no site consultations announced, no supplier renegotiations premised on a closed plant, no briefings to press or analysts framing closure as the base case while this table works. If I find out three weeks from now that "no resolution" was honoured on paper while the groundwork for closure was quietly advanced, that is bad faith, and I will treat it as such immediately, not wait for a review.

Third — and this is the substance point, not just framing — **the pause is a process instrument for these three weeks only. It does not touch my final position.** I am not trading "closures off the table permanently" for "closures paused temporarily" as if these were the same demand at different volumes. They are not. My red line remains what it was in round one: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, full stop, in the final settlement. The pause tells me management won't force a vote in three weeks. It tells me nothing about what happens in week four if the audit is contested — which brings me to my next point.

**On the audit and what happens if it's contested.** Nobody has answered this yet and I want to press it directly, because I don't trust the phrase "if the numbers don't add up" to mean the same thing to Management and Investors as it does to me. Management said: if the package doesn't close the gap, "I will bring the closure resolution forward regardless." Investors said: if it closes "a fraction," they'll "mark it down as a deferral" and reallocate capital. Both statements assume management's definition of "closing the gap" is the final word. It is not. What counts as closing the gap — the baseline, the time horizon, which externalities are weighted how — is itself something this table has to agree jointly, in advance of seeing the numbers, not something management gets to arbitrate after seeing whether our package embarrasses its closure case. I want an independent auditor, jointly appointed, with a defined dispute-resolution process if the two sides' models disagree on durability or externality weighting — not "management's model wins by default because it was first out the door." The State's demand for joint methodology sign-off before the three weeks start is the right instinct; I want that methodology to include an explicit, binding answer to what happens when — not if — the two sides read the same facts differently.

**On what "closing the gap" can never mean, regardless of what the audit shows:** I want to be completely clear, because I think Management's and Investors' framing this round is quietly narrowing the outcome space to "our package plus attrition succeeds, or closures happen." There is a third possibility neither of you has named: if the audited package closes most but not all of the gap, the answer is deeper working-time reduction, longer attrition horizons, deferred investment timing, or an extended contribution period — not layoffs, and not closures. I will not accept a framing where "the number falls short" automatically means the fallback is compulsory redundancy. That is not a rule of arithmetic; it's a choice, and I am telling you now which choice I will not accept.

**On the guarantee's "exceptional circumstances" clause — I want this defined now, not left as a placeholder everyone nods at.** Management's clarification — narrow, pre-agreed, renegotiation not override — is better than the original formulation, and I acknowledge that directly. But "narrow and pre-agreed" is doing a lot of work in a sentence nobody has actually tested yet. Give me examples. Is it a defined, independently verified collapse in European demand beyond a stated percentage threshold, sustained over a stated period? Is it a defined competitor cost gap beyond a stated margin? If you cannot name the trigger with a number attached, it is not narrow, it is "competitiveness" wearing a disguise, and I will not sign it. I want this specified in the same three weeks, alongside the cost modelling, not deferred to some later drafting session after the workforce has already voted.

**On dividends — I want to respond to the Investors' distinction directly, because I don't accept it.** You've drawn a clean line: executive pay is a legitimacy signal, dividends are a capital-cost function, and the two shouldn't be treated as equivalent sacrifice. I understand the finance logic. But from the shop floor, the distinction you're drawing is exactly the one that determines whether this deal survives contact with our membership, and I don't think it survives on your terms. If the workforce absorbs reduced bonuses, longer hours before retirement, and years of wage restraint to fund "the investment programme," and distributions continue on an unconstrained trajectory because cutting them "doesn't free up cash for anything" — that argument may satisfy a debt analyst, but it will not satisfy fifty thousand members being asked to ratify their own income reduction. The Family Holding has committed to a framework, presented before ratification, sized against a credible package. Good — I'll hold you to that, and I want it in the same three-week window, in range terms, not after. If the range turns out to be trivial relative to the workforce ask, I will say so plainly when I see it, and it will affect whether I bring this package to our members at all.

To close where I stand entering the audit period: reciprocal pause accepted, board-minuted, with the conditions stated. Joint methodology and independent dispute resolution required before the clock starts. "Closing the gap" does not default to layoffs or closures if it falls short — that outcome space stays open to deeper alternative measures. Exceptional-circumstances triggers must be numerically defined this round, not later. Dividend framework range must appear within the three weeks, not after. None of this is new distance from my position — it's the specification your own process now requires, and I intend to hold every party to it exactly as precisely as I'm being asked to specify our own numbers.
