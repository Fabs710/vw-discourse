I need to address something the Works Council and Union have just done, because it changes the calculus for everyone at this table, and I'd rather name it plainly than let it sit as if nothing has shifted.

**On the arithmetic point:** You are right that no audit finding converts your seats. That has never been in dispute from my side. But I want to be precise about what you've actually said this round, because it's more than a restatement of the veto — it's a declaration that the veto will be exercised the same way regardless of what the audit finds. That is a different thing from holding a red line; it's removing your own participation from the substantive question this entire three-week process was built to answer. The State has just said, honestly, that this forces a recalibration of how it uses its own leverage. I want to be equally honest about how it affects mine.

**Here is my problem with it, stated directly:** I am the party most exposed to what happens if this company's cost base is not fixed by 2028 or 2030. Not through a single vote, not through a public statement, but through the slow erosion of the asset my family has held across generations. I have supported the audit, the independent auditor, the secondary reviewer, the asymmetric durability standard — all of it — because I believed it would produce a shared, factual basis this table could actually act on together. If the answer, regardless of what that process finds, is "our vote does not move," then the audit's function narrows considerably. It still tells us how deep the alternative package needs to be. It still exposes whether management's cost claims are real. Both of those remain valuable, and I'm not withdrawing support for the process on that basis. But it does not do what I hoped it would do, which is give this table a genuinely shared basis for resolving the closure question if the numbers come back badly. I'd rather know that now than discover it in week four.

**On the substance you've declined to answer — Management's question, which I want to put again in my own words:** if the audited package leaves a large, structural gap — not a modest residual, a fundamental failure to restore competitiveness — what happens to the enterprise? Not to the negotiating posture, not to which party's leverage prevails. To the enterprise. You've told me your answer is always deeper and longer measures. I want to say plainly what I said in round three: an indefinitely extendable shortfall is not patience, it's a slower form of the exact value destruction I was put here to prevent. I am not going to pretend that answer resolves my concern just because it is stated with conviction. It doesn't. It defers the question.

**Where I actually stand, unchanged from round three but sharpened:** if the audited package closes the substantial majority of the gap durably, I will use the board's weight to support extended, deepened alternative measures over closures — genuinely, not as a rhetorical concession. If it reveals a structural failure to close the gap, I will not block a serious board discussion of the alternatives, including closures, regardless of whether Works Council and Union tell me in advance their votes won't move. That is not a threat aimed at you. It is a description of what stewardship of this asset actually requires of me, and I will not soften it because the arithmetic on your side is fixed. Two different things can be true at once: your veto is real, and I will still form and act on my own judgment about what protects this company's long-term value.

**On Management's "regardless" framing — I stand exactly where I did in round three, and the State's sharpening this round is correct.** A resolution brought knowing it will fail, purely to build a public record, is manufactured instability. That damages the enterprise regardless of who "wins" the vote — it costs us eighteen months of strikes, reputational damage, and management credibility, for a symbolic outcome. I will not lend the family holding's standing to that. But I want to say something the Works Council and Union should hear directly: if you are the reason a genuine, numbers-based closure discussion cannot happen at all — not theatre, a genuine discussion following a genuinely bad audit result — because your votes are fixed in advance of the facts, you cannot then also expect me to treat any attempt by management to raise that discussion as illegitimate theatre. Those two positions together would mean no closure discussion is ever legitimate, regardless of what the numbers show, which is not a position I can accept as compatible with my duty to this company's value.

**On durability's asymmetry — I support the point as stated by Works Council, Union, and Management together, in both directions.** Closure's irreversibility deserves a higher evidentiary bar. But the State's question to labour — will you actually support tightening a "temporary" measure if conditions improve — is a fair one, and I'd like an answer, because a one-way ratchet dressed as flexibility is exactly the kind of instability I was warned against internally when this process began.

**On dividends — no change from round three.** The range comes within the window, sized to a genuine proportion of workforce sacrifice, and I will say so at the board stage if it isn't. That commitment stands regardless of how the shortfall question resolves.
