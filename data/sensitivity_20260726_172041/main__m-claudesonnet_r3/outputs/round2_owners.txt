[Controlling Family Holding — Round 2]

I'll respond directly to the point put to me, since it was asked plainly and avoiding it now would just be evasive.

**On the state government's request for confirmation:** You asked whether the family holding will confirm that a framework on management and ownership contribution — range, mechanism, timing — will be presented to the board *before* the workforce is asked to ratify its own concessions, not after. I can confirm that. Sequencing is not the same question as magnitude, and I don't think it should be conflated. I am not going to negotiate the family holding's distribution policy figure by figure in this multi-party session — that genuinely is a board matter, and I've said why: it involves considerations of the group's broader capital structure and multi-year investment needs that go beyond this restructuring alone. But *when* that framework is presented is a fair process question, and I accept the logic behind it. Asking the workforce to sign first and trust that ownership sacrifice follows "in good faith" afterward is not a structure I would accept if our positions were reversed, and I won't ask the workforce to accept it either. So: the framework — not necessarily the final number, but the shape, range, and mechanism — will be on the table in parallel with the guarantee and investment terms, before the board is asked to ratify the workforce's contribution. That is a genuine concession on process, and I want it understood as one, not as a formality I'm conceding because I was cornered into it.

**On the Union's sharper challenge — that drawing the line exactly where it costs ownership is self-serving:** I want to engage with that rather than deflect it again, because it's a fair challenge and deserves a real answer, not a repeated reference to "board matters." The honest position is this: the family holding's economic interest in this company is long-term and multi-generational, not a quarterly distribution stream we're protecting for its own sake. If a credible, durable restructuring requires a period of reduced or reinvested distributions to fund investment and demonstrate shared sacrifice, that is not a sacrifice we would regard as unreasonable — it is consistent with how we think about this asset. What I am not prepared to do is treat dividend policy as a variable to be set by this table's negotiating dynamics independent of whether the underlying restructuring is actually sound. If the three-week process produces a credible, quantified, durable package, I expect the board to treat ownership contribution as a real and proportionate component of it, not a token gesture. That is a commitment, not a hedge.

**On the three-week timeline and joint methodology:** I support it, and I support the state's insistence on joint sign-off on methodology and externalities before the comparison is built, so we don't end up with two documents instead of one. I would ask to see both models — the closure case and the alternative — in parallel with the state, as it has requested. This is exactly the kind of test I said in round one I wanted to see before the board is asked to bless anything, and I'm glad it's now been operationalised with a deadline rather than left as a vague aspiration.

**On the reciprocal pause versus public suspension:** I think management's offer — a time-boxed, mutual pause tied to no further strike escalation — is the right structure, and I'd encourage the Works Council and Union to take it rather than hold out for an unconditional public statement that would, as management explained, remove the urgency that is actually driving this table toward a real proposal rather than an aspirational one. I recognise the union's concern that ambiguity can look like leverage-preservation, but a concrete three-week reciprocal commitment is not ambiguity. I'd rather this process run its three weeks on those terms than escalate over the framing of the pause.

**On conditional guarantees and joint monitoring:** I support management's clarified position — metrics fixed at signature, a narrow, pre-agreed set of exceptional circumstances triggering renegotiation rather than unilateral override — and I support the state's addition that the monitoring body include the state as well as labour and management, given its independent board standing. A guarantee structure that any single party can unwind alone is not one I would ask the board to ratify either; that instability would be exactly the kind of value erosion I said in round one I want to avoid.

**Where I remain firm:** I will not support, and will use the board to resist, an outcome where the workforce concessions are ratified on a promise of ownership sacrifice to be determined later, unquantified, after the fact. I equally will not support a settlement — however much labour peace it buys in the short term — that fails the quantified comparison and leaves the underlying cost gap open. Those are not new positions; I am simply confirming they still hold as this process gets more concrete. The three-week test is the right mechanism to establish which of these paths we are actually on, and I want to see both models before the board is asked to act on either.
