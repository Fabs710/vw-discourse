# Moderator Summary — After Round 2

**1. Areas of Agreement**

- **Joint cost model proceeds.** All parties will contribute economists/expertise; Works Council and Union confirm conditional participation.
- **Model assumptions to be jointly designed upfront**, not reviewed after conclusions are produced — explicitly supported by Works Council, Union, State Government, and Management.
- **The "gap" will be decomposed** into (a) capacity/utilisation mismatch and (b) return-on-sales/margin aspiration. Management, State, Family Holding, and Investors have all now explicitly accepted this distinction, with a shared boundary emerging: once capacity mismatch is addressed via shift models/attrition, any residual margin gap tied to pricing, product mix, or capital allocation is **not** to be closed via labour cost.
- **Management will produce a concrete compensation-contribution proposal within two weeks** (mechanism, numbers, duration matched to restructuring window) — committed by Management, demanded by Works Council, Union, State, and Family Holding.
- **Family Holding has specified a concrete capital-side contribution**: time-limited dividend adjustment for the restructuring window, matched in timing to management's contribution, conditional on the resulting plan being durable.
- **Investors accept the decomposed-gap boundary** and confirm a multi-year reduced payout is acceptable if it buys a genuinely competitive company with a defined endpoint.
- **Broad convergence that closures cannot pass this board** (State and Family Holding both confirm; Works Council and Union rely on this arithmetic).

**2. Core Unresolved Disagreement**

The central live dispute is **whether the joint model should quantify a closure scenario at all**:

- **Works Council, Union, State Government, Family Holding**: closures cannot achieve two-thirds support; modelling them as a co-determined, "live" variable wastes process credibility, risks becoming political/PR "ammunition," and signals bad faith. State Government has stated its veto is unconditional and not contingent on model output; Family Holding has moved toward this position this round.
- **Management, Institutional Investors**: refusing to quantify (not just refusing to execute) closures denies the board and markets a rigorous benchmark against which to judge whether the alternative package is genuinely sufficient, and reads as suppressing an inconvenient number rather than confidence in the alternative plan.

This is no longer a dispute about outcome (closures vs. no closures) — it is a dispute about **whether the comparison itself should exist and carry joint standing.**

**3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed**

- **How "exhaustion" of alternatives will be certified**, and by whom — Works Council/Union want joint workforce-management-state agreement on adequacy; State has agreed exhaustion must be joint but has not detailed the certification mechanism.
- **What happens if the alternative-measures package, plus management and capital contributions, still falls short** after decomposition — no party has specified the actual next lever, sequencing, or arbiter for this scenario.
- **Scope and depth of the "alternative measures" stress test** (extended short-time work, deeper attrition, output reallocation, new-product volume shifts) has been named but not yet structurally defined or sized.
- **Whether management's internal (non-joint) modelling of closures**, if it proceeds regardless of joint-process exclusion, will be treated as legitimate input to the board decision or dismissed as out-of-process.
- **Timeline for capital/management contributions to be verified and finalized** relative to the labour package — sequencing/parity has been demanded but not locked into a joint calendar.

**4. Refocus for Round 3**

Parties should address:

1. **Resolve the closure-quantification procedural dispute directly** — is there a compromise (e.g., management runs it internally/non-jointly; a defined, narrow closure benchmark is permitted without co-determined standing; or it is excluded entirely)?
2. **Define the mechanism for jointly certifying "exhaustion"** of alternatives — who signs off, on what evidence, on what timetable.
3. **Begin specifying the depth/scale of alternative measures** to be stress-tested, so the model has concrete scenarios to run, not just categories.
4. **Confirm a joint calendar** aligning management's two-week compensation proposal, the family's dividend-adjustment terms, and the labour contribution package so all sides' figures land on the same timetable.
