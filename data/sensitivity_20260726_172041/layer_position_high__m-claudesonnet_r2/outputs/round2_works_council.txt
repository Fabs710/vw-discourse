[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

Let me respond to the specifics, because I don't want this to slide into a vague "let's run the model and see" consensus that quietly reopens the closure question we already answered.

**On the cost model itself: yes, conditionally.**

We will put our economists into that process, and we will do so in good faith. But I want to be precise about what "closing the structural gap" is allowed to mean in that model, because management's and the investors' framing keeps sliding toward: run the numbers, and if alternative measures fall short, closures return to the table. That is not a neutral analytical stance — it's a predetermined conclusion dressed up as an open question. If the model is built with closures as the implicit fallback and site guarantees as the thing that gets sacrificed if the arithmetic doesn't cooperate, then the model isn't testing our proposal in good faith, it's building the case for what management already announced to the press before this board even convened. We lived that sequence once. We're not doing it again.

So here is our condition for participating: the model has to test **multiple ways of closing any residual gap that isn't closures** — deeper or longer attrition, extended short-time work, a larger or longer temporary pay contribution, output reallocation between plants to raise utilisation, accelerated volume from new product lines into underused capacity. If a shortfall appears after alternative measures, capital, and management contributions are stacked, the next lever is *more of those things*, not a plant gate closing. That's the sequencing we need locked in before we sit down with the modelling team, not discovered afterward when we're told the numbers "left no choice."

**On the Institutional Investors' point — I want to answer it directly, not dodge it.**

You said a veto against closures "regardless of what the analysis shows" reads as a structural constraint on self-correction and will be priced accordingly. I understand that's how markets read it. But I'd ask you to also price in what happens to this company's execution capability, its labour relations, and its ability to deliver *any* transition — EV, software, whatever comes next — if the workforce concludes that guarantees are conditional fictions that evaporate under financial pressure. That has a cost too, and it's not hypothetical: it's warning strikes now, and it would be sustained, escalating industrial action through a closure fight that could run for years given our board veto. If you're pricing risk, price that risk honestly as well. A company that tries to force closures through a board where it structurally cannot get two-thirds isn't derisking — it's manufacturing a multi-year standoff. That's worse for your capital than a negotiated settlement with real but non-closure cost measures.

**On the State Government's position — I appreciate the alignment, and I want to sharpen it.**

You said your veto holds against closures "unless every alternative avenue has been genuinely exhausted." I understand why you're framing it that way to preserve your own credibility as a serious fiduciary actor rather than a reflexive blocker. But I want to be honest with you: from where we sit, "exhausted" cannot mean "management declares them exhausted and you defer to their model." It has to mean workforce-side economists and yours agree the alternatives were genuinely tested at real scale, not tested half-heartedly to clear a procedural box on the way to a closure vote that was decided months ago. I'd ask you to commit now to using your board seats to insist that the model's assumptions and scenarios are jointly agreed before it's run, not just jointly reviewed after. That's the difference between real co-determination in the process and being handed conclusions to react to.

**On Management's "provisionally met" shared sacrifice — good, but I need it dated and specified, not gestural.**

You said you accept the principle. I'll believe it when I see the actual proposal on management compensation structures — not a promise to bring one "pending figures." Put a number and a mechanism on the table in this round or next, tied to the same restructuring window you're asking us to accept cuts within. If the labour contribution has to be quantified and verified, so does yours, on the same timetable, not lagging behind ours by months while our members are already out on the shop floor absorbing warning-strike pay losses.

**On the Family Holding's position — noted, and it's more encouraging than management's framing, because you at least state plainly that closures can't pass this board.** I'd only push you to go one step further: if you already know closures require two-thirds and won't get it from us or the state, say clearly that pursuing a closure-centric scenario in the model is a waste of the process's time and credibility. Don't let the model spend weeks quantifying an option that cannot pass governance. Redirect that analytical effort into stress-testing the deeper alternative-measures scenarios instead. That's not us dictating the model's scope for our own comfort — it's basic efficiency, given the board arithmetic everyone in this room has already acknowledged.

**Where I will not move, restated plainly because I sense some parties are hoping this softens over rounds:**

No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures. Not "unless the model says otherwise" — those are the boundary conditions the model operates *within*, not variables it's testing. We came into this discussion having already offered serious, real concessions: capacity reduction, shift changes, temporary pay contribution, attrition-based headcount reduction. That is substantial movement from where our members' instinct sits, and it deserves to be met with process guarantees now — joint design of the model's assumptions, a defined timetable for management's and capital's own contribution figures, and the state's commitment to test "exhaustion" jointly rather than deferring to management's judgment.

Give us that, and the joint modelling exercise proceeds with real cooperation from our side. Try to build a process where closures remain quietly enthroned as the fallback answer to any shortfall, and you'll find our participation in the model becomes a formality while the warning strikes continue and escalate outside it. I'd rather not waste the next month discovering that the hard way.
