# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. Areas of Agreement

- **Exhaustion panel mandate scope:** All parties converge on a written, narrow adviser mandate: certification of the *arithmetic* of savings estimates only. Two exclusion clauses now have broad support and should be drafted into the letter itself:
  - No adviser judgment on "adequacy" or "sufficiency" of the response (Works Council, Management)
  - No adviser comment on wage-mechanism design or reversion triggers — reserved to Union's seat (Union, Management)
- **Product/investment decisions as part of the ledger:** Broad agreement (Works Council, Union, State, Family Holding, Investors, and Management "not dismissing") that if the capacity number is large, forward-looking product allocation and capital-sequencing decisions belong on the margin/ROS side of the ledger — distinct from, and not a substitute for, addressing existing idle capacity.
- **Investors' position clarified:** Investors have now stated plainly that "insufficient" means "push for more depth," not "closures return." This directly answers the question three parties (Works Council, Union, State/Family Holding) put to them.
- **Two-week clock and parallel decomposition:** Management proposes running the decomposition in parallel with compensation/dividend figures rather than sequentially; no party has objected.
- **State's clarification accepted:** The State confirmed its political-clock comment was not intended to pressure the Union to move before management/family figures land — Union and Works Council's parity concern is not being challenged.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

**A. What happens if the deepened menu is judged insufficient (the central fork):**
- Works Council and Union: no closures under any circumstance, regardless of number or menu outcome — depth and time are the only executable levers.
- Management: introduces a third possibility — "no deal" (refusal to sign an insufficient package) — explicitly declining to pre-agree that any model output must be absorbed by the existing toolkit.
- State and Family Holding: reject "no deal" as a legitimate fallback, arguing the exhaustion panel + three-of-four threshold + full-board escalation *is* the agreed mechanism for resolving sufficiency disputes, and that Management must use that mechanism rather than reserve a unilateral veto over its output.
- Investors: side with State/Family Holding against "no deal" as a market-credibility matter, not just governance logic.
- **Unresolved:** Management has not yet stated whether it accepts the panel's certification (subject to board-vote escalation) as binding on itself, or whether it maintains an independent right to reject a certified package.

**B. Depth without limit vs. depth with a ceiling:**
- Investors have introduced a new constraint: they will support deepening (duration, contributions) but not indefinite deferral of the return-on-sales target — naming a risk that "more time" could become open-ended timeline extension rather than genuine resolution. This has not yet been addressed by Works Council, Union, Management, or the State.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **The ceiling on "depth and time":** No party has yet specified what bounds — in duration, size, or timeline — apply to the deepened menu. Investors have flagged this explicitly; others have not responded.
- **Management's commitment on product/investment re-examination:** Family Holding and Investors have asked Management to convert its "didn't dismiss" into an explicit, actionable commitment (scope, process, timing). Not yet done.
- **Management's relationship to the panel mechanism:** Whether Management accepts the exhaustion panel's certification as dispositive (subject only to the agreed board-vote escalation), or reserves a separate unilateral judgment outside that mechanism, remains unanswered and is now the central open question heading into Round 5.
- **What "no deal" would actually trigger:** Neither Management nor any other party has specified what process, if any, would follow a Management refusal to sign — this remains genuinely open per Management's own framing, and contested per State/Family Holding/Investors' framing.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

Round 5 should prioritize:
1. **Management's direct answer** on whether it accepts the exhaustion panel's certification (with board-vote escalation on disagreement) as the operative resolution mechanism for the sufficiency question — or names a different, specific remedy inside that architecture.
2. **A concrete response to Investors' ceiling concern** — what bounds (time, size, schedule) apply to "depth and duration" so it is not read, or usable, as indefinite deferral.
3. **Management's specific commitment** on how and when the product/investment-decision review will be structured, to convert this round's verbal agreement into an actionable element of the ledger.
