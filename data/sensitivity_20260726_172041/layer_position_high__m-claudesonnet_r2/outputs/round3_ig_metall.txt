I want to build directly on the works council's answer to the Investors, because I think there's a sharper way to make the same point in bargaining terms, and then I want to move the process forward on the pieces that are actually converging.

**On the closure-quantification fight — I agree with the works council's distinction, and I'll add the union's angle on why "just let the number exist" is not neutral.**

Investors, your argument assumes a number is either accurate or suppressed, and that legitimacy is purely an epistemic question — does the figure reflect reality. But a jointly co-determined model isn't just an epistemic exercise. It's an institutional artifact that gets used in future disputes, board turnovers, ratings conversations, situations none of us can fully see from this table today. The works council is right that once our economists co-sign a methodology that produces a closure figure, that figure carries our institutional fingerprints whether or not we intended it to be usable against us later. You're asking us to lend legitimacy to a document whose only possible future use, given the board arithmetic, is as leverage in a fight we've already told you we will not lose.

Here's the sharper version for you specifically: you said "if you're not willing to let that comparison exist, that tells us something about your confidence in the number." I'd invert that. Management is welcome to run that comparison on its own internal resources and defend it as management's judgment to its own board, exactly as the works council said. If that number is compelling, management can make that case to the family and the state on its own authority — they don't need our economists' signatures to find it persuasive. The fact that management wants our participation in producing it tells me the number needs joint legitimacy to do the work management wants it to do. That's not a confidence signal about the alternative package. It's a legitimacy request for a document I'm not going to help write, because I know exactly what it's for.

So: internal management modelling, not joint. That's the line. It's not new — it's a restatement of where the works council landed, and I hold it with equal firmness, backed by a different instrument. If this process tries to build a jointly-standing closure benchmark over our objection, I will treat that as the process abandoning good faith, and my response won't be procedural — it'll be in the only currency I fully control. I'd rather not spend this round repeating that, but I want it clearly on record that this isn't a works-council-only position I'm passively endorsing. It's a shared red line, held by two different levers.

**On the exhaustion-certification mechanism the works council proposed — I support it, with one addition.**

The four-way panel with a three-of-four threshold, escalating to full board vote on deadlock, is sound. I'd add: the union should have a formal seat on that panel independent of the works council's seat, not folded into a single "labour side" slot. The works council operates under co-determination law and board mandate; we operate under collective bargaining law and can act where the works council legally cannot — for instance, on the wage framework itself, which is our exclusive competence, not the works council's. If "exhaustion" ends up touching the collective agreement's substance — the pay contribution mechanism, the reversibility trigger, the duration — that has to clear our sign-off specifically, not just a generic labour seat. This isn't turf-guarding for its own sake; it's because the peace obligation has already lapsed, and anything this panel certifies as "exhausted" or "insufficient" regarding the wage contribution will need our ratification to actually hold as an enforceable agreement, model or no model.

**On the alternative-measures menu the works council put numbers to — I'll add the wage-specific piece, since that's ours to specify precisely.**

On the temporary pay contribution: I'll go further than "we're open to it in principle." Here's a concrete structure to test in the model, so it's not left as an abstraction while everyone else's figures get sharpened:

A time-limited reduction in specific pay components — not base collectively-agreed rates across the board, but variable and supplementary elements (e.g., certain bonus tranches, some allowances) — for the defined restructuring window, with an automatic reversion clause triggered by agreed profitability or utilisation benchmarks, reviewed jointly at fixed intervals rather than left to management's discretion to declare "recovered." I want the trigger jointly verified, on the same panel structure as exhaustion certification, so that reversion isn't something we have to re-fight for the moment targets are met.

What I will not put a number on yet, and I want to be transparent about why: parity of specificity. Management committed to a compensation proposal in two weeks. The family specified a dividend-adjustment mechanism this round, even if the endpoint isn't fully dated. We have offered structure and mechanism now, matching that level of concreteness. We will offer the number — the actual percentage and duration — once management's compensation number and the family's dividend number are on the table with equal precision, on the same clock. Not because we're withholding as a tactic, but because our members are already absorbing real pay losses from warning strikes to hold this position open, and I am not going to ask them to accept a specific, quantified sacrifice while the other side of "shared" remains a structural principle. That asymmetry is exactly the thing that turns a negotiation into a grievance, and I said that plainly last round. It still holds.

**To Management directly, on the ROS/decomposition point — I want to sharpen my agreement, not soften it.**

You accepted the decomposition and the boundary that residual margin gap tied to pricing, product mix or capital allocation isn't labour's to close. Good — hold that. But I want the model's output structured so this boundary is *visible*, not just conceptually agreed. I want a single page, before any negotiation over sizing, that states: X of the gap is capacity/utilisation, addressed by [menu above]; Y of the gap is margin/ROS aspiration, addressed by [capital and management contributions plus product/pricing decisions]. If that page ever gets presented with the two folded back together as one number "labour needs to help close," I will treat that as bad faith regardless of what the joint assumptions said at the outset, and I'll say so publicly, not just in this room.

**Restated plainly, so nobody mistakes three rounds of technical convergence for softening:**

No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures. No dismantling of the collective wage framework — a temporary, reversible, jointly-triggered contribution to specific pay components is not a dismantling, and I want that distinction respected, not blurred into "the union accepted opening the wage agreement." We've offered real, structured, sizeable movement. We expect matching precision from management and capital on the same timetable, and we expect the closure comparison kept out of joint standing. Meet all of that, and this proceeds as genuine cooperation. Don't, and the warning strikes were the preview, not the ceiling.
