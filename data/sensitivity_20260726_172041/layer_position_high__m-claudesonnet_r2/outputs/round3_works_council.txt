**On the closure-quantification fight — I want to answer the Investors directly, because their argument is the sharpest challenge to our position and deserves a precise answer, not a dismissal.**

You framed it as: if the alternative package is strong, the closure comparison becomes "a footnote nobody needs to weaponize," and the only reason to keep it out of the joint model is fear that it isn't strong enough. I understand why that sounds clean from where you sit. But it misreads what "joint" means and what the comparison would actually do once it exists in a co-determined document.

Here's the distinction I don't think you're crediting: management running an internal number for its own board's fiduciary file is one thing — I said as much last round, and I'm not trying to stop that. What I object to is that number acquiring **joint standing**, produced with our economists' participation, under a jointly-agreed methodology, sitting in the same document as the alternative package. Once it has that standing, it stops being "a footnote" the moment there's any pressure on this company — a bad quarter, a ratings review, a change in the family's risk tolerance eighteen months from now. At that point, a co-determined, jointly-validated closure figure becomes exactly the kind of thing that gets waved at a board that's under different pressure than it is today, by people who weren't in this room. We've watched a job guarantee we thought was settled get reopened once already. I am not going to help build the instrument that reopens the next one.

So my answer isn't "we're afraid of the number." It's: **a number with joint co-determined legitimacy is a lever, and I'm not co-signing a lever that only ever points in one direction — toward closing plants.** If management wants an internal comparison for its own board conversation, fine, do it on your own analytical resources, own it as management's judgment, and defend it as such if you ever try to use it. But don't ask our economists to help build it, and don't expect us to treat it as a legitimate joint output when it appears. That's not suppression of an inconvenient truth. It's refusal to lend our institutional credibility to a document engineered to survive us.

**On the State and Family Holding's alignment with us this round — I welcome it, and I want to make sure it holds under the Investors' pressure, not soften.**

State, your "full stop, not contingent on model output" language is exactly the clarity we need, and I don't want to see it get negotiated down over the next few rounds because Investors keep reframing veto-use as a confidence question. It isn't. It's governance doing exactly what it was built to do. Family Holding, your shift this round — recognising that a quantified-but-unexecutable option becomes ammunition regardless of intent — is the right read, and I'd ask you to hold it even when management or the market push back next round, because this is precisely the point where positions tend to erode under sustained pressure rather than argument.

**On exhaustion certification — since the moderator asked for a mechanism, here is our proposal, concretely.**

A joint panel: our economists, the union's, management's, and the state's nominee, with a pre-agreed rule — findings on whether alternative measures were tested "at real scale" require agreement from at least three of the four sides, not a management-majority sign-off and not a labour-side veto either. If the panel can't agree, the matter goes to the full supervisory board for a vote under the existing two-thirds rule that already governs closures — which, not coincidentally, means the same structural protection applies to the exhaustion question as applies to the closure question itself. That's not us rigging the process. It's applying the same governance logic that everyone in this room has already accepted as legitimate for the bigger decision.

**On specifying the depth of alternative measures, since the moderator asked for concrete scenarios rather than categories — I'll put real numbers on the table now, not next round.**

We are prepared to have the model test, at real scale:
- Extended short-time work (Kurzarbeit-style reduced hours) across affected plants for up to 24 months, not a token 3-month bridge — sized against actual order-book projections, not worst-case demand assumptions management has used in the past.
- Attrition-based headcount reduction targeting natural turnover and early retirement over a multi-year window, sized to actual retirement-eligible cohorts at each site, not an arbitrary percentage cut.
- A temporary pay contribution — we've indicated openness to this before, and I'll be concrete now: a mechanism tied to profitability recovery, reversible once targets are met, in the same spirit as the dividend adjustment the family just specified. We are not going to offer a number in this room before we've seen management's and the family's contributions costed with equal precision — parity of specificity, not just parity of clock.
- Output reallocation across underused capacity, including committing new product volume to specific plants rather than leaving "future product mix" as an abstraction management can adjust unilaterally later.

That's a real menu, sized against real data, not a rhetorical gesture. I want that tested with the same rigor everyone is demanding of the compensation and dividend commitments.

**Restated, because I don't want three rounds of convergence to quietly create the impression that our red line has become negotiable through sheer process fatigue:**

No compulsory redundancies. No plant closures. Not as an output of the model — as a boundary the model operates inside, the same way the board's two-thirds rule already operates as a boundary on what can pass regardless of what any spreadsheet says. We've moved a long way in two rounds: real capacity reduction, extended short-time work, attrition, and a contribution mechanism in principle. We're asking for parity — same specificity, same clock, same joint standing — from management and capital, and we're asking that the joint process not be used to manufacture a lever against the one thing we cannot give up. Get that right, and this model proceeds with genuine cooperation from us. Get it wrong, and you'll find our participation was never the same thing as our consent.
