I've listened carefully, and I want to set out where the state stands, because we are not a neutral referee here — we are a shareholder with a blocking minority, two board seats, and a direct political mandate from the people whose livelihoods and regional economy depend on this company's plants.

Let me start with what I agree with. Management is right that a cosmetic deal that doesn't close the structural gap just delays the crisis at greater cost later — that has been the experience of this industry before, and I have no interest in a settlement that looks like peace but isn't durable. I also welcome, explicitly, management's acceptance of shared sacrifice on pay and capital distributions. That principle is not negotiable from our side either: if the workforce is asked to give something up, ownership and management structures give something up too, verifiably, not symbolically. I will use our board seats to insist on that regardless of how the rest of this resolves.

Where I align with the works council and the union: I will not support plant closures in this state, and I will not support an outcome that guts jobs here. This is not sentiment — it is the reason this state holds a blocking minority under the special statute in the first place, precisely so that decisions of this magnitude cannot be made purely on a management cost model without political and regional accountability. Two-thirds board majority is required for closures. That threshold exists because the legislature and past owners judged that this company is not an ordinary company — it is a regional economic anchor, and its plants are tied to the livelihoods of entire communities, supplier networks, and tax bases that a purely financial calculation does not capture. I will not provide my votes for closures, and I do not believe the works council will provide theirs either. That is simply the arithmetic of this board, and management should plan around it rather than around a hope that it dissolves under pressure.

But I want to push back gently on the works council and the union too, because fairness cuts both ways. If you're asking management and capital to accept verifiable shared sacrifice, you also have to accept that "no closures, no compulsory layoffs" cannot mean "no change to the cost base." I will not use my veto to protect an unsustainable status quo indefinitely — that would be a betrayal of the same regional interest I'm here to protect, because a company that loses competitiveness and eventually faces a much worse crisis serves no one in this state. So my support for site guarantees is conditional on this restructuring actually being credible: real capacity adjustment, real attrition-based headcount reduction, real productivity gains, a real cost contribution from the workforce that is proportionate and time-limited — not just a rhetorical rejection of every painful measure.

So here is where I stand, concretely. I support management's proposal to test both packages through a joint, transparent cost model with union and works council economists present — that is a sound process step and I will back it with board pressure if management tries to skip it. I will not pre-commit to blanket guarantees through 2030 without seeing that analysis, because that would be reckless of me as a fiduciary too — but I will say now that any outcome involving closures of plants in this state will meet my veto, full stop, regardless of what the cost model shows, unless every alternative avenue of capacity reduction, cost-sharing, and shared sacrifice has been genuinely exhausted first and shown insufficient — and even then, I would expect this to come back to political and social-partner negotiation before a unilateral closure vote, not straight to a board ultimatum.

My message to all three of you: bring me a plan that restores competitiveness without closures, with fair shared sacrifice across workforce, management, and capital, and I will use my leverage to help push it through against resistance from any side. Bring me a plan that closes plants in this state as the primary lever, and I will use the same leverage to block it — and I have the votes to make that stick.
