We're going into this discussion with total clarity: plant closures are off the table, and compulsory redundancies are off the table. Not as an opening bid to be traded away, but as the line the workforce has drawn and that we will defend with everything at our disposal.

Let me be direct about why. Management is trying to break the historic job-security pledge that generations of workers built this company's trust on. That pledge wasn't a favour — it was the foundation of a social contract that made the workforce accept technological change, productivity drives, and previous rounds of "efficiency" measures without a fight. Tearing it up unilaterally, and presenting closures as a fait accompli in the press before this board has even properly negotiated, is exactly the kind of move that destroys trust and guarantees confrontation rather than resolution.

We are not blind to the competitive pressure the company faces. We know the European market has shrunk, we know the cost base is above target, we know Chinese competitors are moving fast. We are not going to pretend the status quo is sustainable, and we are prepared to talk seriously about capacity reductions, working-time models, and a negotiated employee contribution — including on cost, if it is fair, temporary or tied to conditions, and shared with management and shareholders rather than dumped entirely on the shop floor.

What we need in return, non-negotiably: binding site guarantees for every German plant through 2030, no compulsory layoffs, and a genuine co-determination role in shaping whatever restructuring plan emerges — not consultation after the fact, but real influence starting now. If those conditions are met, we can discuss almost anything else: shift patterns, voluntary programmes, early retirement schemes, temporary pay adjustments, output reallocation between plants.

If they are not met, understand what happens next. We hold half the supervisory board seats. Closures require a two-thirds majority. We will not provide it. We have already organised warning strikes across German sites, and we will escalate further — because our members are watching, and because we've been burned before when guarantees were treated as disposable.

So my position is simple: give us guarantees on jobs and sites, give us a real seat at the table in designing the solution, and we will negotiate hard but in good faith on everything else. Try to force closures or forced layoffs through, and you will get resistance, not cooperation — and you will not get your two-thirds majority.
