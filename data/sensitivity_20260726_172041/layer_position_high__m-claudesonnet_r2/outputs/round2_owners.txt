I've listened to this round closely, and I want to address the one procedural fight that's actually consequential, because everything else is converging faster than the rhetoric suggests.

**On modelling closures as a "benchmark":**

Management, I understand your logic, and last round I left room for exactly this. But the state has made the sharper point, and I'll say plainly: I now lean toward the state's view, not yours, on this specific question. You want the comparison on paper to defend the alternative package credibly to your own board. Fair motive. But the state and the works council are right that a quantified-but-unexecutable option doesn't stay a private analytical comfort — it becomes ammunition in public framing, in this room, and in how the investors read the process. "The workforce blocked a plan that would have saved billions" is a headline that damages the company's labour relations and its reputation regardless of whether the vote was ever winnable. That's a real cost to enterprise value, and it's one I'm specifically here to protect against.

You don't need a joint, co-determined closure model to satisfy your own board's fiduciary comfort. Run that internally if you must — I won't stop you — but don't ask the works council and union to co-design assumptions for a scenario that cannot pass this board's two-thirds threshold. That's not rigor, it's spending three parties' trust and a month of process on a dead letter. Redirect the joint exercise, as the works council asked, into stress-testing the alternative package at real depth: deeper attrition scenarios, extended short-time work, output reallocation, the full menu. That's where the actual answer lives, because that's the only menu that can actually be enacted given this board's arithmetic.

**On the decomposed gap — capacity versus margin aspiration:**

This is the most useful analytical move raised in either round, and I want it done properly. The union is right that these are different problems, and management's concession that unit labour cost at excess capacity connects them is also fair — I'm not going to pretend the connection is fake. But I'll hold the same boundary the state is holding: once the capacity mismatch is fixed through shift models and attrition, any residual margin gap that's about pricing, product mix, or capital allocation is not labour's to close. That's not sentiment on my part — it's discipline. A settlement that quietly uses "competitiveness" to extract labour concessions for a problem labour didn't cause is not durable, and an undurable settlement is exactly the value-destructive outcome I'm here to avoid. If part of the ROS target requires capital-side answers — pricing strategy, portfolio decisions, investment sequencing — bring those to this board too, not just to the shop floor.

**On management's compensation proposal, two-week deadline:**

Good. I'll hold you to it, and the family will use its board weight to insist the mechanism is real — deferred, tied to the same metrics, matched in duration to the restructuring window. No gestures.

**On our own contribution, since it's fair to ask again:**

I said last round we'd accept a proportionate, time-limited dividend adjustment tied to a credible plan. I'll be more concrete now: we're prepared to have the family holding's payout expectations adjust for the defined restructuring window, on the same clock as management's compensation contribution, provided — and this is not a soft condition — the plan that emerges is the one that actually restores margins durably. We are not going to accept an open-ended transfer with no defined endpoint, and we are not going to accept a contribution sized to make a shortfall analysis look better on paper while the underlying competitiveness problem persists. That's the boundary. Inside it, we'll move.

**One thing I want on the record, because I sense this discussion is drifting toward treating "no closures" and "credible fix" as automatically compatible, and they are not automatically anything — they have to be proven compatible through the work:**

I am not signing off, now or later, on an outcome that leaves the structural cost problem unresolved dressed up as labour peace. The works council and union have offered real movement — capacity reduction, shift changes, a temporary pay contribution — and that's meaningful. But "meaningful movement" and "sufficient movement" are different questions, and only the joint model answers the second one. If the alternative package, plus real management and capital contributions, doesn't close the gap at the depth required, I will not indefinitely use the family's governance weight to protect a settlement that just defers the crisis. That is the actual limit here — not closures versus no closures as such, but resolved versus unresolved. Everyone in this room, including the works council, has said they don't want to be back here in eighteen months. I take that seriously enough to mean it applies to me too.

So: redirect the model as the state proposes, decompose the gap properly, hold everyone's contribution to the same clock, and bring me a plan that's actually durable. That is what moves the family's votes. Political pressure alone does not, and neither does a well-modelled option nobody can execute.
