# Moderator Summary — Round 3

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

**Closure modelling — resolved procedurally.** All six parties now converge: management will run closure-comparison analysis internally, on its own resources, for its own board's purposes. It will **not** carry joint co-determined standing, and works council/union economists will not co-sign it. Management confirmed acceptance; Investors accepted the outcome while flagging that an unvalidated internal number carries less evidentiary weight than a jointly-tested one would have. State and Family Holding both attached a condition: if the internal number surfaces in board/investor conversations, it must be clearly labeled as management's own judgment, not a joint output — any blurring of that provenance will be called out publicly.

**Gap decomposition — structurally agreed.** The model will produce a visible, separated accounting: capacity/utilisation gap (addressed via the labour-side menu) versus margin/ROS aspiration gap (addressed via capital/management contributions). Union's demand for a single explicit page showing this split, before sizing negotiations begin, was accepted by management.

**Guardrail on a "large" capacity number — agreed across State, Family Holding, and Investors.** If the honest capacity share turns out large, the response is to go *deeper* on the existing toolkit (extended short-time work, more aggressive attrition, wider output reallocation), not to reopen closures. Management has not disputed this framing but has reserved the right to argue the menu is insufficient at whatever depth is offered.

**Exhaustion-certification panel — largely settled.** Four-way panel (works council, union, management, state nominees), three-of-four threshold, escalation to full supervisory board on deadlock. Union secured an independent seat separate from works council, specifically for matters touching the collective agreement. A proposed fifth, narrowly-mandated independent financial adviser (arithmetic verification only) has conditional support from State and Family Holding, contingent on a written, narrow mandate; Family Holding additionally wants the adviser's selection/mandate to go to the supervisory board for sign-off.

**Contribution architecture — parity structure agreed in principle.** Management's compensation proposal (deferred bonus reduction, seniority-scaled) and the union's wage-contribution structure (variable/supplementary pay components, time-limited, reversion trigger) will use the **same panel and same verification/trigger architecture**. Numbers due in two weeks from management and the family; union will provide its number on the same clock once those land.

**Alternative-measures menu — specified at real scale.** Works council put concrete parameters on the table: 24-month extended short-time work sized to order books, attrition sized to actual retirement-eligible cohorts, output reallocation tied to specific plant commitments, plus a reversible pay contribution mechanism (union added structural detail: specific pay components, automatic reversion trigger, jointly verified).

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Sufficiency vs. process quality.** Management, Investors, and Family Holding all reserve the right to judge the eventual menu insufficient regardless of how cooperatively it was built. Works Council and Union maintain the menu as specified is real and substantial. This is not yet a disagreement over facts — it's a disagreement that will only become concrete once the decomposed numbers exist.
- **What a "large" capacity number licenses.** Management has explicitly declined to pre-agree that going deeper will be sufficient if the number is large; State, Family Holding, and Investors have explicitly pre-agreed that depth — not closures — is the only permitted response. This is a live tension, not yet a resolved one, since management has not accepted the guardrail, only registered it.
- **Closure red line itself.** Unchanged. State's veto remains "full stop, not contingent on model output." Works Council and Union maintain no-compulsory-redundancy/no-closure as a boundary condition, not a model output. Management has not conceded the substantive legitimacy of closures as an option — only its removal from the joint process.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **No actual numbers yet.** Compensation (management), dividend endpoint (family), and wage contribution (union) are all committed in structure but not in figure. The entire parity architecture is currently a promise about a future clock, not yet tested against real numbers landing simultaneously.
- **What happens if the decomposed capacity share is large and the menu, even deepened, still falls short in management's or Investors' judgment.** No party has specified what the actual next step is in that scenario — this is the central unresolved fork the guardrail discussion has been circling without landing on.
- **The independent adviser's mandate.** Conditionally supported but not yet drafted; the boundary between "arithmetic verification" and "commentary on sufficiency/scale" is agreed in words but not yet tested in a written mandate.
- **Political/patience clock.** State flagged that public tolerance for extended timelines is not unlimited; this has not been reconciled with the union's parity-before-numbers position beyond mutual acknowledgment.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

1. **Produce the decomposition.** This is now the load-bearing deliverable — the split between capacity/utilisation gap and margin/ROS gap needs to move from agreed methodology to actual figures.
2. **Land the two-week numbers on schedule** — management's compensation figure, family's dividend endpoint, union's wage-contribution percentage/duration — on the same clock, as committed.
3. **Draft the independent adviser's mandate letter** in writing, for supervisory board sign-off, before the panel is constituted.
4. **Address directly, rather than defer again:** what governs the outcome if the capacity share is large and the deepened menu is still contested as insufficient by management or Investors. This fork has been named by multiple parties but not yet resolved, and further deferral will likely harden rather than soften positions.
