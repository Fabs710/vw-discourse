# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

**1. Areas of Agreement**
- All five parties accept the underlying premise: the current cost base is not competitive and some form of change is required. No party is defending the pure status quo.
- Broad consensus on proceeding to a **joint, transparent cost model**, with union/works council economists included, to test whether alternative measures (shift models, attrition, voluntary/early retirement, capacity reallocation, labour-cost contribution) can close the structural gap.
- Agreement in principle on **shared sacrifice**: management, the family holding, and institutional investors have each accepted — provisionally and pending figures — that management pay structures and/or dividend policy should carry a verifiable, proportionate, time-limited contribution if labour is being asked to contribute.
- All parties state a preference for a negotiated, durable outcome over a prolonged public confrontation or a cosmetic deal that resurfaces the crisis later.

**2. Core Disagreements**
- **Timing and sequencing of guarantees**: Works Council and Union want binding site guarantees (through 2030) and a no-compulsory-layoff commitment secured *now*, as a precondition for negotiating everything else. Management, the Family Holding, and Institutional Investors insist guarantees cannot be pre-committed *before* the cost model results are known, as this would predetermine the outcome regardless of what the analysis shows.
- **Status of closures as a tool**: Works Council, Union, and the State Government treat closures as categorically off the table (the State citing its veto and blocking-minority rationale). Management and Institutional Investors want closures kept notionally on the table as a fallback if alternative measures prove insufficient, arguing that removing it in advance caps the toolset before the arithmetic is known.
- **Reading of the State's veto**: The State frames its blocking-minority veto as a firm, standing feature of the company's governance structure tied to regional accountability. Institutional Investors warn this will be read by capital markets as a structural constraint on self-correction if the numbers don't add up, with consequences for valuation and rating.

**3. Unaddressed Trade-offs**
- No party has yet quantified what "closing the structural gap" actually requires in figures — the entire discussion of contributions (labour, management, capital) is conditional on a model that does not yet exist in shared, agreed form.
- The relationship between a possible **shortfall finding** (i.e., alternative measures don't fully close the gap) and what happens next is unresolved: management/investors imply closures return to the table; Works Council/Union/State imply the answer is more shared sacrifice, not closures. This is a live fault line, not yet negotiated.
- The scope, duration, and governance of the "shared sacrifice" contributions (dividend adjustment, management pay) — how much, for how long, measured against what triggers — has not been specified by any party.
- The process for union/works council co-determination *during* the model-building phase (not just after) has been asserted as a demand but not yet operationalized or agreed by management.

**4. Refocus for Next Round**
Parties should move from position statements to **process design**: 
- What are the agreed parameters, assumptions, and governance of the joint cost model (who runs it, what data, what timeline, what counts as "closing the gap")?
- What would each side accept as the process and criteria for evaluating a shortfall, *before* the model is run — to avoid re-litigating fundamentals if/when results arrive?
- What concrete, verifiable form would management/capital-side shared sacrifice take, and on what schedule, to be tabled alongside the labour-side offer?
