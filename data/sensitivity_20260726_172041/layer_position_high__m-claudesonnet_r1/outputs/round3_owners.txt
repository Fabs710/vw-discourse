I need to respond to three direct demands now on the table: a substantive dividend range rather than a process commitment, the works council's reframing of the shortfall question as a "consent question, not a measurement question," and management's request that I state my actual position on the evidentiary bar. I'll address each, because avoidance has run its course on this round.

**On the dividend range.** The union, the state, and investors have all now made the same point, and I won't pretend it's unreasonable: "a stated position on a clock" without a number is a promise to have a promise. I take the correction. I will bring a provisional range to round four — not a single figure, because that genuinely does require supervisory board process I won't shortcut by personal pronouncement, but a bounded range the board is working within, scaled proportionately to the workforce contribution and the management pay reduction already tabled. I'd rather be pinned to a range I have to defend than repeat "a position is coming" a third time and lose credibility on the one commitment that's entirely within the holding's own governance to deliver.

**On the works council's and union's reframing — I want to be precise about where I actually agree and where I don't, because I think both sides are half right and the room is at risk of treating "consent question" as though it settles the matter in labour's favor by definition.**

You're correct that a verified number doesn't answer who bears a shortfall — that's a governance decision, not an arithmetic one. I said that myself in round one when I distinguished "closing the gap" from "who pays for it." I'm not walking that back.

But I want to name something plainly, because sidestepping it now would be dishonest rather than diplomatic: the works council's formulation — "if a genuine irreducible gap remains, that comes back to the board as a live decision requiring a two-thirds vote" — is, as management pointed out, not the same claim as "closure is excluded from what this process can produce." Management is right that those are different statements, and I think the works council knows it, because the actual operative content of your position is "our veto stays fully intact regardless of the evidence," dressed in the language of "the process can't pre-decide it." That's your right under the statute. It is not, however, a position I can describe to my own board as "labour has moved toward accepting a closure-inclusive outcome under defined conditions." It hasn't. It has moved toward accepting that a number will be produced, while reserving the right to block whatever that number implies. I'd rather we all say that clearly than let round four proceed on a fiction that consent has been structurally conditioned when it hasn't.

**Where I actually land on the evidentiary bar management is asking the state and the works council to define:** I support management's proposal to define, jointly and now, not just the threshold percentage but the evidentiary conditions under which a shortfall becomes genuinely irreducible. Not because I think this converts the works council's veto into something it isn't — it can't, structurally, and I'm not asking the state to promise its vote in advance either, and I think the state's answer on that was actually the most honest one given today: a real decision, not a slogan, made when the evidence exists. What I am asking for is that the works council and union commit, now, to engaging with that evidentiary bar in good faith when it's built — including the regional impact assessment the state has rightly added — rather than treating "our veto is unconditional" as the final word regardless of what a jointly-designed, jointly-verified process eventually shows. If the answer to that is "no, our board votes are not something any evidence could move," I need to hear that plainly, because it changes what I'm asking the holding to invest time and goodwill in.

**On timeline, I'll register a preference rather than relitigate the state's number.** The state's 36-48 month window with a front-loaded first checkpoint is closer to something I can support than either the works council's 2028-2030 horizon or management's 24-36 month compression. My reasoning is not urgency for its own sake — it's that a five-to-six-year runway with the market moving the target throughout it is exactly the "slower fix that quietly becomes a deferred failure" risk investors flagged, and I share that concern as a long-horizon owner, not just as a matter of matching management's tempo. Long-term stewardship doesn't mean unlimited patience with a shrinking margin for error.

**On the checkpoint mechanism with teeth, as management proposed it — two consecutive checkpoints below trajectory triggering an accelerated board return with the full instrument set restored.** I support that as a design principle, with one modification: "full instrument set restored" should trigger renegotiation of the package, not an automatic assumption that closure is thereby approved. It reopens the board's decision; it doesn't pre-decide it. That distinction matters to me for the same reason it matters to the works council, even though we're likely to land differently on the eventual vote.

To close where I actually am: real movement from me this round on the dividend range, support for the state's timeline and impact-assessment additions, and support for jointly defining the evidentiary bar for shortfall — but I won't pretend, to make the room comfortable, that "consent stays with the board" and "closure is excluded from the process" are the same sentence. They aren't, and the holding needs the honest version going into round four, not the version that reads better in a summary.
