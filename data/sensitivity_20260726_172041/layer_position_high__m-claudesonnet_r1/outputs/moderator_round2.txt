# Moderator Summary — End of Round 2

## 1. What Has Been Agreed (or Substantially Converged)

- **Joint governance of an independent verification process**: All parties now support jointly selected assessors, jointly defined methodology/benchmarks, and works council/union presence throughout, rather than a management-run or unilaterally-designed process.
- **A threshold-based mechanism**: Management's proposal to fix, jointly and in advance, what percentage of the verified gap the alternative package must close — rather than judging "adequacy" after the fact — has been accepted in principle by the Works Council, Union, State Government, and Family Holding as a structural improvement over both "unconditional veto" and "management decides later."
- **Reciprocal disclosure on a shared timeline**: Management has disclosed an order-of-magnitude cost gap and committed to a specific executive variable-pay reduction figure next round; the Family Holding has committed to bringing a stated dividend-policy position to the supervisory board on the same ~8-week clock as the independent process.
- **Conditional strike posture**: The Union has offered to hold (not escalate) current strike action if joint process governance and reciprocal disclosures are delivered by next round — matched by Management's reciprocal commitment.
- **Process timeline**: An eight-week window for the independent assessment has been proposed by Management and not contested.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Whether closure is excluded by design or held in reserve by sequence**: Works Council and Union hold that compulsory redundancies/closures are outside the process entirely, regardless of the verified number. Management and the Family Holding reject fixing that outcome in advance. The State's "sequencing" compromise (non-closure instruments must be genuinely exhausted first) is the current bridge — but investors and Management both question whether "exhausted" has any real time limit, while labour representatives worry it still leaves closure as an eventual default.
- **Calibration of the threshold**: Investors want the bar benchmarked to competitive parity with rival cost structures; others have not yet specified what standard the threshold should be measured against.
- **Definition of "durable"**: The Works Council/Union view a longer timeline as inherently more durable; Investors and the Family Holding explicitly reject that equivalence, distinguishing "durable fix over more time" from "same inadequate fix, deferred."
- **Independence of the verification number**: Investors and the Works Council/Union both flag capture risk — from opposite directions (investors fear the number converges to management's anchor; labour fears the methodology and scope are pre-set by management).

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **No agreed definition yet of the threshold percentage itself**, nor the benchmark it should be measured against (competitor parity vs. breakeven vs. some other standard).
- **No agreed time limit on the "sequencing" of non-closure instruments** before closure becomes discussable — the State has proposed the principle; no one has proposed a number.
- **No mechanism yet for interim checkpoints** during a multi-year implementation window, raised by Investors but not yet addressed by other parties.
- **Family Holding's dividend commitment remains a process commitment ("a position on a clock"), not a substantive figure or range** — flagged by Investors and Union as needing more specificity.
- **No agreed definition of "closing the gap"** in terms of what counts (timeline assumptions, whether further future cuts are embedded) — raised by Works Council, not yet resolved.

## 4. Refocus for Round 3

Parties should come prepared to move from principles to specifics on four open design questions:
1. **What is the threshold percentage** (or range) and **against what benchmark** is it set?
2. **What is the time-bound limit**, if any, on sequencing non-closure instruments before closure can be discussed — a number of months/quarters, not a principle?
3. **What checkpoint structure**, if any, governs the implementation period once a package is agreed?
4. **What specific figures** — Union's contribution range, Management's executive pay reduction, Family Holding's dividend range — will actually be tabled, on the shared clock all sides have now agreed to?

The next round should test whether the structural convergence achieved here (joint governance, threshold-before-number, reciprocal disclosure) can survive being reduced to actual numbers and deadlines.
