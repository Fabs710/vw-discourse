Let me respond directly, because I think Round 1 produced real movement and I don't want that lost in process language.

**To the works council:** we are aligned, and I want to say plainly why that alignment matters structurally, not just rhetorically. You hold the board veto; we hold the collective agreement and the strike weapon. Those are different instruments protecting the same red line, and neither of us is going to let "an independently verified number" become a substitute for an actual guarantee. A number tells you the size of a problem. It does not tell you who bears it. That's a political and distributional question, not a technical one, and I will not let this process get reframed as if verification alone answers it. So: full agreement on your framing that governance of the process — who selects assessors, who defines "closing the gap," what timeline counts as durable — has to be settled jointly and now, before either of us hands over real numbers as a gesture of good faith that gets pocketed.

**To management:** you asked for a number, not a framework. Fair — and I said in round one we'd bring one. Here is the actual condition under which that number appears on the table in the next round, concretely: we need, in parallel, (1) the methodology behind your cost-gap figure disclosed to an independently selected assessor the works council and union both have confidence in, not one management pre-selects and presents as neutral, and (2) an actual figure — not a gesture — on executive compensation restraint and a dividend-policy position from the family holding for the restructuring window. You said symbolic contributions don't close a structural gap measured in hundreds of millions, and you're right — which is exactly why I'm not asking for symbolism. I'm asking for a number precisely so we can put it next to ours and see, transparently, what fraction of the gap each side is actually closing. If your side won't quantify, don't ask ours to.

**On sequencing**, I want to be sharper than "parallel negotiation" as a general concept, because it can mean anything. My actual position: guarantees and the savings package get finalized in the *same document, at the same time* — not guarantees contingent on your later judgment of "close enough," and not our contribution banked while guarantees stay aspirational. If the verified number falls short of your target, the works council is right that the answer is a longer timeline, deeper working-time measures, or production-line consolidation within existing sites — not closure held in reserve as the fallback. That is not a negotiating position I'm prepared to trade away for speed.

**To the regional state government:** I appreciate that you didn't abandon the substance of protecting jobs, and I think your instinct toward an independent process is the right one in principle. But I'd push you exactly where the works council pushed you: "I won't commit to an absolute veto regardless of what the numbers show" sounds reasonable in the abstract, and it is exactly the sentence that, in practice, becomes the hinge on which closures get justified later. If you want our trust in this process, put your blocking-minority weight behind the *governance* of the process now — insist jointly with us on who runs it — rather than reserving your judgment for the output. A fair process with a captured design produces a captured output.

**To the family holding:** you said dividend restraint is "a supervisory board matter, not settled by pronouncement here." I understand the institutional point, but I'd note the double standard risk: you're asking our members to accept a quantified pay-increase pause and bonus deferral as a concrete, biting contribution, while your own contribution remains a promise to have a future conversation. That asymmetry is exactly what erodes legitimacy on the shop floor and exactly what fuels the strikes. I'm not asking for a number today either — I'm asking for a date by which one exists, tied to the same timeline as our contribution figure. Reciprocal specificity, not reciprocal vagueness.

**To institutional investors:** I want to push back harder than the works council did on one thing you said — that fairness "does not, on its own, restore margins," as though fairness and durability are competing tests. They're not. A package perceived as unfair by the workforce that implements it does not stay durable — it produces exactly the industrial disruption you're warning against, repeatedly, for years, because trust breaks and every future ask gets fought from zero. Fairness is not a moral add-on to the economics here; it is a precondition for the economics working over the medium term you claim to care about. You said you're indifferent to the instrument but not the outcome — fine, but the instrument determines whether the outcome holds. A closure-heavy package that "closes the gap" on paper while leaving a demoralized, distrustful workforce and years of legal and industrial conflict is not the durable fix you're pricing for. I'd ask you to weigh that risk into your own model, because right now you're treating social stability as a nice-to-have rather than a load-bearing input.

**On strikes**, since it was raised directly: we are not going to suspend warning strikes because a verification process has been proposed with no governance yet agreed. That would surrender our only leverage at exactly the moment it has value. What I will commit to, concretely: if joint governance of the independent process is agreed in this room — assessors, methodology access, timeline definition — by the next round, and management and the family holding table their own quantified contributions on the same schedule as ours, we will hold current strike action at its present level rather than escalating to full-day or indefinite strikes while that process runs. That is a real, reciprocal, time-bound offer — not a stand-down, a pause conditioned on matching movement from your side. If we get stonewalling dressed as "we need to see your number first" while nothing concrete comes back on process governance or ownership contribution, we escalate. That is not brinkmanship; it's proportionality.

To summarize where I actually am: quantified contribution — yes, next round, conditioned on joint process governance and reciprocal disclosure. No compulsory redundancies, no plant closures — not negotiable, not something a verified gap number can override. Shared sacrifice — needs actual figures from management and ownership on the same timeline as ours, not sequenced after. That's a real move from round one. It is not unconditional, and it should not be mistaken for softening on the red line.
