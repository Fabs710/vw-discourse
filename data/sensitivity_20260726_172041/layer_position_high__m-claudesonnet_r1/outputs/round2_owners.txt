[Controlling Family Holding — Round 2]

I'll respond to the direct challenge, because it was fair and I won't sidestep it: the union and the state government both flagged that "dividend policy is a supervisory board matter" risks becoming permanent vagueness while labour is asked for hard numbers on a fixed clock. That asymmetry, left unaddressed, would be a legitimate grievance, not a manufactured one. So let me correct it now rather than let it sit as an open question through another round.

I will commit the holding's board representatives to bring a stated dividend-policy position for the restructuring window to the supervisory board on the same eight-week timeline management has proposed for the independent assessors — a position the board can act on when the process reports, not an indefinite "future conversation." I'm not naming a figure today, because that genuinely is a board decision and I won't pre-empt it by personal pronouncement in this room — that would be exactly the kind of unilateral governance move I'd object to if someone else did it. But a stated position, on the same clock, with the same specificity expected of the workforce's contribution — yes. That's a real move from round one, and it's the right one.

On the substance of where this negotiation has landed, I want to register clear support for two things and one caution.

First, I support management's threshold mechanism as a structural improvement over both extremes on the table. Fixing the bar before the number is known, rather than after, removes exactly the discretion problem the works council raised, and it removes the "closure held in reserve regardless of the number" problem the state raised in response. That's a better design than either side's round-one position, and I'd rather see the room build on it than relitigate it back to first principles.

Second, I support the state's refinement of it — sequencing non-closure instruments first, with closure only discussable after they've been genuinely tried and verified insufficient, not sitting in parallel from day one. That is consistent with what I said in round one: we are not committed to closures as the instrument, and if a credible alternative delivers durable savings, that may be the better outcome for the asset, not merely the acceptable one. I'd add only this: "genuinely tried and verified insufficient" has to mean verified against a real timeline, not an open-ended one that quietly becomes indefinite. A sequence that never actually concludes is just the works council's absolute veto by a different route, and I said in round one I won't support a process that treats governance as an obstacle to be routed around. I don't think anyone here intends that, but I want it said plainly so it doesn't happen by drift.

The caution: I heard the works council say that if the alternative package falls short, "so be it — a slower fix beats a fast one that destroys co-determination." I understand the principle behind that, and I'm not dismissing it lightly. But I want to be direct about where the holding's actual limit is, since we've now had two rounds of process design and I don't want it mistaken for open-ended flexibility on outcome. If the verified process — jointly governed, fairly designed, all instruments genuinely exhausted — still shows a structural, recurring shortfall that leaves this company uncompetitive on a multi-year horizon, "so be it, we take longer" is not a position the holding can support indefinitely. Not because we're indifferent to jobs or sites — we've said the opposite and meant it — but because a company that remains structurally uncompetitive doesn't protect the workforce either; it just delays the crisis onto worse terms, with less capital, less trust, and less room to manoeuvre than exists today. That is the actual asset we are the long-term stewards of, and permanent impairment of it serves no one at this table, including the thirty thousand livelihoods the works council rightly centres.

So my position going into round three: back the joint process, back the threshold-and-sequencing mechanism as refined by the state, table a dividend position on the same clock as everyone else — that's real movement, and I mean it. But I won't pre-commit to an outcome-blind guarantee that survives a verified, exhausted-alternatives shortfall, because that crosses from prudent stewardship into value-destructive denial. Bring the process home with a credible number, and I expect the holding to be a constructive party to whatever guarantees that number supports. If the number comes back showing a real gap that no amount of working-time and capacity measures closes, I will not pretend the answer is simply more time.
