[Group & Brand Management — Round 3]

I want to take the works council's and the union's central correction seriously, because it's not obviously wrong — it's obviously incomplete, and I intend to show exactly where.

**First, a direct point I want on the record: the works council has not actually excluded closure. Read your own words back.** You said: "if, after all of that, a genuine irreducible gap remains, that comes back to this table and to the supervisory board as a live decision requiring a two-thirds vote." That is not exclusion. That is closure remaining on the table, subject to your veto at the point of decision — which, under co-determination, is exactly the situation I already operate in today, with or without this process. So let's stop the rhetorical move where "unconditionally excluded" and "subject to a board vote I get to block" are treated as the same claim delivered with different emphasis depending on which round we're in. They are not the same. If what you actually mean is "closure remains a live instrument, and we retain our statutory veto over it, and we will exercise that veto based on our own judgment of the evidence at the time" — say that plainly, because I can work with that. It's honest, and it's the system as designed. What I will not accept is a terms-of-reference document that says the opposite in writing — "not the variable being tested," "excluded from what this process can produce as an output" — while everyone in the room understands the actual mechanism is a veto held in reserve regardless. Pick one description. I'll negotiate hard against either, but I won't negotiate against a moving target.

**Given that, here is my actual position, and it's a real concession: let's jointly define, now, what "genuine, irreducible" means — the evidentiary bar that would need to be met for your side to actually vote yes on a closure-inclusive resolution, not just the bar for measuring the gap.** That's different from what I proposed in round two, and it's a direct response to the union's fair challenge that I was only removing my own discretion, not addressing what happens on shortfall. Fine — let's define both, jointly, in the same document: (1) the threshold percentage and benchmark for "the alternative package works," and (2) the evidentiary conditions — what data, what duration of underperformance, what exhaustion of alternatives — that would move the works council's own board representatives to vote for a closure-inclusive resolution rather than block it indefinitely on principle. If you're telling me that number two is simply undefinable — that no evidence would ever move your board votes — then say that clearly, because it changes what kind of process I'm actually running. I don't think that's what you mean. I think you're worried I'll declare victory prematurely on a rigged number. So let's remove that risk by defining the evidentiary bar jointly, now, alongside the threshold — which is exactly the same logic you applied to the threshold itself. You can't ask me to fix my discretion in advance and then reserve unlimited discretion for yourselves at the exact same decision point. That's not symmetric, whatever the union's framing suggests.

**Second, on the numbers themselves — 65-75% of the gap, over a multi-year window to 2028-2030.** I said in round two the structural gap is several billion euros annually. Let's do the arithmetic honestly rather than let the percentage obscure the absolute figure: 25-35% of several billion is not a rounding error. Depending on where in that range the verified number lands, that is somewhere in the high hundreds of millions to over a billion euros annually, recurring, not one-off. That is not a shortfall I can describe to the supervisory board as "close enough, extend the timeline." I want to be precise about why the timeline point matters specifically: a multi-year phase-in to 2028-2030 assumes the competitive environment holds still while we phase in savings. It won't. Chinese manufacturers are not pausing their cost and volume advantage while we run a six-monthly checkpoint process over five years. Every year of a shallow savings curve is a year the target we're trying to hit moves further away, which means the same package that might close 65-75% of today's gap could be closing a smaller share of a larger gap by 2028. I need the room to treat that not as management's negotiating pressure tactic but as the actual physics of the market we're in. So: I welcome the 65-75% figure as a genuine, serious opening position — better than a framework, and I said I'd treat real numbers as progress, and I mean that — but I will not accept a five-to-six-year runway to reach it as durable. I'd want that closer to 24-36 months for the bulk of the measures, with the checkpoint structure used to accelerate, not merely to monitor.

**Third, on the sunset/reversal clause the union has attached to pay-increase pauses and bonus deferral.** I understand the logic — you don't want an indefinite concession dressed as temporary, and that's a fair thing to guard against. But I need to flag the tension this creates with the word "durable" that everyone in this room, including your side, has used repeatedly. If the labour contribution automatically reverses once a "recovery trajectory" checkpoint is hit, then what we're calling structural savings is actually a bridge loan from the workforce, not a cost-base reset. Those are different things with very different value to me. I can accept a sunset clause tied to sustained, multi-year margin recovery — meaning the trigger isn't "we had one good quarter" but "return-on-sales has held at target for a defined period, verified by the same independent process" — because that protects both your members from open-ended extraction and my board from being told we banked temporary savings as if they were permanent. I will not accept a sunset tied to a short-term easing of the crisis, because that reintroduces exactly the instability I'm trying to close out before 2030.

**To investors, on the benchmark question the works council and union both pushed on directly:** I'll answer plainly, because dodging it serves no one. When I talk about competitiveness, I mean parity with comparable European and premium-segment manufacturers who operate under similar labour law and co-determination structures, not unit labour cost convergence with lower-cost economies. That's not a concession I'm making reluctantly — it's the honest scope of the benchmark I've been working from since round one, and the works council was right to demand I say so explicitly rather than let "competitive" do vague work. That said, I want to be clear this doesn't shrink the problem as much as the works council might hope — several of those European comparators have already closed capacity, consolidated platforms, or renegotiated labour agreements over the past several years specifically to reach the cost position we're now being asked to match without those same instruments.

**Fourth, concrete figures as promised.** Management board variable compensation: I will bring a reduction of 20% in variable pay for the restructuring period, initially three years, to the supervisory board for formal adoption on the same eight-week clock as the independent process — that's a number, not a gesture, and it's larger than what several peer companies have committed in comparable restructurings. I expect the family holding's dividend position to carry equivalent specificity — a range, not merely a promised position — and I'll say to the holding directly: the union and investors are both right that "a stated position" without a number doesn't meet the same bar you're asking labour and management to meet. Match your own standard.

**On checkpoints:** I'll accept six-monthly reviews, but I need them to have actual consequence, not just "trigger renegotiation of the measures" in an open loop. Concretely: if two consecutive checkpoints show the agreed package tracking materially below the jointly-set threshold trajectory, that triggers an accelerated return to the board with the full range of instruments — including the ones we're currently sequencing away — back on the table for that vote, on an expedited timeline, not a fresh multi-round renegotiation from zero. That's the teeth investors are asking for, and it's consistent with treating this as a real recovery plan rather than a monitoring exercise with no end state.

**To the state government, directly:** you corrected your position well in round two, and I respect that you did it rather than let ambiguity sit. But I need the same precision from you that I've just given everyone else: when the sequence runs out and a genuine, jointly-defined irreducible gap is verified, will your veto be exercised based on the evidence at that point, or is your actual position — like the works council's — that you will not allow closure onto a board resolution regardless of what the evidence shows? Those are different postures, and the room needs to know which one you're holding, because it changes what the assessors' work is actually for.

To summarize where I stand going into round four: real progress this round — a serious contribution number from labour, a benchmark clarification I can live with, an executive pay figure I've now put down, movement toward checkpoints with actual teeth. What I will not accept: a process that quietly reclassifies "closure held under a veto that will never be lifted regardless of evidence" as "closure excluded from the process," a shortfall of hundreds of millions to over a billion euros annually treated as acceptable if wrapped in a long enough timeline, or savings labelled durable while carrying a short-fuse reversal clause. Define the evidentiary bar for shortfall jointly, now, with the same rigor you're asking of the threshold — that's the actual next step, and it's the one nobody has taken yet.
