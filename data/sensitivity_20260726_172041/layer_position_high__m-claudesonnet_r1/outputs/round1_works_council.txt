We need to be crystal clear from the outset: plant closures are off the table, and compulsory redundancies are off the table. Not as an opening bid to be traded away, but as the line this workforce will not cross. We have half the seats on that supervisory board and the two-thirds rule for a reason — co-determination exists precisely so that a management team under margin pressure cannot simply liquidate German sites and livelihoods to hit a return-on-sales target set in a spreadsheet. That veto is real, and we will use it.

Let me be direct about what has happened here. Management is proposing to break job-security commitments that were made to this workforce as a solemn pledge, and they are doing it while presenting closures as a fait accompli rather than something to be negotiated with us. That is exactly the kind of unilateral move that destroys trust, and I will tell you plainly: trust is already low. Warning strikes are underway across our sites, and they will intensify if this proposal is treated as settled rather than as an opening position.

Now, to be equally clear about what we are not: we are not blind to the competitive pressures the company faces. We are not going to pretend costs are irrelevant, or that Chinese competition and a shrinking European market are fictions. We are willing to talk about capacity reductions, working-time models, and a real, quantified employee contribution — shorter hours, pauses on pay increases, deferred bonuses, efficiency changes on the shop floor. Our members have accepted painful adjustments before when the burden was shared and the endpoint was secure jobs, not eliminated ones.

What we need to move at all is this: binding site and job guarantees running to 2030, covering every plant currently on the table for closure, with no compulsory redundancies. Second, we need a genuine seat at the table in shaping the restructuring plan itself, not a presentation of decisions already made for us to ratify. If those two conditions are met, we will engage seriously and constructively on the scale of savings needed elsewhere.

Without them, this goes nowhere. We will not authorize any board resolution that closes a German plant or greenlights forced layoffs, and we have the votes to stop it. I would rather this company find fifty ways to cut costs without touching a single site or a single guaranteed job than sign off on a plan that treats our members as the first line of defense for shareholder returns. That is where we stand, and that is where we intend to remain until I hear something that actually addresses site and job security rather than assuming we will eventually fold under pressure. We will not.
