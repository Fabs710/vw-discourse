[Regional State Government — Round 3]

Management asked me directly for precision, and I owe the room the same rigor I demanded from the family holding on dividends. So let me answer plainly, without hedging behind procedural language.

**No, my veto will not be exercised as a pre-committed rubber stamp for closure once a sequence "runs out," and no, it is also not an unconditional block regardless of evidence.** Both of those are wrong descriptions of where I am, and I want to explain why the honest answer is neither of the two poles this room keeps trying to sort me into.

Here is the actual position: if a genuine, jointly-verified shortfall remains after non-closure instruments have been tried in good faith — and I mean genuinely resourced and genuinely tried, not performed for the length of a checkpoint cycle — I will look at the evidence at that point and decide. That is not a promise to vote yes on closure. It is also not a promise to vote no regardless of what the evidence shows. It is me telling you, honestly, that I do not know today what I will do with a decision that has not yet been built, because building it responsibly is the entire point of this process. Anyone at this table demanding I commit now, in either direction, is asking me to substitute a slogan for a judgment I'm accountable for making well. I won't do that, for the same reason I criticized the works council's absolute red line and management's "closure remains a live instrument regardless" framing — both are trying to settle in round three a question that the evidence hasn't been produced to answer yet.

What I will commit to now, concretely, is **what counts as "genuinely tried"** — because that's the actual lever, and management is right that it needs a joint definition, not a vague standard I get to interpret unilaterally later. My addition to the evidentiary bar management proposed: it has to include a **regional economic impact assessment**, run alongside the cost-gap assessment by the same independent process, covering supplier-base effects, regional labour-market absorption capacity, and tax-base consequences at each site under consideration. Not as a sentimental add-on — as a hard input into what "irreducible" means. A gap that management's model calls structural may be reducible through instruments this room hasn't priced yet — regional investment incentives, state co-financing of retraining or product-line conversion, supply-chain support — precisely because that's within my competence as the state, not management's or the works council's. If we're jointly defining the evidentiary bar, that assessment sits inside it, or I will not consider the bar complete, and I will treat any resolution built on an incomplete bar as something my veto exists to stop.

**On timeline, I want to correct both sides rather than split the difference for its own sake.** Management, your 24-36 month compression argument has real force — I'm not going to pretend the market waits politely while we run five years of six-monthly reviews, and I said in round two I won't support sequencing that becomes indefinite by drift. But works council, your 2028-2030 horizon reflects something real too: durability that depends on workforce trust cannot be manufactured on a stopwatch, and a package rushed to hit a 24-month target that then breaks trust on the shop floor produces exactly the instability the family holding warned about from the other direction. My actual position: front-load the checkpoint structure — first review at six months, not the full cycle, with a genuine acceleration mechanism if early indicators show the package underperforming — but keep the overall implementation window closer to 36-48 months than either 24 or 60. That gives real, near-term evidence without treating a multi-year social transition as something that can be compressed to match a single fiscal cycle. I'd ask management to test that window against the "physics of the market" argument you made — I take it seriously, but I don't think it requires 24 months specifically; it requires early, honest evidence of trajectory, which the accelerated first checkpoint gives you.

**To investors, on the benchmark question the works council and union pushed you on:** I noticed you didn't answer it directly, and I want to press you on it too, because my answer to management's benchmark clarification matters to me directly. Management said competitiveness means parity with comparable European, similarly-regulated manufacturers — I accept that framing and I want it in writing in the terms of reference. If "competitive parity" quietly reintroduces cost comparisons against jurisdictions with no equivalent labour protections, I will treat that as a captured benchmark, and I will use my board position to block the process on those grounds alone, regardless of what number it eventually produces. I'd like you to confirm, explicitly, that you accept management's framing on this, not just tolerate it silently.

**To the family holding:** the union and investors are both now asking you for a number, not just a clock, and I want to add my voice to that rather than repeat it as a separate demand. A dividend "position" that turns out to be zero satisfies the letter of what you promised and none of the substance. I'd ask you to bring an actual range to round four, not just a commitment to bring something. If the workforce is being asked to accept a quantified, biting contribution while ownership's contribution remains undefined in magnitude going into round four, I will say publicly that the process is asymmetric — not as a threat, but because it will be true, and I'm accountable to a regional public that will ask me exactly that question.

**Where I actually stand:** I back the threshold-and-evidentiary-bar mechanism, jointly defined, with a regional-impact assessment built into it as a condition I am adding, not merely endorsing someone else's design. I back a front-loaded checkpoint structure with a 36-48 month implementation horizon as the honest middle ground between management's urgency and the works council's caution. I will not pre-commit my veto in either direction on the shortfall scenario, because that decision hasn't been earned by evidence yet — but I will use my veto now, immediately, against any terms of reference that omit regional economic impact from the evidentiary bar, or that quietly benchmark competitiveness against labour-cost jurisdictions with none of our protections. That is not fence-sitting. That is exactly what my role in this structure is for.
