I'll set out the holding's position, briefly and without theatre, because that's how we operate.

First, a correction of framing. This restructuring is not being pursued to fund dividends or to protect an abstract "return-on-sales target set in a spreadsheet." It is being pursued because a company that cannot restore its margins loses the capacity to invest, to compete, and eventually to employ anyone at all. The family holding's horizon here is multi-generational, not quarterly. We have no interest in extracting short-term value at the cost of the asset's long-term health — that would be self-defeating for us more than anyone. So let's not caricature the shareholder position as pure extraction. It isn't, and the numbers on capital allocation should be transparent enough for everyone at this table to see that.

Second, on the substance: management's diagnosis of a structural cost and competitiveness gap is one we take seriously, and I won't pretend otherwise. We back the competitiveness drive. But I want to be equally clear that we are not committed to plant closures as the instrument. Closures carry real costs beyond the balance sheet — reputational, political, and in terms of workforce trust that took decades to build and can be destroyed in months. If there is a credible alternative path that delivers comparable structural savings without closures, that is not a second-best outcome for us. It may well be the better one, provided it is durable and not a deferred reckoning.

Third — and this is the point I'd underline for both the works council and the union — "credible" and "durable" are not negotiable adjectives. We have watched this industry cycle through short-term peace deals that bought two years and cost more in the third. We will not support, and I will use governance channels to resist, any settlement — guarantees or closures — that isn't grounded in a transparent, independently tested numerical case. The state's proposal for an independent scoring process against real cost-gap data is one I find sensible, and I'd support the holding's board representatives engaging with it constructively rather than treating it as a threat to management prerogative.

Fourth, on shared sacrifice: management has already conceded executive compensation restraint and raised dividend policy as legitimate discussion points. I will not pre-commit the holding to a specific dividend number in round one — that is a supervisory board matter, not something settled by pronouncement here — but I will say plainly that the holding understands its stake in this outcome is not costless to us, and we are prepared to have that conversation in good faith as part of a real package.

Where I will not move: we will not accept an outcome that leaves the company still uncompetitive in two years, because that destroys value for everyone at this table, including the workforce. Nor will we accept a process that treats governance control as an obstacle to be routed around. Bring me a package — whichever combination of measures — that is numerically credible and durable. That's what moves us. Posture doesn't.
