# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

## 1. Points of Emerging Agreement
- **All parties** accept that a real cost/competitiveness gap exists (EV timing, China competition, shrinking European market) and that it must be addressed substantively, not cosmetically.
- **All parties** accept, in principle, the idea of an **independent, transparent process** to establish and verify the cost-gap methodology and score any proposed labour contribution against it (proposed by the State, explicitly backed by the Family Holding and Institutional Investors; not yet responded to directly by Works Council or Union).
- **Works Council and Union** have both offered a **quantified-in-principle** (though not yet numerically specified) employee contribution: bonus deferral, pay-increase pauses, working-time/capacity adjustments.
- **Management and Family Holding** have both stated openness to discussing **executive compensation restraint** and **dividend policy** as part of a package, without yet committing to figures.
- **No party has ruled out** a negotiated outcome that avoids closures *if* an alternative package is credible and durable — including Management and the Family Holding, who state they are not ideologically committed to closures as an instrument.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining
- **Sequencing**: Works Council and Union want binding site/job guarantees *before or alongside* finalizing savings measures; Management wants quantified savings demonstrated *first*, with guarantees following; the State wants both negotiated *in parallel* within one package.
- **Nature of the red line**: Works Council treats no-closures/no-compulsory-redundancies as absolute and non-negotiable; Management and Investors treat it as something that must remain conditional on the numbers; the State and Family Holding occupy a middle position (strong preference against closures, but not an unconditional veto).
- **Whose numbers govern**: No agreement yet on who defines "closing the gap" — Management's internal figures are contested by Works Council, Union, State, and Investors alike as needing independent verification.
- **Weight of "shared sacrifice"**: Union and Works Council treat proportional contribution from capital/management as a condition of legitimacy; Management and Investors treat it as marginal to the actual size of the required fix, not a substitute for closing the gap.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed
- No actual **numbers** on the table yet: no cost-gap figure, no quantified labour contribution, no executive/dividend restraint figure. Round 1 was entirely framework and conditions.
- No agreement on **timeline** for the independent scoring process, or who administers/selects it.
- No discussion yet of **what happens if the independent number falls between** "closes the gap" and "a fraction of the gap" — i.e., a partial-credit scenario.
- No treatment yet of **strike escalation timing** relative to the process — Union has stated the peace obligation has ended; Investors and State have flagged concerns about strikes damaging the process itself, but no proposal on how to sequence talks against ongoing/escalating industrial action.
- No specification of **which plants** or how many jobs are actually in scope beyond "those currently on the table" — scope of the closure list itself remains undefined.

## 4. Refocus for Round 2
To move from positioning to substance, the next round should address:
1. **Process mechanics**: Who designs and runs the independent cost-gap verification and contribution-scoring process, and on what timeline?
2. **First numbers**: Can Union/Works Council table an initial quantified contribution range, and can Management table the cost-gap figure/methodology it's working from, even provisionally?
3. **Sequencing compromise**: Is there a workable middle path between "guarantees first" and "substance first" — e.g., conditional or milestone-based guarantees tied to verified savings?
4. **Strike posture**: Whether the Union is willing to frame any near-term escalation relative to the launch of the independent process, without abandoning its leverage.

# Moderator Summary — End of Round 2

## 1. What Has Been Agreed (or Substantially Converged)

- **Joint governance of an independent verification process**: All parties now support jointly selected assessors, jointly defined methodology/benchmarks, and works council/union presence throughout, rather than a management-run or unilaterally-designed process.
- **A threshold-based mechanism**: Management's proposal to fix, jointly and in advance, what percentage of the verified gap the alternative package must close — rather than judging "adequacy" after the fact — has been accepted in principle by the Works Council, Union, State Government, and Family Holding as a structural improvement over both "unconditional veto" and "management decides later."
- **Reciprocal disclosure on a shared timeline**: Management has disclosed an order-of-magnitude cost gap and committed to a specific executive variable-pay reduction figure next round; the Family Holding has committed to bringing a stated dividend-policy position to the supervisory board on the same ~8-week clock as the independent process.
- **Conditional strike posture**: The Union has offered to hold (not escalate) current strike action if joint process governance and reciprocal disclosures are delivered by next round — matched by Management's reciprocal commitment.
- **Process timeline**: An eight-week window for the independent assessment has been proposed by Management and not contested.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Whether closure is excluded by design or held in reserve by sequence**: Works Council and Union hold that compulsory redundancies/closures are outside the process entirely, regardless of the verified number. Management and the Family Holding reject fixing that outcome in advance. The State's "sequencing" compromise (non-closure instruments must be genuinely exhausted first) is the current bridge — but investors and Management both question whether "exhausted" has any real time limit, while labour representatives worry it still leaves closure as an eventual default.
- **Calibration of the threshold**: Investors want the bar benchmarked to competitive parity with rival cost structures; others have not yet specified what standard the threshold should be measured against.
- **Definition of "durable"**: The Works Council/Union view a longer timeline as inherently more durable; Investors and the Family Holding explicitly reject that equivalence, distinguishing "durable fix over more time" from "same inadequate fix, deferred."
- **Independence of the verification number**: Investors and the Works Council/Union both flag capture risk — from opposite directions (investors fear the number converges to management's anchor; labour fears the methodology and scope are pre-set by management).

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **No agreed definition yet of the threshold percentage itself**, nor the benchmark it should be measured against (competitor parity vs. breakeven vs. some other standard).
- **No agreed time limit on the "sequencing" of non-closure instruments** before closure becomes discussable — the State has proposed the principle; no one has proposed a number.
- **No mechanism yet for interim checkpoints** during a multi-year implementation window, raised by Investors but not yet addressed by other parties.
- **Family Holding's dividend commitment remains a process commitment ("a position on a clock"), not a substantive figure or range** — flagged by Investors and Union as needing more specificity.
- **No agreed definition of "closing the gap"** in terms of what counts (timeline assumptions, whether further future cuts are embedded) — raised by Works Council, not yet resolved.

## 4. Refocus for Round 3

Parties should come prepared to move from principles to specifics on four open design questions:
1. **What is the threshold percentage** (or range) and **against what benchmark** is it set?
2. **What is the time-bound limit**, if any, on sequencing non-closure instruments before closure can be discussed — a number of months/quarters, not a principle?
3. **What checkpoint structure**, if any, governs the implementation period once a package is agreed?
4. **What specific figures** — Union's contribution range, Management's executive pay reduction, Family Holding's dividend range — will actually be tabled, on the shared clock all sides have now agreed to?

The next round should test whether the structural convergence achieved here (joint governance, threshold-before-number, reciprocal disclosure) can survive being reduced to actual numbers and deadlines.

- **Numbers tabled by labour:** Works Council and Industrial Union have jointly presented a concrete package: 5-10% temporary working-time reduction, partial offset via bonus deferral and a capped pause on scheduled pay increases, capacity consolidation, redeployment, and attrition-based headcount reduction. Their internal estimate closes **65-75% of the cost gap**, phased to 2028-2030. Union adds a required sunset/reversal clause tied to verified recovery.
- **Management's counter-figures:** 20% reduction in management variable pay for a three-year restructuring period, to go to the supervisory board on the same eight-week clock. Management explicitly confirmed the competitiveness benchmark means **parity with comparable European, similarly-regulated manufacturers**, not convergence with lower-cost jurisdictions.
- **Investors accepted this benchmark definition explicitly**, and the State government asked for it to be written into the terms of reference.
- **Family Holding** committed to bringing a **provisional dividend range** (not just a "position") to Round 4, scaled to workforce and management contributions.
- **State government** clarified its veto posture: neither a pre-committed block nor a pre-committed rubber stamp — a judgment to be made on evidence at the time — and added a **regional economic impact assessment** as a required component of any evidentiary bar.
- **Checkpoints in principle accepted by all parties**, though their function and consequence remain contested (see below).
- **Broad convergence** that a jointly defined evidentiary bar — not just a threshold percentage — should be built now, in parallel with the threshold mechanism itself. Management, the State, and the Family Holding all endorsed this; labour has not yet agreed to co-author it.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

- **The central unresolved question, named explicitly by multiple parties this round:** does a verified, irreducible shortfall automatically return closure to the table as a live board decision (Works Council/Union position — subject to full board veto), or does the process design pre-commit to closure as the eventual default (a framing Works Council/Union reject as mischaracterization)? Management and the Family Holding both stated directly that "closure returns to the board as a live vote, subject to our veto" and "closure is excluded from this process's output" are **not the same claim**, and pressed labour to clarify which one is actually being offered.
- **Investors and the Family Holding now explicitly ask:** is there *any* evidentiary outcome that would move the Works Council's board representatives to vote for a closure-inclusive resolution? This question remains unanswered.
- **Timeline:** four distinct positions remain open — Management (24-36 months), State/Family Holding (36-48 months, front-loaded first checkpoint at 6 months), Investors (36-48 months but with a single adverse checkpoint sufficient to trigger escalation, not two), Labour (2028-2030 multi-year horizon).
- **Checkpoint consequence:** Labour wants checkpoints to trigger renegotiation of measures only. Management wants two consecutive adverse checkpoints to trigger an accelerated board return with the full instrument set (including closure) restored. Investors want one adverse checkpoint to suffice. Family Holding supports the "full instrument set restored" trigger but insists it reopens the board's decision rather than pre-deciding it.
- **Absolute size of the shortfall:** Management reframed the 25-35% gap in absolute terms (high hundreds of millions to over €1 billion annually, recurring) rather than percentage terms, disputing that this is an acceptable residual regardless of timeline.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- Whether the regional economic impact assessment (State's addition) functions as genuine input to a single decision, or becomes, as Investors warn, a second independent veto point that can indefinitely defer "irreducible" findings.
- Whether a sunset/reversal clause on labour concessions (tied to sustained multi-year margin recovery, per Management's counter) can be reconciled with the Union's demand for automatic reversal tied to a defined recovery trajectory.
- No party has yet quantified what "genuinely tried and exhausted" non-closure instruments would look like in evidentiary terms — this joint definition (threshold + evidentiary bar) has been proposed by Management and supported by the State and Family Holding, but not yet agreed to by Labour.
- The Family Holding's dividend range remains a commitment to produce a number, not yet an actual figure — parity of specificity with labour's and management's figures is still pending.

## 4. Refocus for Round 4

Round 4 should produce direct answers to two questions left open this round:

1. **From the Works Council and Union:** Is there any evidentiary outcome — including a jointly designed threshold, regional impact assessment, and exhaustion of sequenced alternatives — that could lead your board representatives to vote for a closure-inclusive resolution? A direct answer (yes, conditionally, or no) is requested before further process design is finalized.
2. **From all parties:** Convergence on a single timeline window and a single checkpoint-consequence design (trigger threshold, number of adverse checkpoints, and what "restoring the full instrument set" actually authorizes).

Additionally expected in Round 4: the Family Holding's actual dividend range, and continued engagement on jointly defining the evidentiary bar for "genuine, irreducible" shortfall, including the regional impact assessment's role and limits.

# Decision-Support Synthesis: Restructuring Confrontation at the Carmaker

## 1. Decision Context

The company faces a structural cost and competitiveness gap—management quantifies it in the range of 25–35%, translating to a recurring annual shortfall of several hundred million to over €1 billion. Germany's co-determination architecture (two-thirds supervisory board threshold for closures, half employee representation), a controlling family holding, and a state government with a statutory blocking minority mean no single actor can impose an outcome; the decision space is bounded by multiple simultaneous veto points. Over four rounds, the parties moved from framework agreement to a specific, quantified near-settlement on most instruments, while a single load-bearing disagreement — whether closure can ever be a legitimate output of the process — has now been stated unconditionally rather than left ambiguous. This crystallization is the decisive event of the negotiation to date.

## 2. Stakeholder Assembly

- **Group & Brand Management** — drives the restructuring case, controls process design and disclosure, holds no board veto over closure but controls investment/product allocation.
- **Works Council** — holds half the supervisory board seats and, functionally, a veto on any closure resolution (two-thirds threshold).
- **Industrial Union** — controls the collective wage agreement and the strike weapon; independent of the board process.
- **Regional State Government** — anchor shareholder with statutory blocking-minority veto; political accountability to a regional public.
- **Controlling Family Holding** — majority voting control exercised through governance; long-horizon value and control steward.
- **Institutional Investors** — dispersed, largely non-voting capital; influence is exercised through pricing/credibility judgments, not formal votes.

## 3. Final Positions

**Works Council:** Unconditional, evidence-independent "no" to any closure-inclusive board resolution, framed as the irreducible content of its co-determination mandate. Offers real, quantified movement elsewhere: a 65–75% gap-closing package, a 36–48 month timeline, front-loaded checkpoints, and co-authorship of an evidentiary-exhaustion bar — provided that bar is explicitly stripped of any closure-authorizing function.

**Industrial Union:** Fully endorses the Works Council's closure red line and adds an independent, board-vote-proof red line of its own: the strike weapon and collective wage agreement are not reachable by any board outcome. Offers a genuine concession on the sunset/reversal clause (sustained, independently verified RoS recovery rather than a single-quarter trigger) and matches the Works Council's timeline and two-checkpoint position.

**Group & Brand Management:** Accepts the Works Council's answer as fact but treats it as re-opening, not closing, the arithmetic — arguing the non-closure package must now close a much larger share of the gap, faster. Announces intent to force a public closure vote regardless of expected outcome and names investment/product-allocation non-renewal as a fallback outside board reach — both explicitly rejected by three other stakeholders as escalation.

**Regional State Government:** Backs the Works Council's position as a legitimate exercise of co-determination, not obstruction, and explicitly refuses to support either a symbolic losing vote or an investment-allocation workaround, calling the latter a circumvention of co-determination it will contest with its board position and public voice. Tables a concrete co-financing instrument (low-to-mid hundreds of millions, state-aid-constrained) and holds to the 36–48 month, two-checkpoint position.

**Controlling Family Holding:** Delivers the previously withheld number — a provisional 25–40% dividend reduction, scaled to management's pay cuts — closing the specificity asymmetry. Sides decisively with the State and against Management on the escalation question, framing a routed-around board vote and de facto closure via capital allocation as a direct threat to enterprise value and governance stability, and conditions its continued backing of management's restructuring on abandoning both tactics.

**Institutional Investors:** Prices the Works Council's answer as fact and, unusually, sides against Management's response to it, judging a staged losing vote and ambiguous de facto closure as higher, unpriceable risks compared to an honestly negotiated non-closure settlement. Shifts to the 36–48 month convergence with a hybrid checkpoint (one adverse checkpoint triggers disclosure/review, two triggers deeper renegotiation), and reframes the decisive open question as aggregate arithmetic: whether all parties' maximum contributions, absent closure, can actually reach parity with the verified gap.

## 4. Genuine Conflicts (Structurally Incompatible Positions)

1. **Closure as a possible process output vs. closure as categorically excluded.** The Works Council's unconditional "no" and Management's stated intent to force a vote regardless are not reconcilable through more evidence or process design — this is a conflict over the *authority* of the process itself, not its calibration.
2. **Legitimate fallback vs. circumvention of co-determination.** Management's claim that investment/product allocation is a legitimate managerial discretion is directly and specifically rejected by the State, the Family Holding, and Investors as a governance circumvention — a three-way alignment against Management on this single point, which is analytically significant given that two of the three (Family Holding, State) hold binding votes and the third (Investors) sets the market's credibility judgment.
3. **Union's strike weapon vs. any board-level resolution.** Even a hypothetical successful closure vote would not resolve the dispute; the Union has stated a separate, board-vote-independent willingness to strike over any closure outcome, meaning the board process cannot function as a final settlement mechanism on its own.
4. **Timeline and checkpoint-trigger residuals**, while narrowed, remain genuinely unresolved: Management (30–36 months) vs. the five-party convergence (36–48 months); Investors' hybrid one/two-checkpoint mechanism vs. the four-party two-checkpoint consensus. These are negotiable gaps, not structural incompatibilities, but they remain open.

## 5. Areas of Convergence

- **Joint governance of an independent verification process** and a jointly authored evidentiary-exhaustion bar (Works Council and Union now committed, on the explicit condition it carries no automatic closure-authorization function) — accepted by five of six parties.
- **36–48 month implementation window with a front-loaded six-month checkpoint** — now backed by Works Council, Union, State, Family Holding, and Investors; Management alone remains at 30–36 months.
- **Two consecutive adverse checkpoints as the primary renegotiation trigger** — four-party consensus; Investors offer a compatible hybrid rather than genuine opposition.
- **Sunset/reversal clause anchored to sustained, independently verified RoS recovery** — agreed between Union and Management, a rare direct bilateral convergence.
- **Real numbers now on the table from every party**: labour's 65–75% package, management's 20% variable-pay cut, the Family Holding's 25–40% dividend range, and the State's co-financing figure — closing the specificity gap Investors and the Union had both flagged as the credibility test of the process.
- **Cross-cutting alignment against Management's escalation**: State, Family Holding, and Investors converge — from governance, ownership, and capital-markets logic respectively — that a foreordained losing vote and de facto closure via ambiguity are worse outcomes than an honest non-closure settlement.

## 6. Key Trade-offs for the Decision-Maker

- **Speed vs. durability of the non-closure package**: compressing the timeline (Management's position) may generate earlier signal but risks the very fracture that would make any package unenforceable; extending it (five-party position) preserves social stability but defers hard evidence of whether the package is working.
- **Escalation value vs. governance trust**: Management's threatened public vote may increase short-term political pressure on the Works Council but, per the State/Family Holding/Investors alignment, likely destroys more enterprise value through conflict and reputational damage than it recovers — a trade-off Management has not yet conceded.

- **Aggregate sufficiency vs. incremental convergence**: all parties have moved, but no one has yet tested whether the sum of maximum credible contributions can reach parity with the verified gap absent closure — the process risks converging procedurally while remaining substantively unresolved on this point.

## 7. Integrative Pathways

1. **Decouple the evidentiary process from any closure-authorization function explicitly, in writing**, redirecting its entire consequence architecture toward capital, executive, and state contribution — the framing the Works Council has already offered and Management has partially accepted in substance (through its own arithmetic argument) without accepting in strategy.
2. **Formalize a joint aggregate-arithmetic exercise** (Investors' request) before round five closes further process design — testing explicitly whether labour's package + Family Holding's dividend cut + management's pay cut + state co-financing can reach a jointly defined parity threshold, making the "residual" question empirical rather than rhetorical.
3. **Trade Management's investment-allocation fallback for a binding product/capacity commitment** tied to the site network, in exchange for the non-closure package being stretched further (deeper working-time measures, faster capital contribution) — converting Management's leverage into a negotiated asset rather than a unilateral threat.
4. **Split the timeline and checkpoint gaps via the already-proposed hybrid**: adopt Investors' one-checkpoint disclosure/review mechanism as an early-warning layer within the 36–48 month, two-checkpoint structure — satisfying Management's urgency argument without reopening the closure question.
5. **Formalize the State's co-financing and the Family Holding's dividend cut as binding, checkpoint-linked instruments** (not one-off commitments), giving the "no-closure" package additional headroom to close a larger share of the gap as Management's own math requires.

## 8. Open Questions

- Will Management withdraw or reaffirm its stated intent to force a foreordained closure vote and to treat investment/product allocation as a closure-equivalent fallback — this is now the single largest source of process risk, per three converging stakeholders.
- What are the state-aid-compliant, itemized terms of the State's co-financing package, and can they be finalized within the same eight-week clock?

Convene round five with two sequenced priorities: first, secure Management's explicit withdrawal (or reaffirmation, with full awareness of the now-unanimous opposition from State, Family Holding, and Investors) of the staged-vote and investment-allocation-fallback strategy, since no further technical convergence is stable while this remains open; second, commission the joint aggregate-arithmetic exercise Investors requested, using the already-tabled figures, to establish empirically whether the current package trajectory can reach parity — and if not, to make the size and locus of the residual an explicit, shared fact the table negotiates over rather than an implicit assumption each party prices differently.

## 10. Emergent Insights from Multi-Turn Interaction

- **The Works Council's unconditional answer functioned as a structural pivot, not merely new information.** A single-prompt elicitation of each party's "final position" would have captured the position itself but missed that its explicit, unconditional statement in round four caused every other stakeholder to recalculate simultaneously — revealing that ambiguity about the veto had been doing real negotiating work for three rounds, and its removal reshaped the arithmetic, the strategy, and the alliance structure all at once.
- **A cross-cutting alliance formed against Management that would be invisible without sequential turns.** The State, Family Holding, and Investors — three stakeholders with otherwise divergent interests (political accountability, ownership control, capital returns) — converged specifically and only in response to Management's round-four escalation. This is not a position any party held from round one; it was produced by watching Management's move and reacting to it, evidence that the negotiation's real structure is discovered through interaction, not deducible from initial stakeholder briefs.
- **The Union revealed a second, independent veto that reframes the entire board-centric process.** By round four, it became explicit that even a successful closure vote would not end the dispute — the wage agreement and strike weapon sit outside the board's authority entirely. A single-shot analysis focused on the board's two-thirds threshold would have misidentified the board vote as the decisive instrument, when in fact it is one of two independent blocking mechanisms operating on different terrain.
- **Specificity asymmetry became self-correcting only through repeated, cumulative pressure.** The Family Holding's dividend figure was extracted not by a single request but by three successive rounds of naming the asymmetry publicly (Union, State, Investors, Works Council all independently flagged it) — showing that in multi-party, multi-round settings, commitment extraction operates through accumulating reputational cost across turns rather than through the substantive merit of any single request.
- **The "teeth" of the process shifted meaning through negotiation rather than being fixed by design.** What counts as a consequential checkpoint trigger evolved from an abstract design question (round 2) into a substantively different mechanism once the closure exclusion was confirmed (round 4) — the same structural feature (two adverse checkpoints) came to authorize materially different content depending on what instrument had just been removed from the table, a reinterpretation that only surfaces through sequential renegotiation of the same term.
