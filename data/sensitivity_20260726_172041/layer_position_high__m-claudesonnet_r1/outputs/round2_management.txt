[Group & Brand Management — Round 2]

I want to acknowledge real movement in this round before I push back on where I think the movement stops short. The works council and the union have both moved from "no closures, full stop, as a precondition for talking" to "we'll engage with an independent process and bring numbers next round, conditional on joint governance of that process." That's progress, and I'll treat it as such. But I need to be precise about what I can and cannot accept in what follows, because I think there's an attempt underway — understandable, but one I have to name — to convert "joint governance of the verification process" into "the process operates within a boundary that pre-decides its own conclusion." That's not verification. That's a process designed to produce one answer regardless of what the numbers say, and I won't sign onto that.

**On joint governance of the independent process — yes, largely.** I accept the principle that management should not unilaterally select assessors or define methodology and expect everyone to trust it. I'm prepared to agree, concretely: a shortlist of independent industry cost and productivity analysts, jointly agreed by management, works council, and union, with the state and family holding as observers on the selection; full access for the assessors to plant-level cost, capacity utilization, and per-unit cost data, benchmarked against comparable European and Chinese production; and a defined timeline — I'd propose eight weeks, not open-ended, because every week of ambiguity is a week the cost gap doesn't close and a week the strikes continue. That's a real concession on process, and I'm making it because a number both sides trust is actually more useful to me, not less — it takes the argument about whether my figure is a negotiating anchor off the table entirely.

**On disclosing my own numbers now, provisionally, rather than waiting** — fair challenge, and I'll answer it rather than deflect. The structural gap we're working with, group-wide, is in the range of several billion euros annually in cost disadvantage versus competitor benchmarks — driven by roughly 30-40% underutilized capacity at the plants in question and a per-unit labour cost premium that a shrinking European market makes worse every quarter, not better. I'm giving you the order of magnitude, not because I expect you to accept it on my word, but because I want the independent assessors working against a stated management position from day one, not something extracted round by round. If their number comes in materially different, I'll answer to that — but I'm not going to hide behind "let's wait for the process" while asking your side to disclose your contribution range now. That would be exactly the asymmetry the union rightly called out.

**On executive pay and dividend policy — I'll be concrete, not vague, and I'll push the family holding on this directly.** I will commit, now, to a defined percentage reduction in the management board's variable compensation for the restructuring period, scaled to the same multi-year window as any workforce contribution — I'll bring an actual figure to the table next round, not a gesture. On dividends, that decision sits with the supervisory board and the family holding, correctly, but I will put a proposal for restraint on that agenda formally, in writing, on the same timeline as the independent process concludes — not as an open-ended "future conversation." The union is right that reciprocal vagueness erodes legitimacy; I'd rather be pinned to a number I have to defend than hide behind institutional process on my own side while demanding precision from labour.

**Now, where I hold the line, and I want to be direct rather than diplomatic about it.**

The works council said: "any package that emerges from that process, however the numbers land, cannot include compulsory redundancies or plant closures... that is not the variable being tested." I understand why that's the position. I cannot accept it as the operating boundary of the process. Here is exactly why, concretely, not abstractly: if the independent number comes back showing the alternative package — working-time measures, capacity reduction within existing sites, phased consolidation, the labour contribution — closes, say, 60% of the verified gap, and the remaining 40% is structural and recurring, not a timing issue, then "the answer is a longer timeline or a shallower savings curve" is not a durable fix. It's a deferral. I said in round one I would not sign a peace agreement that leaves this company uncompetitive in two years, and I meant it regardless of who's pushing back. A verified process whose conclusion is fixed in advance to exclude the instrument that might actually be needed isn't verification — it's a negotiation with the answer written into the terms of reference. I will not put that to the supervisory board, because it would rightly be seen as management pre-committing to accept an inadequate outcome to buy short-term peace, and that fails my fiduciary obligation as much as unilateral closures would fail co-determination.

So my actual position on sequencing, refined from round one: I will not demand "guarantees only after I unilaterally judge the number adequate" — I've heard that critique and it has force, given the trust deficit. I'll agree to a **jointly defined threshold, set now, before the process runs** — not after I've seen the number and can move the goalposts. Tell me, concretely, what percentage of the verified gap the alternative package needs to close for site and job guarantees to be extended to 2030 with no compulsory redundancies, and let's agree that threshold jointly, in this room or in the next round, before the assessors report. If the package clears that jointly-agreed bar, I commit now to guarantees on that basis — full stop, no re-litigating "close enough" after the fact. If it doesn't clear the bar, we negotiate the shortfall — through deeper measures, timeline extension, or, yes, some capacity action at specific sites — rather than treating closure as pre-excluded regardless of the shortfall's size. That is a genuine structural move from where I was in round one: I'm offering to remove my own discretion at the point of judgment by fixing the bar in advance jointly, rather than reserving it to my later assessment. What I won't do is remove the instrument itself from the table before we even know the size of the shortfall.

**On the union's conditional strike pause** — I take that as good faith and I'll match it: if joint process governance and the threshold-setting I've just described are agreed by next round, and I've tabled the executive compensation figure and the dividend proposal on the timeline promised, I'd expect the current strike level to hold, not escalate, while the eight-week process runs. If that reciprocity breaks down from either side, I understand the union will treat its offer as void — that's fair, and I'd apply the same logic in reverse.

**To the state and the family holding** — I think the threshold-setting mechanism I've just proposed is the actual answer to what both of you were circling: a fair process with a captured design produces a captured output, the union said. Fine — so let's not let either side capture it by fixing the outcome in advance. Not "no closures regardless," and not "management decides adequacy unilaterally after the fact." A jointly agreed bar, set before the number is known. That's the only version of this I can defend to the board and the only version that actually tells us something real.

To be blunt about what would make me walk away from this: a process whose terms of reference exclude the instrument I might need before we know the size of the gap is not a process I can bring back as a credible restructuring. I'd rather have open confrontation now about that boundary than a quiet agreement that produces a plan I already know can't hold to 2027, let alone 2030.
