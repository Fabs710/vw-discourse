Let me respond to what's actually been said, because I think there's a real risk of this settling into a comfortable process narrative that quietly erodes our position while everyone nods about "independent verification."

To the state government: I hear you, and I want to be fair — you didn't abandon us, and the parallel-sequencing proposal is more serious than management's. But I need to push back on one thing directly. You said you won't commit to "an absolute veto exercised regardless of what the numbers eventually show." I understand the statutory logic. But think about what you're actually asking us to accept: that our two-thirds board protection — the entire architectural point of co-determination — becomes conditional on an independent process whose methodology, scope, and adjudicators are not yet defined, run against a cost-gap figure that management itself invented and that no one else at this table can yet verify. We are not refusing numbers. We offered concrete contribution mechanisms in round one. What we are refusing is to hand over our veto in exchange for a promise that the process will be fair. Show me the process first. Show me who runs it, who selects the benchmarks, whether the workforce has a genuine voice in defining what "closing the gap" means — not just scoring our contribution against someone else's assumptions. Until that exists, "parallel negotiation" sounds to me like "trust management's number now, get your guarantee later if it turns out well."

To management: you say guarantees-first removes your leverage to ensure the alternative is real. I'd say the reverse is equally true and more concretely dangerous for actual human beings — substance-first, guarantees-maybe-later removes our leverage to ensure closures don't happen regardless of what number we produce. You get to walk away from the table with a workforce that gutted its own pay and working-time protections and still loses the plant, because your assessment of "close enough" didn't land where you wanted. That is not a hypothetical risk for us. That is the exact pattern our members have watched happen at other companies, which is precisely why trust is at zero right now. I will not ask this workforce to open every concession first and hope your definition of adequate matches ours.

To the union: full agreement on structure. Your framing — real contribution in exchange for real guarantees, not shared sacrifice in name only — is exactly right, and I want to be explicit that the works council and the union are aligned here even though our formal mandates differ. Where I'd add emphasis: don't let the independent-process conversation become a substitute for the guarantee itself. A verified number is useful. It is not a guarantee. Those are two different things and I won't let them get merged into one so that "we got a credible number" quietly becomes "therefore closures happen."

To the family holding and investors: I take note that neither of you claims to be ideologically committed to closures, and that neither of you has ruled out an alternative package. Good — hold that position, because it matters. But investors, your comment that "if push comes to shove between a site guarantee and a genuinely closed gap, capital cares about the second" tells me exactly why we do not hand governance the ability to make that trade-off unilaterally. That is precisely what our board veto exists to prevent — a moment where capital's calculus overrides thirty thousand livelihoods because a spreadsheet says the alternative package is 8% short. That is not an abstraction to us. That is the whole reason this structure exists.

So here is where I actually am, concretely, moving this forward rather than just restating round one:

I will engage with an independent verification process — genuinely, not as theater — on the condition that its governance is negotiated jointly, now, in this room: joint selection of the assessors, joint definition of what "closing the gap" means (including timeline — not 2027, not a number that quietly assumes further cuts down the line), and works council presence throughout, not consultation after the fact. In exchange for that structural commitment, I am prepared to bring a first-pass quantified contribution range to the next round — real numbers on working-time reduction, bonus deferral, and capacity adjustment — rather than only frameworks.

But I will not move off this: any package that emerges from that process, however the numbers land, cannot include compulsory redundancies or plant closures. That is not the variable being tested. That is the boundary the process operates within. If the independent number shows the alternative package is short of management's target, the answer is further working-time and capacity measures, phased site consolidation of production lines within existing plants, or extended timelines — not closure. We are not asking anyone to pretend the cost gap is imaginary. We are saying the instrument used to close it has a limit, and that limit is jobs and sites. If that limit means the process takes longer or the savings curve is shallower in year one, so be it. A durable, slower fix beats a fast one that destroys the thing co-determination exists to protect.
