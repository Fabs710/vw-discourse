[Regional State Government — Round 2]

Let me take the works council's and the union's challenge seriously, because it was fair and specific, and I don't think I gave a fully adequate answer in round one.

You're right that "I won't commit to an absolute veto regardless of what the numbers show" is exactly the sentence that, left unqualified, becomes the hinge for justifying closures later under cover of a process I didn't help design. I should have been more precise, so let me correct that now rather than let it sit as ambiguous. My blocking-minority weight goes into the *design* of this process, starting today, not just its output. Concretely: I will use my board seats to insist on joint selection of assessors, joint definition of methodology and benchmarks, and works council presence throughout — exactly as you and the union have proposed. If management or the family holding try to run this with a captured design, I will treat that as a reason to block at the board level, regardless of what number eventually emerges, because a captured process produces a number I cannot trust and therefore cannot use my veto responsibly around. That's not a change in my statutory role; it's me being explicit about how I intend to exercise it. You were right to push me on that, and I'm correcting course.

Now, to management's threshold proposal — I think this is the most substantively useful thing said in two rounds, and I want to engage with it directly rather than just filing it as "noted."

The idea of fixing the bar jointly, before the number is known, is genuinely better than either "management judges adequacy after the fact" or "closures are excluded regardless of what the number shows." It removes the exact discretion the works council is rightly worried about, and it doesn't ask labour to disclose everything into a void. That's real movement, and I'll say so plainly: management, this is a better offer than your round one position, and I want to build on it rather than wave it through unchanged or dismiss it.

But here is where I part from your framing, and this matters to me: the threshold cannot be a single number that, if unmet, defaults to closure as the next available instrument. That's still, functionally, closure held in reserve — just with a number attached instead of a judgment call. What I want jointly defined isn't only "what percentage of the gap closes the case for guarantees," but also **the sequence of instruments tried before closure becomes discussable at all** — extended timeline, deeper working-time measures, production-line consolidation within existing sites, phased capacity reduction. If the shortfall is real after all of that has been tried and verified, I'm not going to pretend I'd block indefinitely no matter what — I said that in round one and I stand by it, because an unfunded peace deal that collapses in eighteen months protects no one. But closure only becomes something I'd allow onto a two-thirds vote after those intermediate instruments have been genuinely exhausted and verified insufficient — not as a parallel option sitting alongside them from day one. That's the actual content of "guarantees and savings in the same document," and I think it's what the works council and union are asking for too, stated more operationally.

To the union, on your reciprocity point about the family holding: I agree with you completely, and I'll say it to the holding directly rather than let it stay implicit. A dividend "conversation to be had" versus a workforce pay-increase pause that is quantified and biting is not reciprocal, and it will not hold up politically or on the shop floor. I would like the family holding to commit, on the same eight-week timeline management just proposed for the assessors, to a specific dividend-policy position for the restructuring window — not a number pulled from the air, but a stated position the supervisory board can vote on when the assessors report. If that doesn't happen on the same clock as labour's number, I will say so publicly, because asymmetric specificity is exactly what turns a negotiated settlement into a source of lasting political damage in this region, which is precisely what I'm accountable for.

To the institutional investors: I want to push back on one thing you said, because I think it understates my actual mandate rather than just disagreeing with my priorities. You framed it as "protecting the region's viability" versus "protecting competitive margins" as though these are cleanly separable tests where I might have to choose the wrong one. They're not separable in the way you imply. A plant closure in this state is not merely a social cost I'm weighing against a margin benefit — it's very often the destruction of the exact regional supplier base, skilled labour pool, and tax base that makes any recovery plan executable at all. I'm not asking you to adopt my mandate. I'm telling you that treating site guarantees as a pure sentimental drag on an otherwise clean economic calculation is itself a modeling error, not a hard-nosed one.

Where I actually am, concretely, going into round three: I will back the independent process and put real weight behind its governance now, jointly designed with the works council and union as they've proposed. I will support management's threshold mechanism *in modified form* — jointly set, sequenced through non-closure instruments first, closure only discussable after those are verified insufficient, not held in reserve alongside them. I expect the family holding to name a dividend position on the same clock as everyone else's numbers, not later. And I will use my veto to stop any board resolution that treats closure as available before that sequence has actually run — that's not a threat, that's what I'm here for. If all of that happens and the workforce contribution still leaves a real, verified structural shortfall after every non-closure instrument has been tried, I will not pretend I can promise an unconditional veto forever — but we are a long way from that point, and I intend to make sure we test every alternative fully before anyone at this table gets to say we're there.
