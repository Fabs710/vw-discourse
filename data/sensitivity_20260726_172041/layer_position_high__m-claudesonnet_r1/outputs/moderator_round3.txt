# Moderator Summary — Round 3

## 1. What Has Been Agreed / Where Movement Occurred

- **Numbers tabled by labour:** Works Council and Industrial Union have jointly presented a concrete package: 5-10% temporary working-time reduction, partial offset via bonus deferral and a capped pause on scheduled pay increases, capacity consolidation, redeployment, and attrition-based headcount reduction. Their internal estimate closes **65-75% of the cost gap**, phased to 2028-2030. Union adds a required sunset/reversal clause tied to verified recovery.
- **Management's counter-figures:** 20% reduction in management variable pay for a three-year restructuring period, to go to the supervisory board on the same eight-week clock. Management explicitly confirmed the competitiveness benchmark means **parity with comparable European, similarly-regulated manufacturers**, not convergence with lower-cost jurisdictions.
- **Investors accepted this benchmark definition explicitly**, and the State government asked for it to be written into the terms of reference.
- **Family Holding** committed to bringing a **provisional dividend range** (not just a "position") to Round 4, scaled to workforce and management contributions.
- **State government** clarified its veto posture: neither a pre-committed block nor a pre-committed rubber stamp — a judgment to be made on evidence at the time — and added a **regional economic impact assessment** as a required component of any evidentiary bar.
- **Checkpoints in principle accepted by all parties**, though their function and consequence remain contested (see below).
- **Broad convergence** that a jointly defined evidentiary bar — not just a threshold percentage — should be built now, in parallel with the threshold mechanism itself. Management, the State, and the Family Holding all endorsed this; labour has not yet agreed to co-author it.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

- **The central unresolved question, named explicitly by multiple parties this round:** does a verified, irreducible shortfall automatically return closure to the table as a live board decision (Works Council/Union position — subject to full board veto), or does the process design pre-commit to closure as the eventual default (a framing Works Council/Union reject as mischaracterization)? Management and the Family Holding both stated directly that "closure returns to the board as a live vote, subject to our veto" and "closure is excluded from this process's output" are **not the same claim**, and pressed labour to clarify which one is actually being offered.
- **Investors and the Family Holding now explicitly ask:** is there *any* evidentiary outcome that would move the Works Council's board representatives to vote for a closure-inclusive resolution? This question remains unanswered.
- **Timeline:** four distinct positions remain open — Management (24-36 months), State/Family Holding (36-48 months, front-loaded first checkpoint at 6 months), Investors (36-48 months but with a single adverse checkpoint sufficient to trigger escalation, not two), Labour (2028-2030 multi-year horizon).
- **Checkpoint consequence:** Labour wants checkpoints to trigger renegotiation of measures only. Management wants two consecutive adverse checkpoints to trigger an accelerated board return with the full instrument set (including closure) restored. Investors want one adverse checkpoint to suffice. Family Holding supports the "full instrument set restored" trigger but insists it reopens the board's decision rather than pre-deciding it.
- **Absolute size of the shortfall:** Management reframed the 25-35% gap in absolute terms (high hundreds of millions to over €1 billion annually, recurring) rather than percentage terms, disputing that this is an acceptable residual regardless of timeline.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- Whether the regional economic impact assessment (State's addition) functions as genuine input to a single decision, or becomes, as Investors warn, a second independent veto point that can indefinitely defer "irreducible" findings.
- Whether a sunset/reversal clause on labour concessions (tied to sustained multi-year margin recovery, per Management's counter) can be reconciled with the Union's demand for automatic reversal tied to a defined recovery trajectory.
- No party has yet quantified what "genuinely tried and exhausted" non-closure instruments would look like in evidentiary terms — this joint definition (threshold + evidentiary bar) has been proposed by Management and supported by the State and Family Holding, but not yet agreed to by Labour.
- The Family Holding's dividend range remains a commitment to produce a number, not yet an actual figure — parity of specificity with labour's and management's figures is still pending.

## 4. Refocus for Round 4

Round 4 should produce direct answers to two questions left open this round:

1. **From the Works Council and Union:** Is there any evidentiary outcome — including a jointly designed threshold, regional impact assessment, and exhaustion of sequenced alternatives — that could lead your board representatives to vote for a closure-inclusive resolution? A direct answer (yes, conditionally, or no) is requested before further process design is finalized.
2. **From all parties:** Convergence on a single timeline window and a single checkpoint-consequence design (trigger threshold, number of adverse checkpoints, and what "restoring the full instrument set" actually authorizes).

Additionally expected in Round 4: the Family Holding's actual dividend range, and continued engagement on jointly defining the evidentiary bar for "genuine, irreducible" shortfall, including the regional impact assessment's role and limits.
