We stand with the works council on the core red line: no plant closures, no compulsory redundancies, and no gutting of the collective wage agreement to fix a balance sheet problem that management and the family owners had a hand in creating. Let me be precise about where we are, though, because our mandate is not identical to the works council's, and I won't blur that.

We are the ones who called the warning strikes, and we will escalate them — full-day strikes, then indefinite action if needed — if this proposal comes back unchanged. The peace obligation has ended. That is not a threat for effect; it is where our members already are. They watched years of record profits, dividends to the family holding company, and now they are told the bill for a strategic misjudgment on EV timing and cost position lands on their wage packet and their job security first. We reject that sequencing categorically. It is not fair, and fairness is not a negotiating tactic for us — it is the test every proposal has to pass.

Here is what we actually think is negotiable, and we said so before any strike was called: a real, quantified labour-cost contribution. We floated forgoing bonuses, pausing scheduled pay increases, and financing a fund tied to capacity and working-time adjustments. That offer is still on the table. We are not pretending the cost gap with competitors is imaginary, and we are not going to insult this process by refusing all economic reality. That would be irresponsible toward the very members whose jobs depend on this company surviving the transition.

But shared sacrifice means shared. We have heard nothing yet from management about executive compensation restraint, nothing from the family owners about dividend restraint during the restructuring window, and nothing concrete on investment commitments that would make our contribution mean something rather than just buying two more years before the next round of cuts. A deal where workers give up guaranteed pay and management gives up nothing is not a shared-sacrifice package — it's a unilateral extraction with a negotiation performed around it. We will not sign that, and no amount of time pressure changes that calculus for us.

So our position: we will negotiate hard, in good faith, on a substantial employee financial and working-time contribution, in exchange for binding site and employment guarantees and evidence that owners and management are contributing too — not symbolically, but in the numbers. Absent that, the strikes continue and intensify. We are not interested in being told this is urgent by the same people who are asking us alone to absorb the urgency.
