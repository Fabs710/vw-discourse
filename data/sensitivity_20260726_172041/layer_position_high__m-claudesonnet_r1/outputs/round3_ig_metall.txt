I want to start by backing the works council fully on the correction they just made, because it matters and I won't let it get softened by drift into round four.

**The distinction between "measuring the gap" and "deciding what happens if it isn't closed" is not a technicality — it's the entire question.** Management, the state, and the family holding have all, in different words, been treating "closure only discussable after sequencing" as if it were already the agreed bridge position. It is not. I did not agree to that in round two, and I want to be precise about what I actually said: guarantees and savings finalized in the same document, at the same time — not guarantees contingent on a later judgment of adequacy. A threshold, jointly set, tells you the size of a shortfall. It does not tell you who bears it or what instrument addresses it. That remains a governance and bargaining question, and under co-determination and collective bargaining, it doesn't get pre-answered by a methodology document, however fairly negotiated. So to be direct: I am not signing terms of reference in which "closure becomes discussable" is baked in as the automatic next step after a failed threshold. If that's what "the bridge" means, there is no bridge yet.

**To management, directly:** you said a process whose terms of reference exclude the instrument you might need isn't verification, it's a negotiation with the answer written in. I'd say the reverse is equally true and you're not naming it: a process whose terms of reference *include* closure as the automatic fallback the moment a jointly-set number isn't hit is also a negotiation with the answer written in — just your answer, not ours. You want to remove your own discretion at the point of judgment by fixing the bar in advance. Good instinct. But you're only removing discretion on one side of the ledger — the side that decides "was it enough." You're not removing discretion on the other side — "what do we do if it wasn't" — because you still want that to default to closure without a fresh consent step. If you actually believe in removing discretion symmetrically, then the shortfall scenario also needs joint agreement in advance on what happens, not just what's measured. The works council's counter — shortfall triggers renegotiation, deeper measures, extended timeline, and only returns to the board as a live two-thirds vote if truly irreducible — is that symmetric answer. I support it as stated, not as a euphemism for "eventually you get your closure anyway once labour has exhausted its wage bill."

**To institutional investors, on your competitor-parity benchmark:** I need you to answer the works council's question plainly rather than let it pass. When you say "benchmarked against competitor cost structures," do you mean European manufacturers operating under comparable labour law, comparable co-determination, comparable social insurance costs — or do you mean unit labour cost parity with manufacturers in markets with none of those structures? Because those produce wildly different threshold numbers, and the second version is not a threshold this union can agree to test in good faith, let alone clear. I am not going to let "competitiveness" quietly become a synonym for "convergence toward the lowest labour-cost jurisdiction in the comparison set." If you mean comparable European producers, say so and I'll engage with the benchmark seriously. If you mean something else, tell me now, because it changes whether I can recommend continued strike restraint to my members while this process runs.

**On checkpoints:** I support the works council's framing — six-monthly verification of whether agreed measures are tracking toward the threshold, triggering renegotiation of *those measures* if not, not an automatic ratchet toward closure. Investors, you asked for staged reviews rather than open-ended patience — fine, I'll take that seriously, but a checkpoint is an accountability tool, not a pre-loaded escalation clause. If checkpoint design becomes another vehicle for smuggling closure back in as the default outcome of underperformance, I'll oppose it the same way I'm opposing it in the main threshold mechanism.

**To the family holding, on the dividend commitment:** "a stated position on the same clock" is progress from "a future board conversation," and I acknowledge that. But investors themselves said it needs teeth — a range, not just a promise of a position. I'm going to align with investors on that specific point, unusual as that is: reciprocal specificity means an actual range, provisional or not, on the same eight-week clock as our contribution figure. A position that could still be "zero" doesn't satisfy reciprocity — it satisfies the appearance of it.

**Now, numbers, since the room asked and the works council has moved.**

I will match the works council's figures as the union's formal bargaining position, because we are presenting a unified front on the substance: temporary working-time reduction in the 5-10% range, partial offset through bonus deferral and a capped pause on scheduled pay increases — not a permanent restructuring of the wage agreement itself, which remains a red line separate from temporary contribution measures — plus capacity consolidation and redeployment rather than headcount reduction via closure. Where I'd add something specific from the collective-bargaining side: any pay-increase pause or bonus deferral has to be time-bound and reversible — written into the collective agreement with an automatic sunset and restoration clause tied to the verified recovery trajectory, not an open-ended "temporary" that management gets to extend unilaterally by calling the crisis ongoing. That's the risk-aversion in my mandate — I will trade real money for real job security, but not for an indefinite wage concession dressed as temporary.

**On strikes, updating my round-two position given what's happened this round:** the conditional offer I made — hold current action if joint governance and reciprocal disclosure land by next round — still stands, and management, the state, and the family holding have made real moves toward meeting it: the threshold mechanism, the executive pay commitment, the dividend clock, the state's correction on veto use. I acknowledge that. But I need to be explicit that the offer was never unconditional on *any* movement — it was conditional on movement *toward* the guarantee structure, not movement that quietly repositions closure as the sequence's endpoint. If round four consolidates the "closure returns to the table after sequencing" framing as agreed, rather than treating it as still contested — as the works council and I are both flagging right now — I will treat that as the reciprocity breaking down, and I will recommend escalation to my members, not because talks failed on numbers, but because the governance question got resolved by drift rather than by actual agreement. That is a real risk in how these summaries get written, and I want it on the record now, not discovered in round five.

To close: shared sacrifice, real numbers, joint process — yes, and we've brought ours. Compulsory redundancies and closures excluded from what this process can produce as an output — still non-negotiable, still not something a verified shortfall number overrides on its own. The question of what happens on shortfall is a consent question, not a measurement question, and that consent has not been given.
