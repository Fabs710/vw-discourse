We reject plant closures and forced redundancies. Full stop. That is not a bargaining opener I will soften over time — it is the line this workforce has drawn, and I will not pretend otherwise to smooth this conversation.

Let me be clear about what has happened here. Management is proposing to break a job-security pledge that this workforce built its trust on for years. That pledge was not a gift from management's goodwill — it was earned, negotiated, defended by generations of workers and works councillors before me. To now discover it treated as an obstacle to be swept aside, unilaterally, framed as an unavoidable "necessity" — that is a breach of trust, and I will not respond to it with quiet accommodation. That is why we have already mobilised warning strikes across German sites, and I will escalate further if management proceeds as if this is a fait accompli.

My priorities, in order:

First, every German plant stays open. No closures. I'm not interested in "one or two plants" framed as reasonable moderation — that is still a closure, still communities gutted, still a historic first that we will not accept as precedent.

Second, no compulsory redundancies. If there is overcapacity, there are instruments to manage it that do not throw workers onto the street: natural attrition, early retirement schemes, working-time reduction, transfers, voluntary programmes. We have used these before in past downturns. They work.

Third, co-determination is not a formality to be worked around — it is the mechanism through which any credible plan must be built. I expect a genuine seat at the table shaping the restructuring plan itself, not a plan presented to us for rubber-stamping after the fact. You need two-thirds of this board. Do the arithmetic.

Now — I am not blind to the competitive pressure the company faces, and I am not simply saying "no" to everything. I am prepared to discuss a substantial employee contribution: capacity adjustments, working-time models, even a negotiated, time-limited pay component — provided it is matched by binding, enforceable site and job guarantees running to 2030, and provided the burden is shared fairly, including from the top of this company, not carried solely by the shop floor while shareholders and executives are protected.

What I will not accept is a plan that asks the workforce to bleed while shareholders and management's own targets stay untouched, or a closure "for the first time in the company's history" dressed up as modernisation. If that is what's put in front of this board, you will not get your two-thirds majority, and you will get more strikes, not fewer.

That is where I stand. The door is open to a negotiated settlement — but only on those terms.
