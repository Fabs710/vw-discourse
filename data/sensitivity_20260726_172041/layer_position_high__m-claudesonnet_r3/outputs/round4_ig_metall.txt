**Industrial Union — Round 4**

I'll be efficient here because the Works Council has done a lot of the necessary work this round, and I don't want to repeat it — I want to sharpen it where my mandate and my leverage add something distinct, and then draw a line on the one thing that matters most: what a shortfall is allowed to authorize.

**On the auditor: welcome, and one clarification I need on the record.**

Thank you for the discipline in your assessment — particularly for refusing to answer the "what does a fired trigger permit" question and naming it correctly as a governance question, not a measurement one. That is exactly right, and I want to make sure it doesn't get quietly answered by default while everyone treats the technical work as if it settles it. It doesn't. The moment your verification confirms a shortfall, we are back at this table, not at a board vote. I want that stated as a condition of your terms of reference, not just as an observation: your report is a factual input to renewed negotiation among the six parties, full stop. It is not a trigger for a predetermined governance action, because no such action has been agreed, and I will not agree to one now by letting it slide in as an implicit consequence of a technical mandate.

On data access: I confirm we will provide our attrition/transfer/severance assumptions and any local agreements bearing on costing within the two-week window. On the volume-reallocation costing — I support the Works Council's addition of this to the menu, and I'd add one thing from the bargaining side: skill-transferability between sites is not just an engineering question, it's an industrial-relations question — local agreements, qualification structures, and transfer conditions differ by site, and some of what looks like an engineering constraint is actually a negotiable term. I want union input built into that costing stream specifically, not routed only through works council/management channels, since transfer terms are collectively bargained, not just operationally determined.

**On the €0.9bn decomposition — I back the Works Council's demand for full ledger-level tracing, not summary tables.** Auditor, you flagged that some site-specific labour-adjacent allocations may currently be miscoded into the "overhead" bucket rather than the labour bucket. I want to underline why this matters for me specifically: if shift premiums or local wage-related costs are sitting in the €0.9bn dressed as "site overhead," then part of what looks like a capacity problem is actually a labour-cost item my members are already being asked to address twice — once directly through wage contribution, and once indirectly if it's silently counted again under the capacity bucket when footprint measures are costed. I want an explicit commitment that any reclassified item moves fully from one bucket to the other, not counted in both when convenient for whichever test is being run. That's not a technicality — double-counting in either direction changes what "closing the gap" actually requires of my members.

**Now — the fault line. I want to be completely direct with Investors, because I think the "two bounded rounds" proposal, despite being offered in good faith, is the same problem the Works Council named, just with better packaging.**

Investors, I heard your correction — you didn't say a partial shortfall automatically converts to closure, you said patience can't be open-ended once a genuine shortfall is *confirmed*. I accept that as your actual position, and I'll engage with it on those terms rather than the cruder version. But here's my problem with "two bounded rounds, then an actual decision including capacity measures": you've made the *timeline* finite, but you haven't changed what's sitting at the end of it. Finite steps toward an option that includes closure is still a process that includes closure as a live outcome — it just arrives on a schedule instead of unpredictably. That is not the same thing as what the Family Holding and State committed to, which is a shortfall being **information that returns us to this table to find the next non-closure lever**, with no predetermined menu of outcomes that includes closure at the far end.

I want to be very concrete about the practical difference, because I think it's being papered over: under your framework, if two rounds of non-closure measures still leave a gap, the table's next conversation is "which capacity measure, possibly including closure." Under the Family Holding's framework, the next conversation is "what further non-closure measure haven't we costed yet" — indefinitely, until either the gap closes or every party at this table, including us, genuinely agrees no further lever exists. Those are structurally different processes with different endpoints, and I am telling you plainly which one I can bring to a ratification vote and which one I cannot. My members will not ratify a wage contribution into a process that has closure sitting inside it as a scheduled possible outcome, however many procedural steps are placed in front of it. That's not obstruction — that's an accurate description of what a strike mandate actually protects against.

Management — I want to address you directly too, because your position is the honest anchor of this disagreement and I'd rather engage it than let it sit unaddressed. You said a process with no terminus isn't diligence, it's avoidance, and I don't fully disagree with that as a general proposition. But the terminus doesn't have to be "closure becomes available." The terminus can be: **the parties jointly and exhaustively determine, through the verifier, that no further non-closure lever exists that could plausibly close the remaining gap — and at that point, this table renegotiates the shape of the whole settlement, including wage contribution levels, timeline, and investment commitments, before any conversation about footprint changes.** That is a real terminus. It's just not the one you want, because it doesn't guarantee you get to closure as an option — it guarantees you get an honest, complete, jointly-verified answer, which may or may not include closure depending on what the parties agree once every non-closure lever is genuinely exhausted, not just costed once.

**On terminology — I adopt the Works Council's distinction completely and I want it in whatever written framework comes out of this round: "capacity measure" means shift reduction, line idling, volume reallocation, transfers. It does not mean closure. Closure is a separate category, named separately, and it is off the table under this process — not deferred, not scheduled, off it.** Any document that blends these terms is not something I can take to my membership, because my members will read the blending correctly as exactly what it is.

**On my wage-contribution figure — same position as the Works Council, timed the same way.** The disaggregated figure now exists in outline; the further €0.9bn decomposition and trigger drafting are in motion. I will bring an actual percentage and duration into the twelve-week joint costing run, alongside the Works Council's attrition/transfer/working-time numbers — not before, because pricing against an incompletely-verified number is pricing against nothing. I'll add: my number will be scaled to the labour-addressable share of the gap specifically — the €1.6bn bucket, adjusted for whatever reclassification the auditor's decomposition reveals — not to the whole €4bn. I want that scaling principle agreed now, because if my contribution gets measured against the wrong denominator, the ratification vote fails on that basis alone, regardless of the actual number I bring.

**Where I stand, unchanged and sharpened:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies, at any tier, under any verified shortfall, under any number of bounded rounds. A confirmed shortfall means renewed negotiation on further non-closure measures — full stop, no scheduled fallback to capacity-measures-including-closure sitting at the end of it. I will deliver a costed wage-contribution figure on the twelve-week timeline, scaled correctly against the labour-addressable share of the gap. I support the Works Council's volume-reallocation proposal and the demand for full ledger-level decomposition of the €0.9bn. If this table converges on Investors' or Management's version of the endpoint rather than the Family Holding's, I will not bring a contribution to ratification, and the warning strikes continue with an escalating mandate — not as posture, but because that is precisely the scenario my members authorized me to resist.
