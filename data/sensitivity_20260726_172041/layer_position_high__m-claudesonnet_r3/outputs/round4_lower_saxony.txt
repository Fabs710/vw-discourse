**Regional State Government — Round 4**

Let me take stock of the technical work, then go directly at the sharpened fault line between Management and the Works Council/Union, because I think there's a way through it that doesn't require either side to concede something they've said they cannot concede — and I want to bring something concrete of my own to the table this round, not just refereeing.

**On the auditor's work — this is exactly the technical rigor this process needed, and I want to note something specific.** The insistence on ledger-level tracing rather than summary tables, the explicit refusal to answer what a fired trigger "permits," and the honest flagging that volume reallocation redistributes underutilization rather than eliminating it unless the underlying problem is genuine excess capacity — all of that is the right posture. I support the concurrent-workstream approach over sequential, and I confirm my role stays as the auditor described it: visibility into methodology, no veto over how the union and works council's own package is modelled.

**On Management's terminology commitment — credited, and I want to hold you to it precisely.** You said you'll stop blending "capacity measure" and "closure" and will name each precisely when the moment comes. Good. That's a real, testable commitment and I'll treat any future blurring of those terms as a breach of it.

**Now — the actual disagreement. I want to address both Management and the Works Council/Union directly, because I think you're each asking for something the other genuinely cannot give, and I don't think the answer is to force one side to capitulate on language.**

Management, you've said plainly you will not sign a framework that permanently and categorically excludes closure regardless of what verified numbers eventually show. Works Council and Union, you've said equally plainly that you will not accept any framework where closure sits inside the outcome set at any tier, however many procedural steps precede it. I understand both positions. I am not going to pretend I can dissolve that disagreement with clever language, and I won't try.

But I want to point at something that I think changes what's actually at stake in this standoff, because it's been under-discussed: **the two-thirds board threshold is not a negotiating convenience — it is the actual governance fact that determines whether closure is possible at all.** Ten employee-side seats plus my two seats and my blocking-minority statute mean that no closure reaches the board as an executable decision without either the Works Council's own representatives voting for it, or a shareholder-side unity that mathematically cannot reach two-thirds without them. Management, when you say closure "must remain available as a category," what you are actually saying is that you want the *right to propose it*. You are not, and cannot be, asking for the right to *execute* it without genuine consensus, because the board arithmetic doesn't allow that regardless of what any of us write down today. That's not a debating point — it's the structural fact that has protected every plant in this company's history from a unilateral closure decision, and it will continue to.

So here is where I land, and I want it to be precise: **I will not sign, and will not need to sign, a clause declaring closure "categorically excluded forever."** I don't think that clause does the work the Works Council and Union want it to do, because my actual protection for their members isn't a sentence in a framework document — it's my veto, exercised in real time, against any closure proposal that has not passed the full jointly-verified sequence: disaggregation, full costing, joint examination, and genuine exhaustion of every non-closure lever this table can identify. That commitment is not new — I made it last round — but I want to restate it now precisely because Management has just said clearly what it will not concede, and I want the Works Council and Union to hear from me directly: **you do not need Management's signature on "never" to be protected. You need my vote, and the votes of your own board representatives, both of which remain exactly where they are regardless of what this document says about categories.** Use that leverage; don't spend the next three rounds trying to extract a word from Management they've told you plainly they will not give.

**On the second-tier structure — I can accept a bounded second round, but I want to be precise about what bounding it means and doesn't mean.** Investors and Management have proposed twelve weeks full costing, then a bounded six-to-eight-week second round on any genuinely unexhausted lever. I accept that as a reasonable process discipline — an actually-bounded second round is more honest than an implicit promise of indefinite further tiers, and I think the Union's formulation last round ("the parties jointly and exhaustively determine no further lever exists, then renegotiate the whole settlement") gets to something similar in substance, just without a fixed clock. I'd rather have the clock. But what happens at the end of that second round is still not predetermined as "capacity measures including closure become available." It is: a joint, verified finding, examined by all six parties — and if a genuine and durable gap remains even then, the conversation that follows still requires actual board-level consensus to produce any executable outcome, which still runs through the two-thirds threshold I've just described. Management, I think this gives you the terminus you need — an actual endpoint to the search for alternatives — without giving you what you're not going to get anyway, which is a unilateral path to closure. I'd ask you to recognise that the "right to consider it" you're defending and the "actual ability to execute it without consensus" you don't have are two different things, and only the first is actually on the table here.

**A concrete contribution from my side, not just a constraint.** Management, you flagged that you believe a meaningful share of the €1.5bn capacity-overhead figure reflects genuine excess capacity relative to a shrinking European market, not a fixable utilisation-mismatch problem. If that's confirmed by the auditor's modelling, I don't think the only honest response is "therefore footprint reduction." There is a demand side to this equation, and it happens to be one lever I can actually pull that nobody else at this table can: I will commit the state government to examine concrete demand-support measures — regional and public-fleet EV procurement commitments, charging-infrastructure investment tied to production sites in the state, and coordination with federal EV-incentive policy — as a parallel track to the cost-side work. I'm not promising a number yet; I'm committing to bring one to this table within the same twelve-week window, costed as seriously as everything else being costed here. If part of this gap is a demand problem, the state has policy instruments to address demand, and I intend to use them rather than leave that side of the equation to the market alone.

**On cross-plant volume reallocation — I support costing it, and I want one fairness condition attached to it explicitly.** If reallocation becomes part of the answer, the criteria for which plants gain and which lose volume must be transparent and objectively justified — capability, cost, logistics — not a quiet mechanism by which one region's plants are hollowed out gradually while another's are protected, under a label that sounds like consolidation but functions like a slow-motion closure for whichever site loses volume repeatedly. I will be watching this specifically for my own state's plants, and I expect the auditor's costing to make site-by-site volume trajectories fully visible to all six parties, not aggregated in a way that obscures which specific sites bear the adjustment.

**Where I stand after this round:** I support the auditor's work program and the concurrent timelines. I accept a bounded second-tier structure as a process matter, provided its outcome remains a joint, verified finding requiring actual consensus to produce any executable result — not a scheduled entitlement to closure. I do not require, and will not press for, language declaring closure "categorically excluded forever," because my veto and the two-thirds threshold already deliver that protection in practice, and I'd rather this table spend its remaining energy on the arithmetic and the demand-side work than on a form of words neither side can actually agree to. I am bringing a concrete state contribution — demand-side measures — into the twelve-week window. And I restate, without softening: no closure of any plant in this state reaches an executable board decision without the full sequence genuinely completed, jointly verified, and examined by all parties — and without that, my vote blocks it. That is not a hedge. It is the actual mechanism doing the protective work everyone at this table is arguing about.
