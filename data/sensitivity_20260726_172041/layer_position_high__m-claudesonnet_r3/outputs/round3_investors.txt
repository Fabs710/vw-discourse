Institutional Investors (Capital Markets) — Round 3

A lot happened this round, and most of it is genuine progress. Let me be precise about what I credit, what I still need, and where I think the process is drifting in a direction I will not fund indefinitely.

**Disaggregation: credit given, and it sharpens my own analysis.** €1.6bn labour, €1.5bn capacity-driven overhead, €0.9bn mixed. This matters to us specifically because it changes what "closing the gap" actually requires. A wage contribution and working-time package can plausibly address a large share of the €1.6bn. It cannot, by construction, address the €1.5bn capacity-driven bucket — that requires actual footprint or shift/line reduction, full stop. I want to say this plainly because I think there's a temptation at this table to let the labour package's size be judged against the whole €4bn when it was never going to close the overhead bucket by design. The right test is: does the labour package close its addressable share of labour cost, and does a separate, costed capacity-consolidation measure — shift reduction, line idling, transfers — close the overhead share? Two different instruments, two different tests, added together. I'd ask everyone to adopt that framing explicitly rather than continuing to measure one undifferentiated package against one undifferentiated number.

**2030 with tight triggers: settled, and I'll hold everyone to the "tight" part.** I supported this last round and I'm glad management withdrew 2028. I want to be the one who says plainly: the durability of this settlement to us depends entirely on the triggers being real thresholds, not vague language that never fires or that fires at the slightest wobble. If the triggers are drafted loosely in either direction — unfireable or hair-trigger — this reopens the whole risk question for us. I want visibility into trigger drafting, not just a report that it happened.

**The dividend/distribution question: good, this is closed, and I want to note something for the record.** The Family Holding converting "raise it formally" into an actual commitment in this room is exactly the kind of deal certainty we price. I'll say plainly: a bounded freeze on ordinary and special distributions, tied to the same horizon and triggers as everything else, does not damage our position. It's a small, time-limited cost for a much larger prize — a credible, durable settlement instead of two more years of strikes and margin erosion. I am not going to pretend this is a sacrifice for us. It's cheap insurance.

**Now, the sixteen-week process and the shortfall question — this is where I need to correct the record on my own position, because the Works Council, Union, and State have all characterized my Round 2 comment in a way that isn't quite what I said, and I want to be precise rather than let a mischaracterization stand.**

I did not say "a partial shortfall automatically converts to closure." I said patience with the process cannot become open-ended once a genuine, verified shortfall is confirmed. Those are different statements. I actually agree with most of what's been built since: disaggregate the number, cost the full non-closure menu — including capacity measures nobody had priced yet — run it through a bounded, jointly-verified sixteen-week process, and treat a confirmed shortfall as information requiring a further round of negotiation on the next tier of alternatives before closure becomes a live board item. The State's formulation and the Family Holding's adoption of it is, in fact, close to what I actually want: bounded, but not rigged; a real deadline, but not an automatic trigger for the worst outcome.

Where I differ slightly from the State and Family Holding is on how many further rounds of "the next tier of alternatives" this can absorb before it stops being diligence and starts being deferral. I'll be concrete about why this matters to us specifically, not as an abstract complaint: every quarter this runs without a credible path to closing the gap, margins stay impaired, the share price prices in continued uncertainty, and capital that could be deployed elsewhere sits exposed to an unresolved structural problem. That is a real cost to the people I represent, and it's not weightless just because it's not a wage or a job.

So here is my actual position, stated as a limit, not a preference: I will support the sixteen-week bounded process, joint verification, joint examination, and — if a genuine shortfall remains — one further negotiation round on additional non-closure measures (deeper attrition, extended solidarity fund, further shift/line consolidation), with its own bounded timeline, say six to eight weeks, agreed now rather than invented later. If, after that second round, a verified and durable shortfall still remains, I expect this table to reach an actual decision — which may include a capacity measure — rather than opening a third open-ended tier. That is the honest version of "not indefinite": two bounded rounds, not one infinite one, and not zero.

I want to be direct with the Works Council and Union: I understand and largely accept your objection that a bounded timeline shouldn't function as a closure countdown. I've just given you a structure — two defined rounds, jointly verified — that answers that concern concretely rather than asking you to trust a vague promise of "further negotiation" with no shape to it. I'd ask you to accept that structure in return, rather than treating any endpoint at all as inherently suspect. A process that truly never terminates is not a protection for your members either — it's a slow bleed that erodes the company's ability to invest in the products that protect their jobs long after this dispute is settled.

**On the top-level contribution:** closed satisfactorily for our purposes now that the Family Holding has committed. I'll only repeat what I said last round — it buys legitimacy, not competitiveness, and everyone should stop treating it as if it does either job by itself.

**Where I actually stand after this round:** the mechanism is now close to complete — disaggregated figure, two-instrument test (labour package vs. capacity measures against their respective buckets), 2030 horizon with tight triggers, dividend question closed, sixteen-week bounded costing process. What remains for me is the arithmetic itself and a defined, bounded second-round mechanism if the first pass falls short. Bring me those two things — the actual year-by-year numbers against the disaggregated figure, and agreement on a bounded (not open-ended) second negotiation tier — and I will call this a credible path back to competitive margins. Without a decision point that actually arrives within a reasonable, defined horizon, I will not treat an indefinitely renewable process as equivalent to a credible restructuring, regardless of how good-faith every round of it looks on paper.
