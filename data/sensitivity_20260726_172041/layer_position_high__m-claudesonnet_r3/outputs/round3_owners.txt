**Controlling Family Holding — Round 3**

I've been asked directly, twice now, to close out the dividend/distribution question in this room rather than defer it to a future board meeting. That's fair pressure, and I'll answer it directly rather than retreat into "we'll raise this formally." But I want to be precise about what I'm committing to and why, because this is the point where the holding's own interest is most directly engaged, and vague warmth would be dishonest.

**On the distribution freeze extension — yes, and here is the actual commitment.** I will co-sign, in substance, the extension of the freeze to special distributions and share buybacks, on the same horizon and the same objective triggers as the rest of the package — running to 2030, reopening only on the narrow, jointly-verified conditions management and the State are now finalising. I accept the Union's and State's point about sequencing: an unresolved ownership contribution sitting alongside a finalized labour concession is not a stable basis for a ratification vote, and I'm not going to be the reason this collapses on a timing technicality when the substance is something I can actually support. So: closed, on those bounded terms, in this room, now. I will carry this to the full board as a recommendation the family holding backs, not as an open question.

I want to be equally clear about what this is not. It is not an open-ended commitment to future capital allocation beyond this restructuring horizon, and it is not a precedent for shareholder distributions being treated as a standing lever whenever a future dispute arises. It is bounded to this package, this horizon, these triggers. I say this not to weaken what I just agreed to, but because I owe this table honesty about where my flexibility ends as well as where it begins.

**On disaggregation — I welcome it, and I want to note what it does to my own assessment.** Splitting the €4 billion into labour cost, capacity-driven overhead, and mixed overhead sharpens the actual question the board will face: not "does the labour package match a plant closure in size," but "does the combination of wage contribution and capacity/footprint measures — shifts, lines, transfers — actually close each bucket on its own terms." That's a better test than the one we started with, and I think it serves everyone, including management, because it stops the negotiation being conducted in a single undifferentiated number that invited exactly the suspicion the Works Council and Union raised.

**On 2030 with tight triggers — settled, from where I sit, and I won't re-litigate it.** I said last round this was a long-term-value question, not a labour-sympathy question, and nothing since has changed that view. I'll only add: I expect the trigger design to actually be tight — specific percentage thresholds, specific milestone-shortfall margins, verified externally — because a vague trigger is functionally the same as no horizon limit at all, and that would undo the protection management legitimately needs. I support the 2030 horizon on the condition the triggers are real, not as a slogan.

**On the sixteen-week bounded process and the shortfall question — this is where I want to say something that neither side has quite said yet, and it matters for how the board will actually vote.**

I understand management's concern about a process with no terminus — an indefinite search for the next lever is not diligence, it's avoidance, and eventually it just becomes value destruction by delay, which is exactly the kind of outcome I cannot support. I also understand the Works Council's and Union's concern that a bounded timeline can quietly become a closure timeline if "genuine and durable shortfall" is asserted rather than jointly examined. The State's formulation — sixteen weeks, one external verification, joint examination of the result, and a shortfall treated as information requiring renewed negotiation on the next tier of non-closure measures rather than automatic authorization for closure — is the right one, and I want to adopt it as my own position, not merely note that I heard it.

Here is the reason I land there, stated in terms of what I actually have to protect: a board vote built on an assertion of shortfall that hasn't been jointly examined is not one I can defend to my own family shareholders, because it would be indefensible the moment it's challenged — by the union, by the works council's board seats, by public scrutiny, by history. Equally, a process that has no endpoint at all is not one I can defend either, because it would mean sitting on a structural gap indefinitely while margins erode, which is its own form of value destruction, just slower and quieter. The State's framing gives me both things I need: a real deadline, and a safeguard against that deadline being weaponised into a predetermined outcome. I will support exactly that structure at board level.

What I will not support — and I want this on the record as a limit, not a preference — is any reading of the sixteen-week process where a verified partial shortfall is treated by management, or by a bare majority of shareholder-side board votes, as sufficient in itself to bring closures forward without the co-designed process the Works Council and Union have asked for actually running its course first. That is not me taking labour's side reflexively. It is me recognising that a plan pushed through without genuine two-thirds legitimacy is not a plan — it's the start of the next round of conflict, on worse terms, with less trust, and a court fight layered on top. That outcome is worse for enterprise value than sixteen more weeks of rigorous, jointly-verified work. I said in round one that a plan lacking a real board majority is not a win for anyone. I still believe that, and it's now sharper: it's not a win for the holding specifically, because we're the ones who have to live with the governance consequences for the next generation, not just this restructuring cycle.

**On co-design of the verification methodology — I support the State's distinction exactly as stated.** The union and works council should be full co-designers of how their own package is modelled; the state and the holding should have visibility and input into terms of reference, not a veto over labour's methodology. I don't need to compete for control of that process. I need the output to be credible enough that I can put my name behind it either way it comes out.

**Where I actually stand after this round, stated plainly:** the dividend/distribution question is closed, on the bounded terms above — that's real movement, not a gesture. The 2030 horizon with tight triggers has my backing. I support the sixteen-week bounded costing process, but only paired with joint verification and joint examination before any closure discussion opens, and only with a shortfall treated as a call to negotiate the next tier of alternatives, not a green light. My unchanged limit remains what I said last round: I will not support an outcome that permanently impairs this company's competitiveness, and I will not support a plan that destabilises the governance structure that has allowed it to be managed for the long term — including a plan rammed through without the legitimacy a two-thirds board majority actually requires. Bring me the disaggregated arithmetic, jointly verified, and I will judge it on its merits at that point, exactly as I said I would in round one. Nothing has moved me off that yet, because nothing yet has been tested.
