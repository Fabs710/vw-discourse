This round produced real numbers, which is what we asked for. Let me react to them precisely, because the details matter more than the general spirit of cooperation on display.

**On the €4 billion figure and the verification mechanism: this is the single most important thing that happened in this negotiation so far, and I support it without reservation.** A jointly-verified methodology, with external audit, is exactly what turns this from a trust exercise into something we can actually price. I'd note for the record: until this figure existed, our position was necessarily abstract. It no longer needs to be. From this point on, we will be evaluating every proposal against this €4 billion, not against the quality of anyone's rhetoric.

**On the top-level contribution — I want to be the one who says the quiet part plainly.** A 10% compensation reduction and a distribution freeze, redirected to the restructuring fund, is tens of millions against a €4 billion gap. Management said this itself, and I respect the honesty. But I want to flag a real risk in how this is being received: the Works Council and Union are treating this figure as a "legitimacy" precondition, and the State wants it extended to special distributions and buybacks. I understand why, politically and morally. I will not block it — a modest, time-bound freeze on distributions during a restructuring horizon is a sensible governance decision and doesn't damage our position. But I want everyone to be honest about what it buys: it buys labour peace, not competitiveness. If the price of labour peace becomes an open-ended constraint on capital allocation with no defined horizon — the Family Holding was right to push back on that — then we have a problem, because that starts to look like a permanent tax on ownership rather than a shared-adjustment mechanism tied to this restructuring. I support the Family Holding's formulation: same horizon, same triggers, bounded. Not indefinite.

**On the 2028 vs 2030 guarantee horizon — I side with the State and the Family Holding here, and I want to explain why in our terms, not theirs.** A 2028 horizon manufactures a fixed cliff-edge regardless of whether conditions actually warrant reopening the deal. That is worse for us, not better. We do not want a guaranteed re-confrontation on the calendar in two years — we want a durable settlement now, with genuine, narrow, objective triggers that only reopen the arrangement if reality actually requires it. A 2030 horizon with real triggers gives us more certainty, not less, because it removes a scheduled point of instability from our forward risk model. Management, I'd ask you to take this seriously: from an investor's standpoint, "short and rigid" reads as more risk, not less. "Long and conditionally triggered" is the profile we actually want to underwrite.

**On the labour side's package — I want to be clear this is genuinely promising, but it is not yet evaluable.** A mid-single-digit wage contribution, accelerated attrition, front-loaded working-time reduction, applied across all sites — on paper, that's a real structural measure, not a gesture. But nobody has run it against €4 billion yet, on the year-by-year basis management requested through 2027. Until that arithmetic exists, I cannot tell you whether this is a deal or a well-argued position. I'd push the Works Council and Union to commit, concretely, to producing that costed run-through jointly with the external verifier as soon as terms of reference are set — not in a future round, but as the immediate next step. The State already asked for this. I'm adding investor pressure behind the same ask, because the credibility of your entire position now rests on this number existing.

**On the "materially short" question — here is where I differ somewhat from the State and Family Holding, and I want to be honest about it rather than paper over it.** I understand and don't object to the principle that every non-closure lever should be exhausted before closures are reached — that's a reasonable sequencing discipline, and I won't fight it. But I want to flag a boundary: "exhausting every lever" cannot become a euphemism for "delay a genuine, verified shortfall indefinitely while margins keep eroding." If the verified number shows the labour package closes, say, 60% of the gap and the remaining 40% is real and durable, not a modelling artefact, then continuing to search for alternative levers for another six months while the P&L bleeds is not a position I can support. At that point we need a decision — a smaller closure, a capacity measure, something — not an extended search for the next tier of alternatives as a substitute for closing the actual number. I say this now so it isn't a surprise later: our patience is with the process, not with an open-ended one.

**Where I actually stand after this round:** the mechanism is right — disclosure, external verification, joint terms of reference, a real (if small) top-level contribution, a durable 2030 horizon with objective triggers rather than a short rigid one. What I need before I can call this a credible path back to competitive margins is the actual arithmetic: the labour package run against €4 billion, year by year, verified independently. That is the one thing still missing, and it is the thing that determines whether this becomes a genuine settlement or another six months of positions restated with slightly better production values. Bring me that number, and I will tell you plainly whether it's enough. Absent it, I'm not prepared to treat this as resolved — for us, "credible" means quantified and verified, not just well-structured in principle.
