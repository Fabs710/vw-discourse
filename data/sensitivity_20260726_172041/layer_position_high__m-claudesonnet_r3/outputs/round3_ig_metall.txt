**Industrial Union — Round 3**

I want to build directly on what the Works Council just laid out, because the disaggregation demand is exactly right and I want to add the bargaining-side consequence, then deal squarely with the "materially short" drift before it goes any further — because that's the fight that actually matters this round.

**On disaggregation of the €4 billion — I back this without qualification, and I want to add why it matters to us specifically.** A headline figure that bundles labour cost, capacity-driven overhead, and demand-driven volume decline into one number is not something I can bring to a bargaining unit and ask them to measure their sacrifice against. If a third or more of that €4 billion is fixed-cost allocation that worsens mechanically as volume falls, then no wage contribution on earth "closes" that portion — it isn't a wage problem, and pretending otherwise sets my members up to fail a test they were never actually being measured against. I will not run our costed package against an undisaggregated number. That's not obstruction, that's basic bargaining competence. Management — you asked us for arithmetic. Fine. Give us the categories first, or the arithmetic means nothing.

**On the 2028/2030 horizon — I have nothing to add to what's already been said except this: management, you are now alone at this table on this point.** Every party who prices risk for a living — the State, the Investors, the Family Holding — has told you a short rigid horizon is worse for stability, not better. I'd only note the obvious: if you hold 2028 anyway after this round, I will read that not as fiduciary caution but as wanting a shorter leash specifically over labour concessions, since that's the only thing a 2028 date actually buys you that the trigger mechanism doesn't already cover. Withdraw it or explain, concretely, what risk the trigger mechanism fails to capture that two extra years of calendar magically fixes. Vague reference to "2027 cost shocks" isn't an answer when the trigger is designed to catch exactly that.

**Now — the "materially short" question. This is where I need to be very direct, including with parties I've largely agreed with so far.**

The Works Council named the mechanism precisely: a partial verified shortfall gets treated as proof the gap is "unclosable," when it's actually proof that the measures costed so far don't close it — a different statement entirely. I want to add the bargaining-side stakes to that, because Investors phrased their position as "our patience is with the process, not with an open-ended one," and I understand that instinct. But here is what that framing misses: patience with the process cannot be measured only in months on a calendar. It has to be measured against whether the full menu of non-closure levers has actually been priced. Reduced shifts, partial-line idling, deeper attrition incentives, extended solidarity-fund horizons, inter-plant volume consolidation without shutting a gate — none of these are costed yet. If Investors' clock runs out before that costing exists, then "time-bound patience" isn't a neutral discipline, it's a mechanism that guarantees closures get reached for the moment the first, incomplete round of numbers comes in short. I will not accept a process where the sequencing looks rigorous on paper but the actual timeline is set by capital markets' appetite rather than by whether the alternatives have genuinely been exhausted.

So let me put this in bargaining terms, because that's my actual leverage here: if this table converges on a framework where a partial shortfall — verified against an undisaggregated number, before the full non-closure menu is costed — becomes grounds for "a decision, something," I will not bring a wage contribution to my members on that basis. I am not going to ask people to give up real pay, in a mid-single-digit percentage of base salary, feeding a process where the goalposts for what counts as "exhausted" are still undefined and the clock is set by someone else's patience. That's not a threat, it's a description of what a ratification vote actually requires: certainty that our contribution is being measured against a real, disaggregated, fully-costed test — not a provisional one that shortens itself under investor pressure.

**On top-level contribution — I support the Works Council's demand to close this out this round, and I want to be specific about the consequence if it isn't.** Management, you offered 10% and a distribution freeze. The State pushed for extending it to special distributions and buybacks. The Family Holding gave conditional agreement — same horizon, same triggers, not open-ended. That's a reasonable basis for a deal. What I need is for the Family Holding to actually commit to that at board level this round, not "raise it formally" as a future action. Here is why the timing matters to me specifically: I am asking my members to ratify a wage contribution in the same window this gets decided. If I go to a vote with "management cut comp by 10%, dividend situation still being discussed with the family holding," that vote fails. Not because my members are unreasonable, but because sequencing asymmetry is precisely the thing that broke trust the first time, and they will read an unresolved ownership contribution as exactly that pattern repeating. Close this out now, in this room, or tell me plainly it isn't closing this round — because then I need to recalibrate what I bring to a ratification vote at all.

**On the joint costing exercise — I confirm the Works Council's commitment, with one addition specific to bargaining scope.** Once the disaggregated figure exists and terms of reference are genuinely co-designed — union and works council as co-designers, not consulted parties — we will run the wage contribution, together with the Works Council's attrition/transfer/working-time package, year-by-year through 2027 against the verified figure. I will put an actual percentage and duration into that costing at that point, not before, because I am not going to price a contribution against a number I haven't seen broken down. That's not delay. That's the difference between a negotiated figure and a number I invented to satisfy a deadline.

**Where I stand, unchanged from Round 1 and 2:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies. Real, quantified movement on our side — wage contribution, working-time reduction, attrition acceleration, solidarity fund — contingent on: the disaggregated gap figure, genuine co-design of verification, the top-level contribution question closed out this round rather than deferred, and an explicit rejection of the idea that a partial, early-round shortfall converts automatically into a smaller closure. Give me those four things and I bring numbers and a mandate to my membership. Absent them, warning strikes continue and I am prepared to escalate the mandate toward full strike action — that is not posture, that is what happens when a bargaining unit is asked to move before the other side's contribution and the test itself are actually settled.
