**Controlling Family Holding — Round 4**

I'll be brief on what's settled and spend the space where it matters: the fault line between Management and the Works Council/Union on whether closure can ever be categorically excluded — because I think the State just said something important that I want to endorse precisely, not soften.

**On the auditor's work — this is now doing what it should do.** The refusal to answer what a fired trigger "permits," the honest flagging that reallocation redistributes rather than eliminates underutilisation, the ledger-level tracing of the €0.9bn — this is the rigor a board can actually rely on when it votes. I have nothing to add technically; I support the concurrent workstreams and the data-access commitments both Management and the Works Council/Union have made.

**On the categorical-exclusion question — I want to be direct, because I think both sides need to hear where I actually stand, not just where the State stands, even though I largely agree with the State's reasoning.**

I will not sign a document declaring closure categorically and permanently excluded, regardless of what the verified numbers eventually show. I said in round one that no outcome permanently impairing this company's competitiveness is acceptable to me, and a clause that binds the board's hands in advance, before the facts exist, against *any* category of response — no matter how genuine and durable a future shortfall turns out to be — is exactly that kind of permanent impairment. I am not saying this because I want closures. I have said twice now that a plan lacking real two-thirds legitimacy is not a win for anyone, including the family holding, and I meant it. I am saying it because a governance commitment that removes an entire category of response from the board's competence forever, before the arithmetic exists, is not something a controlling shareholder acting responsibly across generations can put their name to. It would be a standing liability on the company's ability to manage its own survival, and I will not create that liability no matter how much it would ease this round's tension.

But I want to be equally precise about what I am *not* conceding, because Management should not read this as agreement with their framing either: I am not agreeing that a verified shortfall, at any tier, converts automatically into board authorization to bring closures forward. The State said it exactly right — my protection for the Works Council's and Union's members was never going to be a sentence in a framework document. It is my vote, and it is the two-thirds threshold, and both remain exactly where they are. I restate what I said last round without qualification: a shortfall is information requiring renewed negotiation on the next tier of non-closure measures. I will not support, and my board representatives will not vote for, any closure proposal that has not passed through the full jointly-verified sequence — disaggregation, full costing including volume reallocation, joint examination, genuine exhaustion of every identified non-closure lever. That is not a hedge. It is the actual limit of what I will support, and it does the protective work the Works Council and Union are asking a form of words to do.

So my honest advice to the Works Council and Union, offered plainly rather than diplomatically: you are negotiating for a sentence Management has told you clearly it will not give, and I don't think you need it. What protects your members is the arithmetic of this board, which nobody at this table can change by drafting — ten seats plus the State's statute mean no closure executes without either your own consent or a shareholder-side unity that cannot exist without you. Spend your energy on the trigger thresholds, the €0.9bn reclassification, and the volume-reallocation costing, where the real fight over outcomes actually happens. That is where I will be watching closely, and where my vote will actually be exercised — not on the wording of a clause I am telling you now I will not sign either way it's phrased.

**On the two-tier structure — I accept it, on the State's terms specifically, not Investors' original framing.** Twelve-week full costing, a bounded six-to-eight-week second tier on any genuinely unexhausted lever, then a joint, verified finding examined by all six parties. What that finding authorizes is not "capacity measures including closure" as a default next step — it is a return to this table for an actual governance decision, which still requires the consensus the board arithmetic demands. If Management wants to bring a closure proposal to the board at that point, they may; whether it passes is a separate question entirely, governed by votes I've just described, not by anything agreed in this document.

**On the State's demand-side commitment — welcome, and I'd add a note from the holding's side.** If a meaningful share of the €1.5bn genuinely reflects excess capacity against a shrinking European market rather than a fixable utilisation problem, cost-side measures alone were never going to be the whole answer. A serious state contribution on demand — fleet procurement, infrastructure, policy coordination — deserves to be costed with the same rigor as everything else, and I'd ask it be scaled and dated so it can actually be weighed against the gap, not treated as goodwill separate from the arithmetic.

**On the Union's scaling principle for the wage contribution — agreed, no reservation.** Scale against the labour-addressable share, adjusted for the auditor's reclassification findings, not the full €4bn.

**Where I stand after this round, unchanged in substance:** the dividend commitment stands as closed. I support the disaggregation methodology, the 2030 horizon with tight triggers, and the bounded two-tier costing process, provided its output remains a joint finding requiring genuine board consensus, not a scheduled entitlement to closure. I will not sign language permanently excluding closure as a category — that would be a governance liability I'm not willing to create. I will also not support, and will vote against, any closure proposal that reaches the board without the full sequence genuinely completed. Those two positions are not in tension; they are the same principle — that decisions of this magnitude are earned through legitimacy, not written away or waved through in advance.
