# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Two-instrument framing adopted**: labour package tested against its addressable share of the gap; capacity measures tested against theirs. The €1.6bn/€1.5bn/€0.9bn disaggregation stands as the shared baseline.
- **2030 horizon with tight triggers**: confirmed by all parties.
- **Dividend commitment**: closed, per Family Holding's prior offer.
- **Terminology separation**: all parties now explicitly distinguish "capacity measures" (shift reduction, line idling, volume reallocation, transfers) from "closure" as a separate category. Management has committed to stop blending the terms.
- **Independent auditor/verification body**: summoned and accepted by all parties, with initial technical assessment delivered. Scope: further disaggregation of the €0.9bn (ledger-level tracing, not summary tables), drafting/stress-testing percentage-based triggers, and costing the full non-closure menu including cross-plant volume reallocation.
- **Reclassification safeguard**: any cost item reclassified between labour and overhead buckets moves fully from one to the other — no double-counting. Agreed by Management, Union, Works Council.
- **Wage-contribution scaling principle**: the Union's/Works Council's costed figures will be scaled against the labour-addressable share of the gap (adjusted for reclassification), not the full €4bn. Agreed across the table.
- **Concurrent workstreams**: €0.9bn decomposition, trigger drafting, and menu costing run in parallel, not sequentially, within a twelve-week window; data access commitments made by Management (ledger, allocation keys, utilization data) and Works Council/Union (attrition, transfer, severance assumptions, local agreements).
- **Volume reallocation** added to the costed menu, with Union flagging that skill-transferability/local agreement terms are an industrial-relations question requiring union input, not purely an engineering one.
- **State demand-side commitment**: proposed (EV procurement, infrastructure, policy coordination), to be costed on the same footing and timeline as cost-side measures, with Investors requesting independent scoring of how much gap it can plausibly close.
- **Board arithmetic clarified as the operative safeguard**: State and Family Holding both state that no closure can execute without either Works Council consent or shareholder unity that cannot reach two-thirds without them — positioned as the real protection, independent of document wording.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

**A. Whether closure can ever be categorically excluded from the process, in writing.**
- **Works Council / Union**: demand explicit language that closure is off the table at any tier, under any verified shortfall, permanently — a shortfall triggers renewed negotiation on non-closure levers only.
- **Management**: will not sign any framework categorically and permanently excluding closure regardless of what verified numbers eventually show; insists this is a governance liability, not a negotiating position to be softened.
- **State / Family Holding**: decline to press for categorical exclusion language, arguing the two-thirds board threshold and their own votes already deliver the protection Works Council/Union seek — and neither will vote for closure without the full sequence being genuinely exhausted.
- **Investors**: agree the categorical-language fight is moot given board arithmetic and want both sides to stop spending rounds on it.
- **Unresolved**: Works Council and Union have not yet accepted the "board arithmetic is sufficient protection" argument in place of written exclusion; they have not indicated whether they will drop the demand for explicit language.

**B. What a fully-exhausted process concludes with.**
- **Investors**: propose a hard ceiling — twelve weeks costing, six-to-eight week bounded second tier, then a **mandatory board vote** on whatever settlement exists, on a fixed date, closure-inclusive or not.
- **Management**: aligns closely with this two-tier structure, ending in board consideration of the "full range of options" if a durable gap remains after genuine exhaustion.
- **Works Council / Union**: reject any structure where the terminus includes closure as a live option; Union's alternative terminus is that exhaustion triggers renegotiation of the "whole settlement" (wages, timeline, investment) with no fixed clock — not a board vote with closure on the table.
- **Unresolved**: whether the process ends in a **dated, mandatory board vote** (Investors/Management) or an **open-ended return to renegotiation** (Union/Works Council) — this is now the sharpest procedural fault line.

## 3. Key Trade-Offs Still Unaddressed

- **Whether Works Council/Union will accept "board arithmetic as protection" as sufficient**, dropping the demand for categorical written exclusion — this has been proposed by three parties (State, Family Holding, Investors) but not yet responded to directly by Works Council or Union.
- **Whether a fixed decision date (Investors' demand) is compatible with "no predetermined outcome" (Works Council/Union's demand)** — a dated vote is not the same as a scheduled closure, but this distinction has not been tested against Works Council/Union's actual red line.
- **Volume reallocation's real ceiling**: Management asserts a meaningful share of the €1.5bn is genuine excess capacity (unfixable by reallocation), not utilization mismatch. This is an open empirical question the auditor will model, but its outcome will materially shape which side's framing survives.
- **State's demand-side commitment**: not yet scaled, dated, or costed — currently a direction of travel, not a number.
- **Fairness criteria for volume reallocation**: State has raised (but not yet resolved with other parties) the need for transparent, objective site-by-site allocation criteria to prevent reallocation functioning as slow-motion closure for losing sites.
- **The Union's specific concern about union input into transfer/skill-transferability costing** has been raised but not yet confirmed as a designed-in feature of the auditor's methodology.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

1. **Works Council and Union**: respond directly to the State/Family Holding/Investors argument that board arithmetic (not document language) is the operative protection against closure. Either accept this and redirect energy to triggers/reclassification/costing, or explain specifically why written exclusion remains necessary despite that arithmetic.
2. **Test whether a fixed, dated board vote (Investors' proposal) can be reconciled with "no predetermined outcome" (Union/Works Council's requirement)** — is a dated vote with an undetermined result acceptable, or does any scheduled vote itself constitute the "closure sitting at the end of it" that Union/Works Council reject?
3. **Advance the concrete technical work**: begin joint trigger drafting, launch the €0.9bn ledger-level decomposition, and confirm data-access timelines — all parties have committed to starting within two weeks.
4. **State**: bring initial scope/scale/timeline for the demand-side commitment so it can be weighed against the cost-side arithmetic.
5. **Management**: clarify what "full range of options" concretely means procedurally at the end of a bounded second tier — does it require a new negotiation round, or does it go straight to a board vote as Investors propose?
