# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

## 1. What has been agreed (areas of apparent convergence)

- **Shared-sacrifice principle**: All six parties now explicitly accept that management compensation, dividend policy, and/or shareholder distributions should visibly contribute to any package. No party has rejected this outright; the dispute is about scale and mechanism, not the principle.
- **A conditional test for closures**: Management, the Family Holding, the State, and Institutional Investors have converged on a common framework — *if* a costed alternative package (capacity reduction, working-time measures, wage contribution, solidarity fund) demonstrably closes the structural cost gap on a credible timeline, closures are not the necessary answer. Management has committed to evaluate such a package in good faith rather than defaulting to closures.
- **Rejection of a pure stalemate**: Every party — including Management and Investors — has acknowledged that a plan lacking the two-thirds board majority (i.e., one that provokes indefinite strikes and litigation) is not a viable "win" for anyone. Value-destruction through prolonged conflict is recognized across all sides as worse than a negotiated compromise.
- **Site guarantees to 2030**: Broad conceptual support for guarantees exists, contingent on being tied to a funded, credible plan rather than being unconditional — this framing has not been contested by any party.
- **Transparency on numbers**: All parties have accepted, at least in principle, the State's call for the cost-gap figures and alternative package costings to be put on the table openly.

## 2. Where core disagreements remain

- **Closures as such**: Works Council and Union maintain an unconditional "no closures, full stop" as a matter of principle and mandate, not contingent on the numbers. Management, Family Holding, Investors, and (with more nuance) the State treat closures as contingent on whether alternatives close the gap — meaning the door to closures remains open on their side if the labour-side counter-proposal falls short.
- **What counts as "comparable scale"**: No shared definition yet exists of how large a wage/working-time/capacity contribution must be to be considered equivalent to closures. Each side has asserted the *right test* exists but not agreed numbers.
- **Nature of guarantees**: Works Council/Union want binding, enforceable guarantees to 2030 as a precondition for their contribution. Management wants guarantees conditional on delivered savings and revisitable — a structural tension not yet resolved.
- **Scale of "shared sacrifice" at the top**: Union and Works Council want a specific number in the same package, sufficient to be more than symbolic. Management has signalled openness but cautioned that no plausible executive/shareholder contribution closes a structural gap "measured in the hundreds of millions" — implying a ceiling on what this can practically deliver, which the labour side has not yet accepted.

## 3. Key trade-offs still unaddressed

- **No cost-gap figure has actually been disclosed yet.** Every party's position is conditional on numbers that have not yet been put on the table. Until this happens, "comparable alternative" remains an abstract standard.
- **No costed counter-proposal has been fully quantified.** The Union referenced a counter-proposal (attrition, working-time models, solidarity fund) but headcount/cost/timeline figures were not specified in this round.
- **Timeline tension**: Investors and Management want results within two to three years; Works Council/Union's traditional instruments (attrition, early retirement) are inherently slower. Whether a hybrid timeline can satisfy both has not been addressed.
- **Sequencing of guarantees vs. contributions**: Whether binding site guarantees are offered *before* labour concessions are finalized, or only *after* savings are delivered, remains unresolved — this is a trust/sequencing problem distinct from the scale question.
- **Governance mechanics**: How the State's veto, the Family Holding's influence, and the two-thirds requirement interact procedurally if numbers are disclosed but parties still disagree on adequacy — no dispute-resolution mechanism has been proposed.

## 4. Refocus for Round 2

To move from stated positions to a negotiable settlement, the next round should focus on:

1. **Management to disclose the actual structural cost-gap figure** (or the methodology/timeline for doing so) that any alternative package must close.
2. **Works Council/Union to quantify their counter-proposal** — specific attrition/working-time/wage-contribution numbers and projected savings over a defined multi-year horizon — so it can be tested against the gap.
3. **All sides to address the guarantee-sequencing question**: what is offered first, what is conditional on what, and over what verification period.
4. **A concrete figure or mechanism for the "top-down" contribution** (executive pay, dividends), even if illustrative, to test whether it can satisfy the labour side's legitimacy threshold without being treated as a substitute for structural measures.
