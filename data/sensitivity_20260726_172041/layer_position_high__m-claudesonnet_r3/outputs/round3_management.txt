**Group & Brand Management — Round 3**

Let me deal with each of these in turn, credit what's fair, and be equally clear about where I'm not moving.

**On disaggregation — fair demand, and I'll answer it now rather than in a future round.** Of the €4 billion: roughly €1.6 billion is direct unit labour cost differential — wages, working-time structure, and shift-premium regimes versus comparable competitor plants. Roughly €1.5 billion is capacity-driven fixed-cost absorption — the mechanical effect of running plants designed for higher volumes at today's underutilised throughput, which worsens as European demand contracts further. The remaining roughly €0.9 billion is a mix of logistics, tooling, and site-specific overhead that doesn't cleanly sort into either category. The Works Council is right that this changes the honest comparison: a wage contribution addresses the first bucket most directly; a capacity/shift/consolidation measure addresses the second; the third is a genuine mixed bag that needs line-by-line work with the verifier. I'm giving you this now because I'd rather have the argument on real categories than keep defending a bundled number that invites exactly the suspicion you named. This is the version that goes to the external verifier as the starting methodology.

**On 2028 versus 2030 — I've heard five parties independently reach the same conclusion, and I'm not going to hold a position that has no supporting logic left under it once the trigger mechanism actually does the work I designed it to do.** I'll concede this: 2030, with narrow, objective, jointly-verified reopening triggers. But I want the concession to be honest about what I need in exchange, because this isn't a free withdrawal on my part. The triggers have to be genuinely specific — not "significant market decline" but a defined percentage threshold against a defined baseline, and not "savings shortfall" but a defined percentage of milestone target missed, verified by the same external party, with the trigger design finalised alongside the terms of reference, on the same timeline as the costing exercise. If the triggers end up vague or gamed to never fire, then a 2030 horizon really is a blank guarantee dressed up as conditional, and I will not accept that version. Tight triggers, long horizon — that's the trade, and I think it's the right one.

**On "materially short" and the fallback question — this is where I hold my ground, and I want to be precise about why, because I think both the Works Council and the Union have just tried to redefine the test in a way I cannot accept.**

I agree completely that a partial shortfall against an undisaggregated, incompletely-costed number is not evidence the gap is unclosable — it's evidence the current menu doesn't close it yet. I accept that reduced shifts, partial-line idling, deeper attrition incentives, and inter-plant volume consolidation should all be costed before anyone reaches for a gate. I said that myself last round when I raised the smaller-closure scenario prematurely, and I'll say now: it was premature to raise a fallback before the full non-closure menu was even on the table. Fair correction.

But here is what I will not accept, and I want to be direct rather than let this slide by unaddressed: the Works Council's formulation — that a verified shortfall simply proves "the parties haven't tried hard enough yet" — has no natural endpoint. There is always another lever to propose costing. Another six months of attrition modelling. Another shift pattern. Another transfer scenario. At some point "exhaustion of alternatives" has to mean something operationally, not function as an infinitely renewable extension clause. I am not running a company against a shrinking market with a process that has no decision point built into it. Investors were right to name this, and I stand with that concern, not against it.

So here's my actual position, and I want it heard as a limit, not an opening bid: I will commit to costing the full non-closure menu — the Works Council's list plus the additional capacity measures raised today — in the same joint exercise, on the same timeline, through the same external verifier, finishing by a fixed date I'll propose below. If, once that full menu is costed and verified, a genuine and durable shortfall remains — not a modelling artefact, not an early-round underestimate, but a verified gap after the complete menu has been priced — then this table has to be able to reach a decision, including a capacity or footprint measure, without that decision being treated as a bad-faith betrayal of the process. That is not "closures with a countdown timer." It is refusing to accept a process with no terminus, which is a different thing entirely, and I think the difference matters enough that I won't concede it away.

Concretely: disaggregated figure and terms of reference finalised within four weeks. Full costing — labour package plus the complete non-closure capacity menu — run against the verified, disaggregated number within twelve weeks of that. That is fast by the standard of these processes and slow by the standard of what our order book and cost position can actually absorb, and I'm proposing it as the honest middle, not as a trap.

**On the top-level contribution — I hear both the Works Council and Union naming the sequencing risk to a ratification vote, and I think you're right to push on this now rather than let it drift.** I've made my commitment: 10% compensation reduction, distribution freeze redirected to the fund. The extension to special distributions and buybacks is not mine to unilaterally grant — that decision sits with the Family Holding's governance, not management's. I will say this plainly: I support the extension on the bounded terms the Family Holding described — same horizon, same triggers — and I would ask the Family Holding directly, since it's their call to make, not to leave this as "raise it formally at board level" when the Union has told you exactly why that timing doesn't work for a ratification vote. If this doesn't close this round, I want that said clearly rather than assumed resolved, because I'm not going to let ambiguity here cost me a labour-side mandate I need.

**On the joint costing exercise — I welcome the commitment from both the Works Council and Union, and I'll match it with the deadlines above.** One addition: I want the union's actual wage-contribution percentage and duration in that costing run, not withheld until some later point once the disaggregated figure exists — the figure exists now, as of this round, so the clock on your side starts alongside ours.

**Where I actually stand, unchanged in substance, sharpened in method:** the package has to close the disaggregated, verified gap — labour package plus the full non-closure capacity menu — within a defined timeline, or this board will have to consider capacity measures including, if unavoidable, closures. I've moved on horizon length, and I've given you the disaggregation you asked for. I have not moved, and will not move, on the requirement that this process produce an actual decision within a bounded period rather than an open-ended search for the next lever. That is not obstruction of your process. It is the condition under which I can defend any of this to the board, the market, or my own conscience about what this company needs to survive to 2030.
