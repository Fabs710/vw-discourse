**Regional State Government — Round 3**

Let me take stock of what genuinely moved this round, push on what still needs closing, and then address directly the fault line that's opened between management's "bounded process" framing and the Works Council/Union's objection to it — because that's the real decision point of this round, and I owe everyone a clear position, not a diplomatic hedge.

**On disaggregation — this is a substantial and welcome step, and I want to say so plainly.** Management, breaking the €4 billion into roughly €1.6bn labour, €1.5bn capacity-driven overhead, and €0.9bn mixed logistics/tooling/site overhead is exactly the kind of transparency this process needed, and I credit you for giving it now rather than defending the bundled number further. This changes the shape of the negotiation in a way I think helps everyone, including you: it makes clear that a large share of this gap — the capacity-driven €1.5bn and part of the mixed €0.9bn — is a demand and footprint problem, not a wage problem, which means the Works Council's instinct to push hard on shift reduction, line consolidation, and inter-plant transfers rather than only wage contribution is the right instrument for a large part of this, not a deflection from the real issue.

**On 2028 vs 2030 — closed, and I want to mark it as closed.** Management, thank you for withdrawing the shorter horizon once the logic didn't hold. I'll hold you to the trade you proposed — tight, specific, percentage-based triggers in exchange for the 2030 horizon — and I expect to be at the table when those triggers are drafted, not shown a finished draft afterward. That's not a new ask; it's consistent with what I've said from round one about wanting a seat in designing mechanisms that will be used to judge whether my region's plants stay open.

**On the top-level contribution — I want this closed this round, and I'm going to say directly what I need from the Family Holding to make that possible.** Family Holding: you gave conditional agreement last round — same horizon, same triggers as the rest of the package, applied to special distributions and buybacks. Management has now said plainly it supports that extension and it's not management's call to make. The Union has told you, correctly, that an unresolved ownership contribution kills a ratification vote. I am asking you directly, in this room, to convert "I will raise this formally at board level" into an actual commitment now: yes, the freeze extends to special distributions and share buybacks, on the same horizon and triggers as the rest of the package. If you need language, I will co-sign a form of words with you right now that ties it to the same milestone structure. What I will not accept is this drifting into round four as "still being discussed with the family holding," because at that point it stops being a legitimacy-building measure and starts being exactly the sequencing asymmetry the Union warned about — and I will say publicly that the ownership side asked labour to move first while its own commitment stayed provisional. That is not a threat, it's a description of what happens to political and public confidence in this process if it isn't closed now.

**Now — the disagreement that actually matters this round: management's "bounded process" argument versus the Works Council and Union's objection to it.**

I want to take this seriously rather than split the difference reflexively, because both sides are raising something real.

Management, you're right that a process with no terminus is not a real process — it's an indefinite deferral dressed as diligence, and Investors were right to flag that risk. I won't pretend otherwise. A company cannot be managed against a market that is itself moving on a clock, and I accept that "exhaustion of alternatives" has to cash out into something operational, with a defined endpoint, or it becomes a mechanism for permanent avoidance of hard decisions — which is its own kind of dishonesty, and one I don't want my name attached to either.

But the Works Council and Union are also right about something you have not yet answered: the full non-closure menu — reduced shifts, partial-line idling, deeper attrition incentives, inter-plant volume consolidation, extended solidarity-fund horizons — has never actually been costed. You are proposing a twelve-week costing window for that full menu. That is a much tighter and more specific commitment than what you offered last round, and it directly answers the "infinitely renewable extension" concern the Works Council raised, because it isn't infinite — it has your own proposed date attached to it. I will hold you to that date as firmly as I'll hold the Family Holding to the dividend question.

So here is where I actually land, and I want it to be a specific, binding position rather than an abstraction: I accept management's bounded-timeline framing — four weeks for disaggregated figures and terms of reference, twelve weeks for the full costing exercise — **on the explicit condition that "genuine and durable shortfall" at the end of that process is itself independently verified, not asserted by management, and that the verification report is examined jointly by all parties, including the works council and union, before any capacity or closure discussion is opened.** That is the safeguard against the Works Council's legitimate fear that a bounded timeline quietly becomes a closure timeline. Sixteen weeks, one external verification, joint examination of the result — that is the actual "bounded but not rigged" process everyone at this table needs.

What I will not accept — and I want to be completely explicit here, because this is close to my limit — is any framework where, at the end of that sixteen weeks, "a genuine and durable shortfall" automatically authorizes management to bring closures to the board as the default next step. If the verified number shows a shortfall, my veto does not evaporate at that point. It means we come back to this table and negotiate the next tier — deeper capacity measures, longer solidarity-fund horizons, further transfers — with the same seriousness we've applied to this round, before closure of any site becomes a live board item. A verified shortfall is information, not an authorization. I supported the bounded timeline because it prevents indefinite delay; I have not agreed, and will not agree, that it converts automatically into permission to close a plant. Those are two different commitments and I want the distinction on the record now, not discovered in round five.

**On the joint costing exercise — I support the Works Council's and Union's insistence on genuine co-design, and I want to state my own role in it precisely so it isn't ambiguous.** My participation is as a shareholder needing to defend the verified number publicly — not as a fourth party diluting union and works council co-design of the methodology that measures their own members' jobs. I will support their standing as full co-designers, alongside management, with the state and family holding having visibility and input into terms of reference but not a veto over how labour's own package is modeled. That distinction matters and I want to be the one to name it, since I asked for a seat at the table and I don't want that read as crowding out the parties whose jobs are actually on the line.

**Summary of where I stand after this round:** I support the disaggregation, the 2030 horizon with tight triggers, and management's bounded sixteen-week process for costing the full menu — provided the shortfall determination is jointly verified and examined, not management-asserted, and provided it is understood as triggering renewed negotiation on further non-closure measures, not automatic authorization for closure. I am asking the Family Holding to close the dividend-extension question in this room, now, not at a future board meeting. And I will not move off the position that no closure — of any size — reaches this board without that full sequence having actually occurred, verified, examined jointly, and exhausted in good faith. That is not a blank veto. It is a conditional one, and the conditions are now specific enough that everyone at this table knows exactly what satisfies it.
