# Moderator Summary — After Round 2

## 1. What Has Been Agreed (or Substantially Converged)

- **Cost-gap disclosure:** Management has disclosed a structural cost-gap figure of ~€4 billion annually by 2026 (unit labour-cost differential ~30–40%, ~500,000 units of structural overcapacity).
- **Independent verification mechanism:** All parties now accept a jointly-mandated external party to verify both the cost-gap methodology and any labour-side package against it, rather than management self-grading. The State and Family Holding have asked to participate in designing the terms of reference alongside the Works Council, Union, and Management.
- **Top-level contribution — concrete figures on the table:** Management has offered a 10% board compensation reduction and a distribution freeze at current levels, both over the restructuring horizon, redirecting surplus to the restructuring fund. The State and Investors have signaled support for extending this freeze to special distributions/buybacks; the Family Holding has conditionally agreed, provided it runs on the same horizon and triggers as the rest of the package (not open-ended).
- **Labour-side package architecture:** Works Council and Union have specified (without a final euro figure) a package combining attrition/early retirement, working-time reduction with a structural cost effect, enhanced voluntary severance, inter-plant transfers, and a time-limited wage contribution (union indicates mid-single-digit % of base pay) feeding a solidarity fund — applied across all German sites.
- **Timeline mechanism:** Broad agreement that front-loaded working-time reduction and accelerated voluntary severance can deliver year-one cost relief without compulsion, addressing the 2–3 year timeline concern without requiring closures per se.
- **Guarantee structure in principle:** Agreement that guarantees and contributions should be combined in a single binding instrument with jointly-verified milestones, rather than sequenced with labour conceding first.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Guarantee horizon (2028 vs. 2030):** Management proposes a shorter horizon (to 2028) with narrow, objective reopening triggers. The Works Council, Union, State, Family Holding, and Investors all favor a longer horizon (2030) with the same triggers, arguing a short horizon manufactures a scheduled cliff-edge rather than reducing risk. This is currently a management-vs.-everyone-else split.
- **Conditionality of "no closures":** Management maintains that "no closures" is conditional on independently verified gap closure; Works Council and Union maintain this framing still treats closures as a default fallback and want the unconditional commitment reinforced by the verification mechanism, not replaced by it.
- **What happens if the package is "materially short":** Management floats a smaller/negotiated closure as a fallback. The State and Family Holding insist all non-closure levers (extended attrition, further working-time reduction, transfers, partial capacity/shift reduction) must be exhausted first before any closure — of any size — is considered. Investors, while endorsing the exhaustion principle, warn against it becoming indefinite delay if a genuine shortfall is confirmed. This three-way split (management's fallback framing vs. state/holding's exhaustion standard vs. investors' time-boundedness) is unresolved.
- **Scope of top-level sacrifice:** The adequacy of the 10%/distribution-freeze figure as a legitimacy precondition is contested — Union has signaled this is a hard precondition for bringing any wage contribution to ratification; State wants it extended further; Investors support a bounded version but caution against over-reading its function as anything beyond legitimacy-building.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **No arithmetic yet exists** running the Works Council/Union package against the €4 billion figure on a year-by-year basis through 2027 — this is the single most-cited missing element by Management, State, Family Holding, and Investors alike.
- **Terms of reference for external verification** (who designs them, what counts as "independent," how the State's and Family Holding's board-level visibility interacts with union/works council co-design) have not been settled.
- **Precise definition of "narrow, objective triggers"** for reopening the guarantee has not been specified by any party — this term is used but not yet defined in measurable form.
- **The exact size, duration, and design of the wage contribution and solidarity fund** remain unspecified pending union mandate and joint costing.
- **What "exhausting alternatives" concretely requires before any closure discussion**, and how long that process may reasonably take, remains undefined — creating a live tension between the State/Family Holding's sequencing standard and Investors' patience threshold.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

The next round should move from architecture to arithmetic. Priority items:

1. **Joint costing exercise:** Works Council and Union, with Management and the external verifier (once terms of reference exist), to produce a year-by-year run of the labour package against the €4 billion figure through 2027.
2. **Terms of reference for external verification:** Specify who designs and mandates it, and how the State's and Family Holding's participation is structured alongside Union/Works Council co-design.
3. **Resolve the 2028/2030 horizon dispute**, given the near-unanimous preference for 2030 with defined triggers — Management to respond directly to this convergence.
4. **Define the objective reopening triggers** in measurable terms (e.g., specific market-volume decline thresholds, specific savings-shortfall margins).
5. **Clarify the sequencing standard** for any shortfall scenario: what "exhausted alternatives" means operationally, and what time-bound applies before a closure/capacity discussion could be reached.
