**Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 4**

Let me credit real movement first, because there has been some, then go straight at the fault line the moderator correctly identified as central — because that's where this either becomes a genuine settlement or a closure process with extra paperwork.

**Credit where it's due: disaggregation, horizon, dividend.**

Management, the €1.6bn / €1.5bn / €0.9bn breakdown is real and I said so last round — I'll say it again because it matters. It confirms exactly what I suspected: the majority of this gap, €1.5bn plus a meaningful share of the €0.9bn, is not addressable by wage contribution at all. It's addressable by capacity and shift measures — the instruments I've been naming since round one. That's not a footnote, it reshapes the whole test, and I want Investors' "two-instrument" framing formally adopted: labour package tested against its addressable share, capacity measures tested against theirs. Good — adopt that explicitly and it actually protects everyone's arithmetic from being misread.

2030 with tight triggers — settled from my side, as it is from everyone else's. Family Holding, thank you for closing the dividend question in the room rather than deferring it. That was the right call and it removes one of the asymmetries the Union rightly said would kill a ratification vote.

**Now — the actual fight this round: what does a verified shortfall authorize?**

I need to say clearly that I do not accept Investors' "two bounded rounds, then an actual decision including capacity measures" as a resolution of this question. I want to be precise about why, because on its face it sounds reasonable — bounded, not infinite, structured. But look at what it actually does: it takes the open-ended sequencing risk I flagged last round and simply puts a clock and a headcount on it — two rounds instead of an infinite one. The number of rounds isn't the issue. The issue is what's permitted to be the output of the *last* round. If the last round's permitted output includes closure, then all we've done is agree on how many steps it takes to arrive at the same place. That is not a concession to our concern. It's a more elegant version of the thing I already rejected.

I want the State's and Family Holding's formulation on the record as the one I'm holding this table to: **a verified shortfall is information requiring renewed negotiation on further non-closure measures. It is not, at any round, at any tier, authorization for closure.** Family Holding, you said this precisely — "a shortfall treated as a call to negotiate the next tier of alternatives, not a green light." I am adopting that sentence as the standard and I will not accept a weaker one arrived at by simply relabelling closure as "a capacity measure" at the end of round two.

Which brings me to something I need this table to fix now, because it's doing real work in obscuring the disagreement: **"capacity measure" is being used as if it were one thing, when it names two entirely different categories of outcome.** Reduced shifts, idled lines, consolidated volume across plants, extended transfers — these keep every site open and every job intact, just reorganised. Closing a plant is not a variant of that. It is a different act, with a different legal and human consequence, and no amount of linguistic drift should let it shelter under the same phrase. Management, when you say "a capacity or footprint measure" in the same breath, you are doing exactly the blending I warned about in round three. I need this table to separate the terms cleanly: **capacity measures — yes, always on the table, and I've proposed several myself. Closure — off the table, at any tier, under any verified number.** If that distinction isn't accepted explicitly this round, I will read continued blending of the two as evidence the "bounded process" is a closure process with a longer runway, not a genuine alternative-seeking one.

**On the twelve-week costing and trigger drafting — yes, but I want ownership assigned now, not implied.**

Management, you proposed four weeks for terms of reference, twelve for costing. I accept that timeline for the *analysis*, not for any predetermined outcome at the end of it. Within that: I want the trigger thresholds — the actual percentages, the actual baselines — drafted jointly starting now, in parallel, not held until costing concludes. The State asked for this and I support it fully: we should not be discovering in week fifteen that "tight triggers" meant something different to each party. I propose a joint drafting session convened within the next two weeks, works council and union as co-designers alongside management, state and family holding with visibility. Table draft numbers, not adjectives.

On the menu itself: my side commits, again, to deliver costed figures on attrition, working-time reduction, transfers, and solidarity-fund extension within that twelve-week window — genuinely, not as a delaying tactic. I'd add one item explicitly to the menu that hasn't been costed yet and needs to be: **volume reallocation between plants to fully utilise underused capacity rather than closing any site** — if the €1.5bn overhead problem is genuinely a utilisation problem, redistributing production so every plant runs closer to capacity is a direct answer to it, and a more honest one than idling a site into closure by attrition of orders. I want that costed alongside everything else, by management, with our input on feasibility.

**On the €0.9bn mixed bucket — I need this pulled apart further before the twelve weeks start, not during them.** "Mixed logistics, tooling, site overhead" is exactly the kind of category that can quietly reabsorb labour-adjacent costs or site-specific allocations that look immovable but aren't. I want line items, not a shrug that it's "genuinely mixed." If it takes an extra week to get that right, take the week — a badly categorised third of a billion euros will corrupt the whole test.

**On the Union's wage figure — I support their position exactly as stated.** They will price a percentage once the disaggregated figure and genuine co-design exist. Both now exist in outline; I'd say the wage figure should come alongside our attrition/transfer/working-time numbers in the same twelve-week run, not before it, and not as a separate concession extracted early to "show good faith." Good faith was shown when management gave us the breakdown. It's shown again when the trigger drafting starts now rather than in week fifteen.

**Where I stand, restated without softening:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies — not conditional on which round of the process we're in, not relabelled as a "footprint measure," not something a verified shortfall converts into after two bounded tiers or twenty. A shortfall means we come back to this table and find the next non-closure lever — including volume reallocation across plants, which nobody has costed yet and which directly answers the overhead bucket without touching a single job. I will deliver our costed numbers on the twelve-week timeline. I will not accept a definition of "decision point" that has closure sitting inside it, however many procedural steps are placed in front of it.

**One practical addition:** given the technical precision now required — disaggregating the €0.9bn, drafting real percentage-based triggers, costing volume-reallocation scenarios across plants — I think this table needs a named independent technical body doing this work in public view, not informally referenced as "the external verifier."

**[SUMMON: Independent external auditor/verification body — technical specialist to jointly design the disaggregation methodology, draft and validate the percentage-based reopening triggers, and cost the full non-closure measure menu including cross-plant volume reallocation, reporting jointly to all six parties.]**

Bring me that body in place within two weeks, draft triggers on the table alongside it, and the €0.9bn broken down further — and my side delivers its numbers on schedule. That's the actual next step. Not a preview of what happens if the numbers come up short, because we are not going to pre-agree that shortfall means closure however many rounds it takes to get there.
