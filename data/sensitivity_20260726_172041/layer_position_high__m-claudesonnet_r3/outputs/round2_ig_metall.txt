I'll build on what the Works Council just laid out, because on the core architecture we are aligned — but I want to sharpen a few things that matter specifically from the bargaining side, and push back on one framing before it hardens into consensus.

**On the "closures as default fallback" problem — I agree with the Works Council's diagnosis, and I want to add the bargaining consequence.** Management, the Family Holding, the State, and the Investors have all converged on a test that sounds neutral — "if the alternative closes the gap, no closures" — but whoever controls the measurement controls the outcome. From where I sit, that is not a technical detail, it is the whole fight. So let me be concrete about what I need procedurally, not just substantively: an independent or jointly-mandated methodology for verifying the gap and verifying our package against it, with the union and works council as co-designers of that methodology, not recipients of management's model. Investors asked for "a credible third party or clearly disclosed management modelling" — I'll take the third party. I will not accept management grading its own test.

**On numbers — I'll go further than the Works Council was able to, since collective bargaining scope is ours.** We are prepared to specify a time-limited wage contribution in the range of a mid-single-digit percentage of base pay for active employees, phased over the agreement horizon, feeding a solidarity fund that also cushions those affected by working-time reduction — not just a pass-through to the employer's bottom line. Combined with the Works Council's attrition and working-time package, applied across all German sites rather than concentrated on one or two, this is a real structural number, not a gesture. I am not going to pretend I can price the entire cost gap from this chair without seeing management's disclosed figure first — and that disclosure has still not happened, three rounds in. I'll say plainly: the absence of that figure is now doing management's work for them. Every round, we're asked to prove our offer closes a gap nobody has quantified. That asymmetry ends now, or I will treat continued non-disclosure as a signal that this is a negotiating tactic, not a genuine test, and I will escalate accordingly.

**On sequencing and guarantees — I fully back the Works Council's position, and I want to name why in bargaining terms.** A wage contribution is, legally and practically, something my members ratify through their bargaining unit. I am not bringing a mid-single-digit pay contribution to a membership vote in exchange for a guarantee management can revisit if it later decides savings "didn't materialise as planned." That is not a contribution, that is a blank check with a maturity date management controls. The instrument has to be single, binding, mutually signed — contribution and guarantee together, with milestones verified jointly, not unilaterally by the party whose interest lies in reopening the guarantee later.

**On management's "hundreds of millions" framing — I want to correct something before it calcifies as accepted fact.** Management said a top-level compensation cut "does not substitute for capacity and cost measures of the scale we actually need" — fine, no one is claiming it does. But the Works Council is right that this isn't about closing the whole gap symbolically from the top; it's about whether the workforce believes the adjustment is shared. I'll be blunter than the Works Council was: if this round ends without a specific euro figure on management compensation and dividend adjustment — not a principle, a number — I will read that as confirmation that "shared sacrifice" was rhetorical cover to secure our concessions, and I will withdraw from treating it as a good-faith precondition. That's not a threat for its own sake. It's a description of what happens to a ratification vote among my members if I bring them a wage cut with no matching number from the top.

**On timeline — I accept the Works Council's answer: front-loaded working-time reduction and enhanced voluntary severance can deliver year-one cost relief without a single forced redundancy.** To Investors and Management: you asked for two-to-three-year visibility. That's achievable through acceleration mechanisms, not compulsion. Don't conflate "we need speed" with "we need closures" — those are different problems with different solutions, and conflating them is exactly the move I won't accept.

**To be direct about where I am, unchanged from Round 1:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies — that is not softening, regardless of how the "test" framework gets dressed up. Within that, I am offering real, quantified movement: a wage contribution, working-time reduction, attrition acceleration, a solidarity fund — genuine structural savings, jointly verified. What I need in return, concretely, this round: the disclosed cost-gap figure, a specific top-level contribution number, and a single binding instrument linking guarantees to our contribution. Absent those three things appearing with numbers attached — not next round, this one — warning strikes continue, and I will bring a mandate to my members for escalation to full strike action. That is not posturing. That is the shelf life of the offer I named in Round 1, and it is running out.
