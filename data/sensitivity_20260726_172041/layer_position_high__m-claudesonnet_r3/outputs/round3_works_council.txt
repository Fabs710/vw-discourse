**Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 3**

Let me start with what actually moved, credit it, and then go straight at what hasn't — because there's a pattern forming across this table that I need to name before it hardens into "consensus."

**On the €4 billion figure: disclosure is real progress. But a number without disaggregation is not yet a number I can test against — and I want to be precise about what I'm asking for.**

Management, you gave us a headline figure and two components — a 30-40% unit labour-cost differential and roughly 500,000 units of structural overcapacity. I need this broken down by site, by cost category, and by cause, before anyone can honestly say whether our package "closes" it. Specifically: how much of the €4 billion is labour cost as such, versus fixed overhead allocated per unit that mechanically worsens whenever volume falls — meaning it isn't a labour problem at all, it's a demand problem dressed up as a labour problem? Because if a meaningful share of that €4 billion is capacity-driven overhead rather than wage cost, then the honest comparison isn't "our wage contribution versus your plant closure" — it's "our shift reduction and capacity consolidation versus your plant closure," and those are different instruments with a very different footprint on jobs. I am not accusing you of inflating the number. I am telling you that until it's disaggregated, "close the gap" is not yet a testable instruction — it is a slogan with four billion attached to it. That disaggregation is now a precondition for the costing exercise everyone keeps asking us to produce, and I will not hand over our numbers to be measured against a black box.

**On the 2028 vs 2030 horizon: I want to be blunt about what's happening here.** Every single other party at this table — the State, the Family Holding, the Investors, and us — has independently arrived at the same conclusion: 2030 with narrow, objective triggers is more stable, not less, than 2028. That is not labour's position winning by headcount. That is every party whose job is to price risk telling management that a shorter, rigid horizon is worse risk management, not better. Management, at this point continuing to hold 2028 isn't a fiduciary position, it's a negotiating anchor with no supporting logic left standing under it. I'd ask you directly: if every shareholder-side voice at this table is telling you 2030-with-triggers reduces risk rather than increasing it, what is actually left of your objection besides wanting a shorter leash on us? Withdraw it. This one shouldn't need a fourth round.

**On "materially short" and the fallback to a smaller closure — this is where I need to correct the drift I'm hearing, including from parties I've otherwise found aligned with us.**

The State and Family Holding set a real standard: exhaust every non-closure lever — extended attrition, further working-time reduction, transfers, partial capacity reduction through reduced shifts or lines — before any closure, of any size, reaches the board. I support that standard completely. But Investors then added a qualifier: if the verified shortfall is "real and durable," continuing to search for alternatives for "another six months" isn't something they'll support — at that point they want "a decision... a smaller closure, a capacity measure, something."

I want to flag this precisely, because it's the mechanism by which "exhaustion of alternatives" quietly turns back into "closures with a countdown timer." A verified shortfall of, say, 40% of the gap is not evidence that no further non-closure measure exists — it's evidence that the measures on the table today don't close it. Reduced shifts, partial-line idling, further transfer of production between plants to concentrate volume without shutting a site, deeper attrition incentives, a longer solidarity-fund horizon — none of these have been costed yet either, and none of them require a closed gate. "Real and durable" doesn't mean "unsolvable by anything except closure." It means the parties haven't tried hard enough yet. I am not going to accept a framework where six months of good-faith negotiation on our side buys us a shortened patience clock that ends at closure. If investors want speed, the honest way to get it is to run the disaggregated numbers and the full menu of non-closure capacity measures in parallel, now — not to pre-agree that a partial verified gap converts automatically into "something" that includes closing a gate.

**On top-level contribution — I stand with the Union's position stated plainly, and I want to add the works council's specific stake in it.**

Management, 10% and a distribution freeze is a real number — I said that last round and I'll say it again, it is not nothing. But the State asked you to extend the freeze to special distributions and buybacks, and the Family Holding gave conditional agreement — same horizon, same triggers. I want that closed out this round, not left as "we'll raise it at board level." Because here is the concrete consequence if it isn't: I sit on the supervisory board, and I will be asked to vote on a restructuring package that asks my members to accept wage contributions and working-time cuts while ownership's exposure is bounded to ordinary dividends only. That asymmetry is votable against. I am telling you plainly what happens at the board table if this isn't resolved before we get there, not as a threat, as a description of the arithmetic of a two-thirds majority you do not have without our seats.

**On the joint costing exercise — yes, we will do it, on the following basis, and I want this on the record as a commitment, not a delay.**

Once the disaggregated gap figure exists and the terms of reference for external verification are jointly set — with the works council and union as full co-designers, not consulted parties, alongside management and the state's participation — we will run our attrition, transfer, working-time, and wage-contribution package year-by-year through 2027, jointly with the verifier. I will not run our numbers against an undisclosed methodology, and I will not accept a verification process where the state and family holding have "visibility" while we merely respond to conclusions reached elsewhere. Co-design means co-design for everyone at this table who has skin in the outcome, and that includes us most of all, since it's our members' jobs being measured.

**Where I stand, unchanged, and I want this said without qualification given the drift I've flagged above:** no closures — none, not smaller, not partial, not dressed as a "capacity measure" — and no compulsory redundancies. The verification mechanism exists to prove our package can close the disaggregated gap, not to produce a moment where someone at this table declares the gap "unclosable" and reaches for a gate. If the arithmetic, once honestly disaggregated and run against the full non-closure menu — including capacity measures neither management nor investors have costed yet — genuinely cannot close it, I will engage with that finding in good faith, as I said I would. But we are not there. We are at the point where the number needs breaking down, the full menu of non-closure capacity levers needs costing, and the top-level contribution needs closing out — before anyone talks about what happens if it falls short. Bring me the disaggregated figure and finish the co-design of the verifier, and my side delivers its costed numbers in return. That is the actual next step, not a preview of the fallback.
