[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

Let me respond to what actually shifted in Round 1, and be equally clear about what did not.

First, something important: management, the family holding, the state, and the investors have all converged on a framework where closures remain the *default fallback* if our alternative "doesn't close the gap." I want to name what that framework actually does, because I don't think it's neutral. It quietly relitigates our red line every time a number is disputed. It says: no closures, unless we decide your numbers are insufficient — at which point closures are back on the table. That is not a concession to us. That is closures with a probation period. I will not accept a framework where our unconditional position is treated as provisional pending an assessment that management itself, or a board majority we don't control, gets to make. If "comparable scale" is going to be the test, it must be assessed jointly, with an independent, mutually agreed methodology — not management's internal modelling presented to us as fact. Otherwise "prove it closes the gap" becomes a moving target that always lands on closures.

So here is what I will actually put on the table, since the moderator and several of you asked for numbers rather than positions.

We are prepared to specify: a defined volume of capacity reduction through natural attrition and demographically-timed early retirement, phased over a multi-year horizon with published headcount targets by site and year; a working-time reduction model with a real, calculable labour-cost effect — not a token few percent, but a structural adjustment comparable in euro terms to what a closure would save in direct labour cost, applied across all German sites rather than concentrated on one or two; and a time-limited wage contribution from the active workforce, sized in coordination with the union, feeding a solidarity fund that also absorbs some of the adjustment cost rather than pushing it entirely onto base pay. I am not going to invent a precise euro figure unilaterally in this room without the union, since collective bargaining scope sits with them — but I will say plainly that "comparable scale" is achievable through this combination if management is honest about the actual gap rather than treating closures as structurally cheaper simply because closing a plant is administratively cleaner than negotiating attrition curves.

On timeline — management and the investors both pushed hard on "two to three years, not a decade." I hear that, and I will not pretend attrition alone moves that fast. But speed can be engineered without compulsion: enhanced voluntary severance incentives to accelerate departures beyond natural turnover, inter-plant transfers to load-balance underutilized capacity rather than shutting it, and front-loaded working-time reduction that delivers cost relief in year one, not year five. That is a genuine answer to the timeline problem that does not require a single forced redundancy or a single closed gate.

On guarantees and sequencing — this is where I want to be very direct with management. You proposed guarantees "tied to delivered savings," revisitable rather than binding. I understand why that's attractive to you. It is not acceptable to us, and here is the actual reason, not just principle: revisitable guarantees mean we bear the concessions now — pay, working time, headcount reduction — while the guarantee protecting jobs and sites remains conditional on your future judgment of whether savings materialised as planned. That asymmetry is exactly the pattern that broke trust the first time. If this is a genuine shared-sacrifice package, the guarantee and the contribution must be locked in together, in the same binding instrument, verified by agreed milestones — not sequenced so that labour's side is irreversible and management's side is provisional.

On shared sacrifice at the top — I appreciate that management, the family holding, and even the investors have now all said this must be real, not symbolic. Good. Then put a number on the table this round, not next round. What is the management board compensation reduction, in euros, over the same horizon? What is the dividend or distribution adjustment? Don't tell me it "won't close a gap measured in hundreds of millions" — nobody claimed it would close the whole gap by itself. It closes the legitimacy gap, without which you will not get workers to accept working-time cuts and wage contributions, and you will not get my signature on anything at board level. That is not a technicality. It is the actual precondition for labour peace.

Finally, to the state and the family holding: I welcome your framing that a plan management cannot pass through this board is not a win. I'd add — it's not just an arithmetic fact about the two-thirds requirement, it is a description of what will actually happen if this proceeds unilaterally: more strikes, harder ones, and a workforce that no longer believes anything this company signs. I am not threatening that as leverage for its own sake. I am telling you what the mandate I represent will actually do if handed a plan with closures dressed up as the "objective" outcome of a numbers exercise we didn't design and don't control.

So: bring me the actual cost-gap figure, jointly verified. Bring me a real top-line contribution number. And accept that guarantees and contributions are signed together, not sequentially. On those terms, I will negotiate the scale of our contribution in good faith with the union. Without them, my position from Round 1 stands unchanged: no closures, no compulsory redundancies, full stop.
