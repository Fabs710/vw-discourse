[Controlling Family Holding — Round 2]

I'll be more concrete this round than I was in Round 1, because several of you have now put numbers and mechanisms on the table, and a general statement of support is no longer sufficient — it would be evasive at exactly the point where this negotiation needs the holding's actual position, not just its blessing of others'.

**On the €4 billion figure and external verification: I support this, and I support the State's addition.** A jointly-designed methodology — union, works council, management, and the State as a shareholder with a public mandate — is the right construction. I would add the holding's perspective on why this matters to us specifically, beyond general fairness: this figure now becomes the basis on which the supervisory board tests any settlement, including the one I have to defend to my own family shareholders across generations. An externally verified number protects everyone from relitigating adequacy indefinitely, and it protects the holding from being asked to bless a plan on trust rather than evidence. I will support the terms of reference including State participation, and I would ask that the holding's board representatives also have visibility into the methodology as it's designed — not as a fourth party competing for control of it, but because we ultimately have to vote on what it produces.

**On the "closures as default fallback" objection from the Works Council and Union — I understand the concern, but I don't accept the characterisation, and I want to explain why in terms specific to governance, not just repeat management's answer.** A conditional framework is not the same as a predetermined outcome dressed up as neutrality — but only if the condition is tested by a mechanism neither side controls unilaterally. That is precisely what the external verification now on the table is for. If I believed management were going to grade its own homework and call it objectivity, I would say so plainly, because that would be a governance failure I have a direct stake in preventing — a board vote built on a self-serving number is not one I can defend to my own family, let alone to the market. With joint terms of reference and an independent verifier, the test is real, not rhetorical. I would ask the Works Council and Union to treat the concession on verification as the actual answer to the objection you raised, rather than continuing to describe the framework as inherently rigged after the mechanism you asked for has been offered.

**On management's top-level contribution — 10% compensation reduction and a distribution freeze — I will support this at board level, and I want to be direct about what I will and won't go further on.** I hear the State's push to extend the freeze to special distributions and buybacks, and I understand the legitimacy logic behind it. I am not going to reject that out of hand — shared sacrifice has to be real, and I said that in Round 1 without waiting to be forced. But I want to be precise, because this is the point where the holding's own interest is directly engaged, not abstract: freezing ordinary dividends during a restructuring horizon is a reasonable and defensible governance decision. Extending that indefinitely to any surplus capital allocation without a defined horizon and without linking it to the same milestone structure as the rest of the package is a different thing — it starts to look like an open-ended constraint on ownership decisions rather than a shared-sacrifice contribution tied to this specific restructuring. So my position is: yes to extending the freeze to special distributions and buybacks, provided it runs on the same horizon and the same objective, jointly-verified triggers as everything else in this package — not as a separate, unbounded commitment. I will raise this formally at board level on that basis, and I expect it to be received as a genuine move, not a minimal one.

**On the 2028-versus-2030 guarantee horizon — I side with the State here, and against management's proposed shortening, and I want to explain why from a long-term value standpoint, since that's the actual test I apply.** Management's underlying worry — a fixed commitment that becomes indefensible against a further market shock — is legitimate, and I won't dismiss it. But the answer to that worry is precisely what management itself proposed: narrow, objective, jointly-verified reopening triggers. If the triggers are real, the horizon doesn't need to be short to protect the company from an unforeseen shock — the trigger does that work. Shortening to 2028 instead doesn't reduce risk, it manufactures a second cliff-edge in two years regardless of whether any actual shock has occurred, and a company staring down a second confrontation in 2028 on a fixed calendar, rather than on a verified condition, is a worse outcome for enterprise value than a 2030 horizon with real triggers. I would ask management to reconsider on this specific point. This is not me taking the labour side's position reflexively — it's me applying the same test I've applied throughout: what protects durable value versus what merely feels safer on a spreadsheet.

**On the Works Council's and Union's package — attrition, working-time reduction, wage contribution, solidarity fund, applied across all German sites — I want to see it run against the €4 billion figure before I express a view on adequacy, and I say that neutrally, not as a delaying tactic.** I don't share the instinct to assume it will fall short, nor to assume it will fully close the gap. That is exactly why the verification exists. What I will say is that the scale you've described — a mid-single-digit wage contribution, accelerated attrition, front-loaded working-time reduction across the whole footprint — sounds, on its face, like a genuine structural measure rather than a symbolic one, and if it verifies close to €4 billion on the timeline management asked for, I will argue for it over closures at board level. I said that in Round 1; nothing since has changed that. If it verifies materially short, I will not pretend otherwise to keep the peace — but I share the State's view that "materially short" should trigger a genuine exhaustion of the next tier of alternatives, not an immediate reach for closure, however small.

To be clear about where the holding's actual limit sits, since I was asked implicitly to make this concrete rather than abstract: I will not support any outcome — whether closures reached without exhausting real alternatives, or a settlement built on unverified or cosmetic numbers, or a prolonged conflict that drags into years of strikes and board deadlock — that permanently impairs this company's competitiveness or destabilises the governance structure that has allowed it to be managed for the long term. Within that boundary, I am genuinely open to whichever mechanism — closures, the labour side's package, or some combination — actually gets verified as restoring competitive margins with durable labour peace. That is not a soft position dressed as flexibility. It is the actual test I will apply when this reaches the board.
