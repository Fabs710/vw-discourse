**Group & Brand Management — Round 4**

Let me address the technical work first, then go directly at what's become the real question this round: the Works Council and Union are now asking me to agree, explicitly and in writing, that closure is permanently off the table regardless of what the verified numbers show. I need to say clearly: I will not agree to that. Not because I'm attached to closures — I've said twice now I'd rather not go there — but because what's being asked isn't a negotiating position, it's a demand that I surrender the one thing that makes this whole process credible to my board, my investors, and frankly to my own judgment about what this company needs.

**On the auditor's work — genuinely useful, and I accept the data-access obligations.** Ledger-level tracing of the €0.9bn, the reclassification safeguard the Union raised on shift premiums potentially double-counted between labour and overhead buckets — agreed, and I'll commit now that anything reclassified moves fully from one bucket to the other, verified by the auditor, not left ambiguous. Full general ledger, allocation-key vintage, depreciation schedules — you'll have data access within the two-week window. I also accept running the €0.9bn decomposition, trigger drafting, and menu costing concurrently rather than sequentially, as the auditor recommended — that's the right call technically and I don't have a reason to resist it.

On volume reallocation: cost it. I'm not afraid of that number. But I want to say plainly what I expect it to show, because I don't think it does what the Works Council hopes. The auditor named this precisely: reallocation redistributes underutilization, it doesn't eliminate it, unless the underlying problem is a mismatched-utilization problem rather than a genuine excess-installed-capacity problem relative to a shrinking European market. I think a meaningful share of the €1.5bn is the latter — total addressable demand smaller than total installed capacity across all our German sites combined. If that's what the auditor's model shows, moving volume between plants doesn't close the gap; it just moves which plant is underutilized. I'm not pre-judging the number, I'm telling you what I think it will say, and I want that scenario modelled honestly rather than treated as the alternative that obviously makes closure unnecessary. It might. It might not. Let's see the arithmetic before anyone declares victory on either side.

**Now — the actual disagreement, and I want to be fully direct about it, because I think both the Works Council and the Union just asked me to accept something I explicitly flagged as unacceptable last round, and I heard it, and I'm not moving.**

The Union's proposed terminus: if the parties jointly determine no further non-closure lever exists, the table "renegotiates the shape of the whole settlement... before any conversation about footprint changes." Read carefully, that sentence still doesn't say what happens if, after that renegotiation, a gap remains. It just adds another layer before we get to the same question. The Works Council's position is more direct: closure is off the table, full stop, at any tier, under any verified number. I respect the clarity of that even as I reject it.

Here is my problem, stated as plainly as I can: **I am being asked to agree, in advance, that no matter what the disaggregated, jointly-verified numbers eventually show, one category of response is permanently excluded from consideration.** That is not a bounded process with a safeguard against bad faith. That is a predetermined outcome with a sixteen-week (or infinite) research exercise attached to it for appearances. I did not come to this table to run an elaborate verification process whose conclusion is fixed before the data exists. If that's genuinely the offer, I should say so now rather than let four more rounds of careful technical language obscure it: **I cannot sign a framework that rules out capacity-related site measures as a category, regardless of what the verified arithmetic shows.** That would mean that if the full non-closure menu — attrition, transfers, shift reduction, volume reallocation, wage contribution, solidarity fund — is costed honestly and a durable structural gap still remains, this company has no lawful, board-legitimate path to close it. That is not a compromise I can defend to the board, to the market, or to my own responsibility for this company's survival to 2030. I will not accept it, and I want to be clear that's a limit, not an opening bid to be negotiated down from.

I want to be precise about what I am *not* saying, because I think there's value in narrowing the actual gap between us rather than letting it look larger than it is. I am not saying closure is the likely outcome. I am not saying I want closure. I am not rejecting the "information, not automatic authorization" framing the State and Family Holding proposed — I accepted that framing last round and I still accept it. A shortfall doesn't hand me a mandate to walk into the board with a closure proposal the day the number comes in. What I'm rejecting is the further step being asked of me now: that even after every non-closure lever has been genuinely exhausted, tested, and still comes up short, closure remains categorically unavailable regardless. That's a different, much larger demand than "don't jump to closure prematurely" — which I've already conceded — and I think it's important this table sees the distinction, because right now it's being presented as if I already agreed to it. I didn't.

**On Investors' two-round structure — I think it's closer to workable than what's being proposed as the alternative, but I'll go further than Investors did, because I think there's a genuine synthesis available here.** Let the process work like this: disaggregated figure and terms of reference — four weeks. Full menu costed, including volume reallocation and reclassified buckets — twelve weeks, verified jointly. If a durable, verified gap remains at that point, the parties negotiate a second, bounded round — Investors suggested six to eight weeks — on further non-closure measures specifically identified as not yet exhausted, not invented on the spot to buy time. If, after that second bounded round, the verified gap is still real and durable — confirmed by the same external body, examined jointly by all six parties as the State insisted — then the board has to be able to consider the full range of options, including capacity and footprint measures, because at that point every non-closure lever this table can identify will genuinely have been priced and tried. That is not a closure countdown. It's a refusal to accept that "we haven't found the right lever yet" can be asserted indefinitely once every actual lever anyone has proposed has already been costed and applied.

I want to answer the Works Council's point about terminology directly, because it's a fair challenge and I should meet it rather than let it sit as an accusation of bad faith. You're right that "capacity measure" has been used loosely by me to blend shift reduction with closure, and that blending does real damage to trust — I'll stop doing that. From here: **capacity measures** — shifts, line idling, volume reallocation, transfers — are a distinct category from **closures**, and I'll use the terms precisely as you've defined them. But precision of language doesn't mean I accept that closure, cleanly and separately named, is excluded from the set of things this board can ever consider. It means I'll stop hiding behind vague language and state plainly, when the moment comes, exactly what I'm proposing and why — which is a commitment to honesty in the process, not a concession on scope.

**On the Union's wage-figure scaling principle — agreed.** Scale the wage contribution against the labour-addressable share of the gap, adjusted for the auditor's reclassification findings, not against the full €4bn. That's the correct denominator and I accept it without reservation.

**Where I actually stand after this round:** I accept the auditor's work program, the concurrent timelines, the reclassification safeguard, and the "information not automatic authorization" framing for a first shortfall finding. I propose — and will hold to — a two-tier bounded structure: twelve-week full costing, then a bounded second round of six to eight weeks on any genuinely unexhausted non-closure lever, then a joint, verified determination. I will not agree, now or in any future round, that closure is categorically and permanently excluded from that final determination regardless of what the verified numbers show after that full sequence. If the Works Council and Union need to hear it this bluntly to plan their own strategy accurately: that is the line I will not cross, and no amount of procedural elegance in how we get to that question will change what I do once we're standing in front of it with a genuine, durable, verified gap and no further lever left to cost.
