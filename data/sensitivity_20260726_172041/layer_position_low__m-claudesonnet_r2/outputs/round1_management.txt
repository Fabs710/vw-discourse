Now, where I actually agree with you, and this matters: I did not open today by saying "closures, full stop, non-negotiable, discuss the rest." If you can put a credible, binding, quantified alternative package on the table that gets me to the same structural cost and capacity outcome — real capacity reduction, not deferred reduction; a genuine labour-cost contribution with numbers attached, not a symbolic gesture — then closures are not something I need for their own sake. I have no ideological attachment to shutting a plant. I have a mandate to fix a cost base, and I'm indifferent to the mechanism as long as the mechanism actually works and holds.

So here's my direct challenge back to both of you: you've said "attrition, voluntary programmes, working-time reduction, solidarity contribution" — those are categories, not a plan. Categories don't close a cost gap of the size we're carrying. Show me the arithmetic. How many jobs, over what timeframe, at what cost reduction per unit, with what enforceability if volumes keep falling? If that package, honestly scored, closes the structural gap and gets this brand back toward its return target on a durable basis — I will take closures off the table for those sites and put my credibility behind it at the board. If it's a partial measure dressed up as a full solution, I won't sign it, and I'll say so now rather than let you discover that in month six.

On executive pay and shareholder distributions: I'm not going to pretend that's irrelevant to the politics of getting a deal ratified, and I understand why you raise it. On binding guarantees to 2030: understand what you're asking me to sign. If we're going to talk about guarantees at that horizon, they need to be tied to conditions, not unconditional — reviewable against actual market and cost performance, not a blank check regardless of what happens to European demand.

So: bring me the plan. Numbers, not categories. I'm not moving off the need for a structural, durable fix — that's not posture, it's the job I have to do.
