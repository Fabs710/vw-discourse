I'll keep this shorter than the operational rounds, because my job here is governance, not running the working session — but two things happened this round that the board needs to hear clearly from me now, not after the fact.

**First, on the state government's clarification of its veto position.** I want to be precise about what was just said, because it matters more than it may have sounded in the moment. The state government has told this room that its threshold for ever supplying a closure vote is not "the package underperforms against a downside case" but something closer to sustained, multi-year, demonstrable failure after every intermediate lever has been tried. I understand the political logic behind that position, and I don't dispute the government's right to hold it — that's the arithmetic of the statute, and it was true before this conversation started.

But I want this on the record: if the practical effect of that position is that the review mechanism has no real consequence attached to it regardless of what the stress test shows — if the outcome is pre-decided independent of performance — then that is not a "reviewable guarantee." That is the unconditional guarantee I already said I will not support, wearing different language. I am not accusing the state government of bad faith; I'm naming a structural risk to the whole architecture we're building. A review mechanism that both sides know in advance carries no real weight on one side is not durable, it's asymmetric, and asymmetric arrangements are exactly the kind of thing that erodes enterprise value over time because they remove accountability from one party while leaving all the downside risk with the company and its shareholders. I would ask the state government to consider whether it can commit to good-faith evaluation of the stress-test results when they arrive, rather than stating the conclusion before the analysis exists. I recognize you may not be able to move far from your statutory role here — but say so plainly if that's the case, because it changes how I read the durability of whatever gets signed.

**Second, on the downside volume stress test — I support this fully, and I want to say why it matters to me specifically.** Management is right to insist the working-time model be tested against a shrinking-demand scenario, not just current volumes. This is precisely the kind of rigor that protects long-term value rather than a settlement that looks resolved today and unravels in eighteen months when the next downturn hits. I welcome the state government bringing independent economic analysis into that session — a second set of eyes on the assumptions is good governance, not obstruction, and I'd say the same if positions were reversed.

What I need from that session, concretely: an honest answer, not a negotiated one, about whether the alternative package closes the structural gap under a realistic downside case. If it does, I will support taking closures off the table for the affected sites and will say so to the full board myself — I meant that last round and I mean it now. If it doesn't hold up under stress, I expect this room to engage with that result on its merits rather than treat the session as procedural cover for a guarantee everyone had already decided to sign.

**On the numbers architecture from the Betriebsrat and the union** — this is real progress and I welcome it. Attrition, working-time reduction, jointly-funded voluntary severance, a scored wage contribution: that's a plan, not a category, for the first time in this discussion. I have no preference for closures over this if it works. I want to be equally clear I have no preference for this over closures if it doesn't.

**On top-of-house contribution** — proportionate and scored, as management committed. I won't obstruct it. I also won't pretend it substitutes for the structural fix, and I don't think anyone in this room is claiming it does anymore, which is progress.

**Where I stand going into the working session, unchanged from round one but sharpened by what I heard today:** I will back a quantified, stress-tested, durable alternative that closes the gap. I will not back an arrangement — under any label, "reviewable" or otherwise — where the outcome is fixed in advance regardless of whether the package performs, because that is not stability, it's a one-sided commitment with the family holding's name on the downside. Bring me the stress-test result. That, honestly evaluated by everyone in this room, is what should determine where this goes next — not positions restated more firmly than last round.
