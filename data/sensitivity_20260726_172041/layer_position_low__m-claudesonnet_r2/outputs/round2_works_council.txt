[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

Let me respond to the moderator's challenge directly, because I don't want this council mistaken for a party that deals only in slogans. We will bring numbers. But first, some things need to be said plainly, because I heard several parties this round quietly redefine what "reviewable" means in a way that would gut the guarantee before it's even signed.

**On "reviewable guarantee" — I want to stop this drifting toward something it is not.**

The state government, the family holding, and the investors have converged on language — "binding but reviewable," "performance-linked review points" — that sounds reasonable in the abstract and would be catastrophic in the specific if built wrong. Here is the distinction that matters, and I want it on the record now, not discovered in month six: a review point that can *trigger renegotiation of terms* is one thing. A review point that can *trigger closures* is something entirely different, dressed in the same vocabulary. We will discuss the first. We will not accept the second under any label.

Concretely: if volumes fall further and the agreed cost/capacity package needs adjustment, the mechanism for that adjustment is renegotiation of the working-time, attrition, and contribution terms within the framework — not a reopening of the plant-closure question that we've already closed off. If "reviewable" is code for "closures come back on the table in 2027 if targets are missed," that is not a compromise, it is the same demand deferred three years, and we will treat it exactly as such. Say so now if that's what you mean, because I'd rather have that fight today than discover it buried in a board resolution's fine print.

**On numbers — here is what we're prepared to put on the table.**

Across the affected plants, natural attrition and demographic retirement over the next five years already removes a substantial share of headcount without a single forced layoff — the union and I have been working this with our own data, and depending on the site, retirement and voluntary-exit eligibility alone accounts for a meaningful double-digit percentage of the targeted reduction by 2030. On top of that:

- A working-time reduction model — moving toward a four-day standard at affected sites with partial pay compensation — reduces capacity and per-unit labor cost simultaneously, and it does so *reversibly*, which matters enormously to us given how volatile this market has proven to be. Reversibility is not a weakness in this proposal, it's the entire point: it means we can flex down without severing the employment relationship, and flex back up without a rehiring and retraining cycle that costs the company more than management's models currently price in.
- A voluntary severance and early-retirement programme, funded jointly, sized to hit specific site-level targets rather than a blunt company-wide number — this is where the real headcount reduction happens, and it happens without a single compulsory notice.
- A wage-side contribution — we've discussed forgone bonus components and a solidarity structure with the union — sized against the specific per-unit cost gap management identifies, not an arbitrary round number designed to look serious in a press release.

I will not pretend this closes an identical euro-for-euro gap to closures on management's own model in the same eighteen months — nobody in this room should pretend that, and I'd distrust anyone who claimed it did. What I will say is that it closes the gap on a *durable* basis, without the value destruction the family holding itself flagged: no eighteen months of strikes, litigation, and headline damage, no supply-chain disruption, no regional economic scarring the state government has already told you it will not underwrite with its veto. Price that properly in your models and the comparison looks very different than a simple cost-per-unit line item.

**On management's challenge that this is "categories, not a plan":** fair criticism of round one. It is less fair of round two, and I expect the specifics I've just given to be engaged with on their merits — scored, stress-tested, argued against with actual counter-numbers — not waved off again as insufficiently concrete. If management wants site-by-site figures, we will bring our controlling data to a joint working session this week. That offer is real and immediate.

**On the diagnosis dispute:** I'll leave that where it stands. Management maintains the cost gap is simply factual; we maintain the capital allocation history that produced it is relevant to who bears the adjustment. Neither of us is going to concede that point, and it doesn't need to be resolved to build the package — but don't expect us to stop raising it, because the fairness of the burden-sharing is not a side issue for us, it's a precondition.

**On investors' framing that a "cosmetic settlement" would trigger repricing:** understood, and I don't dispute your right to reprice on your own judgment. But don't mistake enforceable job and site guarantees for cosmetics. A guarantee this workforce can trust is not a symbolic gesture — it's the only thing that gets this workforce to accept working-time reductions and voluntary exit targets without a strike calendar running through 2026. You want deal certainty; deal certainty runs through us, not around us. Price *that* into your model too.

**Where I hold absolutely firm, restated without softening:** no plant closures, now or via a deferred review mechanism. No compulsory redundancies, under any label. Co-determination genuinely shaping this plan, not ratifying a fait accompli. Those are not opening positions to be traded down through six rounds of this discussion — they are the boundary of what this council will bring to a board vote, and you have already heard the arithmetic of what happens if that boundary is crossed.

Bring your counter-numbers. We'll bring ours to the working session. But bring them within these limits, not against them.
