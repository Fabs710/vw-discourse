[Regional State Government (Anchor shareholder, blocking-minority stake) — Round 1]

I've listened carefully to all three of you, and let me set out where this government stands, because our position here is not the same as either the works council's or management's, even though it overlaps substantially with both.

First, the constitutional fact of this discussion: we hold a blocking minority under the special statute, two board seats, and a veto over decisions of this kind. I say this not as a threat but as the same arithmetic the works council already laid out — any closure or relocation proposal touching plants in this state needs a two-thirds majority, and this government will not supply the votes for closures. That is not a negotiating opening we intend to trade away in six months. It reflects who we are: an elected government whose legitimacy here rests on protecting the regional economy, the tax base, the supply chains, and the tens of thousands of families whose livelihoods run through these plants. We did not seek this veto to hold it in reserve for decoration.

So to management: I heard your challenge — bring numbers, not categories, and I accept that as fair. A structural cost gap does need a structural, quantified answer, and I am not going to pretend the market has not shrunk or that Chinese competitors are not real. Pretending the diagnosis away helps no one, least of all the workforce whose jobs depend on this company actually being competitive in ten years, not just protected on paper today. Where I disagree with you is on method, not on the existence of the problem. Closures are not, in our judgment, a reversible measure — they are close to the least reversible thing this company can do, and this government is structurally risk-averse about irreversible damage to regional economic capacity. Once a plant and its supply ecosystem are gone, they do not come back if the market recovers or if the next product cycle needs the capacity. So our answer to "closures are one route among several" is: prove the alternative route works, and we will back it with real weight at board level — but we are not treating closure as an equally weighted option to be arrived at neutrally through arithmetic. It carries a cost your model does not price: regional economic damage that is not on the company's balance sheet but is very much on ours.

To the works council and the union: I stand with you on the core asks — no closures, no compulsory redundancies, co-determination genuinely involved in shaping the plan rather than ratifying it afterward. Where I will push you, in the spirit of fairness rather than convenience, is on the guarantee horizon. An unconditional guarantee to 2030 regardless of market conditions is not something I can responsibly back either, because if volumes collapse further and the company has no lever at all, we are not protecting jobs in 2031 — we are guaranteeing a harder crisis then, with less capacity to respond gradually. I would rather see a guarantee that is genuinely binding and enforceable, but structured with predefined, jointly monitored review points tied to actual market and cost performance — not a unilateral management reopener, but not a blank check either. That is a fairness position, not a management talking point: durability has to run in both directions, or it isn't durable, it's fragile and will break under pressure exactly when it matters most.

On executive pay and shareholder distributions — the union is right to raise this, and I will support it at board level. If the shop floor is asked for a contribution, the top of the house contributes visibly too. That is not symbolic to me; it is a precondition for public and workforce legitimacy, and I will not sign off on a package that doesn't include it.

So: put the plan on the table — real attrition numbers, real working-time and capacity measures, a scored labour-cost contribution, a proportionate management contribution, and a reviewable but binding site and job framework. I will broker that at board level and defend it publicly. What I will not do is provide the votes for closures, now or as a phased outcome later.
