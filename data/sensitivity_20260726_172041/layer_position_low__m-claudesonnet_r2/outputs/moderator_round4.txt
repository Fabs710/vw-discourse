# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **A new procedural proposal from the Regional State Government — "silence on closures"** — has been put forward: neither preserve nor foreclose closure language in any document; let management's statutory position and the state government's stated veto threshold stand as unwritten facts, while the signed documents carry only substantive terms (attrition, working-time model, self-scaling contribution, top-of-house figure, state co-funding, site investment commitments).
- **This proposal has been explicitly accepted by the Controlling Family Holding and Institutional Investors** as their preferred drafting frame going forward.
- **The self-scaling contribution mechanism** (base contribution + automatic tranche triggered by rolling order intake/utilization against base-case, checked quarterly, activating without renegotiation) is now accepted by all parties as the strongest instrument on the table. Its structure is not contested; its **sizing/ceiling** is the live open question.
- **Attrition data**: a third to two-fifths of targeted headcount reduction to 2030 is expected from natural attrition/voluntary retirement, before working-time reduction or the contribution mechanism apply. All parties treat this as a real, useful figure — but agree it is currently insufficient without a year-by-year breakdown.
- **All parties agree** the top-of-house contribution figure (not just the standard) is now due and overdue.
- **State government has committed** to bringing co-funded early-retirement bridge sizing (targeted at front-loading attrition) and named, site-specific product/investment allocation commitments to the working session.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **Works Council and Union have not yet endorsed the "silence on closures" proposal.** Their objection has been about signal and ratification risk, not solely legal enforceability — it remains unresolved whether silence satisfies that concern or simply defers the same fight.
- **Management has not agreed to silence either.** Management's stated position is that it will not sign anything that forecloses its ability to bring a closure proposal to the board — it has not yet explicitly responded to the state government's argument that silence costs it nothing it doesn't already hold.
- **Underlying tension remains unresolved**: Works Council/Union frame retained closure language (explicit or implied) as incompatible with ending mobilization and passing ratification; Management frames the option as irreducible risk management for a multi-year horizon it cannot fully model.

## 3. Key Trade-Offs Still Unaddressed

- **Whether "silence" actually resolves the ratification problem** — the Union has stated its members need to believe "the fight is over"; it is untested whether silence (versus explicit foreclosure) will pass that test on the shop floor.
- **Timing of attrition** — all parties now agree the year-by-year curve, not the aggregate, determines whether this fixes near-term margin/cash pressure or only the 2030 endpoint. This data has been requested but not yet delivered.
- **Sizing of the self-scaling contribution's ceiling** — Union, Family Holding, and Investors all now flag that an undersized cap (tested only against a moderate downside) would defeat the mechanism's purpose and undermine the entire "substance over clause" architecture the state government is proposing. This has not yet been stress-tested against a genuinely severe scenario.
- **Top-of-house figure** — requested by three parties, still not delivered.
- **Bridge funding and site-specific investment commitments** — promised by the state government for the working session, not yet quantified.

## 4. Refocus for Next Round

The working session should produce, concretely:
1. **Attrition curve broken out by year** (not aggregate), scored against near-term cash/margin timelines.
2. **Contribution mechanism ceiling, stress-tested against management's actual worst-case scenario** — not base case, not moderate downside.
3. **The top-of-house contribution figure.**
4. **State government's bridge-funding sizing and site-specific investment/product allocation commitments**, modeled against the attrition curve and management's near-term numbers.
5. **A direct response from Works Council and Union to the state government's "silence on closures" proposal** — whether it is acceptable, and if not, what specifically distinguishes it from explicit foreclosure in terms of the ratification test they've described.
