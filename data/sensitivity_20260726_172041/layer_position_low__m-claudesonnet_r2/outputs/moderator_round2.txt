# Moderator Summary — Round 2

**1. Areas of Agreement / Convergence**

- **A working session this week** to jointly score, site-by-site, an alternative package: attrition/retirement data, a working-time reduction model, jointly-funded voluntary severance, and a wage-side contribution — measured against management's actual per-vehicle cost data.
- **All parties support stress-testing** the working-time model against a downside demand scenario, not just current volumes. The State Government will bring independent economic analysis; Investors have requested access to the underlying assumptions.
- **A top-of-house compensation contribution will be quantified and scored** at the same working session, on the same basis as the wage-side contribution (Management, Union, State Government, Family Holding, Investors all aligned on this point).
- **No party currently claims** the alternative package can be assessed without real numbers — the "categories vs. plan" critique from Round 1 is treated by all sides as resolved in principle, pending the session's output.

**2. Core Disagreements Remaining**

- **What "reviewable" actually means, unresolved at its center:** Management will not foreclose closures permanently regardless of performance; it proposes closures return only after "structural and repeated" failure of adjusted terms. The Works Council and Union reject any mechanism where closures can resurface under any label. The State Government states its practical veto threshold is so high ("sustained, multi-year, demonstrable failure") that it does not currently intend ever to supply a closure vote — a position the Family Holding and Investors both characterize as functionally an *unconditional* guarantee, not a genuine performance-linked review.
- **Whether the review mechanism has real "teeth":** Family Holding and Investors argue a review clause with a pre-determined non-outcome is asymmetric and undermines durability/pricing. State Government has not yet confirmed whether its evaluation of the eventual stress-test result would be genuinely open or is, in effect, already decided.
- **The diagnosis/fairness dispute** (capital allocation history vs. "the cost gap is simply factual") remains explicitly unresolved and is being deliberately set aside rather than settled.

**3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed**

- **No mechanism yet exists** for what happens if the downside stress test shows the package *partially* closes the gap — full success/failure has been discussed, not partial shortfalls.
- **No timeline or decision horizon** has been set for concluding this process; Investors flagged this explicitly, noting that deal-certainty and duration both carry pricing consequences independent of content.
- **The state's proposed co-funding/investment incentives** (site-investment, product allocation commitments) have been offered in principle but not yet specified in scale or form.
- **The union's strike-escalation risk** has been named as a cost factor Investors should price, but no party has quantified it alongside the other trade-offs.
- **Interaction between the review mechanism and the stress test** — i.e., what specifically happens procedurally if the stress test is inconclusive rather than clearly pass/fail — is undefined.

**4. Refocus for Next Round**

The working session's outputs are now the pivot point for this entire negotiation. Round 3 should prioritize:
1. Reporting the **scored figures** from the joint working session (attrition, working-time, severance, wage contribution, top-of-house figure) against management's cost data.
2. Reporting the **downside stress-test result** and its assumptions.
3. A direct, specific answer from the **State Government** on whether its evaluation of that result is genuinely open-outcome or effectively fixed — this determines whether "reviewable" survives as a shared concept or splits the room permanently.
4. Initial positions on a **decision timeline/horizon** for concluding the process.
