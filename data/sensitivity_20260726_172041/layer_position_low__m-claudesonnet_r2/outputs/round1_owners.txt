I've listened to all three positions, and I'll be brief, because that's consistent with how this holding operates — through the board, not through public sparring.

First, to state plainly what management already knows and what I expect this board to act on: the family holding backs the competitiveness objective without reservation. The cost gap management describes is real, it shows up in the numbers, and pretending otherwise to preserve social peace in the short term is precisely the kind of decision that erodes this company's value over a decade, not a quarter. I will not put the holding's weight behind a package that merely defers the structural problem. That is not a threat either — it's a statement of what I consider value-destroying, and value destruction is the thing I'm here to prevent.

Second, on method — closures versus alternative structural savings — I have no fixed ideological position, and frankly I don't need one. What I need is a plan that actually closes the cost and capacity gap on a durable basis, whatever form it takes. If the works council and the union can bring a quantified, binding alternative that gets the brand back toward its return target without the disruption, reputational cost, and multi-year uncertainty that plant closures in Germany would bring — including the very real risk that a closure fight consumes eighteen months in litigation, strikes, and headline damage before a single euro of savings materializes — then that is very likely the better outcome for long-term enterprise value, not merely the more comfortable one. Instability is expensive. Prolonged conflict with the workforce and the state government is expensive. I weigh that seriously.

But I want to be equally clear about what does not move me. An unconditional, unreviewable guarantee to 2030 is not something I will support, regardless of who is asking. The state government's formulation — binding, enforceable, but with jointly monitored review points tied to actual market and cost performance — is the only version of a durable guarantee I regard as credible. A blank check written today against a market nobody can forecast for six years is not stability, it's a deferred crisis with our name on it, and it would represent exactly the kind of governance failure I will not put the family holding's name behind.

On executive and shareholder contribution: proportionate, symbolic contribution from the top as part of a package is reasonable and I won't obstruct it. But let's not confuse it with substance — it's a legitimacy measure, not a structural one, and it should be sized and treated as such.

So my position to this board, when it comes to a vote: bring me a credible, quantified, durable plan — from either direction — and I will support it. Bring me instability without a plan, or a guarantee with no reopener, and I will not.
