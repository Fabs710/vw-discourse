We will not accept plant closures. Not one. Let me be direct about that from the outset, because I know how this game is played — someone puts a "closure" number on the table as an opening position so that the "compromise" a few months later looks reasonable to outside observers. That is not going to happen here. We remember the last time job security was treated as a negotiable variable, and we are not walking into that twice.

Let me be equally direct about what we will do. We have two-thirds voting requirements protecting us on this board for a reason. Any closure or relocation proposal requires a two-thirds supervisory board majority. We hold ten of twenty seats. Management can present whatever restructuring narrative it likes about European market shrinkage and Chinese competition — and yes, we accept the market is difficult — but it cannot close a single German plant without us, and we will not give that vote. That is not a threat, it is arithmetic.

Our priorities, in order:

First: every German plant stays open. No closures, now or as a staged "phase two" dressed up as something else.

Second: no compulsory redundancies. If there is workforce reduction, it happens through voluntary programmes, natural attrition, early retirement schemes — not through forced layoffs that hollow out communities that have built their lives around these sites.

Third: co-determination stays intact and meaningfully involved in shaping whatever plan emerges — not informed after the fact, not handed a fait accompli dressed as consultation.

Where we have genuine room to move: we are not blind to the cost problem, and we are not reflexively hostile to change. We can discuss capacity adjustments, working-time reductions, shift changes, even a negotiated employee contribution on pay — but only as part of a package that includes binding site and job guarantees running to 2030, not vague assurances management can walk back in eighteen months. We've been burned on exactly that point before.

What we will not do is negotiate under a closure threat as the starting condition. If management's opening position is "closures are on the table," our answer is warning strikes, mobilised across every site, and a public campaign that makes clear to shareholders, to the state government, and to the public exactly whose sacrifice is being demanded and whose is not — because I notice this restructuring conversation rarely starts with executive compensation or shareholder returns.

So: put alternative savings on the table — real numbers, real structural changes short of closure — and we will engage seriously and in good faith. Put closures or forced layoffs on the table as a precondition, and we escalate. That is where we stand.
