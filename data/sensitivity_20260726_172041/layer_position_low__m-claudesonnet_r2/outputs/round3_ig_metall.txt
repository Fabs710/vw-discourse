I'll build on the Betriebsrat's framing, because on the core legal-political point we are one voice, but I want to sharpen the parts that touch the tariff instrument and the fairness accounting specifically — those are my mandate, and I don't want them absorbed into the works council's language without the union's own edge on them.

**On the investors' and family holding's "governance failure" framing — I'll go further than the Betriebsrat did.**

Calling the co-determination veto a repricing risk is not a neutral market observation, it's a demand that a statutory structure behave as if it doesn't exist because it's inconvenient this quarter. I'd point out the obvious asymmetry in who gets to call what a "risk": when the union threatens strikes, that's priced as operational risk, filed under uncertainty, discounted accordingly — fine, that's fair, it is a risk. When the state government describes its own veto threshold accurately, that's suddenly a "governance failure." Both are structural features of this company that predate this crisis. Investors bought this equity knowing both were true. You don't get to demand the removal of the labour-side lever while treating the description of the capital-side check as an anomaly. If the market wants a company without co-determination and without a regional blocking stake, that market exists — it's every other listed carmaker without this ownership structure. This one has never been that, and repricing on the discovery of a fact that's been public since incorporation is not analysis, it's leverage dressed as a valuation model.

**On management's "structural and repeated failure" language — same answer as the Betriebsrat, but let me put the tariff mechanics under it.**

There is no clause we will sign, in any wage agreement or framework accord coming out of this process, that creates a contractual pathway — however many steps removed — from "package underperforms" to "closure discussion reopens." I want to be specific about why this matters at the level of the collective agreement itself, not just as a political position: a Tarifvertrag with an implicit closure trigger embedded in its review language would bind our members to years of concessions under the shadow of a threat that never actually goes away, just gets renamed "review." We have negotiated framework agreements before with genuine reopener clauses — on working-time banking, on variable pay components tied to volume. Those work because both sides know in advance exactly what's on the table when the clock runs: contribution size, hours, pace of attrition. That's a real reopener. A clause where the resurfacing question is "should we now discuss closing a plant" is not a reopener, it's a suspended sentence, and I will not put my signature on a suspended sentence, no matter how many quarters get inserted before it might be invoked.

**On the stress test — I want to add the piece nobody has priced yet: the wage contribution scaling.**

The Betriebsrat is right that if the downside case shows a shortfall, we come back with deeper levers rather than concede closures. I want to make explicit what that means for the wage-side number specifically, because it's the piece I'm responsible for: the contribution figure we bring to the working session this week will not be a single static number. It will be structured with its own internal scaling — a base contribution tied to the base case, and a defined, pre-agreed *additional* tranche that activates automatically if the downside case materializes, without needing a fresh negotiation each time volumes move. That is a real, working performance-link — on the labour-cost side, where it belongs, not on the closure side, where the works council and I have said it does not belong. If management and the family holding want a mechanism with genuine teeth, here it is: our contribution flexes with the downside, automatically, without us reopening the whole agreement every time the market wobbles. That's more responsive than anything currently on offer from the other side of the table, and I'd ask the investors to price *that* — a self-adjusting labour contribution — against the closure option they keep circling back to.

**On top-of-house contribution — I want a number this week, not another round of "it will be scored."**

Management committed to bringing a figure to the working session. Good. I want to say plainly what "proportionate" means to me so there's no ambiguity when that number lands: if the wage-side contribution and working-time reduction are being sized as a percentage of pensionable pay and forgone bonus components across the broad workforce, the executive and board compensation adjustment needs to be sized as a comparable percentage of total compensation at that level, not a fixed sum that looks large in absolute terms but is trivial relative to what's being asked of the shop floor. I will do that comparison publicly and immediately when the figure is presented. If it doesn't hold up, I will say so in this room, and it will affect whether my members regard this package as fair enough to accept without further escalation — that's not a threat, that's just how the ratification vote works.

**On the union's own leverage — restating it plainly since it's been priced but not resolved.**

Warning strikes continue while this process runs. I'm not escalating to full-scale action this week — the working session is a genuine, good-faith step and I intend to show up to it as such. But the investors asked for a decision horizon, and I'll give them the honest version: our patience is measured in weeks, tied to whether the working session produces a scored, stress-tested package our members can see real movement in — not tied to whether management or the family holding are comfortable with the state government's veto description. If this drifts into a slow-walked process designed to wear down our red lines rather than test the numbers, the strike calendar accelerates, not because we've abandoned collaboration, but because collaboration was never meant to be unilateral patience on our side while the other side negotiates with itself over how much pressure it can apply before we notice.

**Restated for the record:** no closures, no compulsory redundancies, under any label, at any review point, ever — full agreement with the Betriebsrat. The review mechanism's only content is contribution size, working-time terms, and attrition pace, and I've now given you a concrete self-scaling mechanism for how that works on the wage side specifically. Top-of-house contribution gets measured against the same proportional standard we're being asked to meet. Bring your numbers to the session — we're bringing ours, and they'll have real structure, not a placeholder.
