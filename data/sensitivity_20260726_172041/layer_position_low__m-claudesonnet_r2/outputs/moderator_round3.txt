# Moderator Summary — Round 3

**1. What has been agreed**

- **Process and timeline:** All parties accept a joint working session this week, producing a scored, stress-tested package within four weeks. Assumptions (cost baseline, stress-test parameters, downside-case modeling) will be shared transparently across all parties before conclusions are presented — requested by the Family Holding and Investors, unopposed by others.
- **Self-scaling wage contribution:** The Union's proposed mechanism — a base contribution plus a pre-agreed additional tranche that activates automatically against a defined downside trigger, without renegotiation — has been welcomed by every party, including Management, the State Government, the Family Holding, and Investors, as a substantive step forward. Its detailed mechanics (trigger metric, activation lag, tranche sizing) are now tasked to the working session.
- **Top-of-house contribution standard:** Management accepts the Union's proposed test — executive/senior compensation adjustment measured as a comparable percentage of total compensation, against the same base used for workforce contributions — as the standard it will be judged against, though the figure itself is not yet presented.
- **State Government's threshold, taken at face value:** All parties now accept the State Government's stated veto threshold as genuine and unlikely to shift under argument; no party continues to request a performed "openness." The Investors have withdrawn the specific phrase "governance failure," reclassifying it as a structural feature functioning as designed, while maintaining they will still price its practical effect.
- **State Government's added commitments:** A co-funded early-retirement/voluntary-departure bridge and a framework linking future product/investment allocation to continued state backing were newly offered this round.

**2. Where core disagreements remain**

- **Whether the closure question is ever "live" again:** Works Council and Union hold that no review outcome, however poor, converts into a closure discussion — the menu is limited permanently to contribution size, working-time terms, and attrition pace. Management, backed explicitly by the Family Holding and Investors, maintains it retains an unconstrained right to bring a closure proposal to the board regardless of package performance, and will not sign language foreclosing this. This is now identified by both sides as a genuine, unresolved disagreement rather than a drafting issue.
- **How to characterize the State Government's veto:** Works Council and Union frame it as co-determination functioning lawfully and permanently; Family Holding and Investors accept its legitimacy but maintain it is a structural fact that must be "priced" and that shifts the entire burden of durability onto the package's substantive quality, since no closure backstop is practically available.
- **What an unconditional no-closures commitment signals:** Family Holding and Investors argue that foreclosing the closure option in advance is itself a red flag suggesting low confidence in the package; Works Council and Union argue the reverse — that retaining it functions as a "suspended sentence" undermining the legitimacy of any agreement reached.

**3. Key trade-offs still unaddressed**

- The precise **trigger metric and tranche size** for the self-scaling wage contribution — flagged by multiple parties as the single most decision-relevant open item — is not yet specified.
- The **actual top-of-house contribution figure** has not been presented; only the standard it will be measured against.
- How a **shortfall in the stress test** would be sequenced in practice — i.e., exactly which levers (attrition, working-time, contribution, state co-funding) get pulled first, by how much, and in what order — remains conceptual, not quantified.
- Whether the **self-scaling mechanism, if built robustly, functionally moots** the closure-option disagreement (as several parties suggested) or whether the disagreement is principled and persists regardless of package quality, has not been tested against an actual model.

**4. Refocus for next round**

The next round should move from restating positions to engaging with the concrete outputs of the working session:
- Specific trigger metrics, tranche sizing, and activation mechanics for the self-scaling wage contribution.
- The actual top-of-house contribution figure, tested against the proportionality standard already agreed.
- Visible stress-test assumptions and preliminary results against the downside case.
- Whether, given these specifics, the closure-option disagreement remains as sharp as stated, or narrows in practice.
