# Moderator Summary — End of Round 1

## 1. Areas of Convergence
All five parties have expressed openness to a **plan-based approach rather than a fixed ideological position** on closures. Specifically:
- Management, the Family Holding, and Institutional Investors state closures are **not a precondition** but one route to a structural fix — they will accept a credible alternative if it closes the cost/capacity gap on a durable basis.
- The Works Council and Industrial Union have offered concrete **categories** of alternative measures: attrition, voluntary programmes, working-time reduction, and a negotiated labour-cost/solidarity contribution.
- All parties accept some form of **top-of-house contribution** (executive pay, shareholder distributions) as a legitimacy component of any package, though there is disagreement on its weight.
- There is broad (though not universal) convergence around the *principle* of a **binding but reviewable** guarantee framework, rather than either a pure blank-check or a fully open-ended management reopener — though the State Government, Family Holding, and Investors favor conditionality; the Works Council and Union have not yet accepted this.

## 2. Core Disagreements Still Open
- **Closures as an option at all**: Works Council and Union maintain an absolute rejection, backed by a 10/20 board bloc plus the State Government's veto — a combined blocking position. Management, Family Holding, and Investors treat closures as conditionally removable, not categorically excluded.
- **Guarantee horizon and enforceability**: Works Council/Union want an unconditional binding guarantee to 2030. Management, State Government, Family Holding, and Investors all reject an "unconditional" guarantee and want performance-linked review points instead. This is currently the sharpest unresolved gap.
- **Diagnosis framing**: Works Council/Union frame the cost crisis as partly a product of prior management/capital decisions; Management maintains the cost-gap diagnosis is factual and not up for reframing.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Not Yet Addressed
- **No quantified alternative package exists yet.** Management and Investors have explicitly demanded numbers — job counts, timelines, cost-per-unit impact, enforceability mechanics — and none have been produced.
- **What "review points" would actually trigger** — no party has specified what metrics, thresholds, or governance process would activate a reopener, nor how joint monitoring would work in practice.
- **Scale and form of top-of-house contribution** — flagged by multiple parties as symbolic/legitimacy-only, but no figures or mechanisms proposed.
- **Sequencing and timeline** — how quickly an alternative package must be scored and validated before management/board patience (and investor tolerance) runs out is undefined.

## 4. Refocus for Round 2
The discussion has established broad conditional willingness to negotiate a non-closure path. The next round should move from **positions to specifics**:
1. Works Council and Union: bring a quantified alternative — job/attrition numbers, timeline, cost-per-unit savings, and a proposed labour-cost contribution figure.
2. Management: specify the precise size of the structural gap being targeted, so the alternative package can be scored against it.
3. All parties: begin defining what a "reviewable guarantee" would actually look like — proposed metrics, review intervals, and governance of the review process.
