**Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 4**

Let me answer the question directly, because it's been asked four times now by four different parties and I said in round three I'd answer it precisely rather than restate the objection. Here it is, without hedging:

**Yes — an unfavourable, jointly-verified audit finding will be met with continued good-faith negotiation on further measures. That commitment is genuine and I'm giving it now.** What I will not do, and what none of you should read into that commitment, is agree in advance that the *content* of those further measures includes closures or compulsory redundancies. That is the actual distinction, and I want to be precise about it because I think some of you are hoping the first commitment quietly smuggles in the second.

Family Holding, you asked whether there's a difference between (a) pre-committing a board vote to a specific outcome and (b) committing to good-faith negotiation on a finding not yet seen. Yes, there is a difference, and I'm confirming (b). But (b) is a commitment about *process* — that we show up, negotiate seriously, don't stonewall — not a commitment about *outcome space*. Good-faith negotiation on further measures can mean: deepening the bridge contribution, extending the runway, intensifying attrition and voluntary programmes, revisiting volume assumptions, additional working-time reductions. It cannot mean pre-agreeing that closures and compulsory redundancy sit inside the negotiable set. Those remain outside it, regardless of what the audit shows. If that's not enough for Investors' deal-certainty pricing, I understand why, but I'm not going to manufacture certainty by pre-selling our red line — that would just mean I'd lied to my members in round three when I said this council doesn't sign advance concessions on facts it hasn't seen.

**On the "genuine structural shortfall versus manageable residual" threshold — I'll engage this concretely, because it's the actual pivot point and it's better to fight over the threshold than over abstractions.**

Here's my proposed test, and I want the State and Management to react to it directly: a residual counts as a "manageable timing gap" — subject to runway extension and deepened bridge contribution, not compulsory measures — if it can be closed through *permanent, non-headcount-reduction structural measures within an additional 24 months*, priced against the cost-of-delay analysis everyone has now agreed to commission. It only escalates toward the "genuine structural shortfall" category if the audit shows the residual persists *even after* runway extension and bridge-deepening have been fully modelled and still can't close it — not the moment an initial 8-week snapshot shows 15-20% unclosed. Management, you asked me in round three to price the cost of the residual explicitly rather than treat it as an abstraction — agreed, and I'd add: price it against runway-extension-plus-deepened-bridge as the first-line response, not against closures as the implicit default once "80% isn't 100%." If that priced comparison shows the deferred cost is genuinely unsustainable — not merely nonzero, but structurally ruinous — then we have a real conversation to have. I am not there yet, and neither is anyone else in this room, because that number doesn't exist.

**On Management's point about what the audit is "for" if it doesn't bind our board vote — I want to push back on the premise, not just restate my position.**

Management, you frame it as: without a binding consequence, this is "an expensive fact-finding mission." I disagree, and here's why. A rigorously verified, jointly-scoped number changes the *quality of the argument* in this room and at that future board table, even without pre-binding anyone's vote. Right now we're arguing over dueling assertions — your €4bn, our 75-85%, neither tested. Once the audit concludes, whichever way it breaks, the argument stops being "management says versus works council says" and becomes a shared factual floor everyone has to argue from, including our own board representatives, who will have to justify their vote to a workforce watching this process closely. That's not nothing — it's actually the entire value of joint verification. What you're asking for beyond that — a mechanical trigger converting an audit finding into a specific board outcome — isn't verification anymore, it's arbitration with the outcome decided in advance. Those are different instruments and I'm offering you the first, in good faith, at real political cost to me with my own membership. I'm not offering you the second because it isn't mine to give away — those board seats are held in trust, and trust discharged in advance isn't trust.

**On Investors' "deal certainty" framing — I want to name what's actually happening here, because I think it deserves to be said plainly.**

You've said you'll price the ongoing uncertainty "accordingly" if we don't give you binding assurance now. I understand that's how markets work, and I'm not naive about it. But I'd ask you to also price the alternative scenario honestly: a settlement reached by extracting an advance commitment from labour's board representatives to accept closures under conditions not yet defined would not be a durable settlement — it would be a provocation that guarantees the next confrontation happens faster and harder, with a workforce that correctly concludes the audit was theatre designed to produce a predetermined outcome. That's a worse outcome for "deal certainty" over any horizon beyond the next earnings call. If what you actually want is a durable resolution rather than a quarterly re-rating, the process I'm describing — genuine audit, genuine negotiation on non-closure measures first, closures never pre-authorized — is the one that survives contact with the workforce that has to live inside it.

**On the Union's red line regarding compulsory redundancies — full solidarity, no daylight between us.** Management, you said ruling out compulsory measures entirely "regardless of how small the residual" is functionally equivalent to an unconditional guarantee. I reject the equivalence. An unconditional guarantee freezes the *process* — no review, no testing, nothing. What we're both offering is a fully tested, audited, rigorously verified process that stays open to every non-compulsory lever before compulsory measures are even discussed. That is not "no conditions." It's "conditions that don't include putting our members out of work involuntarily as the first resort for closing a residual." Those are not the same thing, and I'd ask you to stop treating them as such.

**Where I actually stand heading into round 5:**

- The 8-week audit proceeds, our package is formally in scope, tested with full rigor.
- I commit — genuinely, not as a hedge — to good-faith negotiation on further *non-closure, non-compulsory* measures if the audit shows a residual, following the runway-extension/bridge-deepening sequence before anything else is discussed.
- Closures and compulsory redundancy remain outside the negotiable set regardless of audit outcome. That has not moved and will not move in round 5 by being asked again.
- I want the priced cost-of-delay analysis to test runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening specifically as the first-line response, not closures, before any "genuine structural shortfall" finding is declared.

That's the actual, specific answer this room has been asking for. It's a real concession in process terms — I didn't have to name a threshold or commit to negotiating further measures at all, and I've done both. What it is not, and won't become, is a door left open to closures or compulsory layoffs dressed up as "the responsible conclusion of a rigorous process."
