**Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 3**

Let me address the "shared verification, shared decision" convergence head-on, because the State, the Family Holding, Management, and Investors have now all lined up behind it as if it resolves the disagreement. It doesn't, and I want to explain precisely why, using a fact that seems to have gone unmentioned this entire round despite being the single most important structural feature of this whole negotiation.

**The two-thirds board threshold already governs any closure decision. Nothing discussed this round changes that.**

Every version of the "return to the table" mechanism you've all described — audit concludes, parties reconvene, joint decision follows — still terminates, if it ever gets to closures, in a supervisory board vote requiring two-thirds. Labour holds half the seats. No verification process, however jointly scoped, however independently audited, can manufacture the four additional labour votes management would need to actually close a plant. That arithmetic doesn't change whether the milestone is hit or missed, whether the audit is friendly or hostile, whether "come back to the table" means genuine co-decision or a management proposal dressed up as consultation. The board vote is sovereign, and it requires our consent, full stop.

I raise this not to be cute about procedure, but because it clarifies what's actually being asked of us. If the board vote already protects the red line regardless of what any review concludes, then what management, the State, and the Family Holding are really asking for is not additional *power* to close plants later — they can't get that power through this mechanism no matter how it's worded. What they're asking for is our **advance moral and political concession** — an agreement, now, that we won't use that veto if a jointly-verified process shows the alternative failed. That is a different thing from "the guarantee isn't real," and it's a different thing from "closures get automatically reopened." It is a request that we pre-commit our judgment to a future process we haven't seen the output of yet.

I can engage with that request. I cannot grant it in advance, unconditionally, today. So here is where I actually land, and I want it to be precise rather than another round of restating the same objection:

**We will participate fully and in good faith in the joint modelling exercise, we will not pre-judge its outcome, and we commit that our board representatives will engage with its findings seriously rather than reflexively.** What we will not do is sign, today, a document that says "if the audit concludes X, the works council's board representatives will vote for closures." Our board seats are held in trust for the workforce, and that trust cannot be discharged by a pre-signed voting instruction. Our judgment at that future point — informed by the audit, informed by what's actually on the table as an alternative at that time — remains ours to exercise. That is not evasion; that is what co-determination means. If the Family Holding and the State are genuinely committed to "shared decision-making, not management fait accompli," as both of you said this round, then you should recognise this is the same principle applied consistently: nobody's board vote gets pre-committed by a document signed before the facts are in, including ours.

**Now, the partial-success scenario the moderator flagged — this is the real question, and I'll answer it directly since nobody else has.**

Suppose the audit concludes our package — attrition, working-time reduction, capacity consolidation, the Union's defined-duration contribution — closes, say, 75-85% of the €4 billion. Investors have already told us their answer: that's not enough, closures are "the responsible path," and they'll say so publicly regardless of the industrial cost. I want to respond to that plainly: a package that closes 80% of a cost gap identified by management's own model, delivered through permanent structural measures on capacity and attrition plus a time-limited bridge, is not a failure. It's a negotiated outcome that any competent restructuring should be able to work with — through a longer horizon, not through closures. If the remaining 15-20% is a timing gap rather than a structural impossibility, the answer is to extend the runway — 2028, 2029 rather than 2027 — not to convert a near-miss into a plant closure. Management itself said this round that closures account for "roughly half" of the €4 billion target. If our package alone gets to 80% through non-closure measures, that is not "a third of the way there" — Management's own illustrative worst case — and I want it on record that I will not accept that comparison being applied to a materially better outcome than the one management floated as its threshold for rejection.

**On the quantified figure:** I'm not going to invent a precise number in this room that hasn't been through the joint audit — doing so would be exactly the unverified-number problem the Investors rightly warned about when applied to management's figure, and I won't hold ourselves to a lower standard than we're holding management to. What I will commit to, concretely: our package is designed, on our own internal modelling, to close the substantial majority of the gap — not a third, not half, but the greater part of it — through permanent capacity and attrition measures plus the Union's bridging contribution. That is what goes into the joint audit for testing. If the audit shows we're wrong and the gap is genuinely far wider than we believe, I will deal with that fact when it's actually verified, not before.

**On owner-side sacrifice — I want to flag something in the Investors' position that concerns me directly, because it has an effect on incentives that nobody else has named.**

Investors, you've proposed that the owner-side sacrifice be sized to the *verified gap-closure outcome* rather than to the workforce contribution. Think about what that actually does: if our alternative package underperforms and more of the €4 billion ends up being closed through closures and redundancies instead, your framework implies the owners contribute *less*, because less "shared sacrifice" is needed when the workforce is instead absorbing the cost through job losses. That inverts the entire principle of shared sacrifice into its opposite — the worse the outcome for workers, the smaller the owner contribution. I will not accept that logic. The owner-side sacrifice needs to be sized to the **total cost being extracted from the enterprise's stakeholders**, and if more of that cost falls on workers through closures, the owner contribution should not shrink — if anything, the case for it grows. I'd ask the Family Holding directly to confirm that "proportionate" in your commitment does not mean "proportionate to how little we end up paying because workers paid more instead."

**To the Family Holding, on "unconditional guarantee equals permanent impairment":** I want to push back on the framing, not just the conclusion. A site and job guarantee through 2030 is not eternal — it has an end date, the same 2030 horizon everyone in this room has been using as the planning frame. Agreeing to hold the footprint and forgo compulsory redundancies for that period, while the alternative package is implemented and tested through the joint audit, is not "ignoring whether the economics hold." It's giving the alternative the time genuinely needed to prove itself before anyone's job or plant is put back on the table. What you're calling "unconditional" is, in fact, time-bound and tied to the very process you say you support. What I'm rejecting is a structure where the guarantee can be pierced *inside* that window the moment an interim milestone wobbles. Those are different asks, and I'd ask you to engage with the one I'm actually making.

To be unambiguous heading into round 4: I will negotiate hard, in good faith, on the shape of the audit, the modelling, the timeline, and the owner-sacrifice mechanism. I will not sign away, in advance, our board vote's discretion, and I will not accept that a package closing the substantial majority of the gap through non-closure measures gets treated as a failure that reopens plant closures. Those remain the terms on which this council can deliver a settlement its members will actually accept.
