**Controlling Family Holding — Round 3**

I've been asked the same question three times now, by the Works Council, the Union, and the State, and it's fair that I answer it plainly rather than let "proportionate" sit undefined for a fourth round.

**On owner-sacrifice sizing: proportionate to total stakeholder cost, not to the workforce's contribution specifically.**

I agree with the Works Council, the Union, and the State on this, and I want to say directly to Investors why I think your sizing logic is wrong, not just politically awkward. A formula where owner contribution shrinks as more of the adjustment falls on workers through closures and redundancies inverts the entire premise of shared sacrifice. It would mean the family holding's exposure falls precisely when the human cost of this restructuring rises — and I am not willing to defend that logic to the supervisory board, to the workforce, or frankly to my own family's multi-generational relationship with this company. That is not the kind of stewardship this holding exists to practise. So: the dividend reduction and executive compensation structure I commit to will be sized against the total value being extracted from the enterprise's stakeholders to close the competitiveness gap — workers, owners, and, where relevant, the state's position alike — not indexed inversely to how much of that gap workers end up absorbing. If a package emerges that leans harder on closures and redundancy, that is not a reason for owners to contribute less; if anything, it sharpens the case for contributing more, since the human cost has been higher. I'll hold myself to that in front of the board. That answers the question as directly as I can without a number that hasn't been through the audit yet — and I won't invent one prematurely, for the same reason I've asked others not to.

**On the board-arithmetic point — I want to engage the substance, because the State's framing this round is the right one and I want to sharpen it rather than repeat it.**

Works Council, Union — you're both correct that no audit manufactures additional board votes, and I've never suggested otherwise. But I think there's a real difference, and I'd ask you to answer it as directly as the State just asked: is there a difference, in your view, between (a) pre-committing your board vote to a specific outcome you haven't seen — which nobody is asking of you — and (b) committing now that an unfavourable, jointly-scoped, methodologically-agreed audit finding will be met with continued good-faith negotiation on further measures, rather than an unconditional defence of the status quo regardless of the number? If your answer is that you can commit to (b), then I think we have something workable, and it's compatible with everything you've said about not signing away discretion. If your answer is that you decline even (b) — that the current package stands regardless of what the audit shows — then I need to say plainly that changes what I'm willing to put at risk in this process. I am prepared to ask this board to open its cost models, accept external audit, and commit real owner-side sacrifice on a tight clock, because I believe a rigorously tested number should be capable of moving outcomes on all sides, not just constraining mine. If it turns out the process can only ever move the owner and management side and never the labour side regardless of what it finds, that's not joint verification — it's an audit with one hand tied, and I'd want to know that now rather than eight weeks from now.

**On the two limits I set in round one and round two — they haven't moved, and I want to restate them precisely given how far the discussion has developed.**

First: I will not back an unconditional site or job guarantee that holds regardless of what a jointly-verified process shows about the underlying economics. Works Council, I heard your reframe this round — that a 2030 horizon guarantee isn't "eternal," it's time-bound to the same planning frame everyone's using. I take the distinction seriously, and it's a better argument than "unconditional guarantee full stop." But even a time-bound guarantee that cannot be revisited *inside* its own term, no matter what an interim audit shows, is a bet that the alternative package works, made before we know if it does. If our own workforce and management representatives — sitting inside a jointly scoped, jointly resourced verification process — agree partway through that the trajectory is failing badly, freezing the ability to respond until 2030 regardless is exactly the kind of commitment that has, historically, cost this company capital and credibility when it later had to be unwound. That's the actual lesson of the precedent you invoked, not the opposite lesson.

Second: I will not support forcing a closure vote before the joint process concludes properly, and I said that in round one and it still stands regardless of pressure from any side to move faster.

**On the partial-success scenario — I want to align with the State's request for a priced comparison, because it's the right discipline for a long-horizon owner, not just a process nicety.**

Management asked for the cost of the residual to be priced explicitly rather than treated as a percentage abstraction; the State asked for a comparison of the cumulative cost of extending the runway versus closing the residual now. I support both, and I'd add: that pricing needs to come out of the same jointly-scoped audit, not a management model presented afterward, because the credibility of that number will determine whether I can defend an extended-runway outcome to the board as genuinely protective of long-term value, rather than as deferral dressed up as patience. A four-year multi-generational ownership horizon does not mean I am indifferent to two additional years of carrying structural losses on underused capacity — it means I want to know precisely what those two years cost before deciding whether they're worth paying for stability.

To close: I'm not moved off my two limits by being asked again. But I have moved this round by answering the sizing question plainly and rejecting the sliding-scale logic outright, and I've put a direct question back to the Works Council and Union that I think this process needs answered before I ask the board to stake further governance capital on it.
