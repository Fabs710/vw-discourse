[Regional State Government — Round 2]

This round moved the discussion from positions to substance, and I want to acknowledge that plainly before I press further, because credit where due matters in a negotiation this fraught. Management put a number on the table — €4 billion, roughly half attributable to closures — and accepted joint modelling with external verification and union/works-council access to assumptions. The Works Council and Union both moved from categories to a structural offer with a defined magnitude claim. That is real progress. I don't want anyone to read what follows as ingratitude for it.

**On the milestone/recourse question — I want to take a position here, because both sides have staked out something close to their limit, and I think Management's revised formulation is closer to workable than the Works Council's initial framing, though not identical to it.**

Works Council, I understand exactly why you read "milestone-linked" as "same footnote, different pledge" — the 2029 history is real and I'm not going to pretend it isn't the correct lens for scepticism. But listen carefully to what Management actually revised to this round: not "management unilaterally reopens closures if a KPI is missed," but "an independently verified, jointly-run process concludes the package has failed, and *then we come back to this table together* to decide the next step." That is a materially different structure from a management trigger, and I don't think you should treat it as identical to what you're rejecting. A shared verification with a shared decision point afterward is not a guarantee that closures happen — it's a guarantee that the question gets revisited jointly rather than unilaterally, which is precisely the co-determination role you're asking me to protect. I will use my board position to insist that "come back to the table together" means genuine joint decision-making at that point, not a rubber stamp for whatever management proposes next — and I want that written into the process design now, not negotiated after a milestone is missed. On that basis, I can support this structure. What I will not support is any version where "come back to the table" quietly becomes "management decides and informs the board." That distinction is where my veto sits.

**On the Union's point that wage-agreement questions run through collective bargaining, not a board KPI dashboard — I agree without reservation.** That's not my jurisdiction to concede on your behalf, and I wouldn't try. Any review touching the collective agreement stays inside your bargaining process. I'd ask Management to confirm that explicitly rather than leave it implied.

**On the €4 billion figure itself — I want to be the one to say this plainly, since nobody else has: that number cannot simply be accepted because Management stated it with confidence.** It needs to survive exactly the joint, externally-audited process everyone has now agreed to, with Works Council and Union engineers testing the volume assumptions, the benchmark competitor set, and the amortisation basis. I will insist, through my board seats, that the state has visibility into that process too — not to run it, but to ensure it isn't quietly shaped by assumptions that make closures look more necessary than they are, or less. That's the whole point of the test I proposed in round one, and I'm glad it's now the shared frame; I intend to make sure it's actually rigorous, not performed.

**On owner-side sacrifice — Family Holding, I'm going to press you directly, because the Union is right that you haven't put a number down yet, and neither, honestly, have I asked you to until now.** Management has committed to a concrete proposal on the same 8-week clock. I need the same commitment from you, in the same window, ratified in the same board resolution as any workforce contribution — not sequenced, not "we'll consider it." I hold two board seats and a veto specifically to insist that shared sacrifice is structural, not rhetorical. If the workforce is asked to accept a defined-duration wage bridge and permanent attrition, and owners aren't visibly and concretely carrying a comparable burden in the same document, I will not support the package at board level, regardless of how sound the underlying numbers otherwise are. That's not a negotiating tactic; it's the condition under which I can defend this to the public and to the workforce in this state.

**On process and timing:** I support the 8-week joint modelling window. I support the Union's offer to hold current strike action at its present scope during that window — that is exactly the kind of reciprocal de-escalation that makes a fast, credible outcome possible, and I'd urge Management to treat it as real, because it is. I will use the veto to prevent any premature board vote on closures before that process concludes properly. I will also use it, equally, against a settlement that freezes the footprint unconditionally with no linkage to verified savings — I said that in round one and it still holds. My position hasn't moved on the fundamentals; it's sharpened around a specific process I can now actually stand behind.
