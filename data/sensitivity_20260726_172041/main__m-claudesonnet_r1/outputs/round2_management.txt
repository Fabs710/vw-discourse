[Group & Brand Management — Round 2]

I want to engage the substance directly, because both the Works Council and the Union have made real moves this round — categories of savings, a defined-duration contribution concept, a joint-modelling proposal — and I'd rather test those seriously than restate positions. But I also need to correct a framing that's crept into this room and push back hard on one specific point, because if I let it stand unchallenged we'll build a structure I cannot actually sign.

**On "milestone review can never lead back to closure" — I cannot accept that, and I want to explain precisely why, not just assert it.**

Works Council, you called a milestone-linked guarantee "the same offer with a different footnote" as 2029. I understand the history that makes you read it that way, but I think you've mischaracterized what I'm actually proposing, and the difference matters. I am not asking you to trust management's discretion to reopen closures whenever it likes. I am saying: if we jointly agree an alternative package is supposed to deliver a defined structural saving by a defined date, and an independently verified process shows it has not delivered — not "missed a soft KPI," but genuinely failed to close the competitiveness gap — then the company cannot have pre-committed, in a board-ratified document, to keep operating an uncompetitive footprint regardless. That's not me hedging my bets. That's the actual fiduciary and commercial reality: I cannot sign a document that removes the only remaining lever if the alternative doesn't work, because at that point I've guaranteed the outcome I was mandated to prevent. That is the one line in this entire negotiation I will not move on, however it's framed, however many rounds we spend on it. A joint, transparent, co-owned process to design and monitor the alternative — yes, fully. A permanent renunciation of recourse if that alternative fails — no.

What I *can* offer, and this is a real concession: the trigger doesn't have to be management-controlled. Let's build the verification into the joint modelling structure you're both asking for — independent auditor, agreed methodology, works council and union representatives with real access to the assumptions, not a number handed down and rubber-stamped. If that jointly-run process concludes the package is working, the guarantee holds, full stop, no relitigating. If it concludes it isn't, we come back to this table — together — to decide the next step, which could include closures but isn't a foregone management decision either. That is different in kind from what you're calling a "deferred sentence." It's a shared verification with shared consequences, not a unilateral reopening clause.

**On numbers — I appreciate that both of you moved here, but I need to press further, because categories aren't a figure.**

Let me put our number on the table, since three parties have now asked me to be concrete and it's fair that I go first with the benchmark that anchors everything else: our analysis puts the structural cost-per-vehicle disadvantage at roughly 15–20% against our most efficient European competitor footprint, which translates into a required annual structural saving on the order of €4 billion by 2027 across the German operations under discussion. Closures and associated headcount reduction, as currently modelled, account for roughly half of that; the remainder could in principle come from productivity, capacity utilisation, and cost measures regardless of the closure question. That's the gap. When I say your offer — attrition, working-time reduction, line consolidation, a defined-duration contribution — needs to be "the same order of magnitude," this is the magnitude I mean. If your joint modelling gets us within a credible band of that €4 billion, on a timeline that doesn't just push the shortfall into 2028 and 2029, I will treat it as a genuine alternative to closures. If it gets us to a third of the way there, it isn't, and I'll say so plainly rather than let ambiguity carry us into a deal that unravels in two years.

I'll also say: I hear the challenge on assumptions — Union, you're right that a target built entirely on our volume and benchmark assumptions with no ability for you to interrogate it isn't a comparison, it's a rubber stamp. Fine. Bring your industrial engineers and actuaries in, test the assumptions, challenge the benchmark set. I'm not afraid of that scrutiny — the numbers are real. But I need this to run on a tight clock, not an open one. I'd propose 8 weeks for the joint modelling exercise, external auditor appointed jointly, output binding on both sides as the basis for the next decision. Not indefinite, because every week of this also costs us the market and cost-of-capital pressure the Investors flagged, and that's real too.

**On the time-limited contribution — Union, I understand your members' concern about a permanent contract change ratified under duress, and I don't need it to be permanent everywhere. But I need to separate two things.** Attrition, working-time restructuring, and capacity consolidation need to be *structural and lasting* — those are the pieces that actually retire cost permanently, and I won't accept a version where the headcount or capacity comes back once the pressure is off. A time-limited wage or bonus contribution can bridge us while those structural measures take effect — that's a legitimate use of a defined-duration instrument, and I can work with it on that basis. What I can't accept is a package where the *entire* saving is time-limited, because then in three years we're back to the same cost base with none of the underlying capacity problem solved. So: structural and permanent on capacity and headcount, time-limited acceptable specifically on the wage/bonus bridge component. That's a distinction I need built into whatever you bring back.

**On sequencing and owner-side sacrifice — you're both right that "we'll raise it with the board" isn't a number, and I won't hide behind that any longer than this round.** I don't control dividend policy or the family holding's compensation decisions unilaterally — that's a genuine governance fact, not an excuse — but I will commit to this: I will bring a specific proposal, not a vague gesture, to the board within the same 8-week window as the joint modelling exercise, and I will support it being ratified in the same board resolution as any workforce contribution, not sequenced afterward. I'd ask the Family Holding directly, since you're in this room: can you commit to a concrete dividend and executive-compensation figure on the same timeline? Vague solidarity from your side undermines my ability to close this as much as it undermines the Union's ability to sell it to members.

**On "scale of the target is not negotiable" — let me refine, not retreat.** The competitiveness gap itself — the €4 billion, the 15-20% — is not a negotiating position; it's the market and the balance sheet, and I won't pretend it's smaller than it is to make this conversation easier. What is genuinely open, and I said this last round and mean it, is *how* that gap gets closed — the mix between closures, capacity measures, attrition, and cost contribution. That's not a distinction without a difference. It's the entire question this negotiation is about.

To close: I am moving further than round one. I've put a number on the table, I've accepted joint modelling with real co-determination and external verification, I've accepted a bridging role for time-limited contributions, and I've committed to a concrete owner-sacrifice proposal on the same clock. What I have not moved on, and will not move on, is holding open genuine recourse if the alternative package is verified to fail — and I won't accept a target softened below the actual cost gap to make agreement easier today at the price of being back here, weaker, in 2027.
