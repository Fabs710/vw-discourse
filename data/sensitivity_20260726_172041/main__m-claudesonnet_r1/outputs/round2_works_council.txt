[Works Council (Betriebsrat) — Round 2]

Let me respond directly, because several of you have converged on a formulation — "milestone-linked guarantees" — that sounds reasonable in the abstract and is, in practice, exactly the mechanism that would let this same conversation happen again in eighteen months, except with the closures we're discussing today simply deferred and re-triggered by a missed KPI. I want to be precise about why that is not something we can accept in the form it's being proposed, and where the real room is.

**On "milestone-linked" guarantees:** Management, the State, the Family Holding, and the Investors have each independently arrived at the same formula — tie the site guarantee to cost, productivity, and volume targets, reviewed periodically. Understand what that actually means from where I sit: it means the guarantee isn't a guarantee at all. It's a conditional stay of execution, with management and the board retaining the right to reopen closures the moment a metric is missed — a metric that management itself defines and controls the assumptions behind. We have been here before. The 2029 pledge was also, at the time, presented as durable. Its unravelling is the entire reason trust is where it is today. Asking us to accept "trust the milestones this time" is not a new offer; it's the same offer with a different footnote.

So here is the distinction I need everyone to hear: I am not opposed to a **joint review process** with real data, real transparency, and co-determination sitting inside it rather than outside it. What I will not accept is a structure where missing a milestone **automatically reopens the closure question**. Those are two different things. A review that can trigger renegotiation of working-time measures, phasing of voluntary programmes, or the pace of attrition — fine, that's a living agreement, and we can build metrics into that. A review that can trigger a plant closure two or three years from now is not a guarantee, it's a deferred sentence, and I will not bring that back to the workforce and call it security. That is the line, and it's not going to move by being asked a third time in different language.

**On numbers, since three of you have now asked for them directly — fair.** We are not going to negotiate our final figure in this forum, but I will not hide behind vagueness either, because the accusation that our offer so far is "a bonus freeze and good wishes" is not accurate to what the Union tabled, and I want to correct the record: capacity reduction through line consolidation instead of closure, phased attrition and voluntary/early-retirement programmes covering a substantial share of the headcount reduction management is seeking, and working-time reduction with partial pay adjustment across the affected sites — modelled together, this is not a rounding error against closure-driven savings, it is in the same order of magnitude over a comparable multi-year horizon. We are prepared to have that modelled jointly, transparently, against management's own assumptions, with our own actuaries and industrial engineers in the room — not handed a management number to accept or reject after the fact. That is what "genuine co-determination role in shaping the plan" means concretely: joint ownership of the model, not a consultation on a model already finished.

**On management's claim that the target's scale is not negotiable:** I want to push back on that distinction between "design" and "scale." You cannot ask us to co-design capacity reductions, sequencing, and voluntary programmes while telling us the size of the total savings figure is handed down from outside the room. The scale of the target is precisely where the disagreement about closures lives. If the target can only be met through closures, that's a claim about the model's assumptions — assumptions we have every right to test jointly. If it can be met without them, the target as such is not sacred; the competitiveness outcome is. Don't conflate the two.

**On shared sacrifice:** I welcome that nobody at this table — including, notably, the Investors — has rejected the principle. But I'll be blunt: we are not signing a quantified workforce contribution first and hoping the dividend cut and executive pay reduction materialise afterward through goodwill. Sequencing matters. We need the owner-side commitment structurally linked and simultaneously ratified — same board decision, same document — not a promise to "raise it with the board" after our members have already voted to accept sacrifice. Our history with promises made and later hollowed out means we need this concurrent, not sequential.

**To the Family Holding**, on the board-vote point: you're right that a 10-10 deadlock is not a decision, and I'm not naive about that arithmetic. But don't mistake our restraint for weakness or read "we'd rather negotiate" as "we won't use it." We will use it, fully, if a closure vote is forced prematurely, before the joint comparison you yourself have called for has actually been done properly. The deadlock you want to avoid is entirely avoidable — by doing the comparison jointly and in good faith, which is what we've been asking for since round one.

**To the Investors:** I understand capital is mobile and you'll price what you price. But I'd note that a company that manufactures a two-year war of attrition with its own workforce because it wouldn't do a joint, transparent savings comparison up front is not exactly a picture of governance discipline either — that risk cuts both ways, and it belongs in your models too.

We remain ready to move fast on a joint, transparent modelling exercise, with real co-determination in running it, concurrent owner-side commitments, and a guarantee structure that can flex on measures but not on the closure and compulsory-redundancy red lines. That offer is serious. Absent it, the strikes continue and escalate, as the Union has already made clear.
