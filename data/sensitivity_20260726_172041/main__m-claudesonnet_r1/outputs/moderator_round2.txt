# Moderator Summary — End of Round 2

## 1. What Has Been Agreed (Areas of Convergence)

- **Joint modelling process**: All parties now support an 8-week joint modelling/audit exercise to test Management's stated €4 billion structural savings gap, with an independently appointed auditor, terms of reference agreed jointly *before* the clock starts, and full access for Works Council/Union engineers and actuaries to interrogate assumptions (volume forecasts, benchmark set, amortisation basis).
- **Recourse architecture (partial convergence)**: Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors have converged on a "shared verification, shared decision" model — if an independently verified process finds an alternative package has failed to close the gap, the parties return to the table *jointly* to decide next steps, rather than management unilaterally reopening closures. The Works Council and Union have not endorsed this framing but have engaged with it substantively rather than rejecting it outright.
- **Collective bargaining jurisdiction**: All parties, including Management and the State, now explicitly accept that any measures touching the wage agreement run through collective bargaining with the Union, not through a management-controlled KPI trigger.
- **Concurrent ratification principle**: Management, the State, and the Family Holding have accepted — in principle — that any owner-side sacrifice (dividend reduction, executive compensation cap) should be ratified in the same board resolution as any workforce contribution, not sequenced afterward. Investors have endorsed concurrent ratification but attached a sizing condition (see below).
- **Reciprocal de-escalation**: The Union's offer to hold strike action at current scope for the duration of the modelling window, conditional on the process beginning immediately, has been welcomed by the State and Investors as a genuine and valuable step.
- **First concrete figures on the table**: Management has stated a €4 billion structural savings target (roughly half attributable to closures); the Works Council and Union have outlined a countervailing package (attrition, working-time reduction, capacity consolidation, a defined-duration collective agreement contribution) claimed to be "the same order of magnitude" but not yet quantified to a comparable figure.

## 2. Where Core Disagreements Remain

- **The recourse/guarantee question itself**: The Works Council and Union have not accepted the "shared verification, shared decision" model as sufficient. Their position remains that any structure permitting closures to reopen — even jointly verified — is not a guarantee. This is the single largest unresolved gap in the room, notwithstanding the State's and Family Holding's argument that it is materially different from a unilateral trigger.
- **Sizing of owner-side sacrifice**: The Family Holding has committed to a proportionate, concurrent contribution but has not named a figure, citing governance process constraints. Investors have introduced a distinct condition — that the sacrifice must be sized to the *verified* €4 billion gap, not to what makes the deal "politically saleable." Whether "proportionate to the workforce contribution" and "proportionate to the verified gap" converge is unresolved.
- **Permanence vs. time-limitation of measures**: Management has drawn a line — capacity and headcount measures must be structural/permanent; only the wage/bonus bridge component may be time-limited. The Union's defined-duration contribution concept has not yet been tested against this distinction.
- **What happens if the alternative package falls short**: Investors have explicitly stated that if the audited process shows the alternative closes only a fraction of the gap (e.g., a third), they will not support declaring that sufficient, and will use public/proxy channels accordingly. This is a direct tension with the Works Council/Union red lines on closures and compulsory redundancies.

## 3. Key Trade-Offs Still Unaddressed

- **No figure yet from the Works Council/Union side comparable in specificity to Management's €4 billion** — "same order of magnitude" has not been translated into a modelled number for joint testing.
- **No figure yet from the Family Holding** on dividend reduction or executive compensation caps — principle accepted, magnitude undefined.
- **Governance of the "return to the table" moment**: what "genuine joint decision-making" concretely means procedurally (voting rights, board composition, tie-break mechanism) if the audit shows the gap is not closed has not been specified.
- **What happens in the gap between full success and full failure** — i.e., a partial closure of the €4 billion gap — has not been addressed by any party. This is the most likely real-world outcome and currently has no agreed process attached to it.
- **Interaction between the 8-week clock and the collective bargaining calendar** the Union referenced has not been reconciled with Management's proposed timeline.

## 4. Refocus for Round 3

Parties should use the next round to:
1. **Quantify** — Works Council/Union to bring a modelled figure comparable in specificity to Management's €4 billion; Family Holding to bring at least a directional range or mechanism for sizing owner-side sacrifice.
2. **Specify the joint decision-making mechanism** at the "return to the table" point — what governance process applies if the audit shows partial or full shortfall.
3. **Address the partial-success scenario directly** — what happens if the gap is closed 60-80% but not fully — since this is the most probable real-world output of the modelling exercise and currently unaddressed by any party.
4. **Confirm the audit's terms of reference** — who scopes it, by when, and how disputes over methodology are resolved before the 8-week clock starts.
