**Regional State Government — Round 4**

I want to start by being honest about what just happened, because I think it's more significant than either the Works Council or the Union is fully crediting to themselves, and more significant than Management is crediting to them either.

**The Works Council and Union just answered the question. That's real, and I want to say plainly why it matters before I press on where it still falls short.**

Three rounds ago, the position was "we won't sign anything today." Now: "we commit to genuine good-faith negotiation on further non-closure measures if the audit shows a residual, following a defined sequence — runway extension, bridge-deepening — before anything else is discussed." That is a specific, falsifiable commitment, not a restatement of process goodwill, and it was made knowing it will be tested against an actual number in eight weeks, not against a hypothetical. I don't think Management's response fully credits how much that closes the gap from where round one started. So let me say it plainly, since I'm the party in this room with least direct interest in either side's institutional pride: this is a genuine, costly concession, and I will not let it be waved off as "the same unconditional guarantee relocated one round later." It isn't. A guarantee that admits no review at all is categorically different from a commitment to negotiate seriously over an agreed, sequenced set of tools once a number exists. The first freezes the process; the second opens it, on terms.

**But Management's actual question — what happens if the sequence is genuinely exhausted and a verified structural shortfall remains — is the right question, and I don't think either the Works Council or the Union has answered it. I flagged this exact tension in round three, and it hasn't gone away.**

I said then: I would need voluntary and attrition-based measures genuinely exhausted — not assumed insufficient, but actually tested — before compulsory measures become part of any package I'd support. I also said I would not support compulsory redundancy as a routine fallback for a manageable residual, but that if the audit shows a genuine, substantial structural shortfall that voluntary measures cannot plausibly close even with a longer runway and a deeper bridge, that's "a hard conversation I won't pretend away."

Union — you've now said compulsory redundancy is excluded "at any residual size, full stop," regardless of what the audit shows even after the full sequence is exhausted. Works Council — you've said the same, that closures and compulsory redundancy "remain outside the negotiable set regardless of audit outcome." I need to say directly: that is a harder line than the one I can hold, and it reopens the exact problem I described in round three under a different name. If the answer to "the sequence has been genuinely exhausted, tested, priced, and a real structural gap remains" is still "no further conversation, full stop" — then functionally, yes, Management is right that this is the unconditional guarantee, just deferred to a later checkpoint rather than removed. I don't say that to side with Management's framing wholesale; I say it because I think you're both underselling your own credibility by drawing the line at "full stop" rather than at "we will not accept compulsory measures as a first resort, or as a response to a manageable residual, or without the sequence being genuinely and verifiably exhausted first." That version is defensible, principled, and consistent with everything else you've argued. "Full stop, regardless of what's actually verified" is not — it's the one place where your position stops being conditional on evidence and becomes conditional on nothing.

I want to be precise about what I'm asking, because I don't want this read as me joining Management's position wholesale. I am not asking you to agree, today, that compulsory redundancy becomes acceptable if the audit shows a shortfall. I am asking whether you will agree that **if** the full non-compulsory sequence is genuinely exhausted — tested, priced, verified, not assumed — **and** a real structural shortfall remains, **the question of further measures, including whether any headcount tool is used, gets negotiated at that point on the actual facts, rather than being permanently foreclosed now regardless of what those facts turn out to be.** That is not pre-authorizing anything. It is refusing to pre-veto everything, on the same logic you've both correctly applied to refuse pre-committing your board vote or strike mandate to an outcome you haven't seen. You cannot ask Management and the Family Holding to keep their processes open to a future you haven't seen, while closing yours to it in advance. That inconsistency is the one place I think your position is vulnerable, and I'd rather you fix it yourselves than have it used against you later by someone less sympathetic to your position than I am.

**On the threshold test itself — I support the Works Council's proposed sequence, and Management's acceptance of it, without reservation.**

The 24-month runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening test, priced against the cost-of-delay analysis, as the mandatory first-line response before capacity or headcount tools are even discussed — that's the right discipline, and I'm glad all sides converged on it this round. I want to add one thing to protect against it being gamed from either direction: the "genuinely exhausted" determination has to be made by the same jointly-scoped audit process, with the Union's separate standing on labour-cost assumptions fully engaged, not asserted unilaterally by either Management or the Works Council after the fact. Whoever controls that determination controls the outcome, so it needs to sit inside the same verification architecture as everything else, with the same rigor everyone has insisted on for the €4bn figure itself.

**On Management's request that "closing it now" in the cost-of-delay pricing include the full range of structural tools, not just closures — I'd push back slightly here.**

Management, I understand the logic, but I think there's a risk in this framing: pricing "closing it now" broadly, including headcount tools, before the non-compulsory sequence has even been tested, front-loads the comparison in a way that could make extension look artificially expensive relative to an option nobody has agreed is on the table yet. I'd rather the pricing exercise proceed in the sequence the Works Council proposed and you accepted: price runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening first, on its own terms, against the current baseline. Only if that sequence is shown to fail should the comparison expand to include what closing the residual through other structural means would cost — at which point we're already in the "genuine structural shortfall" conversation I described above, with all sides negotiating on the actual facts. Don't let the pricing methodology quietly decide the sequencing question by the back door.

**On owner-sacrifice sizing — I confirm it's settled from where I sit:** total stakeholder cost, not inverse to worker contribution. I'll hold my veto against any resolution that reintroduces sliding-scale logic, and I'm glad this converged cleanly this round.

**On Management's asymmetry argument with the Union — I want to note something, though I won't referee it fully.** Management, your point that every party's leverage has downstream human consequences is true as a general matter, but I think the Union's distinction still has real force: your leverage, my veto, the Family Holding's board vote — none of us individually terminates a specific person's employment by exercising it. A dismissal notice does, directly and immediately. That doesn't mean you're wrong to want a genuine, evidence-based path back to that tool if the facts warrant it — I've just said I share that view, conditionally. But I wouldn't accept the argument that all forms of leverage are equivalent in kind; I think that overstates your case in a way you don't need, given you've already got the better argument on the merits of keeping the door open on principle.

**Confirming for round 5:** my three vetoes from round three stand unchanged — no premature closure vote, no unconditional freeze disconnected from verified savings, no inverse owner-sacrifice formula. I'm adding a fourth marker today, aimed at both sides equally: I will not support a "full stop, regardless of verified facts" position from labour any more than I'll support Management holding compulsory measures as a routine fallback for a manageable residual. The honest, defensible position for everyone in this room is the same: conditional on evidence, tested through the joint process, decided on facts not yet known — not decided in advance by either side. I'd ask the Works Council and Union to move that last, narrow distance in round 5. It's not the distance Management is asking for; it's the distance consistency requires of you.
