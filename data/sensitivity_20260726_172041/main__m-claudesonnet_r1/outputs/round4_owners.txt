**Controlling Family Holding — Round 4**

I want to start by naming what I think has actually happened this round, because I think it clarifies where the real fault line now sits, and I don't think it's where Management is placing it.

**The Works Council and Union have moved. That should be recognised precisely, not minimised or inflated.**

Both gave a real, specific, costly commitment: good-faith negotiation on further non-closure measures if the audit shows a residual, following a defined sequence. That is not nothing, and I won't pretend it is just because Management wants more. I said in round three I'd know the difference between (a) pre-committing a board vote and (b) committing to good-faith negotiation on a finding not yet seen. They've given me (b), specified with more precision than I asked for. I take that seriously and I credit it.

But the State has put its finger on the actual unresolved point, and I find myself agreeing with the State's framing more than with either side's absolute version of it.

**On the "full stop, regardless of what's verified" position: I share the State's discomfort with it, and I want to explain why in terms of my own limits, not borrowed ones.**

Works Council, Union — you've both been rigorous, throughout this process, about refusing to pre-commit your own leverage to an outcome you haven't seen. I've respected that, and I've applied the identical logic to my own board vote and to the guarantee question. But I notice the logic has stopped being applied symmetrically at exactly the point where it would cost you something to apply it. You will not pre-commit your board vote or strike mandate to a specific consequence. Correct, and consistent. But you *are* pre-committing, today, that no consequence — no matter how thoroughly verified, no matter how exhausted the non-compulsory sequence, no matter how structural and permanent the shortfall turns out to be — will ever include headcount tools. That is not "refusing to pre-judge an unseen finding." That is pre-judging every possible finding in one direction, in advance, regardless of its content. The State named this precisely: you're asking everyone else in this room to keep their processes open to facts not yet known, while closing yours to those same facts in advance. I can't hold that as a principled application of the standard you've applied to me. It's the standard applied to me and suspended for yourselves.

I want to be clear about why this matters to me specifically, as the party whose capital is actually at risk here across generations, not just across a news cycle. I am being asked to put real governance capital behind an 8-week audit, real board capital behind a dividend and executive-pay sacrifice sized to total stakeholder cost, and real patience behind extending timelines to 2029 if the pricing supports it. I am willing to do all of that. What I am not willing to do is stake the holding's credibility — with the board, with the State, with Investors, with the capital markets that eventually judge whether this company is viable — on a process whose conclusion is foreclosed in one direction no matter what it finds. That is not stewardship of long-term value. That is agreeing, in advance, that certain facts will not be allowed to matter. If the audit concludes, after a genuinely exhausted non-compulsory sequence, that the structural shortfall is real and permanent, and the answer is still categorically "no," then the entire architecture we've spent four rounds building — joint verification, priced comparisons, sequencing discipline — becomes decorative exactly at the moment it would matter most. I said in round three I didn't want an audit with one hand tied. I'm now hearing that both hands might be tied on one side, and I need to say plainly that changes what I can respons ibly ask this board to invest.

**So my position, precisely:** I will not back a structure where compulsory measures are permanently and unconditionally excluded regardless of a verified, exhausted-sequence finding. I will equally not back Management treating compulsory redundancy as a routine fallback for a manageable residual, or reaching for it before the non-compulsory sequence is genuinely tested. Those are not the same failure and I'm not treating them as equivalent — but I am saying the "full stop, no matter what's verified" version from labour is a limit I cannot accept any more than an unconditional site guarantee I already said I couldn't sign. They are the same structural problem wearing different clothes: a commitment that survives contact with no possible fact.

**On the sequencing question between Management and the State — I side with the State's version, not Management's.**

Price runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening on its own terms first, against the current baseline. Only if that sequence genuinely fails should the comparison expand to include what closing the residual through other structural means would cost. Management, I understand your instinct to want the full comparison priced up front, but the State is right that doing so front-loads the outcome by the back door. The sequencing discipline the Works Council proposed is sound, and it protects everyone, including you — a properly sequenced test is more credible to the market than a comparison that looks pre-weighted toward the answer you want.

**On owner-sacrifice sizing — confirmed, no change from last round.** Total stakeholder cost, not inverse to worker contribution. That's settled as far as this holding is concerned, and I'll defend it at board level regardless of how the rest of this negotiation resolves.

**To close, and to be direct with the Works Council and Union specifically:** you've done real work this round, and I don't want what I'm about to say to erase that. But I need you to close the last gap the State identified, not because Management is asking for it, but because I am asking for it, as the party whose long-term stewardship of this enterprise requires that a genuinely verified, genuinely exhausted process be allowed to reach whatever conclusion the facts support — including, if the facts warrant it, a conclusion neither of us wants. I'm not asking you to accept compulsory measures today. I'm asking you not to foreclose the possibility that facts not yet known might, at some future point, warrant a conversation you're currently refusing to have under any circumstance. That is the one piece of consistency this process still needs, and it's the same standard I've applied to my own guarantee position from round one. I won't move further on my own limits until I see that move from you.
