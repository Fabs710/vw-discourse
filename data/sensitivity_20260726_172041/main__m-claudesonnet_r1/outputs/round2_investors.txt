[Dispersed Capital-Market Shareholders (Institutional Investors) — Round 2]

This round produced what round one didn't: an actual number, an actual process, and a plausible structure for owner-side sacrifice. I want to be precise about which of these developments moves us and which doesn't, because there's a difference between "encouraging direction" and "something we can actually price."

**On the €4 billion figure and the 8-week joint modelling window — this is the single most important development of the round, and we support it without reservation.** A quantified competitiveness gap, an external auditor, a tight clock, and full access for works council and union engineers to interrogate the assumptions is exactly the discipline we called for in round one. This is not a cosmetic gesture — it's a mechanism that could actually produce a number the market believes. We'd note one thing for the record: the credibility of that audit matters to us as much as it matters to the Works Council, for the opposite reason. You need it to test whether closures are really necessary. We need it to test whether the €4 billion figure is real and not managed downward to make a negotiated settlement look sufficient when it isn't. Both risks are real, and a genuinely independent, jointly-scoped audit — as the Family Holding proposed, terms of reference agreed before the clock starts — is the only version of this we'd treat as more than theatre.

**On the milestone/recourse architecture — we back the State's and Family Holding's reading over the Works Council's original framing, and I want to say directly why.** A shared-verification structure, where failure to close the gap triggers a joint return to the table rather than a unilateral management reopening, is not the "deferred sentence" the Works Council described — it's the only version of a guarantee we'd find investable. An unconditional freeze regardless of verified outcome is, as I said last round, close to our definition of an unacceptable result: it prices as a five-year fuse, not a settlement. So on this specific architectural question, our interests sit with Management, the State, and the Family Holding. If this gets softened into an unconditional guarantee at the next stage of drafting, we will say so clearly, and it will show up in how we price the equity, not just in how we comment here.

**On owner-side sacrifice — I want to press the Family Holding a bit further than "proportionate, sized to the workforce contribution."** We said in round one we're not opposed to a dividend reduction and executive pay cap as part of a credible package, and we hold to that. But I'd flag a sequencing risk nobody has named yet: if the dividend cut is ratified concurrently with the workforce contribution, as everyone now seems to agree it should be, it needs to be sized against the *verified* €4 billion gap-closure figure, not against whatever number emerges from negotiation dynamics in the room. A dividend cut calibrated to make the deal politically saleable, rather than calibrated to what the audited numbers actually show, is precisely the "capital destroyed twice over" scenario I described last round — real cost to our members, attached to a package that still leaves margins impaired. So: concurrent ratification, yes. But the size has to track the audited gap-closure outcome, not the negotiating atmosphere on the day the resolution is drafted. I'd ask the Family Holding to confirm that's what "proportionate" means in practice, because "proportionate to the workforce contribution" and "proportionate to the verified savings gap" are not automatically the same number if the workforce contribution itself falls short of closing the gap.

**On the Works Council's point that our risk models should account for the cost of a two-year war of attrition — agreed, and I said as much in round one.** That's precisely why we support the 8-week clock and the Union's offer to hold current strike action at present scope during that window. That reciprocal de-escalation is the single most useful thing that happened this round from our standpoint, because prolonged conflict is a cost to us regardless of who eventually wins the substantive argument. We'd encourage all sides to treat that de-escalation as fragile and time-limited, not as leverage to be spent slowly — every week inside the 8 weeks is fine; a second round of the same conversation after the window lapses is not.

**Where we are not moved, and want to be explicit about it:** if the audited process concludes the alternative package closes, say, a third of the €4 billion gap — Management's illustrative worst case — we will not support dressing that up as sufficient because it was hard-won politically or because a board majority prefers it to conflict. That outcome is the one we flagged in round one as unacceptable: a settlement that leaves the cost base structurally impaired. In that scenario, our view is that closures and headcount reduction, however painful, are the responsible path, and we would say so publicly through our own channels — research notes, engagement letters, proxy voting where we hold it — regardless of the industrial relations cost of saying it. That is not a threat, it's the honest limit of what our mandate allows us to support.

**Concretely, going into the modelling window:** we want to see, at minimum, (1) the terms of reference for the audit agreed and published before the clock starts, as the Family Holding proposed; (2) a concurrent, size-linked owner-sacrifice commitment ratified in the same resolution as any workforce contribution, calibrated to the verified outcome rather than the negotiation; and (3) the 8-week timeline held firm, because further extension itself becomes a cost we will price. Deliver that, whichever mix of closures and alternative measures it produces, and we will back it. Fall short of the €4 billion on a comparable timeline, and we will not treat "we tried, and it was politically difficult" as a substitute for competitive margins.
