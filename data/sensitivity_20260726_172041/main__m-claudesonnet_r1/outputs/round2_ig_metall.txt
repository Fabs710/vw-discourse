I stand fully with the Works Council's distinction on milestone-linked guarantees, and I want to sharpen it from the collective-bargaining side, because there's a version of "milestone-linked" that touches our contract territory directly, and I need to be explicit about it before it gets baked into a framework by default.

**On milestones and the wage agreement specifically:** If the metric that triggers renegotiation is cost-per-vehicle, and the lever management pulls to hit that metric is reopening pay and guarantees rather than renegotiating in good faith with us, then a "milestone review" becomes a mechanism for bypassing collective bargaining altogether — a standing threat that overrides the agreement every time volumes dip. I won't accept that architecture. Any review process that touches labour cost has to run *through* the collective bargaining process, with us at the table, not as an automatic trigger written into a board document that we're not party to. The works council can negotiate site and capacity questions; wages and guarantees are our jurisdiction, and that doesn't get delegated into a KPI dashboard.

**On the numbers, direct response to Management and Investors:** You asked for a serious, scaled figure rather than "bonuses and goodwill." Fair challenge, and the Works Council just answered it — phased attrition, voluntary programmes, working-time reduction with partial pay adjustment, capacity consolidation, modelled jointly against your own assumptions. I'll add the union-specific piece: we are prepared to negotiate a time-limited collective agreement amendment — a defined-duration cost contribution, not a permanent gutting of the framework — sized to close a real, jointly-verified gap. Time-limited matters to us for a specific reason: a permanent contract change ratified under this kind of pressure, with the ink barely dry on a settlement, is not something our members will accept, and it isn't something I'd bring to them. A defined term with a renegotiation point built into the *bargaining calendar*, not a management-controlled milestone trigger, is a structure I can actually sell.

**On sequencing of owner-side sacrifice** — I agree completely with the Works Council: concurrent, same-document, not sequential. I'd go further and say this is where I test whether "shared sacrifice" from Management, the Family Holding, and Investors is real or rhetorical. Every one of you has said the words. None of you has put a number on the table. The Works Council and I have put concrete mechanisms on the table — attrition scale, working-time reduction, a defined contribution. Where is the equivalent specificity from your side? "We'll raise it with the board" is not a term sheet. Before I ask my members to ratify anything, I need a dividend and executive-compensation figure on the table in the same round this comparison concludes — not a good-faith gesture promised for later.

**To Management, on "the target's scale is not negotiable":** I want to be very direct about why I don't accept that framing, separate from the Works Council's point about model assumptions. You've set a target that assumes closures as the reference case. But that target itself is a modelling choice — cost-per-vehicle at what volume assumption, amortized over what capital base, compared against which competitor benchmark. If the joint modelling exercise the Works Council is proposing runs on your assumptions with no ability to interrogate them, then "scale is not negotiable" just means "our first number wins." That's not a comparison, that's a rubber stamp with extra steps. I will not bargain against a number I'm not allowed to test.

**On time pressure — mine, not just the Investors':** I said in round one that every week without a credible reciprocal framework increases pressure from our membership to escalate. I want to be concrete about what that means going forward: I am prepared to hold current strike action at its present scope — not escalate further — for a defined, short period, if and only if the joint modelling exercise begins immediately, with real co-determination and union participation in running it, and a concurrent owner-sacrifice figure is put on the table within that same window. That is a genuine, time-limited de-escalation offer. It is not indefinite patience, and it is not unconditional. If this drags into a second round of vague reassurances without numbers from the other side, the strikes escalate, not because I want a fight but because that is the mechanics of what my mandate requires.

To be unambiguous about my red lines, since we're now several rounds into this: no compulsory redundancies, no closures, and no unilateral reopening of the collective wage agreement outside collective bargaining — milestone-linked or otherwise. Inside those lines, I am ready to move fast with a real number. Outside them, there is no deal, regardless of how the milestone language is dressed up.
