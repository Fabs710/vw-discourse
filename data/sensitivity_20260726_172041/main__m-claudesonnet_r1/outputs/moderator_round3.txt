# Moderator Summary — After Round 3

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Board arithmetic clarified and accepted by all parties**: The two-thirds supervisory board threshold governs any closure decision; labour's board seats cannot be procedurally overridden by any audit outcome. This is now a shared factual premise, not a disputed claim.
- **Audit/verification process**: All six parties support the 8-week joint modelling exercise proceeding, with the Works Council's alternative package (attrition, working-time reduction, capacity consolidation, Union bridge contribution) formally entered into scope alongside Management's €4bn figure.
- **Distinct Union standing confirmed**: Management has explicitly granted the Union separate standing (distinct from the Works Council) on labour-cost and working-time assumptions within the audit's terms of reference.
- **Owner-sacrifice sizing principle**: The Works Council, Union, State, and Family Holding have converged on rejecting any formula where owner contribution shrinks as worker-side pain (closures, redundancies) increases. The Family Holding has explicitly stated the dividend/compensation sacrifice will be sized to *total stakeholder cost*, not inversely to workforce contribution. Investors have clarified they did not intend, and do not support, the inverse-sizing formula, and accept this framing.
- **Priced cost-of-delay analysis requested and accepted**: Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors all support commissioning, within the audit, an explicit priced comparison between (a) extending the runway on any residual gap and (b) closing it through further structural measures now.
- **No party has moved off its round-one/round-two hard limits**; all restated them this round rather than revising them.

## 2. Core Disagreements Remaining

- **Whether "shared verification" implies any binding consequence.** Management, the State, and the Family Holding are now asking the Works Council and Union for a specific, narrower commitment: that an unfavourable, jointly-verified audit finding will be met with continued good-faith negotiation on further measures, rather than an unconditional defence of the current package. The Works Council and Union have not yet answered this specific formulation — they have committed to good-faith *engagement* and non-prejudgment, but declined to commit to any specific response to a given audit outcome, on the grounds that this would pre-commit board votes and strike mandates in advance.
- **Compulsory redundancy as residual-gap-filler.** The Union holds an absolute red line against compulsory redundancy under any residual scenario. Management refuses to renounce it entirely in advance, treating that as equivalent to the unconditional guarantee it also won't sign. The State stakes out a middle position (voluntary measures must be genuinely exhausted; small residuals shouldn't default to compulsory measures) but has not fully resolved the gap between the Union's absolute line and Management's conditional reservation.
- **Whether a time-bound (to 2030) guarantee that cannot be revisited mid-term is acceptable.** The Works Council's reframing (time-bound, not "eternal") has been acknowledged as a stronger argument by the Family Holding, but the Family Holding still rejects a guarantee that cannot respond to an interim audit showing the alternative package is failing.
- **What counts as "success" in a partial-closure scenario.** The Works Council models 75–85% gap closure via non-closure measures; this figure has not yet been tested in the audit. Management, the State, and Investors withhold judgment pending the priced cost-of-delay analysis rather than accepting the percentage alone as dispositive.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **Reciprocity of restraint**: Investors and Management have both flagged that Management's costly, verifiable commitments (opening models, fixed clock, owner-sacrifice proposal) are not currently matched by an equally binding labour-side commitment regarding the *consequence* of an unfavourable audit finding. This asymmetry is unresolved and, per Investors, will affect deal-certainty pricing regardless of audit quality.
- **Definition of "genuine structural shortfall" versus "manageable residual/timing gap"** — raised by the State as the pivot point for whether compulsory measures or an extended runway is the appropriate response — has not been operationalized. No party has proposed a threshold or method for distinguishing the two categories.
- **Precise mechanics of extending the runway** (2028/2029) as an alternative to closures/compulsory measures — floated by multiple parties — has not been costed or agreed as a concrete fallback structure.
- **The Union's request for depth/duration flexibility in the bridge contribution** as the mechanism for closing a residual gap has been noted as acceptable in principle by Management but not yet specified in terms of scale or trigger conditions.

## 4. Refocus for Round 4

Round 4 should concentrate on:

1. **A direct answer from the Works Council and Union** to the specific question now posed by Management, the State, the Family Holding, and Investors: will an unfavourable, jointly-verified audit finding be met with a commitment to further good-faith negotiation on additional measures — distinct from pre-committing a board vote or strike mandate outcome?
2. **Specifying the boundary between "genuine structural shortfall" and "manageable/timing residual"** — what evidentiary threshold or method would the audit use to distinguish them, and what response each would trigger.
3. **Testing whether "extend the runway" and "deepen the bridge contribution" can be jointly specified as the agreed first-line response to a partial residual**, before compulsory measures are discussed at all — potentially narrowing the Union/Management dispute to a smaller, more defined disagreement.
4. Continued technical scoping of the audit terms of reference is proceeding satisfactorily and does not require further plenary time unless a party objects.
