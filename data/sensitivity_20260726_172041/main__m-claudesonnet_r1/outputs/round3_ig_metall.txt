**Industrial Union — Round 3**

I want to build directly on what the Works Council just laid out, because the board-arithmetic point is not a procedural footnote — it reframes what everyone else in this room has been asking for, and I want to make sure that reframing sticks before we move to round 4.

**On the two-thirds threshold: this changes what "shared verification, shared decision" actually means, and I want the State and Family Holding to say so plainly.**

The Works Council is right that no audit, however rigorous, manufactures four extra labour votes on the supervisory board. So when Management, the State, and the Family Holding describe the recourse mechanism as "come back to the table together, genuine joint decision-making" — that isn't a concession they're granting us. It's a description of the legal reality that already exists. What's actually being requested, stripped of the framing, is that labour's board representatives commit in advance not to exercise the veto their seats already carry, if a process concludes a certain way. I don't object to naming that request honestly. I object to it being presented as though the "shared decision" architecture is itself the concession, when the concession being asked for is ours, made in advance, on a fact-pattern we haven't seen yet.

So let me be precise about where I land, separately from the Works Council's board seats but on the same logic: I will not commit today that the Union will refrain from calling further industrial action if a future audit reaches a conclusion I haven't tested and don't yet know the content of. That's not obstruction — it's the same principle everyone in this room has invoked for their own veto. The State won't pre-commit its blocking minority. The Family Holding won't pre-commit to a dividend number before the audit. Investors won't pre-commit to accepting a partial outcome as sufficient. Nobody is signing blank cheques on their own leverage. I'm not signing one on ours either.

**On the partial-success scenario — I want to answer the moderator's flagged question from the collective-bargaining side specifically, because it interacts with something Management said this round that I need to push back on.**

Management drew a line: capacity and headcount measures must be permanent, only the wage/bonus bridge can be time-limited. I accepted that distinction in round two as workable in principle. But now that we're talking about a package that the Works Council models as closing 75-85% of the gap, I want to flag the actual mechanism by which the remaining shortfall gets closed matters enormously to me, and it hasn't been specified. If the answer to "we're at 80%, not 100%" is "so the wage/bonus bridge needs to be deeper or longer," I can work with that — that's exactly the kind of defined-duration instrument I proposed, and depth or duration is a legitimate thing to negotiate inside the collective bargaining process. If the answer is "so we convert the remaining 20% into headcount reduction via compulsory measures," that crosses my red line regardless of how small the residual gap is. Twenty percent of a €4 billion target is not a rounding error, and I'm not going to let it get closed through compulsory redundancies dressed up as "just the remainder." So: extend the runway, deepen the bridge contribution, revisit the volume assumptions behind the €4 billion figure itself — all negotiable. Compulsory redundancy as the residual-gap-filler — not negotiable, however small the residual.

**On Investors' sizing logic for owner-sacrifice — I want to associate myself completely with the Works Council's objection, and sharpen it from the bargaining side.**

If owner sacrifice shrinks whenever workers absorb more pain, that isn't shared sacrifice — it's a formula where our restraint funds their restraint. I'd put it even more sharply than the Works Council did: under that logic, the *worst* outcome for the workforce (deepest closures, most compulsory job losses) produces the *smallest* owner contribution, because "less sacrifice was needed elsewhere." That is backwards, and I will not bring a package built on that formula to my members for ratification. I need the Family Holding to answer this directly, not with "proportionate" left undefined: proportionate to what, specifically? If it's proportionate to total value extracted from all stakeholders combined, that's a number I can work with. If it's a sliding scale where the owner side pays less as workers pay more, I'm out before the audit even starts, because the entire premise of my members' cooperation with the joint modelling exercise is that this isn't a mechanism to make our concessions carry the whole adjustment.

**On the audit terms of reference, since the moderator flagged this as unresolved: I want equal footing with the Works Council in scoping it, not delegated access to their seat.**

The Works Council's engineers and actuaries testing volume assumptions and benchmark sets is necessary but not sufficient from where I sit — the wage and working-time assumptions embedded in the €4 billion figure are collective-bargaining matters, and I want our own bargaining economists directly in the room scoping that specific slice of the methodology, not represented by proxy through the works council's technical team. Management, when you finalize terms of reference in the next 8 weeks, I need that confirmed explicitly — separate standing for the Union on the labour-cost assumptions specifically, distinct from the Works Council's standing on capacity and site assumptions. Those are related but not identical jurisdictions, and I don't want that conflated the way "milestone-linked" nearly got conflated with a labour-cost trigger in round one.

**On de-escalation — I'll confirm the offer stands, with one clarification.** I said last round I'd hold current strike action at present scope for the duration of the modelling window, conditional on the process beginning immediately with real co-determination and union standing in it. That condition now specifically includes the distinct labour-cost standing I just described. If that standing is granted and the audit begins on the timeline Management proposed, the restraint holds. If the terms of reference exclude the Union from the labour-cost methodology, or if this stretches past the 8 weeks into a second round of "we'll finalize scoping soon," the restraint doesn't hold — not as a threat, but because my mandate from the membership was never open-ended, and I've been honest about that from round one.

To close: no compulsory redundancies, no closures, no unilateral reopening of the wage agreement outside collective bargaining, and no owner-sacrifice formula that shrinks as worker pain grows. Inside those lines — deeper bridge contributions, longer runways, rigorous joint testing of the real number — I am moving fast and in good faith. Outside them, the strikes are still the mechanism, and that hasn't changed.
