We need to be clear-eyed about what actually got us here, because both of you spoke as if the only thing at issue is trust broken by management. I'll take that head-on in a moment. But first the arithmetic, because that's what nobody in either of your statements actually engaged with.

Our German plants are running at a fraction of installed capacity in a market that has structurally shrunk and will not come back to the volumes those factories were built for. Labour cost per vehicle here is far above what our competitors — including the Chinese manufacturers now taking share in Europe — operate at. That is not a management narrative; it's the balance sheet. If we do nothing structural about both overcapacity and cost, the return-on-sales target is missed again next year, and the year after, and the money we need for 2030 products and technology simply isn't there. That is the risk I am mandated to prevent, and I will not pretend it's negotiable at the level of principle. Some form of durable, structural reduction in cost and capacity is happening. The open question is the form it takes — that's what I'm here to work through with you.

Now, to your specific positions.

Works Council — I hear the site-guarantee demand, and I understand why the 2029 pledge's history makes you distrust anything short of it in writing. But a binding, board-ratified guarantee for every plant through 2030 is not something I can offer honestly, because it would pre-commit us to exactly the outcome I cannot accept: locking in the current footprint regardless of how volumes and costs evolve. That would be a cosmetic deal, not a structural one, and I said at the outset that a settlement which leaves the cost base uncompetitive is not one I can sign off on, whatever the board arithmetic. So let's not pretend that door is open — it isn't, not in that unconditional form.

Where I do think there's real room: I am open to achieving the required savings through capacity reduction, shift and line consolidation, voluntary and early-retirement programmes, and working-time adjustment rather than compulsory redundancies and closures — if, and only if, the numbers actually add up to a comparable structural saving, on a comparable timeline. That's a genuine offer, not a rhetorical one. But it has to be real capacity coming out, not deferred, and it has to be paired with a genuine, quantified cost contribution from the workforce side — not a symbolic solidarity fund.

Union — your counter-proposal is a start, and I take seriously that it's a real offer rather than a gesture. But "forgoing bonuses" and a voluntary solidarity fund does not come close to closing the structural gap between our labour cost per unit and the competition's. If you want me to treat it as the alternative to closures, it needs to be scaled to match what closures and headcount reduction would actually save — not a fraction of it. Put a number on the table that's serious, and I'll engage with it seriously.

On shared sacrifice — I don't reject the principle. Executive pay and dividend policy are legitimate parts of this conversation, and I won't pretend otherwise; if we're asking for a workforce contribution, management and owners visibly carrying part of the burden makes any deal easier to sell internally and to your members. I'm prepared to bring that to the family owners and the board. But don't mistake that concession for an equivalence — a capped bonus round is not a substitute for structural cost savings measured in hundreds of millions annually. It's a condition that makes a deal politically possible, not the deal itself.

On co-determination in shaping the plan: I'll engage your representatives substantively on the design of the restructuring — where capacity comes out, how it's sequenced, what the voluntary programmes look like. I won't accept a veto over the scale of the target. The target is set by what the market and the cost base require, not by negotiation.

So here's where I actually am, plainly: give me a credible, quantified alternative package — capacity reduction, working-time measures, a real employee financial contribution, phased attrition — that gets us to comparable savings on a comparable timeline to closures, and I will take closures off the table and bring visible owner and executive contributions to match. Offer me a guarantee that freezes the footprint and a savings figure that doesn't close the gap, and I will not sign it, and I will take this to a board vote and to the public, because a deal that leaves us uncompetitive in 2027 helps nobody, including your members. That's not a threat, it's the honest limit of my mandate.
