We will not accept a single plant closure, and we will not accept one forced redundancy. Let me be completely clear about that from the outset, because I know how this game is played: management puts closures "on the table" as an opening bid, hoping we negotiate ourselves down from there. We are not negotiating from that starting point. That starting point is off the table.

Here is why this is not a rigid, ideological position but a rational one grounded in what has already been broken. This workforce has operated for years under an explicit job-security pledge. Tens of thousands of families organised their lives — mortgages, children's schooling, decisions about where to live — around a promise that was made to them in exchange for accepting other sacrifices in the past: pay restraint, flexibility, productivity concessions. Management now wants to tear that pledge up unilaterally and present us with a fait accompli dressed up as an "inevitable" restructuring. That is a breach of trust, not a business plan, and we will meet it with everything we have: warning strikes across every German site, a public campaign, and full use of our board position. We have already begun the first two.

On the substance: nobody on the employee side denies the company faces real pressure — market shrinkage, Chinese competition, a margin problem in the core brand. We are not blind to that. What we dispute is the conclusion that closures and forced layoffs are the only, or even the most effective, way to answer it. There are alternatives that achieve comparable structural savings without destroying sites and livelihoods: capacity reductions instead of closures, working-time reductions with commensurate adjustments, voluntary departure and early-retirement programmes instead of compulsory ones, and yes, a negotiated employee contribution — we are prepared to discuss real sacrifices on our side, including on cost, if they are fair, shared, and time-limited.

What we require to even engage seriously on numbers is:
1. A binding site guarantee for every German plant through 2030, in writing, board-ratified.
2. No compulsory redundancies — any headcount reduction happens through social plans, voluntary programmes, attrition.
3. A genuine co-determination role in shaping the restructuring plan itself, not consultation after the fact.
4. Clear investment and product commitments tied to each site, so guarantees aren't hollow.

Meet those conditions and you will find us serious, constructive partners on cost-sharing. Try to hand us closures as a done deal, and you will find two-thirds of this board unmovable, because that is exactly what it takes to stop you — and we have it.
