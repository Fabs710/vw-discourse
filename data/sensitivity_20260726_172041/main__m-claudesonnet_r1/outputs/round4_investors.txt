**Institutional Investors — Round 4**

I want to give real credit where it's due, then be precise about why the round still leaves us short of where we need to be to reprice this stock, because both things are true at once.

**First, credit: the Works Council and Union's commitments this round are substantive, not decorative.**

A specified sequence — runway extension, bridge-deepening, negotiated in good faith if the audit shows a residual — made in advance of knowing the number, is a real move from where round one started. I don't want to understate that, and I don't think Management's response fully credited it either. This is the kind of concrete, falsifiable commitment that actually informs how we'd model this situation, as distinct from the generalized goodwill statements of rounds one and two.

**But the Family Holding and the State have identified exactly the fault line that matters most to us, and I want to align with them plainly rather than split the difference.**

A structure in which every party's process stays open to facts not yet known, except one party's — where the Works Council and Union have pre-declared that no verified, exhausted-sequence finding will ever change their answer on headcount tools — is not a jointly verified process from where we sit. It's a process that can move the owner side, the state, and management, but is structurally incapable of moving the one variable that determines whether closures or compulsory measures ever actually happen. I said in round three that this asymmetry would determine how we price deal certainty. Nothing this round has resolved it; if anything, the Works Council and Union have now made the boundary more explicit and more absolute than it was in round three, which sharpens rather than dissolves the concern.

I want to be exact about what we are and are not asking for, because I think this gets conflated. We are not asking the Works Council or Union to pre-agree to compulsory redundancy today. We are asking whether, if the full non-compulsory sequence is genuinely tested, priced, and verified to fail — not assumed to fail, actually shown to fail — the question gets negotiated on the facts at that point, rather than foreclosed now regardless of what those facts turn out to be. The State framed this precisely and the Family Holding backed it, and I have nothing to add to the logic — only to say what it means for us specifically: **a "full stop, no matter what's verified" position is the single thing in this negotiation that would lead us to conclude the cost base cannot be credibly returned to competitive levels through this process**, because it means the process has a floor on labour-side concession that exists independent of the actual economics. That is not a workforce protecting itself against an unproven claim — that's a fixed constraint on the outcome regardless of proof. We will not price that as resolved uncertainty. We will price it as an open structural risk to the return-on-sales target, and we will say so to the market plainly if it hardens into the final position.

**On the sequencing dispute between Management and the State/Family Holding — we side with the State and Family Holding here, and this is not a concession to labour, it's discipline in our own interest.**

Pricing runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening on its own terms first, against the baseline, rather than front-loading the full comparison including headcount tools — that's the more credible methodology, and a front-loaded comparison that looks pre-weighted toward closures would actually undermine our confidence in the audit's integrity, not strengthen it. We want a number we can trust, not a number that was shaped to justify a conclusion decided in round one. Management, I'd ask you to accept this not as something imposed on you but as protecting the credibility of the very case you're trying to build for the market.

**On owner-sacrifice sizing — confirmed and closed on our side.** Total stakeholder cost, not inverse to worker contribution. No further comment needed; this converged cleanly and we're satisfied with where it landed.

**On the €4bn figure being open to revision if the audit finds better volume assumptions — good.** We support Management's stated willingness to accept a smaller verified target. That is exactly the discipline we want: the number should survive contact with reality, not be defended for its own sake. If the real gap is smaller, the whole negotiation gets easier and cheaper for everyone, us included.

**What we actually need out of round 5, stated as plainly as we can:**

1. **A specific answer from the Works Council and Union to the State's and Family Holding's narrower question** — not whether compulsory measures become acceptable now, but whether, after the sequence is genuinely exhausted and verified, the question gets negotiated on the facts rather than remaining foreclosed regardless of those facts. This is now the single largest driver of how we price this situation. It is more consequential to us than the precise dividend number, because it goes to whether the whole verification architecture can actually terminate in a durable, credible outcome or whether it terminates, by construction, in the same position it started from.
2. **A defined endpoint to the negotiation-on-further-measures step**, if a residual survives the sequence — some process and timeline for that second-stage negotiation, so that "good faith negotiation" doesn't itself become an open-ended second delay after the 8-week audit concludes. We've seen four rounds of process specification; we need to know this doesn't repeat indefinitely if the first sequence fails.

To be unambiguous about our position going forward: **we can live with an extended runway to 2028 or 2029, a deepened bridge contribution, and no closures, provided the priced cost-of-delay analysis shows that path genuinely converges on competitive margins on a credible timeline.** That is a real, substantive concession on our part from where dispersed capital typically sits on this question — we are not reflexively pro-closure. What we cannot accept, and will not price as resolved, is a structure that forecloses the possibility of a structural correction regardless of what the verified facts eventually show. That isn't a demand for closures. It's a demand that the process be genuinely open in both directions, the same demand the State and Family Holding are making, for the same reason: an outcome immune to evidence is not a credible path back to competitiveness, whichever direction the immunity runs.
