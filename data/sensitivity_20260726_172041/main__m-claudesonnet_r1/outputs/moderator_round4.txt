# Moderator Summary — Round 4

## 1. What Has Been Agreed

- **Audit scope and process**: 8-week jointly-verified audit proceeds, with Works Council package and Union's labour-cost/volume assumptions formally in scope.
- **Sequencing discipline**: Runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening is accepted by all parties as the mandatory first-line response to any residual, priced against the cost-of-delay analysis, before any further escalation is considered. Management has explicitly accepted this sequencing (not front-loading headcount tools into the initial comparison).
- **Owner-sacrifice sizing**: Total stakeholder cost, not inverse to worker contribution — confirmed as settled by all six parties.
- **Target figure flexibility**: Management has confirmed it will accept a smaller verified gap than €4bn if the audit's volume assumptions support that.
- **Process commitments from labour**: Works Council and Union have both made specific, costed commitments to good-faith negotiation on further *non-closure* measures (deepened/extended bridge, working-time, attrition, runway) if the audit shows a residual — a concrete advance on prior rounds, acknowledged as genuine by Management, State, Family Holding, and Investors alike.
- **Union strike restraint** continues through the audit window, conditional on labour-cost standing being honoured in the actual terms of reference.

## 2. Core Disagreement Remaining

A single fault line now dominates, articulated most sharply by the State, Family Holding, and Investors, and pressed by Management:

- **Works Council and Union position**: Closures and compulsory redundancy remain outside the negotiable set *regardless of audit outcome, at any residual size, full stop* — even if the non-compulsory sequence is genuinely tested, priced, and shown to fail.
- **State, Family Holding, Investors, Management position**: This is not a conditional, evidence-responsive commitment but a fixed exclusion immune to verified facts — structurally inconsistent with the standard labour itself applies to others (refusing to pre-commit board votes/strike mandates to unseen outcomes). They are asking not for pre-authorization of compulsory measures, but for confirmation that *if* the sequence is genuinely exhausted and a verified structural shortfall remains, the question gets negotiated on the facts rather than foreclosed in advance.

This is now explicitly named by four parties (Management, State, Family Holding, Investors) as the single largest unresolved issue — more consequential to deal-certainty pricing than any other open point.

## 3. Key Trade-offs Still Unaddressed

- **Whether the "genuinely exhausted" determination itself is jointly verified** (State's proposal) versus asserted unilaterally by either side — not yet agreed as a mechanism.
- **What happens procedurally if the sequence fails**: Investors have asked for a defined endpoint/timeline for the "further negotiation" stage itself, so it doesn't become an open-ended second delay. Not yet addressed by any party.
- **The scope of the cost-of-delay pricing comparison**: Management wants the full range of structural tools priced against extension; State, Family Holding, and Investors want sequencing preserved (price extension/bridge first, on its own terms). This has been resolved in favor of sequencing by three parties, but Management has not yet formally conceded the point.
- **Executive compensation specifics** under the owner-sacrifice principle — flagged by Management as forthcoming but not yet detailed.

## 4. Refocus for Round 5

The discussion should concentrate on one narrow, specific question, now posed identically by the State, Family Holding, and Investors:

> **If the full non-compulsory sequence is genuinely tested, priced, and verified to fail — not assumed, but shown — does the question of further measures, including any headcount tool, get negotiated on the facts at that point, or does it remain categorically excluded regardless of what is verified?**

The Works Council and Union are asked to respond to this specific formulation directly — distinguishing, if they can, between "ruling out compulsory measures as a first resort or for a manageable residual" (a position with broad support in the room) and "ruling it out unconditionally regardless of verified, exhausted-sequence findings" (the position now contested by four other parties). A second, secondary item for Round 5: whether any party will propose a concrete mechanism or timeline for the second-stage negotiation, should the first sequence fail.
