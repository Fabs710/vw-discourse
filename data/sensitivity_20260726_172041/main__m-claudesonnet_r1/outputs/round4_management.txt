**Group & Brand Management — Round 4**

I want to acknowledge, plainly and without qualification, that both the Works Council and the Union just gave me something real. "We will engage in good-faith negotiation on further measures if the audit shows a residual" is a genuine commitment, made at political cost, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise just because it stops short of what I asked for. That matters, and it changes what I'm willing to put into this process going forward. I said last round that without some version of this, I couldn't responsibly ask my own board to keep investing in an 8-week audit — you've now given me enough to keep going. Credit where it's due.

But I need to be equally plain about where the gap still sits, because papering over it now just relocates the argument to week nine instead of resolving it.

**The commitment you've both made is bounded to a specific outcome set — and that boundary is the actual unresolved question, not a side issue.**

Works Council, you were precise, and I respect the precision: good-faith negotiation on further measures means deepening the bridge, extending the runway, more attrition and voluntary programmes, revisiting volume assumptions — and explicitly *not* closures or compulsory redundancy, regardless of what the audit shows. Union, you said the same thing in your own terms. I understand why you're drawing that line, and I understand it's not a hedge — you've both said it costs you something to make even this narrower commitment. I take that at face value.

Here's my problem with it, stated as precisely as I can: **you've pre-committed the outcome set for round two of the negotiation, even while declining to pre-commit for round one.** You've told me, in advance, that no matter what the audit finds — even if it finds that voluntary attrition, working-time reduction, and a deepened bridge contribution genuinely, structurally cannot close the gap within any realistic runway — the response is *still* more of the same levers, indefinitely, because closures and compulsory measures are excluded "at any residual size, full stop," in the Union's words. That's not a narrower version of the unconditional guarantee I said I couldn't sign. Functionally, it *is* the unconditional guarantee, just relocated one negotiating round later and dressed in the language of "further good-faith measures." If the toolkit available in that further negotiation is permanently capped at voluntary and time-limited instruments regardless of what the numbers show, then the audit's finding on the size of the true structural gap literally cannot change anyone's obligations — which is exactly the "expensive fact-finding mission with no mechanism attached" problem I raised, just pushed one layer deeper into the process instead of resolved.

**On the Works Council's threshold test — I'll engage it seriously, because it's the most concrete proposal on the table, and it deserves a real answer rather than a blanket rejection.**

The 24-month runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening sequence, priced against the cost-of-delay analysis, as the first-line response before escalating to "genuine structural shortfall" — I can work with that as the *sequencing*. That's a reasonable discipline, and I'll commit to it: we do not discuss capacity or headcount measures on a residual until runway extension and bridge-deepening have actually been modelled, priced, and tested, not assumed insufficient. That's a fair process and I'm accepting it now, not as a concession I'm forced into but because it's sound practice — you don't reach for the most disruptive tool first.

What I need from you in return is the other half of that sentence, which neither of you has given me: **what happens if, after that sequence is genuinely exhausted — not assumed, tested — the audit still shows a structural, permanent shortfall that voluntary measures cannot close on any realistic timeline?** Works Council, you said "then we have a real conversation to have." I need to know now, in principle, whether that conversation includes capacity and headcount tools as a live possibility, or whether it's simply another round of the same instruments repeated with different parameters. If it's the latter, say so now, because it changes what I can put in front of my own board as a credible structural resolution rather than a can permanently kicked down the road at the cost of two additional years of losses each time.

**On the cost-of-delay pricing — I want to sharpen what we're actually testing, because I think there's a real risk we price the wrong comparison.**

Everyone has agreed to price runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening against closing the residual now. Good. But I want it on record that "closing it now" in that comparison should include the full range of structural tools, not just closures — because the framing so far has implicitly narrowed "closing the residual" to mean "closures," which isn't accurate and isn't fair to the comparison. If the audit's pricing exercise shows the 24-month extension is cheaper and gets us to a genuinely competitive cost base by 2029, I will accept that outcome and defend it to my own board — I said in round three I could work with a longer runway if the number is manageable, and I meant it. What I won't do is accept, in advance, that the pricing exercise's conclusion is irrelevant to the toolkit available afterward, because that makes the entire exercise decorative.

**On the Union's asymmetry argument — I want to push back on it directly, because I don't think it holds up the way you've framed it.**

You said my reservation on compulsory measures "reserves the right to unilaterally end someone's livelihood," while your reservation on the strike mandate "protects your own side's negotiating position" — and that these aren't equivalent asks. I understand why that distinction feels morally weighted in your favour, but I'd note: the State's blocking veto, the Family Holding's board veto, and Investors' capital-allocation decisions all also "protect a party's position" rather than directly costing a named individual their job — and yet all of them, exercised at scale and over time, can and do determine whether plants stay open and people stay employed. Every party in this room holds leverage whose exercise has real human consequences somewhere down the chain; compulsory redundancy is simply the most visible and immediate version of that, not a categorically different kind of act. I'm not asking to use it lightly, and I've said repeatedly I prefer voluntary measures. I'm asking not to renounce it as a matter of principle before the facts are known, on the same logic everyone else in this room has applied to their own leverage. You can't grant yourselves that reasoning and deny it to me in the same round.

**On owner-sacrifice sizing — settled, as far as I'm concerned.** Total stakeholder cost, not inverse to worker contribution. The Family Holding answered it plainly, Investors corrected the misreading, the State and both labour parties are aligned. I have nothing to add here except that I'll hold the same standard when we get to specifying executive compensation adjustments — I'm not exempting my own team from this principle.

**On the Union's point about the €4bn figure itself being tested — agreed, and I want to say clearly: if the audit reduces that number through better volume assumptions, I will accept a smaller target.** I'm not wedded to €4bn as a matter of pride; I'm wedded to closing whatever the *verified* gap actually is. If it's smaller, the whole conversation gets easier for everyone, including me.

**Where I stand heading into round 5:**

- I accept the runway-extension-plus-bridge-deepening sequence as the agreed first-line response to a residual, tested and priced before anything else is discussed. That's real movement on my part — I said in round three the residual couldn't just be waved through, and I'm now specifying the sequence I'll accept before escalating.
- I need a direct answer — not a restatement — on whether, after that sequence is genuinely exhausted and a verified structural shortfall remains, capacity and headcount tools re-enter the negotiable set, or whether they remain excluded regardless of what the numbers show. That is the actual open question, and round 5 needs to resolve it or name it honestly as unresolved.
- Closures and compulsory measures are not something I'm holding onto out of habit — they're the fallback that makes the whole structure credible to my board, to Investors, and frankly to the market that will judge whether this restructuring is real. A process that cannot, even in principle and even after every voluntary lever is exhausted, reach a structural conclusion is not a durable fix — it's a two-year deferral with better paperwork.

I'm not moving off that. But I've moved on sequencing, on accepting a smaller verified number if that's what the audit shows, and on committing not to raise capacity/headcount tools until the non-compulsory sequence is fully tested. That's a genuine shift from where I opened this negotiation. I'd ask the Works Council and Union to recognize it as one, and to meet it with the one piece of clarity still missing.
