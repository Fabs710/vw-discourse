[Controlling Family Holding — Round 2]

I've been asked directly, twice now, by the State and the Union, to stop saying "we'll raise it with the board" and put something concrete on the table. That's a fair challenge, and I'll answer it — but let me first address the two structural questions this round has actually resolved, because the sacrifice question only makes sense in that context.

**On the milestone/recourse architecture:** I want to be explicit that I agree with the State's reading of Management's revised position, not the Works Council's original characterization of it. There is a real difference between "management reopens closures unilaterally when a KPI is missed" and "an independently verified, jointly-run process concludes the package hasn't closed the gap, and the parties come back to the table together to decide the next step." The first is a hollow guarantee dressed up in new language, and I would not have supported it either. The second is something I can back, on one condition the State has already named: "come back to the table together" has to mean genuine joint decision-making at that point — board-level, co-determination intact — not a management fait accompli with the appearance of consultation. If that's written into the process design now, as the State proposes, then I support this structure. Works Council, I understand your instinct to read any conditionality as 2029 repeating itself. But an unconditional guarantee that ignores whether the underlying economics actually hold is not a guarantee either — it's a promise the balance sheet cannot keep, and we would be back here in three years with less capital, less trust, and fewer options than we have today. That outcome is not one I will underwrite, however it's framed. Protecting the enterprise's long-term value is the one thing I will not trade away in this negotiation, and an unconditional freeze on the footprint, if the €4 billion gap turns out to be real, is exactly the kind of permanent impairment I cannot accept.

**On the €4 billion figure and the joint modelling process:** I support the 8-week window, external audit, and full works-council and union access to the underlying assumptions. I'd add one thing from the ownership side: I will ask the supervisory board to ensure the audit's terms of reference are agreed jointly before the clock starts, not designed by management alone and presented for sign-off. If this process is going to bear the weight everyone in this room is now placing on it — including my own willingness to commit owner-side sacrifice — its credibility has to be beyond reasonable dispute on all sides. That's not a concession to any one party; it's what makes the whole structure durable enough that I'd actually stake governance capital defending it.

**Now, the number you're asking for.** I will not pretend I can commit the family holding's dividend policy unilaterally in this forum — that runs through the full supervisory board and, for certain elements, the annual general meeting, and anyone who tells you otherwise is overstating their own authority. But I will say plainly what I am prepared to bring, and to defend, within the same 8-week window and in the same board resolution as any workforce contribution: a multi-year dividend reduction and a capped, performance-linked executive compensation structure, sized proportionately to the scale of the workforce contribution that emerges from the joint modelling — not a token gesture, and not sequenced after the fact. I will not name a precise percentage today, because doing so before the €4 billion figure has been tested would be exactly the kind of unverified number I'm asking others not to accept from Management uncritically. But the principle I will commit to now, in this room: if the workforce and management sides are being asked for a verified, quantified contribution, the owners carry a comparable, visible burden, ratified concurrently, not promised for later. That commitment is real, and I intend to hold myself to it in front of the board, not just in this discussion.

**On sequencing more broadly** — Works Council and Union, I hear the concern about promises made and later hollowed out, and I don't dismiss it lightly given the family holding's own long horizon depends on this company's credibility, not just this quarter's. Concurrent ratification, same document, is something I can support in principle. What I cannot support is concurrent ratification of a number that hasn't been tested. The sequencing you need is: joint modelling concludes, owner sacrifice and workforce contribution are ratified together, in the same resolution. That's compatible with what you're asking; it just requires the modelling to actually finish first, which is why I share the urgency around the 8-week clock rather than an open-ended one.

**One last point, to the Union directly:** I agree the wage agreement is your jurisdiction, not a board matter, and nothing I've said should be read as suggesting otherwise. That boundary is not something the family holding has any interest in crossing.

Where I stand after this round: I back the joint modelling process, the shared-verification recourse structure with genuine joint decision-making at the review point, and a concrete, proportionate owner-side sacrifice ratified concurrently with any workforce contribution — once the numbers are actually tested. I will not back an unconditional site guarantee regardless of what the audit shows, and I will not back forcing a board vote before that process concludes. Those two limits have not moved since round one, and they will not move by being asked again.
