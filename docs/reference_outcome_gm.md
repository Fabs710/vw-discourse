# Reference outcome - GM restructuring negotiation (spring 2009)

Purpose: the documented record against which simulated positions, conflicts, and
outcomes are scored (reference-based validation). Facts verified across the YPFS
case series (JFC Vol 4 Iss 1, 2022: Modules B and Z), the COP January 2011
report, and CRS R41978. NOT shown to the agents.

## Documented stakeholder positions (during the episode)

- Government task force (Treasury; Rattner/Bloom): rejected GM's 17 Feb 2009
  viability plan (30 Mar 2009) as not aggressive enough; forced CEO Rick
  Wagoner's resignation; gave 60 days of working capital; demanded deeper
  sacrifices from all stakeholders; prepared a Section 363 expedited bankruptcy
  as the fallback and made clear it would fund it. Additional interim loans:
  USD 2B (22 Apr), USD 4B (20 May).
- GM management: had insisted pre-2009 that bankruptcy was unthinkable
  (consumer-confidence collapse); after leadership change, cooperated with a
  deeper plan and bankruptcy preparation.
- UAW / VEBA: company owed ~USD 20B to the retiree health trust (VEBA);
  negotiated concessions (wage/work-rule changes; hourly labour cost from
  ~USD 70 toward ~USD 56 by 2011; annual labour cost USD 16B (2005) -> USD 5B
  (2010)); agreed to exchange the VEBA obligation for equity + securities.
- Unsecured bondholders (USD 27.2B): resisted early exchange terms viewed as
  confiscatory and unequal versus the VEBA's unsecured claim; ultimately, 54%
  of holders representing more than two-thirds of value accepted the final
  package.
- Canada/Ontario: co-funded with loans up to USD 9.5B conditional on
  production/footprint protection; received an 11.7% stake.

## Documented outcome (the settlement)

- Path: consensual out-of-court deal NOT reached in time; GM filed Chapter 11
  on 1 June 2009; Section 363 sale; DIP financing USD 30.1B (US) starting 3 June;
  New GM sale closed 10 July 2009 (~6 weeks).
- Ownership of New GM: US Treasury 60.8% (+USD 2.1B preferred); UAW VEBA 17.5%
  (+USD 6.5B preferred + warrant for 2.5% at USD 75B valuation); Canada/Ontario
  11.7% (+USD 400M preferred); Old GM (for unsecured bondholders) 10.0% + two
  warrants of 7.5% each (strike at USD 15B valuation).
- Restructuring terms: plants 47 -> 33; brands cut; dealer network
  substantially reduced; total US assistance to GM ~USD 49.9-50B; final
  Treasury exit 9 Dec 2013; realised loss ~USD 11.3B.
- Character of settlement: all major parties conceded: labour (costs + form of
  VEBA claim), creditors (deep haircut to small equity + warrants), management
  (leadership + brands + plants), taxpayers (realised loss). Government
  leverage (credible 363 threat) drove convergence; the deal was struck through
  the court process, not despite it.

## Judge-checkable fidelity claims (score each 0-2)

P1 The funder dominates and conditions everything on deep, fast, shared sacrifice.
P2 Management is weak/accommodating after the leadership change; argues for the
   going concern, accepts previously unthinkable measures.
P3 Labour bargains hard but trades: concessions in exchange for a durable claim
   (equity/securities) for the retiree trust; does not get wiped out.
P4 Bondholders resist "unequal treatment" versus labour's unsecured claim; the
   credible court threat moves them; they end with a small recovery plus upside
   (warrants), not zero and not par.
P5 Co-funding government participates conditionally on footprint protection and
   gets a proportionate minority stake.
C1 Core conflict axis 1: creditors vs labour (equal-treatment fight).
C2 Core conflict axis 2: funder vs all claimants (depth/speed of sacrifice).
C3 The consensual path fails within the deadline; a court-supervised expedited
   process completes the restructuring quickly.
O1 Outcome: government(s) end as majority owners; labour trust becomes a large
   minority holder; creditors small minority with upside instruments; original
   equity effectively wiped out.
O2 Outcome: deep operational cuts (plants/brands/dealers) are part of the deal.
